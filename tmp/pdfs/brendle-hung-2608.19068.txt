                                                                            1. Introduction
                                              A central topic in differential geometry is to understand the interplay be-
                                           tween curvature and topology of Riemannian manifolds. A natural question
                                           is which manifolds admit metrics of positive sectional curvature. The three-
                                           dimensional case is well understood by the famous work of Hamilton [4].
                                           Hamilton showed that a three-manifold that admits a metric of positive sec-
                                           tional curvature is diffeomorphic to a quotient of S 3 by standard isometries.
                                           Hsiang and Kleiner [5] studied four-manifolds with positive sectional cur-
                                           vature and continuous symmetries. They showed that a four-manifold with
                                           positive sectional curvature that admits a non-trivial Killing vector field
                                           must be homeomorphic to S 4 or CP2 . In particular, a metric on S 2 × S 2
                                           that admits a non-trivial Killing vector field cannot have positive sectional
                                           curvature.
                                              The following is the main result of this paper.
                                           Theorem 1.1. There exists a metric on S 2 × S 2 with positive sectional
                                           curvature.
                                              In a fundamental paper, Cheeger [3] introduced new tools for constructing
                                           metrics with nonnegative sectional curvature via group actions. In particu-
                                           lar, Cheeger’s construction gives non-trivial examples of metrics on S 2 × S 2
                                           with nonnegative sectional curvature (see also [1]). The resulting metrics
                                           were studied further by Müter [6]. We refer to them as Cheeger-Müter

   In the next step, we fix a Cheeger-Müter metric, and consider a small
perturbation of the metric. We construct a third order perturbation of the
metric of the form
                       g̃s = g + s h(1) + s2 h(2) + s3 h(3) .
Here, g denotes a Cheeger-Müter metric, h(1) , h(2) , h(3) are symmetric (0, 2)-
tensors on S 2 × S 2 , and s is a positive real number.
   Let us fix a point in Mgeneric . For each s, we consider the minimum sec-
tional curvature of the metric g̃s at the given point, where the minimum is
taken over all two-planes at the given point. We show that the first varia-
tion of the minimum sectional curvature is equal to 0. Moreover, we show
that the second variation of the minimum sectional curvature is nonnegative
everywhere and is strictly positive away from a special 2D torus. Finally,
we show that, along this special torus, the third variation of the sectional
curvature is equal to a non-zero constant µ.
   We now distinguish two cases, depending on the sign of µ. If µ is positive,
then the minimum sectional curvature of the metric g̃s at any given point
in Mgeneric is strictly positive if s > 0 is sufficiently small. Similarly, if µ
is negative, then the minimum sectional curvature of the metric g̃s at any
given point in Mgeneric is strictly positive if −s > 0 is sufficiently small. In
order to analyze the behavior near ∆+ and ∆− , we argue by continuity,
exploiting the fact that Mgeneric is a dense subset of M . This step requires
additional arguments (see Section 2).
   Finally, we note that perturbations of product metrics were studied in the
work of Bourguignon, Deschamps, and Sentenac [2].
