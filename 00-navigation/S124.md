## General theorem for every one-vertex tournament extension

[Canonical weighted-cut law](../05-knowledge/results/session124_tournament_cut_response_20261031/README.md):
for any parent tournamentT and new vertex with out-neighbor setS,

    H(T+x_S)=H(T)+2 sum_{u in S,v outside S,u→v} b_uv,
    b_uv=(W_uv+W_vu)/2 >= H(T−{u,v}) >= 1,

where W_uv counts ordered nonempty two-path covers ending/starting at u/v.
The weights are positive integers and the canonical orientation is T itself.
Proved consequences include strict submodularity, exact SCC-suffix equality,
gain gcd, constrained/minimum-positive min-cut algorithms, and small-gain
pair-deletion/triangle-packing restrictions. The weight calculation is exponential
subset DP; the optimization is polynomial only after the weights are supplied.

Cardinality-only responses are characterized by regularity plus equal weights.
Arc-transitive tournaments of order at least3 obey H+b|S|(n−|S|); finite
Paley7/11 controls give genuinely restrictive arithmetic examples. Neither this
formula nor a cut optimizer establishes recursively closed realization banks.

cut law and same-order edge reversal to inductively establish oddness, then

oddness or of integral weights. Rédei's parity theorem remains classical.

[Affine parity interfaces](../05-knowledge/results/session124_affine_parity_interfaces_20261031/AFFINE_INTERFACES.md)

solution does not prove Hamiltonian connectedness or a numerical glue-word
realizer. The categorical bridge concerns the proved constraint layer only.
