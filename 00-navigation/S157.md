# Session 157: the 7 and 21 gaps

Every finite tournament has a number of directed Hamiltonian paths different from 7 and 21. The proofs here use the established Busch lower bound for strong tournaments, elementary strong-component factorization, and exact finite checks. They do not prove that all other positive odd counts occur.

- [Short exclusion proof](../05-knowledge/results/session157_gaps_20261103/gaps/gaps-note.md): a hand-checkable small-order argument for 7 and a 512-orientation certificate for 21.
- [Odd-cycle packing approach](../05-knowledge/results/session157_gaps_20261103/cycles/README.md): an independent finite reduction, including symmetry tables whose completeness is computationally checked.
- [Supplementary finite certificate](../05-knowledge/results/session157_gaps_20261103/supplement/NOTE.md): direct path enumeration and a 35-class strong order-six census.
- [Strong one-apex modular universality](../05-knowledge/results/session157_gaps_20261103/universal/NOTE.md): every parity-eligible residue modulo every fixed modulus occurs infinitely often as a strong one-apex count. This does not imply exact integer coverage: in particular, [Session 149](https://github.com/eliottcassidy2000/math/blob/9b75ef18bff76bd9e4ecb9fb6e8711f7d91c4a3c/00-navigation/S149.md) records that 13 itself has no strong one-apex witness.
- [Explicit single-prime family](../05-knowledge/results/session157_gaps_20261103/prime_power/NOTE.md), [strong two-apex 3-adic family](../05-knowledge/results/session157_gaps_20261103/strong3/NOTE.md), and [paired one-apex family](../05-knowledge/results/session157_gaps_20261103/third/NOTE.md) show complementary modular mechanisms and their scope limits.

Source credits, reproduction commands and boundaries of each claim appear in the linked notes. The global spectrum classification remains open.
