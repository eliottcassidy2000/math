# Session 168: computation, cellular clocks, and Cayley–Dickson transports

This session composes previously studied tournament interfaces with Cayley–Dickson algebra, cellular automata, and Turing-machine ideas. The constructions distinguish scheduled, marked physical arc flips from autonomous tournament dynamics. They do not settle the tournament Hamiltonian-path spectrum conjecture or classify the blue/black complement-line metagraph.

1. [Marked tournament computation](../05-knowledge/results/session168_computational_structures_20261103/turing/NOTE.md): equal-profile binary modules implement bounded register and tape steps by literal arc flips, and a growing marked system simulates arbitrary computation. Program control, ordered module observation, tape allocation and insertion are part of the model; neither a fixed finite graph nor Hamilton count alone is claimed universal.
2. [Cayley–Dickson bit circuits](../05-knowledge/results/session168_computational_structures_20261103/cayley_dickson/NOTE.md): explicit signs and kernels give controlled, scheduled arc-flip implementations of signed-basis transports. The one-sided group order and twist recurrence have prior sources credited in the note. The single-flip clique obstruction does not prohibit other unknown class realizations.
3. [Reversible cellular lifts](../05-knowledge/results/session168_computational_structures_20261103/cellular/NOTE.md): a six-state physical cover of a ternary switch lifts controlled reversible gates and specified reversible local dynamics to equal-profile tournaments. The cell geometry and gate schedule are marked sidecars; logical return can retain a physical deck bit.
4. [Tower transport structure](../05-knowledge/results/session168_computational_structures_20261103/tower/STRUCTURE.md): lower-central structure and graph-cover capacity distinguish full transport monodromy, requiring at least 2n independent cycles, from merely connected canonical monodromy, requiring n. These are formal signed-unit covers, not physical tournament-class realizations; the order-six base graph's counts are inherited.
5. [Signed transport and cellular clocks](../05-knowledge/results/session168_computational_structures_20261103/clocks/NOTE.md): an explicit local modular clock and marked signed-unit register yield exact period families 26·3^a, 52·3^a, and 104·3^a within a constant-profile encoding. The four-lap ceiling concerns the additional multiplier of one fixed-level signed-unit register over a given periodic clock, not the period of arbitrary cellular dynamics.

Each packet gives proofs, finite certificates, source credits, and scope boundaries. Independent audits and reproducible checks accompany the constructions where supplied. Equal path-cover profiles conceal state and history; they do not by themselves furnish an intrinsic computation rule.
