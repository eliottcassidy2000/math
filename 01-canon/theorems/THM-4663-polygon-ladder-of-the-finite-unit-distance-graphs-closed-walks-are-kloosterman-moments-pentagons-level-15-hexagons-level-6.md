## Proof

**1.** `G_q` is a Cayley graph of the abelian group `F_(q^2)`; its eigenvalue at the character `z ↦ ψ(Tr(bz))` is
`Σ_(u ∈ μ) ψ(Tr(bu))`, which is `q + 1` at `b = 0` and `-K_q(N(b))` otherwise (THM-4628 (b): the norm-one torus sum
is minus the Kloosterman sum). Multiplication by `μ` permutes the `q + 1` elements of each norm class, so each
`a ∈ F_q^×` occurs `q + 1` times. Vertex-transitivity gives `W_k = q^(-2) tr A^k`.

**2.** `W_2` is the degree. A closed 3-walk is `(u_1, u_2, -(u_1 + u_2))` with `N(u_1 + u_2) = 1`; with
`ω = u_2/u_1 ∈ μ` this is `(1 + ω)(1 + ω^(-1)) = 1`, i.e. `ω^2 + ω + 1 = 0`: two solutions in `μ` iff `3 | q + 1`, none if
`q ≡ 1 (mod 3)`, the double root `ω = 1` in characteristic 3. Codegree: for `d ≠ 0`, `u` and `d - u` both have norm one
iff `t = d̄u` satisfies `t^2 - ct + c = 0`, `c = N(d)` (and then `N(t) = c` is automatic when the roots are conjugate):
two solutions when `c(c - 4)` is a nonsquare (conjugate roots), none when it is a nonzero square (the roots lie in
`F_q` and `N(t) = t^2 ≠ c` unless `t = 2`), one when `c = 4`. For `W_4`: given `u_1, u_2`, the closing pairs
`(u_3, u_4)` number `codeg(u_1 + u_2)`: 2 for `u_2 ≠ ±u_1` (the solutions `(-u_1, -u_2), (-u_2, -u_1)`), 1 for `u_2 = u_1`
(`N(2u_1) = 4`), and `q + 1` for `u_2 = -u_1`. So `W_4 = 2(q^2 - 1) + (q + 1) + (q + 1)^2 = 3q(q + 1)`.

**3.** Exact integers `M_k(p)` for `k ≤ 8` (four word-size primes `l ≡ 1 mod p`, a primitive `p`-th root of unity in
`F_l`, the Kloosterman vector as a cyclic convolution of length `p - 1`, Chinese remainder theorem; Weil's bound
`|M_k| ≤ (p - 1)(2 sqrt p)^k` certifies the range; float FFT agreement for `p < 200`). The `M_5` polynomial part was
fitted on the 218 inert primes and holds there exactly; on the 209 split primes the residual is exactly `p^2 a_p`
(98 principal, 111 nonprincipal, one global sign). `M_6`, `M_8` hold exactly on all 428 primes. The symmetric powers
follow from `(α + β)^k = Σ_j (C(k,j) - C(k,j-1)) (αβ)^j tr Sym^(k-2j)` with `αβ = p`; direct numerical
`S_j` (roots of `X^2 + K(a) X + p`) agree for `p = 11, 13, 17, 19`.

**4.** In a `d`-regular graph the non-simple closed 5-walks from a vertex are (free reduction) the triangle walks with
one inserted backtrack, `(4d - 3)` per directed triangle, and the lollipops (an edge to `x`, a triangle at `x` whose
two `x`-neighbours avoid the start), here `(q+1)(2q - 2)`; with `2(q+1)` directed triangles this is `10q(q+1)` for
`q ≡ 2 (mod 3)` (Harary-Manvel's `5(d - 1) W_3`), and zero otherwise. Then `C_5 = q^2 (W_5 - [q ≡ 2] 10q(q+1)) / 10`.

**5.** Classify a closed 6-walk from `v` by its free reduction (lengths 0, 4, 6): (a) reduction empty: the closed
6-walks of the `d`-regular tree, `d(5d^2 - 6d + 2)`; (b) a 4-cycle through `v` (`q^2 - 1` reduced walks, since
`W_4 = d(2d - 1) + 2·#C_4(v)` gives `#C_4(v) = (q^2 - 1)/2`) with one inserted backtrack, `5d - 4` distinct walks each;
(c) reduced of length 6 other than a 6-cycle: lollipops (stem `vx`, a 4-cycle at `x` with neither `x`-neighbour equal
to `v`; every edge lies in `q - 1` four-cycles by arc-transitivity), `(q+1)(q-1)^2`; and, when `q ≡ 2 (mod 3)`, a
triangle traversed twice and figure-eights of two triangles at `v` (together `4q(q+1)`: reduced concatenations of two
directed triangle walks at `v`), figure-eights at another vertex `u` of the first triangle (`8(q+1)(q-1)`: at each of the two split
points `2q - 1` second triangles keep the walk reduced, and the continuation of the first triangle gives the doubled
triangle already counted in the `4q(q+1)` family, leaving `2q - 2`), less the `8(q+1)` walks counted twice (per directed triangle at `v`: one
walk with the second triangle on the shared edge `xy` read from both split points, two walks that are also
figure-eights at `v`; and per stem, the two traversals of the 4-cycle `x z v z'` through the common neighbours, which
are also figure-eights at `v`). Summing gives `D(p)`; `C_6 = q^2 (W_6 - D)/12`. Direct counts agree for all 21 primes
`5 ≤ p ≤ 83`.

**8.** `hom_0(θ(2,2,3)) = Σ_d R_2(d)^2 R_3(d)` (`R_k(d)` = walks of length `k` from 0 to `d`, a function of `N(d)`).
By statement 2, `R_2(d) = codeg(d) ∈ {0, 2}` for `N(d) ∉ {0, 4}` and `= 1` at `N(d) = 4`, so
`R_2^2 = 2R_2 - [N(d) = 4]` off `d = 0`; with `Σ_d R_2 R_3 = W_5` and `R_2(0) = p + 1` this gives the identity. The
