# THM-2208 -- rank eleven plus one relation is one primitive slope

Put

```text
Q=91^6=567869252041,
H=78*182^13=18750922831149193194381342621696.        (1)
```

Let `v in Z_(>0)^13` be primitive and repetition-free, and suppose its
`1/14`-safe set has Haar measure zero. THM-2052 gives eleven independent
integer relations of support at most three and height at most `Q`. Put them
in a matrix

```text
B in Z^(11 x 13),       rank(B)=11,       Bv=0,       (2)
```

and set

```text
S=ker_Q(B),             C=S_R intersection R_(>=0)^13. (3)
```

THM-2199 applied to `S` and `v` supplies an integer relation

```text
a.v=0,        ||a||_infinity<=H,
a notin row_Q(B).                                      (4)
```

The conclusion below is attached to the finite state `(B,a)`, not to `B`
alone: different admissible rows `a` can cut the same two-cone at different
slopes.

## 1. The two-ray formula

Since `dim_Q(S)=2` and `v` is strictly positive, `C` is a full-dimensional
pointed rational cone in the real plane `S_R`. Hence it has exactly two
extreme rays. Let their primitive nonnegative integral generators be

```text
u_0,u_1.                                               (5)
```

Strict positivity puts `v` in the relative interior, so

```text
v=alpha u_0+beta u_1,          alpha,beta>0.           (6)
```

Write

```text
q_i=a.u_i.                                             (7)
```

The restriction of `a` to `S` is nonzero by (4). If one `q_i` vanished,
the equality

```text
0=a.v=alpha q_0+beta q_1
```

would force the other to vanish as well, contradicting that nonzero
restriction. Thus `q_0,q_1` are both nonzero, and (6) forces opposite
signs. The unique ray of `C` annihilated by `a` is therefore generated by

```text
w=|q_1|u_0+|q_0|u_1.                                  (8)
```

Because the stack `[B;a]` has rank twelve, its rational kernel is precisely
the line `Qv`. It follows that

```text
v=prim(w),                                             (9)
```

where `prim` divides an integral vector by its coordinate gcd. This proves
that every admissible finite state `(B,a)` has at most one primitive
positive candidate.

## 2. Explicit ray and candidate bounds

Each extreme ray of the two-cone lies on at least one coordinate wall.
Choose a coordinate `j` vanishing on that ray. The matrix obtained by
stacking the row `e_j` under `B` has rank twelve, and its signed maximal
cofactors reduce to `11 x 11` minors of `B`. Every row of such a minor has
at most three nonzero entries, each of size at most `Q`. Hadamard therefore
gives

```text
||u_i||_infinity
 <=(sqrt(3)Q)^11
 <3^6 Q^11=:M.                                        (10)
```

The final speed bound must be taken directly from `[B;a]`, rather than by
separately multiplying the bounds on `q_i` and `u_i`. In every
`12 x 12` minor of `[B;a]`, the first eleven row norms are at most
`sqrt(3)Q`, while the final row norm is at most `sqrt(12)H`. Hence

```text
max_i v_i
 <=sqrt(12*3^11) Q^11 H
 =1458 Q^11 H                                         (11)
```

and primitive normalization only decreases the cofactors. Numerically the

## 3. A finite relation-state decision procedure

The complete universe of possible support-two or support-three
height-`Q` relation rows has size at most

```text
R_3=C(13,2)(2Q)^2+C(13,3)(2Q)^3
   =418987514864008676803457209861414151720.          (12)
```

Thus `R_3^11` ordered matrices overcount all choices of `B`. For a fixed
`B`, (10) and (4) give

```text
0<|a.u_i|<=13HM.                                      (13)
```

Consequently

```text
R_3^11(13HM)^2                                        (14)
```

is a crude explicit overcount of relation-space/projective-slope states.
One can instead enumerate the finitely many height-`H` rows `a`; either
description is exact after checking independence and the opposite-sign
gate in (7).

For every surviving `(B,a)`, formula (9), or equivalently the maximal
cofactors of `[B;a]`, produces its sole primitive candidate. Positivity,
distinctness, and primitive normalization are exact integer tests. The
weak safe-set decision is then finite exact rational endpoint/cell
arithmetic; the legacy
`THM-668-pair-sum-ruler-witness-structure.md` gives a particularly small
event set.

This is not the first finiteness theorem: THM-763 gives a vastly smaller
scalar speed-sum box. The gain here is structural. THM-2052's unbounded
two-anchor parameter and THM-2196's two carry directions collapse to one
primitive candidate once the bounded twelfth relation is retained. No
fixed residue quotient may replace the actual integer rays and their
Archimedean slope: THM-2188 has identical bounded rank/congruence packets
with opposite safety outcomes.

An independent hostile audit checked the rank-eleven/rank-twelve cases,
full-dimensionality of the positive two-cone, nonvanishing and signs of
`q_0,q_1`, coordinate-wall ray cofactors, the direct `[B;a]` derivation of
the factor `1458`, and the exact-terminal scope. QED.
