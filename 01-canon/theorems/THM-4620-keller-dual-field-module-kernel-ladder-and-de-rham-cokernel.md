# THM-4620 — Keller dual-field modules

## Setting

`K` is a field of characteristic zero, `K[x] = K[x_1, ..., x_n]`, and `F = (F_1, ..., F_n)` is a Keller map:
`det JF ∈ K^×`. Put `B = (JF^T)^(-1)`, a matrix of polynomials, and

    V_j := Σ_k B_(jk) ∂_k        (j = 1, ..., n),

the *dual fields*. Then `V_j F_i = δ_(ij)`, `dh = Σ_j (V_j h) dF_j` for every `h`, the `V_j` are
`K`-derivations of `K[x]` and of `K(x)`, they commute, and on `K(x) ⊇ K(F)` (a finite separable
extension, the `F_i` being algebraically independent) `V_j` is the unique extension of `∂/∂X_j`
acting on `K(F) = K(F_1, ..., F_n)` (THM-4562). The Weyl algebra `A_n = K<X_1..X_n, ∂_1..∂_n>` acts on
`K[x]` by `X_i ↦ F_i·`, `∂_j ↦ V_j`; this is Bavula's twisted module `^σP_n`, written `V_F` here.

Fix `j` and write `G_j := (F_i)_(i ≠ j) : A^n → A^(n-1)` (a smooth morphism of relative dimension one),

    R_j := Ker(V_j on K[x]),   L_j := Ker(V_j on K(x)),   N_j := ∪_m Ker(V_j^m on K[x]),
    C^(j)_η := the generic fibre of G_j = Spec( K[x] ⊗_(K[G_j]) K(G_j) ),

`d_j :=` the number of geometric connected components of `C^(j)_η`, and for the components
`C_1, ..., C_(d_j)` (smooth affine curves over an algebraic closure of `K(G_j)`, of genus `γ_i` with
`r_i ≥ 1` places at infinity)

    μ_j := Σ_i (2 γ_i + r_i - 1).

In the plane, `F = (f, g)` with `det J(f, g) = 1` (replace `g` by `g/det J` otherwise): `V_2 = D_f := {f, ·} = f_x ∂_y - f_y ∂_x`
(kernel `K[f]`, slice `g`) and `V_1 = -D_g`; in general `V_2 = D_f/det J`, `V_1 = -D_g/det J`.

## Statement

1. **Kernel ladder.** `R_j ⊇ K[G_j]` is algebraically closed in `K[x]`; `L_j = Frac(R_j)` is the
   algebraic closure of `K(G_j)` in `K(x)` and `[L_j : K(G_j)] = d_j`. For every `m ≥ 1`

       Ker V_j^m = (+)_(i<m) F_j^i R_j,      N_j = R_j[F_j]  (F_j transcendental over R_j).

2. **t-primary torsion.** For every nonzero `ρ ∈ K[t]`, on `K[x]` and on `K(x)`,

       Ker ρ(V_j) = Ker V_j^(ord_0 ρ).

   Equivalently: `V_j` has no eigenvector with nonzero eigenvalue in `K̄(x)`, and no generalized
   one. So the only kernel identities (in Chini's sense) satisfied by the endomorphism `V_j` of the
   `K`-vector space `K[x]` are the `t`-primary ones `Ker ρ(θ) = Ker θ^(ord_0 ρ)`, each a first-order
   sentence in the language of `K`-vector spaces with one endomorphism.

3. **Cokernel = relative de Rham cohomology.** `coker(V_j : K[x] → K[x]) ≅ H^1_dR(K[x]/K[G_j])`, the
   first relative de Rham cohomology of `G_j`; it is a module over `A_(n-1) = K<F_(i≠j), V_(i≠j)>`,
   holonomic (CITED: direct images of holonomic modules), and

       coker(V_j) ⊗_(K[G_j]) K(G_j) ≅ H^1_dR(C^(j)_η / K(G_j)),   of dimension μ_j.

4. **Generic rank zero; surjective dual fields.** If `μ_j = 0` — in particular if `V_j` is surjective on
   `K[x]` — then every geometric component of `C^(j)_η` is a line, `C^(j)_η ≅ A^1_(L_j)` with coordinate
   `F_j`, `K(x) = L_j(F_j)`, and

       deg F = [K(x) : K(F)] = d_j = [L_j : K(G_j)].

   If moreover the generic fibre of `G_j` is geometrically connected (`d_j = 1`, i.e. `L_j = K(G_j)`,
   equivalently `R_j ⊗_(K[G_j]) K(G_j) = K(G_j)`), then `F` is an automorphism, and consequently
   `coker V_j = 0`: a cokernel of generic rank zero can be a nonzero torsion module only when `d_j ≥ 2`.
   Conversely,
   if `V_j` is locally nilpotent then `V_j` is surjective (THM-4562's slice lemma gives
   `K[x] = R_j[F_j]`), so local nilpotence also forces `deg F = d_j`. The connectedness hypothesis
   cannot be dropped in dimension `n ≥ 4`: for a non-invertible Keller map `F'` of `A^(n-1)` (THM-1300
   for `n - 1 = 3`), `F = F' × id_(A^1)` has `V_n = ∂_n` surjective and `d_n = deg F' > 1`. Dimension
   three is HYP-9255.

5. **The plane (KNOWN: Gurjar–Masuda–Miyanishi 2013, Remark 2.5 and Cor. 2.4; re-proved here).** Let
   `(f, g)` be a Keller pair. Then `R = K[f]`, `L = K(f)`, `d = 1`, and the following are equivalent:
   * (a) `F = (f, g)` is an automorphism of `A^2`;
   * (b) `D_f` is surjective on `K[x, y]`;
   * (c) `H^1_dR(A^2/A^1; f) = coker D_f = 0`;
   * (d) `μ(f) = 2γ + r - 1 = 0`, i.e. the generic fibre of `f` is geometrically `A^1`;
   * (e) `D_f` is locally nilpotent (KNOWN);
   * (f) `K[x, y] = K[f, g]` (trivial).

   Moreover `coker D_f` is a holonomic `A_1`-module (the Gauss–Manin system of `f`, with
   `∂_f = -D_g`) which is either `0` or of generic rank `μ(f) ≥ 1`; it is never a nonzero torsion
   module. Hence `JC_2` is equivalent to: *every Keller derivation `D_f` of `K[x, y]` is surjective*
   (Gurjar–Masuda–Miyanishi, Remark 2.5), and to: *no component of a Keller pair has a generic fibre
   with positive first Betti number*. (d)⇒(a) also follows from Abhyankar–Moh–Suzuki; the proof below
   avoids it.

6. **Holonomic package (CITED, Bavula).** `V_F` is holonomic, of finite length bounded explicitly in
   `deg F`, and cyclic; `F` is invertible iff `V_F = A_n · 1`. Here `A_n · 1 = K[F] ≅ K[X]` is the
   standard simple module, so `K[F]` is a simple submodule of `V_F`, and over the open set `U` where
   `F` is proper (the complement of the Jelonek set) `V_F/K[F]` is the trace-zero flat bundle of rank
   `deg F - 1` of the finite étale cover `F^(-1)(U) → U`.

## Proofs

**Preliminaries.** `V_j` is a derivation with `V_j(F_i) = δ_(ij)`; on `K(F)` it is `∂/∂X_j`; on the
finite separable extension `K(x)/K(F)` a derivation is determined by its restriction to `K(F)`
(THM-4562). `R_j` is a subring containing `K[G_j]`. It is algebraically closed in `K[x]`: if `h` is
algebraic over `R_j` with minimal polynomial `p`, then `0 = V_j(p(h)) = p'(h) V_j(h)` with `p'(h) ≠ 0`
(characteristic zero, minimality), so `V_j h = 0`.

**(1).** `L_j ⊇ K(G_j)` is a field of constants of a nonzero derivation of the function field `K(x)`
(transcendence degree `n`), so `trdeg L_j ≤ n - 1`; it contains `K(G_j)` of transcendence degree
`n - 1`, hence is algebraic over `K(G_j)`. Conversely the algebraic closure of `K(G_j)` in `K(x)` is
killed by `V_j`, because a derivation vanishing on a field vanishes on every separable algebraic
extension of it. So `L_j` is that algebraic closure, `R_j = L_j ∩ K[x]`, and `Frac(R_j) = L_j`
(each element of `L_j` lies in `K[x] ⊗_(K[G_j]) K(G_j)`, which is integrally closed in `K(x)` as a
localization of the UFD `K[x]`, so it has the form `h/s` with `s ∈ K[G_j]`, `h ∈ R_j`).
`[L_j : K(G_j)] = d_j` because `C^(j)_η` is geometrically integral over `L_j` (its function field
`K(x)` contains no element algebraic over `L_j` outside `L_j`, and characteristic zero gives
separability), and base change to an algebraic closure then splits it into `[L_j : K(G_j)]`
components.

The ladder: `V_j(F_j^i r) = i F_j^(i-1) r` for `r ∈ R_j` (Leibniz), so `(+)_(i<m) F_j^i R_j ⊆ Ker V_j^m`,
the sum being direct (apply `V_j^(m-1), V_j^(m-2), ...`). Conversely if `V_j^m h = 0` then
`r := V_j^(m-1) h ∈ R_j` and `h - F_j^(m-1) r/(m-1)! ∈ Ker V_j^(m-1)`; induct. Transcendence of `F_j`
over `R_j` is the directness statement. Hence `N_j = R_j[F_j]`.

**(2).** `K[x] ⊂ K(x) ⊂ K̄(x)` compatibly with `V_j` (the same polynomial coefficients), `F` is Keller
over `K̄`, and a kernel over `K` is the `K`-rational part of the kernel over `K̄`; so it suffices to
prove the statement on `K̄(x)`, and we assume `K = K̄`. Write `ρ = t^m Π_i (t - λ_i)^(m_i)` with `λ_i ≠ 0`. By the
Chinese remainder theorem for the commuting operator `V_j`, `Ker ρ(V_j) = Ker V_j^m (+) (+)_i Ker (V_j - λ_i)^(m_i)`.
It remains to show `Ker (V_j - λ)^k = 0` on `K(x)` for `λ ∈ K^×`, `k ≥ 1`; by induction on `k` it
suffices to show `V_j h = λ h ⇒ h = 0`.

*Rational ODE lemma.* Let `K'` be a field of characteristic zero, `t` an indeterminate, `c ∈ K'^×`,
and `a ∈ K'(t)` with `da/dt = c a`. Then `a = 0`. Indeed write `a = p/q` in lowest terms with `q`
monic; `p'q - pq' = c p q` gives `q | p q'`, so `q | q'`, so `q' = 0` (degree), so `q = 1`; then
`p' = c p` forces `p = 0` by comparing degrees.

*Eigenvector lemma.* Let `h ∈ K(x)` with `V_j h = λ h`, `λ ∈ K^×`, and let
`m(T) = T^d + a_(d-1) T^(d-1) + ... + a_0 ∈ K(F)[T]` be its minimal polynomial. Applying the derivation
`V_j` to `m(h) = 0`:

    0 = m^V(h) + m'(h) · λ h,     m^V(T) := Σ_i V_j(a_i) T^i   (degree ≤ d - 1).

The polynomial `N(T) := m^V(T) + λ T m'(T) - λ d m(T)` has degree `≤ d - 1` (the `T^d` terms cancel) and
`N(h) = 0`, so `N = 0` by minimality: `V_j(a_i) = λ (d - i) a_i` for `0 ≤ i < d`. Each `a_i` lies in
`K(F) = K(G_j)(F_j)` on which `V_j = ∂/∂F_j`, and `λ(d - i) ≠ 0`, so the ODE lemma (with
`K' = K(G_j)`, `t = F_j`) gives `a_i = 0`. Thus `m(T) = T^d`, `h^d = 0`, `h = 0`.

**(3).** The `dF_i` form a basis of the free module `Ω^1_(K[x])` (Keller). Hence
`Ω^1_(K[x]/K[G_j]) = Ω^1_(K[x]) / Σ_(i≠j) K[x] dF_i = K[x] dF_j`, the relative differential is
`d_rel h = (V_j h) dF_j` (from `dh = Σ_i (V_i h) dF_i`), and `Ω^2_rel = 0`. So the relative de Rham
complex is `K[x] --V_j--> K[x]`, with `H^0 = R_j` and `H^1 = coker V_j`. It is the complex computing
the D-module direct image `G_(j,+) O_(A^n)` (an affine smooth morphism of relative dimension one), so
`coker V_j = H^0(G_(j,+) O)` is holonomic over `A_(n-1)` (Bernstein; CITED); directly, `F_(i≠j)·` and
`V_(i≠j)` commute with `V_j` and satisfy the Weyl relations, which gives the `A_(n-1)`-module
structure. Tensoring the two-term complex with the flat `K[G_j]`-module `K(G_j)` gives the complex
`O(C^(j)_η) → O(C^(j)_η) dF_j`, the de Rham complex of the smooth affine curve `C^(j)_η` over `K(G_j)`;
de Rham cohomology commutes with field extension, and for a smooth affine curve over an algebraically
closed field of characteristic zero with `r` places at infinity and genus `γ`, `dim H^1_dR = 2γ + r - 1`
(CITED). Summing over components gives `μ_j`.

**(4).** Surjectivity gives `coker V_j = 0`, hence `μ_j = 0`; assume now only `μ_j = 0`. Then `γ_i = 0` and `r_i = 1` for every
component, i.e. every geometric component is `P^1` minus one point, `A^1`. Over `L_j` the curve
`C^(j)_η` is geometrically integral (proof of (1)) with geometric model `A^1`; its smooth completion is a
conic with a unique point at infinity, which is therefore `L_j`-rational, so the completion is
`P^1_(L_j)` and `C^(j)_η ≅ A^1_(L_j) = Spec L_j[h]`. The derivation `V_j` of `L_j[h]` kills `L_j`, so
`V_j = p(h) d/dh`; `V_j F_j = 1` gives `p(h) F_j'(h) = 1` in `L_j[h]`, so `F_j' ∈ L_j^×` and
`F_j = α h + β` (`α ∈ L_j^×`): `L_j[h] = L_j[F_j]`. Taking fraction fields, `K(x) = L_j(F_j)` and
`[K(x) : K(F)] = [L_j(F_j) : K(G_j)(F_j)] = [L_j : K(G_j)] = d_j`, since `F_j` is transcendental over
`L_j`. If `d_j = 1` then `F` is birational and étale; by Zariski's main theorem (CITED) a quasi-finite
separated birational morphism to a normal variety is an open immersion, `F : A^n → U ⊆ A^n`. The
complement of `U` contains no divisor (its equation would be a non-constant unit of `O(U) ≅ O(A^n)`),
so it has codimension `≥ 2`, `O(U) = O(A^n)` by normality, the inverse `U → A^n` extends to a
polynomial map `G : A^n → A^n` with `G ∘ F = id`, and then `F ∘ G = id` on the dense set `U`, hence
everywhere: `F` is an automorphism. The local-nilpotence remark: `K[x] = R_j[F_j]` (THM-4562) and
`∂/∂F_j` is surjective on a polynomial ring. The padding example is a direct check: `∂_n` is
surjective on `K[x_1, ..., x_n]`, and the generic fibre of `(F', ) ∘ pr` is (generic fibre of `F'`)
`× A^1`, a union of `deg F'` lines.

**(5).** `R = Ker D_f = K[f]` is KNOWN for a Keller component (Cheng–McKay–Wang 1995, Theorem 1; Moskowicz
arXiv:1802.04685, Theorem 2.3; imported by THM-2230): `Jac(f, g) ∈ K^×` and `Jac(f, w) = 0` force `w ∈ K[f]`.
Directly: `R` is a ring of constants of a nonzero derivation of `K[x, y]`, so it is `K` or `K[h]` for a
single polynomial `h` (Nagata–Nowicki, CITED); `f ∈ R` gives `R = K[h]`, `f = p(h)`, and
`1 = D_f g = p'(h) D_h g` forces `p'(h) ∈ K^×`, so `R = K[f]`, `L = K(f)`, `d = 1`.
(a)⇒(b): if `F` is an automorphism then in the coordinates `(f, g)` the derivation `D_f` is `∂/∂g`.
(b)⇒(c) is (3). (c)⇒(d): `coker D_f = 0` gives `μ(f) = 0` by (3). (d)⇒(a): by (4), whose proof
uses only `μ = 0`, with `d = 1`.
(a)⇔(e)⇔(f) are classical. The Gauss–Manin statement: `coker D_f` is a holonomic `A_1`-module by (3)
(`[ -D_g, f· ] = 1`), and if its generic rank `μ(f)` were `0` then (d) would hold, so `F` would be an
automorphism and `coker D_f = 0`.

**(6)** is Bavula's theorem (CITED); the identification `A_n · 1 = K[F]` holds because `V_j` acts on
`K[F]` as `∂/∂X_j` and `F_i·` as `X_i·`, and `K[X]` is simple over `A_n` in characteristic zero. Over
the open set `U` on which `F` is proper, `F` is finite étale of degree `deg F` (fibres have constant
cardinality there; van den Essen's fibre theorem, CITED), `F_* O` is a vector bundle with the
Gauss–Manin connection, and the trace splits off `O_U`.  ∎

## Reading

* **Chini's lens.** The theory of a generic vector-space endomorphism is organized by which finite
  kernel identities `Σ_k ∩_l Ker ρ_(jkl)[θ] = Σ_k ∩_l Ker η_(jkl)[θ]` hold. For a dual field the
  answer is complete: `Ker ρ(θ) = Ker θ^(ord_0 ρ)` for every `ρ`, and `Ker ρ + Ker η = Ker lcm`,
  `Ker ρ ∩ Ker η = Ker gcd` reduce every identity to this one family. The first-order sentence
  `∀v ∃w (θ w = v)` separates the standard module `(K[X], ∂_j)` from `(K[x], V_j)` whenever `μ_j > 0`;
  THM-4621 exhibits this for all three dual fields of THM-1300 with explicit witnesses.
* **Where the plane differs from dimension three.** In the plane `d = 1` automatically, so
  surjectivity, vanishing of `H^1` of the generic fibre, and invertibility coincide. In dimension
  three the generic fibre of a coordinate pair can be connected with positive `b_1` (THM-1300: all
  three fibrations are punctured lines with `b_1 = 2, 4, 2`), and whether it can be a union of
  several lines is HYP-9255.
* **What is not claimed.** Nothing here bounds `μ(f)` for a hypothetical planar counterexample, and
  for `d_j ≥ 2` it is not claimed that `μ_j = 0` implies `coker V_j = 0` (the cokernel could in
  principle be a nonzero torsion module over `K[G_j]`); for `d_j = 1` it does, by (4).
* **Literature.** Gurjar–Masuda–Miyanishi prove the planar criterion and, more generally, that a
  normal affine curve with a surjective derivation is `A^1` (their Theorem 1.2, via
  `dim H^1 = 2g - 1 + n`); the dictionary above is the `n`-dimensional, dual-field form of the same
  mechanism.
