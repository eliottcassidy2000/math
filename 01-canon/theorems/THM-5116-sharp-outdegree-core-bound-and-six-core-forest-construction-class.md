# A finite structural test for the augmented construction class

Call a tournament outdegree-(k-1)-degenerate if every nonempty induced
subtournament has a vertex with outdegree at most k-1. A minimal forbidden
induced tournament for this property has at most

    (k+1)(k+2)/2

vertices, and the bound is sharp for every k>=1.

Indeed let S be its s vertices of outdegree exactly k. Minimality implies
that every vertex is beaten by some vertex of S; deleting that vertex must
lower an outdegree k to k-1. Counting arcs from S to its complement gives

    n-s <= ks-s(s-1)/2,
    n <= (k+1)s-s(s-1)/2 <= (k+1)(k+2)/2.

For sharpness at k>=2, take a strong tournament S on k+1 vertices. For each
u in S create k-d_S^+(u) owned vertices. An owned vertex loses to its owner
and beats every other member of S. Orient all owned vertices transitively.
Every S vertex has outdegree k and every outside vertex has at least k.
Deleting any vertex makes some S vertex peelable at outdegree below k;
reverse reachability through strong S then peels all of S, after which the
transitive remainder peels. Thus every proper induced subtournament is
outdegree-(k-1)-degenerate. For k=1 use the directed triangle.

For k=2, exact enumeration leaves six minimal forbidden cores: one at order5
and five at order6. Outgoing bitmask representatives are

    (6,12,24,17,3),
    (6,12,48,5,11,27), (6,12,24,33,11,23),
    (6,12,24,33,43,7), (6,24,26,33,41,7),
    (6,24,42,33,13,19).

The all-order bound plus complete order5/6 enumeration makes this a complete
forbidden list, not a guess from small examples.

This six-forbidden class equals the closure of a singleton under:
- adding a sink;
- adding a vertex that beats exactly one chosen old vertex which reaches
  all old vertices, with unrestricted later choice of such a root;
- recursive ordinal sums.

The singleton-to-edge boundary is allowed. For the converse, split a
non-strong tournament into its ordered strong components. In a nontrivial
strong member of the hereditary class, a vertex of outdegree at most1 must
have outdegree exactly1; delete it. Its sole outneighbor reaches every
vertex of the deletion tournament, so the guarded operation reconstructs
it after recursively constructing that parent. This proves both directions.

Ordinal sums are necessary for equality of graph classes: source=>C3 is
not generated by guarded additions alone, and a strong order5 obstruction
also exists. All six forbidden-core path counts, namely15,33,37,45, already
occur in allowed guarded fixed-root constructions. Excluding a graph does
not exclude its scalar count.

and its finite obstruction characterization does not prove its scalar
