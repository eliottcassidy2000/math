# THM-3964 -- the repeated graph debt is a smooth two-chart surface

**RESERVED / PROVISIONAL PROOF CANDIDATE / VERIFIED-EXACT / INDEPENDENT
HOSTILE AUDIT REQUIRED.** Work over an algebraically closed field `k` of
characteristic zero. Let `r,c in k[t]`, with `c!=0`, and put

```text
q=3rP-r^3+c(P-r^2)^2,
F=T^3-3PT-q,
A=k[P,t,T]/(F),                         X0=Spec A.        (1)
```

The hidden ramification polynomial has the graph `h=r(t)` with multiplicity
at least two. The theorem computes the full normalization and then uses its
exact boundary rank and Euler ledger to exclude every member from a
same-field Keller plane.

## 1. Node coordinates and the cubic field

Make the polynomial coordinate change

```text
x=T+r,                         y=P-r^2.                  (2)
```

Then

```text
F=x^3-3r x^2-3xy-cy^2.                                 (3)
```

Over `K=k(t)`, draw a line `y=vx` through the singular point. The residual
intersection gives

```text
x=cv^2+3v+3r,                 y=vx,
P=cv^3+3v^2+3rv+r^2,
T=cv^2+3v+2r.                                           (4)
```

The polynomial `P(v)` has degree three because `c!=0` in `K`, while
`v=y/x` belongs to `K(P,T)`. Hence

```text
[K(v):K(P)]=3,                 K(P,T)=K(v),              (5)
```

so the monic cubic `F` is irreducible and `A` is a domain.

The singular locus of `(3)` is exactly the graph

```text
Gamma=V(x,y) isomorphic to A1_t.                         (6)
```

Indeed `F_y=-3x-2cy`, and substitution into `F_x` gives either `y=0`,
hence `x=0`, or the candidate
`y=3(3-4cr)/(4c^2)`. Substitution of the latter into `F` gives
`-(4cr-3)^3/(16c^3)`, so it returns to `x=y=0` when it lies on the surface.
At a zero of `c`, the two equations reduce immediately to `x=y=0`.

## 2. The exact finite normalization

The slope `v` need not be integral over a zero of `c`. Use instead

```text
u=cv=cy/x.                                                (7)
```

It satisfies the monic equation and two companion relations

```text
u^2+3u+3cr-cx=0,
cy=ux,
(u+3)y=x(x-3r).                                          (8)
```

Thus `u` is integral over `A`. Put

```text
B=A[u] subset K(v),                         X=Spec B.     (9)
```

It is finite and birational over `A`. Since `(u,u+3)=B`, two charts cover
`X`.

On `D(u+3)`, eliminate `y`:

```text
B_(u+3)=k[t,u,x,(u+3)^(-1)]/
        [c(x-3r)-u(u+3)],
y=x(x-3r)/(u+3).                                        (10)
```

The equation is primitive and linear in `x`, hence its ring is a domain.
Its decisive derivatives are `c` and `-(2u+3)`. A singular point would have
`c=0,u=-3/2`, contradicting the equation `u(u+3)=0`.

On `D(u)`, eliminate `x`:

```text
B_u=k[t,u,u^(-1),y]/
    [c^2y-u^2(u+3)-3rcu],
x=cy/u.                                                  (11)
```

This equation is primitive and linear in `y`. Its decisive derivatives are
`c^2` and `-3u(u+2)-3rc`; at `c=0`, with `u` a unit, the equation forces
`u=-3`, where the second derivative is nonzero. Thus this chart is also
smooth, independently of every root multiplicity of `c`.

Both displayed rings surject onto the corresponding localizations of `B`;
their domains have the same fraction field and dimension, so the maps are
injective. The charts cover, proving

```text
B is regular and is the full finite normalization of A.  (12)
```

Over a root of `c`, the reduced fibre has two disjoint affine-line
components:

```text
Q_i^+: u=0,       y=x(x-3r)/3;
Q_i^-: u=-3,      x=0, y free.                           (13)
```

This is the same finite-versus-escaped-branch mechanism as THM-3963, now
with a moving double-root graph.

## 3. Exact conductor and index

Equation `(8)` makes `B=A+A u` as an `A`-module. Moreover

```text
xu=cy in A,
yu=x(x-3r)-3y in A.                                     (14)
```

Therefore `(x,y)` annihilates the class of `u` in `B/A`. The induced map

```text
A/(x,y)=k[t] -> B/A,                     1 |-> class(u) (15)
```

is surjective. It is generically nonzero because `(3)` is a singular
irreducible plane cubic whereas `K[v]` is its normalization; since `k[t]`
is a domain, `(15)` is injective. Hence

```text
B/A isomorphic to k[t],
conductor_(A in B)=(x,y).                                (16)
```

The preimage of the conductor graph has the exact address equation

```text
B/(x,y)=k[t,u]/(u^2+3u+3cr),                             (17)
```

with discriminant `9-12cr`. This conductor double cover must not be
confused with the normalized ramification double cover below.

## 4. Ramification, saturation, and the hyperelliptic character

On `D(c)`, use the genuine coordinate `v=u/c`. Differentiating `(4)` at
fixed `t` gives

```text
dP/dv=3(cv^2+2v+r).                                     (18)
```

Put

```text
Q=u^2+2u+cr,                       z=u+1,
D(t)=1-c(t)r(t).                                           (19)
```

Then the horizontal ramification has function field

```text
Q=0  iff  z^2=D(t).                                      (20)
```

The naive equation `Q=0` also contains vertical debt over `c=0`; saturation
is essential. On chart `(10)`, an exact Jacobian numerator is

```text
N=(2u+3)(2x-3r)(u+3)-cx(x-3r),                          (21)
```

and the cancellation-free identity

```text
cN-3(u+3)^2Q
 =[c(x-3r)-u(u+3)](-cx+3u^2+15u+18)                    (22)
```

holds. Thus `(20)` is the generic horizontal character, while at a zero of
`c` the true ramification point on `Q_i^+` is

```text
u=0,                         x=3r/2.                     (23)
```

On the normalized curve `z^2=D`, the point `z=+1` over each root of `c`
extends to `(23)`, because

```text
x=2r+(z-1)/c=r(2z+1)/(z+1).                             (24)
```

The companion point `z=-1` escapes the affine normalization: `c=0,u=-2`
contradicts `(10)`. Hence the normalization of the reduced horizontal
ramification curve is the projective normalization of `z^2=D`, with one
specified `z=-1` point removed over every distinct root of `c`, and with
all points over `t=infinity` removed.

## 5. Units and the complete class lattice

Factor

```text
c=c0 product_(i=1)^n p_i(t)^(m_i),                      (25)
```

with distinct monic linear `p_i`, `m_i>=1`, and `c0 in k^*`. Localizing at
`c` gives

```text
B_c=k[t,c^(-1),v],
B_c^*=k^* times product_i p_i^Z,             Cl(B_c)=0.  (26)
```

The only deleted primes are the two reduced components `Q_i^+,Q_i^-` of
`(13)`, and

```text
div_B(p_i)=Q_i^++Q_i^-.                                  (27)
```

Nagata localization therefore gives

```text
B^*=k^*,
Cl(B)=direct-sum_(i=1)^n Z q_i,
q_i=[Q_i^+]=-[Q_i^-].                                   (28)
```

At the generic point of `Q_i^+`, equation `(10)` gives
`ord(u)=m_i`, and `Q` has the same generic order `m_i`; at `Q_i^-`, `Q`
is a unit. If the character `(20)` is nonsquare, its reduced horizontal
prime `R` consequently satisfies

```text
div_B(Q)=R+sum_i m_i Q_i^+,
[R]=-(m_1,...,m_n) in Cl(B)=Z^n.                         (29)
```

These formulas distinguish the genuine ramification divisor from the
vertical factor introduced by the unsaturated equation `Q=0`.

## 6. An affine-plane boundary would be exactly `n` disjoint affine lines

Assume first that `c` is nonconstant, so `n>=1`. Suppose a same-field
Keller plane gave a dense open immersion

```text
U isomorphic to A2 -> X.                                  (30)
```

The boundary has no isolated irreducible component. Indeed, if an isolated
point `p` lay off the divisorial boundary, choose an affine neighbourhood
`W` with `W\U={p}`. The intersection `W intersect U` is affine because `X`
is separated and both opens are affine. Normal `S2` Hartogs gives
`Gamma(W,O)=Gamma(W\{p},O)`. Since both are affine, their canonical spectra
would identify `W\{p}` with `W`, a contradiction.

THM-3922 and `(28)` now imply that the divisorial boundary has exactly `n`
prime components

```text
boundary(X,U)=D_1 union ... union D_n,                    (31)
```

whose classes freely form a basis of `Cl(B)`. THM-3951 says every `D_i` is
rational and unibranch. Since `D_i` is a closed curve in the affine surface
`X`, its normalization is `P1` minus at least one point. Unibranchness makes
normalization a topological bijection, so

```text
chi_c(D_i)<=1.                                           (32)
```

The fibration `t:X->A1` is an affine-line bundle away from the `n` roots of
`c`; over each root its reduced fibre is the two disjoint affine lines in
`(13)`. Therefore

```text
chi_c(X)=(1-n)*1+n*2=1+n.                                (33)
```

Additivity with `chi_c(U)=1` gives

```text
chi_c(boundary)=n.                                       (34)
```

Adding the curves one at a time shows
`chi_c(union D_i)<=sum chi_c(D_i)<=n`; each intersection with the preceding
union subtracts the nonnegative cardinality of a finite set. Equality in
`(34)` forces equality in every row. Consequently

```text
every D_i is A1, and the D_i are pairwise disjoint.       (35)
```

This boundary-shape identity is stronger than using the Euler characteristic
of the maximal etale locus alone: it first proves purity and fixes the exact
number of boundary primes via the class lattice.

## 7. The nonsquare character is never an affine-line boundary

Suppose `D` is nonsquare in `k(t)`, so the reduced horizontal ramification
is one irreducible prime `R`. Every Keller open must delete `R`, making it
one of the `D_i` in `(31)`. Its projective normalization is the projective
normalization of `z^2=D`. By `(24)`, its affine realization in `X` omits

```text
one z=-1 point over each of the n roots of c,
and at least one point over t=infinity.                   (36)
```

Thus its normalization has at least `n+1>=2` punctures. It cannot be `A1`,
contradicting `(35)`. This also subsumes the earlier genus and
non-unibranch obstructions. The class formula `(29)` remains informative:
before the global boundary count it would have required
`gcd(m_1,...,m_n)=1`, but even a primitive rational character fails `(36)`.

If `c` is a nonzero constant (`n=0`), then `B=k[t,v]=A2` and the
ramification polynomial `cv^2+2v+r` is a nonconstant principal function.
Deleting its zero divisor makes it a forbidden unit. Hence every nonsquare
case is excluded.

## 8. Neither split-square component packet can be the boundary

Now suppose `D=g(t)^2` in `k(t)`. UFD parity and algebraic closedness let us
take `g in k[t]`. For a root `alpha_i` of `c`, one has
`g(alpha_i)=+1` or `-1`; write

```text
I_+={i:g(alpha_i)=+1},
I_-={i:g(alpha_i)=-1}.                                  (37)
```

The two reduced ramification primes and their exact divisor ledgers are

```text
R_+:z=g,       div(u+1-g)=R_+ + sum_(i in I_+) m_iQ_i^+,
R_-:z=-g,      div(u+1+g)=R_- + sum_(i in I_-) m_iQ_i^+. (38)
```

Their normalizations are copies of `P1_t`. Both lose the point over
`t=infinity`. In addition, `(24)` removes the `z=-1` address, so

```text
R_+ loses every c-root in I_-,
R_- loses every c-root in I_+.                           (39)
```

For both curves to be affine lines as required by `(35)`, `(39)` would
force `I_-=empty` and `I_+=empty` simultaneously. But the two sets partition
the nonempty set of roots of `c`. This contradiction excludes every
nonconstant-`c` split-square case. If `g` has a zero, the two components
also meet there, independently violating the disjointness row of `(35)`.

For constant `c`, the same principal-ramification-unit argument as above
applies. Hence every split-square case is excluded as well.

## 9. Scope

Combining Sections 6--8 with the exact normalization proves

```text
no member of the polynomial-graph repeated-hidden-root family (1)
admits a same-function-field planar Keller A2 chart.       (40)
```

The theorem freezes the index, conductor addresses, saturation, units,
class group, ramification character, and the global boundary obstruction.
It is still a strict subfamily of the arbitrary repeated-factor locus in
THM-3961: an irreducible repeated hidden factor need not be a polynomial
graph `h=r(t)`. That nongraph debt, arbitrary `P^2q_2(P,t)`, nonmonogenic
fields outside this grammar, and JC(2) remain open.
