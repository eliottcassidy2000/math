# THM-4640 — the repair algebra at the −1 anchor

## Setting

* As in THM-4555: `f_c(x) = (3x+1)/2^c`. A word `u = (a_1, …, a_k)` acts by applying `f_(a_1)` first. Its total is `A = Σ a_i`, and it is **reduced** if `a_1 ≥ 2`.
* Reduced words `u, u'` **collide** if `f_u(−1) = f_(u')(−1)`. The anchor `−1` is the fixed point of `f_1`.
* **Head and representation.** The head of `u` is `(a_1, …, a_(k−1))`. Its **representation** is the increasing sequence `s = (e_1 < … < e_m)` with `m = k − 1` and `e_j = a_1 + … + a_j − 1`.
  * Reduced words have `e_1 ≥ 1`.
  * Conversely, every increasing sequence of positive integers is the representation of exactly one head.
* **Numerators.** `K(()) = −1` and `K(t·e) = 3K(t) + 2^e`. Equivalently `K(s) = −3^m + Σ_(j=1)^m 3^(m−j) 2^(e_j)`.
* A representation is **canonical** if `e_1 ≥ 2`, i.e. it has no leading exponent 1.

## Statements

1. **Numerator form (PROVED).**
   * For every reduced word `u` with representation `s` and total `A`: `f_u(−1) = K(s)/2^(A−1)`, and `K(s)` is odd.
   * Hence reduced `u, u'` collide iff `A_u = A_(u')` and `K(s) = K(s')`. This recovers THM-4555 (ii).
   * The first generation is the anchor row: `K((a)) = 2^a − 3 = 1/c_a`, where `c_a = 1/(2^a − 3)` is the fixed point of `f_a` (THM-4600). It gives `−1, 1, 5, 13, 29, 61, …`.
   * **Positivity and finiteness.** If `s` is canonical and non-empty, then `K(s) ≥ 3^(m+1) − 2^(m+2) ≥ 1` and `2^(e_m) ≤ K(s) + 3`, with equality iff `m = 1`.
     * So `K(s) < 0` only for `s ∈ {(), (1)}`, where `K = −1`.
     * Every `K` has finitely many canonical representations, all with exponents `≤ log_2(K + 3)`. A census by maximum exponent `E` therefore sees every representation of every `K < 2^(E+1) − 3`.
   * **Anchor rigidity (FINITE-EXACT).** The smallest value with two canonical representations is the anchor `125 = 2^7 − 3`, with `(7) ~ (3, 4, 5)`.
     * For `2 ≤ a ≤ 31`, the anchor `2^a − 3` has a second canonical representation exactly for `a ∈ {7, 13, 17, 21, 28, 31}`, one each (`anchor_rigidity.out`).
     * So `5 = K((3))` and `13 = K((4))` are rigid, but only because every value below 125 is.
2. **Right congruence and the root (PROVED).**
   * `u ~ u'` implies `uw ~ u'w`, and `uw ~ u'w` implies `u ~ u'`.
   * `K((1)·s) = K(s)`. In words: `(2, a, w) ~ (a+2, w)`, the root collision `(a) ~ (2, a−2)` of THM-4555 with a suffix.
   * A collision is **primitive** if the two words have different last letters. Cancelling the longest common suffix reduces every collision to a primitive one (the root included).
3. **Primitive collisions are ladders: the compiler at −1 (PROVED).**
   * Let `(h, c)` and `(h', c')` collide with `c ≠ c'` (an empty head has value `−1`). Then `c' − c = 2i` is even, and

         f_(h')(−1) = 4^i f_h(−1) + (4^i − 1)/3.

   * Conversely, every pair of heads with this relation gives the infinite ladder of collisions `(h, c) ~ (h', c + 2i)` for all `c ≥ max(1, 1 − 2i)`.
   * In numerators: `K(s) = K(s')` with last exponents `e` and `e + 2i` iff `K(s_−) − K(s'_−) = 2^e (4^i − 1)/3`, the **repair term**. Here `s_−` drops the last exponent.
   * This is THM-4601 (iv)'s ladder relation `F_v(1) = 4^i F_u(1) + (4^i − 1)/3` with the anchor moved from `+1` to `−1`.
   * By THM-4555 (iv), a primitive collision whose longer word is `D ≥ 1` letters longer gives a uniform switch: every odd `n` whose word starts with `1^r` followed by the shorter word, `r ≥ D`, merges at equal time with `(n + 1)/2^D − 1`.
   * The root twin `(1)·s` of the shorter representation gives a second switch, with distance `D − 1`, on reset-2 sources. An example is THM-4555 (vi)'s `(2, 6, c) ~ (4, 1, 1, c+2)`.
4. **Parity (PROVED).**
   * (a) Ladder gaps `2i` are even.
   * (b) All canonical representations of one `K` have the same length parity, and either all or none of them start with exponent 2. Indeed `K mod 8` is `3^(m−1)` if `e_1 = 2` and `−3^m` if `e_1 ≥ 3`, so it determines both.
   * Hence length differences of canonical primitive pairs, and switch distances `D`, are even. On root twins `D` is odd.
5. **Gersonides carries (PROVED; (d) FINITE-EXACT).** Allow arbitrary (not necessarily increasing) exponent sequences, with `val(B) = Σ 3^(|B|−j) 2^(B_j)`.
   * (a) **Two positions.** Two equal-length sequences with equal `val` that differ in exactly two positions, at 3-power gap `Δ`, are related by one of two carries:
     * the **unit carry** `(b+1, b) ~ (b, b+2)` (`Δ = 1`): `3·2^(b+1) + 2^b = 3·2^b + 2^(b+2)`, i.e. `4 = 3 + 1`;
     * the **Catalan carry** `(b+3, x, b) ~ (b, x, b+6)` (`Δ = 2`): `9·2^(b+3) + 2^b = 9·2^b + 2^(b+6)`, i.e. `9 = 8 + 1`.
   * Together with the anchor relation `f_1(−1) = −1` (`2 = 3 − 1`), they use the adjacencies `2 = 3 − 1`, `4 = 3 + 1` and `8 = 9 − 1`. Gersonides' equation `|2^a − 3^b| = 1` with `a ≥ 1` has exactly the solutions `(a, b) = (1, 0), (1, 1), (2, 1), (3, 2)`, whose powers of 2 are `{2, 4, 8}`. So the generators are indexed by `{2, 4, 8}`, the powers of 2 adjacent to a power of 3.
   * (b) No relation differs in exactly one position.
   * (c) Both carries need a descent. So no primitive collision between admissible (increasing) representations, padded with leading 1s to equal length, differs in fewer than three positions. The only exception is the root collision itself, whose padded forms coincide. The carries are **repairs**: they restore admissibility of descending sequences.
   * (d) The carries do not generate the primitive collisions.
     * For none of the 133 primitive pairs with exponents `≤ 14` is the long side reachable from the padded short side by carries, even allowing exponents up to 30 (`carry_derivation.out`).
     * The smallest sporadic collision, `K = 125`, `(7) ~ (3, 4, 5)`, rests on the 6-term relation `27 = 3 + 8 + 16`. Its padded side `(1, 1, 7)` admits no carry at all.
6. **Finite relation types (CITED + reduction).** A primitive collision whose padded representations differ in `r` positions is a vanishing sum of `2r` terms `±3^a 2^b`.
   * By the theorem on S-unit sums (Evertse 1984; van der Poorten–Schlickewei 1982), its non-degenerate sub-sums (no vanishing proper sub-sum) fall into finitely many classes, for each number of terms, up to multiplication by `2^a 3^b`.
   * So for each `r` the collisions are built from finitely many primitive relation types. Their number is not computed here.
7. **Census (FINITE-EXACT).**
   * **Primitive values by maximum exponent.** The number of `K` with representations having two distinct last exponents, all exponents `≤ E`, is `1, 2, 4, 7, 13, 23, 40, 73, 132, 244, 462, 876, 1666, 3188, 6129, 11846, 23037, 44922, 87836` for `E = 6, 7, …, 24`. The new values per step grow by factors 1.91–1.96.
   * **Up to `E = 22`** (canonical representations):
     * 23,036 non-root primitive values; 20,393 have two representations, 2,611 three and 32 four;
     * gaps `2i ∈ {2, 4, 6}` occur 24,732, 874 and 138 times;
     * length differences `2, 4, 6, 8` occur 18,861, 6,184, 689 and 10 times.
   * **Up to exponent 14,** 533 primitive pairs. Their padded representations differ in 3–10 positions (histogram `{3: 4, 4: 20, 5: 72, 6: 180, 7: 156, 8: 68, 9: 24, 10: 8}`), plus the root.
   * **Word level** (every reduced word of total `≤ 16`, exact Fractions): 300 of 15,073 values carry a primitive collision, 14 of them the root.
   * **Integer check.** Twelve induced switch families were verified on actual integers.
8. **Switch coverage (FINITE-EXACT rationals).** Let `c(E)` be the Haar probability that an odd 2-adic source has a downward uniform switch at −1 from a primitive collision with exponents `≤ E`, root twins included.
   * Letters are i.i.d. with `P(a) = 2^(−a)`.
   * `c(E) = 0.0117, 0.0205, 0.0273, 0.0328, 0.0375, 0.0417, 0.0454, 0.0489` for `E = 8, 10, …, 22`.
   * `E` times the increment per two steps stays near 0.072, so `c(E)` grows like `0.036 ln E` through `E = 22` (NUMERICAL; HYP-9270).

## Proofs

**1.**
* Induct along the word. With `f_u(−1) = N/2^(A−1)` and `N` odd, appending `c` gives `(3N + 2^(A−1))/2^(A−1+c)`. The new numerator is odd for `A ≥ 2`.
* Unwinding the recursion, the numerator after the head is `K(s)` with `e_j = S_j − 1`. The last letter only adds to the total.
* For a single letter, `f_a(−1) = −2/2^a = −1/2^(a−1)`, and `K(()) = −1`.
* Equal values with odd numerators force equal powers of 2, hence equal totals and equal `K`.
* `K((a)) = −3 + 2^a`, and `f_a(1/(2^a − 3)) = 1/(2^a − 3)`.
* **Positivity.** A canonical increasing `s` has `e_j ≥ j + 1`, and `K` is increasing in each `e_j`.
  * So `K(s) ≥ K((2, 3, …, m+1)) = −3^m + Σ_j 3^(m−j) 2^(j+1) = −3^m + 4·3^m − 4·2^m = 3^(m+1) − 2^(m+2)`, which is `≥ 1` for `m ≥ 1`.
  * For `m ≥ 2`, `K(s) = 3K(s_−) + 2^(e_m) ≥ 3 + 2^(e_m)`. For `m = 1`, `K(s) = 2^(e_1) − 3`.
  * A non-canonical `s` is `(1)·t` with `t` canonical or empty, and `K((1)·t) = K(t)` (statement 2).
* **Rigidity** is a complete search: by positivity, every canonical representation of `2^a − 3` has exponents `≤ a` (`anchor_rigidity.py`, which also checks the lemma on all 524,287 canonical `s` with exponents `≤ 20`). ∎

**2.**
* `f_(uw)(−1) = f_w(f_u(−1))`, and `f_w` is an injective affine map.
* `K((1)·s) = K(s)` because `3(−1) + 2 = −1`. In words, a representation starting with exponent 1 belongs to a head starting with letter 2. ∎

**3.**
* Let `y = f_h(−1)` and `y' = f_(h')(−1)`. Equal values give `(3y + 1)/2^c = (3y' + 1)/2^(c')`, so `3y' + 1 = 2^(c'−c)(3y + 1)`.
* In numerators, `3K(s_−) + 2^e = 3K(s'_−) + 2^(e')`. This forces `2^e ≡ 2^(e') (mod 3)`, so `e' − e` is even. Rearranging gives the repair term and the compiler form.
* The converse is the same identity read backwards; every `c` with both last letters positive works.
* The switch is THM-4555 (iv) applied to the colliding reduced words.
* The twin uses `K((1)·s) = K(s)`. The word of `(1)·s` is reduced and starts with 2, and it is one letter longer. ∎

**4.**
* (a) is in 3.
* (b): for `j ≥ 2`, `e_j ≥ 3`, so those terms vanish mod 8. Then `K ≡ −3^m + 3^(m−1) 2^(e_1) (mod 8)`.
  * `e_1 = 2` gives `3^(m−1) ∈ {1, 3}` according to the parity of `m − 1`.
  * `e_1 ≥ 3` gives `−3^m ∈ {7, 5}`.
  * The four residues are distinct, so they determine both the parity of `m` and whether `e_1 = 2`. ∎

**5.**
* (a) **Reduction.** Let the two positions have 3-powers `3^(t+Δ)` and `3^t`. Equal `val` gives `3^Δ(2^a − 2^b) = 2^d − 2^c`.
  * With `a > b` (otherwise swap the sequences), the right side is positive, so `d > c`.
  * Comparing 2-adic valuations gives `b = c`.
  * So `3^Δ(2^x − 1) = 2^y − 1` with `x = a − b ≥ 1` and `y = d − c ≥ 1`.
* **Solving it.**
  * `2^x − 1` divides `2^y − 1`, so `x | y`. Write `y = kx` with `k ≥ 2`.
  * If `x = 1`: `2^k − 1 = 3^Δ`. Mod 8 this forces `k = 2`, `Δ = 1`.
  * If `x ≥ 2`: `1 + 2^x + … + 2^((k−1)x) = 3^Δ`. Every prime factor of `2^(kx) − 1` that is primitive (Bang–Zsigmondy) must then be 3. But 3 already divides `2^2 − 1`, so a primitive 3 needs `kx = 2`, impossible for `x ≥ 2`. Hence `kx` is Zsigmondy's exception 6, and `(x, k) = (3, 2)` gives `1 + 8 = 9`, so `Δ = 2`, `y = 6`. The other split `(x, k) = (2, 3)` gives `21`, not a power of 3.
* **The carries.** Reading back the exponents gives `(b+1, b) ~ (b, b+2)` and `(b+3, ·, b) ~ (b, ·, b+6)`.
* **Gersonides.** `|2^a − 3^b| = 1` with `a ≥ 1` has exactly the solutions `(a, b) = (1, 0), (1, 1), (2, 1), (3, 2)`, classically.
* (b) One differing position gives `3^t(2^a − 2^b) ≠ 0`.
* (c) A padded admissible sequence is non-decreasing. Each carry has a strict descent on one side, at positions `j, j+1` (unit) or `j, j+2` (Catalan). So both sides of a two-position relation cannot be non-decreasing.
* (d) BFS over all sequences reachable by carries in both directions (`carry_derivation.py`). ∎

**6.**
* A collision `K(s) = K(t)` with `|s| ≤ |t|` gives `val(1^(|t|−|s|) s) = val(t)` after padding (statement 2).
* The positions where the sequences agree cancel. The rest is a vanishing sum of signed `{2,3}`-units, and the cited finiteness theorem applies to its non-degenerate parts. ∎

**7–8.**
* `repair_algebra.py`:
  * evaluates every reduced word of total `≤ 16` with exact Fractions;
  * checks statements 1–3 on each, including the ladder identity on every primitive class;
  * cross-checks the vectorized census against brute force for `E ≤ 12`;
  * solves `3^Δ(2^x − 1) = 2^y − 1` for `Δ ≤ 60`, `x ≤ 400`;
  * checks the padded-difference bound;
  * verifies switches on integers.
* `switch_coverage.py` computes the union measure of the switch cylinders on a trie of heads, exactly. ∎

## Reading

* **What "repair" means here.**
  * At the −1 anchor, the only two-term repairs are the Gersonides carries. They turn a descending (inadmissible) pair of exponents into an admissible one, and they are indexed by `{2, 4, 8}`.
  * Genuine collisions between admissible words need at least three positions. They are higher, non-degenerate `{2,3}`-unit relations, not compositions of carries; `125: 27 = 3 + 8 + 16` is the first.
  * Their number grows like `C·2^E/√E` (HYP-9270). Each is an infinite ladder.
* **Anchors inside the algebra.**
  * The first generation of the −1 algebra is the anchor row `2^a − 3`, so the `+1` anchor (`a = 2`) and `c_3 = 1/5`, `c_4 = 1/13` appear as numerators of one-letter heads.
  * The `+1` ladder compiler of THM-4601 is this algebra at `+1`.
* **What it buys, and what it cannot.**
  * Primitive collisions give exactly the uniform trailing-ones switches of THM-4555 (iv).
  * Their Haar coverage of odd sources is only about 5% at exponent 22, growing like `0.036 ln E` (HYP-9270). So the −1 mechanism alone cannot descend most trailing-ones sources.
  * Sources with no trailing one are never switched, but they descend in one step. THM-4601 treats one residual branch of the trailing-ones sources, the one whose post-run point lies 2-adically near the `+1` anchor, with a second anchor.
* **Wilson.** 5 and 13 enter as `K((3))` and `K((4))`, i.e. as anchors. They are rigid, but only because every value below 125 is. 563 has no representation at all. The dictionary is THM-4641.
