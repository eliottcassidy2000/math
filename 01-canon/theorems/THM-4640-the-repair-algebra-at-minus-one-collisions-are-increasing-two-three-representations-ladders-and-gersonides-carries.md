# THM-4640 — the repair algebra at the −1 anchor

## Setting

* As in THM-4555: `f_c(x) = (3x+1)/2^c`. A word `u = (a_1, …, a_k)` acts by applying `f_(a_1)` first. Its total is `A = Σ a_i`, and it is **reduced** if `a_1 ≥ 2`.
* Reduced words `u, u'` **collide** if `f_u(−1) = f_(u')(−1)`. The anchor `−1` is the fixed point of `f_1`.
* **Head and representation.** The head of `u` is `(a_1, …, a_(k−1))`. Its **representation** is the increasing sequence `s = (e_1 < … < e_m)` with `m = k − 1` and `e_j = a_1 + … + a_j − 1`.
  * Reduced words have `e_1 ≥ 1`.
  * Conversely, every increasing sequence of positive integers is the representation of exactly one head.
* **Numerators.** `K(()) = −1` and `K(t·e) = 3K(t) + 2^e`. Equivalently `K(s) = −3^m + Σ_(j=1)^m 3^(m−j) 2^(e_j)`.
* A representation is **canonical** if `e_1 ≥ 2`, i.e. it has no leading exponent 1.

## Statements

1. **Numerator form (PROVED).**
   * For every reduced word `u` with representation `s` and total `A`: `f_u(−1) = K(s)/2^(A−1)`, and `K(s)` is odd.
   * Hence reduced `u, u'` collide iff `A_u = A_(u')` and `K(s) = K(s')`. This recovers THM-4555 (ii).
   * **Attribution.** The formula and the collision criterion are already in THM-4555's Remarks and its 2026-10-07 update (the gap lemma from openai/math #004). Classically, `K = (B_u − 3^k)/2` with `B_u = Σ_(j=1)^k 3^(k−j) 2^(S_(j−1))` the numerator of Böhm–Sontacchi (1978) and Lagarias (1985). The sums `val(s)` of statement 5 are 3-smooth representations in the sense of Blecksmith–McCallum–Selfridge (1998).
   * The first generation is the row of reciprocal anchors: `K((a)) = 2^a − 3 = 1/c_a`, where `c_a = 1/(2^a − 3)` is the fixed point of `f_a` (THM-4600). It gives `−1, 1, 5, 13, 29, 61, …`.
   * **Positivity and finiteness.** If `s` is canonical and non-empty, then `K(s) ≥ 3^(m+1) − 2^(m+2) ≥ 1` and `2^(e_m) ≤ K(s) + 3`, with equality iff `m = 1`.
     * So `K(s) < 0` only for `s ∈ {(), (1)}`, where `K = −1`.
     * Every `K` has finitely many canonical representations, all with exponents `≤ log_2(K + 3)`. A census by maximum exponent `E` therefore sees every representation of every `K < 2^(E+1) − 3`.
   * **Distinct lengths.** Two different representations of the same `K` have different lengths: for fixed `m`, `e_1 = v_2(K + 3^m)`, and peeling off one term at a time recovers `s`.
   * **Anchor rigidity (FINITE-EXACT).** The smallest value with two canonical representations is the anchor numerator `125 = 2^7 − 3`, with `(7) ~ (3, 4, 5)`.
     * For `2 ≤ a ≤ 31` (audit A: `≤ 40`), `2^a − 3` has a second canonical representation exactly for `a ∈ {7, 13, 17, 21, 28, 31}`, one each (`anchor_rigidity.out`).
     * So `5 = K((3))` and `13 = K((4))` are rigid, but only because every value below 125 is.
2. **Right congruence and the root (PROVED).**
   * `u ~ u'` implies `uw ~ u'w`, and `uw ~ u'w` implies `u ~ u'`.
   * `K((1)·s) = K(s)` (the **padding relation**). In words: `(2, a, w) ~ (a+2, w)`, the root collision `(a) ~ (2, a−2)` of THM-4555 with a suffix.
   * A collision is **primitive** if the two words have different last letters. Cancelling the longest common suffix reduces every collision to a primitive one (the root included).
3. **Primitive collisions are ladders: the compiler at −1 (PROVED).**
   * Let `(h, c)` and `(h', c')` collide with `c ≠ c'` (an empty head has value `−1`). Then `c' − c = 2i` is even, and

         f_(h')(−1) = 4^i f_h(−1) + (4^i − 1)/3.

   * An empty head pairs only with the head `(2)`, at `i = −1`: this is the root.
   * Conversely, every pair of heads with this relation gives the infinite ladder of collisions `(h, c) ~ (h', c + 2i)` for all `c ≥ max(1, 1 − 2i)`.
   * In numerators: `K(s) = K(s')` with last exponents `e` and `e + 2i` iff `K(s_−) − K(s'_−) = 2^e (4^i − 1)/3`, the **repair term**. Here `s_−` drops the last exponent.
   * This is THM-4601 (iv)'s ladder relation `F_v(1) = 4^i F_u(1) + (4^i − 1)/3` with the anchor moved from `+1` to `−1`.
   * By THM-4555 (iv), a primitive collision whose longer word is `D ≥ 1` letters longer gives a uniform switch: every odd `n` whose word starts with `1^r` followed by the shorter word, `r ≥ D`, merges at equal time with `(n + 1)/2^D − 1`.
   * The root twin `(1)·s` of the shorter representation gives a second switch, with distance `D − 1`, on reset-2 sources. An example is THM-4555 (vi)'s `(2, 6, c) ~ (4, 1, 1, c+2)`.
4. **Parity (PROVED).**
   * (a) Ladder gaps `2i` are even.
   * (b) All canonical representations of one `K` have the same length parity, and either all or none of them start with exponent 2. Indeed `K mod 8` is `3^(m−1)` if `e_1 = 2` and `−3^m` if `e_1 ≥ 3`, so it determines both.
   * Hence length differences of canonical primitive pairs, and their switch distances `D`, are even and, by statement 1, at least 2. On root twins `D` is odd.
5. **Gersonides carries (PROVED; (d) FINITE-EXACT).** Allow arbitrary (not necessarily increasing) exponent sequences, with `val(B) = Σ 3^(|B|−j) 2^(B_j)`.
   * (a) **Two positions.** Two equal-length sequences with equal `val` that differ in exactly two positions, at 3-power gap `Δ`, are related by one of two carries:
     * the **unit carry** `(b+1, b) ~ (b, b+2)` (`Δ = 1`): `3·2^(b+1) + 2^b = 3·2^b + 2^(b+2)`, i.e. `4 = 3 + 1`;
     * the **Catalan carry** `(b+3, x, b) ~ (b, x, b+6)` (`Δ = 2`): `9·2^(b+3) + 2^b = 9·2^b + 2^(b+6)`, i.e. `9 = 8 + 1`.
   * Together with the padding relation `f_1(−1) = −1` (`2 = 3 − 1`), they use the adjacencies `2 = 3 − 1`, `4 = 3 + 1` and `8 = 9 − 1`. Gersonides' equation `|2^a − 3^b| = 1` with `a ≥ 1` has exactly the solutions `(a, b) = (1, 0), (1, 1), (2, 1), (3, 2)`, whose powers of 2 are `{2, 4, 8}`. So the two-position relations and the padding relation are indexed by `{2, 4, 8}`, the powers of 2 adjacent to a power of 3. They do not generate the primitive collisions ((d)).
   * (b) No relation differs in exactly one position.
   * (c) Both carries need a descent. So no primitive collision between admissible (increasing) representations, padded with leading 1s to equal length, differs in fewer than three positions. The only exception is the root collision itself, whose padded forms coincide. The carries are **repairs**: they restore admissibility of descending sequences.
   * (d) The carries do not generate the primitive collisions.
     * For none of the 133 canonical primitive pairs with exponents `≤ 14` is the long side reachable from the padded short side by carries, even allowing exponents up to 30 (`carry_derivation.out`). Audit A confirms this with 0–3 extra leading 1s on both sides, exponent floor 0 or −6, and no state cap (largest component 85 states).
     * The smallest sporadic collision, `K = 125`, `(7) ~ (3, 4, 5)`, is the non-degenerate 6-term relation `2^7 + 27 = 3 + 2^5 + 3·2^4 + 9·2^3`. Its padded side `(1, 1, 7)` admits only the Catalan carry to `(4, 1, 1)` (`val = 152`), a dead end.
6. **Finite relation types (CITED + reduction; census FINITE-EXACT).** A primitive collision whose padded representations differ in `r` positions is a vanishing sum of `2r` terms `±2^a 3^b`.
   * By the theorem on S-unit sums (Evertse 1984; van der Poorten–Schlickewei 1982), its non-degenerate sub-sums (no vanishing proper sub-sum) fall into finitely many classes, for each number of terms, up to multiplication by `2^a 3^b`.
   * So for each `r` the collisions are built from finitely many primitive relation types. Their number is not computed here.
   * **Degeneracy is common (audit A, `a3b_degenerate`).** Of the 1,749 canonical primitive pairs with exponents `≤ 18`, 737 are degenerate.
     * With exponents `≤ 16`, 193 of the 203 degenerate pairs contain the 3-term Gersonides relation `4 − 3 − 1 = 0` as a vanishing sub-sum.
     * The first is `259`, `(2, 8) ~ (2, 3, 5, 6)`, which splits as `18·(3 + 1 − 4) + 4·(64 + 3 − 27 − 24 − 16) = 0`. The collision `125` is non-degenerate.
7. **Census (FINITE-EXACT).**
   * **Primitive values by maximum exponent.** The number `N(E)` of `K` with representations having two distinct last exponents, all exponents `≤ E` (the root `K = −1` included), is `1, 2, 4, 7, 13, 23, 40, 73, 132, 244, 462, 876, 1666, 3188, 6129, 11846, 23037, 44922, 87836` for `E = 6, 7, …, 24`.
     * Audit A's independent census agrees and continues `172073, 337478, 662823, 1303189, 2563623` for `E = 25, …, 29`.
     * The ratio `N(E)/N(E−1)` rises from 1.81 at `E = 14` to 1.967 at `E = 29`.
   * **Up to `E = 22`** (canonical representations):
     * 23,036 non-root primitive values; 20,393 have two representations, 2,611 three and 32 four;
     * gaps `2i ∈ {2, 4, 6}` occur 24,732, 874 and 138 times;
     * length differences `2, 4, 6, 8` occur 18,861, 6,184, 689 and 10 times.
   * **Up to exponent 14,** 133 canonical primitive pairs. Their padded representations differ in 3–10 positions (histogram `{3: 1, 4: 5, 5: 18, 6: 45, 7: 39, 8: 17, 9: 6, 10: 2}`). With the four twin variants of each, plus the root, they give the 533 pairs counted in `repair_algebra.out`.
   * **Word level** (every reduced word of total `≤ 16`, exact Fractions): 300 of 15,073 values carry a primitive collision, 14 of them the root. Audit A repeated this for every reduced word of total `≤ 20`.
   * **Integer check.** Twelve induced switch families were verified on actual integers. Audit A verified 7,182 integer sources across 2,394 families, twin switches included.
8. **Switch coverage (PROVED (a)–(d); values FINITE-EXACT).** Source model: an odd 2-adic `n` whose letters are i.i.d. with `P(a) = 2^(−a)`, so its word is `1^r` followed by a reduced word. Write `can(E)` and `twin(E)` for the Haar mass of sources with a downward switch from a canonical primitive pair with exponents `≤ E`, respectively from its root twin.
   * (a) **The root is the reset switch.** The root collision `(c) ~ (2, c−2)`, `c ≥ 3`, `D = 1`, is THM-4555's reset switch `n ↦ (n−1)/2`. It applies exactly to the sources `1^r c …` with `r ≥ 1`, `c ≥ 3`, of mass `1/4`.
   * (b) **Canonical switches lie inside the reset domain; twins live on reset-2 sources.**
     * A canonical shorter head starts with a letter `≥ 3`, and `r ≥ D ≥ 2`, so its cylinder lies in `{r ≥ 2, first post-run letter ≥ 3}`, of mass `1/8`.
     * A twin's shorter head starts with the letter 2, and `r ≥ D − 1 ≥ 1`, so its cylinder lies in `{r ≥ 1, first post-run letter 2}`, the reset-2 sources, of mass `1/4`.
   * (c) **Exact identities and the bound.** `twin(E) = 2·can(E)` for every `E`. Let `c(E) = can(E) + twin(E)` be the coverage without the root. Then `c(E) = 3·can(E) ≤ 3/8`, and the coverage with the root is `1/4 + twin(E)`.
   * (d) **The limit.** `twin(E) → 1/4` as `E → ∞`, so `c(E) → 3/8`. Including the root, the coverage tends to `1/2`: almost every trailing-ones source has a −1 switch.
   * (e) **Values (exact rationals, `switch_coverage.out`).**

     | `E` | 8 | 12 | 16 | 20 | 22 | 24 |
     |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
     | `c(E)` | 0.011719 | 0.027283 | 0.037497 | 0.045416 | 0.048872 | 0.052095 |
     | twin share of the reset-2 mass, `4·twin(E)` | 0.0312 | 0.0728 | 0.1000 | 0.1211 | 0.1303 | 0.1389 |
     | with the root, `1/4 + twin(E)` | 0.257812 | 0.268188 | 0.274998 | 0.280277 | 0.282582 | 0.284730 |

     * The convergence in (d) is slow: at `E = 24` the twins cover 13.9% of reset-2 sources, and the total is 56.9% of the trailing-ones mass. No rate is claimed here.
     * Audit A's Monte Carlo on 200,000 random odd 400-bit integers gives frequencies 0.01214, 0.02555 and 0.2482 for the canonical, twin and root parts at `E = 16`, against the exact 0.012499, 0.024998 and 1/4. The merge was confirmed on all 54,747 covered integers.

## Proofs

**1.**
* Induct along the word. With `f_u(−1) = N/2^(A−1)` and `N` odd, appending `c` gives `(3N + 2^(A−1))/2^(A−1+c)`. The new numerator is odd for `A ≥ 2`.
* Unwinding the recursion, the numerator after the head is `K(s)` with `e_j = S_j − 1`. The last letter only adds to the total.
* For a single letter, `f_a(−1) = −2/2^a = −1/2^(a−1)`, and `K(()) = −1`.
* Equal values with odd numerators force equal powers of 2, hence equal totals and equal `K`.
* `K((a)) = −3 + 2^a`, and `f_a(1/(2^a − 3)) = 1/(2^a − 3)`.
* **Attribution formula.** `f_u(x) = (3^k x + B_u)/2^A`. With `S_0 = 0`, `B_u = 3^m + Σ_(i=1)^m 3^(m−i) 2^(S_i)`, so `B_u − 3^k = 2K(s)`.
* **Positivity.** A canonical increasing `s` has `e_j ≥ j + 1`, and `K` is increasing in each `e_j`.
  * So `K(s) ≥ K((2, 3, …, m+1)) = −3^m + Σ_j 3^(m−j) 2^(j+1) = −3^m + 4·3^m − 4·2^m = 3^(m+1) − 2^(m+2)`, which is `≥ 1` for `m ≥ 1`.
  * For `m ≥ 2`, `K(s) = 3K(s_−) + 2^(e_m) ≥ 3 + 2^(e_m)`. For `m = 1`, `K(s) = 2^(e_1) − 3`.
  * A non-canonical `s` is `(1)·t` with `t` canonical or empty, and `K((1)·t) = K(t)` (statement 2).
* **Distinct lengths.** `K + 3^m = Σ_j 3^(m−j) 2^(e_j)`. The `j = 1` term has the smallest power of 2, and its cofactor `3^(m−1)` is odd, so `v_2(K + 3^m) = e_1`. Subtracting that term and repeating recovers `e_2, …, e_m`.
* **Rigidity** is a complete search: by positivity, every canonical representation of `2^a − 3` has exponents `≤ a` (`anchor_rigidity.py`, which also checks the lemma on all 524,287 canonical `s` with exponents `≤ 20`). ∎

**2.**
* `f_(uw)(−1) = f_w(f_u(−1))`, and `f_w` is an injective affine map.
* `K((1)·s) = K(s)` because `3(−1) + 2 = −1`. In words, a representation starting with exponent 1 belongs to a head starting with letter 2. ∎

**3.**
* Let `y = f_h(−1)` and `y' = f_(h')(−1)`. Equal values give `(3y + 1)/2^c = (3y' + 1)/2^(c')`, so `3y' + 1 = 2^(c'−c)(3y + 1)`.
* In numerators, `3K(s_−) + 2^e = 3K(s'_−) + 2^(e')`. This forces `2^e ≡ 2^(e') (mod 3)`, so `e' − e` is even. Rearranging gives the repair term and the compiler form.
* **Empty heads.** If `h` is empty, `f_(h')(−1) = −(1 + 2·4^i)/3`.
  * By positivity, the negative head values are `−1` (the empty head) and `−1/2^j`: these come from the heads `(a)` and `(2, a)`, whose own representations are `()` and `(1)`.
  * For `i ≥ 1` the value is `≤ −3 < −1`, which is impossible.
  * For `i ≤ −1` it lies in `[−1/2, −1/3)`. The only `−1/2^j` there is `−1/2`, at `i = −1`, and it forces `h' = (2)`.
* The converse is the same identity read backwards; every `c` with both last letters positive works.
* The switch is THM-4555 (iv) applied to the colliding reduced words.
* The twin uses `K((1)·s) = K(s)`. The word of `(1)·s` is reduced and starts with 2, and it is one letter longer. ∎

**4.**
* (a) is in 3.
* (b): for `j ≥ 2`, `e_j ≥ 3`, so those terms vanish mod 8. Then `K ≡ −3^m + 3^(m−1) 2^(e_1) (mod 8)`.
  * `e_1 = 2` gives `3^(m−1) ∈ {1, 3}` according to the parity of `m − 1`.
  * `e_1 ≥ 3` gives `−3^m ∈ {7, 5}`.
  * The four residues are distinct, so they determine both the parity of `m` and whether `e_1 = 2`.
* Distinct representations have distinct lengths (statement 1), so canonical `D ≥ 2`. ∎

**5.**
* (a) **Reduction.** Let the two positions have 3-powers `3^(t+Δ)` and `3^t`. Equal `val` gives `3^Δ(2^a − 2^b) = 2^d − 2^c`.
  * With `a > b` (otherwise swap the sequences), the right side is positive, so `d > c`.
  * Comparing 2-adic valuations gives `b = c`.
  * So `3^Δ(2^x − 1) = 2^y − 1` with `x = a − b ≥ 1` and `y = d − c ≥ 1`.
* **Solving it.**
  * `2^x − 1` divides `2^y − 1`, so `x | y`. Write `y = kx` with `k ≥ 2`.
  * If `x = 1`: `2^k − 1 = 3^Δ`. Mod 8 this forces `k = 2`, `Δ = 1`.
  * If `x ≥ 2`: `1 + 2^x + … + 2^((k−1)x) = 3^Δ`. Every prime factor of `2^(kx) − 1` that is primitive (Bang–Zsigmondy) must then be 3. But 3 already divides `2^2 − 1`, so a primitive 3 needs `kx = 2`, impossible for `x ≥ 2`. Hence `kx` is Zsigmondy's exception 6, and `(x, k) = (3, 2)` gives `1 + 8 = 9`, so `Δ = 2`, `y = 6`. The other split `(x, k) = (2, 3)` gives `21`, not a power of 3.
* **The carries.** Reading back the exponents gives `(b+1, b) ~ (b, b+2)` and `(b+3, ·, b) ~ (b, ·, b+6)`.
* **Gersonides.** `|2^a − 3^b| = 1` with `a ≥ 1` has exactly the solutions `(a, b) = (1, 0), (1, 1), (2, 1), (3, 2)`, classically.
* (b) One differing position gives `3^t(2^a − 2^b) ≠ 0`.
* (c) A padded admissible sequence is non-decreasing. Each carry has a strict descent on one side, at positions `j, j+1` (unit) or `j, j+2` (Catalan). So both sides of a two-position relation cannot be non-decreasing.
* (d) BFS over all sequences reachable by carries in both directions (`carry_derivation.py`; audit A's `a5_carries.py`). `val((1,1,7)) = 18 + 6 + 128 = 152 = 144 + 6 + 2 = val((4,1,1))`. ∎

**6.**
* A collision `K(s) = K(t)` with `|s| ≤ |t|` gives `val(1^(|t|−|s|) s) = val(t)` after padding (statement 2).
* The positions where the sequences agree cancel. The rest is a vanishing sum of signed `{2,3}`-units, and the cited finiteness theorem applies to its non-degenerate parts.
* The degeneracy census decomposes each vanishing sum into minimal vanishing sub-sums (audit A, `a3b_degenerate.py`). ∎

**7.**
* `repair_algebra.py`:

  * cross-checks the vectorized census against brute force for `E ≤ 12`;
  * solves `3^Δ(2^x − 1) = 2^y − 1` for `Δ ≤ 60`, `x ≤ 400`;
  * checks the padded-difference bound;
  * verifies switches on integers.
* Audit A's `a2_census.py`, `a2b_census28.py` and `a2c_census29.py` recompute `N(E)` by a different method, to `E = 29`. ∎

**8.**
* (a) THM-4555 identifies the root collision with the reset switch. A source `1^r c …` with `r ≥ 1` has mass `2^(−r−1)` for the exact run, and `P(c ≥ 3 | c ≥ 2) = 1/2`. Summing gives `1/2 · 1/2 = 1/4`.
* (b) A canonical shorter representation has `e_1 ≥ 2`, so its head starts with `a_1 = e_1 + 1 ≥ 3`. Its `D` is even and at least 2 (statement 4). A twin head is `(2, a_1 − 2, …)`, and its distance is `D − 1 ≥ 1`. The masses: `P(r ≥ 2) · P(a ≥ 3 | a ≥ 2) = 1/4 · 1/2 = 1/8`, and `P(r ≥ 1) · P(a = 2 | a ≥ 2) = 1/2 · 1/2 = 1/4`.
* (c) The twin map `(h, c_min, D) ↦ ((2, a_1 − 2, …), c_min, D − 1)` is a bijection between the canonical and twin cylinder families. It preserves prefix relations among heads and the thresholds `c_min`.
  * Under the source law, the first post-run letter has weight `2^(1−a)` and later letters `2^(−a)`. So the twin head `(2, a_1 − 2)` has weight `2^(−1) · 2^(−(a_1−2)) = 2^(1−a_1)`, the same as the canonical `a_1`.
  * Lowering the run requirement from `D` to `D − 1` doubles `P(r ≥ D)`.
  * Hence every union of twin cylinders has exactly twice the mass of the corresponding canonical union.
  * The canonical and twin regions are disjoint (first post-run letter `≥ 3` against `2`), so `c = can + twin`. The bound is (b).
  * The root's domain contains every canonical cylinder, so the coverage with the root is `1/4 + twin`.
  * `switch_coverage.py` asserts all three identities exactly, as rationals, for every `E ≤ 24`. It sums the run lengths in closed form: for `r ≥ max D` the cylinder set is constant.
* (d) **Equal-time merges are collisions.** Let `n` be a reset-2 source, with word `1^r w`, `w` starting with 2. Suppose `n` and `m = (n−1)/2` merge at equal odd-step count `t` (THM-4581's absorption, 6(e)).
  * One Terras step of `n` is the extra halving `n − 1 ↦ (n−1)/2`, so in Terras time `n` makes exactly one more halving than `m`.
  * Write `m`'s word as `1^(r−1) w'`. Equal odd counts give `|w'| = |w| + 1`. One extra halving gives equal word totals, `A_w = A_(w')`.
  * After the runs, the points are `x = 2·3^r t − 1` and `y = 2·3^(r−1) t − 1`, with `x + 1 = 3(y + 1)`. Since `f_w(x) = f_w(−1) + 3^(|w|)(x+1)/2^(A_w)`, and likewise for `w'`, equal values and equal totals give `f_w(−1) = f_(w')(−1)`.
  * So the merge is a collision at −1 with `D = 1`, i.e. a root twin (canonical pairs have even `D`). After suffix cancellation it is a primitive pair with some finite maximum exponent.
* (d) **The limit.** By THM-4581 6(e) (PROVED, audited), Haar-almost every reset-2 source merges with `(n−1)/2`. Each such source therefore lies in some twin cylinder of finite exponent, and monotone convergence gives `twin(E) → 1/4`. Then `can(E) = twin(E)/2 → 1/8` and `c(E) → 3/8`, and the root-inclusive coverage tends to `1/4 + 1/4 = 1/2`.
* The audit (M2) also notes THM-4581 (3) at the start `(2, 8)`, which gives the same for `(n+1)/4 − 1` (`D = 2`, canonical families). ∎

## Reading

* **What "repair" means here.**
  * At the −1 anchor, the only two-term relations are the Gersonides carries. They turn a descending (inadmissible) pair of exponents into an admissible one, and with the padding relation they are indexed by `{2, 4, 8}`.
  * Genuine collisions between admissible words need at least three positions, and they are not compositions of carries.
  * As unit sums, though, about 40% are degenerate, mostly through the 3-term Gersonides relation `4 − 3 − 1 = 0` (statement 6). The first collision, `125`, is non-degenerate: `2^7 + 27 = 3 + 2^5 + 3·2^4 + 9·2^3`.
  * `N(E)` grows a little slower than `2^E` (HYP-9270). Each primitive collision is an infinite ladder.
* **Anchors inside the algebra.**
  * The first generation of the −1 algebra is the row `2^a − 3` of reciprocal anchors. So the `+1` anchor (`a = 2`) and `c_3 = 1/5`, `c_4 = 1/13` appear through the numerators of one-letter heads.
  * The `+1` ladder compiler of THM-4601 is this algebra at `+1`.
* **What it buys.**
  * Primitive collisions give exactly the uniform trailing-ones switches of THM-4555 (iv).
  * The root alone is the reset switch, covering 1/4 of odd sources. Canonical switches add nothing beyond it. The twins are the whole story on reset-2 sources, where they reach 13.9% at exponent 24 and almost everything in the limit (THM-4581).
  * Sources with no trailing one descend in one step. So, up to a null set, every odd source has either a one-step descent or a −1 switch to a smaller partner, but only through ever deeper collisions.
  * This is the density statement of THM-4581 in switch form, not a proof of descent for every integer: a switch reduces `n` to a smaller partner, and the uncovered set at each finite `E` has positive measure.
  * THM-4601 treats the reset-2 branch, whose post-run point lies 2-adically near the `+1` anchor, with a second anchor and families uniform in the run length.
* **Wilson.** 5 and 13 enter as `K((3))` and `K((4))`, the reciprocal anchors. They are rigid, but only because every value below 125 is. 563 has no representation at all. The dictionary is THM-4641.
