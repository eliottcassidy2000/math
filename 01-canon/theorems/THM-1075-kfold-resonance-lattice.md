# THM-1075 — the right object

THM-1070 concluded that the B7 ledger needs a bound using the JOINT
alignment of the k combs, and named the k-fold analogue of THM-965's
folded identity as the route. This file finds it. It is not a longer

## The identity

With h the indicator of (−λ, λ) on the circle, ĥ(0) = 2λ and
ĥ(n) = sin(2πnλ)/(πn):

> **mu(∩_i D_{a_i}) = Σ_{n ∈ Λ(a)} ∏_i ĥ(n_i),  Λ(a) = ker(Z^k → Z, n ↦ Σ n_i a_i)**

Λ(a) is the **resonance lattice**, of rank k−1.

## The cumulant form (the actual "k-fold folded identity")

Splitting lattice vectors by their support S ⊆ [k]:

> **mu = Σ_{S} (2λ)^{k−|S|} · δ(S)**,  δ(S) = Σ over FULL-support vectors of Λ(a_S)

with δ(∅) = 1 and δ({i}) = 0 (since n·a_i = 0 forces n = 0). So the
expansion begins at pairs, δ({i,j}) is exactly the THM-965 fold term, and
δ(S) for |S| ≥ 3 is genuine k-body structure. Verified at k=3:

## Why k=2 was sharp — THM-1070's degradation, explained

Rank k−1 = 1 is **cyclic**. A single generator means a single Bernoulli
series, which sums to the tent function fold_M(r) = r(M−r): that is
THM-965. For k ≥ 3 the lattice has rank ≥ 2 and no single Bernoulli
closes it. The containment method was sharp at k=2 for the same reason —
one generator is one alignment, and one alignment is the only thing a
per-step worst case can capture without compounding.

## Four consequences

1. **The modulus is in the transform.** ĥ(n) = sin(πn/7)/(πn) vanishes
   **exactly when 7 | n**. Every resonance vector with any coordinate
   divisible by 7 = 1/(2λ) contributes nothing. The seven that runs
   through this whole problem is a property of the Fourier kernel.

2. **Exact pair independence, an arithmetic criterion.** The pair lattice
   is generated by (b/g, −a/g), g = gcd(a,b), so all vectors die iff

   > **mu(D_a ∩ D_b) = 1/49 exactly  ⟺  7 | (a/g)  or  7 | (b/g)**

   Verified 16/16 in exact rational arithmetic. The sharp test is
   (14, 21): both speeds divisible by 7, yet mu = 1/21 ≠ 1/49, because
   the *reduced* pair (2,3) is not. This is the S357 independence locus,
   now with a proof-shaped reason.

3. **Dilation invariance in one line.** Λ(ka) = {n : k·Σ n_i a_i = 0} =
   Λ(a). The lattice does not see the scale, so every quantity built from
   it is dilation-invariant — THM-1050 immediate, and THM-1055's absent
   speed threshold immediate with it: **Λ is an arithmetic object and
   magnitude is not one of its inputs**. Confirmed: mu(2,3) = mu(4,6) =
   mu(6,9) = mu(10,15) = mu(20,30) = 1/21.

4. **The 3-body term does not decay with magnitude** — δ₁₂₃ is +0.00083
   at (31,37,41) but +0.0039 at (101,103,107). Non-monotone, as
   consequence 3 requires. Only lattice geometry matters.

## Status, honestly

This is a **reframe that unifies**, not yet a certificate. What is
established here: the lattice, its support form, the k=2 explanation, the
7-vanishing, the independence criterion (exactly), and dilation.  THM-1092
now supplies the missing rigorous analytic meaning: product-Fejer finite sums
converge in `L1`, and each support term converges to its centered joint moment.
What is still **not** established is any useful bound on `delta(S)` for
`|S| >= 3`.

**The remaining work is now well-posed**, which it was not before:
bound δ(S) by the SHORT FULL-SUPPORT VECTORS of Λ(a_S) that avoid
7 | n_i. The k=2 case shows the shape — |δ| there is the shortest-vector
term g²/(π²ab) up to a factor ≈ 1.2–1.6, and that is exactly THM-1025's
sawtooth floor g²/(4ab). For |S| ≥ 3 this is a Minkowski/successive-minima
question about an explicit lattice, i.e. **the small-divisor problem**:
Λ(a) and its short vectors are the standard object of KAM theory, and
Diophantine conditions on a are precisely what control them. That is a
mature literature and a better-posed target than another measure estimate.
