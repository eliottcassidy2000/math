# THM-3600 -- Danielewski arm-plane atlas, singular shear, and no-filling gate

**PROVED + VERIFIED-EXACT + INDEPENDENTLY HOSTILE-AUDITED.**  The
unconditional arm-atlas theorem and the compiler-dependent corollary are
separated below.  This theorem constructs no polynomial Darboux pair and
proves no case of `JC(2)` false.

All varieties, rings, differentials, and roots below are over `C`.

## 1. The unconditional arm-plane atlas

Let `n>=1` and let

```text
Sigma(b)=gamma product_(i=1)^h (b-beta_i),
gamma!=0,                 beta_i pairwise distinct,     h>=1.       (1)
```

Put

```text
A=C[b,c,e]/(c^n e-Sigma(b)),             Y=Spec A,      (2)

{b,c}=c^n,       {c,e}=-Sigma'(b),       {b,e}=-n c^(n-1)e. (3)
```

The squarefree hypothesis makes `Y` smooth and `(3)` symplectic.  Its arm
divisors are

```text
L_i=V(c,b-beta_i) ~= A1_e.                             (4)
```

Retain exactly one arm:

```text
U_i=Y minus union_(j!=i) L_j,
a_i=(b-beta_i)/c^n in C(Y).                            (5)
```

Then `a_i` is regular on `U_i`, and

```text
U_i ~= A2_(a_i,c).                                     (6)
```

Indeed, put `Sigma_i=Sigma/(b-beta_i)`.  On `D(c)` use the first
presentation in `(5)`, while near `L_i` use

```text
a_i=e/Sigma_i(b).                                      (7)
```

These agree by `(2)`, and `D(c)` together with `D(Sigma_i)` covers `U_i`.
The mutually inverse chart formulas are

```text
b=beta_i+c^n a_i,
e=a_i Sigma_i(beta_i+c^n a_i).                        (8)
```

At `c=0`, formula `(8)` retains the whole free `e`-line because
`Sigma_i(beta_i)!=0`.

### 1.1 A proof trap

One must not replace `U_i` by `D(Sigma_i(b))`.  If `j!=i`, the points

```text
b=beta_j,             c!=0,             e=0           (9)
```

belong to `U_i` but not to `D(Sigma_i)`.  Regularity of `a_i` comes from the
two presentations in `(5),(7)`, not from globally inverting `Sigma_i`.

### 1.2 Singular symplectic shears

The `U_i` cover `Y`.  For distinct `i,j`,

```text
U_i intersection U_j=Y minus V(c)
                    ~= A1_b x Gm_c,                   (10)
```

and the transition is

```text
a_j=a_i+(beta_i-beta_j)c^(-n),            c |-> c.    (11)
```

It is a regular symplectic automorphism of the overlap and

```text
{a_i,c}=1,             da_j wedge dc=da_i wedge dc.   (12)
```

It does not extend to a polynomial automorphism of either full plane.  If
`a_j` were polynomial in `(a_i,c)`, multiplying `(11)` by `c^n` and reducing
modulo `c` would give `0=beta_i-beta_j`.

For `n>=2`, `(11)` is the time-one Hamiltonian shear generated by

```text
H_ij=(beta_i-beta_j)c^(1-n)/(1-n),
dH_ij=(beta_i-beta_j)c^(-n)dc.                         (13)
```

For `n=1`, the generator would be logarithmic rather than rational.

### 1.3 The exact intersection

Embed every chart ring in the common function field `K=C(b,c)`.  Then

```text
A=intersection_(i=1)^h C[c,a_i]        inside K.       (14)
```

The inclusion from left to right follows from `(8)`.  Conversely, a rational
function in every chart ring is regular on every `U_i`; since these opens
cover the affine separated variety `Y`, it lies in `Gamma(Y,O_Y)=A`.

The ambient field and the use of **all** charts are load-bearing.  A single
chart forgets regularity along all other arms.

### 1.4 Exact Hermite--Pade membership gate

There is a constructive, non-weight form of `(14)`.  Fix the `i`-chart, put

```text
y=c^n,
F(c,a_i)=sum_(r=0)^(n-1)c^r F_r(y,a_i),                (14a)
```

and, for a nonzero component, let `M_r=deg_(a_i)F_r`.  With a new variable
`z`, clear its possible shear poles by

```text
N_r(y,z)=y^M_r F_r(y,(z-beta_i)/y) in C[y,z].          (14b)
```

Then

```text
F lies in A
iff
N_r lies in (y,Sigma(z))^M_r for every r.              (14c)
```

Indeed, in the `j`-chart put `z=beta_j+ya_j`.  Polynomiality there is
equivalent to divisibility by `y^M_r` after this substitution, hence to

```text
N_r in (y,z-beta_j)^M_r.
```

The arm ideals are pairwise comaximal, and Lagrange interpolation gives

```text
intersection_j (y,z-beta_j)^M
 =product_j (y,z-beta_j)^M
 =(y,Sigma(z))^M.                                      (14d)
```

Writing an element of the last ideal as
`sum_(k=0)^M y^(M-k)Sigma(z)^k H_k(y,z)` and using
`Sigma(b)=ye` gives

```text
F_r=sum_(k=0)^M e^k H_k(y,b),
```

which proves sufficiency constructively.  Thus simultaneous singular-shear
regularity is an exact multiarm Hermite--Pade condition, not merely a weight
filter.

### 1.5 Laurent shear normal form and sharp degree invoices

An equivalent form makes the first degree costs explicit.  Fix the
`i`-chart and put

```text
z=b-beta_i=c^n a_i,
delta_j=beta_j-beta_i,
D_i(z)=product_j(z-delta_j)=gamma^(-1)Sigma(z+beta_i),
w=D_i(z)/c^n=e/gamma.                                  (14e)
```

Regroup the monomials of `H in C[c,a_i]` uniquely in the Laurent ring as

```text
H=sum_(m in Z)c^m P_m(z),                P_m in C[z].  (14f)
```

Then

```text
H in A
iff
D_i(z)^ceil(-m/n) divides P_m(z) for every m<0.        (14g)
```

Indeed, `(8)` identifies

```text
A=C[c,z,w]/(c^n w-D_i(z))
```

up to the harmless scalar `gamma`.  A negative Laurent piece
`c^mP_m(z)` is regular exactly when the indicated power of `D_i` converts it
to `c^(m+nM)w^M` with nonnegative `c`-exponent.  Equivalently, substituting
`z=delta_j+c^na_j` tests all singular shears separately.  This proves
`(14g)` without assuming a torus grading.

Let `d=deg_(a_i)H`.  The divisor degree `deg D_i=h` in `(14g)` gives

```text
ord_c(lc_(a_i)H) >= n(d-floor(d/h)),
deg_(a_i)([c^r]H) <= floor(d/h)       for 0<=r<n,
d >= h deg_(a_i)H(0,a_i).                             (14h)
```

In particular, if `d<h`, then `H in C[c,z]`.  Therefore if two global
functions both have `a_i`-degree below `h`, their chart Jacobian is divisible
by `c^n`; they cannot form a Darboux pair.  This is a sharp all-degree
multiarm invoice and a direct construction target beyond a finite support
search.

## 2. Non-weight symplectic and Hamilton--Jacobi forms

On every `U_i`, the physical form is

```text
omega=db wedge dc/c^n=da_i wedge dc.                  (15)
```

The raw Liouville potentials `lambda_i=a_i dc` obey

```text
lambda_j-lambda_i=(beta_i-beta_j)c^(-n)dc.             (16)
```

For `n>=2`, the exact correction in `(13)` produces a global regular
primitive.  Explicitly, the forms

```text
alpha_i=(c da_i+n a_i dc)/(n-1)                        (17)
```

agree under every transition `(11)` and therefore glue to
`alpha in Omega_A^1`.  Direct differentiation gives

```text
d alpha=omega.                                         (18)
```

On the dense chart `c!=0`,

```text
alpha=db/[(n-1)c^(n-1)].                               (19)
```

The atlas proves that the apparently polar expression `(19)` is regular.
Equivalently, `(17)` agrees with the Bezout-cleared primitive in THM-3581.

### 2.1 Cohomology and the exponent-one boundary

All nonempty intersections of at least two distinct `U_i` equal
`A1 x Gm`.  The Cech--de Rham spectral sequence for this affine cover uses

```text
H_dR^q(A2)=C for q=0 and 0 otherwise,
H_dR^q(A1 x Gm)=C, C[dlog c], 0 for q=0,1,>=2.         (20)
```

The simplex incidence complex then gives

```text
H_dR^1(Y)=0,                 dim H_dR^2(Y)=h-1.        (21)
```

The same cover and the unit groups `C^*` on `U_i` and `C^*c^Z` on the
overlap recover

```text
O(Y)^*=C^*,                   Pic(Y)=Z^(h-1).          (22)
```

When `n=1`, equation `(16)` becomes

```text
(beta_i-beta_j) dc/c.                                  (23)
```

Its residue is nonzero for some pair when `h>=2`.  In the atlas description
of `H_dR^2(Y)`, the class of `omega` is represented by the nonconstant root
vector `(beta_i)` modulo constants.  Hence

```text
n=1, h>=2  =>  [omega]!=0  =>  no P,Q in A with {P,Q}=1. (24)
```

This boundary is sharp: when `h=1`, `a_1=e/gamma` after normalization and
`Y~=A2`.

### 2.2 Exact Darboux descent and Hamilton--Jacobi equation

Let `n>=2` and `kappa in C^*`.  Since `H_dR^1(Y)=0`, equations `(18)` and
`dP wedge dQ={P,Q}omega` give the equivalence

```text
{P,Q}=kappa
iff
kappa alpha=P dQ+d phi             for some P,Q,phi in A. (25)
```

Combining `(14),(17)` makes this a simultaneous polynomial-plane system.
A Darboux pair is equivalently a family

```text
F_i=(P_i,Q_i):A2_(a_i,c) -> A2                        (26)
```

such that

```text
Jac_(a_i,c)(P_i,Q_i)=kappa,
F_i=F_j o tau_ij on c!=0,                              (27)
```

where `tau_ij(a_i,c)=(a_i+(beta_i-beta_j)c^(-n),c)` is
the transition to the `j`-chart.  Equivalently, one may require compatible
polynomial triples `(P_i,Q_i,phi_i)` satisfying

```text
kappa(c da_i+n a_i dc)/(n-1)=P_i dQ_i+d phi_i.         (28)
```

For a fixed global `Q`, local solutions of `{P_i,Q}=1` differ on overlaps by
sections of the sheaf `ker{-,Q}`.  A global mate exists exactly when that
Cech cocycle can be killed by local first integrals.  The kernel cannot be
replaced by `C[Q]` or `C(Q)` without a separate generic-fibre hypothesis.

### 2.3 No chart-coordinate or chart-automorphism escape

For `i!=j`, the valuation at the generic point of the `j`-arm is

```text
ord_(L_j)(a_i)=-n.                                    (28a)
```

Indeed `c` is a uniformizer there and `b-beta_i` is a unit.  Hence
`a_i notin A` whenever `h>=2`.

Suppose a global Darboux pair `(P,Q)` restricted to one arm plane were a
polynomial automorphism.  Then

```text
C[c,a_i]=C[P,Q] subset A,
```

which would put `a_i` in `A`, contradicting `(28a)`.  More sharply, neither
`P` nor `Q` can be a polynomial coordinate of even one arm plane.  If, say,
`P` were a coordinate, choose and rescale a mate `v` so that
`C[c,a_i]=C[P,v]` and `{P,v}=1`.  The equation `{P,Q}=kappa` then forces

```text
Q=kappa v+f(P),                                        (28b)
```

so `(P,Q)` would be an automorphism, already excluded.  Consequently any
global Darboux pair would itself give a nonautomorphic polynomial Keller map
on every `U_i~=A2`, and hence a direct counterexample to `JC(2)`.  This is an
equivalence of construction debt, not evidence that such a pair exists.

```text

B'=P^(n-1)S^(n-1),

b=B(t)=a c^n,                e=aE(b),                  (30)
```

where `S` is nonconstant and squarefree, `S(0)!=0`, and every value
`B(sigma)` at a root `sigma` of `S` is nonzero.  The target is

```text
Y: c^n e=bE(b).                                        (31)
```

Choose the central root `beta_0=0`.  Its arm-plane coordinate is

```text
a_0=b/c^n,
e=a_0 E(c^n a_0),
a_0 o Phi=q/S(t)^n=a.                                  (32)
```

The exact preimage of the central plane chart is

```text
Phi^(-1)(U_0)=X_src=A2_(x,q) minus V(S(t))
             =Spec C[x,q,S(t)^(-1)].                  (33)
```

Indeed, a point maps to a side arm exactly when `S(t)=0`; the conditions
`B(sigma)!=0` and `gcd(P,S)=1` exclude the other factors of `c`.
THM-3581's exact-image theorem misses only side origins, none of which lies
in `U_0`.  Therefore

```text
(a_0,c):X_src -> U_0~=A2                               (34)
```

is a surjective etale morphism of generic degree `deg B`.  Every nonzero
central-arm collision of the compiler remains inside `X_src`.

If `r=deg S`, its deleted curves

```text
C_sigma={x^n q=sigma},          S(sigma)=0,            (35)
```

are `r` disjoint copies of `Gm`.  Hence

```text
chi_c(X_src)=1,              Pic(X_src)=0,
O(X_src)^*=C^* product_(S(sigma)=0)(t-sigma)^Z.        (36)
```

Thus `X_src` is an affine Euler fake plane whose exact missing coordinate is
the critical-puncture unit lattice, not Euler characteristic or Picard rank.

## 4. The critical-puncture no-filling lemma

The denominator in `(30)` cannot be removed by choosing a different normal
affine-plane model of the same function field.

Let `K/C` have transcendence degree two, let `a,c,t in K` with `t`
nonconstant, and suppose

```text
B(t)=a c^n                                              (42)
```

for a nonconstant polynomial `B in C[T]`.  Let `R subset K` be a normal
finitely generated `C`-domain with

```text
Frac(R)=K,                 a,c in R,                   (43)
```

and suppose `(a,c):Spec R->A2` is etale.  Then every critical point

```text
B'(sigma)=0,                 B(sigma)!=0               (44)
```

satisfies

```text
t-sigma in R^*.                                         (45)
```

Indeed, after dividing by the leading coefficient, `(42)` is a monic
equation for `t` over `R`.  Normality gives `t in R`.  If `t-sigma` were a
nonunit, it would lie in a maximal ideal `m`.  Differentiating `(42)` in
`Omega_R^1` and reducing modulo `m` gives

```text
0=c^n da+n a c^(n-1)dc.                                (46)
```

Etaleness makes `da,dc` a basis of the cotangent space, so `(46)` forces
`c=0`.  But evaluating `(42)` then gives

```text
B(sigma)=a c^n=0,                                      (47)
```

contrary to `(44)`.

Thus every normal etale model must retain all nonzero critical factors as
units.  In particular, no ring `R~=C[u,v]` can satisfy `(43)` and make the
same pair `(a,c)` polynomial Keller, because an affine plane has only
constant units.  For a compiler, the natural ring in `(33)` pays the exact
invoice by inverting every `t-sigma`.

These forced units are quantitatively independent.  If
`sigma_1,...,sigma_m` are the distinct critical points satisfying `(44)` and

```text
product_(j=1)^m(t-sigma_j)^u_j in C^*,
```

then transcendence of `t` and the valuations at `t=sigma_j` force every
`u_j=0`.  Therefore

```text
rank(R^*/C^*)>=m.                                      (47a)
```

For a critical-value compiler, the nonzero critical points are exactly the
roots of `S`, so `(47a)` matches the `deg S` deleted curves and the unit rank
in `(36)`.  The roots of `P` have critical value zero; they are not forced
units, remain in `X_src`, and carry the nontrivial central collisions.

The lemma concerns the **same** functions `(a,c)` and the same function
field.  It does not exclude a different Darboux pair on `Y`.

## 5. Sharp boundaries and assumption challenges

1. **Squarefreeness is load-bearing for `(6)`.**  For
   `Sigma=b^2(b-1), n=2`, the proposed substitution at `beta=0` gives
   `e=c^2a^2(c^2a-1)` and reaches only `e=0` when `c=0`; it misses the
   generic points of the free arm.  The multiple-root target is singular.
2. **One arm is trivial.**  When `h=1`, `Y~=A2`; there is no shear or
   multiarm intersection debt.
3. **Exponent one is the logarithmic boundary.**  The atlas survives, but
   `(23)` has nonzero residue and the physical symplectic form is not exact
   for `h>=2`.
4. **A zero critical value escapes the filling contradiction.**  The step
   `(47)` uses `B(sigma)!=0`.  In the compiler, a zero side value also
   collides with the central factor and destroys the smooth target model.
5. **Normality is used exactly once.**  It promotes the integral element
   `t` into `R`.  For a finite-type scheme, etaleness over the regular plane
   already makes `R` regular and normal, so nonnormality is not an independent
   etale escape.  A nonnormal conductor model may keep `t` only in its
   normalization, but necessarily pays by losing etaleness.
6. **Etaleness at the added divisor is essential.**  A compactification may
   add `t=sigma` only by allowing the differential of `(a,c)` to degenerate.
7. **Local polynomiality is insufficient.**  A Keller pair on one `U_i`
   need not survive any other singular shear.  The intersection and
   compatibility conditions `(14),(27)` are the missing sidecars.
8. **The Hamiltonian kernel is typed.**  Local mates form a torsor under
   `ker{-,Q}`, not automatically under one-variable polynomials or rational
   functions of `Q`.
9. **No Jacobian-conjecture conclusion follows.**  The punctured source is
   not `A2`, the target `Y` is not `A2` for `h>=2`, and the no-filling lemma
   fixes only the displayed rational pair.
10. **Separatedness prevents the trivial collision.**  If it is dropped,
    glue `h` copies of `A1` identically along `Gm` to form the line with
    `h` origins `Z_h`.  The affinization
    `Z_h x A1 -> A2` is locally the identity and hence etale, but identifies
    the `h` origin-lines.  The polar shear `(11)`, rather than an identity
    gluing, is exactly what makes the Danielewski atlas separated.
