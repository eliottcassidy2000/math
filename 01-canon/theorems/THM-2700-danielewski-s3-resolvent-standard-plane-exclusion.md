# THM-2700 -- Danielewski S3 resolvent and standard-plane exclusion

**PROVED + VERIFIED-EXACT + INDEPENDENTLY HOSTILE-AUDITED.**

THM-2655 turns a hypothetical quartic `S4` Keller map into a precise demand:
the regular locus of its full cubic-resolvent normalization must carry a
connected `V4` torsor whose three nonzero characters form the standard
two-dimensional `F2[S3]` module.  This theorem computes that module for a
large, natural family of `S3` splitting surfaces and finds that it is too
small for representation-theoretic, rather than numerical, reasons.

The decisive distinction is this:

```text
Danielewski boundary primes: fixed separately by C3;
D4 triality boundary arms:   cycled transitively by C3.             (1)
```

The first pattern can supply at most a trivial two-torsion line.  The second
is the sharp hostile and supplies the required standard plane.

## 1. The canonical `S3` surface and its polynomial quotient

Let `0!=g(x) in C[x]` and put

```text
R_g=C[x,r,s]/(rs-g(x)).                                      (2)
```

Fix a primitive cube root `zeta`.  Define

```text
rho(x,r,s)=(x,zeta r,zeta^(-1)s),
tau(x,r,s)=(x,s,r).                                          (3)
```

Then `rho^3=tau^2=1` and `tau rho tau=rho^(-1)`, so these
automorphisms generate `S3`.  The action is faithful, and

```text
q=r^3+s^3                                                   (4)
```

is invariant.

First take `C3=<rho>` invariants.  A monomial `r^i s^j` is fixed precisely
when `i-j=0 mod 3`.  Using `rs=g(x)`, every such monomial reduces to a
polynomial in

```text
x, R=r^3, S=s^3,       RS=g(x)^3.                           (5)
```

The reflection interchanges `R` and `S`.  Its symmetric invariants are
generated by `R+S=q` and `RS=g^3`, the latter already lying in `C[x]`.
Therefore

```text
R_g^(S3)=C[x,q].                                             (6)
```

In particular, the quotient is a polynomial affine plane even when `R_g`
is singular or nonfactorial.

The hypersurface `(2)` is Cohen--Macaulay.  Its singular points satisfy

```text
r=s=g(x)=g'(x)=0.                                            (7)
```

They are finite, so `(2)` is regular in codimension one and hence normal.
It is smooth exactly when `g` is squarefree, with nonzero constants included
in the squarefree boundary.

## 2. The actual cubic splitting field, discriminant, and different

The three conjugates of `r+s` are

```text
r+s,
zeta r+zeta^2 s,
zeta^2 r+zeta s.                                             (8)
```

Their elementary symmetric functions give

```text
P(T)=T^3-3g(x)T-q.                                           (9)
```

Since the faithful invariant-field extension has degree six, `Frac(R_g)`
is the full splitting field of `(9)` over `C(x,q)`, not merely a field with
the same discriminant.  Directly,

```text
Disc_T(P)=27(4g^3-q^2).                                     (10)
```

On the splitting surface this becomes

```text
Disc_T(P)=-27(r^3-s^3)^2.                                  (11)
```

The relative Kahler different is equally explicit.  Over
`S=C[x,q]`, write

```text
R_g=S[r,s]/(rs-g(x), r^3+s^3-q).                            (12)
```

This is a finite complete intersection.  Its relative differential matrix
is

```text
[ s       r   ]
[ 3r^2    3s^2],                                            (13)
```

whose determinant is `-3(r^3-s^3)`.  Hence

```text
Fitt_0 Omega_(R_g/S)=(r^3-s^3).                             (14)
```

Thus the family has exactly the expected cubic discriminant square and
reflection divisor.  Those data alone will not be enough: the Kummer module
below is the stricter gate.

## 3. Complete divisor class group and unit computation

Suppose now that `g` is nonconstant and write

```text
g(x)=c product_(i=1)^d (x-a_i)^(m_i),
c!=0,  a_i distinct,  m_i>=1.                               (15)
```

Let

```text
D_i=(r,x-a_i).                                               (16)
```

After inverting `r`, relation `(2)` eliminates `s` and gives the Laurent
UFD

```text
(R_g)_r=C[x,r^(+-1)].                                       (17)
```

Nagata's sequence therefore generates `Cl(R_g)` by the `D_i`.  The units
of `(17)` are `C^* r^Z`, and

```text
div(r)=sum_i m_i D_i.                                       (18)
```

There are no other relations.  Consequently

```text
Cl(R_g)=Z^d/<(m_1,...,m_d)>
       =Z^(d-1) direct_sum Z/m_0,
m_0=gcd(m_1,...,m_d).                                       (19)
```

The same localization proves

```text
R_g^*=C^*.                                                   (20)
```

Indeed a unit becomes `c r^k` in `(17)`; for nonconstant `g`, regularity of
both it and its inverse along the divisors in `(18)` forces `k=0`.

The group action on `(19)` is the load-bearing information.  The order-three
element preserves every prime `D_i`.  If

```text
E_i=(s,x-a_i),                                               (21)
```

then `tau(D_i)=E_i`, while

```text
div(x-a_i)=D_i+E_i.                                         (22)
```

Thus

```text
rho[D_i]=[D_i],              tau[D_i]=-[D_i].               (23)
```

In particular every element of `Cl(R_g)[2]` is fixed by all of `S3`.
Equation `(19)` gives the exact alternatives

```text
Cl(R_g)[2]=0                 if m_0 is odd,
Cl(R_g)[2]=F2_triv           if m_0 is even.                (24)
```

This is why the free rank `d-1` and the mere size of the class group do not
help.  The quartic gate asks for a standard plane, while `(24)` supplies at
most one trivial line.

## 4. Exact Kummer module and quartic-resolvent exclusion

Let

```text
U_g=(Spec R_g)_reg.                                          (25)
```

Normality and the codimension-two complement give

```text
Gamma(U_g,O)^*=R_g^*,             Pic(U_g)=Cl(R_g).          (26)
```

The etale Kummer row is

```text
0 -> R_g^*/R_g^(*)2 -> H^1_et(U_g,mu_2)
  -> Cl(R_g)[2] -> 0.                                       (27)
```

Because `C` is algebraically closed, `(20)` has no nonzero unit
squareclass.  Combining `(24)` and `(27)` yields, as `F2[S3]` modules,

```text
H^1_et(U_g,mu_2)=0                 if m_0 is odd,
H^1_et(U_g,mu_2)=F2_triv           if m_0 is even.           (28)
```

If `g` is a nonzero constant, then

```text
R_g=C[x,r,r^(-1)],
Cl(R_g)=0,
R_g^*/R_g^(*)2=<[r]>=F2_triv.                               (29)
```

Here `rho` only scales `r` by a complex squareclass and `tau` sends its
exponent to its negative, so the line is again trivial.

Let `W` be the standard irreducible two-dimensional `F2[S3]` module.  From
`(28)`--`(29)`,

```text
W does not embed in H^1_et(U_g,mu_2) for any 0!=g in C[x].  (30)
```

Now suppose that a degree-four planar `S4` Keller map had full
Galois-resolvent normalization `S3`-equivariantly isomorphic, over its
polynomial target base, to `(2)`--`(6)`.  THM-2655 produces on its regular
locus a connected `V4` torsor whose three nonzero characters form `W`.
This contradicts `(30)`.  Therefore

```text
no planar quartic S4 Keller map has a Danielewski full
Galois-resolvent normalization (2) with the action (3).       (31)
```

A polynomial automorphism of the invariant base `C[x,q]` does not change
the equivariant normalization or its Kummer module, so `(31)` is
coordinate-invariant in that precise sense.  An abstract isomorphism that
forgets the `S3` action is not enough.

## 5. Secondary `mu_4` level

THM-2695 identifies the secondary obstruction as

```text
Pic(U_g)[2]/2Pic(U_g)[4].                                   (32)
```

For the cyclic torsion factor in `(19)`, its unique order-two element is
twice an order-four element exactly when `4|m_0`.  Thus, when `m_0` is even,

```text
the trivial line in (28) is mu_4-nonliftable
iff m_0=2 mod 4.                                             (33)
```

This refinement does not rescue the quartic carrier: at either secondary
level the line is still `S3`-trivial, not `W`.  The constant-case unit line
lifts through the unit Kummer row.

## 6. Sharp boundaries

### 6.1 Linear and squarefree controls

If `g` is linear, relation `rs=g(x)` eliminates `x` and the source is
`A2_(r,s)`.  The quotient

```text
(r,s) |-> (rs,r^3+s^3)                                      (34)
```

is the standard rank-two `S3` reflection quotient.  It is an instance of
THM-2696's **general** proper-reflection-quotient lemma.  It is not the
three-dimensional constant-different slice of that theorem.

If `g` is squarefree with at least two roots, `R_g` is smooth and generally
nonfactorial, but `(19)` is torsion-free.  Thus smoothness and a large class
group still do not supply the required plane.  Repeated roots give isolated
`A`-type singularities and can create the single torsion line in `(24)`,
but not its missing `C3` orbit.

### 6.2 The `D4` triality hostile

The trace-zero surface from THM-2655 is

```text
X_D4=Spec C[a,b,c,d]/(a+b+c,d^2-abc),                      (35)
```

with `S3` permuting `a,b,c` and fixing `d`.  Its invariant ring is

```text
C[e_2,d],                                                   (36)
```

because `e_1=0` and `e_3=abc=d^2`.  The `D4` resolution lattice has Smith
diagonal

```text
(1,1,2,2),                                                  (37)
```

so its two-torsion discriminant group is `F2^2`.  Diagram triality cycles
the three nonzero classes; this is exactly the standard `S3` plane.

Thus `(35)` passes the abstract THM-2655 carrier gate.  Its three moving
arms explain the failure of the Danielewski argument: `C3` permutes them,
whereas it fixes every `D_i` in `(23)`.  Equality of discriminants and the
presence of a reflection divisor do not distinguish these two cases.

## 7. Scope and next gate

The theorem excludes an infinite, normal, polynomial-quotient family of
possible full Galois-resolvent normalizations.  It includes smooth
nonfactorial surfaces and singular `A`-type surfaces, and it computes every
constant and repeated-root boundary.

It does **not** classify arbitrary `S3` surfaces over `A2`, exclude the
triality carrier `(35)`, identify every quartic graph resolvent with `(2)`,
or prove `JC(2)` or `DC(2)`.  The next useful geometric test is now typed:
compute the `C3` action on boundary/divisor classes, not only the class-group
rank, discriminant, or local singularity name.  A boundary-tree formulation
would detect the standard plane exactly when a moving three-arm block has an
even mod-two intersection determinant; that broader lattice statement is
not used here.
