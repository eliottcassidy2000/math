# THM-1430 — The graph side of switching is exactly E_n, and the even-graph bridge is odd-n only

**Credits and non-duplication.** kind-pasteur's THM-1415 (same day) computed switching
classes of **tournaments** up to isomorphism — `1, 2, 2, 6` at `n = 3..6` — and proved
these are **not** `A002854 = 2, 3, 7, 16`, explicitly recording as **refuted** their own
guess that the base-path-free quotient would land on the repo's `E_n`. mac-mini's THM-474
established `tilings = switching classes of tournaments = oriented two-graphs`.

This note does the side nobody had run: **undirected Seidel switching**. It confirms kps's
negative result is not a failure of the method but a genuine tournament/graph asymmetry —
the graph quotient hits `E_n` on the nose.

---

## 1. The census (verified two ways)

Seidel switch `σ_S(G)`: complement every edge between `S` and `V∖S`; leave edges inside
`S` and inside `V∖S` alone. Switching class = orbit under all `2^n` switches. Two-graph
`Δ(G) = { T ∈ C(V,3) : G has an odd number of edges inside T }` — a complete switching
invariant.

| `n` | switching classes of graphs, up to iso | `A002854(n)` | `V(E_n)` (repo) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | **2** | 2 | 2 |
| 4 | **3** | 3 | 3 |
| 5 | **7** | 7 | 7 |
| 6 | **16** | 16 | 16 |
| 7 | **54** | 54 | 54 |

**Method A** (`seidel_switching_census_opus_S404.py`): every switching class contains a
graph with a chosen vertex isolated, so classes correspond to graphs on `n−1` vertices
modulo the identifications generated by *"these two `(n−1)`-graphs are descendants of one
common two-graph"*; union-find those. **Method B** (independent, `n ≤ 6`): canonicalise
the odd-triple bitmask `Δ(G)` directly under `S_n` over all `2^C(n,2)` graphs. Both give
`2, 3, 7, 16`.

**Consequence.** `V(E_n)` — the vertex set of the repo's even-graph metagraph, a
first-class object per CLAUDE.md — **is the set of two-graphs on `n` vertices.** The whole
classical two-graph literature (Seidel, Taylor, Babai–Cameron; regular two-graphs,
equiangular lines, conference matrices) applies to `E_n` directly.

## 2. The bridge is ODD-`n` only (the sharp part)

The classical statement "each switching class contains a unique even graph" is **not**
uniform in `n`. Counting even graphs inside each class:

| `n` | parity | classes | even graphs per class (value: #classes) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | odd | 2 | `{1: 2}` |
| 4 | even | 3 | `{0: 1, 4: 2}` |
| 5 | odd | 7 | `{1: 7}` |
| 6 | even | 16 | `{0: 10, 16: 6}` |
| 7 | odd | 54 | `{1: 54}` |

- **`n` odd:** every class contains **exactly one** even graph. A canonical bijection
  `switching classes ↔ even graphs`. Verified at `n = 3, 5, 7` (2, 7, 54 classes, all
  multiplicity one).
- **`n` even:** the bijection **fails pointwise**. Classes carry either `0` or `2^(n−2)`
  even graphs (`4` at `n=4`, `16` at `n=6`), and most classes carry none (10 of 16 at
  `n = 6`). The totals still agree with `A002854` — that is Mallows–Sloane, a **counting**
  identity, not a bijection.

**This retro-explains an existing repo restriction.** CLAUDE.md records the projection
`T_cycle = (I + L(K_n))·T mod 2` as valid for **odd `n` only**, without saying why. §2 is
the reason: the switching-class ↔ even-graph correspondence is only a bijection at odd `n`.
The odd-`n` hypothesis is not a technical convenience — it is exactly where the bridge is
single-valued.

**Care with CLAUDE.md's wording.** "V(E_n) = A002854(n), the sequence of non-isomorphic
even graphs" is correct as a count at every `n`, but should not be read as naming a
canonical even-graph representative per class when `n` is even. At `n = 6`, ten of the
sixteen classes have no even representative at all.

## 3. Seidel spectra and regular two-graphs

The Seidel matrix `S = J − I − 2A` transforms under switching by conjugation with a
`diag(±1)`, so its **spectrum is both a switching and an isomorphism invariant**. A
**regular two-graph** is one whose Seidel matrix has exactly two distinct eigenvalues —
the equiangular-line / conference-matrix objects.

| `n` | distinct Seidel spectra | regular (2-eigenvalue) spectra |
|---|---|---|
| 3 | 2 | 1 — `{−3, 1}`, mult `[1,3]`… (trivial: complete/empty) |
| 4 | — | trivial only |
| 5 | 7 | 2, both trivial (`{−1,4}`, `{−4,1}`) |
| 6 | 16 | 3, of which **one is genuine**: `±√5`, multiplicities `[3, 3]` |

`n = 6` carries the unique non-trivial regular two-graph in this range — the order-6
conference matrix — consistent with the classical list (regular two-graphs exist at
`n = 6, 10, 14, 16, 18, 26, …`).

## 4. The order-14 object, catalogued (and explicitly not a bridge)

A symmetric conference matrix of order `n` is equivalent to a regular two-graph on `n`
points. Paley's construction needs `n−1` an odd prime power `≡ 1 (mod 4)`. At **`n = 14`**:
`n−1 = 13`, and `13 ≡ 1 (mod 4)`. **So `C₁₄` exists**, built from the quadratic residues
mod 13, `QR = {1,3,4,9,10,12}`. Verified: symmetric, zero diagonal, entries in `{0,±1}`,
`C·Cᵀ = 13·I`, Seidel spectrum `±√13` with multiplicities `[7, 7]` — a **regular two-graph
on 14 points**.

**HONEST SCOPING — this is NOT claimed as a connection to LRC(14).** Per HYP-8230's rule,
coincidence of index is not evidence of mechanism. The LRC fourteen is `2·7`, arising from
parity and `λ = 1/n`; this fourteen is `13 + 1` with `13 ≡ 1 (mod 4)`. Different
arithmetic. Nor is the `[7, 7]` eigenvalue split evidence: a conference matrix of order `n`
*always* has eigenvalues `±√(n−1)` each with multiplicity `n/2`, so `[7,7]` is forced by
`n = 14` alone and carries no information. Recorded as a catalogued object at an index the
repo cares about, nothing more.

## 5. What this does and does not settle

- **Settles:** `E_n` = two-graphs; the graph/tournament asymmetry in switching is real and
  kps's THM-1415 negative result is correct and complementary, not a dead end;
  the odd-`n` restriction on the repo's projection is structural.
- **Does not settle:** whether any two-graph invariant transfers usefully to the tournament
  side, where THM-1415 shows the quotient is far coarser (56 iso classes → 6 at `n = 6`).
- **Open, cheap:** extend the census to `n = 8` (expect 243) and `n = 9` (expect 2038). The
  descendant union-find needs graphs on `n−1` vertices up to iso, so `n = 8` needs the 1044
  seven-vertex graphs — feasible with a better canonical form than brute-force `S_n` min.

**Method note for future sessions.** The first run of the census returned `2, 2, 3, 1, 2` —
wrong, and wrong in a way that looked like over-merging. The bug: an `(n−1)`-vertex bitmask
was reused directly as an `n`-vertex one, but the pair orderings differ (bit 2 is `(1,2)`
in `pairs(3)` and `(0,3)` in `pairs(4)`). Brute-force ground truth at `n ≤ 6` caught it
immediately. **Always index-lift explicitly between vertex counts, and always keep an
independent slow method for small `n`.**
