# THM-4724 — the affine fixed-point criterion, the even-n switching half of HYP-9172, and uniform circulant rules

**PROVED (pre-audit).** Note: [odd_primes_20261020_affine_criterion_switching_even_n_uniform_rules](../../05-knowledge/results/odd_primes_20261020_affine_criterion_switching_even_n_uniform_rules.md).

## 1. The affine fixed-point criterion

Let `U <= V = F_2^{E(K_n)}` be an `S_n`-submodule and `A = T/U` the set of tournaments modulo reversal along `U`: an affine space over `V/U` on which `S_n` acts affinely, `g[T] = [gT]`. Let `P <= S_n` have odd order, `h ∈ N(P)`, `L = <P, h> = P<h>`.

**Theorem A.** `|Fix_A(L)| = Σ_{X ∈ (U-perp)^L} (-1)^{r(h, X)}`. Explicitly: `Fix_A(L)` is empty if some `L`-invariant graph in `U-perp` has an odd number of edges reversed by `h`, and has exactly `|(U-perp)^L|` elements otherwise.

*Proof.* (1) `Fix_A(P)` is non-empty and is an affine space over `W := (V/U)^P`: the obstruction to a fixed point of an odd-order group acting affinely on an `F_2`-affine space lies in `H^1(P, V/U) = 0` (average the cocycle, `|P|` being invertible in `F_2`). (2) `h` normalises `P`, so it preserves `Fix_A(P)` and acts there affinely with linear part `h|_W`; `L`-fixed points of `A` are the `h`-fixed points of `Fix_A(P)`; their number is `|W^h| = |(V/U)^L|` if there is one and `0` otherwise. (3) `|(V/U)^L| = |(U-perp)^L|`: since `|P|` is odd, `V = V^P ⊕ V_0` with `V_0 = ker π`, `π = |P|^{-1} Σ_{g ∈ P} g`, and `(V/U)^P = V^P/U^P`; the invariant pairing is nondegenerate on `V^P` (`V^P ⊥ V_0`); for `x ∈ V^P`, `<x, u> = <x, πu>`, so `(U-perp)^P = (U^P)^{⊥} ∩ V^P`; hence `W = V^P/U^P ≅ ((U-perp)^P)^*` as `N(P)`-modules; and for the cyclic group `<h>` on a finite `F_2`-space `M`, `dim M^h = dim ker(h-1) = dim coker(h-1) = dim (M^*)^h`. (4) Criterion: with a base point `a_0 = [T_0] ∈ Fix_A(P)` and `h a_0 = a_0 + c`, `c ∈ W`, a fixed point `a_0 + w` exists iff `c ∈ (h-1)W` iff every `h`-invariant functional on `W` kills `c`; under (3) the `h`-invariant functionals are the `X ∈ (U-perp)^L`, and `<X, c> = <X, hT_0 - T_0>` = the parity of the edges of `X` on which `hT_0` and `T_0` disagree = the number of self-paired `h`-orbits on `E(X)` mod 2 = `r(h, X)` (the disagreements along an `h`-orbit of edges telescope to the parity of the orbit's self-pairedness, independently of `T_0`; Royle et al., Lemma 4.3). (5) A nonzero linear functional on an `F_2`-space has as many zeros as ones, which turns the case distinction into the signed sum. ∎

**Corollary R(U)** (restricted twisted Burnside principle, every `U`, every `n`). For `Γ <= N(P)`: `#(Γ-orbits on Fix_{T/U}(P)) = #(Γ-orbits on the Γ-even members of (U-perp)^P)`. *Proof.* Cauchy–Frobenius on both sides; the terms agree by Theorem A. ∎ `U = 0` is THM-4721 Theorem R; `U = B`, `n` odd, is THM-4722 Theorem R'; `P = 1` is the per-permutation identity of THM-4531 Theorem G.

**Corollary (THM-4721 Lemma P).** For `U = 0` and `h` of even order, `Fix_T(L) = ∅` (tournaments have no even-order automorphisms), so by Theorem A some `L`-invariant graph has `r(h, X) = 1`. ∎ (The explicit `F_2`-sum of THM-4721 is no longer needed.)

**Corollary S(U)** (Sylow refinement, every `U`, every `n`). For a Sylow `p`-subgroup `P` of `S_n`, `p` odd: `tau_{T/U}(P) = tau_{U-perp}(P)`. *Proof.* As THM-4721 Theorem S: two `P`-fixed classes (resp. `P`-invariant even graphs) that are isomorphic are `N(P)`-equivalent, because `sPs^{-1}` and `P` are Sylow in the stabiliser of the image, whatever the parity of that stabiliser's order; an odd `P`-invariant graph in `U-perp` has an odd automorphism normalising `P` (Frattini, `P` Sylow in the index-two even subgroup); then Corollary R(U) with `Γ = N(P)`. ∎ In particular the refined identity `tau_C(P) = tau_EE(P)` holds for switching classes and even Euler graphs at every `n`, which was HYP-9341 (a)'s conjecture (checked exhaustively at `n = 6` in THM-4722's `switching_even_n.out`).

## 2. The switching half of HYP-9172 (a) at even n, by the Sylow route

**Lemma 0** (THM-4531 / the packet's Lemma 3, re-proved). The stabiliser of a switching class contains no involution fixing a vertex. *Proof.* Each class contains a unique member in which the vertex `v` is a source (switch the in-neighbours of `v`; two such members differ by a cut with no edge at `v`, hence by nothing). An automorphism of the class fixing `v` fixes that member, so it is a tournament automorphism; tournaments have no involutory automorphisms. ∎

**Lemma L.** Let `n >= 6` be even. (i) If `n >= 8`, `n ≠ 10`, there are primes `p > q > n/2` with `q >= 5` (Ramanujan: `π(x) - π(x/2) >= 2` for `x >= 11`; `n = 8`: 7, 5), and no stabiliser of a switching class contains a `p`-cycle and a `q`-cycle. (ii) For `n = 6` (resp. `n = 10`) no class stabiliser contains a 5-cycle (resp. 7-cycle) and a Sylow 3-subgroup of `S_n`.

*Proof.* (i) Suppose `L` stabilises a class and contains a `p`-cycle `c` and a `q`-cycle `d`. Their supports meet (`p + q > n`), so one `L`-orbit `O` contains both; `m = |O|` satisfies `p <= m <= n < 2p`. `L|_O` is primitive: a block system would have fewer than `p` blocks, so `c` would fix every block and act inside blocks of size `< p`, hence trivially. Jordan: if `q <= m - 3` then `L|_O ⊇ A_m` with `m >= 7`, which contains an involution with a fixed point; a lift of such an involution to `L` has even order `2k`, and its `k`-th power is an involution of `L` fixing a vertex (if `k` is odd it restricts to the given involution; if `k` is even it fixes `O` pointwise), contradicting Lemma 0. So `m <= q + 2 <= p`, i.e. `m = p = q + 2`. Then `L|_O` is a transitive group of prime degree `p` containing the `q`-cycle `d`; by Burnside it is `2`-transitive or lies in `AGL(1, p)`; `AGL(1, p)` has no element of order `q` (`q` divides neither `p` nor `p - 1 = q + 1`); a `2`-transitive group of odd degree `p` has even order and every involution of a group of odd degree fixes a point — again an involution of `L` fixing a vertex. (ii) `n = 6`: `|L|` would be divisible by 45, and the only subgroups of `S_6` with that property contain `A_6`, which has `(12)(34)`. `n = 10`: a Sylow 3-subgroup has order 81; let `O` be the `L`-orbit of the 7-cycle's support, `m ∈ {7, 8, 9, 10}`. For `m = 10`, Jordan gives `A_10 <= L|_O`, excluded as above. For `m = 9`, `L|_O` is primitive of odd degree containing a 7-cycle: if it has even order its involutions fix points (excluded); if it has odd order it cannot be `2`-transitive, so the stabiliser of a point `x` fixes the second point `y` outside the 7-cycle's support (otherwise it is transitive on the other eight points), and `L_x = L_y` defines a block system with blocks of size 2 on 9 points — impossible. For `m = 8` and `m = 7`, `L <= L|_O × S_{10-m}` has 3-part at most `9 · 1` resp. `9 · 3`, below 81. ∎

**Theorem H''.** For every even `n >= 6` there is no `S_n`-equivariant map from switching classes to Euler graphs that is bijective from class types onto even-Euler-graph types. *Proof.* THM-4531 Lemma N1 reduces this to Hall's condition for the stabiliser-containment graph. Let `P_1, P_2` be the two Sylow subgroups of Lemma L (`<p-cycle>`, `<q-cycle>`, or the Sylow 3-subgroup), `S` the set of class types whose stabiliser contains a conjugate of `P_1` or `P_2`. By Lemma L no class is counted twice, so `|S| = tau_C(P_1) + tau_C(P_2)`. Its neighbourhood lies in `E_1 ∪ E_2` (even Euler graphs with the respective symmetry), and the empty graph lies in both, so `|N(S)| <= tau_EE(P_1) + tau_EE(P_2) - 1 = |S| - 1` by Corollary S(B). ∎ Together with THM-4722 Theorem H' (odd `n`) and Theorem H (tournaments), this gives a second proof of HYP-9172 (a) in full, independent of the packet's valuation argument; both are now in the repository.

## 3. Uniform rules for the prime-circulant bijection: the derivative family and the forcing analysis

THM-4721 Corollary C gives, for each prime `p`, an `AGL(1,p)`-equivariant bijection from circulant tournaments to even circulant graphs. A *dilation rule* is a map `S ↦ f_φ(S) = {x ∈ Z_p^* : φ(1_S(c_1 x), ..., 1_S(c_k x)) = 1}` with fixed nonzero integers `c_i` and a fixed Boolean `φ`; it is multiplier-equivariant for every `p`, and HYP-9341 (c)'s derivatives are `D_c = f_XOR` with atoms `x, cx`. Write `K_max(p)` for the odd part of `Z_p^*` (the `2^e`-th powers, `2^e || p-1`); the tournaments invariant under `K_max(p)` are the most symmetric circulant ones (`QR_p` for `p ≡ 3 (mod 4)`).

**Lemma F (forcing).** For `p ≡ 3 (mod 4)` and for `p ≡ 5 (mod 8)`, the only even circulant graph invariant under `K_max(p)` is the empty graph, so every `K_max(p)`-invariant tournament must be sent to the empty graph by any equivariant type bijection. *Proof.* A symmetric union of `K_max`-cosets is a union of antipodal coset pairs in `Z_p^*/K_max ≅ Z_{2^e}`; for `e = 1` the options are `∅` and `K_p`; for `e = 2` they are `∅`, the squares, the non-squares and `K_p`; the squares and non-squares have stabiliser the squares, which contains `-1`, and `|S| = (p-1)/2 ≡ 2 (mod 4)` is not divisible by `4`, so by THM-4721's evenness criterion they are odd; `K_p` is odd. ∎ (For `e >= 3` this can fail: at `p = 41` the union of the `K_max`-cosets `{0, 1, 4, 5}` of `Z_8` is an even circulant graph (connection set of size 20, stabiliser `Z_10`).)

**Theorem W.** (i) Let `S` be a `K_max(p)`-invariant tournament set. If `c ∈ K_max(p)` then `D_c(S) = ∅`; if `c ≡ -1 (mod K_max(p))` then `D_c(S) = Z_p^*`, the odd complete graph; so in the second case `D_c` is not even-valued and in particular not a type bijection. (ii) For every integer `c ≠ 1` the set of primes `p` with `c ≡ -1 (mod K_max(p))` is infinite. Consequently no derivative rule `D_c` is a type bijection for all primes; the primes at which it fails include, for `c` not a square, infinitely many `p ≡ 3 (mod 4)` (those with `(c/p) = -1`), and for `c = d^2` with `d` not `±1, ±2` times a square, infinitely many `p ≡ 5 (mod 8)` (those with `(d/p) = -1`).

*Proof.* (i) `K_max`-invariant `S` means `1_S(cx) = 1_S(x)` for `c ∈ K_max`, and `1_S(cx) = 1_S(-x) = 1 - 1_S(x)` for `c ∈ -K_max`. (ii) Case `c` not a square (including `c = -m^2`): for `p ≡ 3 (mod 4)`, `K_max = QR_p` and `c ≡ -1 (mod K_max)` iff `(c/p) = -1`; the characters `(c/·)` and `(-1/·)` are distinct unless `c = -m^2`, so by Dirichlet infinitely many `p ≡ 3 (mod 4)` have `(c/p) = -1`; if `c = -m^2` every `p ≡ 3 (mod 4)` does. Case `c = d^2`, `d ≠ ±1`: for `p ≡ 5 (mod 8)`, `K_max` is the group of fourth powers and `-1` is a square but not a fourth power; `-c = (id)^2` (with `i^2 = -1`) is a fourth power iff `id` is a square iff `(d/p) = -1` (as `i` is a non-square when `p ≡ 5 (mod 8)`); if `d` is not `±1, ±2` times a square, `(d/·)` is independent of the characters mod 8 and infinitely many `p ≡ 5 (mod 8)` have `(d/p) = -1`; if `d = ±2 m^2` then `(d/p) = (2/p) = -1` for all `p ≡ 5 (mod 8)` (this is the identity `-4 = (1+i)^4`: `D_4` fails at every `p ≡ 5 (mod 8)`, as at 5, 13, 29, 37, 53, 61 in the table); if `d = ±m^2`, i.e. `c = m^4`, pass to `p ≡ 9 (mod 16)` and the eighth roots of unity: `-m^4 = (ζ_8 m)^4` is an eighth power iff `ζ_8 m` is a square iff `(m/p) = -1` (`ζ_8` is a non-square for `p ≡ 9 (mod 16)`), which happens for infinitely many such `p` unless `m` is `±1, ±2` times a square; the remaining cases `c ∈ {m^8, 16 m^8}` continue up the 2-power Kummer tower `Q(ζ_{2^{e+1}}, (-c)^{1/2^e})`, where Chebotarev's theorem supplies infinitely many primes with `v_2(p-1) = e` and `-c` a `2^e`-th power (CITED; the elementary part covers every `c` that is not an eighth power up to a factor 16). ∎

**Forcing analysis for general dilation rules.** For `p ≡ 3 (mod 4)` the rule `f_φ` sends `QR_p` to the empty graph iff `φ` vanishes on the two patterns `([χ_p(c_i) = s])_i`, `s = ±1`; as `p` varies over primes `≡ 3 (mod 4)` the sign vectors `(χ_p(c_i))_i` realise every assignment compatible with the multiplicative relations among the `c_i` and `-1` (quadratic reciprocity and Dirichlet), so a rule that is correct at the symmetric tournaments for all such `p` must have `φ = 0` on all these patterns. This does not constrain `φ` on patterns that no character realises (for atoms `x, 2x, 4x`: the pattern `(1, 1, 0)`), so the general non-existence of a uniform dilation rule remains OPEN; HYP-9341 (c)'s exhaustive search (all Boolean combinations of at most three of ten atoms, primes 5..23) is the current evidence, and Theorem W explains its best survivors: they are the variants of `D_4`, which pass exactly the primes `p ≡ 3 (mod 4)` in that range and fail at `5, 13, 17`.

## 4. The power-sum detector of circulant symmetry

For a tournament set `S ⊂ Z_p^*` put `σ_k(S) = Σ_{s ∈ S} s^k ∈ F_p`. Then `σ_k(aS) = a^k σ_k(S)`, `σ_k(S) = 0` for even `k ≠ 0 (mod p-1)` (`S ⊔ -S = Z_p^*`), and if `S` is invariant under a multiplier `κ ≠ 1` then `σ_k(S) = 0` for every `k` with `κ^k ≠ 1`, in particular for every `k` coprime to `p - 1`. Call `S` *traceless* if `σ_k(S) = 0` for all `k` coprime to `p - 1`. The `F_p`-valued Fourier transform on `Z_p^*` (`p` does not divide `p - 1`) identifies `(σ_k)_k` with the coefficients of `1_S` in the basis `x ↦ x^k`, so traceless means: the Fourier support of the odd function `f_S = 1_S - 1_{-S}` avoids the unit indices, i.e. lies in `⋃_{ℓ | p-1, ℓ odd prime} ℓZ`.

**Proposition D.** (i) If the odd part of `p - 1` is a prime power `ℓ^a`, then `S` is traceless iff `S` is invariant under the subgroup of order `ℓ`. (ii) If `p ≡ 3 (mod 4)` and `p - 1 = 2 ℓ_1 ℓ_2` with distinct odd primes, then `S` is traceless iff its multiplier stabiliser is nontrivial. (iii) The equivalence fails for `p = 61` (`60 = 4·3·5`: 1444 traceless sets, 360 of them rigid) and for `p = 211` (`210 = 2·3·5·7`, although `p ≡ 3 (mod 4)`), by an explicit "slice-mixed" construction.

*Proof.* (i) Non-unit odd indices are exactly the multiples of `ℓ`, i.e. the characters trivial on the order-`ℓ` subgroup. (ii) Write `Z_{p-1} = Z_2 × Z_{ℓ_1} × Z_{ℓ_2}`; traceless means `f_S = u + v` with `u` independent of the `Z_{ℓ_1}`-coordinate and `v` independent of the `Z_{ℓ_2}`-coordinate. Fix the `Z_2`-coordinate: the `ℓ_1 × ℓ_2` array `u(y) + v(x)` is `±1`-valued; if `u` took two values `u ≠ u'`, every `v(x)` would lie in `{±1 - u} ∩ {±1 - u'}`, forcing `u' = u ± 2` and `v` constant, and three values of `u` are impossible (`4 ≠ 0, ±2` in `F_p`, `p > 5`); so on each `Z_2`-slice `f_S` is independent of `x` or of `y`. Oddness (`-1` is the element `(1,0,0)`) makes the two slices negatives of each other, hence of the same type, so `f_S` is invariant under the subgroup of order `ℓ_1` or `ℓ_2`. (iii) At `p = 61` take `Z_60 = Z_4 × Z_3 × Z_5` and `f = φ(t_5), ψ(t_3), -φ(t_5), -ψ(t_3)` on the slices `t_4 = 0, 1, 2, 3` with nonconstant `φ, ψ`: it is odd (`-1 = (2,0,0)`), `±1`-valued, its Fourier support lies in `3Z ∪ 5Z`, and no multiplier preserves the mixed slice types (`traceless_p61.out`, including the meet-in-the-middle count). At `p = 211` mix the `Z_7`-slices between a function of `t_5` and a function of `t_3` (`traceless_p211.out`). ∎

Consequence for uniform rules: for a rigid circulant tournament in the regimes (i)–(ii) the least unit index `k` with `σ_k(S) ≠ 0` and the unique `a` with `a^k σ_k(S) = 1` give a *canonical representative* `a S` of its multiplier orbit, uniformly in `p`; so a uniform rule need only be a bijection between normalised rigid tournament sets and (similarly normalised, by `σ_k` with `gcd(k, p-1) = 2`) rigid even symmetric sets. Theorem W shows that no local rule does this; the normalised matching problem is the honest residue of HYP-9341 (c).
