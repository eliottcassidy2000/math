# THM-3937 -- the whole linear-conic fold-three family is globally rigid

**PROVED CANDIDATE + VERIFIED-EXACT; INDEPENDENT HOSTILE AUDIT PENDING.**
Work over an algebraically closed field `k` of characteristic zero. For an
arbitrary `r in k`, put

```text
q=t^3+r t X-X^2,
H=q^2-4X^3,
B_r=k[t,X,W]/(W^2-H),                 S_r=Spec(B_r).       (1)
```

Then `B_r` is a normal domain with unique singular point the origin, and

```text
B_r^*=k*,                              Cl(B_r)=Z/3.        (2)
```

The two primes

```text
D+=(X,q+W),                            D-=(X,q-W)          (3)
```

are inverse generators. On the singular surface and its regular locus,
respectively,

```text
Pic(S_r)=H^1_et(S_r,mu_3)=0,
Pic(S_r^reg)=H^1_et(S_r^reg,mu_3)=Z/3.                   (4)
```

The unique nonzero smooth-locus cyclic character, up to inversion, is the
Cardano cover

```text
z^3=(q+W)/2,          v^3=(q-W)/2,          zv=X,
u=z+v,
u^3-3Xu-(t^3+r t X-X^2)=0.                               (5)
```

The cubic ring in `(5)` is normal and monogenic for every `r`. Consequently
no member of the complete explicit THM-3932 family admits a second normal
generically `S3` cubic field or a normal nonmonogenic cubic order having this
exact squarefree branch and quadratic resolvent.

This is a uniform extension of THM-3935, which is the member `r=0`. It closes
variation inside this family, not nonnormal suborders, altered branch
multiplicities, nonlinear-color folds, or JC(2).

## 1. Uniform normality and the vertical-prime ledger

Every closed fibre of `S_r -> A1_t` is irreducible. Fix `t=lambda` and write

```text
A=lambda^3,                         B=r lambda.
```

The quartic on that fibre is

```text
(-X^2+BX+A)^2-4X^3.                                     (6)
```

If `(6)` were a square, it would be `(X^2+pX+c)^2`. Comparing the `X^3`
and constant coefficients gives

```text
p=-B-2,                              c=+A or -A.          (7)
```

For `c=A`, the `X^2` and `X` rows reduce to

```text
A+B+1=0,                            A(B+1)=0,             (8)
```

so `A=0,B=-1`. For `c=-A`, they instead give `B=-1,A=0`.
But `A=lambda^3=0` forces `lambda=0` and hence `B=r lambda=0`, a
contradiction in both charts. Thus `(6)` is never a square. In particular,
`B_r` is a domain and each vertical divisor is the sole reduced prime

```text
V_lambda=(t-lambda),                 div(t-lambda)=V_lambda. (9)
```

The affine singular locus is also uniform. The partial derivatives of the
hypersurface equation, after harmless scalar factors, are

```text
W,
q(2X-r t)+6X^2,
q(3t^2+rX).                                             (10)
```

On the surface, `X=0` forces `t=W=0`. Suppose instead that `X!=0`; then
`q!=0`. For `r=0`, equations `(10)` force `t=0,X=3`, but that point has
`H=-27` and is not on the surface. For `r!=0`, put

```text
X=-3t^2/r,                z=t/r^2,                a=2+9z.
```

The surface and `X`-derivative equations become

```text
r^3 a^2+108=0,
r^3 a(2a-1)+162=0.                                     (11)
```

Subtracting twice the first row from the second gives `r^3a=-54`; putting
this back in the first gives `a=2`, hence `z=t=X=0`, contrary to `X!=0`.
The origin is therefore the unique singular point. The hypersurface is
`S2` and regular in codimension one, so it is normal.

Localizing the Weil divisor sequence at `k[t]\{0}` now gives

```text
B_r^* -> Gamma(C_r,O)^* -> direct_sum_lambda Z[V_lambda]
      -> Cl(B_r) -> Pic(C_r) -> 0,                       (12)
```

where `C_r=Spec(B_r tensor_k[t] k(t))`. Every middle generator is already
principal by `(9)`, so

```text
Cl(B_r)=Pic(C_r).                                        (13)
```

## 2. The exact elliptic model and its deleted sections

Let `K=k(t)`. The quartic discriminant is

```text
-256 t^12[16t^3+8r^2t^2+(r^4+36r)t+r^3+27],             (14)
```

so the generic curve is smooth of genus one with two `K`-rational infinity
points. On a dense open, set

```text
z=(q+W)/(2X^2),
x=t z,
y=Xz(z+1)+(1-r t z)/2.                                  (15)
```

Then `(15)` lies on

```text
E_r: y^2=x^3+(t+r^2/4)x^2-(r/2)x+1/4.                   (16)
```

This is birational, not merely a rational map. An exact inverse is

```text
z=x/t,
X=t^2(2y-1+r x)/(2x(x+t)),
W=2X^2z-(t^3+r t X-X^2).                                (17)
```

Write the two quartic infinity branches using `epsilon=1/X` and
`eta=W/X^2`. Since

```text
eta^2=(-1+r t epsilon+t^3epsilon^2)^2-4epsilon,           (18)
```

the `eta=+1` branch maps to

```text
Q=(0,-1/2),                                               (19)
```

while the `eta=-1` branch maps to

```text
-2Q=(-t,-(1+r t)/2).                                    (20)
```

Thus the affine generic quartic is exactly the projective elliptic curve
with those two sections removed, and their difference is

```text
(-2Q)-Q=-3Q.                                             (21)
```

## 3. The elliptic surface is rigid even at its collision parameter

The Weierstrass invariants of `(16)` are

```text
b2=4t+r^2,                 b4=-r,       b6=1,       b8=t,
c4=(4t+r^2)^2+24r,
c6=-(4t+r^2)^3-36r(4t+r^2)-216,
Delta=-16t^3-8r^2t^2-(r^4+36r)t-r^3-27.                 (22)
```

As a cubic in `t`, the discriminant of `Delta` and its resultant with `c4`
are

```text
disc_t(Delta)=-256(2r^3+27)^3,
Res_t(Delta,c4)=4096(2r^3+27)^2.                         (23)
```

Hence, if `2r^3+27!=0`, there are three finite `I1` fibres. At the sole
collision value `2r^3+27=0`, put

```text
t0=r^2/12,                         t1=-2r^2/3.
```

Then

```text
Delta=-16(t-t0)^2(t-t1).                                (24)
```

At `t0`, both `c4,c6` vanish to order one and `Delta` to order two; this is
a type `II` fibre. At `t1`, `c4=-27r/2!=0`, so the fibre is `I1`. Thus the
finite ledger is always

```text
3I1,                         or                         II+I1. (25)
```

In particular it never adds a reducible root lattice.

At infinity, put `s=1/t`, `x_inf=s^2x`, and `y_inf=s^3y`. The integral
model is

```text
y_inf^2=x_inf^3+(s+r^2s^2/4)x_inf^2-(r/2)s^4x_inf+s^6/4. (26)
```

Uniformly,

```text
v_s(c4)=2,                  v_s(c6)=3,       v_s(Delta)=9. (27)
```

The model is minimal and the fibre is `I3*`, with root lattice `D7`. The
Euler invoice is twelve, so this is a rational elliptic surface with Picard
number ten. Shioda--Tate and `(25)` give

```text
rank E_r(K)=10-2-rank(D7)=1.                             (28)
```

The section `Q` in `(19)` has

```text
2Q=(-t,(1+r t)/2),
3Q=((1+r t)/t^2,
    (r^2t^2+3r t+t^3+2)/(2t^3)).                         (29)
```

In the infinity chart it is `(0,-s^3/2)`. Three successive point blowups
have exceptional equations

```text
y_1^2=0,                    y_2^2=0,       y_3^2=1/4,    (30)
```

so `Q` meets a terminal `D7` spin component. Its inverse-Cartan correction
is `7/4`, and `Q` is disjoint from the zero section. Therefore

```text
<Q,Q>=2-7/4=1/4.                                          (31)
```

There is no torsion. Torsion injects into the order-four component group of
`I3*`, so any nonzero torsion subgroup would contain two-torsion. A rational
two-torsion point would give a root in `k[t]` of

```text
x^3+(t+r^2/4)x^2-(r/2)x+1/4.                             (32)
```

Degree comparison leaves `x=-t+b`. The `t^2` coefficient gives
`b=-r^2/4`, the `t` coefficient then gives `r=0`, and the constant term is
still `1/4`, a contradiction. Finally, the trivial lattice `U+D7` has
absolute discriminant four and the rational surface's Neron--Severi lattice
is unimodular. Thus the Mordell--Weil determinant is `1/4`; `(31)` attains
it, proving

```text
E_r(K)=ZQ                                                     (33)
```

for every `r`, including the special fibre-collision row.

## 4. Class group, units, and the codimension-two torsor gap

Because the two deleted sections have difference `-3Q`, equations
`(13),(21),(33)` give

```text
Cl(B_r)=Pic(C_r)
       =E_r(K)/<3Q>
       =ZQ/<3Q>
       =Z/3.                                              (34)
```

Moreover,

```text
div(X)=D++D-,
div(q+W)=3D+,
div(q-W)=3D-.                                             (35)
```

The Cardano prime is genuinely nonzero in `(34)`. At the origin the
divisorial ideal `(X,q+W)` is reflexive: its quotient is `k[t]`, and the
depth lemma applies to the Cohen--Macaulay hypersurface `B_r`. But its two
generators have independent linear initials `X,W`, so it is not locally
principal. Thus `D+` is not Cartier. Since it generates the cyclic group in
`(34)`,

```text
Pic(S_r)=0,                        Pic(S_r^reg)=Cl(B_r)=Z/3. (36)
```

A unit on the generic affine curve can have divisor only on the two deleted
sections. Such a divisor would make a nonzero multiple of `3Q` principal,
contradicting the infinite order of `Q`. Hence `C_r^*=K^*`. A rational
function in `K^*` with zero valuation on every vertical prime `(9)` is a
scalar, proving

```text
B_r^*=k*.                                                 (37)
```

Since `k^*` is cube-divisible, Kummer theory and `(36)-(37)` give exactly
the two rows in `(4)`. The Cardano cover is quasi-etale over `S_r`: it is a
torsor on the regular locus but cannot extend etale across the singular
origin.

## 5. The only normal cubic completion is monogenic

The two inverse nonzero classes in `H^1_et(S_r^reg,mu_3)` are represented by
the two radicands in `(5)`, whose product is `X^3`. Thus they define the same
cyclic function-field extension. Quadratic descent has no further ambiguity:
the deck involution acts by inversion, every lift has square one, and all
lifts are conjugate by the `C3` action. Hence every normal generically `S3`
cubic completion with this exact branch and resolvent has the Cardano Galois
closure and the same cubic field.

It remains important that the displayed cubic ring really is the normal
integral closure for every parameter. Its monic cubic has no rational root.
Indeed a rational root is a polynomial root; a total-degree comparison makes
it affine-linear, and coefficient comparison gives no solution for any `r`.
Thus the cubic ring is a domain. Its partial derivatives are

```text
3(u^2-X),                    2X-3u-r t,       -3t^2-rX.   (38)
```

For `r!=0`, substituting `X=u^2` and `t=(2u^2-3u)/r` into the last row gives

```text
u^2[r^3+3(2u-3)^2]=0,                                   (39)
```

so the singular locus is finite. For `r=0`, the only extra derivative
candidate is `(t,u,X)=(0,3/2,9/4)`, which does not lie on the cubic surface.
The hypersurface is therefore `R1+S2`, hence normal. It is visibly monogenic,
being generated by `u` over `k[t,X]`.

This proves the uniform nonentry claim. There are two instructive parameter
walls: the normalization-address collision `r^3=-27` and the elliptic-fibre
collision `2r^3+27=0` are distinct. Neither changes `(34)`. The failed
counterexample mechanism is now exact: moving within the fold-three family
changes finite singular fibres but never creates a second global anti-invariant
three-class. A viable successor must change the global elliptic/root-lattice
or boundary-section invoice, not merely the linear-conic parameter.
