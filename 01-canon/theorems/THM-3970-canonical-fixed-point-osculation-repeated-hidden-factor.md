# THM-3970 -- hidden repetition is oriented second-order fixed-point contact

**PROVED + VERIFIED-EXACT + INDEPENDENTLY HOSTILE-AUDITED.** Work over an
algebraically closed field `k` of characteristic zero. Put

```text
A=k[P,t],                      B=k[h,t],
A -> B: P maps to h^2,
K=q(h^2,t)-2h^3,               q in A.                  (1)
```

Define the three canonical polynomials

```text
s=q_P/3,
J=P-s^2,
R=q-3sP+s^3.                                             (2)
```

They convert every repeated hidden factor into an exact target-plane
osculation packet.

## 1. The derivative canonically orients the hidden factor

Let `M(h,t)` be irreducible, not associated to `h`, and assume

```text
M^2 divides K.                                           (3)
```

Differentiating in `h` gives

```text
K_h=2h q_P(h^2,t)-6h^2=6h(s(h^2,t)-h).                  (4)
```

Since `(3)` implies `M|K_h` and `M` does not divide `h`, one obtains the
oriented fixed-point law

```text
M divides h-s(h^2,t).                                   (5)
```

This orientation matters. Reflection `iota:h maps to -h` sends `(5)` to
the opposite law on the reflected factor.

## 2. Contraction remembers the whole oriented curve

Write

```text
M^-(h,t)=M(-h,t)                                        (6)
```

up to a nonzero scalar. The factors `M` and `M^-` are distinct. Otherwise
reflection of `(3)` would make `M^2` divide both `K(h,t)` and `K(-h,t)`;
it would then divide their difference

```text
K(-h,t)-K(h,t)=4h^3,                                    (7)
```

forcing the irreducible `M` to be associated to `h`.

Let

```text
p=(M) intersection A=(C(P,t)),                          (8)
```

where `C` is irreducible. This contraction is a nonzero height-one prime:
the even norm `MM^-` is a nonzero element of it, and `B` is finite over
`A`. Moreover

```text
C(h^2,t) is associated to M(h,t)M^-(h,t).               (9)
```

Indeed `C(h^2,t)` is divisible by `M`, hence by its reflection `M^-`.
Conversely the even product `MM^-` lies in `(M) intersection A`, so it is
divisible by `C`. The two mutual divisibilities prove `(9)`.

The map of image curves loses no oriented information. In `B/(M)`, equation
`(5)` writes `h` as the image of the polynomial `s(P,t)`. Since `B/(M)` is
generated by `h,t`, the injective contraction map is also surjective:

```text
A/(C) isomorphic to B/(M).                              (10)
```

The reflected curve gives the opposite lift over the same target curve.

## 3. The exact second-order target invoice

The fixed-point polynomial satisfies

```text
J(h^2,t)=(h-s(h^2,t))(h+s(h^2,t)).                     (11)
```

Thus `(5)` and contraction give

```text
C divides J.                                            (12)
```

There is also the exact identity

```text
K=R(h^2,t)-(h-s(h^2,t))^2(2h+s(h^2,t)).                (13)
```

Equations `(3)`, `(5)`, and `(13)` imply `M^2|R(h^2,t)`.
Because `R(h^2,t)` is even, reflection gives `(M^-)^2|R(h^2,t)` as well.
The factors are coprime, so `(9)` gives

```text
C^2 divides R.                                          (14)
```

The descent in `(14)` is literal: the quotient by `C(h^2,t)^2` is fixed by
reflection, and the fixed subring of `B=A direct-sum hA` is exactly `A`.

Together `(12)` and `(14)` say that the target curve `C=0` is a fixed-point
curve for `s`, and `q` has second-order contact there with the cusp law
`3sP-s^3`.

## 4. Oriented converse and differential sidecar

Conversely, suppose an irreducible `C in A` satisfies

```text
J=CD,                         R=C^2c                    (15)
```

for `D,c in A`. Let `M!=h` be an irreducible factor of `C(h^2,t)` with the
orientation

```text
M divides h-s(h^2,t).                                   (16)
```

Then `M^2` divides both terms on the right of `(13)`, hence

```text
M^2 divides K.                                          (17)
```

Thus `(12),(14),(16)` are necessary and sufficient for the oriented
repeated factor. Without `(16)`, a factor may belong to the reflected
hidden polynomial instead; without `(14)`, fixed-point incidence alone is
only first-order contact.

Differentiating the canonical remainder directly gives

```text
R_P=-3s_PJ.                                             (18)
```

Substitution of `(15)` and cancellation of `C` freezes the exact osculation
sidecar

```text
2cC_P+c_PC=-3s_PD.                                      (19)
```

This row is a cheap necessary test for proposed packets. It is not by itself
sufficient for the divisibilities in `(15)`.

## 5. The second-order invoice collapses to one target gcd

The square in `(14)` does not need to be tested separately. First, an
irreducible factor `C` of `J` cannot be vertical. If `C` belonged to `k[t]`,
algebraic closure would give `C=t-a` after rescaling, and reduction modulo
`C` would assert the impossible polynomial identity

```text
P=s(P,a)^2  in k[P].                                  (20)
```

Thus `C_P` is nonzero and, by irreducibility in characteristic zero,
`gcd(C,C_P)=1`. If additionally `C|R`, write `R=Cu`. Identity `(18)` says
`C|R_P`; differentiating `R=Cu` and reducing modulo `C` gives

```text
C divides C_P u,
```

so `C|u` and hence `C^2|R`.

There is consequently an exact target-plane criterion:

```text
nonzero repeated irreducible factors M of K
  <----> non-P irreducible factors C of gcd(J,R).       (21)
```

The reverse arrow includes no factor-selection ambiguity. For such a `C`,
consider the homomorphism

```text
B -> A/(C),                h maps to s(P,t), t maps to t. (22)
```

This is well defined because `P=s^2` modulo `C`, and it is onto because
`A/(C)` is generated by `P=s^2` and `t`. Its kernel is a height-one prime in
the UFD `B`, hence is generated by a unique irreducible `M` up to a scalar.
This `M` is not `h` because `C` is not `P`; it has contraction `(C)`, divides
`h-s(h^2,t)`, and `(13)` gives `M^2|K`. (The smaller displayed ideal
`(C(h^2,t),h-s(h^2,t))` need not itself be prime; the kernel formulation is
essential.) The forward construction and `(10)` recover the same prime. Thus
`(21)` is a bijection, not merely an existence test. The excluded prime
`C=P` is the contraction of `M=h`; that zero branch is governed directly by
`q(0,t)=0` and is outside the criterion.

This gcd form is the cheapest exact screen for the entire monogenic hidden
repetition problem: compute `s,J,R` in the target ring and factor only
`gcd(J,R)/P^epsilon`, rather than factor the generally much deeper polynomial
`K(h,t)` and then inspect multiplicities.

## 6. Exact nongraph controls

Put

```text
rho=P+t,                         C0=P-rho^2,
q_sigma=3rho P-rho^3+sigma C0^2,       sigma in k.      (23)
```

Then

```text
q_sigma(h^2,t)-2h^3
 =(h-rho(h^2,t))^2
   [sigma(h+rho(h^2,t))^2-(2h+rho(h^2,t))].             (24)
```

The oriented factor

```text
M=h^2-h+t                                               (25)
```

is irreducible over `k(t)` because its discriminant is `1-4t`, and it is
genuinely nongraph over the original `t`-line. Its contraction is `C0`.

For comparison with the canonical rather than chosen fixed-point row, put

```text
A_sigma=1+(2sigma/3)(1-2rho).
```

Direct differentiation and division give

```text
s=rho+A_sigma C0,
J=C0(1-2rho A_sigma-A_sigma^2 C0),
R=C0^2(sigma-3A_sigma+3rho A_sigma^2+A_sigma^3 C0).     (26)
```

Thus the abstract contraction invoice is visible coefficient by
coefficient.

At `sigma=0`, the target cubic has degree three in `P` but fails the domain
gate exactly:

```text
F=(T+rho)(T^2-rho T+rho^2-3P).                         (27)
```

For every `sigma in k^*`, the original `q_sigma` has degree four in `P` and
the cubic is irreducible. The target shear `a0=P+t` exposes its normalization
as `k[a0,v]=k^[2]`:

```text
P=sigma v^3+3v^2+3a0v+a0^2,
t=-sigma v^3-3v^2-3a0v-a0^2+a0,
T=sigma v^2+3v+2a0.                                    (28)
```

The first row has degree three in `v`, proving the field degree over
`k(P,a0)=k(P,t)`. Its parameter is recovered generically as
`v=(P-a0^2)/(T+a0)`, so `T` generates that degree-three field. The displayed
monic cubic `F` is therefore the minimal polynomial of `T` and is
irreducible. Moreover, after division by `sigma in k^*`, the first row of
`(28)` is a monic equation for `v` over the cubic coordinate ring, while
`a0=P+t` already belongs to that ring. Hence `k[a0,v]` is finite and
birational over it; since the former is normal, it is the full
normalization. Its source-to-target Jacobian is, up to sign,

```text
3(sigma v^2+2v+a0).                                    (29)
```

This nonconstant principal ramification curve must be deleted by a Keller
open and would become a forbidden unit on `A2`. Hence nongraph repetition
can coexist with an irreducible cubic and even an affine-plane
normalization; the obstruction has moved from normalization debt to the
principal different.

## 7. Scope

The theorem replaces an arbitrary hidden-factor search by the target data

```text
(s,C,D,c),              J=CD,              R=C^2c,       (30)
```

plus an orientation choice in the quadratic reflection cover. It does not
classify which such packets have affine-plane normalizations, solve the
collision fibres left by THM-3969, treat nonmonogenic cubic orders, or prove
`JC(2)`.
