# THM-3847 -- two places are compatible with the branch, but not with the index

**PROVED + VERIFIED-EXACT + INDEPENDENTLY HOSTILE-AUDITED.**  Work over an
algebraically closed field `k` of
characteristic zero.  Fix `beta in k*`, put `R=k[A,C]`, and define

```text
p=3/2+AC,                         u=1+AC+A^2 beta.                 (1)
```

The depressed cubic

```text
F_beta(T)=T^3-2pT+2u                                               (2)
```

has discriminant

```text
disc_T(F_beta)=4A^2 Delta_beta,                                    (3)

Delta_beta=-27A^2 beta^2+8AC^3-54AC beta+9C^2-54 beta.             (4)
```

There is a normal integral finite-flat cubic `R`-algebra `S_beta` with the
following three simultaneous properties.

1. Its fraction field is generated by a root `t` of `(2)`, and its branch
   divisor is the irreducible curve `Delta_beta=0`.
2. The normalization of that branch curve is

   ```text
   W^2=-2C^2+9 beta,                                                (5)
   ```

   a smooth affine conic whose projective completion has exactly two points
   at infinity.  Thus the branch normalization is isomorphic to `G_m`, not
   `A1`.
3. The cubic order is globally monogenic.  More sharply, in a natural free
   basis `(1,t,z)` its binary index form is

   ```text
   I_beta(X,Y)=AX^3+3X^2Y-2CXY^2+2 beta Y^3,                        (6)
   ```

   and it represents `1` over `R`.

Let `pi_beta:Spec S_beta -> A2_(A,C)` be the finite map.  If `X` is any
nonempty affine open subscheme of `Spec S_beta` on which `pi_beta` is etale,
then

```text
Gamma(X,O_X)^* != k*.                                              (7)
```

In particular no such `X` is an affine plane.  The gain from the
three-puncture THM-3842 branch packet to two punctures therefore incurs a
different obstruction: the cubic index now represents a unit.

The assumption that `beta` is constant and nonzero is essential.  Replacing
it by a nonconstant profile is a useful design operation, but this theorem
does **not** assert a uniform puncture count, normality statement, or
constant-unit obstruction for that operation.

## 1. Exact cubic order and the trace shift

Define

```text
G_beta(Z)=Z^3-4CZ^2+(4C^2+6 beta)Z-4A beta^2-12 beta C             (8)
```

and set

```text
S_beta=R[Z]/(G_beta),                 z=class(Z),
t=(2Cz-z^2-4 beta)/(2 beta).                                         (9)
```

Because `beta` is a scalar unit, `(9)` is integral over `R`.  Direct
reduction modulo `(8)` gives

```text
t^2+t-2=Az,
tz=z+2Ct-2C-2A beta,
z^2=2Cz-2 beta t-4 beta,                                           (10)
```

and

```text
t^3-2pt+2u=0.                                                      (11)
```

The change from the power basis `(1,z,z^2)` to `(1,t,z)` has determinant
`1/(2 beta)`, hence `(1,t,z)` is another free `R`-basis.  Equations `(10)`
give its complete multiplication table.

The polynomial `(8)` is irreducible.  Indeed, specializing `C=0` gives

```text
Z^3+6 beta Z-4A beta^2.                                            (12)
```

A reducible cubic over `k(A)` would have a rational root `r(A)`.  It would
satisfy

```text
A=(r^3+6 beta r)/(4 beta^2),                                      (13)
```

which is impossible: if `r` is nonconstant, the degrees of the two rational
maps in `(13)` are respectively `1` and `3 deg(r)`; a constant `r` is also
impossible.  A monic factorization of `(8)` would specialize to a monic
factorization of `(12)`, so Gauss's lemma proves irreducibility over
`Frac(R)` and integrality of `S_beta`.

The exact discriminants in the two bases are

```text
disc(1,t,z)=4 Delta_beta,
disc_Z(G_beta)=16 beta^2 Delta_beta.                                (14)
```

Thus `(3)` is precisely the square-index passage from the depressed power
basis to the integral basis `(1,t,z)`.

## 2. The branch has exactly two places at infinity

Regard `(4)` as a quadratic in `A`.  Its leading coefficient and
discriminant are

```text
lc_A(Delta_beta)=-27 beta^2,
disc_A(Delta_beta)=-8(-2C^2+9 beta)^3.                              (15)
```

Put `L=-2C^2+9 beta`.  Since `beta!=0`, the quadratic `L` has two simple
roots, so it is not a square in `k(C)`.  Formula `(15)` is therefore not a
square in `k(C)`.  The quadratic `(4)` is irreducible over `k(C)`, and its
constant leading coefficient makes it primitive; hence `Delta_beta` is
irreducible in `k[A,C]`.

Choose `epsilon in k` with `epsilon^2=-8`.  The map from `(5)` to the branch
is

```text
A={8C^3-54 beta C-epsilon L W}/{54 beta^2}.                         (16)
```

Substituting `W^2=L` and `epsilon^2=-8` into `(4)` gives zero.  Both source
and target are finite of degree two over `A1_C`, and `(16)` identifies their
function fields.  The smooth conic `(5)` is therefore the finite
normalization of the branch.

Its projective completion is

```text
W^2+2C^2=9 beta Z_0^2.                                             (17)
```

At `Z_0=0` the equation `W^2+2C^2=0` has exactly two distinct projective
solutions.  No other point is removed by the affine chart.  Consequently
the branch normalization is `P1` minus two points, hence `G_m`.  This is a
real improvement over a three-puncture branch passport, but it is still not
a polynomial curve with normalization `A1`.

## 3. Normality of the finite cubic completion

The monic presentation `(8)` makes `S_beta` finite free of rank three, hence
a two-dimensional hypersurface and Cohen--Macaulay.  It satisfies `S2`.
Away from `Delta_beta=0`, `(14)` makes the map etale, so `S_beta` is regular
in codimension one there.

At the generic point of the sole branch divisor, the discriminant has
valuation one by irreducibility and squarefreeness of `Delta_beta`.  Over the
corresponding DVR, the discriminant-index formula for the order and its
integral closure reads

```text
ord(disc S_beta)=ord(disc Sbar_beta)+2 ord([Sbar_beta:S_beta]).     (18)
```

The left side is `1`; the index term is a nonnegative even integer.  It must
vanish.  Thus the order is integrally closed at every height-one prime over
the branch.  Serre's `R1+S2` criterion proves that `S_beta` is normal.

### 3.1 Independent source-plane and branch-pullback closure

There is a shorter global description.  Equation (8) is linear in `A` with
unit coefficient `-4 beta^2`, so solving it gives

```text
A={Z^3-4CZ^2+(4C^2+6 beta)Z-12 beta C}/{4 beta^2},       (18a)
S_beta isomorphic to k[C,Z].                              (18b)
```

Thus the completion is literally a smooth affine plane.  Its polynomial map
to `A2_(A,C)` has Jacobian

```text
partial A/partial Z=G_beta'(Z)/(4 beta^2).                (18c)
```

On this source plane the branch equation factors exactly as

```text
Delta_beta(A(C,Z),C)
 =-G_beta'(Z)^2(3Z^2-8CZ+24 beta)/(16 beta^2).             (18d)
```

The square is the ramified sheet and the last quadratic is its simple
companion.  If `rho^2=-2` and `U=W+rho C` on the normalization (5), then

```text
U(W-rho C)=9 beta.                                        (18e)
```

This is an explicit Laurent coordinate: the affine normalization is `G_m`
and its projective completion deletes exactly two points.  Formula (16) is
pointwise injective.  Away from `L=0`, `(A,C)` recovers `W`; at `L=0`, the
conic forces `W=0`.  Its two singular images are therefore cusps rather than
the finite self-identification required of a Russell arm by THM-3843.

Consequently the constant-profile construction fails two independent plane-
atlas gates.  Its different becomes the nonconstant unit (21) after deleting
ramification, while its `G_m` ramification normalization also triggers the
THM-3841 deleted-divisor/Jelonek obstruction.  A viable replacement must
repair both the unit/index debt and the polynomially-uniruled branch passport.

## 4. The index represents one

For `theta=Xt+Yz`, take the determinant of `(1,theta,theta^2)` in the basis
`(1,t,z)`.  The multiplication table `(10)` gives exactly `(6)`.  In
particular

```text
I_beta(0,1)=2 beta in k*.                                          (19)
```

Choose `lambda in k*` with `lambda^3=(2 beta)^(-1)`.  Then

```text
I_beta(0,lambda)=1,
S_beta=R[lambda z].                                                 (20)
```

Thus the two-place branch did not solve the cubic-completion problem: it
crossed to the forbidden monogenic side of the THM-3801 index gate.

This conclusion can also be seen without invoking that theorem.  The
relative different in the global presentation `(8)` is generated by

```text
G_beta'(z)=3z^2-8Cz+4C^2+6 beta.                                   (21)
```

On every open on which `pi_beta` is etale, `(21)` is invertible.  It is not
a scalar: if it were in `k`, then `(21)` would give a quadratic equation for
`z` over `Frac(R)`, contradicting the irreducible cubic `(8)`.  Hence every
affine etale open has the nonconstant unit `(21)`, proving `(7)`.  This is
the exact algebraic debt paired with the improved branch passport.

## 5. Why the cheap nonconstant profile also fails

The depressed-cubic identity `(3)`--`(4)` continues formally if `beta` is
replaced by a polynomial profile `b(C)`, and the index calculation `(6)`
continues after localizing at `b`.  The global integral order and the
decisive unit statement do not:

```text
I_b(0,1)=2b(C)                                                     (22)
```

need not be a unit.  At the same time the geometry of the branch can acquire
new components and new missing places.  For the cheap hostile profile

```text
b(C)=C(2C+1)/9,                                                    (23)
```

the quadratic radicand becomes the attractive linear polynomial

```text
-2C^2+9b(C)=C,                                                     (24)
```

but the branch itself factors as

```text
Delta_b=-(C/3)(4A^2C^3+4A^2C^2+A^2C
                    +12AC^2+18AC+9C+18).                          (25)
```

Thus the same tuning that lowers the visible radicand introduces an extra
branch component and makes the index value noninvertible.  Independently,
the residual factor in (25) has normalization `P1` minus three points: after
writing `C=V^2` and choosing `rho^2=-2`, its map is

```text
A(V)=-3(V+rho)/(2V(V+rho/2)^2),                            (25a)
```

with missing parameters `0,-rho/2,infinity`.  The later THM-3850 proves the
uniform statement that every nonconstant one-variable profile has at least
one nonpolynomial branch component.  The present example is its cheap hostile
control, not a remaining construction lane.

## 6. Exact replay and scope

The assertion-free companion verifies `(3)`--`(6)`, the normalization map,
the complete multiplication table and all 27 associativity triples, both
characteristic polynomials, both discriminants, the index identity, the
different, and the hostile profile `(23)`--`(25)`.  It has 55 active gates;
normal and optimized replay must byte-match the frozen transcript.
The independent companion starts instead from the source-plane map (18a),
proves (18b)--(18e), reconstructs the index form in the power basis, and
checks the three-puncture hostile (25a) in 54 active gates.  Normal,
optimized, and frozen outputs agree.

This theorem concerns one explicit normal cubic completion.  It does not
exclude nonmonogenic cubic completions, nonconstant-profile deformations,
higher-degree target changes, or arbitrary planar Keller maps.  In
particular it does not prove `JC(2)`.
