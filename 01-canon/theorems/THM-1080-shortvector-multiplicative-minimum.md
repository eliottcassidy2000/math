# THM-1080 — what controls the k-body terms

THM-1075 left δ(S) unbounded for |S| ≥ 3 and named short vectors as the
route. This file identifies the invariant, and finds that it has an
arithmetic meaning nobody had located.

## 1. The invariant is a MULTIPLICATIVE minimum

Since |ĥ(n)| ≤ 1/(π|n|) and ĥ vanishes on 7ℤ, the controlling quantity is

> **m₇(Λ) = min { ∏|nᵢ| : n ∈ Λ, all nᵢ ≠ 0, 7 ∤ nᵢ }**

At k=2 the lattice is generated by (b/g, −a/g), so m₇ = ab/g² exactly and
1/(π²m₇) = g²/(π²ab) — which is THM-1025's sawtooth floor g²/(4ab). The
invariant reproduces the known answer. At k=3:

| family | \|δ\| | m₇ | witness | 1/(π³m₇) | ratio |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| (2,3,5) | 0.019419 | 1 | (−1,−1,1) | 0.032252 | 0.60 |
| (11,13,17) | 0.005576 | 6 | (−2,3,−1) | 0.005375 | 1.04 |
| (31,37,41) | 0.000828 | 30 | (−2,5,−3) | 0.001075 | 0.77 |
| (101,103,107) | 0.003872 | 6 | (−2,3,−1) | 0.005375 | 0.72 |
| (6,10,15) | 0.000334 | 60 | — | 0.000268 | 1.25 |

Ratios lie in 0.45–1.93: **the right scale, within a factor of ~2**, not a
proved upper bound. Note it explains THM-1075's non-monotonicity —
(101,103,107) has larger δ than (31,37,41) because m₇ is 6 against 30.

min ∏|nᵢ| over a lattice is the **multiplicative minimum**: the quantity
of the Littlewood conjecture and of products of linear forms
(Cassels–Swinnerton-Dyer; Einsiedler–Katok–Lindenstrauss). Our lattices
are rational, so m₇ > 0 and is attained.

## 2. m₇ = 1 ⟺ SCHUR TRIPLE — the arithmetic meaning

∏|nᵢ| = 1 forces every nᵢ = ±1, so the relation is ±aᵢ ±aⱼ ±aₖ = 0:

> **m₇(a,b,c) = 1 ⟺ a + b = c** (a Schur triple / additive relation)

Verified 0/12. Consequence, over 300 random triples:

| class | median \|δ\| |
|---|---|
| Schur triples | 0.012390 |
| sum-free triples | 0.001149 |
| | **10.78× ratio** |

So **additive structure in the speed set is what drives the k-body
terms**: "hard family" = "additively rich family". This is Schur /
sum-free territory, and it retro-explains why the corpus kept finding
arithmetic progressions as the adversarial construction — APs are the
additively richest 13-sets.

## 3. Exact k-fold independence, a finite mod-7 check

ĥ kills any vector with a coordinate divisible by 7, hence

> **δ(S) = 0 exactly ⟺ every full-support n ∈ Λ(a_S) has some 7 | nᵢ**

a condition on Λ mod 7. Verified 0 mismatches at k=3 (e.g. (1,7,14) gives
δ = 0 exactly). At k=2 it reduces to S368's 7|(a/g) or 7|(b/g).

## 4. The AP lattice DECOUPLES — the extremal corner has a limit

For an AP aᵢ = a + id with gcd(a,d)=1, Σnᵢaᵢ = a·N₀ + d·N₁ with N₀ = Σnᵢ,
N₁ = Σ i·nᵢ; vanishing forces N₀ = ds, N₁ = −as. **The s = 0 part,
{Σnᵢ = 0, Σ i nᵢ = 0}, does not depend on a or d at all**, and s ≠ 0
needs |N₀| ≥ d. So as d grows δ converges to a universal constant per k —
verified U₃ = +0.007287 and U₄ = +0.003186, with residuals falling to
~2×10⁻⁴ by d = 21.

On the real object, exact rational 13-term AP measures converge:

| (a,d) | (1,21) | (1,34) | (1,55) | (1,89) | (3,34) | (7,55) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| uncovered | 0.12854 | 0.12656 | 0.12717 | 0.12640 | 0.12650 | 0.12634 |

**Limit ≈ 0.1264 > 0.** And the tight cases are classified: among
primitive APs with a,d ≤ 16 the ONLY one with uncovered = 0 is
**{1,…,13}**; every tight AP is a dilate k·{1,…,13}, exactly as dilation
invariance requires. So the additively-richest corner is, empirically,
lonely with a wide margin apart from the single classical extremal
family — though this is verified, not proved.

## Status

Everything above is measured, several parts exactly. What is **not**
proved: that 1/(π³m₇) is an upper bound (ratios exceed 1), the AP limit
in general, or the AP tightness classification beyond a,d ≤ 16. The value
of the section is that it converts "bound δ(S)" into two questions with
mature literatures — multiplicative minima of rational lattices, and
additive (Schur/sum-free) structure of the speed set.
