# THM-3187 -- central-sign parity quotient and odd-observable necessity

**PROVED + VERIFIED-EXACT + INDEPENDENTLY HOSTILE-AUDITED.**

THM-2889 constructs a faithful four-channel representation of the abstract
joint LRC carrier.  Its quaternionic center acts by `-I_4`, and the proposed
right-endpoint comparison has clutch `(13,13,-1)`.  The theorem there checks
that one particular Hermitianization loses this sign.  The loss is not an
accident of that construction: it is forced for every even-degree observable.

## 1. Central parity lemma

Let `K` be a field of characteristic different from two, let `V` be a
finite-dimensional `K`-vector space, and let `z` be a central involution in a
group acting linearly on `V`.  Assume

```text
rho(z)=-I_V.                                                 (1)
```

If `F:V->W` is a homogeneous polynomial map of degree `r`, then

```text
F(rho(z)v)=F(-v)=(-1)^r F(v).                               (2)
```

Consequently every even-degree `F` is constant on the pairs `{v,-v}` and
factors uniquely through the set-theoretic quotient

```text
V -> V/{+/-1}.                                               (3)
```

Every odd-degree `F` changes sign.  Oddness is only a sensitivity condition:
an odd observable can still vanish on the particular vector under study.

More generally, let `F(v_1,...,v_k)` be multihomogeneous of degrees
`r_1,...,r_k`.  If `z` co-shifts the inputs in `J subset {1,...,k}`, then

```text
F((rho(z)v_j)_(j in J),(v_j)_(j notin J))
 =(-1)^(sum_(j in J) r_j) F(v_1,...,v_k).                   (4)
```

### Proof

Every monomial of a degree-`r` homogeneous map acquires exactly `r` minus
signs under `v -> -v`, proving `(2)`.  Equation `(4)` applies the same count
separately in each input.  For even `r`, `(2)` makes the value constant on
every orbit of the central sign and therefore defines the unique quotient
map in `(3)`.  QED.

## 2. Tensor consequences

The central action on the standard degree-`r` functors is

```text
rho(z) on V^(tensor r), Sym^r(V), Lambda^r(V)
       =(-1)^r I.                                           (5)
```

In the four-dimensional THM-2889 carrier this gives

```text
V              : -I_4,
Sym^2(V)        : +I_10,
Lambda^2(V)     : +I_6,
Lambda^3(V)     : -I_4,
Lambda^4(V)     : +I_1.                                    (6)
```

The Hermitian carrier `V tensor conjugate(V)` also has central action `+I`.
Thus all rank-one Grams, Hermitian self-quadratic coordinates, jointly
co-shifted bilinear or sesquilinear polarizations, symmetric squares,
exterior squares, and the four-channel alternating determinant
`Lambda^4(V)` factor through `(3)`.  A polarization against a fixed second
input is deliberately excluded and is governed by `(7)` below.  Any algebra
generated by the listed even-degree observables remains even and is equally
blind.

For a sesquilinear pairing `B`, `(4)` separates two cases:

```text
B(-v,-w)= B(v,w),              jointly co-shifted;
B(-v, w)=-B(v,w),              fixed reference.             (7)
```

Therefore a nonzero pairing against a charged reference can retain the sign
only if that reference is not transported by the same central action.  This
is precisely the distinction between a co-moving Gram and the same-shell
reference service studied abstractly in THM-2420.  The lemma does not create
such a reference or prove its physical compatibility.

## 3. The THM-2889 right-endpoint clutch

Use THM-2889's faithful representation `rho:B->GL(V)` and write

```text
z=(0,0,-1),                    tau=(13,13,+1),
kappa=(13,13,-1)=z tau.                                  (8)
```

THM-2889 has already composed the right horn word and rewritten its group
element in the common comparison gauge as

```text
e8*e9=kappa*(direct endpoint group element).                (9)
```

After that group multiplication is complete, apply the ordinary left linear
representation.  If `v_dir` is the represented vector at the canonical
direct endpoint, the proposed via edge has the abstract endpoint

```text
v_via=rho(kappa)v_dir=-rho(tau)v_dir.                     (10)
```

For every homogeneous degree-`r` observable,

```text
F(v_via)=(-1)^r F(rho(tau)v_dir).                         (11)
```

Hence an even observable may still see the known torus displacement
`(13,13)`, but it cannot decide whether the torus-only clutch `tau` also
contains the central sign.  In particular the balanced Hermitian diagonal
of THM-2889 detects the torus weights while necessarily identifying
`kappa` with `tau`.  Taking more quadratic coordinates, their full Gram,
their four-channel alternating determinant, or any finite collection of
even-degree invariants cannot repair this loss.

Conversely, a nonzero odd observable changes sign in `(11)`.  Algebraically
one can always choose a linear functional nonzero on a specified nonzero
vector.  Physically, however, a canonical linear phase or a non-co-shifted
charged reference is exactly the datum that the LRC construction does not
yet provide.  THM-2410's positive local Gram cannot supply it, and THM-2420's
zero-reference hostile shows that an address and a self-Gram do not force a
reference amplitude.

## 4. Holotopy interpretation and cheapest live test

The central clutch is a path-comparison label.  Applying a homogeneous
degree-`r` functor sends that label to `(-1)^r`.  Even functors therefore kill
the clutch class; odd functors retain it.  This is the exact functorial
content of the holotopy language here.  It is not a claim that the feasible
or physical state space has a nontrivial topological loop.

The cheapest decisive physical test remains THM-2889's construction of

```text

```

on the same marked packet as `e8`.  This theorem sharpens the required
readout: after constructing that edge, test it with either

1. an odd charged current which is nonzero on the endpoint, or
2. a polarized pairing against a canonical charged reference which does not
   co-shift under `z`.

A static Gram or another even tensor is ruled out before any further finite
calculation.
