# THM-2020 — finite-place separation certificates for Gaussian moment channels

This theorem turns an algebraic equality over the complex numbers into an
exact carry computation at a suitable finite place. It is complementary to
the archimedean degree-gap and saddle arguments: no term is required to be
large in complex absolute value.

## 1. The fixed-support moment polynomial

Fix distinct monomials

\[
 S=\{(a_i,b_i):1\le i\le k\},\qquad
 P=\sum_{i=1}^k c_i Z^{a_i}\bar Z^{b_i},\qquad
 q_i=a_i-b_i.
\]

For \(m\ge1\), put

\[
 \mathcal R_m(S)=
 \left\{r\in\mathbb N^k:
   \sum_i r_i=m,\ 
   \sum_i q_i r_i=0
 \right\}.
\]

For \(r\in\mathcal R_m(S)\), charge balance gives the common radial exponent

\[
 A(r):=\sum_i a_i r_i=\sum_i b_i r_i.
\]

The exact Wick expansion is therefore

\[
 M_m(c):=\mathbb E[P^m]
 =\sum_{r\in\mathcal R_m(S)} C_m(r)c^r,
 \qquad
 C_m(r)=\frac{m!\,A(r)!}{\prod_i r_i!}\in\mathbb Z_{>0}.       \tag{1}
\]

The channel address is the full vector \(r\), not merely its charge relation
or its radial height. Distinct \(r\)'s give distinct coefficient monomials
\(c^r\).

## 2. Any fixed-support counterexample has algebraic coefficients

Let

\[
 I_S=(M_1,M_2,\ldots)\subset\mathbb Q[c_1,\ldots,c_k].
\]

### Algebraic-point reduction

> If \(V_{\mathbb C}(I_S)\cap(\mathbb C^*)^k\) is nonempty, then
> \(V_{\overline{\mathbb Q}}(I_S)\cap(\overline{\mathbb Q}^{\,*})^k\)
> is nonempty.

Indeed, the ascending chain

\[
 (M_1)\subset(M_1,M_2)\subset\cdots
\]

stabilizes because the polynomial ring is Noetherian. Thus \(I_S\) is
generated by a finite initial segment of the moments. Localize its coordinate
ring at \(c_1\cdots c_k\). A complex torus point makes this finitely generated
\(\mathbb Q\)-algebra nonzero. A maximal-ideal quotient is a field finitely
generated as a \(\mathbb Q\)-algebra, hence is a finite algebraic extension of
\(\mathbb Q\) by Zariski's lemma. Embedding that residue field into
\(\overline{\mathbb Q}\) gives the required algebraic torus point.

Consequently, to exclude a complex nullcone point on an exact support it is
enough to exclude algebraic coefficient vectors. This is a supportwise
reduction; it supplies no support-uniform moment bound.

## 3. The finite-place unique-minimum theorem

Let \(c=(c_1,\ldots,c_k)\in(K^*)^k\), where \(K\) is a number field. Outside
finitely many rational primes, every \(c_i\) is a unit at every prime of \(K\)
above that rational prime.

For a rational prime \(p\), call \((p,m,r_*)\) a **finite-place separation
certificate** for \(S\) if \(r_*\in\mathcal R_m(S)\) and

\[
 v_p(C_m(r_*))<v_p(C_m(r))\qquad
 (r\in\mathcal R_m(S),\ r\ne r_*).                 \tag{2}
\]

> **Finite-place separation theorem.** If a fixed support \(S\) has
> certificates (2) for infinitely many distinct rational primes \(p\), then
>
> \[
> V_{\mathbb C}(I_S)\cap(\mathbb C^*)^k=\varnothing.             \tag{3}
> \]

### Proof

Assume that the torus null locus is nonempty and use the preceding reduction
to choose a point \(c\in(K^*)^k\). Choose one certificate prime \(p\) outside
the finite set at which some \(c_i\) is not a unit, and choose a prime
\(\mathfrak p\mid p\) of \(K\). On rational numbers,
\(v_{\mathfrak p}\) is a positive constant multiple of \(v_p\), while
\(v_{\mathfrak p}(c^r)=0\). Hence the \(r_*\)-term in (1) has strictly smaller
\(\mathfrak p\)-adic valuation than every other term. The ultrametric
inequality says that a finite sum with a unique least-valuation term has the
same valuation as that term and cannot be zero. Thus \(M_m(c)\ne0\), a
contradiction. ∎

This is the exact finite-place analogue of exposing one Newton face, but its
observable is divisibility rather than complex magnitude.

## 4. Legendre turns the certificate into a carry computation

Write \(s_p(n)\) for the sum of the base-\(p\) digits of \(n\). Legendre's
formula gives, exactly,

\[
\begin{aligned}
 v_p(C_m(r))
 &=v_p(m!)+v_p(A(r)!)-\sum_i v_p(r_i!)\\
 &=\frac{A(r)-s_p(m)-s_p(A(r))+\sum_i s_p(r_i)}{p-1}. \tag{4}
\end{aligned}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
 v_p(C_m(r))
 =v_p(A(r)!)+
   \frac{\sum_i s_p(r_i)-s_p(m)}{p-1}.              \tag{5}
\]

The second summand is the base-\(p\) carry count for the multinomial addition
\(\sum_i r_i=m\). Thus the proof-bearing finite-state address is

```text
(balanced channel r, radial height A(r), base-p digits, carry state),
```

not a scalar charge or support count. This is a literal, rigorous transfer
of the repository's automatic/carry warning: discarding the automaton state
discards the valuation certificate.

## 5. A support-level corollary: a wide isolated lowest channel

The following elementary condition supplies certificates for every sufficiently
large prime.

> **Wide-gap corollary.** Suppose there are \(r^{(0)}\in\mathbb N^k\), integers
> \(R=|r^{(0)}|\), \(A_0=A(r^{(0)})\), and \(\Delta\ge A_0\), with \(A_0>R\), such
> that for every \(n\ge1\):
>
> 1. \(nr^{(0)}\) is the unique minimum-height member of
>    \(\mathcal R_{nR}(S)\);
> 2. every other \(r\in\mathcal R_{nR}(S)\) satisfies
>    \(A(r)\ge nA_0+\Delta\).
>
> Then \(S\) satisfies (3).

For a sufficiently large prime \(p\), take

\[
 n=\left\lfloor\frac{p-1}{A_0}\right\rfloor.
\]

Then

\[
 nR<nA_0<p\le(n+1)A_0\le nA_0+\Delta.              \tag{6}
\]

All factorials \(m!=(nR)!\) and \(r_i!\) are \(p\)-units. The minimum
channel also has \(A(nr^{(0)})<p\), so its radial factorial is a \(p\)-unit.
Every other channel has \(A(r)\ge p\), so \(A(r)!\) is divisible by \(p\).
The minimum channel is therefore uniquely least, for every sufficiently large
prime. The finite-place theorem applies.

The condition is sufficient, not claimed necessary. Its large fixed jump
\(\Delta\) is exactly what avoids an unproved assertion about primes in
bounded intervals.

## 6. The canonical first-return collision dies at the next rung

Consider

\[
 P=aZ^6+b\bar Z^2+c\bar Z^{18}.                    \tag{7}
\]

At moment \(m=4n\), write the occupation vector as \((x,y,z)\). Charge balance
and length give

\[
 6x-2y-18z=0,\qquad x+y+z=4n,
\]

and hence

\[
 (x,y,z)=(n+2z,\,3n-3z,\,z),\qquad 0\le z\le n.    \tag{8}
\]

The radial heights are

\[
 A=6x=6n+12z.                                      \tag{9}
\]

Thus \(r^{(0)}=(1,3,0)\), \(R=4\), \(A_0=6\), and the next-channel gap is
\(\Delta=12\). The wide-gap corollary applies. Therefore (7) is never in the
nullcone when \(a\ne0\) and at least one of \(b,c\) is nonzero. Including the
degenerate one-sided cases, NC2 holds on the whole three-monomial family (7).

This closes the exact support used in MISTAKE-211 without pretending its two
first-return atoms are separately zero.

There is also a short local certificate. The fourth moment is

\[
 M_4=4\,6!\,ab^3+4\,18!\,a^3c.                    \tag{10}
\]

For \(abc\ne0\), cancellation in (10) forces

\[
 c=-\frac{6!}{18!}\frac{b^3}{a^2}.                 \tag{11}
\]

The three eighth-moment channels \(z=0,1,2\), after extracting the common
factor \(a^2b^6\) and substituting (11), are

\[
\begin{array}{c|r|c}
z&\text{channel coefficient}&v_3\\ \hline
0&13\,412\,044\,800&5\\
1&-19\,536\,878\,592\,000&4\\
2&131\,727\,403\,906\,560\,000&5.
\end{array}                                                     \tag{12}
\]

The middle term is uniquely least 3-adically, so the sum cannot vanish. In
fact

\[
 M_8=131\,707\,880\,440\,012\,800\,a^2b^6\ne0.    \tag{13}
\]

This is an exact counterweight to first-return isolation: the atoms may cancel
at their first common level, but a finite-place address can separate their
higher descendants.

## 7. Transfer audit: what does and does not carry over

### A genuine second tool, conditional on an asymptotic input

THM-1635's surviving Wiener--Parseval stage is exact: if

\[
 S_m=\sum_{j=1}^J A_j e^{im\theta_j}+o(1)\longrightarrow0
\]

with distinct \(\theta_j\pmod{2\pi}\), then Cesàro averaging of \(|S_m|^2\)
gives \(\sum_j|A_j|^2=0\). This would rigorously separate a finite packet of
distinct central-resonance saddle phases. For NC2 the missing step is the
uniform finite saddle expansion with an \(o(1)\) remainder; the analogy does
not supply that expansion.

### Lee--Yang/Asano does not apply to raw moment polynomials

Already for \(P=aZ+b+c\bar Z\),

\[
 M_2=b^2+2ac.
\]

Take

\[
 a=1+i,\qquad b=-1+i,\qquad c=(1+i)/2.
\]

All three variables lie strictly in the upper half-plane, yet
\(b^2+2ac=-2i+2i=0\). Hence even this raw Gaussian moment polynomial is not
upper-half-plane stable. A Lee--Yang zero-free region or Asano contraction
cannot be imported without first constructing a different stable object.

### The tournament involution is a determinant tool, not a channel pairing

The Vandermonde tournament involution works because the paired objects have
equal magnitude and opposite clean signs. In (10), the two channel weights
are \(4\,6!\) and \(4\,18!\), and arbitrary coefficient magnitudes and phases
are load-bearing. No score-preserving sign reversal pairs them. Tournament
expansions remain useful after a determinant or resultant has been constructed;
they do not justify termwise cancellation or noncancellation in (1).

## 8. Tournament Analysis and assumption challenge

For the exact finite-place computation, the tournament vertices are balanced
channels \(r\), not runners or charges. At a fixed \(p\), orient \(r\to r'\)
when \(v_p(C_m(r))<v_p(C_m(r'))\); break valuation ties lexicographically. The
tie Hamiltonian path is the lexicographic channel order. This is a transitive
tournament: zero directed cycles, singleton SCCs, and one Hamiltonian path. In
the eighth-moment certificate its path is `middle -> low -> high`, reflecting
the valuations `(4,5,5)` after tie-breaking.

Candidate vertices challenged were monomials, charges, primitive circuits,
radial heights, balanced channels, rational primes, prime ideals, digit/carry
states, and proof obligations. The selected channel-at-a-place quotient
preserves the ultrametric noncancellation predicate. It destroys complex
phase and archimedean size, which is harmless only when a unique valuation
minimum exists.

## 9. Exact boundary of the theorem

What is now proved is a reusable certificate theorem and one nontrivial
cross-atom family. A full NC2 proof would follow from the still-open statement

> every finite two-sided exact support admits finite-place separation
> certificates for infinitely many rational primes,

or from a hybrid saying that supports without such certificates fall into the
affine-height/Sheffer/Watson strata handled by the other routes. Legendre's
formula makes that residual a precise carry-minimization problem, but it does
not solve it automatically. In particular, a finite list of certificate
primes is insufficient because an algebraic coefficient vector can be
non-unit at every prime in that list.

The companion script verifies (1), (4), (8)--(13), the all-large-prime
wide-gap certificates, the Lee--Yang counterexample, and the channel
tournament fingerprint by exact integer/rational arithmetic.
