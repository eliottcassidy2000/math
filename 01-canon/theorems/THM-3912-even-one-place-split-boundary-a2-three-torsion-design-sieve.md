# THM-3912 -- the split boundary prices cusp three-torsion

**RESERVED / PROVISIONAL PROOF CANDIDATE + VERIFIED-EXACT; awaiting
independent hostile audit.**

Work over an algebraically closed field `k` of characteristic zero.  Let
`Gamma` be a reduced irreducible plane curve of even degree

```text
d=2m>=2.                                                     (1)
```

Assume that a line `H_infinity` meets `Gamma` at one point `P`, that
`Gamma` is smooth at `P`, and that the total contact there is `d`.  Equivalently,
after target coordinates and scalar normalization, the degree-`d` form of
an affine equation `delta` is

```text
delta_d=lambda L^(2m),                     lambda in k*.    (2)
```

Let `X` be the projective quadratic double plane `W^2=delta_h`, and let
`B_+,B_-` be the two curves above `H_infinity`.  Then

```text
B_+^2=B_-^2=1-m,                   B_+ B_-=m,               (3)
```

so their boundary lattice is

```text
K_d = [ 1-m   m ],             det K_d=1-d,
      [  m   1-m]             Smith(K_d)=diag(1,d-1).      (4)
```

These identities survive any sequence of blowups supported on the removed
boundary: when every new exceptional curve is also removed, the enlarged
lattice is integrally congruent to the old lattice plus copies of `[-1]`.

There is an exact class-group sieve.  Let `Q` be a normal integral affine
surface and let `Y -> Q` be a smooth projective resolution/compactification
such that

```text
Y minus D = Q_reg,                                          (5)
```

where every irreducible component of `D` is included.  Let `R` be the
sublattice of `Pic(Y)` generated by these components.  Assume:

1. `Pic(Y)[3]=0`;
2. `R` is nondegenerate and, up to unimodular `[-1]` summands from boundary
   blowups, has the orthogonal decomposition

   ```text
   R = K_d orthogonal_sum A2(-)^r orthogonal_sum R_0;       (6)
   ```

3. `3` does not divide `det R_0`.

Here `A2(-)` denotes the negative root lattice with matrix

```text
[-2  1].                                                    (7)
[ 1 -2]
```

Then the following are proved **no-three-torsion** rows:

```text
r<=2 and 3 does not divide d-1;
d=4  (mod 18) and r<=1;
d=16 (mod 18) and r=0.                                    (8)
```

In every row in `(8)`,

```text
Cl(Q)[3]=0.                                                 (9)
```

The first line is the coarse design rule: with at most two affine `A2`
blocks and no other three-primary removed block, degree `d` must satisfy

```text
d=1 (mod 3)                                                 (10)
```

before the boundary lattice even permits a cubic Kummer layer.

The exact complementary permission table for `r<=2` is:

| `d mod 18` | boundary order-three square | least `r` permitted |
|---:|---:|---:|
| `4` | `1/3` | `2` |
| `10` | `0` | `0` |
| `16` | `2/3` | `1` |

"Permitted" here means only that the abstract discriminant lattice has an
integrally isotropic order-three class.  It does **not** assert that `R` is
actually nonprimitive in `Pic(Y)`, that `Cl(Q)[3]` is nonzero, or that a
cyclic cover, binary cubic, or Keller map exists.

The cutoff at two `A2` blocks is sharp for this lattice method.  Three
orthogonal `A2(-)` blocks have an intrinsic diagonal order-three class of
integral square `-2`; adjoining it gives an honest integral index-three
overlattice without using the boundary.  Thus no theorem based only on the
block determinants can exclude three cusps.

## 1. Local intersection of the two infinity branches

Choose completed coordinates `x,z` at `P` with `H_infinity=(z=0)` and
`x=L`.  Smoothness of `Gamma` at `P` and contact order `2m` give

```text
delta_h=lambda x^(2m)+z u(x,z),             u(0,0)!=0.     (11)
```

Choose `mu^2=lambda`.  In the completed local double plane, the two boundary
curves have ideals

```text
B_+ : (z,W-mu x^m),                 B_- : (z,W+mu x^m).   (12)
```

The double plane is smooth at this point because `u(0,0)!=0`.  Their local
intersection is

```text
length k[[x]]/(2mu x^m)=m.                                 (13)
```

This proves `B_+B_-=m`.  If `pi:X->P2` is the double projection, then

```text
B_++B_-=pi^*H_infinity,
(B_++B_-)^2=2H_infinity^2=2.                               (14)
```

The deck involution exchanges `B_+` and `B_-`, so their self-intersections
are equal.  Equations `(13),(14)` give `(3)`.

The eigenvectors `(1,1)` and `(1,-1)` of `(4)` have eigenvalues `1` and
`1-d`.  Since `gcd(m,m-1)=1`, the Smith form is exactly
`diag(1,d-1)`, not merely a determinant calculation.

The smoothness clause in `(11)` is load-bearing.  A square leading form with
a singular infinity branch can make the double plane singular and add a
three-primary exceptional block.  The theorem does not apply in that case.

## 2. Why boundary blowups do not change the tariff

For example, blow up the intersection of two removed curves with old Gram
matrix

```text
[s t].                                                       (15)
[t s]
```

In the basis of their strict transforms and the exceptional curve, the new
matrix is

```text
[s-1 t-1  1]
[t-1 s-1  1].                                               (16)
[ 1   1  -1]
```

Replacing each strict transform by its total transform is an integral basis
change of determinant one and turns `(16)` into

```text
[s t  0]
[t s  0].                                                   (17)
[0 0 -1]
```

The same argument works at a smooth point of one component and, with the
appropriate integral multiplicities, at every infinitely near boundary
point.  Hence log resolution multiplies the determinant only by signs and
leaves the discriminant group and its three-primary self-pairing unchanged.

## 3. From class-group torsion to an isotropic discriminant class

The divisor sequence for `(5)` gives

```text
Cl(Q)=Pic(Q_reg)=Pic(Y)/R.                                  (18)
```

Suppose a nonzero class of order three is represented by `x in Pic(Y)`.
Then

```text
3x=y in R,                         y notin 3R,              (19)
```

where the second clause uses `Pic(Y)[3]=0`.  In `R tensor Q`, the vector
`y/3=x` pairs integrally with `R` and has integral self-intersection, because
`Pic(Y)` is an integral intersection lattice.  Thus `y/3` gives a nonzero
order-three class in

```text
A_R=R^*/R                                                   (20)
```

whose self-pairing is zero in `Q/Z`.  Therefore absence of such an
integrally isotropic discriminant class implies `(9)`.  No full-rank or
unimodularity assumption on `Pic(Y)` is needed; the stated `Pic(Y)[3]=0` and
the actual quotient `(18)` are the relevant invoices.

## 4. The A2 anisotropy threshold

For one negative `A2` block with simple roots `E_1,E_2`, put

```text
g=(E_1+2E_2)/3.                                             (21)
```

Then

```text
g E_1=0,                  g E_2=-1,                  g^2=-2/3.
                                                                    (22)
```

Thus `A_A2=Z/3`, and its nonzero elements have self-pairing

```text
-2/3 = 1/3                    in Q/Z.                      (23)
```

In `r` orthogonal blocks, an order-three vector supported on `k` nonzero
coordinates has square `k/3` modulo `Z`.  It is isotropic exactly when

```text
k=0 (mod 3).                                                 (24)
```

This proves anisotropy for one or two blocks.  For three blocks,

```text
g_1+g_2+g_3
```

has integral pairings and square `-2`, so it generates an actual integral
index-three overlattice.  This is a constructed hostile, not just a failure
of the proof.

## 5. The boundary order-three class and the mod-18 refinement

Put `n=d-1=2m-1`.  A generator of the cyclic boundary discriminant group is

```text
eta=((m-1)B_+ + mB_-)/n,                    eta^2=(m-1)/n. (25)
```

If `3` does not divide `n`, the boundary has no three-primary class, and
`(24)` proves the first row of `(8)`.

If `3|n`, the unique order-three subgroup is generated by

```text
xi=(n/3)eta,                         xi^2=n(m-1)/9.         (26)
```

Because `d` is even, the three possible residues are

```text
d=4  (mod 18)  ==> xi^2=1/3 (mod Z),
d=10 (mod 18)  ==> xi^2=0   (mod Z),
d=16 (mod 18)  ==> xi^2=2/3 (mod Z).                       (27)
```

Adding `k` nonzero `A2` coordinates adds `k/3` to `(27)`.  Hence the least
permitted values of `r` are respectively `2,0,1`, proving the table and the
remaining rows of `(8)`.

Equivalently, write `n=3s` and let `c in {0,+1,-1}` record the boundary
order-three coordinate.  If `k` is the number of nonzero `A2` coordinates,
the complete square-integrality test is

```text
s c^2+k=0 (mod 3).                                         (27a)
```

When `3` does not divide `n`, the boundary coordinate is unavailable and
the same test reads `k=0 (mod 3)`.  This coordinate form makes both hostile
boundaries visible: a boundary radical can cancel one or two cusp radicals
according to `s mod 3`, while three cusp radicals cancel intrinsically even
when the boundary block is nonsingular modulo three.

Any nontrivial three-primary isotropic subgroup contains an isotropic
order-three element.  Therefore `(27)` remains decisive even when a higher
power of three divides `d-1`.

## 6. Units, cubic torsors, and the degree-six check

Under the theorem's nondegeneracy hypothesis, a rational function invertible
on `Q_reg` has divisor supported on `D`.  A principal divisor intersects
every component trivially; the nondegenerate Gram matrix of `R` forces every
coefficient to vanish.  Projectivity and integrality of `Y` then give

```text
Gamma(Q_reg,O)^*=k*.                                        (28)
```

In a no-three-torsion row, the Kummer sequence and algebraic closedness of
`k` yield

```text
H^1(Q_reg,mu_3)=0.                                          (29)
```

Consequently such a quadratic resolvent cannot carry the connected
codimension-one-unramified `C3` layer of a normal irreducible `S3` cubic
whose branch divisor has generic exponent one.  This is a cubic-cover
obstruction, not a Keller realization theorem.

For `d=6`, the boundary matrix is

```text
[-2  3]
[ 3 -2],                       det=-5.                      (30)
```

Two `A2` blocks are anisotropic because `3` does not divide `5`.  This is
the general lattice mechanism used by the sharp sextic in THM-3911; that
theorem separately verifies its complete singularity packet, rational
resolution, units, and cubic obstruction.  Nothing here duplicates those
curve-specific checks.

## 7. Scope and replay

This theorem is conditional on the **actual** removed-divisor decomposition
`(6)` and on `Pic(Y)[3]=0`.  It does not apply when:

- the infinity branch is singular;
- an omitted exceptional or boundary block has determinant divisible by
  three;
- the displayed blocks meet and the claimed orthogonal decomposition fails;
- `Pic(Y)` itself has three-torsion; or
- an isotropic class is merely permitted by the table but has not been shown
  to lie in the saturation inside `Pic(Y)`.

It proves no existence assertion in the permission rows and no planar
Jacobian counterexample.  Its output is an exact design sieve for future
one-place discriminants.
