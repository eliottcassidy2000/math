# THM-2685 -- the quartic kernel is an even divisor code

**PROVED + VERIFIED-EXACT + INDEPENDENTLY HOSTILE-AUDITED.**

THM-2681 shows that a generic `V4` Kummer plane can be perfectly real and
still fail when its missing root divisors return.  The general mechanism is
not peculiar to that cubic.  It is the codimension-one residue map for
squareclasses.  This theorem makes that map explicit, identifies the exact
quartic code it carries, and separates the unit and class-group completions.

## 1. Divisor residues of a Kummer packet

Let `X` be a normal integral affine complex variety with function field `K`.
For every prime divisor `E` of `X`, define

```text
partial_E: K^*/K^(*)2 -> F_2,
partial_E([f])=v_E(f) mod 2.                              (1)
```

This is well defined.  Put

```text
partial=direct_sum_E partial_E.                           (2)
```

Every rational function has only finitely many nonzero valuations, so `(2)`
lands in the direct sum.  Let `W` be an `r`-dimensional subspace of
`K^*/K^(*)2`, choose representatives `f_1,...,f_r`, and put

```text
L=K(sqrt(f_1),...,sqrt(f_r)).                             (3)
```

Then `[L:K]=2^r`; let `Y` be the normalization of `X` in `L`.  The following
conditions are equivalent:

```text
Y -> X is quasi-etale;
partial_E|_W=0 for every prime divisor E;
W is contained in ker(partial).                          (4)
```

Indeed, at the DVR `O_(X,E)` write `f=pi^e u`.  Multiplying by a square makes
`e` equal to `0` or `1`.  If every parity is zero, the local extension is
generated by square roots of units and is etale because `2` is invertible.
If one parity functional is nonzero, the corresponding quadratic
subextension contains `sqrt(pi u)` and is tamely ramified of index two.
Normality reduces quasi-etaleness to precisely these codimension-one tests,
proving `(4)`.

Choose a basis of `W` and make the binary boundary matrix `P` whose nonzero
rows are the functionals `partial_E|_W`.  Let `I` be the subgroup of
`Gal(L/K)=W^*` generated by all codimension-one inertia groups.  Then

```text
unramified character space =ker(P),
I=rowspan(P) subset W^*,
rank Gal(L^I/K)=r-rank(P).                              (4a)
```

The first identity is `(4)` applied character by character.  A nonzero row
is the tame inertia generator in the dual Kummer group; all inertia is
generated by these rows, proving the other two identities.  The intermediate
cover with function field `L^I` is the maximal quotient of `Y -> X` that is
unramified in codimension one.  Thus `(4a)` is basis independent and
quantifies the maximal survivor, not only pass/fail.

## 2. The exact unit/class-group completion

Write

```text
K_unr=ker(partial) subset K^*/K^(*)2.                    (5)
```

There is a natural exact sequence

```text
0 -> O(X)^*/O(X)^(*)2 -> K_unr
  -> Cl(X)[2] -> 0,                                     (6)

[f] |-> [ (1/2) div(f) ].                               (7)
```

For `[f]` in `(5)`, all coefficients of `div(f)` are even, so `(7)` is
defined and twice its image is principal.  Conversely, if `2D=div(f)`, then
`[f]` lies in `(5)`, proving surjectivity.  The image in `(7)` is zero exactly
when `(1/2)div(f)=div(g)`; then `f/g^2` has zero divisor and is a global unit
on normal affine `X`.  This proves exactness.

Now let a finite group `Q` act on `X`.  It permutes prime divisors and

```text
partial_(qE)(q[f])=partial_E([f]),                       (8)
```

so `(2)` and `(6)` are `Q`-equivariant.  If `W` is an irreducible
`F_2[Q]`-module, then

```text
W intersect K_unr is either 0 or W.                      (9)
```

When the Kummer cover is quasi-etale, restrict `(6)` to `W`.  Its unit
kernel is again a `Q`-submodule.  Therefore exactly one of the following
holds:

```text
W lies in the global-unit squareclasses; or
W embeds Q-equivariantly in Cl(X)[2].                    (10)
```

For `Q=C3` or `S3`, the standard plane `W=F_2^2` is irreducible: its three
nonzero vectors are `P^1(F_2)`, `C3` cycles them, and
`Aut(W)=GL_2(F_2)=S3`.  Thus `(9)`--`(10)` are genuinely all-or-nothing.

## 3. The canonical quartic parity code

Let

```text
F(T)=T^4+pT^2+qT+r                                      (11)
```

be a separable depressed quartic over a characteristic-zero field, with
transitive group `A4` or `S4`.  Its three squared pair sums are the roots
`u_1,u_2,u_3` of

```text
S(U)=U^3+2pU^2+(p^2-4r)U-q^2.                           (12)
```

Let `E` be the splitting field of `(12)` and `R` a `Q`-stable normal affine
model with `Frac(R)=E`.  The matching group is `Q=C3` or `S3`, and the full quartic
splitting field is

```text
M=E(sqrt(u_1),sqrt(u_2)),
[u_1]+[u_2]+[u_3]=0.                                   (13)
```

For every prime divisor `D` of `R`, form

```text
nu_D=(v_D(u_1),v_D(u_2),v_D(u_3)) mod 2.                (14)
```

Since `u_1u_2u_3=q^2`,

```text
nu_D belongs to {000,110,101,011},                      (15)
```

the even-weight `[3,2,2]` code.  These are exactly the values of the parity
functional on the three nonzero characters of the standard plane.  A
nonzero word in `(15)` is the unique nontrivial tame `V4` inertia label at
`D`; `000` means unramified there.

This is precisely the inertia invisible to discriminant-only tests.  A
weight-two word is a double sign flip, hence a double transposition in `V4`.
It fixes all three matching roots and has permutation index two in the
quartic action, hence an even contribution to the quartic discriminant
squareclass.  Thus neither the resolvent ramification nor the discriminant
squareclass detects it, while `(14)` does.

For a quartic Keller map in the scope of THM-2655, now take `R` specifically
to be the normalization of the affine Keller target in `E=M^V4`.  THM-2655
says that its normalization in `M` is quasi-etale.  Equations `(4)` and
`(15)` therefore force

```text
nu_D=000 for every prime divisor D.                      (16)
```

Equivalently, there are integral Weil divisors `C_i` with

```text
div(u_i)=2C_i,
C_1+C_2+C_3=div(q).                                     (17)
```

Their classes either give the three nonzero points of a standard plane in
`Cl(R)[2]`, or the whole plane comes from global-unit squareclasses, exactly
as in `(10)`.  This is the direct divisor test for any new `A4/S4` resolvent
candidate; its discriminant alone does not see `(14)`.

## 4. THM-2681 is the full-rank negative boundary

On THM-2681's ordered-root normalization

```text
B'=C[c^+-1,r_1,r_2,r_3]/(r_1+r_2+r_3-1),                (18)
```

the all-root-unit chart carries the standard plane

```text
w_1=[r_1/r_3],             w_2=[r_2/r_3].               (19)
```

Restore the prime divisors `D_i=(r_i=0)`.  Their parity functionals on the
basis `(19)` are

```text
partial_(D_1)=(1,0),
partial_(D_2)=(0,1),
partial_(D_3)=(1,1).                                    (20)
```

The residue matrix has rank two.  Hence

```text
W intersect ker(partial)=0,                             (21)
```

and the local connected `V4` torsor cannot extend quasi-etale.  Changing a
basis in `W` or multiplying representatives by squares preserves the rank in
`(20)`.  Thus `(21)` is the coordinate-free content of THM-2681's three
divisor calculations.

## 5. Both completion branches and the base-change hostile are sharp

The quotient model

```text
X=Spec C[a,b,c,d]/(d^2-abc)                              (22)
```

has `div(a)=2P_a`, `div(b)=2P_b`, and `div(c)=2P_c`.
Adjoining `sqrt(a),sqrt(b)` gives the standard connected `V4` cover; its
nontrivial fixed loci have codimension two, so the cover is quasi-etale.
Here the plane is carried by `Cl(X)[2]`, not by global units.  This is the
sharp positive class-group branch already underlying THM-2655.

The obstruction is not stable under arbitrary base change.  On

```text
X_0=Spec C[t,a^+-1,b^+-1],
(u_1,u_2,u_3)=(ta,tb,ab),        q=tab,                  (23)
```

the divisor `t=0` has word `110`, so the cover ramifies.  After the even
ramified base change `t=s^2`, the same squareclasses become

```text
[u_1]=[a],       [u_2]=[b],       [u_3]=[ab],            (24)
```

and form an etale unit plane.  More generally, for a finite dominant map of
normal varieties and a prime divisor `E` over `D`,

```text
partial_E(g^*[f])=e(E/D) partial_D([f]).                 (24a)
```

Thus odd ramification index preserves a nonzero parity word, while even
ramification can erase it.  For a nonfinite birational map, new exceptional
divisors are not controlled by `(24a)` and their valuations must be recomputed.
This is why THM-2681's base-preserving obstruction cannot be promoted across
an unspecified discriminant extension.

For a smooth projective curve, `(1)` recovers the familiar branch/genus tax.
If one character defines a connected nontrivial double cover and has `b`
odd-valuation points, that cover has

```text
g(cover)=2g(base)-1+b/2.                                 (25)
```

Thus the rank-one parity mechanism transfers to the degree-eighteen spectral
curve calculations.  Their constant coefficient-field extensions are not,
however, same-dimensional finite target normalizations (dimension two in the
planar problem and dimension three in THM-1310), and cannot be substituted for
`R` in `(12)`--`(17)`.

## 6. A cheap integral-resolvent prefilter

There is a useful first test before computing `(14)`.  A monic cubic

```text
R(W)=W^3+A W^2+B W+C                                    (26)
```

is literally the integral resolvent, in the displayed `W` coordinate, of a
depressed quartic `(11)` if and only if

```text
AB-C is a square in K.                                  (27)
```

Indeed coefficient comparison gives

```text
p=-A,          r=-B/4,          q^2=AB-C.               (28)
```

After translating `W=V+h`, condition `(27)` becomes

```text
-R(-A-2h) is a square.                                  (29)
```

Thus allowing translations asks for a finite affine `K`-point on
`Y^2=-R(T)`; the projective point at infinity is irrelevant and is always
`K`-rational.  General changes of cubic generator are outside this prefilter.

For THM-2681's normalized cubic

```text
R(T)=T^3-T^2+(bc/4)T-ac^2/4,                            (30)
```

the quantity in `(27)` is

```text
c(ac-b)/4,                                              (31)
```

which is nonsquare in `C(a,b,c)` by its odd valuations at `c=0` and
`ac-b=0`.  Hence `(30)` is not itself a depressed-quartic integral resolvent.
THM-2681 is stronger because it excludes the relevant cubic **field** under
the stated normalization identification, not only this polynomial generator.
