## Statement

For a prime q ≡ 7 (mod 8) let H = border(Paley_q) be the skew-Hadamard matrix of order
n = q+1 in tournament gauge, and C(H) the binary row span of (J−H)/2. Then C(H) is a
doubly-even self-dual [n, n/2] code, and:

| q  | n  | C(H) | identification grade |
|----|----|------|----------------------|
| 7  | 8  | ê₈ = RM(1,3) = eQR(8) | rigorous (unique Type II of length 8) |
| 23 | 24 | **extended binary Golay g₂₄** (wd 1, 759·x⁸, 2576·x¹², 759·x¹⁶, x²⁴) | rigorous (unique [24,12,8] Type II) |
| 31 | 32 | eQR(32), and provably NOT RM(2,5) | rigorous (Type II [32,16,8] ∈ {RM(2,5), eQR(32)}; split below) |
| 47 | 48 | eQR(48) = the extremal [48,24,12] | rigorous (unique extremal Type II of length 48) |

**Conjecture:** C(border(Paley_q)) ≅ eQR(q+1) for every prime q ≡ 7 (mod 8).

**Corollary (Gleason generation).** Gleason's theorem: every Type II self-dual weight
enumerator lies in ℂ[W_{ê₈}, W_{g₂₄}]. Both generators are tournament gauges of Paley
borders (q = 7 and q = 23): the full Type II weight-enumerator world is generated by two
Paley tournaments.

## The q = 31 split (new discriminator data)

The Gleason space at length 32 forces every Type II [32,16,8] code to share ONE weight
enumerator (A₈ = 620, A₁₂ = 13888, A₁₆ = 36518) — RM(2,5) and eQR(32) are isospectral, and
even the pairwise support-intersection profile of the 620 weight-8 words coincides
({0: 26970, 2: 138880, 4: 26040}). The 4-point MULTIPLICITY profile splits them:
#{weight-8 words ⊇ p} over the 4-sets p arising as pairwise intersections is
- RM(2,5): 1240 patterns, each in exactly **7** words (the weight-8 words are the 620
  3-flats of AG(5,2); each 2-flat lies in 7 of them);
- eQR(32) and gauge(Paley₃₁): 12400 patterns with profile **{2: 7440, 3: 3720, 4: 1240}** —
  identical to each other, different from RM(2,5). Hence gauge ≅ eQR(32). ∎

## Remarks

1. **Border remembers, doubling forgets.** THM-482 shows one skew-Sylvester doubling step
   sends ANY even-row skew-Hadamard gauge code to the crystalline d₂ₙ⁺ — all memory of H
   erased. The Paley border instead produces the arithmetic eQR family. Two regimes of one
   gauge: doubling = cooling to the d⁺ fixed point; Paley = the hot/arithmetic branch — the
   additive/multiplicative temperature axis (S720/721) at the level of self-dual codes.
   Consequence: double(border(Paley₂₃)) at order 48 gives d₄₈⁺, NOT eQR(48) — same order,
   same gauge, opposite regime, distinguishing the two skew-Hadamard classes of order 48.
2. **At order 32 the gauge realizes both hard Type II codes:** d₃₂⁺ from the tower (THM-480)
   and eQR(32) from Paley₃₁; with Sylvester realizing the non-self-dual RM(1,5). Open: is
   RM(2,5) a tournament gauge of ANY skew-Hadamard of order 32? (The five Type II [32,16]
   codes vs the skew-Hadamard classes of order 32 — a finite, sharp question.)
3. **Min-distance ladder:** the verified codes have d = 4 (d⁺ family) vs d = 8, 8, 12
   (QR family: extremal at 24 and 48) — the Paley branch maximizes minimum distance
   (extremal Type II), the doubling branch minimizes it (d = 4 always). Quasirandom-vs-
   crystalline again: the repo's flat-autocorrelation/extremal dichotomy in coding form.
