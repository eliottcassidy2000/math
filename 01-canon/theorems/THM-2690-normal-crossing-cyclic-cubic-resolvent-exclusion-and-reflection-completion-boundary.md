# THM-2690 -- a normal-crossing cyclic cubic has no binary carrier

**PROOF-COMPLETE CANDIDATE + VERIFIED-EXACT; AWAITING INDEPENDENT HOSTILE
AUDIT.**

The live `A4` quartic lane has a cyclic cubic matching-resolvent
normalization.  THM-2655 requires its regular locus to carry a connected
`V4` torsor, and THM-2686 says that any nonzero mod-two Kummer character on
that locus already generates the standard two-dimensional `C3` plane.  This
theorem closes an infinite and geometrically natural family of such cyclic
normalizations.  Its divisor group is entirely `3`-primary: there is nowhere
for the required binary plane to live.

The final section records the sharp boundary.  A cyclic cubic cover can carry
the binary plane over a singular base, and adjoining the missing order-two
reflection can turn that singular `C3` quotient into a polynomial `S3`
quotient.  This is the exact `V4 semidirect C3` versus
`V4 semidirect S3` distinction; it is not a free-product identification and
does not transfer the exclusion from `A4` to `S4`.

## 1. A branch-generated class-group gate

Let `X=A^n_C`, let `E/C(X)` be cyclic Galois of degree three, let `R` be the
normalization of `X` in `E`, and put

```text
U=(Spec R)_reg,                 Q=Gal(E/C(X))=C3.          (1)
```

Assume:

```text
Q acts trivially on R^*/R^(*)2;                           (2a)
Cl(R) is generated by classes of Q-fixed prime divisors.  (2b)
```

Then

```text
H^1_et(U,mu_2)=0.                                        (3)
```

Indeed, normality identifies `Pic(U)=Cl(R)` and gives the equivariant Kummer
sequence

```text
0 -> R^*/R^(*)2 -> H^1_et(U,mu_2) -> Cl(R)[2] -> 0.      (4)
```

Condition `(2b)` makes the `Q`-action on all of `Cl(R)`, hence on
`Cl(R)[2]`, trivial.  Since `char(F_2)` does not divide `|Q|=3`, sequence
`(4)` splits as an `F_2[Q]`-module.  Thus `(2a)`--`(2b)` make the entire
middle term a trivial `Q`-module.  THM-2686 gives

```text
H^1_et(U,mu_2)^Q=0,                                      (5)
```

so the middle term itself is zero.

A convenient sufficient test for `(2b)` is Nagata localization.  If a
`Q`-semi-invariant `g in R` has factorial localization `R_g`, and every
height-one prime containing `g` is individually `Q`-fixed, then Nagata's
sequence generates `Cl(R)` by precisely those fixed boundary primes.  This
is a **fixed-divisor** test: an orbit of three boundary primes is not enough.

## 2. The normal-crossing cyclic-cubic family

Fix integers `1<=m<=n`, put

```text
A=C[x_1,...,x_n],             f=x_1...x_m,
R_(m,n)=A[z]/(z^3-f).                                    (6)
```

The valuation of `f` at `x_1=0` is one, so `f` is not a cube in `Frac(A)`.
Consequently `(6)` has fraction field of degree three over `Frac(A)`, with
cyclic deck action

```text
sigma(z)=zeta_3 z,             sigma(x_i)=x_i.            (7)
```

The ring in `(6)` is normal.  For `m=1` one eliminates `x_1=z^3`, obtaining
a polynomial ring.  For `m>=2` it is a hypersurface and hence satisfies
Serre `S_2`.  Its Jacobian singular locus has `z=0` and at least two of
`x_1,...,x_m` equal to zero (for `m=2`, both are zero).  Every component has
codimension two in `Spec R_(m,n)`, so `R_1` also holds.  Thus `(6)` is the
full affine-target normalization in its cyclic cubic field.

Let

```text
P_i=(z,x_i),                  1<=i<=m.                    (8)
```

These are exactly the height-one primes containing `z`.  Localizing gives

```text
R_(m,n)[z^-1]
 =C[z^+-1,x_1^+-1,...,x_(m-1)^+-1,x_(m+1),...,x_n],     (9)
```

where `x_m=z^3/(x_1...x_(m-1))`.  Hence `(9)` is a UFD.  The valuation
vectors of its Laurent-unit generators are

```text
div(z)=P_1+...+P_m,
div(x_i)=3P_i.                                           (10)
```

Nagata's exact sequence therefore gives the presentation

```text
Cl(R_(m,n))
 =Z^m/<3e_1,...,3e_m,e_1+...+e_m>
 =(Z/3)^(m-1).                                           (11)
```

There are no hidden relations: the units of `(9)` are exactly constants
times powers of `z,x_1,...,x_(m-1)`, whose valuation vectors generate the
displayed relation lattice.  The weighted grading

```text
deg(x_i)=3 (i<=m),      deg(z)=m,
deg(x_j)=1 (j>m)                                         (12)
```

is positive and has degree-zero part `C`; since `(6)` is a domain, it forces

```text
R_(m,n)^*=C^*.                                           (13)
```

Every complex constant is a square.  Combining `(11)`--`(13)` with `(4)`
now yields the stronger direct computation

```text
H^1_et((Spec R_(m,n))_reg,mu_2)=0.                       (14)
```

Equations `(7)`--`(10)` also exhibit Section 1's mechanism: every boundary
prime is fixed by `C3`, and all divisor torsion has exponent three.

### 2.1 Every reduced normal-crossing exponent vector

The all-one hypersurface `(6)` is the simplest member of a larger exact
family.  Choose

```text
alpha=(alpha_1,...,alpha_m) in {1,2}^m                  (14a)
```

and let `R_alpha` be the normalization of `A` in

```text
z^3=x_1^(alpha_1)...x_m^(alpha_m).                      (14b)
```

Unlike `(6)`, the displayed hypersurface in `(14b)` need not itself be
normal.  Its normalization is the affine toric ring for the positive
orthant with character lattice

```text
M_alpha=Z^n+Z*(alpha_1,...,alpha_m,0,...,0)/3.           (14c)
```

In the dual lattice, the primitive ray vector along each of the first `m`
coordinate axes is three times the old primitive ray; the remaining rays
stay primitive.  The toric divisor sequence therefore presents

```text
Cl(R_alpha)
 =Z^m/<3e_1,...,3e_m,(alpha_1,...,alpha_m)>
 =(Z/3)^(m-1).                                          (14d)
```

The last Smith equality follows because every `alpha_i` is prime to three:
the first invariant factor is one, and the remaining `m-1` factors are
three.  The cone is pointed and full-dimensional, so `R_alpha^*=C^*`.
Consequently `(14)` and the `A4` exclusion `(15)` hold verbatim for every
reduced normal-crossing exponent vector `(14a)`.  Formula `(6)` retains the
advantage of being an explicit already-normal hypersurface.

## 3. The exact `A4` exclusion

Suppose a quartic polynomial Keller map with generic monodromy `A4` has full
cyclic cubic matching-resolvent normalization, over its affine target,
isomorphic to one of `(6)`.  THM-2655 produces a connected finite-etale
`V4` torsor over the regular locus of that normalization.  Dualizing gives a
rank-two subspace of the group in `(14)`, contradiction.  Therefore

```text
no R_(m,n) in (6) is the full cyclic cubic resolvent normalization
of an A4 quartic Keller map.                              (15)
```

This is invariant under polynomial target automorphisms and explicit
base-ring isomorphisms.  It is not a birational statement: deleting the
boundary primes in `(8)` destroys the class-group calculation, exactly as in
THM-2681.

## 4. Sharp hostile: the order-two reflection changes the base

Odd degree by itself does **not** kill binary Kummer data.  Consider

```text
R_0=C[a,b,c,d]/(d^2-abc),                                (16)
```

with `C3` cycling `a,b,c` and fixing `d`.  THM-2655 computes

```text
Cl(R_0)=(Z/2)^2,                                         (17)
```

with the three nonzero classes cyclically permuted.  Thus `(17)` is exactly
the standard `F_2[C3]` plane required by a `V4` carrier.

Put

```text
e_1=a+b+c,       e_2=ab+bc+ca,
delta=(a-b)(b-c)(c-a).                                  (18)
```

Since `abc=d^2`, exact invariant theory gives

```text
R_0^C3
 =C[e_1,e_2,d,delta]/(delta^2-Delta(e_1,e_2,d^2)),       (19)

Delta=e_1^2 e_2^2-4e_2^3-4e_1^3 d^2
      -27d^4+18e_1e_2d^2.                               (20)
```

The hypersurface `(19)` is singular at the origin, so it is not affine
three-space.  The missing reflection in `S3=C3 semidirect C2` sends
`delta` to `-delta`; taking its invariants removes the alternating generator:

```text
R_0^S3=C[e_1,e_2,d],       Spec(R_0^S3)=A^3.            (21)
```

Thus the same `R_0` is a cyclic degree-three normalization over the singular
base `(19)`, but an `S3` normalization over the smooth affine base `(21)`.
This is the rigorous two/three interaction: `C3` rotates the three nonzero
points of `P^1(F_2)`, while the extra `C2` reflection completes that rotation
to `GL_2(F_2)=S3`.  It does not identify this semidirect product with the
free product `C2*C3`.

The completion is a sharp **carrier** hostile, not a Keller example.
In the distinct source-quotient coordinates `(s_1,s_2,s_3)` of THM-2655,
with `s_3=d`, the induced quartic quotient is

```text
(s_1,s_2,s_3) |->
(s_1^2-2s_2, s_2^2-2s_1s_3, s_3),                      (22)
```

and its Jacobian is `4(s_1s_2-s_3)`, which is nonconstant.  The symbols
`s_1,s_2` in `(22)` are not the target invariants `e_1,e_2` in `(18)`.
Hence `(16)`--`(22)` block the false shortcut from `(15)` to a general `S4`
exclusion while leaving the Keller equation as the decisive missing
coordinate.

## 5. Scope and exact companion

The proved mechanism would close any cyclic cubic normalization satisfying
the fixed-divisor hypotheses `(2)`, not only the coordinate family `(6)`.
It does not say that every cyclic cubic cover has branch-generated class
group, that an odd-degree normalization has no two-torsion, or that a dense
root torus controls its affine completion.  The hostile `(16)`--`(21)` makes
all three failures explicit.

This theorem is an exact `A4` resolvent-family exclusion and an `S4` stopping
boundary.  It proves no general `A4/S4` exclusion, degree-four bound,
Jacobian conjecture, planar Jacobian conjecture, or Dixmier conjecture.
