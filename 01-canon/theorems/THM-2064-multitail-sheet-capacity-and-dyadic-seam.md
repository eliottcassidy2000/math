# THM-2064 -- multi-tail sheet capacity and the primitive dyadic seam

Let `C` be a finite set of positive core speeds, let `a>=1`, and let `W` be a
finite set of tail speeds. Fix a denominator `N` and a core-safe residue

```text
r mod N,       14|cr|_N>=N for every c in C,            (1)
```

where `|.|_m` is least absolute residue. Put

```text
Q=Na,
g_w=gcd(w,Q),             h_w=Q/g_w,
u_w=w/g_w,                d_w=gcd(N,h_w),
t_w=h_w/d_w.                                            (2)
```

Thus `u_w` is a unit modulo `h_w`, and `t_w` is the number of distinct tail
phases seen above one fixed residue modulo `N`.

## 1. The sheet-capacity theorem

Set

```text
L=lcm(N,(h_w)_(w in W)),       T=L/N.                   (3)
```

Among the `T` residues

```text
K_r={k mod L:k=r mod N},                                (4)
```

tail `w` is dangerous at no more than

```text
T ceil(t_w/7)/t_w                                      (5)
```

points. Consequently

```text
sum_(w in W) ceil(t_w/7)/t_w < 1                       (6)
```

implies that some `k in K_r` is safe for every core speed and every tail.
The phase `k/Q` is therefore an LRC(14) witness for

```text
aC union W.                                             (7)
```

### Proof

At `k/Q`, a core speed has phase

```text
ack/(Na)=ck/N,
```

so every member of (4) retains (1). Modulo `h_w`, the solutions of
`k=r mod N` form one coset of the subgroup generated by `N`. That subgroup
has order `t_w`; multiplication by the unit `u_w` preserves its order. The
normalized tail phases are therefore a translate of the equally spaced cycle

```text
{0,1/t_w,...,(t_w-1)/t_w}.                              (8)
```

The strict danger arc `||theta||<1/14` has circular length `1/7`. An open arc
of that length contains at most `ceil(t_w/7)` points of a `t_w`-cycle. The
projection from (4) to (8) is uniform, with every point repeated `T/t_w`
times, proving (5). The union bound and (6) leave at least one point outside
all tail danger sets. QED.

The strict inequality in the danger definition is load-bearing: points at
distance exactly `1/14` are valid weak LRC witnesses. Formula (5) remains
correct when `7|t_w` because an open arc of exactly an integral number of mesh
spacings cannot contain both endpoints.

## 2. One and two tails

For every `t>=2`,

```text
ceil(t/7)/t<1.                                          (9)
```

Thus a single tail with `t_w>=2` can never cover a safe core fiber. This is
the sheet-counting core of THM-2060.

For `t>=3`,

```text
ceil(t/7)/t<=1/3.                                      (10)
```

Since the value at `t=2` is `1/2`, two tails with `t_x,t_y>=2` satisfy (6)
unless

```text
(t_x,t_y)=(2,2).                                       (11)
```

Indeed (10) is immediate for `3<=t<=6`; for `t>=7`,
`ceil(t/7)<=(t+6)/7<=t/3`.

There is also an exact divisibility interpretation:

```text
t_w=1  iff  h_w|N  iff  a|w.                           (12)
```

The first equivalence is the definition of `t_w`; the second follows from
`h_w=Na/g_w` and `g_w=gcd(w,Na)`.

## 3. Primitive imprimitive-core reduction

Let

```text
S=aC union {x,y},       |C|=11,       a>1,              (13)
```

be a primitive set of thirteen distinct positive speeds. If `a|x`, absorb
`x/a` into the twelve-speed core `C union {x/a}`. Settled lower-dimensional
LRC supplies a phase of height at least `1/13`; by the strict gap
`1/13>1/14` and density of rational phases, it supplies a rational clock
satisfying (1) at threshold `1/14`. Primitivity prevents `a|y` as well, so
`t_y>=2` by (12), and the one-tail case proves `S`. The same argument applies
with `x,y` exchanged.

Therefore a hypothetical counterexample in (13) has `t_x,t_y>=2`. Section 2
can now be applied after settled LRC for the eleven-speed core `C` supplies,
again by the strict threshold gap and rational density, a rational clock
satisfying (1). It forces (11). From `t_w=2` one has `h_w|2N`, hence

```text
a|2g_w,       a does not divide g_w.                    (14)
```

It follows that `a` is even, `a/2|g_w|w`, and `w/(a/2)` is odd. Apply this to
both tails. The common divisor `a/2` divides every speed in (13); primitivity
forces `a/2=1`. Hence every remaining counterexample must lie on the exact
dyadic seam

```text
S=2C union {x,y},       x,y odd.                        (15)
```

This proves the sharp sheet-capacity reduction reserved in THM-2061. The
remaining problem is signed: over each core residue there are two lifts, and
each odd tail can endanger at most one. Failure requires the two tails to own
opposite lifts on every core-safe residue. Capacity alone cannot rule that out.

## 4. Relation to the planar coefficient descent

THM-2063 and this theorem use the same lawful move, but there is no reduction
between the problems. In THM-2063, fixing one source fiber makes the leading
coefficient equation one-dimensional and repeated target shears exhaust it.
Here, fixing one core residue makes every tail an equally spaced finite fiber,
and a capacity count exhausts every configuration except the two-sheet seam.
The useful shared invariant is **relative fiber degree** (`deg_y` there,
`t_w` here), not total degree, runner count, or a common nullcone.

## 5. Assumption challenge and tournament analysis

The challenged assumption is that the runners themselves should be the
vertices. The lossless local vertices are the `T` longitudinal sheets in
(4); tails are danger subsets of those sheets. The quotient preserves the
LRC predicate because the core phase is fixed and every lift is enumerated.
It destroys the order and magnitude of the original speeds, which must remain
as a sidecar for changing the clock or analyzing the dyadic seam.

The exact proof object is a finite set-cover hypergraph, not a tournament.
For search scheduling, orient tail `w` toward tail `w'` when
`ceil(t_w/7)/t_w` is smaller, breaking ties by the smaller period `h_w`; the
score histogram ranks which tail contributes less cover. Ties are pervasive,
and any such orientation forgets intersections of danger subsets. In
particular, on (15) both vertices have weight `1/2`, while the entire residual
question is whether their two singleton dangers are opposite. Tournament
fingerprints cannot certify that intersection pattern.
