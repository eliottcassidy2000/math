# THM-3957 -- triple normal-crossing cotangent and conductor sequence

**PROVED + INDEPENDENTLY HOSTILE-AUDITED.**  Let `k` be any field and put

```text
A = k[x,y,z],                    B = A/(xyz),
Btilde = k[y,z] direct_sum k[x,z] direct_sum k[x,y],
E = Omega_(A/k) tensor_A B = B dx direct_sum B dy direct_sum B dz,
r = d(xyz) = yz dx+xz dy+xy dz.                          (1)
```

The three summands of `Btilde` are the normalizations of the components
`H_x={x=0}`, `H_y={y=0}`, `H_z={z=0}`.  Let `T` be the torsion submodule of
`Omega_(B/k)`, where torsion means annihilation by a `B`-nonzerodivisor, or
equivalently vanishing after localization at every generic point.  Then the
conormal map `B -> E`, `h |-> h r`, is injective, and

```text
K := ker(E -> Omega_(Btilde/k))
   = (yz)B dx direct_sum (xz)B dy direct_sum (xy)B dz.    (2)
```

Moreover `K` is also the kernel of `E -> Omega_(B/k)/T`, and there are exact
sequences

```text
0 -> B*r -> K -> T -> 0,                                 (3)

0 -> T -> Omega_(B/k) -> Omega_(Btilde/k)
  -> k[x] dx direct_sum k[y] dy direct_sum k[z] dz -> 0. (4)
```

The last arrow records the difference of the two branch restrictions along
each coordinate axis.  Consequently

```text
Omega_(B/k)/T
 ~= B/(yz) dx direct_sum B/(xz) dy direct_sum B/(xy) dz, (5)
```

but, unlike at the node in THM-3955, its injection into normalized
differentials is not surjective.

Write

```text
alpha=yz dx,                 beta=xz dy,                 gamma=xy dz.
```

Then

```text
T = <alpha,beta,gamma |
     x alpha=0, y beta=0, z gamma=0, alpha+beta+gamma=0>, (6)

T ~= Btilde/B,
Ann_B(T)=(xy,xz,yz).                                      (7)
```

Thus `T` is supported on the three double axes.  It has rank one at the
generic point of each axis and a two-dimensional residue-field fibre at the
triple point.  The normalization cokernel in (4) has rank one generically on
each axis and a three-dimensional fibre at the triple point.

## Proof

### 1. Conormal injection and the ambient kernel

The hypersurface presentation gives

```text
B --r--> E -> Omega_(B/k) -> 0.                           (8)
```

In `B`,

```text
Ann(yz)=(x),       Ann(xz)=(y),       Ann(xy)=(z),
(x) intersect (y) intersect (z)=0.                       (9)
```

Hence `h r=0` forces `h=0`, proving injectivity in (8).

On `H_x`, the pullback of `dx` is zero while `dy,dz` survive.  Therefore the
`dx` coefficient of an element in the kernel of `E -> Omega_Btilde` must
vanish on both `H_y` and `H_z`.  Since

```text
(y) intersect (z)=(yz) in B,                             (10)
```

that coefficient lies in `(yz)`.  The same argument cyclically gives (2).

Multiplication by the displayed monomials identifies

```text
K ~= B/(x) direct_sum B/(y) direct_sum B/(z) = Btilde.   (11)
```

Under (11), the conormal element `h r` corresponds to

```text
(h mod x, h mod y, h mod z),                             (12)
```

the normalization inclusion `B -> Btilde`.  It follows already that

```text
K/(B*r) ~= Btilde/B.                                    (13)
```

### 2. The quotient is exactly regular-element torsion

The normalized differential module is torsion-free for the multiplicative
set of `B`-nonzerodivisors, so every torsion differential lies in the kernel
of `Omega_B -> Omega_Btilde`.

Conversely, put

```text
q=xy+xz+yz.                                              (14)
```

Its restrictions to `H_x,H_y,H_z` are respectively `yz,xz,xy`, each nonzero
in a polynomial domain.  Thus `q` lies in no minimal prime and is a
nonzerodivisor of `B`.  It kills the three classes in (6).  For example, the
`xy` and `xz` terms kill `alpha` already in `E`, while multiplication of the
conormal relation by `yz` gives

```text
y^2 z^2 dx = 0 in Omega_(B/k).                           (15)
```

Hence `q alpha=0`; the other two cases are cyclic.  So every element of
`K/(B*r)` is torsion.  Together with the preceding inclusion, this proves

```text
T=K/(B*r),                                               (16)
```

and establishes (3), (6), and the first assertion of (7).

### 3. The conductor annihilator and the torsion strata

The conductor of `B` in `Btilde` is

```text
J=(xy,xz,yz).                                            (17)
```

Indeed, multiplying an arbitrary element of the `H_x` normalization summand
by `yz` gives a tuple supported only on `H_x`, represented in `B` by the same
polynomial; the cyclic generators behave similarly.  Thus `J Btilde` lies in
`B`.  Conversely, if `b Btilde` lies in `B`, multiply by each normalization
idempotent.  The restriction of `b` to `H_x` must vanish on both boundary
axes, hence is divisible by `yz`; cyclically its other restrictions are
divisible by `xz` and `xy`.  Their sum represents `b` and lies in `J`.

Since `T ~= Btilde/B`, its annihilator is precisely this conductor, proving
the second assertion of (7).  At a generic point of a double axis, exactly
two normalization components remain and their quotient by the diagonal has
rank one.  At the triple point, (6) tensors to three generators with the one
relation `alpha+beta+gamma=0`, giving fibre dimension two.

Equivalently, after orienting the axes one has the useful `B`-module model

```text
T ~= ker(k[x] direct_sum k[y] direct_sum k[z]
         --(f,g,h)|->f(0)+g(0)+h(0)--> k),              (18)
```

with

```text
alpha |-> (0,1,-1),
beta  |-> (-1,0,1),
gamma |-> (1,-1,0).                                     (19)
```

This also displays the rank-one axis stalks and the two-dimensional central
fibre directly.

### 4. The normalization cokernel

Write a normalized differential as

```text
omega_x=p_x(y,z)dy+q_x(y,z)dz on H_x,
omega_y=p_y(x,z)dx+q_y(x,z)dz on H_y,
omega_z=p_z(x,y)dx+q_z(x,y)dy on H_z.                    (20)
```

Define the last map in (4) by the three oriented mismatches

```text
(p_y(x,0)-p_z(x,0)) dx,
(p_x(y,0)-q_z(0,y)) dy,
(q_x(0,z)-q_y(0,z)) dz.                                 (21)
```

It is surjective: a prescribed differential on one axis can be placed on one
of its two incident planes and set to zero on the other planes.  Pullback of
that differential to either transverse axis is zero.

Its kernel is the image of `E`.  For example, the two desired `dx`
coefficients on `H_y` and `H_z` agree on their common `x`-axis by (21), and
the elementary fibre-product lift

```text
p_y(x,z)+p_z(x,y)-p_y(x,0) in B                         (22)
```

has exactly those two restrictions.  The `dy` and `dz` coefficients lift
cyclically.  Since the conormal relation maps to zero, the image is also the
image of `Omega_B`.  Its kernel there is `T` by (16), proving (4).  Finally
`Omega_B/T=E/K` gives (5).

## General coordinate-crossing corollary

The same argument gives a loss formula for every squarefree coordinate
crossing.  Let `r>=2` and put

```text
S=k[x_1,...,x_r],          F=product_i x_i,
B_r=S/(F),                 f_i=F/x_i,
E_r=direct_sum_i B_r dx_i,
Btilde_r=direct_sum_i B_r/(x_i).                         (23)
```

Then, over every field,

```text
ker(E_r -> Omega_(Btilde_r/k))=direct_sum_i (f_i) dx_i,
0 -> B_r*dF -> direct_sum_i (f_i) dx_i
  -> Tors Omega_(B_r/k) -> 0,
Tors Omega_(B_r/k) ~= Btilde_r/B_r,
Ann(Tors Omega_(B_r/k))=(f_1,...,f_r),
Omega_(B_r/k)/Tors ~= direct_sum_i B_r/(f_i) dx_i.       (24)
```

Indeed `Ann(f_i)=(x_i)`, and under `(f_i)~=B_r/(x_i)` the
conormal `dF=sum_i f_i dx_i` is exactly the diagonal normalization inclusion.
The element `sum_i f_i` is a nonzerodivisor in the conductor: on the component
`x_i=0`, only `f_i` survives.

Regrouping normalized differentials by their `dx_i` coefficient gives

```text
coker(Omega_Br/Tors -> Omega_Btilde_r)
 ~= direct_sum_i [
      (direct_sum_(j!=i) B_r/(x_j)) / (B_r/(f_i))
    ] dx_i.                                               (25)
```

Each bracket is the normalization defect of the `(r-1)`-component coefficient
union `S/(f_i)`.  At a point on exactly `m` components, the torsion fibre has
dimension `m-1`.  The differential-defect fibre has dimension zero when
`m=1`, and for every singular stratum `m>=2` has total dimension

```text
m(m-2)+(r-m)(m-1)=r*m-r-m.                              (26)
```

Thus the node case `(r,m)=(2,2)` has no normalization-differential cokernel;
the triple case `(3,3)` has torsion fibre dimension two and defect dimension
three; and `(4,4)` has dimensions three and eight.  Squarefreeness is
load-bearing: for a nonreduced crossing in characteristic `p`, the conormal
differential itself can collapse.

## Hostile boundary and analytic scope

Take `dx` on `H_y` and zero on `H_x,H_z`.  Its restriction to the `x`-axis
disagrees with the zero restriction from `H_z`, so (21) is nonzero and the
normalized differential does not descend.  This is the minimal hostile to
extrapolating THM-3955's node isomorphism to a triple crossing.

All arguments are ideal-theoretic and use no characteristic restriction.  In
characteristic two the signs in (19) coalesce, but all exact sequences and
fibre dimensions remain.  The proof survives localization at `(x,y,z)`, the
completion `k[[x,y,z]]/(xyz)`, and the complex convergent-power-series local
model.

For the S6 manuscript, (3) identifies explicit ambient-kernel witnesses and
(4) identifies a whole conductor-axis normalization defect at each claimed
triple point.  This is a local algebra theorem only: it does not show that a
local witness globalizes, that the manuscript's cusp fibre has the claimed
analytic model, or that its total space is a complex `S6`.
