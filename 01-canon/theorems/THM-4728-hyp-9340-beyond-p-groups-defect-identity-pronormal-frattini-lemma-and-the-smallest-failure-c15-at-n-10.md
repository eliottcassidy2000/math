# THM-4728 — the defect of HYP-9340 for a general odd H, and where it appears through n = 13

## Setting

`H <= S_n` of odd order, `N = N_(S_n)(H)`, `Fix_T(H)` and `Fix_G(H)` the `H`-fixed labelled tournaments and graphs (`2^c` each, `c` the number of `H`-orbits on pairs). `sgn_X(g) = (-1)^#{edges {u<v} of X : g(u) > g(v)}` for `g in Aut X` (the parity of the edges whose reference orientation `g` reverses; independent of the reference orientation); `X` is sign-even iff `sgn_X = 1` on `Aut X`, and `N`-even iff `sgn_X = 1` on `N cap Aut X`. `tau_T(H)`, `tau_E(H)`: numbers of isomorphism types of tournaments, resp. sign-even graphs, whose automorphism group contains a conjugate of `H`. For a type `x` let `m_x(H)` be the number of `N`-orbits on the `H`-fixed labelled objects of type `x`; it equals the number of `Aut(x)`-conjugacy classes of subgroups of `Aut(x)` that are `S_n`-conjugate to `H` (if `x_0` is a representative, the `H`-fixed copies of `x_0` are the `g x_0` with `g^(-1) H g <= Aut x_0`, and `g x_0`, `g' x_0` lie in one `N`-orbit iff the subgroups `g^(-1) H g`, `g'^(-1) H g'` of `Aut x_0` are `Aut x_0`-conjugate). `N`-evenness is constant on `N`-orbits (`sgn_(gX)(g h g^(-1)) = sgn_X(h)`), so `m^ev_X(H)`, the number of `N`-even orbits, is defined. A *witness* is a sign-odd graph type `X` with `m^ev_X(H) >= 1`. In the notation of the Codex census packets, `R = sum_T m_T`, `F_T = E_T`, `F_E = E_even` and `Z = W + E_wit`.

## Proof

**1.** THM-4721 Theorem R with `Gamma = N`: the number of `N`-orbits on `Fix_T(H)` equals the number of `N`-orbits on the `N`-even members of `Fix_G(H)`. Grouping the orbits by isomorphism type, the left side is `sum_T m_T(H) = tau_T(H) + E_T(H)`; on the right every orbit of a sign-even type is `N`-even (`sgn_X = 1` on all of `Aut X`), and an orbit of a sign-odd type counts iff it is `N`-even, so the right side is `sum_(X even) m_X + sum_(X witness) m^ev_X = tau_E + E_even + W + E_wit`. ∎ (The Codex derivation identifies `m_X` with the `Aut X`-orbits on the embedded copies of `H` and `m^ev_X` with the number of those orbits that split under the sign kernel; it is the same count seen from `Aut X`.)

**2.** Let `a in Aut X` have odd sign and put `B = ker sgn_X`, of index `2`. `H` has odd order, so `H <= B` and `H^a = a H a^(-1) <= B`. By pronormality there is `c in <H, H^a> <= B` with `c H^a c^(-1) = H`; then `ca in N_(Aut X)(H)` and `sgn_X(ca) = sgn_X(c) sgn_X(a) = -1`. For a witness the `N(H)`-even `H`-fixed representative `X` has `sgn_X = 1` on `N_(S_n)(H) cap Aut X = N_(Aut X)(H)` and is sign-odd, contradicting the conclusion; so `H` is not pronormal in `Aut X`. ∎

**3.** (a) Orbits of an odd-order group have odd size (orbit-stabiliser), so constituents have odd degree. Prime degree: Burnside's theorem (cited in THM-4644) gives the two alternatives; a 2-transitive group of degree `p` has order divisible by `p(p-1)`, even; the solvable alternative is a subgroup of `AGL(1,p)`, of odd order iff it is `C_p x| C_d` with `d` odd dividing `p - 1`: `d = 1` for `p = 3, 5`; `d in {1, 3}` for `p = 7`; `{1, 5}` for `p = 11`; `{1, 3}` for `p = 13`. Degree `9`: a primitive group of degree `9` and odd order is solvable (Feit-Thompson), hence affine inside `AGL(2,3)` of order `432 = 2^4 27`, so a `3`-group; an imprimitive one lies in `S_3 wr S_3`, whose odd part is `3^4`, so again a `3`-group. If two constituents have coprime orders, the subdirect product projecting onto both has order divisible by their product, hence is the direct product. Goursat: a subdirect product of `G_1 x G_2` is the fibre product of surjections `G_1 -> Q <- G_2`; the common quotients of `F21` (normal subgroups `1`, `C7`, `F21`) and of `C3 x C3` or `C3` are `1` and `C3`; for `Q = C3` and `G_2 = C3 x C3` the kernel of `C3 x C3 -> C3` is one of two factors or one of two diagonals, the two choices in each class being conjugate (by the exchange of the two `3`-orbits, resp. by a transposition in one of them), and the sign of the identification `F21/C7 ~ C3` is absorbed by a transposition of a `3`-orbit (conjugation by `AGL(1,7)` induces the identity on `F21/C7`); for `G_2 = C3` the fibre product over `C3` is the graph of `F21 -> C3` embedded diagonally.
