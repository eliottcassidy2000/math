# THM-2627 -- two quadratic characters force two Jelonek components

**PROVED CANDIDATE + VERIFIED-EXACT + TWO INDEPENDENT HOSTILE AUDITS.**

The key reframe is to count boundary components by the rank of the monodromy
character group, not by trying to assign one visible pole to each sheet move.
For `D4`, the source-deck quadratic and the resolvent/discriminant quadratic
are the two generators of `D4^ab = C2 x C2`.  On the finite-etale Jelonek
complement, Kummer theory has nowhere else to put them.

## 1. Exact component theorem

Let

```text
F: A^n_C -> A^n_C                                         (1)
```

be a polynomial Keller map of generic degree four whose geometric monodromy
in the four-sheet action is `D4`.  Write the reduced Jelonek hypersurface as

```text
A_F = V(f_1 ... f_c),       U = A^n_C \ A_F,              (2)
```

where the `f_i` are pairwise nonassociate irreducibles.  Then

```text
c >= 2.                                                     (3)
```

More precisely, the two nonzero characters described in Section 2 give
linearly independent vectors in

```text
O(U)^*/O(U)^{*2} = F_2^c,                                  (4)
```

whose coordinates are valuation parities along the `f_i`.  Both vectors are
nonzero.  Their supports need not be disjoint.

### Proof

Over `U`, the Galois closure of `F` is a connected finite-etale `D4`-cover.
The localization

```text
O(U)=C[t_1,...,t_n,1/(f_1...f_c)]                          (5)
```

is a UFD, so `Pic(U)=0`; its units modulo squares are freely generated by the
classes of the `f_i`.  The Kummer sequence therefore gives

```text
H^1_et(U,mu_2) = O(U)^*/O(U)^{*2} = F_2^c.                (6)
```

Surjective monodromy `pi_1(U) -> D4` makes pullback injective on characters:

```text
Hom(D4,C2)=F_2^2  -->  H^1_et(U,mu_2).                    (7)
```

Thus `2 <= c`.  Notice that projective infinity cannot replace an affine
coordinate in (4): a rational function in `C(t_1,...,t_n)` having even
valuation at every affine prime is a square up to a nonzero complex constant.

The Keller hypothesis is load-bearing here.  It removes finite critical-value
divisors, leaving the Jelonek boundary as the complete affine branch support.

## 2. The two fields and the `V4` quotient

Use

```text
D4=<r,s | r^4=s^2=1, srs=r^-1>,      z=r^2,               (8)
```

and let `H=<s>` be a point stabilizer.  Put

```text
J=N_D4(H)=<s,z>,
V=D4 intersect A4=<z,rs>.                                 (9)
```

If `L/K` is the Galois closure and `E=L^H` the quartic source field, then

```text
M_deck=L^J,            M_Delta=L^V,
J intersect V=<z>=[D4,D4],
M_deck M_Delta=L^{<z>}.                                  (10)
```

Thus the two quadratic fields are distinct and linearly disjoint over `K`;
their compositum is the maximal biquadratic quotient.  The first character is
the fixed field of the unique nontrivial automorphism of `E/K`.  The second is
the sign character, equivalently the quadratic field of the cubic resolvent
and quartic discriminant.

For a depressed quartic

```text
f(T)=T^4+pT^2+qT+r,                                      (11)
```

the squared-pair resolvent is

```text
S(U)=U^3+2pU^2+(p^2-4r)U-q^2.                            (12)
```

In the `D4` case it has a unique `K`-root `w`.  If `w != 0`,

```text
M_deck=K(sqrt(w)).                                        (13)
```

If `w=0`, then `q=0` and

```text
M_deck=K(sqrt(p^2-4r)).                                   (14)
```

Writing `S=(U-w)Q`, one has

```text
Q=U^2+(2p+w)U+(p^2-4r+2pw+w^2),
delta=Disc(Q)=16r-4pw-3w^2,
M_Delta=K(sqrt(delta)),
Disc(f)=Disc(S)=S'(w)^2 delta.                            (15)
```

Equations (13)--(15) make the two parity rows directly computable in any
quartic coefficient chart.

## 3. Tame inertia census and its exact blind spot

For tame inertia, record the two character bits and the permutation different
exponent `4-(number of cycles)`:

```text
inertia class              deck  Delta  different exponent
identity                     0      0           0
central z=r^2                0      0           2
diagonal reflection s        0      1           1
edge reflection rs           1      0           2
four-cycle r                 1      1           3.        (16)
```

Thus the two bits, together with the different exponent, distinguish the five
tame `D4` inertia types.  But central inertia is invisible to both quadratic
characters.  The genuinely `D4` local family over `K=C(a,b,c)`

```text
f_c(T)=T^4-2acT^2+c^2(a^2-b)                                 (17)
```

has squared roots `c(a+sqrt(b))` and `c(a-sqrt(b))`.  The independent generic
squareclasses give the degree-eight tower, hence geometric group `D4`.
Around `c=0`, both root pairs change sign, giving the central `r^2` action.
Yet the two abelian quadratic classes are `b` and `a^2-b`, both `c`-units,
while

```text
Disc(f_c)=256 c^6 b^2(a^2-b).                               (18)
```

The raw exponent six includes an integral-model index contribution and must
not be confused with the normalized tame different exponent two in (16).

Consequently (3) does **not** say every branch component is seen by one of the
two characters, nor does it identify the pole component of THM-2612.

## 4. Planar resultant gate and higher-dimensional use

In THM-2241's planar monic-resultant chart, the reduced leading Jelonek
polynomial `rad(c(U,V))` must have at least two irreducible factors whenever
the quartic monodromy is `D4`.  Hence an irreducible nonzero `c` excludes the
`D4` lane.  The same theorem applies unchanged to a three-variable G1
candidate, because the proof used only the factorial target, its reduced
Jelonek divisor, and the finite-etale monodromy cover.

No current theorem proves the required irreducibility in all planar or G1
degree-four charts.  This is a new exact gate, not a closure of JC(2), G1, or
DC(2).

## 5. Sharp non-Keller hostile

The dominant planar map

```text
(x,y) |-> (u,v)=(x, x y^4+y^2)                              (19)
```

has generic quartic `uT^4+T^2-v`, `D4` Galois closure, and irreducible
nonproper set `u=0`.  Indeed, when `u != 0` the monic quartic root bound is
locally uniform, while `y=R`, `x=(v_0-R^2)/R^4` escapes to infinity over
`(0,v_0)`.  The squareclasses `1+4uv` and `-v/u` give the generic degree-eight
splitting tower.  Nevertheless

```text
M_deck=K(sqrt(1+4uv)),
M_Delta=K(sqrt(-uv)),
Disc_T(uT^4+T^2-v)=-16uv(1+4uv)^2.                         (20)
```

There is no contradiction because

```text
Jac(x,xy^4+y^2)=2y(2xy^2+1).                              (21)
```

The extra curves `v=0` and `1+4uv=0` are finite
critical-value divisors.  Merely dominant maps therefore do not satisfy the
component gate.

## 6. Scope boundary

The quadratic normalizations are finite-etale over `U`, not over the entire
target.  Neither is automatically a polynomial source quotient or a Keller
map; in particular Campbell's theorem cannot be applied to them.  The theorem
also does not produce two source deck transformations: the two involutions
live on the `V4` quotient of the Galois closure, while only one normalizes the
chosen point stabilizer.  This is exactly the distinction between the
`C2*C2` grammar of the quartic torsor and the `C2*C3` grammar of the modular
group.
