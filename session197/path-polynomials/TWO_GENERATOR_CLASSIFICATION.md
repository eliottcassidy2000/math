# An entire cancellation language with no prime or 7p output

Session 197. **PROVED all-order classification within a specified physical module language.** Both the transitive leaf length and the number of bank factors are unbounded. This is not a claim about arbitrary tournament constructions.

## 1. Language and statement

Use the physical seven-vertex quotient `[14,32,2,22,103,13,47]`, with nonempty modules `(Tr_m,P,U,K1,R,K1,V)`. Fix P=K1, let R=Tr_r for any integer r>=1, and let U,V be nonempty ordered joins of copies of K=K1 and W=`[8,1,3,6]`. Their factor order can be chosen arbitrarily; below we give one canonical construction.

Write the exact Hamilton-path count as

    H_m=A*3^m+B*2^m+C, m>=1.

Then B=0 if and only if r=4 and, for some integers t,s>=0, the numbers of copies are

    U: 4+t copies of K and s copies of W,
    V: t copies of K and s+1 copies of W.                  (1)

Consequently the complete set of pure ternary rays in this language is

    H_m=15360*4^t*1024^s*3^m - 65*25^s,
    m>=1, t>=0, s>=0.                                    (2)

Every such family has a physical strong witness of order

    m+15+2t+8s.                                          (3)

For each fixed t,s, the greatest common divisor of all positive-m counts is exactly 5. Every count is at least 46015. Thus this entire unbounded language produces neither a prime Hamilton-path count nor a count 7p with p prime.

## 2. Eliminating every other transitive leaf length

We use the exact ratio criterion in `RATIO_LATTICE.md`, whose physical coefficient dependency is `UNIT_OFFSET_RIGIDITY.md`. For X let h_X=H(X), b_X=B_X(2), rho_X=b_X/h_X. Ordered joins multiply h, b and rho. The two bank profiles are

    (h_K,b_K,rho_K)=(1,2,2),
    (h_W,b_W,rho_W)=(5,32,32/5).

Since h_(Tr_r)=1 and b_(Tr_r)=2^r, the leaf signs e_X=4h_X-b_X are e_P=2 and e_R=4-2^r. For r=1 both signs are positive, and for r=2 exactly one is zero. Neither case cancels. For r>=3, the opposite-sign criterion is

    rho_U/rho_V=kappa_r,
    kappa_r=(2^r-1)/(2*(2^(r-2)-1)).                       (4)

The group of ratios generated by K and W is exactly the group of positive rationals supported on the primes 2 and 5. Indeed

    rho_K^a rho_W^b=2^(a+5b)*5^(-b),

and any pair of integer 2-adic and 5-adic valuations is obtained by the unique integers a,b. Allowing signed exponents is legitimate for existence: positive exponents go into U, negative exponents into V, and if necessary a common bank copy makes both nonempty without changing the ratio.

Put g=gcd(2^r-1,2^(r-2)-1). Subtracting four times the second integer from the first gives 3, so g divides 3. Reduction modulo 3 shows g=1 for odd r and g=3 for even r. Hence the odd numerator and odd denominator of the reduced ratio (4) are

    N=(2^r-1)/g, D=(2^(r-2)-1)/g,
    gcd(N,D)=1.                                         (5)

At r=3, N=7, which is unsupported. At r=4, (N,D)=(5,1), so kappa_4=5/2 is supported. For every r>=5, both N and D exceed 1: the smaller is at least (2^3-1)/3>1. If (4) were supported on 2 and 5, the coprime odd integers N,D would both have to be positive powers of 5. That is impossible. This excludes every r>=5 without factoring a Mersenne number or appealing to any finite cutoff.

The coprime numerator/denominator argument above is elementary and applies at every leaf length.

## 3. All allocations and exact coefficients

For r=4, let u_K,u_W,v_K,v_W be the four nonnegative copy counts. Equation (4) becomes

    2^(u_K-v_K+5(u_W-v_W))*5^(-(u_W-v_W))=5/2.

Unique prime factorization forces

    u_W-v_W=-1, u_K-v_K=4.

The complete set of nonnegative solutions is precisely (1). Both modules are automatically nonempty: U has at least four K factors and V at least one W factor. No extra padding and no virtual realization are needed.

Multiplicativity gives

    h_U=5^s,             b_U=2^(4+t+5s),
    h_V=5^(s+1),         b_V=2^(t+5s+5).

The inherited coefficient formula at P=K1,R=Tr4 is

    A=30*b_U*b_V,
    B=30*b_V*h_U-12*b_U*h_V,
    C=-13*h_U*h_V.

Substitution gives A=15360*4^t*1024^s, B=0 and C=-65*25^s, proving (2). Different factor orders need not give isomorphic graphs, but the labelled-bin product identity makes these same scalar coefficients valid for every ordering.

A canonical witness takes

    U=Tr_(4+t) -> W -> ... -> W  (s copies),
    V=Tr_t -> W -> ... -> W      (s+1 copies).

Here Tr_0 denotes an empty initial segment, not an empty module. Put these two actual nonempty graphs into the stated quotient. The vertex count is (3). The quotient has the directed Hamilton cycle

    0 -> 2 -> 1 -> 5 -> 3 -> 4 -> 6 -> 0.

A directed cycle of nonempty modules ensures the blowup is strong: vertices in distinct modules communicate along that cycle, and two vertices of one module communicate by going once around the other modules. No internal strongness assumption on U or V is needed.

## 4. Exact common divisor and prime exclusion

For fixed t,s write A and C as above. Then

    A=2^(10+2t+10s)*3*5,
    |C|=13*5^(2s+1).

Thus gcd(A,C)=5 and 3 does not divide C. Every H_m is odd. Since

    H_2-3H_1=-2C,

we have gcd(H_1,H_2)=gcd(3A+C,2C). Its oddness removes the factor 2, and then

    gcd(H_1,H_2)=gcd(3A+C,C)=gcd(3A,C)=5.

Every H_m is divisible by 5, so the gcd over all m>=1 is exactly 5.

Moreover, for all m>=1 and t,s>=0,

    H_m >= 46015*4^t*1024^s >= 46015,

because 3^m>=3 and 25^s<=4^t*1024^s. A positive prime divisible by 5 would have to be 5; a value 7p divisible by 5 with p prime would have to be 35. Both are below this lower bound.

This is an obstruction for the complete language just defined. It does not preclude other leaf graphs, other bank generators, different quotients, or prime/7p values elsewhere in the tournament spectrum.
