# Two exposed vertices: a finite physical interface beyond one-root growth

Session 197, 2026-11-05. **All-order exact construction, minimal linear continuation dimension, and arbitrary-depth target decision.** The full tournament Hamiltonian-path spectrum remains open. The support calculation below is complete only through its stated target bound and within the precisely defined construction class. No literature-priority claim is made.

## 1. The new physical choice

The [S195 combined grammar](https://github.com/eliottcassidy2000/math/blob/ee07a5aac9cdfd8d3834fa834df4fb357fcaf769/session195/combined-growth/PROOF.md) exposes one old vertex. Its complete order-at-most-five gadget bank, even with stationary operations, cannot realize 67 or 173 at any depth. The [S195 endpoint-typed algebra](https://github.com/eliottcassidy2000/math/blob/ee07a5aac9cdfd8d3834fa834df4fb357fcaf769/session195/pathcover-algebra/PROOF.md) permits much more general gluing, but retains path covers of unbounded degree and proves an infinite-rank obstruction for its full context language.

Here retain two distinct, ordered actual vertices `(p0,p1)`. For a subset `S` of these ports, adjoin one vertex v with exactly the old out-neighbors S:

    v -> pi iff i is in S; every other old vertex -> v.

Choose any one of `(p0,p1,v)` to cease being a port. The vertex is **not deleted**. Keep the other two in their inherited order. Write this move `(S,d)`, where S is a two-bit mask and `d=0,1,2` is the forgotten position in that three-element list. There are twelve choices. All old arcs remain unchanged.

Start with rows `[2,0]`, ports `[0,1]`. Thus the class is built entirely with singleton births, with no unexplained finite bank. One may also apply the exact interface theorem to any actual input tournament with two ordered ports.

The architecture combines stationary transitive-clone chains, single reversed seams, genuinely two-port seams, and changes of the exposed vertices. It is not asserted to contain every gadget operation from the older bank. Its two-port moves are not operations on a formal pair of one-root profiles.

**Results.**

1. Twenty-seven nonnegative integer path-cover coordinates form a closed, exact all-order state.
2. The minimal dimension of a homogeneous rational linear scalar-continuation representation of the twelve-generator class, from the stated seed, is **17**.
3. Capping every nonnegative coordinate at `X+1` gives a finite, exact decision algorithm for every target `H<=X`, at arbitrary construction depth.
4. Exact capped closure through 173 reaches every positive odd integer through 173 except 7 and 21. This is not an eventual-coverage theorem.
5. Explicit singleton-growth words reach 67 and 173. Repeated two-port cloning yields strong all-order families

       68*3^m + 26*2^m - 29,
       228*3^m - 36*2^m - 19,        m>=1.

The two-port interface is finite because only the two exposed old vertices can receive a backward arc from a later birth. This restricted future language is essential and avoids the inherited arbitrary-context infinite-rank obstruction.

## 2. Twenty-seven actual cover counts

A path cover is an unordered set of disjoint, nonempty directed vertex paths covering the entire tournament. Singleton paths are permitted; directed cycle components are not.

Retain those covers with **at most one component whose initial vertex is not a port**. Every other component starts at one of the two distinct ports, so such a cover has at most three components.

Encode a component by `(s,e)`, where its initial and terminal vertex have labels `0`, `1`, or `*`; `*` means an actual non-port vertex. Sort the components by their start labels, with `*` last. The component starts are distinct labels. A port cannot occur at endpoints of two different components. If a port is both the initial and terminal endpoint of a component, that component is its singleton path. Ports not appearing at an endpoint are internal vertices of components.

The placement of an internal port among components need not be recorded: later insertion only reads component endpoints and their connectivity. A non-port end is attached to a particular component, identified by its start label. Thus two different components ending at non-ports are never silently identified as the same fragment.

There are exactly 27 possible nonempty endpoint signatures. The state coordinate `z_sigma(T,p0,p1)` counts the covers with signature sigma. `automaton.json` lists them explicitly in the deterministic order generated by `interface.py`. Hamilton paths are exactly the signatures with one component, so

    H(T) = sum_(sigma with one component) z_sigma.             (1)

For a general fixed number k of ports the same construction has finitely many states. Its number of endpoint signatures is

    k! [x^k] (2+x) exp(3x+x^2) - 1.                           (2)

Indeed a port can be internal, a singleton component, or a component start with a non-port end (three single-vertex types); a directed pairing of a start port with a different end port contributes `x^2`; the optional non-port start either ends at a non-port or consumes one end port. Subtract the empty collection with every port internal. For k=2, (2) gives 27. The proof and certificates below use k=2.

## 3. Exact insertion and forgetting theorem

**Theorem 1.** Each of the twelve births has a fixed nonnegative integer 27-by-27 transfer matrix. The matrix depends only on `(S,d)`, and the actual output signature is that matrix applied to the actual input signature.

**Constructive rule.** Temporarily regard v as a third distinct port. For each old cover, choose at most one predecessor component and at most one successor component for v.

- A predecessor component is allowed if its last vertex beats v. This holds automatically for a non-port last vertex. For last vertex pi, it holds precisely when `i notin S`.
- A successor component is allowed if its first vertex is pi with `i in S`. A non-port start cannot be a successor because v loses to every old non-port.
- The predecessor and successor cannot be the same component, because joining both ends of one component through v would create a cycle.

If neither is selected, add singleton v. If only a predecessor is selected, append v to it. If only a successor is selected, prepend v to it. If both are selected, merge those two components through v. All unselected components remain unchanged.

Now forget the chosen one of the three ports and relabel the survivors in their inherited order. Any endpoint at the forgotten vertex becomes a non-port endpoint. If this creates two distinct components with non-port starts, discard the result. Otherwise record its new signature. Every allowed selection contributes one to the transfer coefficient; different choices can legitimately contribute to the same signature.

**Closure and bijection.** Every generated object is an actual cover of the output tournament. Conversely take any counted output cover and delete v from its component. If v was internal, this splits that component into two. Its successor, if any, must be one of the old ports in S. Deleting v therefore cannot create an additional old non-port start. Any old non-port start of the restricted cover was already a non-port start of the output cover. A forgotten old port becomes a port again when restricting to the old boundary, which can only reduce that number. Hence the restricted old cover satisfies the old at-most-one condition.

The position of v uniquely recovers the chosen predecessor and successor, or their absence. They cannot be the same old component, since the output was a path cover, not a cyclic cover. Restriction and insertion are inverse operations on named paths. The endpoint signature determines every admissibility test, and the components are separately identified by their starts. This proves the exact nonnegative transfer law. QED.

This proof checks forgetting physically, rather than treating it as arbitrary erasure of a numerical coordinate. A forgotten vertex remains in the graph and in its path cover, and its new non-port status changes future permitted backward arcs.

## 4. Two one-root states do not suffice

For an individual root r, recall `(h,e,R,d)` from S195: total paths, paths ending at r, ordered two-covers whose second path starts at r, and `H(T-r)`.

Use the five-vertex tournament

    T=[24,17,3,6,12], H(T)=15.

Every vertex has the same one-root profile

    (h,e,R,d)=(15,3,21,5).

The pairs `(0,1)` and `(0,2)` also have the same port-to-port orientation: in each case the second port beats the first. Nevertheless, adjoining a vertex beating exactly those two ports gives, respectively,

    [24,17,35,38,44,3], H=41;
    [24,49,3,38,44,5], H=43.                         (3)

The issue is a cross-cover interaction. Let `C_ab` count two-component covers in which one component ends at a and the other starts at b, and define `C_ba` similarly. These are distinct components, not a single component with both endpoints. The birth beating both ports obeys

    H' = h + (R_a-e_a) + (R_b-e_b) - C_ab-C_ba.      (4)

To prove (4), a path ending at the new vertex contributes `h-e_a-e_b`. A path using the new vertex followed by a port b comes from an R_b-cover, except when its first component ends at a port beaten by the new vertex. Sum over the two possible successor ports. No cover correction with the same port at both distinct-component endpoints is possible. The cases are disjoint.

For (3), the interaction sums are 10 and 8. Thus even both complete old four-coordinate profiles, together with their mutual arc, cannot implement the new move. The 27-coordinate state includes these interactions and higher covers needed by subsequent moves.

## 5. The scalar linear continuation dimension is exactly 17

**Theorem 2.** For the specified seed and all twelve moves, 17 is the least dimension of a rational vector representation with fixed homogeneous linear operation maps and a linear H-readout.

This is a linear statement, not an impossibility theorem about nonlinear encodings.

The exact finite certificate is `rank_certificate.json`:

- It lists seventeen actual suffix words, each of length at most three.
- The j-th proposed coordinate is H after appending that suffix. Each is an explicitly given nonnegative integer linear combination of the 27 covers.
- For each of the twelve moves it gives an integer 17-by-17 matrix whose product with the listed 17-by-27 observation matrix equals the observation matrix after that physical move. The verifier checks these exact identities entry by entry.
- The empty suffix is one of the seventeen, so H is a coordinate.

These identities prove a 17-dimensional representation for every operation word, not just the sampled graphs.

For the lower bound, the file gives seventeen actual prefix words, also of length at most three. Its continuation matrix has entries

    C_ij = H(seed followed by prefix_j followed by suffix_i).

The verifier independently builds each of the 289 graphs, counts its Hamilton paths by vertex-subset dynamic programming, and recomputes

    det C = -2,097,152 = -2^21 != 0.                 (5)

Any d-dimensional homogeneous linear representation factors C through a d-dimensional space. Consequently `d>=17`. Together with the checked universal span identities this proves the theorem.

The 17-state representation has negative entries in some operation matrices. The 27-state nonnegative representation is deliberately retained for the next theorem.

## 6. A finite exact test for arbitrary-depth targets

**Theorem 3 (coordinate saturation).** Fix X>=1. Replace each cover coordinate z by `min(z,X+1)`. Starting at the capped seed, saturate under the twelve matrices, capping after every move. This finite graph decides exactly which H<=X occur at any construction depth.

**Proof.** For a nonnegative integer matrix M and the coordinatewise cap c,

    c(Mz)=c(M c(z)).                                 (6)

For each output sum, if an input coordinate was capped and has positive coefficient, both sides cap to X+1; otherwise the same uncapped summands occur. The identity includes zero coefficients, so a large discarded-port statistic can later disappear without invalidating the method. Negative transfers would invalidate this argument, which is why the raw cover state is used.

Induction makes every capped operation word exact. A reported output h<=X is the true sum in (1), because none of its nonnegative summands was truncated to X+1. Conversely every physical word maps to a capped walk. There are at most `(X+2)^27` capped vectors, so saturation terminates.

We also discard a state once its H-readout exceeds X. Every construction adds vertices without changing old arcs, and Hamilton paths of an induced subtournament inject into those of the enlarged tournament: insert the missing named vertices by a fixed legal Hamilton-path insertion rule and recover the original word by deleting them. Therefore H never decreases, making this pruning safe.

Every retained capped state has an actual word witnessing it, though some of its uncapped cover counts may be larger than the stored value. Coalescing two capped states preserves all future H answers through X by (6). There is no graph-order or operation-depth cutoff. QED.

## 8. Stationary clone clocks and unrestricted interleaving

Keep both old ports and repeatedly apply `(S,2)` for a fixed S. The new vertices form one positive transitive module, with identical incidences to every original vertex. In the case S=3, a new vertex can be followed only by one of the two ports before returning to another clone run.

The [S196 matching-rank cloning theorem](https://github.com/eliottcassidy2000/math/blob/cf3b44b9a442c3cff0f28cab924e73c4c00bb00c/session196/spectral-moves/PROOF.md) already implies a Hamilton count with exponential bases at most 1,2,3. For this particular stationary operation the same bound holds for **every one of the 27 cover coordinates**.

**Theorem 4 (full-interface three-mode clock).** For any actual input and fixed S, every cover coordinate after m stationary births, for m>=1, is an integer linear combination of `1^m,2^m,3^m`. In particular every scalar sequence is determined by its values at m=1,2,3 and satisfies

    z_(m+3)=6 z_(m+2)-11 z_(m+1)+6 z_m, m>=1.        (9)

**Proof.** Cut a counted cover into maximal clone runs. Suppose the cover has c components. At least c-1 components start at distinct old ports. Every gap between consecutive clone runs in one component must use an old port as the successor of its earlier clone run. These gap ports are distinct and are not the ports already consumed as component starts. Consequently the number of clone runs is at most

    c + (2-(c-1)) = 3.

This remains an upper bound when a component contains no clone, or when all c component starts are ports. Compress each nonempty clone run to one symbol. For a quotient with j runs, distributing the m distinct transitive clones among the j ordered nonempty runs gives exactly `j! S(m,j)`, with each run in its unique increasing order. Ordering the components by start labels and their runs along each component fixes the run order. Therefore every coordinate is a nonnegative integer combination of

    1, 2^m-2, 3^m-3*2^m+3,

which proves the statement and (9). QED.

The formula is for **positive** multiplicities. Formal substitution m=0 is not generally the count of the original input. For example, the first family below has formal value 65 at zero, whereas its actual seed has H67.

For the H67 seed (7), stationary S=3 gives

    H_m=68*3^m+26*2^m-29,
    H_1,H_2,H_3,H_4 = 227,687,2015,5895.             (10)

For the H173 seed (8), it gives

    H_m=228*3^m-36*2^m-19,
    H_1,H_2,H_3,H_4 = 593,1889,5849,17873.           (11)

The first three counts determine the coefficients under the proved three-mode theorem. The verifier counts the actual expanded graphs independently through m=5. Strongness holds for every m: the seed is strong, a new vertex reaches a port and hence the whole seed, and every seed vertex reaches an old vertex outside the two ports, which beats every new vertex. The clone chain therefore lies in the same strong component.

Unlike a fixed cloning bank, the general grammar can stop any clock, expose a newly born vertex, use a two-port seam, and restart at a different pair. Its template need not remain fixed. This is actual iterability, not a promise that all these choices supply desired binary or ternary digits.

For another all-order structural control, the three-birth word

    W=[(1,1),(0,0),(1,1)]

implements a rooted directed-triangle one-seam attachment. If the incoming ports are `(a,z)`, its fresh vertices b,c,d satisfy `b->a`, `b->c->d->b`, all other old-to-new arcs point forward, and the outgoing ports are `(b,d)`. Thus W can be repeated indefinitely. From any strong input each output is strong, and the new vertex-disjoint triangles force unbounded feedback vertex number. This compiles the inherited S195 triangle growth into the new singleton-only language; the corresponding old triangle-count formula is not claimed as new. Interleaving W with genuine two-port moves supplies constructions outside a fixed template bank.

## 9. An honest all-order inverse construction on a multiplicative slice

The two-port class is closed under ordered joins, hence under products of Hamilton counts. After a word for T, perform `(0,0),(0,0)`. These two universal-sink births forget both old ports and create a fresh transitive two-vertex seed. Now perform any word for U. Every old T vertex beats every new U vertex, because no T vertex is an exposed port any longer. The result is literally `T -> U`, so its Hamilton paths are precisely a T-path followed by a U-path:

    H(T -> U)=H(T)H(U).                              (12)

There is no unit-offset assertion for this reset. It deliberately makes every subsequent count divisible by H(T).

**Corollary 5 (explicit 23-smooth compiler).** Every positive odd integer N all of whose prime factors are at most 23, except N=7 or 21, has an explicit two-port construction of order `O(log N)`.

The seed file `smooth_seeds.json` contains actual operation words and adjacency rows for

    3,5,11,13,17,19,23,
    35,49,63,77,91,119,133,161,343.

All except 343 lie in the completed bound 173 closure. The H343 seed is independently counted by subset DP and has the elementary word

    [(2,1),(2,1),(2,1),(2,1),(2,2),(2,2),
     (2,1),(0,2),(2,2),(2,1)].

Its graph and ports are also in `seed343.json`.

To compile N, factor it using the fixed list `3,5,7,11,13,17,19,23`. Pair all but possibly an odd residue of its 7-factors into 49. If there are at least three 7-factors and an odd residue, use 343 first. If exactly one 7 remains, combine it with an available prime p among `5,11,13,17,19,23`, using the seed 7p. If no such p occurs, use 63 with two 3-factors. The only cases where the last step is unavailable are 7 and 21. All remaining prime factors have seed constructions. Join their graphs using (12). The empty product uses the H1 starting edge.

`compile_smooth.py N` implements this target-to-word algorithm and returns the literal adjacency rows. Every seed has at most 12 vertices. If `N>1` and `t>=1` seed factors occur, the ordered-join implementation has exactly the sum of their orders and at most `12t` vertices; since each factor is at least 3, `t<=log_3 N`. The exceptional empty product `N=1` uses the two-vertex seed, not a zero-vertex graph. This is a proved infinite arithmetic slice, not extrapolation of interval coverage. Smooth numbers exclude almost all large primes, so the corollary does not close the prime-atom problem. The general multiplication principle is elementary, and no new numerical-record claim is attached to this slice.
