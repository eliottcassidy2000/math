## 1. The atomic pair {1,3} and its amplifications (the clean statement)

{1, 3} is the H-spectrum of the two tournaments on 3 vertices: the transitive T₃ has
H = 1 (one Hamiltonian path), the 3-cycle has H = 3 (= 1 + 2·1, one 3-cycle). Every
pair in question is a scalar multiple:

  **{2, 6} = 2·{1, 3},  {7, 21} = 7·{1, 3}**  (and the ratio-3 shape 6 = 3·2, 21 = 3·7
  is the same {1,3} = {1, 3·1} up to scale).

So {2,6} and {7,21} are the *same pair* — the atomic three-cycle pair {1,3} — amplified
by 2 and by 7. The multipliers are the two Rédei-special sizes: 2 (the doubling prime,
the reflection) and 7 (the seven-wall). This is the tightest link: **they are one pair
seen at two scales.**

## 2. The Rédei theater: {2,6} is the reflection (anti-automorphism) spectrum, {7,21} the count spectrum

The sharpest, VERIFIED reading (boxeph-S150 / HYP-8205): **{2,6} is the
anti-automorphism ORDER spectrum** of tournaments in the exact census n ≤ 6 — the
realized orders of the *reflections* (converse / T↔Tᵒᵖ maps, the anti-automorphisms).
The anti-order law (an anti-automorphism σ has σ² ∈ Aut of odd order m, so |σ| = 2m)
forces every reflection order to be **2·(odd)**, i.e. in {2, 6, 10, 14, …}, and the
census realizes exactly the first two: **{2, 6} = {2·1, 2·3}.** So {2,6} lives on the
Rédei-**sign** side — the reflection coset, the σ-involution, the Rédei sign that
opus-S418 identified with the JC counterexample's discriminant character.

{7, 21}, by contrast, lives on the Rédei-**count** side: the H-spectrum
(H = 1 + 2·#odd-cycles), where {7, 21} are the two permanent forbidden values (§3).

So the two pairs are the two faces of Rédei parity:
- **{2,6} = 2·{1,3}** — reflection orders (the SIGN, anti-automorphisms);
- **{7,21} = 7·{1,3}** and = {7, 3·7} — forbidden Hamiltonian-path counts (the COUNT).

Both are "x and 3x" pairs (6 = 3·2, 21 = 3·7) on the same odd structure, and the
shared **3 is the trisection** — the C₃ / three-cycle, the smallest OCF monoid
generator, the "1 + 2" atom. And each large value doubles as a group order: 6 = |S₃|
= |D₃| (the JC-fiber monodromy, klein-S333's dihedral quotient) = C(4,2); 21 = |F₂₁|
= |C₇⋊C₃| (the Paley T₇ automorphism, the doubling-tower freeze THM-448) = C(7,2).
The reflection-order reading (element orders) and the group-order reading (S₃, F₂₁)
agree on the numbers 6 and 21; the honest primitive is the anti-order spectrum {2,6}.

## 3. The OCF theater (why {7,21}, verified) and the parity of {2,6}

**Verified this session:** {7, 21} are the two PERMANENT forbidden H-values — computing
the H-spectrum exhaustively, the forbidden odd set at n=6 is {7, 21, 35, 39}, and
{35, 39} become achievable by n=7 (the S59l octal sieve), leaving exactly {7, 21} as
permanent gaps (THM-029, THM-079). The trisection is inside them: H = 7 = 1 + 2·3 (the
first H that would need three 3-cycles), and 21 = 3·7. {2, 6}, being 2·{1,3}, is even,
hence trivially outside the (always-odd) H-spectrum — so {2,6} lives not as forbidden
H-values but as the **doubling** 2·{1,3} of the same atom, which is why its natural home
is the group/reflection theater (§2), not the OCF spectrum.

## 4. The dichotomy: {2,6},{7,21} are TRISECTION pairs; {12,24} is the DOUBLING pair

The repo already paired {7,21} with {12,24} (opus-2026-07-04, "base + exceptional
multiple"): 21 = 3·7 (×3), 24 = 2·12 (×2). This session sharpens it: the **multiplier
is the invariant**, and it splits the exceptional pairs into two axes —

- **×3 = trisection axis**: {2, 6}, {7, 21} — the three-cycle / C₃ / S₃ / F₂₁ / the

trisection axis** (2·{1,3} and 7·{1,3}), the ×3 partner-family to the doubling axis's

and the exceptional pairs are {atom}·{scale} sorted by which multiplier built them.
