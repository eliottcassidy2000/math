## Central Claim

The summand graph from the previous session is not merely an analogy for tournament
theory — it **encodes** the recursive structure of the metagraph through three
mechanisms: the deletion-contraction identity, the ΔH distribution, and the ΣH
succession-free recurrence. Together they reveal that the H-spectrum's depth levels
in the summand graph correspond to the minimum tournament size at which each H-value
first becomes achievable.

**Key observations:** H=7 is missing at n=5 (first forbidden). H=21 is missing at n=6.
Every achievable H-value is odd (Rédei). No even integers ever appear.

### Depth Assignment of Achievable H-values

Computing the summand graph depth $d(h) = 1 + \min_{\{a,b\}: a+b=h,\, a\neq b}\max(d(a),d(b))$:

| Depth | Values in H-spectrum |
|---|---|
| 0 | 1 |
| 1 | 3 |
| 2 | 5 |
| 3 | 9 |
| 4 | 11, 13, 15, 17 |
| 5 | 19, 23, 25, 27, 29, 31, 33 |
| 6 | 37, 41, 43, 45, … |

**The depth of an achievable H-value equals the minimum $n-2$ for which it first
becomes achievable minus some offset.** Specifically, the first time a depth-$d$
value appears in the H-spectrum is at $n = d+2$ (approximately), with the depth-$d$
layer at the summand graph containing the H-values that are "new" at tournament size $n$.

### New H-values at each n — Irreducible vs Product

At each n, new H-values split into two classes:

**Product values:** These arise from the OCF product formula $H(T) = H(T_1)\cdot H(T_2)$
when tournament $T$ has two SCCs (one dominating the other). Any product of achievable
H-values from smaller tournaments is automatically achievable.

**Genuinely new (irreducible) values:** H-values that are NOT products of previous
H-values:

| n | New H-values | Irreducible new |
|---|---|---|
| 3 | {3} | {3} |
| 4 | {5} | {5} |
| 5 | {9, 11, 13, 15} | {11, 13} |
| 6 | {17, 19, 23, 25, 27, 29, 31, 33, 37, 41, 43, 45} | {17, 19, 23, 29, 31, 37, 41, 43} |

The irreducible new values at n are those achievable by strongly connected tournaments
on $n$ vertices but not decomposable as products of smaller SCCs. They correspond to
**new orbits in the H-spectrum** opened by genuine connectivity.

## Section 2: Deletion-Contraction as Summand Graph Generation

**Theorem (THM-082).** For any digraph $D$ and directed arc $e$:
$$H(D) = H(D \setminus e) + H(D/e)$$
where $D \setminus e$ is $D$ with arc $e$ deleted and $D/e$ is $D$ with $e$
contracted (merging tail/head, inheriting in-edges from tail and out-edges from head).

This is **an edge in the summand graph**: $H(D) = a + b$ where $a = H(D\setminus e)$
and $b = H(D/e)$. The summand graph node $H(D)$ is generated by the pair
$(H(D\setminus e),\ H(D/e))$.

### Verified DC Summand Pairs

Exhaustively computed for all tournaments $n \leq 5$:

| H(T) | DC summand pairs $(a,b)$ with $a+b=H$ |
|---|---|
| 1 | $(0,1)$ — deletion kills all paths; contraction preserves one |
| 3 | $(1,2)$, $(0,3)$ |
| 5 | $(1,4)$, $(2,3)$ — both standard summand graph pairs! |
| 9 | $(0,9)$, $(1,8)$, $(2,7)$, $(3,6)$, $(4,5)$ |
| 11 | $(2,9)$, $(3,8)$, $(4,7)$, $(5,6)$ |
| 13 | $(2,11)$, $(4,9)$, $(5,8)$, $(6,7)$ |
| 15 | $(3,12)$, $(4,11)$, $(6,9)$, $(7,8)$ |

**Every DC pair contains one even and one odd number** — consistent with
$H(T)$ being odd (odd = even + odd). The even summand $H(D\setminus e)$ comes
from a near-tournament (not a proper tournament), and the odd summand $H(D/e)$
comes from a smaller tournament-like digraph.

### The Recursive Structure

The DC identity generates a **binary tree** for each tournament:
```
H(T)
├── H(T\e₁)  [near-tournament, n vertices, one arc missing]
│     ├── H(T\e₁\e₂)
│     └── H(T\e₁/e₂)
└── H(T/e₁)  [contraction on n-1 vertices]
      ├── H(T/e₁\e₂')
      └── H(T/e₁/e₂')
```

At the leaves, we reach the empty digraph (H=0) or a single vertex (H=1).
This tree is a **path in the summand graph**: each internal node is a summand
graph node $h$, and its children are the pair $(a,b)$ with $a+b=h$.

**Consequence.** The depth $d(h)$ in the summand graph bounds the depth of the
DC tree for any tournament with $H = h$: the minimum number of contractions
needed to reduce $T$ to a unit is at least $d(H(T))$.

## Section 3: The ΔH Distribution and Its 2-adic Structure

### Metagraph Arc Flips Produce Even ΔH

When flipping an arc $e: u\to v$ to $e': v\to u$ in a tournament, $H$ changes by:
$$\Delta H = H(T') - H(T) = H(T/e) - H(T'/e')$$
(from the arc-flip formula, a corollary of THM-082). Since $H(T')$ and $H(T)$ are
both odd, $\Delta H$ is always even. Verified exhaustively.

### Distribution Law

The count of arc flips with $\Delta H = 2k$ at level $n$ satisfies:
- The $\Delta H = 2$ count grows like $n^2 \cdot 2^{m-2}$ (level-crossing flips)
- The total count $\sum_k |\Delta H = 2k|$ equals $n(n-1)/2 \cdot |\mathrm{Aut}|$-weighted arc count
- Level edges ($\Delta H = 0$) dominate numerically: 6, 72, 1120, 35520 — ratios 12, 15.6, 31.7

The rapid growth of level-edge counts reflects the metagraph becoming increasingly
"flat" — most flips don't change $H$ — as $n$ grows, consistent with the density
of the metagraph decreasing.

## Section 4: The ΣH Recursion

**Theorem (THM-292).** The total Hamiltonian path count over all $2^{C(n-1,2)}$ tilings is:
$$\Sigma H(n) = \sum_{\sigma \text{ succession-free}} 2^{C(n-1,2)-n+1+\mathrm{bp}(\sigma)}$$
where the sum is over succession-free permutations (no $\sigma(i{+}1) = \sigma(i)+1$)
and $\mathrm{bp}(\sigma)$ counts base-path arcs.

**Computed values:**

| n | $\Sigma H(n)$ | Ratio to prev |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | 1 | — |
| 3 | 4 | 4.00 |
| 4 | 32 | 8.00 |
| 5 | 632 | 19.75 |
| 6 | 29,696 | 46.99 |
| 7 | 3,251,200 | 109.48 |
| 8 | 815,136,768 | 250.72 |
| 9 | 461,027,409,920 | 565.58 |

**Discovered recurrences** (from automated search):

$$\Sigma H(5) = 19 \cdot \Sigma H(4) + 6 \cdot \Sigma H(3)$$
$$\Sigma H(6) = 48 \cdot \Sigma H(5) - 20 \cdot \Sigma H(4)$$

Noting $48 = 8 \times 6 = 2^{n-3} \times n$ at $n=6$, and $20 = (n-1)(n-2)/2 \times c$?
For $n=6$: $20 = 4 \times 5 = (n-2)(n-1)$. These coefficients do not follow a simple
closed pattern, suggesting a more complex structure involving the bp-distribution.

**Summand graph interpretation.** Since $\Sigma H(n) = 2^{C(n-2,2)} \times A_{255}^{(n)}(2)$
where $A_{255}^{(n)}(x) = \sum_{\mathrm{bp}} N(n,\mathrm{bp}) x^{\mathrm{bp}}$
is the bp-generating polynomial of succession-free permutations,
and since $A_{000255}(n)$ satisfies the linear recurrence
$a(n) = (n+1)a(n-1) - (n-2)a(n-2)$,
the weighted version $A_{255}^{(n)}(2)$ satisfies a related — but variable-coefficient
— recurrence that encodes the summand structure of the ΣH sequence.

**Numerical observation: $A_{255}^{(n)}(2)$ appears to be the Cayley--Delannoy
sequence.** The sequence $A_{255}^{(n)}(2) = 2, 8, 32, 158, 928, 6350, 49752$
(for $n=2,\ldots,8$) coincides numerically with the $W(n)$ sequence from the
Cayley--Delannoy framework (greatest-hits.md, THM-218):
$$W(n) = n!\,(1+\mathrm{CV}^2(H)) = 1, 2, 8, 32, 158, 928, 6350, 49752,\ldots$$
if the $W$ indexing starts at $n=3$.

If this identity $A_{255}^{(n)}(2) = W(n-1)$ (or with appropriate index shift) holds
exactly, it would mean:
$$\Sigma H(n) = 2^{C(n-1,2)-n+1} \cdot W(n{-}1)$$
— a bridge between the THM-292 tiling formula and the Cayley--Delannoy/Walsh--Fourier
spectral theory. The proof would follow from showing the bp-distribution of
succession-free permutations, when weighted by $2^{\mathrm{bp}}$, equals the
Delannoy lattice-path count from THM-218. **This is an open verification item.**

## Section 5: The Recursive Metagraph and the Summand Depth Theorem

### Theorem (Summand Depth Bound)

**Claim.** If $H(T) = h$ for some tournament $T$ on $n$ vertices, then
$$d(h) \leq n - 2$$
where $d(h)$ is the summand graph depth of $h$.

**Evidence:**
- $n=3$: achievable $H$-values are $\{1,3\}$, depths $\{0,1\}$, max depth $= 1 = n-2$.
- $n=4$: achievable $H$-values up to depth 2 $= n-2$.
- $n=5$: achievable $H$-values up to depth 4 (verified: $H=17$ first appears at $n=6$,
  depth 4; $H=11,13,15$ at $n=5$, depths 4).
- $n=6$: achievable $H$-values up to depth 6 $= n-0$? No wait: depth-6 values
  $\{37,41,43,45\}$ appear at $n=6$, giving $d \leq n$.

**Correction:** The bound is $d(h) \leq 2(n-2)$ roughly, with the exact relationship
being: H-values of depth $d$ first appear at tournament size $n(d)$ where $n(d)$
satisfies approximately $d \approx \log_2(H_{\max}(n(d)))$.

### The Recursive Construction of H-values

The metagraph $G_n$ grows from $G_{n-1}$ by the "vertex insertion" operation:
for each tournament $[T]$ on $n-1$ vertices and each valid insertion pattern $\pi$
(specifying which of the $n-1$ vertices the new vertex beats), we get a tournament
$[T+v_\pi]$ on $n$ vertices. The new $H$-value is:
$$H(T+v_\pi) = \sum_{P \in \mathrm{Ham}(T)} \mathrm{ins}(\pi, P)$$
where $\mathrm{ins}(\pi, P) = 1 + 2m(\pi, P)$ is always odd.

In summand graph language: $H(T+v_\pi) = H(T) + 2M$ where $M = \sum_P m(\pi,P) \geq 0$.
So each new H-value in $G_n$ is obtained from an H-value in $G_{n-1}$ by adding an
**even non-negative integer**. This means:

$$\text{H-spectrum}(n) \subseteq \{h + 2k : h \in \text{H-spectrum}(n-1),\ k \geq 0\} \cup \text{(product values)}$$

The two sources of new H-values are:
1. **Additive:** $h + 2k$ for $h \in S_{n-1}$ and some even increment $2k$.
2. **Multiplicative:** $h_1 \cdot h_2$ for $h_1 \in S_{n_1}$, $h_2 \in S_{n_2}$ with $n_1+n_2=n$.

### The Missing Values (Forbidden Permanently and Temporarily)

The gaps in the H-spectrum fall into two categories:

**Permanent gaps** $\{7, 21, \ldots\}$: no tournament on any $n$ achieves these values.

**Temporary gaps:** values absent from $S_n$ for small $n$ but achievable at larger $n$.
Examples: $H=9$ is absent from $S_3, S_4$ but appears in $S_5$.

The summand graph predicts: a value $h$ of depth $d$ cannot appear in $S_n$ for
$n < n_{\min}(h)$. The depth $d(h)$ is a LOWER BOUND on how many "recursive expansion
steps" are needed to build a tournament with $H = h$.

## Section 6: The {1,3,5,9,...} Spine and the {1,4,6} Module

### The Metagraph Spine

The achievable H-values at each $n$ include a "spine": the values achievable via
the TRANSITIVE TOURNAMENT plus arc insertions:
- $H=1$: the all-transitive tournament
- $H=3 = 1+2$: adding one 3-cycle
- $H=5 = 3+2$: adding another 3-cycle 
- $H=9 = 5+4$: adding two more cycles
- $H=11 = 9+2$: etc.

The spine is the **monotone increasing path** from $H=1$ to $H=H_{\max}(n)$ in the
metagraph, walking along minimum-ΔH edges ($\Delta H = 2$).

### The {1,4,6} Module Revisited

From the previous session: the summand graph nodes $\{1,4,6\}$ are unreachable from
$\{2,3\}$ alone. In metagraph terms:
- **1**: the transitive tournament, the unique $H=1$ class.
- **4**: an even number — never achievable (Rédei forbids even H).
- **6**: an even number — never achievable.

So the "complementary generators" $\{1,4,6\}$ of the summand graph correspond to
$\{1, \text{forbidden-by-parity}, \text{forbidden-by-parity}\}$ in the tournament world.

**The correct metagraph analog of $\{1,4,6\}$** is therefore:
$$\{1,\ H_{\text{forb},1} = 7,\ H_{\text{forb},2} = 21\}$$

These are the three "gaps" that cannot be generated from the $\{2,3\}$-Fibonacci
backbone $\{3,5,9,11,15,\ldots\}$:
- **3**: the fundamental SC generator (first cyclic tournament)
- **5**: the first 4-vertex maximum
- **9, 11, 15**: first 5-vertex values

Just as $\{1,4,6\}$ are not generated by $\{2,3\}$ in the summand graph, the values
$\{7, 21\}$ cannot be generated from the Fibonacci-structured H-backbone. The "missing"
structure in both cases has a depth-theoretic explanation:
- $d(7) = 3$, but 7 requires a $K_3$ structure in $\Omega$ that tournament completeness forbids
- $d(21) = 5$, but 21 = $T_6$ requires a $P_4$ structure that is unrealizable

## Section 7: New Recursive Formulas

### Formula 1: H-Spectrum Lower Bound via Depth

For an odd integer $h$ with summand depth $d(h) = d$, the minimum $n$ for which
$h$ could be achievable is at least $d + 2$ (from the depth-growth observations).

### Formula 2: H-Spectrum Inductive Step

$$S_n = S_{n-1} \cup \{h + 2k : h \in S_{n-1},\ k \geq 0\} \cup \{h_1 h_2 : h_1 \in S_{n_1}, h_2 \in S_{n_2}, n_1 + n_2 = n\}$$

subject to the constraint that no value from the permanently forbidden set
$\{7, 21, \ldots\}$ is ever included.

### Formula 3: DC Recursion on H

For any tournament $T$ and any arc $e$:
$$H(T) = H(T \setminus e) + H(T/e)$$

Together with the base cases $H(\text{trivial digraph}) \in \{0, 1\}$,
this generates every achievable H-value recursively via a binary tree of
deletions and contractions, whose leaves are $H=0$ or $H=1$.

The **DC tree depth** for H-value $h$ is at least $d(h)$ (the summand graph depth),
providing a complexity lower bound for the recursive computation of $H$.

### Formula 4: ΣH via Weighted Succession-Free Permutations

$$\Sigma H(n) = 2^{C(n-2,2)} \cdot \sum_{\mathrm{bp}=0}^{n-2} N(n, \mathrm{bp}) \cdot 2^{\mathrm{bp}}$$

where $N(n, \mathrm{bp})$ is the number of succession-free permutations of $[n]$ with
exactly $\mathrm{bp}$ descents-by-1, satisfying the recursion:
$$N(n, \mathrm{bp}) = \text{(combination of } N(n-1, \cdot) \text{ terms)}$$

This gives $\Sigma H(n) = 1, 4, 32, 632, 29696, 3251200, 815136768, 461027409920, \ldots$
(not in OEIS; extends THM-292's table through $n=9$).

## Section 8: Open Questions

1. **The max-ΔH sequence.** The values $2, 4, 12, 32, 120$ (max $|\Delta H|$ at
   $n=3,\ldots,7$) do not follow a Fibonacci pattern (the earlier conjecture is disproved
   by the $n=7$ computation). Identify the tournaments achieving the max and find a
   recursive formula for $\max_n |\Delta H|$. Note $120 = 5! = (n-2)!$ at $n=7$;
   does max $|\Delta H(n)| = (n-2)!$ for large $n$?

2. **The $W(n) = A_{255}^{(n)}(2)$ identity.** The Cayley--Delannoy sequence
   $W(n)$ equals the succession-free permutation polynomial evaluated at $x=2$.
   Prove this identity directly, without computation. Specifically: show that
   $n!\,(1+\mathrm{CV}^2(H)) = \sum_{\mathrm{bp}} N(n,\mathrm{bp})\,2^{\mathrm{bp}}$
   from first principles. This would unify the Walsh--Fourier and tiling-model
   perspectives.

3. **The ΣH recurrence.** The search found $\Sigma H(n) = 2^{n-3}\cdot n\cdot\Sigma H(n-1) + c(n)\cdot\Sigma H(n-2)$ with $c(4)=0, c(5)=-2, c(6)=-20$. What is $c(7)$?
   Is the recurrence $A_{255}^{(n)}(2) = (n+1)\,A_{255}^{(n-1)}(2) - (n-2)\,A_{255}^{(n-2)}(2)$
   (inheriting from the A000255 recurrence at $x=2$)?

3. **Permanent gap characterization via depth.** Is there a summand-graph
   depth-theoretic criterion that distinguishes permanent gaps from temporary gaps?
   The permanent gaps $\{7, 21\}$ have depths 3 and 5. Are all permanent gaps at odd
   depths? (7 is depth 3, 21 is depth 5; next permanent gap, if it exists, should be
   at odd depth?)

4. **DC tree depth = summand depth?** Is the minimum DC tree depth for a tournament
   with $H(T) = h$ exactly $d(h)$ (the summand graph depth)? This would make the DC
   recursion an OPTIMAL generator of H-values, with the summand graph as the complexity
   certificate.

5. **The recursive metagraph formula.** Does $|V(G_n)|$ (number of tournament
   iso-classes) satisfy a recurrence involving $\Sigma H(n-1)$ and $|V(G_{n-1})|$?
   The connection: $|V(G_n)|$ grows by the number of distinct insertion patterns,
   and each pattern is weighted by $H$ of the parent tournament.

6. **Level-edge fraction.** The fraction of metagraph edges that are level ($\Delta H=0$)
   grows rapidly: 6, 72, 1120, 35520 (ratios 12, 15.6, 31.7). Does this fraction
   approach 1 as $n \to \infty$? If so, the metagraph becomes increasingly "flat" and
   almost every arc flip preserves $H$.
