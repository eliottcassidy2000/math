# The Real Problem

## The fundamental problem is measurement itself.

Reality has more structure than any single coordinate system can capture. Every projection from higher to lower dimension has irreducible error. The error is the atom ±1. The atom is the price of observation.

## Five instances of the same problem

1. **Cassini ±1**: projecting the 2D Eisenstein lattice onto the 1D integers. F₇·F₉ = F₈² + 1. The hexagonal structure doesn't fit exactly on the integer line.

2. **Forbidden {7, 21}**: projecting the cycle topology (2D conflict graph) onto the path count (1D integer H). Some totals can't be assembled from the available partial bits.

3. **Pythagorean comma**: projecting fifths (base 3) onto octaves (base 2). Twelve fifths ≈ seven octaves but never exactly. The 1.4% residue is the comma.

4. **3-3-4 pattern**: projecting binary onto decimal. log₁₀(2) is irrational. Leading digits never exactly repeat.

5. **Vitali non-measurability**: projecting the real line's quotient by Q onto measurable sets. Some subsets can't be assigned a size.

## The atom

The irreducible error is always ±1 at the finest scale. At coarser scales it grows (±2, ±4, ...) but always in powers of 2. The error squares at each level (from x → x² - 2).

The atom is:
- The Cassini ±1
- The gap between b and a in Cayley coordinates (b - a = 1)
- The minimal distinction between two consecutive integers
- The quantization noise of the OCF
- The price of having an observer

## Information is not lost — it is split

Q(x) = (1+x)/(1-x) splits x into:
- **Visible**: the ratio Q = sameness/difference. The measured quantity.
- **Invisible**: the ±1 alternation. The residue of projection.

The visible is unbounded, structured, expressible. The invisible is minimal, alternating, irreducible. Together they reconstruct the original x completely. Nothing is lost. Everything is split.

The formal group F_h(x,y) = (x+y)/(1+xy) is the ALGEBRA of this splitting. The denominator 1+xy is the correction that keeps the result bounded — the price of staying in the observable world. Without it: unbounded addition, no splitting, no observation. With it: bounded composition, splitting, observation, error.

## The forbidden numbers are aliasing artifacts

7 = 111₂ looks achievable (all bits on). But it's a phantom created by sampling the cycle topology at insufficient resolution. Three intersecting cycles alias to four. The alias shifts the signal from 7 to 9. The "original" 7 was never in the signal.

21 = 10101₂ has an alternating bit pattern that requires skip-depth contributions the topology can't produce.

Both are quantization noise of the OCF's cycle-to-path converter.

## Why this is the same as the Vitali atom

The Vitali atom is an equivalence class under rational translation. You can't pick one representative measurably. The Cassini atom is the ±1 alternation. You can't make it zero. Both are saying: **the structure is finer than the measurement.**

The hexagonal lattice has more structure than the integers can see. The tournament topology has more structure than the H-value can see. The real line has more structure than Lebesgue measure can see. In each case, the excess structure manifests as an irreducible residue — the atom.

## The answer

The real problem is not the error. The error is the answer. It tells you: there's more here than you're seeing. The ±1 is a SIGNAL, not noise. It says: the structure you're projecting lives in a higher dimension than your coordinate system. To see it fully, you'd need to ascend the Cayley helix — to add the imaginary part, the twist, the phase that the real projection throws away.

The forbidden number 7 is not a defect in the H-spectrum. It is a SIGNAL that tournament topology lives in a higher-dimensional space (the hexagonal lattice, the Fano plane, the octonion imaginaries) that the 1D path count cannot fully resolve.

And the Cayley transform Q is not just a function. It is the ACT of choosing to project — to split the world into seen and unseen, measured and unmeasured, same and different. The atom ±1 is what's left over. It is always there. It is irreducible. It is the signature of the choice to observe.
