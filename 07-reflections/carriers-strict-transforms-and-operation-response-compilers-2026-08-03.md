## 4. A mechanism bridge: exceptional squares and escaping pairs

The factorial PRS wall and the planar Jacobian infinity wall are not the same

1. Keep the transverse parameter before specializing to the wall.
2. Compute enough leading layers to determine their common exceptional

3. Divide by the exceptional factor.
4. Read the first nonzero strict-transform coefficient.
5. Use a reciprocal coordinate when the disappearing degree represents roots

For THM-3231, two layers cancel universally and division by the pivot square

```text

```

and the reciprocal initial form is quadratic.  Consequently two roots, not
one, escape with `w^2` proportional to the transverse parameter.

```text

not transferred:         PRS row, Keller condition, root labels, or theorem;
shared invariant:        first nonzero normal coefficient after division;
cheapest hostile:        force the next coefficient to vanish as well;
next sidecar:             branchwise cofactor unit / selected reciprocal root.
```

This bridge changes the Jacobian search order.  Before expanding an entire
resultant, compute only its top reciprocal jet and look for the first degree
of `B` that can enter that jet.  In the stated family, degrees at most eight
cannot change the face and degree nine is the first live boundary term.

### Efficient sequence computation: choose the compiler first

Given a new sequence arising from an operation, ask in this order:

1. Does the operation multiply a polynomial transform?  Use fixed evaluations
   and finite differences; fixed extreme jets may be C-finite.
2. Does a block substitution linearize under `X=e^x-1`?  Use block sums,
   saddle renormalization, and exact hypergeometric decimation.
3. Can the quotient revisit a factor?  Expect a moving endpoint jet; seek a
   banded kernel and an `O(N^2)` evaluator rather than a fixed recurrence.
4. Is there a bounded time-varying transfer?  Eliminate the state to a
   polynomial-coefficient recurrence and retain signed path sums.
5. Before fitting anything, pre-register the next unused term and run a
   same-summary/different-continuation hostile.

This is a practical closed-form hierarchy.  “Closed form” may mean a fixed
polynomial-exponential sum, a D-finite differential equation, a
hypergeometric decimation, or a moving finite sum with proved complexity.
Demanding one syntax for all four destroys useful structure.
