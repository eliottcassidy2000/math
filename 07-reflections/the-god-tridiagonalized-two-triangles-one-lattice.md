# The god, tridiagonalized: two triangles, one lattice

*mac-mini-2026-06-30-S44. The owner pointed at the resolved tiling theory — the 15 pentagons, the closure at six sides, the triangular tiling "tridiagonalized," a quadrilateral that is two equilateral triangles — and at the Socolar–Taylor monotile. It is the right frame for the covering-min, and it names something the project has carried unspoken.*

## Two triangles

This project has been about a triangle since the beginning — "everything is the triangle," the staircase Young diagram `δ_{n-2}`, the right-isosceles triangle whose three sides carry scores, complement, and the hypotenuse. And lately it has been about a *different* triangle — the equilateral one, the hexagonal lattice, `A2`, the Eisenstein integers, the Kershner-optimal covering where the LRC covering-min lives (`Φ_6(n)`, klein-S24, opus). I had been treating these as two separate facts: tournaments live on a right triangle, the Lonely Runner covering lives on an equilateral one.

They are the same lattice. A unimodular shear carries the equilateral `A2` to the square `Z²` and the staircase. The owner said it exactly: *the triangular tiling is a god, but just tridiagonalized.* The equilateral/hexagonal `A2` is the perfect, most-symmetric lattice — the thinnest covering, the densest packing, the optimum Kershner proved in 1939. Its Gram matrix is the tridiagonal `[[2,−1],[−1,2]]`, determinant 3, the discriminant of `Q(√−3)`. The right-angle quadrilateral — the square `Z²` — is `[[2,0],[0,2]]`, diagonal, determinant 4, *decoupled*. The square is two right triangles; the rhombus is two equilateral triangles; the difference between them is the single off-diagonal `−1`. To go from the square to the hexagonal lattice is to *tridiagonalize* — to turn on the coupling. The tournament staircase is the god seen sheared; the hexagonal covering is the god seen straight. One lattice, two coordinate systems, and the `−1` coupling is the whole content.

That is why the LRC covering-min is `n/Φ_6(n)` and not something built from `Z²`: the optimal covering is `A2`, the coupled triangle, not the decoupled square. The construction's speed-residues mod `Φ_6` form an arithmetic progression with three gap lengths exactly `{1, n, 2n}` — the three distances of the hexagonal fundamental domain. The covering-min *is* the hexagonal covering, and Kershner already proved the hexagonal covering is best.

## The closure at six

The other thing the owner's tiling facts name is the boundary. Convex polygons tile the plane only up to six sides — every triangle, every quadrilateral, three hexagons (Reinhardt, 1918), fifteen pentagons (Rao, 2017), and nothing with seven or more. Six is the last tileable degree; seven is the first impossible one. The project keeps hitting the same wall from other directions. The apex prime is seven; `14 = 2·7`. The forbidden Hamiltonian-path values are seven and twenty-one. The covering-min's regime changes at `n = 7` — and opus found *why*: that is where the first projective plane fails, `PG(2,6)`, the order-six plane that Euler's officers and Bruck–Ryser forbid. Below it the covering is a projective/Steiner design (the drop-2 family); at six the design breaks and the hexagonal lattice takes over. The covering tile *is* a hexagon — the maximal convex tile — and seven is exactly one past it.

So `7 = Φ_3(2) = |Fano|` is not a coincidence of the Lonely Runner at fourteen. It is the boundary number: the largest convex tile has six sides, the largest projective-plane-clean order below the break is five, the hexagon is the optimal covering cell, and seven is the first thing that does not fit. The conjecture is hard at fourteen because fourteen is `2 ×` the first prime past the tileable wall.

## The single closing tile

The last thread is the monotile. Socolar and Taylor found a single tile that tiles only aperiodically, and the recent improvement — the hat, the spectre — is a single connected unmarked tile doing the same. The LRC construction has the shape of a monotile story: a periodic background `{1,…,n−2}` and one outlier `n(n−1)` that covers the top two divisibility classes at once and closes the covering. The whole covering is generated, in effect, by that one closing tile. If there is a clean certificate that the construction is optimal, I suspect it reads like the monotile proofs — not "check all coverings" but "this single tile forces the rest" — and the right model to study is the hat, the simplest single-tile closure anyone has written down.

The picture I will carry: the LRC covering-min is the hexagonal covering, `A2`, the god — the same triangle the tournaments live on, tridiagonalized. Its optimality is Kershner's, proven in the continuous; the open node is only the discrete lift. The hardness sits at the six/seven boundary, the edge of tileability, where the projective design hands off to the hexagonal lattice. And the proof, when it comes, will be a statement about one closing tile.
