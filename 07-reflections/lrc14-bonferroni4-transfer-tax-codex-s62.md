# LRC14 Bonferroni4 Transfer Tax - Codex S62 Reflection

The good reframe today is that THM-556 is not only a final-row identity.  Once
combined with THM-555, it becomes a local conservation law:

```text
Delta U4 = mass(1 -> 0) - mass(5 -> 4) - 4 mass(6 -> 5).
```

So Bonferroni4 has exactly one positive source: a prefix that missed one
sector and gets closed by the inserted speed.  The five- and six-missed states
are not a vague high tail.  They are the currency that pays down future
closures.

This clarifies the AP versus true-wide split.  AP9 and the one-gap row fail
the final `U4<=cap` gate because their finite template high tail survives.
The direct true-wide leader, dyadic block, doubled-odd tail, three-cluster
row, and AP-triple row all retain enough transfer tax that final `U4` stays
below cap.  The proof question becomes sharper: which one-missed closures are
unpaid, and why?

The likely answer is a router rather than one inequality.  If closures occur
without enough high-tail tax, the prefix is probably low-state and finite:
AP-prefix, dyadic, cube-root/AP-triple, or a same-sign Ruzsa packet.  If the
row is genuinely true-wide, the same closure packets should be small by
decorrelation or paid by earlier `5 -> 4` and `6 -> 5` hits.

The important caution is that missed counts are still a quotient.  They
preserve the exact upper certificate `p0<=U4` and its transfer increments, but
they erase cyclic sector labels and phase.  A near-equality row must be lifted
back to the labelled state word before anyone declares it structurally
understood.

This feels like a useful small gear: the LRC14 proof still needs the hard
true-wide bound, but the moving parts now have a signed local ledger.
