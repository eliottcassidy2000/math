# Address-Coordinate Derivative Repair (S665)

Prompt: search the web and repo for the pattern

```text
find a scalar/product collision
attach the missing fraction/address coordinate
unroll it back into a derivative sum
```

S665 treats this as the local, operational version of HYP-2239's
`sum -> product -> fraction -> sum` loop.  The useful correction is that the
fraction face is not decorative.  It is the derivative variable: the retained
address coordinate that says which lift, owner, branch, card, pin, model, or
activity state the scalar shadow came from.

## Core Pattern

A scalar/product collision happens when a quotient forgets assignment data:

- a deck is known but the deleted boundary value is not paired to the card;
- an Euler product is known but the zero/gamma/test-function address is missing;
- a trace/norm pair is known but the branch sheet is missing;
- a determinant/eigenvalue product is known but the adjugate direction is
  missing;
- a Fourier intensity or distance multiset is known but the phase/support or
  pair/end owner is missing;
- a frequency vector is known but the set-pressure owner is missing;
- a full distance support is known but the pinned/concurrency owner is missing;
- a `Res_27` LRC shadow is known but the carry/owner state is missing.

The address coordinate repairs the quotient by making the object differentiable
in the right calculus.  Differentiating then produces a sum ledger: log
derivative prime sums, closed-orbit trace sums, deletion-loss decks,
Kocay-style covering equations, Weyl `m`-function expansions, J-fraction moment
recurrences, pressure-loss sums, or carry-deletion ledgers.

In short:

```text
collision = same visible product/scalar
address   = missing denominator/fraction/lift coordinate
derivative sum = local loss/trace/moment/owner ledger after perturbation
```

## Best Exact Repo Cases

**LRC14 carry state.**  S663 already showed that the additive/product
`Res_27` shadow mixes floor and strict rows, while the full carry-continuant
state has zero mixed groups.  S665 reframes the next theorem as an owner
derivative statement: fixed odd-wall packets and `C=27` product shell should be
paired with carry/owner/deletion losses.

**Tournament decks.**  S660 showed the cleanest finite prototype.  Full decks,
Kelly counts, and global scalar repairs collide through `n=6`; paired
`(card isomorphism type, deleted vertex outdegree)` separates every checked
class.  The derivative is not "deleted score" as a multiset; it is deleted
score attached to the card.

**Finite Vieta branch lab.**  Over `F_17`, the map `(x,y) -> (x+y,xy)` has
maximum fiber `2`; adding the branch coordinate `x-y` makes every fiber
singleton.  Newton power sums then give the additive readout of the two-sheet
collision.

**Goldbach/Lemoine pair plane.**  The even/odd columns
`E=p+q`, `O=p+2q` reconstruct the address by `q=O-E`, `p=2E-O`.  This is a
literal scalar-pair collision repair: the address is the prime-pair coordinate,
and the duplicate diagonal `p -> (2p,3p)` is a fixed-address ray.

**Finite-field Kakeya/Falconer.**  Distance support saturates too early.
Pinned minima, union size, concurrency, and line-owner state are the derivative
coordinates.  This is the finite-incidence analogue of the LRC no-leak owner
program.

**Union-closed pressure.**  Frequency vectors collide at small size; pressure
and pressure-loss coordinates split the fibers.  The derivative is the effect
of adding/deleting a set, not another global frequency scalar.

**A000568 marked observer fiber.**  Unmarked tournament classes are too coarse
for LRC predicates.  Root, threshold, source, and endpoint addresses are the
missing coordinates; deletion/source/gap-threshold sums are the derivatives.

**Determinant and turnpike toys.**  The merged finite labs add two sharp
address witnesses: `diag(2,6)` and `diag(3,4)` have determinant `12` but
different `E11` derivatives (`6` versus `4`), and the point sets
`(0,1,2,6,8,11)` and `(0,1,6,7,9,11)` share the same distance multiset while
their coordinate sums and adjacent gaps differ.  Product/distance shadows are
not enough; adjugate and pair-owner addresses split them.

## External Anchors

The web scan produced theorem-shaped versions of the same grammar:

- Graph reconstruction: deck collisions are attacked by Kocay covering-number
  matrices and by reconstructible vertex-pair parameters.
- Explicit formulae: Euler products become prime-power and zero sums after log
  differentiation once zeros/gamma/test functions are in the address.
- Jacobi determinant formula: determinant products become cofactor/trace sums
  once the adjugate address is retained.
- Sparse phase retrieval: Fourier magnitude/autocorrelation data needs phase
  and support-difference addresses before Prony reconstruction works.
- Turnpike/beltway reconstruction: unassigned distance data needs pair/end
  addresses before placement sums are meaningful.
- Ihara/Selberg: determinant/product zeta data unrolls into primitive
  closed-orbit or geodesic sums.
- Stieltjes and J-fraction theory: moments/Hankel data become reconstructive
  once continued-fraction coefficients are retained.
- Weyl inverse spectral theory: spectral products need the `m`-function or
  spectral-measure address before the potential can be recovered.
- Tutte/activity and matroid Hodge theory: scalar polynomial coefficients are
  repaired by activity orders or Chow-ring/intersection addresses.

These are not proofs of repo conjectures.  They are carrier templates: each
one tells us which missing coordinate historically made a scalar shadow
recoverable.

## Assumption Challenge

For LRC/Tournament Analysis, vertices were not assumed to be runners or arcs.
Candidate vertices included repair lanes, formulas, files, graph cards, deleted
vertices, residues, roots, eigenvalues, moments, bases, models, proof
obligations, pins, sets, and activity marks.  The chosen quotient preserves the
predicate "which address coordinate repairs a collision and yields a derivative
ledger?"  It deliberately destroys raw object identity so the transfer pattern
can be compared across domains.
