## 0. What is proved, what is open, what the sentence really asks

THM-1370 proves that no tournament has exactly 7 or exactly 21 Hamiltonian paths (and MISTAKE-680 records that MathOverflow had it in 2016). THM-4744 proves that below 625 the strong-unattainable values are exactly 1, 7, 21. What is OPEN is the converse: that every other odd number occurs (Chan–Pak, Conjecture 6.2; MISTAKE-559 forbids writing it as a theorem). The repository's constructions already reach far beyond the literature: the digital bank and the strong-ear banks (THM-4097/4102/4104, HYP-9029) give every odd number up to 99,999,999 except 7 and 21 (`square_sum_spectrum_overnight_20261031d`, same-agent review; up to 999,999 with an independent audit, `session124_million_path_spectrum_20261031`). So "prove that {7, 21} are *the* forbidden values" means: prove completeness. Three facts sharpen it.

- **Primes and the 7-lane suffice.** `H` is multiplicative over the strong components of the condensation (THM-1862: `H(T₁ ▷ T₂) = H(T₁) H(T₂)`), so the attained set is the multiplicative monoid generated by the strong values, and THM-4094 (Theorem 6.1) gives the exact reduction: **completeness holds iff every odd prime `p ≠ 7` and every `7p` (`p` an odd prime `≠ 3`) is a strong value**, the carry atoms `49, 63, 343` being attained already. (A first draft here said "primes suffice"; that forgets that `7p` has no factorisation through attained values because 7 itself is missing.)
- **Level.** The statement is `Π⁰₁`, not `Π⁰₂`: a witness for `h`, if one exists, has at most `⌊3 log₃ h⌋` vertices (strong floors `f(m) ≥ 3^{(m−1)/2}` bound each strong component and the number of components; `rotation_owners_spectrum_20261020e/spectrum.md` §4, `camion_busch_gaps_polyhedra_collatz_20261002.md` §4), so "h is attained" is a bounded search and completeness is "no counterexample `h`". In the dictionary of THM-4860's packet this puts Chan–Pak in the Goldbach row: a FRACTRAN program in the style of THM-4856 that enumerates odd `h` and halts when the bounded search fails is a "halts iff counterexample" machine for it. The `Π⁰₂` analogue (every prime has a square-sum partner) is therefore *one level harder* than the spectrum question, which is the opposite of what the surface resemblance suggests.
- **No small additive rung.** Session134 (`session134_constructive_spectrum_frontier_20261101/descent`) proves that an induced inclusion with `H`-gain 2 occurs only at `(1, 3)` and `(3, 5)`, gain 4 only at `(1, 5)` and `(5, 9)`, and `2H(A) ≤ (gain + 1)!`; there is no literal `h ↦ 2h + 1` step. Any construction that sweeps must do so with increments that grow with the base, as the banks and the near-join below do.

## 1. The three themes as analogies

| prime square-sum graph (THM-4860) | Hamiltonian-path spectrum |
|---|---|
| a prime `p` has a *partner* `q` with `p + q` square | an odd `h` has a *realiser* `T` with `H(T) = h` |
| the family `s² − p` is sparse (density `1/√x`) but conjecturally hits a prime for every `p` | the family of counts at order `n` has `≤ A038375(n)` members and is dense: at order 9 it contains every odd number up to 2,881 except 7 and 21 |
| **admissible bounds** `N` exist only in Chebyshev windows; the first window is killed by isolated and leaf primes, the second (615,731 … 634,199) admits a solution | at a fixed order the attained odd values form an initial segment up to a **top band** (gaps start at 35/45, 161/189, 611/661, 2883/3357), and the strong values at consecutive orders form overlapping windows `[c_n, d_n]` with `c_{n+1} ≤ d_n` — HYP-9029's tiling law, OPEN, with data `[65,105], [69,609], [85,2881], [249,14649], [429,80265]` for orders 7–11; the Chebyshev windows are the arithmetic twin of this tiling, and "infinitely many admissible bounds" (HYP-9451) is the twin of "the tiling never breaks" |
| a leaf prime (unique partner) forces an endpoint and kills every bias-1 bound below 700,000 | the **rigid values** 11 and 13 are realised by exactly one strong tournament each (THM-4810: the strong fibre is a singleton exactly at 3, 5, 11, 13; through order 9 the only other singletons lie in the top band), 47 and 67 by two and six strong tournaments through order 9, and none of the four by a tournament of the near-join form of §2 with a base of order `≤ 7`; a leaf-like rigidity, not an obstruction |
| residue classes mod 4 and mod 36 decide which bounds can work | the 2-adic digits of `H` (OCF, THM-002: `H = Σ α_k 2^k` with `α_k` the number of `k`-sets of vertex-disjoint directed odd cycles; THM-1980: the characteristic polynomial fixes `H mod 4` through order 6 and only parity from order 7) are a reformulation, not a mechanism: `H = 7` iff the odd-cycle graph `Ω` is `K_3`, excluded by THM-343, and the real reason for both exclusions is multiplicativity plus the Moon–Busch crevasses `7 ∈ (5, 9)`, `21 ∈ (15, 25)` (THM-1370); no 2-adic proof exists |
| the exact obstruction was a flow (2-matching) with a Hall certificate | the exact obstruction at 7 and 21 is the finite strong spectrum through order 8 plus multiplicativity and Moon's floor; there is no analogue of the flow because no global linear structure is known for `H` |

The analogy says where to look and what to fear. Where the repository already looks: the digital bank is a *numeration* (a bit word drives the state `(h, F, q, t)` by `(h, F, 2q + h, 0)` for a 1 and `(h, F + q + h − t, 2q, h)` for a 0), the strong-ear banks are *insertions with computable increments* (`H(T + x_S) = H(T) + cut_w(S) + Σ_{i∈S} h_i`, THM-4097), and THM-4748's two-reversal formulas give the lines `3·2^j − 1`, `5·2^j − 1`, `2^k − 9`. Where to look: a *construction with a free parameter whose count sweeps an interval*, the analogue of the second Chebyshev window; what to fear: a *covering obstruction*. Erdős proved (1950) that the odd numbers not of the form `p + 2^k` contain whole residue classes (a covering system with moduli 2, 3, 4, 8, 12, 24; Sierpiński and Riesel numbers are the same phenomenon), so a spectrum built from finitely many "shapes" `a + 2^k b` can miss an arithmetic progression forever. The Hamiltonian-path spectrum is built from infinitely many shapes, and the computed density of missed values decays (§2), so the fear is only that a *proof* restricted to a finite family of constructions cannot succeed.

## 2. A construction that sweeps: the near-join with one reversed arc

Take any tournament `T`, a vertex `t₀ ∈ T`, a transitive chain `M = m₁ → … → m_k` (`k ≥ 1`) and a vertex `z`; orient `T → M → z` (every arc from an earlier block to a later one) and reverse the single arc `z → t₀`. Writing `h(U)` for Hamiltonian paths of `U` and `h_t(U)` for those starting at `t`,

> **H(T′) = h(T) + 2^k · B(T, t₀)**, where **B(T, t₀) = Σ_{S ⊆ V(T) − t₀} h(T[S]) · h_{t₀}(T[S^c ∪ {t₀}])**

(proof: a Hamiltonian path either never uses `z → t₀`, and is then a path of `T` followed by `M` and `z`, or uses it exactly once, and is then a path on a subset `S` of `T − t₀`, a subset of `M` in increasing order, `z`, `t₀`, a path of `T[S^c ∪ t₀]` from `t₀`, and the rest of `M` in increasing order; the two `M`-subsets are arbitrary, giving `2^k`). Verified on every tournament of order `≤ 5` and `k ≤ 2`. Two corollaries: `B(T, t₀) ≥ h(T)` (sink: `B = h(T)`, giving the join `T → E_{k+2}`, multiplicative), and **`B(T′, z) = H(T′)`** (verified on all 1,260 cases of order `≤ 6`, `k ≤ 3`), so the construction can be iterated at `z`; iterating at `t₀` is *not* a function of `(h, B, k)` alone (`B(T′, t₀)` takes the values 45 and 47 on two tournaments with the same `(3, 12, 1)`), so the pair `(h, B)` is not a closed state and the hoped-for two-variable numeration — the analogue of the `×49 ± 4` nesting that proved the square-sum theorem (session124, THM-4850) and of the reverse-Collatz maps `x ↦ 2^k x + 1` that generate every odd number — does not close on two integers.

What the family does reach ([`nearjoin.py`](../05-knowledge/results/spectrum_analogies_nearjoin_20261102/scripts/nearjoin.py)): with base tournaments of order `≤ 7` (2,378 distinct pairs `(h, B)`), the values `h + 2^k B` cover every odd number up to 1,437 except `1, 7, 11, 13, 21, 47, 67`; even the single doubling step `k = 1` covers every odd number up to 400 except those seven. Above that the family thins out (a geometric progression per pair), to about 60 % of the odd numbers below `10^5`, and products with attained atoms raise this to 87 %. The seven exceptions are the two forbidden values and five values outside the family's reach (1 trivially, and 11, 13, 47, 67). So the family is "complete up to its base" — exactly the shape an inductive proof needs: *if every odd `h < X` is attained with enough realisers that the sets `{B(T, t) : H(T) = h}` are dense, then `h + 2B` sweeps `(X, 3X)`.* The `B`-sets computed for `h ≤ 31` have 14–42 members each and always contain `h`; what is missing is a theorem that they are dense, i.e. control of `B` from the structure of `T` — the same gap as controlling the square-sum partner set `{s² − p}` from `p`.

## 3. Four more past concepts, typed

- **FRACTRAN and halting (THM-4852, THM-4856).** The Goldbach-type programs halt iff a counterexample exists because the statement is `Π⁰₁`; so is completeness (§0), with the bounded search over orders `≤ ⌊3 log₃ h⌋` as the decidable test. A COMPLETENESSGAME in THM-4856's style is therefore available: enumerate odd `h`, run the bounded search, halt on the first failure. The `Π⁰₂` statements of HYP-9451 (every prime has a partner; infinitely many admissible bounds) have no such program; Collatz-type totality is attacked through *invariant densities and covering of residue classes*, which is the §1 fear in reverse.
- **Numeration and self-similarity (THM-4850, session124).** The square-sum theorem for all `N ≥ 32` was proved by a prefix that scales by 49 and shifts by `± 4`; the spectrum analogue would be an operation `T ↦ T′` with `H(T′) = a·H(T) + b` for *fixed* `(a, b)` with `b ≠ 0` and `a` even — the near-join gives `a = 1`, `b = 2^k B(T, t₀)`, with `b` depending on `T`. The strong floors `f(m) = 3^a 5^b` (THM-1370) are the multiplicative skeleton; what completeness needs is an *additive* rung.
- **Chebyshev windows (THM-4860 (d)).** The admissible bounds are rare because two independent fluctuations (mod 4 and mod 36 classes) must align. The spectrum's "band" is the opposite: at every order the attainable set is an interval minus a thin top; the analogue of "infinitely many windows" is HYP-9029's tiling law, open but holding through order 11. The real analogue of a window is the *order* at which a rigid value appears: 11 and 13 need order 5, 47 and 67 need order 7, and the near-join family never produces them; a proof of completeness must therefore either produce rigid values by a second construction or show that rigidity stops (every odd `h ≥ 69` has a near-join realiser — true for `69 ≤ h ≤ 1437` by the census).
- **Leaf primes (THM-4860 (e′)).** A leaf is a vertex with a unique partner; it is forced to be an endpoint and so it decides parity-type conditions. The rigid values are the leaves of the spectrum: 11 and 13 are each the count of exactly one strong tournament (THM-4810), and their 2-adic digits are those of `7 + 4` and `7 + 6`. A conjecture, checked here through order 9 (HYP-9453 (3)): **every attained odd `h ≥ 15` below the top band is the count of at least two strong tournaments** (the strong singletons through order 9 are exactly 3, 5, 11, 13 and twenty-five values in the band above 2,881; 47 has two strong realisers and 67 six).

## 4. {4, 11} and {4, 13}

The directive's two pairs were tested against every count spectrum at hand, in case they named a sibling of {7, 21}: the directed Hamiltonian-cycle counts `C(T)` (orders 3–9: no missing value at all below the maximum, except a top band from 16 at order 7 and from 215 at order 9; 4, 11, 13 all occur at order 8), the counts of Hamiltonian paths from a fixed start vertex (orders `≤ 8`: no gap below 140), the 3-cycle counts (every value from 0 to the maximum at every order `≤ 9`), and Hamiltonian paths of bipartite tournaments on `≤ 7` vertices (4 occurs; 9, 10, 11 are the first missing values). None of these spectra forbids 4 together with 11 or 13. If the pairs were meant as moduli (`H mod 4` with `H mod 11` or `13`), the known structure is THM-1980's: the spectrum determines `H mod 4` only through order 6; 7 and 21 are `3` and `1 (mod 4)`, `7` and `10 (mod 11)`, `7` and `8 (mod 13)`, with nothing distinguished. The referent should be named before more is computed.
