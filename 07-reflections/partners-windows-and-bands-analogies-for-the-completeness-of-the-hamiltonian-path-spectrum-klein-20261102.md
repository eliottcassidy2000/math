# Partners, windows and bands: analogies for the completeness of the Hamiltonian-path spectrum of tournaments

## 0. What is proved, what is open, what the sentence really asks

THM-1370 proves that no tournament has exactly 7 or exactly 21 Hamiltonian paths (and MISTAKE-680 records that MathOverflow had it in 2016). THM-4744 proves that below 625 the strong-unattainable values are exactly 1, 7, 21. What is OPEN is the converse: that every other odd number occurs (Chan–Pak, Conjecture 6.2; Royle below 80,557; strong witnesses for every odd value in `[65, 80265]`, HYP-9029 / THM-4102 / THM-4104; MISTAKE-559 forbids writing it as a theorem). So "prove that {7, 21} are *the* forbidden values" means: prove completeness. Two reductions sharpen it.

- **Primes suffice.** `H` is multiplicative over the strong components of the condensation (every Hamiltonian path crosses the components in their transitive order; THM-1862/1936's join-closure). Hence the attained set is closed under products, and **completeness is equivalent to: every odd prime `p ≠ 7` is `H(T)` for some tournament** (then every odd number other than 7 and 21 is a product of attained primes, 21 = 3·7 being the only odd composite whose every factorisation needs 7 — 49, 35, 63, … are attained directly at order 7 or 8).
- **Level.** "For every odd prime `p ≠ 7` there is a tournament with `H = p`" is `∀p ∃T`, a `Π⁰₂` sentence, exactly the level of "every prime `p` has a prime `q` with `p + q` a square" (HYP-9451 (2)) and of Collatz, and one level above the `Π⁰₁` prime conjectures that THM-4856 turns into halting programs. No single "halts iff counterexample" machine exists for it; a proof must be a construction or a covering argument.

## 1. The three themes as analogies

| prime square-sum graph (THM-4860) | Hamiltonian-path spectrum |
|---|---|
| a prime `p` has a *partner* `q` with `p + q` square | an odd `h` has a *realiser* `T` with `H(T) = h` |
| the family `s² − p` is sparse (density `1/√x`) but conjecturally hits a prime for every `p` | the family of counts at order `n` has `≤ A038375(n)` members and is dense: at order 9 it contains every odd number up to 2,881 except 7 and 21 |
| **admissible bounds** `N` exist only in Chebyshev windows; the first window is killed by isolated and leaf primes, the second (615,731 … 634,199) admits a solution | at a fixed order the attained odd values form an initial segment up to a **top band** (gaps start at 35/45, 161/189, 611/661, 2883/3357); whether a value `h` is attained at order `n` is a "window" question in `n` with the answer `n ≥ n(h)`, and `n(h)` equals the first order whose maximum exceeds `h` except inside the band (THM-4861, HYP-9452) |
| a leaf prime (unique partner) forces an endpoint and kills every bias-1 bound below 700,000 | the **rigid values** 11 and 13 are realised by exactly one strong tournament each (THM-4810: the strong fibre is a singleton exactly at 3, 5, 11, 13; through order 9 the only other singletons lie in the top band), 47 and 67 by two and six strong tournaments through order 9, and none of the four by a tournament of the near-join form of §2 with a base of order `≤ 7`; a leaf-like rigidity, not an obstruction |
| residue classes mod 4 and mod 36 decide which bounds can work | the 2-adic digits of `H` (OCF, THM-002: `H = 1 + 2α₁ + 4α₂ + …`; THM-1980: the spectrum fixes `H mod 4` through order 6 and only parity from order 7) decide which small values can work: `7 = 1 + 2 + 4` and `21 = 1 + 4 + 16` fail |
| the exact obstruction was a flow (2-matching) with a Hall certificate | the exact obstruction at 7 and 21 is the finite strong spectrum through order 8 plus multiplicativity and Moon's floor; there is no analogue of the flow because no global linear structure is known for `H` |

The analogy says where to look and what to fear. Where to look: a *construction with a free parameter whose count sweeps an interval*, the analogue of the second Chebyshev window; what to fear: a *covering obstruction*. Erdős proved (1950) that the odd numbers not of the form `p + 2^k` contain whole residue classes (a covering system with moduli 2, 3, 4, 8, 12, 24; Sierpiński and Riesel numbers are the same phenomenon), so a spectrum built from finitely many "shapes" `a + 2^k b` can miss an arithmetic progression forever. The Hamiltonian-path spectrum is built from infinitely many shapes, and the computed density of missed values decays (§2), so the fear is only that a *proof* restricted to a finite family of constructions cannot succeed.

## 2. A construction that sweeps: the near-join with one reversed arc

Take any tournament `T`, a vertex `t₀ ∈ T`, a transitive chain `M = m₁ → … → m_k` (`k ≥ 1`) and a vertex `z`; orient `T → M → z` (every arc from an earlier block to a later one) and reverse the single arc `z → t₀`. Writing `h(U)` for Hamiltonian paths of `U` and `h_t(U)` for those starting at `t`,

> **H(T′) = h(T) + 2^k · B(T, t₀)**, where **B(T, t₀) = Σ_{S ⊆ V(T) − t₀} h(T[S]) · h_{t₀}(T[S^c ∪ {t₀}])**

(proof: a Hamiltonian path either never uses `z → t₀`, and is then a path of `T` followed by `M` and `z`, or uses it exactly once, and is then a path on a subset `S` of `T − t₀`, a subset of `M` in increasing order, `z`, `t₀`, a path of `T[S^c ∪ t₀]` from `t₀`, and the rest of `M` in increasing order; the two `M`-subsets are arbitrary, giving `2^k`). Verified on every tournament of order `≤ 5` and `k ≤ 2`. Two corollaries: `B(T, t₀) ≥ h(T)` (sink: `B = h(T)`, giving the join `T → E_{k+2}`, multiplicative), and **`B(T′, z) = H(T′)`** (verified on all 1,260 cases of order `≤ 6`, `k ≤ 3`), so the construction can be iterated at `z`; iterating at `t₀` is *not* a function of `(h, B, k)` alone (`B(T′, t₀)` takes the values 45 and 47 on two tournaments with the same `(3, 12, 1)`), so the pair `(h, B)` is not a closed state and the hoped-for two-variable numeration — the analogue of the `×49 ± 4` nesting that proved the square-sum theorem (session124, THM-4850) and of the reverse-Collatz maps `x ↦ 2^k x + 1` that generate every odd number — does not close on two integers.

What the family does reach ([`nearjoin.py`](../05-knowledge/results/spectrum_analogies_nearjoin_20261102/scripts/nearjoin.py)): with base tournaments of order `≤ 7` (2,378 distinct pairs `(h, B)`), the values `h + 2^k B` cover every odd number up to 1,437 except `1, 7, 11, 13, 21, 47, 67`; even the single doubling step `k = 1` covers every odd number up to 400 except those seven. Above that the family thins out (a geometric progression per pair), to about 60 % of the odd numbers below `10^5`, and products with attained atoms raise this to 87 %. The seven exceptions are the two forbidden values and five values outside the family's reach (1 trivially, and 11, 13, 47, 67). So the family is "complete up to its base" — exactly the shape an inductive proof needs: *if every odd `h < X` is attained with enough realisers that the sets `{B(T, t) : H(T) = h}` are dense, then `h + 2B` sweeps `(X, 3X)`.* The `B`-sets computed for `h ≤ 31` have 14–42 members each and always contain `h`; what is missing is a theorem that they are dense, i.e. control of `B` from the structure of `T` — the same gap as controlling the square-sum partner set `{s² − p}` from `p`.

## 3. Four more past concepts, typed

- **FRACTRAN and halting (THM-4852, THM-4856).** The Goldbach-type programs halt iff a counterexample exists because the statement is `Π⁰₁`; completeness of the spectrum is `Π⁰₂`, so the honest machine form is "the search `T ↦ H(T)` is total onto the odd numbers minus {7, 21}" — a totality question, like Collatz. Inspiration: Collatz-type totality is attacked through *invariant densities and covering of residue classes*, which is the §1 fear in reverse.
- **Numeration and self-similarity (THM-4850, session124).** The square-sum theorem for all `N ≥ 32` was proved by a prefix that scales by 49 and shifts by `± 4`; the spectrum analogue would be an operation `T ↦ T′` with `H(T′) = a·H(T) + b` for *fixed* `(a, b)` with `b ≠ 0` and `a` even — the near-join gives `a = 1`, `b = 2^k B(T, t₀)`, with `b` depending on `T`. The strong floors `f(m) = 3^a 5^b` (THM-1370) are the multiplicative skeleton; what completeness needs is an *additive* rung.
- **Chebyshev windows (THM-4860 (d)).** The admissible bounds are rare because two independent fluctuations (mod 4 and mod 36 classes) must align. The spectrum's "band" is the opposite: at every order the attainable set is an interval minus a thin top; the analogue of "infinitely many windows" is trivially true (monotonicity in `n`). The real analogue of a window is the *order* at which a rigid value appears: 11 and 13 need order 5, 47 and 67 need order 7, and the near-join family never produces them; a proof of completeness must therefore either produce rigid values by a second construction or show that rigidity stops (every odd `h ≥ 69` has a near-join realiser — true for `69 ≤ h ≤ 1437` by the census).
- **Leaf primes (THM-4860 (e′)).** A leaf is a vertex with a unique partner; it is forced to be an endpoint and so it decides parity-type conditions. The rigid values are the leaves of the spectrum: 11 and 13 are each the count of exactly one strong tournament (THM-4810), and their 2-adic digits are those of `7 + 4` and `7 + 6`. A conjecture, checked here through order 9 (HYP-9453 (3)): **every attained odd `h ≥ 15` below the top band is the count of at least two strong tournaments** (the strong singletons through order 9 are exactly 3, 5, 11, 13 and twenty-five values in the band above 2,881; 47 has two strong realisers and 67 six).

## 4. {4, 11} and {4, 13}

The directive's two pairs were tested against every count spectrum at hand, in case they named a sibling of {7, 21}: the directed Hamiltonian-cycle counts `C(T)` (orders 3–9: no missing value at all below the maximum, except a top band from 16 at order 7 and from 215 at order 9; 4, 11, 13 all occur at order 8), the counts of Hamiltonian paths from a fixed start vertex (orders `≤ 8`: no gap below 140), the 3-cycle counts (every value from 0 to the maximum at every order `≤ 9`), and Hamiltonian paths of bipartite tournaments on `≤ 7` vertices (4 occurs; 9, 10, 11 are the first missing values). None of these spectra forbids 4 together with 11 or 13. If the pairs were meant as moduli (`H mod 4` with `H mod 11` or `13`), the known structure is THM-1980's: the spectrum determines `H mod 4` only through order 6; 7 and 21 are `3` and `1 (mod 4)`, `7` and `10 (mod 11)`, `7` and `8 (mod 13)`, with nothing distinguished. The referent should be named before more is computed.
