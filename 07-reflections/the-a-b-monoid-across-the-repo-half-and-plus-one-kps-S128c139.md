# The a/b monoid across the repo: ½ and +1 are two generators, and much of the math is one thing

*kind-pasteur-2026-07-21-S128c139. Owner: apply the function-composition themes (a(x)=x+1,
b(x)=x/2) to more of the repo; see how much is almost the same under the surface. Companion to
THM-1880 (the a/b frame at the transitive tournament) and THM-1555 (the half-dictionary b∘a).*

Two maps: **`a(x) = x+1`** (shift / add a vertex / add a runner) and **`b(x) = x/2`** (halve / the
½). Their composite **`b∘a = (x+1)/2`** is the half-dictionary. The claim of this note: the ½ and
the +1 that recur in nearly every thread are *these two generators*, and threads that look
unrelated are the monoid `⟨a,b⟩` acting on different objects. Some appearances are exact
(verified), some are structural (worth the reframe); both are flagged.

## Exact (verified)

1. **Tournament skew form (THM-1880, this session).** `char_S(transitive) = b(aⁿ + āⁿ) =
   ((x+1)ⁿ+(x−1)ⁿ)/2`, with `E_n²−O_n² = (x²−1)ⁿ` (Pell) and the cot spectrum. `char_A = xⁿ`. The
   two GIT poles are `b`-of-`aⁿ` (transitive, spread cot ladder) and `b`-of-a-constant `(x²+p)^m`
   (Paley, Gauss sum, `var(λ²)=0`). One even form, two ends of stability.

2. **The half-dictionary (THM-1555).** `A = b(a(x)·…)` — literally `A = (J−I+S)/2 = b∘a` on the
   entries: the `{−1,0,1}` skew world and the `{0,½,1}` adjacency world are `b∘a` and its inverse
   `2x−1`. The residue is the `½` because `(1+0)/2 = ½`.

3. **The GMC / Legendre bridge (THM-1620).** The toral generating function is
   `(1 − 2xt + t²)^{−1/2}` — the exponent is `−b(1) = −½`, and the `(½)_{n−2}` Pochhammer that
   drives the tiling **fiber fraction** `(½)_{n−2}/(n−2)!` is `b` iterated. The Hermite/Legendre
   coefficients carry `(−½)ᵏ`. The GMC charge condition straddles the **Newton midpoint `d/2` =
   `b(d)`** (THM-1540/1555). All the `½`s here are one `b`.

4. **The staircase's two reductions (CLAUDE.md).** **Mode A** `n → n−1` is `a⁻¹` (remove the
   hypotenuse = one vertex); **Mode B** `n → n−2` is `a⁻²`. The recursion of the metagraph is the
   monoid stepping down. The triangular arc-count `T_{n−1} = C(n,2)` is the subleading coefficient
   of `E_n` (THM-1880) — so "triangular numbers built from `a` and `b`" is exact.

## Structural (the reframe that clarifies)

5. **LRC.** The gap threshold is `1/(2n) = b(1/n)`; the runner count is `n+1 = a(n)`; the tight
   system is `{1,…,n−1}` and its Farey/Stern-Brocot fractions are generated by `a = x+1` (the
   modular parabolic `T`) and the mediant. The "for all `t`, some `‖vt‖ ≥ b(1/n)`" is the `b` of the
   naive `1/n`. Reading LRC(14)'s `1/7`, `1/13`, `2/25` as `b`/`a`-words on the Farey tree is the
   suggested lens (the wall-quintic and avoidance-polynomial threads live here).

6. **Farey / Stern–Brocot / modular.** `a = x+1 = T`, the parabolic generator of `PSL(2,ℤ)`; the
   Farey thread (THM-826 + 9 others) is `⟨a, S⟩` with `S: x ↦ −1/x`, and `b` is the dyadic scaling
   that makes the *balanced* (rather than mediant) tree. The Pisano-period / "1001 = three sixties"
   motif (A001175) is the order structure of `a` mod something.

7. **The complexity ladder (THM-1775/1810).** The three functionals — trace, `CT`, Gaussian `E` —
   are `⟨a,b⟩` at different depths: the rational floor is `char_A` (pure `a`-shift of the spectrum),
   the algebraic rung is the `b`-exponent Legendre GF, the holonomic rung is the `b`-Laplace of the
   diagonal. "Spectral" = a function of the `a`-shifted trace moments; the `#P` jump (H) is where
   the monoid stops describing it (THM-1780/1870).

## The one-line thesis

> **`a` adds structure (a vertex, a runner, a shift); `b` averages/halves it (the fiber fraction,
> the midpoint, the `Re=−½` line); `b∘a` is the coordinate change between the sign world `{−1,0,1}`
> and the affine world `{0,½,1}`.** Most of the repo's constants are `a`- and `b`-words, and threads
> that look separate — tournament spectra, GMC/Legendre, tiling reductions, LRC thresholds,
> Farey — are the monoid `⟨a,b⟩` acting on their own object. The `½` is never a coincidence; it is
> `b`.
