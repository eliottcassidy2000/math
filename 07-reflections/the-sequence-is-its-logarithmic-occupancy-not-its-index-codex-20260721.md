# The sequence is its logarithmic occupancy, not its index

The reciprocal-sequence prompt exposed a basic ambiguity that had propagated
through several otherwise useful atlases.  If an integer sequence is literally
a subset of the harmonic numbers, then its object is not the indexed word
`a_1,a_2,...`.  It is the counting measure

```text
mu_A=sum_(a in support) delta_a,
```

together with an optional multiplicity sidecar.  Reindexing and repetition are
gauge.  The collision tax measures exactly what that quotient forgets.

This single change aligns three previously separate viewpoints:

- Abel--Stieltjes reads the cumulative distribution `A(x)`.
- Dyadic Dini blocks read relative occupancy at logarithmic scale.
- The heat trace and support Dirichlet series are the Laplace and Mellin
  transforms of the same atomic measure.

The scalar reciprocal mass is only the first moment of this object.  The
simplex/power duality makes that limitation vivid: `d`-simplex numbers and
powers of `d` both have mass `d/(d-1)`, despite polynomial versus exponential
log-block profiles.  Equal mass is not equal geometry.  A faithful sequence
fingerprint should therefore retain at least the block-occupancy vector (or
heat trace), not only its integral.

There is an unexpectedly literal second Kakeya bridge.  After turning an
integer sequence into reciprocal atoms, take all subseries of those atoms.
The resulting achievement set is controlled by whether each atom exceeds the
remaining tail.  The simplex and power supports have the same mass
`k/(k-1)`, yet for `k>=3` the simplex achievement set is exactly `2^(k-2)`
closed intervals while powers of `k` give a Cantor set of dimension
`log_k(2)`.  At `k=2` both exceptional geometries fill `[0,2]`: every real
width between zero and two is a subseries of triangular reciprocals, and also
of binary reciprocals.  Thus the total mass can forget even connectedness.
Kakeya's partial-sum problem and the Kakeya needle-width analogy are not the
same theorem, but this support measure puts them on adjacent faces.

The master figurate array sharpens this from a slogan into an arithmetic
surface.  Every point has a finite rational-argument digamma form.  Exactly on
the divisibility rays `s-2 | d`, the extra shape pole collides with a rising-
factor pole and creates one double pole, hence a rational plus a nonzero
rational multiple of `pi^2`.  The rational simplex boundary, cyclotomic-log
interior, and transcendental `pi^2` ridges are different arithmetic phases of
one positive beta integral.  The square-pyramidal `18-24log2` sits immediately
off a resonance ray; its logarithm is structural, not accidental.

The old ladder also looks different when the operation is kept.  The same
local ratios `2k-1` give a divergent odd harmonic series, their partial sums
give the rational mass `3/4`, and their partial products give an error-function
constant.  Likewise the run-support filtration increases combinatorial counts
while monotonically decreasing reciprocal mass from hyperbolic quadratic
constants through Moser/`G` toward powers of two and Fibonacci.  The operation
that accumulates the local data is part of the sequence's identity.

“Toothpick self-similarity” splits into two analytic universality classes.  The
classical toothpick population has a dyadic quadratic lower bound and finite
mass.  THM-1127's LRC toothpick count has an exact affine phase recurrence, so
its support contains an arithmetic progression and its mass diverges.  Visual
self-similarity alone predicts neither outcome; one must ask whether scaling
acts multiplicatively on values or transports them additively along phases.

The needle interpretation makes the LRC connection precise but also marks its
limit.  A finite support mass is a finite total-width budget and forces almost
every point into only finitely many deterministic arcs.  Divergent mass is the
threshold for almost-sure infinite random coverage under independent rotations.
Deterministic LRC and Kakeya problems live in the gap: arithmetic correlations,
directions, and simultaneous avoidance are exactly the information erased by
the scalar budget.

Tournament Analysis therefore has a more faithful carrier than runners or
arcs: occupied values, logarithmic blocks, heat modes, or proof obligations.
Ordering a few sequence families by finite prefix mass makes only a transitive
atlas tournament and destroys the tail.  The meaningful nontransitive data
would compare scale-by-scale occupancy, where families can overtake one
another.  This challenges the default assumption that tournament vertices
must be the original combinatorial objects.

The honest frontier is now narrower:

1. Prove enough of the global H-value support to decide its reciprocal
   divergence; finite coverage and the two omissions do not suffice.
2. Extend THM-2010's new invariant sequences before assigning constants or
   growth classes; four exact terms are prefixes, not asymptotics.
3. Determine arithmetic types only where the pole structure proves them.
   Fast convergence is not transcendence.
4. Use block profiles or heat traces when two families share a scalar mass.
5. In LRC, identify which deterministic correlations replace independence at
   the Abel--Bertrand needle threshold.

The missing object was not another term of a sequence.  It was the support
measure and the family of transforms through which we choose to observe it.
