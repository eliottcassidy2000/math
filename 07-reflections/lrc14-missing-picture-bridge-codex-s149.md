# LRC14 Missing-Picture Bridge - Codex S149

The repo's LRC14 history is starting to look less like a pile of analogies and more like one proof diagram.  The hidden picture is not another number.  It is a functor that carries a reduced residual through the exact labels that every previous attempt kept rediscovering.

The history spine now reads:

```text
q-threshold / apex survivor
  -> AP and Goddyn-Wong boundary atoms
  -> exact M / Farey branch
  -> C27 shell transfer
  -> q=3 unital pair-completion chart
  -> Haar-Baire open/boundary front
  -> unit-petal discharge, covering strictness, or K33/TournamentStateLift
```

S149 made this concrete by auditing the named rows with S124 and S146 kernels.  AP and GW are still the only local one-swap boundary-only atoms.  The first K33 child `12->36` has strict Haar mass `1/1260`.  The two unit petals `10->20` and `13->26` are strict-open and route to petal rigidity.  The two-hole rows from S138 split exactly as the earlier packet story predicted: `P10+GW` is still unit-petal discharge, while `P10+K33` is a state-lift obligation.

That means the missing theorem should not be phrased as "classify a tournament isomorphism class."  The raw apex tournament forgets too much.  It cannot distinguish AP from residue-liar rows once magnitude and `q_threshold` are removed.  The exact scalar `M` is also too thin because it does not remember whether the mass came from a unit petal, a nonunit K33 packet, or a covering comb.

The quotient that looks proof-relevant is typed:

```text
residual
  | exact M, q_threshold, argmax denominators
  | strict-open Haar mass and boundary owner skeleton
  | C27 hole/double transfer
  | unital chart address
  | K33 incidence or covering-comb label
  v
discharge / AP-GW boundary atom / TournamentStateLift / covering strictness
```

Preserved predicates:

- whether there is an actual LRC14 witness with margin;
- whether safety is strict-open or endpoint-only;
- whether the row is explained by small `q`;
- whether the C27 owner is unit-visible or nonunit;
- whether the packet has crossed the first K33 wall;
- whether it belongs to the covering/shell branch.

Destroyed predicates:

- raw runner names after the typed labels are attached;
- scalar additive energy without owner labels;
- raw apex tournament class without exact denominator and magnitude;
- unital or geometric counts without the chart that says what pair is preserved.

The useful theorem shape is:

> Every primitive reduced LRC14 residual that is not already discharged by q-threshold or positive Haar-open strictness either reduces to AP/GW boundary-only, constructs the HYP-2908 / THM-572 `TournamentStateLift`, or is a covering/shell atom strict by comb-margin or shell-collapse.

The creative part is the vertex choice.  For the tournament method here, vertices can be boundary owner pairs, C27 transfers, unital chart points, K33 incidence obligations, Haar-Baire fronts, covering comb teeth, exact-period packet states, support-six relation packets, or proof obligations.  The best tournament is not a graph of runners.  It is a graph of which quotient is allowed to forget which data.

That feels like the missing connection: all the old analogies were asking the same question in different costumes.  What can be forgotten without losing the lonely-runner predicate?
