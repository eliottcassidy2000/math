# G_n as an Orbifold: The Geometry of Tournament Space

## What G_n IS

G_n = {0,1}^m / S_n is the **orbifold quotient** of the m-dimensional hypercube by the symmetric group acting via signed coordinate permutations.

An orbifold is a space that is "mostly" a manifold but has **singular points** where the group action has nontrivial stabilizers. In G_n:
- **Smooth points**: iso classes with |Aut| = 1 (generic tournaments)
- **Z/3Z singularities**: iso classes with |Aut| = 3
- **Z/5Z singularities**: iso classes with |Aut| = 5 (regular tournament)
- **Z/9Z singularities**: iso classes with |Aut| = 9 (at n=6)

The orbifold Euler characteristic is NOT the ordinary Euler characteristic. It accounts for the singular points:

chi_orb(G_n) = sum over classes C of 1/|Aut(C)|

At n=5: chi_orb = 7*(1/1) + 4*(1/3) + 1*(1/5) = 7 + 4/3 + 1/5 = 7 + 1.333 + 0.2 = 8.533

This is NOT an integer. The orbifold is genuinely singular.

## The Smooth Part

The smooth part of G_n is the set of |Aut|=1 classes. At n=5: 7 out of 12 classes (58%). At n=6: most of the 56 classes. As n grows, the smooth fraction -> 1 (almost all tournaments have trivial automorphism).

The smooth part is where:
- Tilings = H (exact, no Aut correction needed)
- Degree = m - self_loops (no orbit grouping)
- The edge formula E ~ V*m*(1-f)/2 is exact

The singular points are where the interesting structure lives -- the high-symmetry tournaments (regular, near-regular) that are the SINKS of the DAG and the most isolated vertices of G_n.

## The Orbifold as a Covering Space

G_n is COVERED by the labeled tournament space {0,1}^m. The covering map pi: {0,1}^m -> G_n sends each tournament to its iso class. The covering degree varies:
- At smooth points: degree = n! (the full S_n orbit)
- At Z/3Z singularities: degree = n!/3
- At Z/5Z singularities: degree = n!/5

This is a **branched covering**, like the two-sheeted cover of the square root function but with higher ramification at the singular points. The branch locus is the set of tournaments with nontrivial automorphisms.

## The Tiling Fibration as an Orbifold Bundle

The map (tilings) -> (iso classes) is an orbifold fiber bundle:
- Base: G_n (the orbifold)
- Fiber at smooth points: H tilings (since Tilings * |Aut| = H, Tilings = H for |Aut|=1)
- Fiber at singular points: H/|Aut| tilings (reduced by the isotropy group)

The total space (tilings) has 2^{C(n-1,2)} points. The base has A000568(n) points. The average fiber size is 2^{C(n-1,2)} / A000568(n).

This is an **orbifold Seifert fibration** -- a fiber bundle where the fiber size varies at the singular points.

## Why This Matters

The orbifold structure explains several phenomena:

1. **Why level edges only connect |Aut|=1 classes**: The smooth part of the orbifold is where the H function is "generic." At singular points, H is constrained by the automorphism group, making same-H different-class configurations impossible.

2. **Why the regular tournament is most isolated**: It's the DEEPEST singular point (highest |Aut|). In orbifold geometry, deep singularities have small neighborhoods. The Z/5Z singularity at the regular class is "pinched" compared to the smooth bulk.

3. **Why the edge formula has a correction**: The orbifold volume is V = sum 1/|Aut(C)| * |C| = 2^m. The edge "volume" is sum degree(C) * |C| = 2^m * m. But the QUOTIENT edge count is different because of the orbifold singularities.

4. **Why the width is the central binomial**: The width C(n-2, (n-2)/2) counts the maximum number of iso classes at the same H value. In the orbifold, this is the dimension of the "level set" at the widest point. The central binomial appears because the H-function's level sets are BALANCED -- the same combinatorial structure as a random walk's maximum excursion, which is governed by the central binomial coefficient.

## The Orbifold Fundamental Group

The fundamental group of G_n (as a topological space) captures the "winding" structure. At n=5: G_5 is genus 0 (simply connected), so pi_1 = trivial. But the ORBIFOLD fundamental group is non-trivial:

pi_1^orb(G_5) = Z/3Z * Z/3Z * Z/3Z * Z/3Z * Z/5Z

(generated by loops around the four Z/3Z singularities and one Z/5Z singularity). This is related to the MONODROMY of the covering space, which connects back to the Z/2Z monodromy of the fiber fraction's generating function (1-x)^{-1/2}.

## The Deeper Connection

The orbifold G_n is a DEFORMATION of the simplex. At n=2: G_2 is a single point (one iso class). At n=3: G_3 is a line segment (two classes). At n=4: G_4 has 4 points and 5 edges. As n grows, G_n deforms from a low-dimensional object to a high-dimensional orbifold, passing through genus 0 (sphere) at n=5 and then to higher genus at n >= 6.

The deformation is controlled by the Cayley-Dickson tower: each CD level (n = 2^k + 1) introduces a new kind of singularity. At n=5 (quaternionic): the Z/5Z singularity appears. At n=9 (octonionic): new singular types appear. At n=17 (sedenionic): the Paley maximizer changes, altering the DAG structure at the sinks.

The orbifold G_n is the **geometric realization of tournament theory** -- the space where all the algebraic, combinatorial, and topological phenomena we've discovered LIVE. Understanding G_n's orbifold geometry is understanding tournament theory.
