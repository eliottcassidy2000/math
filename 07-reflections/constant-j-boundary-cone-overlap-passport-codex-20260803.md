# Constant-J boundary collisions: cone overlap versus anti-tangent collisions

**Status: PROVED LEMMA / PROMOTED AS
[THM-3328](../01-canon/theorems/THM-3328-boundary-cone-overlap-and-anti-tangent-keller-passport.md).**  This note sharpens the
boundary/multiplicity residual left by
[THM-3303](../01-canon/theorems/THM-3303-keller-simplex-null-moments-force-a-boundary-collision.md).
It proves neither `HFC(3)` nor `JC(2)`.  Its role is to split a boundary
collision into an open two-sheet cell or a sharply constrained incidence.

## The local cone lemma

Let `Omega` be a polygonal domain in `R^2`, let `F` be `C^1` on a
neighbourhood of `closure(Omega)`, and suppose `det DF` never vanishes.  For
`x in partial Omega`, write `C_x` for the open inward tangent cone: the
directions `v` which point strictly into `Omega` from `x`.  Suppose

```text
x != y,             F(x)=F(y)=w.                           (1)
```

If

```text
DF(x) C_x  intersect  DF(y) C_y != empty,                  (2)
```

then there is a nonempty open set `U`, with `w` in its closure, such that
every `z in U` has two distinct preimages in `int(Omega)`, one near `x` and
one near `y`.

To prove this, choose a direction `u` in the intersection in `(2)` and a
small closed angular neighbourhood `K` of `u` still lying in both transformed
cones.  The two inverse charts at `w` satisfy, uniformly for `v in K`,

```text
F_x^(-1)(w+epsilon v)
  =x+epsilon DF(x)^(-1)v+o(epsilon),
F_y^(-1)(w+epsilon v)
  =y+epsilon DF(y)^(-1)v+o(epsilon).                       (3)
```

The leading directions in `(3)` lie a positive angular distance inside
`C_x,C_y`.  For all sufficiently small positive `epsilon`, both inverse
images therefore lie in `int(Omega)`.  Taking the two inverse-chart
neighbourhoods disjoint proves the claim for a truncated open cone `U`.

The statement is deliberately local.  It needs no moment hypothesis,
polynomiality, or global properness.

## Smooth collisions have only one one-sheeted orientation

Orient `partial Omega` positively and let `tau_x,tau_y` be the oriented
boundary tangents at two nonvertex points satisfying `(1)`.  Put

```text
u_x=DF(x) tau_x,       u_y=DF(y) tau_y.                    (4)
```

The two vectors are nonzero.  Since `det DF` has one sign on the connected
domain, the transformed inward cones are the same chosen side of the oriented
lines `R u_x` and `R u_y`.  Two such open half-planes are disjoint exactly
when

```text
u_y=-lambda u_x       for some lambda>0.                   (5)
```

Indeed, nonparallel half-planes meet in an open wedge; positive-parallel
half-planes coincide; and negative-parallel oriented tangents select opposite
sides.  Combining this observation with the cone lemma gives:

> If a smooth boundary collision does not create an open target set of
> multiplicity at least two, its two oriented image tangents must be
> anti-parallel.

Condition `(5)` is necessary, not sufficient, for a one-sheeted collision.  Higher
normal jets can still make two anti-tangent branches overlap.  This is the
first failure boundary of the lemma.

## Exact polynomial passport on a triangle

For a counterclockwise affine parametrization of the three triangle edges,

```text
gamma_i(t)=a_i+t v_i,       0<=t<=1,                       (6)
```

define, for two relative-edge interiors,

```text
E_ij(t,s)=F(gamma_i(t))-F(gamma_j(s)),
T_ij(t,s)=det(DF(gamma_i(t))v_i,DF(gamma_j(s))v_j),
A_ij(t,s)=dot(DF(gamma_i(t))v_i,DF(gamma_j(s))v_j).        (7)
```

If `F` is polynomial with constant nonzero Jacobian, every smooth collision
which can belong to an almost-everywhere one-sheeted survivor must satisfy

```text
E_ij(t,s)=0,       T_ij(t,s)=0,       A_ij(t,s)<0.         (8)
```

For `i=j`, the diagonal `t=s` must be saturated away.  For distinct edges,
the parameter pair at their common endpoint is the same source point and must
be discarded; a distinct-source collision involving a vertex is a cone case.
Thus `(8)` is an exact
algebraic/semialgebraic prefilter: collision elimination need not carry every
pair of edge parameters into the multiplicity calculation.

At a vertex the correct object is the full inward cone generated by the two
incident source rays from the vertex along its edges, not an arbitrarily
chosen tangent.  If two open transformed vertex/edge cones intersect, the
local cone lemma again gives an open double-sheet cell.  Only disjoint-cone
incidences remain in the one-sheet passport.

There is also a useful fixed-degree finiteness statement.  If `deg F<=d` and,
for one pair of distinct edges, the two coordinate collision equations have
no common component, then Bezout gives at most `d^2` complex affine
collisions for that pair.  On one edge both raw coordinate differences are
divisible by `t-s`; assume instead that **after saturation by `t-s`** the two
divided differences have no common component.  They have degree at most
`d-1`, so there are at most `(d-1)^2` off-diagonal complex collisions on that
edge.  If these pairwise component-freeness hypotheses hold for all three
same-edge and three cross-edge pairs, the conditional bound is

```text
3(d-1)^2+3d^2=6d^2-6d+3.                                  (9)
```

A common collision component is not silently counted as finite: it is a
separate positive-dimensional complex collision stratum.  On its
distinct-source real smooth locus the cone/anti-tangent test applies pointwise.

## Consequence for the constant-J HFC sector

THM-3303 proves that a homogeneous `HFC(3)` counterexample whose triangle
restriction `g=p+iq` has constant nonzero real Jacobian must have a boundary
collision.  The present lemma makes the residual exhaustive:

1. the transformed inward cones overlap, producing an open cell on which the
   multiplicity function `N_F` is at least two;
2. two smooth edge interiors meet anti-tangentially and satisfy `(8)`; or
3. at least one collision point is a vertex and the relevant transformed
   inward cones have disjoint interiors.

This is a boundary/multiplicity passport, not an exclusion.  Case 1 supplies
the overlap cell that the one-node disk argument lacked.  Case 2 is a lower-
dimensional collision locus with saturation and sign data; case 3 is a finite
full-cone comparison.  The next exact candidate-specific operation is to
eliminate `(8)` on the three cross-edge and three same-edge systems, then test
the finite vertex-cone cases before studying the remaining multiplicity cells.

## Sharp hostile control

THM-3303's slit-annulus control realizes exactly the exceptional orientation.
On the positively oriented rectangle `[0,2 pi]x[0,1]`,

```text
Phi(t,s)=sqrt(rho^2+s)(cos t,sin t)                       (10)
```

has constant Jacobian `-1/2`.  The colliding vertical sides `t=0,2 pi` have
oppositely oriented image tangents and opposite local inward sides.  Hence
their smooth seam satisfies `(5)` and does not force a local double-sheet
cell.  The example is nonpolynomial and the source is not a triangle, so it
does not settle either conjecture; it proves that the anti-parallel exception
cannot be deleted from the local theorem.

## Typed connection

```text
source:     two labelled boundary points of a local diffeomorphism;
map:        derivative image of each inward tangent cone;
preserved:  existence of two local interior inverse branches;
lost:       higher normal jets and global winding/multiplicity;
sidecar:    oriented edge label, inward side, vertex cone, normal jet;
test:       cone intersection, then the anti-tangent system (8).
```

The mechanism is the boundary version of a response passport: take the next
native operation (the local inverse chart) before replacing the two branches
by a scalar multiplicity.
