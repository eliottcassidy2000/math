## 2. Exact polynomial centralizer

Here and below, `dR wedge dN` means the additive `Q`-linear image

```text

```

not the `N`-submodule generated by those forms.  This distinction is needed
for the quotient notation used in the bridge note.

The adapted change `(x,y,beta) <-> (x,q0,beta)` is polynomial.  In these
coordinates

```text

```

If `dR wedge dh=0`, the `dq0 wedge d beta` coefficient is
`-3x^2 h_beta`; hence `h_beta=0`.  In the function field

```text
Q(x,q0)=Q(R,x),              q0=(2x-R)/(3x^2).
```

The remaining wedge coefficient is the derivative at fixed `R`.  More
precisely, the exact identity checked by the certificate is

```text

```

Thus `h=f(R)` for some `f in Q(R)`.  Setting `q0=0` gives
`f(2x)=h(x,0) in Q[x]`; writing `f` as a reduced fraction shows its
denominator is constant.  Consequently

```text

```

Let `A=Q[R,T,S]`.  Since `Q[R] subset A`, the first isomorphism theorem for


Here `N/A` is an additive vector-space quotient, not a quotient ring.  From
`(2)` and `(4)`, all polynomial primitives are exactly


## 4. A no-descended-graph lemma for destabilization

The following elementary lemma is the useful new consequence.

**Lemma.**  Let `A subset N` be an injective inclusion of `k`-algebras,
`H in N`, and let `D0` be an indeterminate.  Put

```text
D=D0+H,                   B=A[D] subset N[D0].
```

Then

```text
B intersect N=A,                                            (11)
D0 in B iff H in A.                                         (12)
```

Indeed, if a polynomial in `D0+H` with coefficients in `A` lies in `N`, its
highest `D0` coefficient forces its positive degree to vanish, proving
`(11)`.  Since `H=D-D0`, equation `(12)` follows immediately.

Apply the lemma to Long's core.  Because `H notin A`, the independent
compensator cannot be recovered from the four output coordinates by a
core-preserving polynomial operation.  Equivalently, if one tries to
destabilize by a source graph already descending through the core,

```text
D0=g(R,T,S),
```

then the restricted fourth output is `g+H`, which does not descend.  A target
graph `D=a(R,T,S)` instead forces

```text
D0=a-H,
```

so the source graph itself carries the same non-descended sidecar.  One has
not eliminated the compensator; one has moved it.

On the explicit fibre, a constant source slice `D0=c` makes the fourth output
take the two values

```text
c-1/48,                  c-1097/192.                   (13)
```

Thus the threefold core collision splits as `1+2`.  To retain all three
branches at one target `D=d`, the source values must instead be

```text
d+1/48,                  d+1097/192, d+1097/192,
```

which is exactly the branch-sensitive graph `D0=d-H`.

The lemma is deliberately narrow: it rules out `A`-descended graphs and
core-preserving target translations.  It does not rule out changing the
core, using a nontrivial source symplectomorphism, or finding a different
stabilization.  After this derivation, THM-4397 provides an important hostile
control: its Hamiltonian source gauge redistributes terms between `D0` and
the displayed correction.  The polynomial formula `H` is therefore not an
absolute gauge invariant.

## 5. Two hostile implications

Two tempting strengthenings are false.

1. **Non-descent does not mean all colliding branches are separated.**
   Equation `(9)` leaves the `x=1` and `x=-1` pair together.  A constant
   `D0` slice destroys the threefold collision but retains a double point.

2. **Constancy on every reduced geometric fibre does not imply algebraic
   descent.**  In the cusp normalization

   ```text
   Q[t^2,t^3] subset Q[t],
   ```

   the map on algebraically closed points is bijective, so `t` is constant
   on every reduced point fibre, yet `t` is not in `Q[t^2,t^3]` because the
   degree `1` is absent from the semigroup generated by `2,3`.  The
   nonreduced scheme fibre over the cusp retains the missing information.

Accordingly, one differing reduced fibre is a decisive witness of
non-descent, as used here, but the converse test requires scheme-theoretic or
seminormal information.
