# The triangular lattice is the 2D chromatic ceiling — and why it is the *same* ceiling as the density quantum

## The fact

THM-419: **every** 2D lattice unit-distance graph `U(L,D)` has `χ ≤ 3`. Not just
the square (`χ=2`) and triangular (`χ=3`) of THM-418 — *all of them*, including
every imaginary-quadratic order, the `w=2` lattices that the dispatched seed
called "between the triangular lattice and the CM field." There is **nothing
between**: the triangular lattice's `χ=3` is the maximum chromatic number any 2D
lattice can produce, at any norm, at any scale.

The dispatched question ("is there a 2D-realizable group whose norm layer beats
`3n` / forces `χ≥4`?") therefore has a clean answer: **no.** In 2D there is no
"above 3."

## Why the proof splits exactly where it does — the prime 3 is the whole story

The proof has one engine: **a linear coloring `c(v) = φ(v) mod 3`.** It is proper
iff `φ` (one of the 4 lines-through-0 in `𝔽₃²`) misses the connection set's
mod-3 image. The reduction of the quadratic form mod 3 controls everything:

- `Q` **nondegenerate** mod 3: the level set `{Q ≡ D ≠ 0}` is *always* the union
  of exactly two of the four lines (anisotropic: `{±e₁,±e₂}`; hyperbolic:
  the off-axis points). Two lines are free; color with one of them.
- `Q` **degenerate** mod 3 (i.e. `3 | disc`): `Q ≡ a·ℓ²`, and `ℓ` *itself* is the
  coloring. This is exactly THM-418's `(a−b) mod 3` for the triangular form, now
  seen as the universal degenerate case.

The only obstruction is when `3 | D` *and* the form is hyperbolic mod 3 — there
the recursion stalls and a period-2 coloring takes over. Everything else is `3`.

So the "3-chromatic ceiling" is **literally the prime 3** acting on the lattice:
either as an `𝔽₃` line-arrangement (nondegenerate) or as a perfect-square residue
(`ℤ[ω]`, degenerate, `disc = −3`). The triangular lattice is special precisely
because its discriminant *is* `−3`: it is the form whose mod-3 reduction is a
perfect square, the universal degenerate case sitting at the ceiling.

## The unification: same ceiling, two functionals

Stack this session's `χ ≤ 3` against my S706 result THM-416 (`density quantum
≤ 3` in 2D, because `w ≤ 6` roots of unity, Eisenstein):

| 2D lattice invariant | ceiling | mechanism | attained by |
|---|---|---|---|
| Euclidean **density quantum** `w/2` | **3** | `w = #`roots of unity `≤ 6` (THM-416) | Eisenstein `ℤ[ω]` |
| **chromatic number** `χ(U(L,D))` | **3** | prime 3 / `disc ≡ 0 mod 3` (THM-419) | triangular `ℤ[ω]` |

**The same lattice attains both ceilings, and the ceiling is the same number 3.**
This is not a coincidence — it is the cyclotomic/torsion floor seen twice:

- The density quantum is `3` because the **torsion** (roots of unity) of an
  imaginary-quadratic order maxes out at `μ₆` (Eisenstein). Torsion = the lattice's
  internal rotational symmetry.
- The chromatic number is `3` because the unit-distance graph's odd cycles are
  generated by the order-6 rotation's equilateral triangle (THM-418 lower bound),
  i.e. *again* by `μ₆`. Triangles come from torsion; the coloring obstruction is
  the `μ₆`-orbit.

Both ceilings are `μ₆`. In S699o's language (HYP-2279), a single 2D lattice has
**rotation rank 0** — all its symmetry is torsion (roots of unity) — and
`χ = 3 + rank` gives `χ = 3`. THM-419 is the rank-0 case of the rotation-rank
conjecture, made completely rigorous: *rank 0 ⟺ single lattice ⟺ χ ≤ 3.*

## Where this puts `χ(ℝ²) ∈ {5,6,7}`

THM-419 *localizes* the open problem with full rigor. The plane's chromatic
number is `≥ 5` (de Grey), but **no part of a `≥4`-forcing graph can live inside a
single 2D lattice.** Every `χ≥4` configuration must combine incommensurate
lattices / irrational rotation angles — the **Dehn-nontrivial / non-torsion /
rank ≥ 1** regime (HYP-2275, oracle-S685's Niven=Dehn needle; HYP-2279). The
Moser spindle's `arccos(5/6)` junction (rank 1, `√−11`) and de Grey's rotations
(rank ≥ 2) are *exactly* the escapes from this ceiling.

So the difficulty of `{5,6,7}` now has a clean structural statement: it is
**entirely off-lattice**. No lattice computation, no matter how clever the norm or
how popular the shell, will ever touch it. This is the geometric twin of the
analytic "Vitali wall" (HYP-2278, S699g): the fractional/measure bound can't reach
5, *and* the lattice/arithmetic bound can't reach 4. Both the analytic floor and
the lattice floor sit below the combinatorial truth — the value lives in the gap
between them, reachable only by genuinely aperiodic, non-torsion, finite gadgets.

## What it says back to the LRC side (the project's home problem)

There is a precise mirror. On the **Euclidean** side, the popular-norm
representation count `r(D)` is *unbounded* (THM-412: `w·Σχ(d)`), yet the *chromatic*
and *density-quantum* consequences are **capped at 3** — large connection sets do
not buy higher chromatic number. On the **cyclic** (LRC) side, my S704 THM-414
showed the dual: the pair-sum count `r₊(s)` is matching-*capped* at `⌊N/2⌋`, the
worry-set floor is rigid, and only the multiplicative (Euclidean) norm escapes its
rosette. Both faces say the same thing: **the torsion/cyclotomic structure caps
the second-order invariant; escape requires leaving the torsion** — non-torsion
unit-norm rotations (HN/UD) or genuinely irrational speeds (LRC resonances). The
"3" of the triangular lattice and the "2n−1" of the worry-set rosette
(THM-416's LRC corollary) are the same cyclotomic floor read in two metrics.
