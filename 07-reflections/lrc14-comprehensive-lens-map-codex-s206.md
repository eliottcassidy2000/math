# LRC14 Comprehensive Lens Map: What The Lenses Agree On

The useful thing about the current LRC14 surface is that the lenses are no
longer random.  They disagree in local language, but the disagreements are
structured.  A topology lens says "closed boundary cycle."  A Henselian lens
says "status-preserving unit gate."  A barcode lens says "owner stalk."  A
Ramanujan lens says "primitive period deck."  A Haar lens says "first nonzero
zeta."  The automaton lenses say "carry/gap telemetry."  The analytic lenses
say "capacity plus blindness."  These are not competing final proofs.  They
are projections of the same packet with different coordinates forgotten.

That is the main S206 update: the center is a controlled-forgetting packet
sheaf.  The proof should not ask which lens wins.  It should ask which
coordinate a lens forgot, and whether that coordinate is constant,
reconstructible, killed by a dual/cut/cocycle, descended familywise, stopped at
AP/GW boundary topology, or routed to a named residual.

The map also clarifies pair-good decoys.  Decoys are not a mass to be feared
first.  They are generated by blocker teeth.  Once the generator is known, the
barcode and normal-fan lenses say whether the false switch sits outside a
strict bar, in an AP/GW zero-bar boundary row, or on an active bottleneck
owner.  That makes decoys part of the owner-strip descent story rather than a
separate counting problem.

The incoming AP-tail puncture atlas is an especially sharp example of the same
law.  The coarse stalk remembered the owner geometry but forgot the `m mod 13`
clock.  Reattaching that clock as a q13 puncture bit or reciprocal fixed-point
tail certificate made the residual route-pure.  This is exactly what the lens
map means by a legal forgotten coordinate: the missing datum is named, proved
familywise, and reattached before the quotient is trusted.

The owner-strip filtration pushed by the next incoming session fits the same
map even more directly.  It says that post-status residual debt should pass
through primitive-period deck, AP-tail clock, Haar/drop-add zeta, and
endpoint-owner strip current before being called F7 or THM-572 debt.  That
turns "what all these lenses point at" into a finite page order.

The automaton and sequence lenses have the same status.  Moser-de Bruijn,
fibbinary, lacunary gaps, Fermat-Catalan guards, and Mahler-style distribution
language are useful because they expose carry and gap state.  They are not
safe proof quotients.  They become legal only when exact scale, topology,
owner, and route handoff travel with them.

The external analogies now have a stricter admission test.  Perfect numbers,
unit-distance graphs, operator/Pincherle dynamics, Meissel-Mertens AP
constants, and convolution or representation-economy analogies are not
evidence by themselves.  They can be proof carriers only after they state the
LRC predicate preserved, the coordinate destroyed, the sidecar needed, and the
handoff route for failures.

The challenged assumption is that a universal vertex set exists.  For the
S206 tournament, vertices are lens families and proof obligations, not runners
or arcs.  That loses row enumeration and runner identity on purpose.  What it
keeps is the proof-order predicate: boundary/open status, route
schedulability, owner/topology retention, period/scale retention, certificate
strength, and named residual handoff.

The next agent should use `00-navigation/LRC-LENS-MAP.md` as a routing table.
Every new lens should declare `lens_family`, `preserved_lrc_predicate`,
`destroyed_coordinate`, `required_sidecar`, and `handoff_target`, then run
status and route fiber checks before promoting it.
