## Exact matrices and the two base cycles

Put

```text
G = [0 1; 1 1],                 D(m,n)=n^2-mn-m^2.
```

Direct multiplication gives

```text

K=G^18=[1597 2584;2584 4181] = -I+17B,
B=[94 152;152 246].                                      (A)
```

The identity

```text
D(G(m,n))=-D(m,n)                                       (B)
```

is exact over the integers.  Since `-1` is a square modulo `17`, the Legendre
symbol `chi_17(D)` is invariant on projective `G`-orbits.  The binary form
`D` has discriminant `5`, a nonsquare modulo `17`, so it is nonzero on every
line of `P^1(F_17)`.

Modulo `17`, `G^9=4I`; neither `G` nor `G^3=C` is scalar.  Their characteristic
discriminants are respectively `5` and `3`, both nonsquares, so neither has an
`F_17` eigenline.  Thus the projective order is nine and no orbit can have
length one or three.  The eighteen lines therefore split into two 9-cycles.
The exact cycles are

```text

  (0:1),(1:1),(9:1),(12:1),(4:1),(7:1),(15:1),(16:1),(1:0)

chi(D)=-1:
  (2:1),(6:1),(5:1),(3:1),(13:1),(11:1),(10:1),(14:1),(8:1).
```

This proves that the Legendre symbol classifies the two cycles, rather than
merely correlating with them.  There is an important boundary: consecutive
Fibonacci vectors `u_n=(F_n,F_(n+1))` satisfy `D(u_n)=(-1)^n`, and both `+1`
and `-1` are quadratic residues mod `17`.  Hence the Fibonacci sequence fills
the **square** 9-cycle and never reaches the nonsquare 9-cycle.  The Hensel
result below covers all eighteen base lines; the Fibonacci/Berggren reading

## A coefficient-blind regular-fibre lemma

Here is the all-exponent mechanism, stated independently of the displayed
matrix coefficients.

**Lemma.**  Let `p` be odd and let

```text
U=I+pE in GL_2(Z_p).
```

If `E mod p` has no eigenline in `P^1(F_p)` (equivalently, its quadratic
characteristic polynomial is irreducible), then for every `a>=1`, the cyclic
action generated by `U` is freely transitive on every fibre of

```text
P^1(Z/p^a Z) -> P^1(F_p).                               (C)
```

In particular, its projective order on each fibre is `p^(a-1)`.

**Proof.**  Each fibre in (C) has `p^(a-1)` points.  This follows either from
the two standard charts

```text
{(x:1):x mod p^a} union {(1:py):y mod p^(a-1)},         (D)
```

or by lifting one affine coordinate at each level.

For `a>=2`, take `0<j<p^(a-1)` and write `j=p^s q`, where `p` does not divide
`q` and `0<=s<=a-2`.  The binomial expansion, with all higher terms one layer
deeper, gives

```text
U^j = I + q p^(s+1) E       (mod p^(s+2)).               (E)
```

Suppose `U^j` fixed a primitive projective vector `v` modulo `p^a`.  Then
`U^j v=lambda v` for a unit `lambda`.  Reduction modulo `p^(s+1)` in a unit
coordinate of `v` forces

```text
lambda=1+c p^(s+1)          (mod p^(s+2)).
```

The next layer of (E) says

```text
q E vbar = c vbar           (mod p),                     (F)
```

so `vbar` is an eigenline of `E mod p`, a contradiction.  Thus no exponent
strictly between zero and `p^(a-1)` fixes any point.  Finally the same binomial
valuation gives

```text
U^(p^(a-1)) = I             (mod p^a),                   (G)
```

so every orbit has exactly `p^(a-1)` points.  Its size equals the fibre size
in (D), proving free transitivity.  The case `a=1` is the one-point fibre.
QED.

The proof is coefficient-independent in the useful sense: after one
irreducibility certificate for the first layer, it never inspects the fibre,
the lift coefficient, or the exponent level.  It also identifies the sharp
failure mode.  If `E mod p` has an eigenline, (F) is no contradiction; the
companion's split control `E=diag(1,2)` fixes `(1:0)` while its level-two fibre
has seventeen points.

```text

disc(B)=tr(B)^2-4det(B)=5,                              (H)
```

and `5` is a nonsquare.  Applying the lemma to `E=-B` proves, for **every**

## The finite-state transducer


## XOR and harmonic-series preservation

Fix an exponent `a`, put `N=17^(a-1)`, and choose the canonical Fibonacci
base representative `n_0` in `{3,...,11}`.  These nine indices represent the
nine lines in the square base cycle, start inside one period, and avoid the
tied `n=2` `T6` boundary.  Take `lambda_a=[u_(n_0)]`.  A calibration in (J)
identifies any subset
`J subset Z/NZ` with a subset of Hensel states.  Embed the same Boolean data in
the natural numbers by

```text
S_J = union_(j in J) {n_0+18j+18Nt:t>=0}.               (O)
```

This map is injective and preserves exactly

```text
S_(J xor J') = S_J triangle S_J',
S_(J intersect J') = S_J intersect S_J',
S_(J union J') = S_J union S_J',                        (P)
```

and complements relative to the carrier `S_(Z/NZ)`.  Thus the power set of
one Hensel fibre is literally a Boolean/XOR algebra of periodic subsets of
the natural numbers.  An arbitrary compatible base lift is recovered by
translating the calibrated exponent, as below.

Each selected residue class in (O) has modulus `18N`.  Consequently

```text
delta(S_J)=|J|/(18N),
sum_(n<=x,n in S_J) 1/n
  = |J|/(18N) log x + C_J + O(1/x).                     (Q)
```

This is the precise harmonic preservation: the subseries pole equals the
periodic-support density.  The pole remembers only `|J|`, not the subset or
its cyclic phase.  The full residue word, or equivalently its cyclotomic
Fourier coefficients, is the needed sidecar for exact recovery.

Changing the base lift from `lambda_a` to `K^c lambda_a` translates the
coordinate `J` by `c`.  Equations (P) survive, and (Q) is invariant, but the
phase of the natural-number support moves.  This is a genuine torsor gauge,
not a tournament orientation.  An arbitrary subset of `N` certainly defines
a harmonic subseries, but it need not be periodic and need not possess a
density or a logarithmic coefficient; (O)--(Q) are the exact finite-Hensel
subclass.

Along every support (O), the `T4` and `T6` states are frozen because all
indices differ by multiples of eighteen.  Therefore the tournament quotient
forgets **all** of `J`, while the harmonic pole retains only its cardinality.
The calibrated Hensel exponent or the full periodic phase is indispensable.
