# LRC14 Side-Channel Repair Ladder - S190

The useful move after HYP-3017, HYP-3023, HYP-3024, and HYP-3025 was not to
invent another automatic sequence field.  The automatic sidecar had already
failed as a quotient, the automatic zipper had identified exact magnitude as
the first route-pure non-route splitter, and the newer convergence/closed-arc
sidecars made clear that the missing object is a repair order.  The sharper
question was: what is the smallest noncircular side-channel that repairs each
failed predicate, and where does repair still leak?

The full-bank audit gives a clean answer.  Exact `M` attached to the automatic
word eliminates mixed open/boundary fibers, but it still leaves `366`
mixed-route fibers.  So exact scale is the boundary-status repair coordinate,
not the whole theorem route.  Adding `q_threshold` collapses route leakage to
one two-row mixed pair, and boundary topology does the same in a different
place.  Reattaching packet labels, or the full guarded non-route packet
signature, is route-pure on the audited bank.

That feels like the right proof shape: automatic words should schedule which
repair cochain to inspect next.  They should not be asked to prove LRC14
directly.  The candidate local zipper statement is that the first nonzero
cochain among exact scale/q, boundary topology, C27/K33 transfer labels,
HYP-3022/HYP-3023/HYP-3024/HYP-3025 sidecars, and harmonic dual data must either
open a strict component, descend to a known family, be annihilated by an
existing certificate, or leave an explicit F7/THM-572 residual.

Assumption challenge: I considered runners, residues, gaps, safe components,
wall-crossing events, automatic states, endpoint owners, barcode bars, packet
labels, and proof obligations as vertices.  The script uses quotient-repair
carriers because that is the predicate-preserving question.  It preserves
open/boundary status and theorem route as tests on fibers; it destroys runner
identity and raw endpoint ownership unless those are reattached as packet
labels.
