# LRC14 Endpoint-Owner Transfer - S208

The useful little detail, after the S202/S203 hidden-coordinate ledgers landed,
was hiding in plain sight: `B18Z6` looked like an endpoint-current word, but it
was only a count.  It said there were eighteen boundary events and six zero-sum
active pairs; it did not say who owned them.

Incoming S204/HYP-3041 was useful signal rather than noise: its AP-tail
puncture atlas says a mod-14 owner strip can collide until the hidden
`m mod 13` clock is restored.  This S208 pass is the endpoint-current analogue:
the coarse count collides until the hidden owner names are restored.

Incoming S205/HYP-3042 sharpened that into a filtration.  This note is the
small exact plate under its endpoint-owner-strip page: `B18Z6` is the scalar
shadow, and the external owner multiset is the first useful current.

Incoming S207/HYP-3044 is adjacent signal: the two compact topology failures
are not new route-label debt, but owner-stalk collars split by primitive decks.
This S208 plate is the complementary endpoint-current version of that lesson.

Once the owner names are kept, the four residual packets separate cleanly:

```text
petal q=23          12:26x6,6:20x4
covering q=23       2:16x6
K33 lift            12:26x6,8:36x4
single-swap cover   2:72x6
```

That makes the S201 q=23 diagonal sharper.  The two diagonal rows both have
`M=2/23` and both have coarse endpoint word `B18Z6`, but the token transfer
from petal to covering is:

```text
-12:26x6,+2:16x6,-6:20x4
```

The same owner strip also explains something S196b only hinted at.  The two
residual capacitors have different first cheap cuts: petal/covering is cut by
coarse boundary topology, while K33/covering is cut by exact `M+q`.  External
endpoint owners split both.  So this is a common refinement, not just an
ornament on the q=23 square.

The safe-component owner stalk remains useful, but it is slightly less refined
globally: petal and K33 share the same largest-component owner stalk
`('1+',)->('1+', '12-')->('12-',)`.  The external owner strip keeps the
branch token (`6:20` versus `8:36`) while staying local and non-route-labelled.

The candidate theorem target I would hand off:

```text
Inside protected strict-open B18Z6 residual packets, the external endpoint
owner strip is a local address for residual route debt.  A quotient that keeps
only B/Z counts is unsafe unless owner names are reconstructed, dual-killed,
or routed to named residual debt.
```

Assumption challenge: I considered runners, gaps, fixed sections, boundary
events, residues, cover arcs, Fourier modes, Haar tiles, safe-component bars,
endpoint owner tokens, matroid circuits, and proof obligations.  The vertices
that worked were owner-transfer carriers, because the preserved predicate is
strict-open residual route schedulability, not runner identity.
