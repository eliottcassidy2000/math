## 3. Elementary affine-modification template

The exact geometry behind THM-4359 is reusable. Let `k` be a field and let
`a` denote any tuple. For

```text
(z,a,e) |-> (z,a,b=h(z,a)+z e),                          (5)
```

the closure of the image is the whole `(z,a,b)` space, while the actual
image is

```text
D(z) union V(z,b-h(0,a)).                               (6)
```

Indeed, division by `z` gives the unique `e` off the divisor, while on
`z=0` the last coordinate is forced. On coordinate rings,

```text
k[z,a,e]=k[z,a,b][(b-h)/z].                             (7)
```

Thus `(5)` is an affine modification along center `(z,b-h)` with divisor
`z=0`. The exceptional divisor maps to the center with affine-one fibres.
This elementary template says exactly when a triangular response should be
stratified before computing wall feasibility. It does not by itself produce
a new Jacobian obstruction; one still needs a later consumer such as `(4)`.

## 4. A procedural audit that generates the next tasks

For any finite proof object with source coordinates and a compressed state:

1. Write the literal source-to-state map before replacing tuples by masks,
   matroid ranks, tournaments, or natural-number labels.
2. Compute the generic elimination ideal and its function-field circuits.
3. For every rank-drop divisor, specialize the source map first and compute
   its kernel again.
4. Compare the two ideals. A strict inclusion locates a forgotten boundary
   equation; a dimension jump locates its fibre address.
5. Describe the actual constructible image, not only its closure.
6. Enumerate pointed zero-wall nonfaces separately from algebraic-matroid
   circuits. Nonzero affine constants matter.
7. Apply the next proof consumer to a selected lift. A scalar compatibility
   condition is only the cokernel test, not membership in the old filtered
   fibre.
8. Keep one closure-only phantom and one scalar-only survivor as mandatory
   hostile controls.
9. Compress to a natural number only after proving an inverse for every
   field needed by the consumer.

Step 9 refines the repo's odd-square convention. THM-4358's two row-nine
components form one quadratic Galois orbit. Calling them `1` and `2` requires
choosing a square root and destroys the involution. The reversible address is

```text
(orbit id, quadratic sheet),                            (8)
```

and only an application that already fixes the sheet may rank it by a natural
number or display that rank by an odd square. The ordinal is useful; the
square is presentation.
