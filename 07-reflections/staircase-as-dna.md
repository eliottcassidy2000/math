# The Staircase as DNA

The staircase Young diagram delta_{n-2} turns out to be the Rosetta Stone of tournament theory. Every structure we've discovered — Coxeter angles, Walsh spectra, path homology, independence polynomials, shadow compression — has a natural home in the staircase.

## What compels

The hook length formula h(i,j) = 2(k-i-j)-1 is the simplest possible: every hook is odd, depending only on the anti-diagonal (distance from the hypotenuse). This is not a coincidence but a reflection of the fact that the staircase IS the tournament's structure — the oddness of hooks parallels the oddness of H (Redei's theorem), and both flow from the same 2-adic source.

## The self-selection phenomenon

The most striking finding: RSK shapes of high-H tournaments tend to BE staircases. The partition (4,3,2,1) = delta_4 appears as the RSK shape exclusively at H >= 9. The staircase, which IS the tournament, also SELECTS FOR ITSELF when tournaments are analyzed through RSK. This is the mathematical analog of DNA encoding proteins that maintain DNA — a self-referential loop.

## Symmetric tilings = self-evacuating SYT

The 2^{m^2} identity connects two seemingly unrelated counts: binary symmetric configurations of the staircase, and ordered fillings fixed by Schutzenberger's involution. The bridge is Fomin's growth diagram theory, which shows that both objects are different views of the same underlying structure. Growth diagrams on the staircase simultaneously encode the tournament (local growth data), the RSK output (boundary partition), and the complement operation (y=x reflection).

## The complement as transpose

Perhaps the most elegant algebraic fact: for tournaments, the complement T^op IS the matrix transpose T^T. This means RSK(T^op) = RSK(T^T), and since generalized RSK preserves shape under transpose, the complement preserves RSK shape. This is not deep mathematics, but it unifies two operations — complement and transpose — that seem conceptually different for tournaments.

## What this means

The staircase is not just a combinatorial gadget. It's the natural coordinate system for tournament theory. The anti-diagonals are the source-vertex strata. The columns are the target-vertex strata. The y=x symmetry is the complement. The growth diagrams are the tournaments. And the hook lengths measure the importance of each arc — not in an ad hoc way, but through the intrinsic geometry of the Young diagram.

Every future investigation in this project should start by asking: what does this look like in the staircase?
