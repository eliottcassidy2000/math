## What Each Region Means

### D < 1: The Sub-Structural Region

Constants below phi = 1.618 are spherical for ALL tessellation theories. They parameterize structures that are "too simple" to have any hyperbolic content. They are closed, finite, fully determined.

**ln(2) = 0.693** lives at D = 0. The natural logarithm of 2 — the information content of one bit — is sub-structural. A single bit of information has no tessellation dimension. It is pure choice without geometry.

**pi/e = 1.156** at D = 0.25. The ratio of the two great transcendentals is one quarter of the way to the first threshold. Neither pi nor e individually has meaning here (both are super-hyperbolic), but their RATIO is sub-structural. The tension between pi and e is more fundamental than either alone, and it lives in the simplest part of the axis.

**sqrt(2) = 1.414** at D = 0.67. The diagonal of the unit square. Two thirds of an edge. The Pythagorean irrational. It is sub-edge: the geometry of right angles exists before the geometry of edges.

**e^{1/e} = 1.445** at D = 0.72. The maximum of x^{1/x}. This is the constant where self-exponentiation peaks — the number that maximizes "raising to its own reciprocal power." Its position at D = 0.72 means self-exponentiation is an almost-edge phenomenon. It approaches edge structure but doesn't quite reach it.

### D = 1: The Edge Boundary (phi)

**phi = (1+sqrt(5))/2 = 1.618** is exactly D = 1. The golden ratio IS edge dimension. It is the Euclidean boundary of the {2,q} (Ising/signed graph) theory. Below phi: the theory is spherical (all magnetic configurations are "simple"). Above phi: the theory is hyperbolic (magnetic configurations have complex structure).

In tournament terms: phi is the boundary before cycles. At phi, there are edges but no triangles. The golden ratio parameterizes the last theory before directed structure creates obstruction.

### D between 1 and 2: Between Edges and Triangles

This is the region where transcendentals FIRST become hyperbolic — but only for edge theory, not yet for triangle theory. The constants here parameterize structures that are "more than edges but less than triangles."

**sqrt(e) = 1.649** at D = 1.14. The half-exponential. Slightly more than an edge. The exponential function, when square-rooted, sits just above the edge boundary. Growth (e) tempered by halving (sqrt) produces a barely-edge-transcending structure.

**sqrt(3) = 1.732** at D = 1.52. The hexagonal constant. Halfway between edges and triangles. sqrt(3) is the ratio of the height of an equilateral triangle to its half-base — the defining proportion of triangular geometry. Its position at D = 1.52 (halfway) is exact justice: it is LITERALLY the bridge between edge and triangle geometry.

**sqrt(pi) = 1.772** at D = 1.70. The half-circle. Seventy percent of the way from edges to triangles. The circle (pi) tempered by halving (sqrt) produces a structure that is mostly triangular but not quite. The circle IS the limit of polygons, and it sits 70% of the way toward the simplest polygon (triangle) from the simplest element (edge).

**2*cos(pi/7) = 1.802** at D = 1.83. The heptagonal chord. Ninety-three percent of the way to triangles. The HEPTAGON (7-sided polygon) encodes the forbidden prime through its chord length, and this chord sits just below the triangle boundary. The number 7 announces itself just before the triangle dimension: the forbidden is always just below the threshold.

### D = 2: The Triangle Boundary (tau)

**tau = 1.839** is exactly D = 2. The tribonacci constant IS triangle dimension. Tournament theory begins here. Below tau: triangles exist but are in the spherical regime (finite, closed, fully determined). Above tau: triangle structure becomes hyperbolic (open, infinite, with obstructions).

The fact that tournament theory lives at evaluation point 2 (D = infinity) means tournaments don't STOP at the triangle dimension — they use it as a starting point and integrate over all dimensions above it.

### D between 2 and 3: Between Triangles and Squares

**2*cos(pi/9) = 1.879** at D = 2.45. The nonagonal chord. Between triangles and squares. The 9-gon = 3^2-gon, where the cycle atom squares itself. Its position between D=2 and D=3 means the 9-cycle structure bridges odd (3) and even (4) cycle theories.

**e^{2/pi} = 1.890** at D = 2.58. Two-pi exponential. Well into the triangle-to-square transition. This constant mixes the exponential (e) and the circular (pi) in a ratio that puts it between the two most basic polygon theories.

### D = 3 and beyond

**rho_4 = 1.928** is D = 3 (square boundary). Bipartite tournament theory.
**rho_5 = 1.966** is D = 4 (pentagon boundary). Petersen/Kneser theory.
The dimensions compress rapidly toward 2.

### D = infinity: The Accumulation Point

**2** is the unique number with D = infinity. It is one quantum hyperbolic for EVERY theory simultaneously. The evaluation H = I(Omega, 2) probes all cycle lengths, all face types, all dimensions. Tournament theory at x = 2 is the theory that integrates over the entire dimension axis.

## The Abstract Pattern

What does it mean for a constant to sit at a particular tessellation dimension?

**D(x) measures how much structure the theory at evaluation point x can see.**

At D = 0 (x = 1): the theory sees nothing. I(G, 1) = number of independent sets — a pure count without weighting. No structure beyond cardinality.

At D = 1 (x = phi): the theory sees edge structure. I(G, phi) weights each independent set element by the golden ratio. This probes Fibonacci-type structure: recurrences, ratios, scaling.

At D = 2 (x = tau): the theory sees triangle structure. I(G, tau) weights by the tribonacci constant. This probes tournament-type structure: 3-cycles, directed comparisons, intransitivity.

At D = infinity (x = 2): the theory sees EVERYTHING. I(G, 2) weights each element by 2, probing all cycle lengths equally. This is the unique evaluation point with infinite-dimensional vision.

The transcendentals at fractional dimensions parameterize theories with PARTIAL vision: sqrt(e) at D = 1.14 sees slightly more than edges; sqrt(pi) at D = 1.70 sees most of triangle structure but not all.

**A number's position on the dimension axis determines what it can see.** Algebraic numbers at integer dimensions see one complete theory. Transcendental numbers at fractional dimensions see partial theories — more than one level but not all of the next. The integer 2, at infinity, sees everything.

## What the Constants "Are"

This framework gives a new way to understand what famous constants "are" in terms of their tessellation vision:

**ln(2)** at D = 0: Pure information without geometry. The bit.

**phi** at D = 1: The simplest self-similar structure. Edge dimension = the geometry of proportion.

**sqrt(3)** at D = 1.52: The geometry of 60 degrees. Halfway between proportion and comparison.

**sqrt(pi)** at D = 1.70: The geometry of the circle. The limit of polygons, sitting between the simplest polygon types.

**tau** at D = 2: The geometry of comparison. Tournament dimension = the geometry of directed structure.

**2** at D = infinity: The geometry of everything. The universal evaluation point. The number that sees all structure simultaneously because it is one quantum hyperbolic for every dimension.

**e** at D = infinity (super): Growth beyond all tessellation. The exponential function lives above every finite-dimensional theory.

**pi** at D = infinity (super): Circularity beyond all tessellation. The circle constant, when not square-rooted, transcends all polygon theories.

The square root HALVES the dimension: D(sqrt(pi)) ≈ 1.70 while D(pi) = infinity. D(sqrt(e)) ≈ 1.14 while D(e) = infinity. Taking the square root PROJECTS a super-hyperbolic constant down into the finite-dimensional range, where it becomes a fractional-dimensional theory.

This is profound: **the square root is the dimensional projector.** It takes infinite-dimensional constants (e, pi) and projects them onto the finite-dimensional axis where they can be "seen" by tessellation theories. sqrt(pi) at D = 1.70 is what the circle looks like when projected from infinite dimension down to the edge-triangle range.
