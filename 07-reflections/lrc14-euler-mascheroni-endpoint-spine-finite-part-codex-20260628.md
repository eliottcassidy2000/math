# LRC14 Euler-Mascheroni Endpoint-Spine Finite Part

The Euler-Mascheroni prompt became useful only after forcing it through the
endpoint-spine certificate from HYP-3429, the canonical corridor-fence proof of
HYP-3431, and the harmonic-budget collision warning of HYP-3432.  Gamma is not
a floor constant here.  It is the finite part of a labelled harmonic tail.

For the canonical tower `{1,...,11,13,84m}`, the audit through `m=180` finds a
clean eventual law: from `m=5` onward the best endpoint spine is the E-only
tail wall `E:84m`, with length `1/(49m)`.  The selected interval is not
mysterious either.  With `a_m=ceil(48m/7)`, it is

```text
[(14a_m+1)/(588m), (14a_m+13)/(588m)].
```

That is exactly one safe component for the half-speed `42m`, since the
tail-only safe length is `(12/14)/(42m)=1/(49m)`.  The surprise is not the
length; the surprise is that this particular shrinking component is not cut
away by the fixed speeds `{1,...,11,13}` once the transient `m<5` has passed.

The proof target is therefore sharper than "estimate a harmonic sum."  Prove a
modular address lemma:

```text
I_m survives the odd two-color bad core for every m>=5.
```

The likely route is a seven-residue calculation plus interval separation.  The
address keeps `I_m` near the limiting coordinate `u=8/49`, and the component
width shrinks like `1/m`; after the finite transient, each fixed-speed bad
wall should have a uniform signed clearance or a residue-class certificate.

This also gives a guardrail for analytic constants across the repo.  Harmonic,
Mertens, Soldner, and log-log constants should enter as finite-part
normalizers only after the certificate still remembers which endpoint family
or owner-current family produced the tail.  HYP-3432 is the negative-control
ledger for this rule, while HYP-3433 is the positive labelled-tail case.  A
naked constant forgets the coordinate that made the asymptotic legal.

Next practical moves:

```text
1. Symbolically prove the canonical address law for m>=5.
2. Print exact clearances from I_m to every fixed odd branch wall by m mod 7.
3. Generalize from 84m to other tail towers V(m), looking for c/m endpoint
   lengths and finite parts after subtracting c log M.
4. Attach the finite-part sidecar to HYP-3429's E-only exception route and
   keep mixed E/B0 or E/B1 spines separate.
```

Assumption challenge: runners, raw total survivor measure, and gamma itself
are the wrong vertices for this pass.  The useful vertices are endpoint
families, tail addresses, branch masks, and proof obligations.  This quotient
destroys most component geometry, so it is legal as a tail-normalization and
address-routing device, not as the final LRC14 predicate.
