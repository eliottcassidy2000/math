## The main change of object

next native operation before scalarizing.  Instead of asking whether the

Restarting the centered Laurent recurrence at row two gives

```text
center = valuation-parity backbone XOR later collision current.
```

The backbone is exact:

```text
H_1(n)=nu_2(n+1) mod 2.
```

It is non-eventually-periodic, has density `1/3`, and has a logarithmic-bit
evaluator.  Therefore Rule 30 already contains a canonical nonperiodic closed
sequence.  The open issue is sharper than “find structure”: every later
adjacent-`11` source is capable of cancelling it after the radial grades are
collapsed.

Two grades expose that cancellation:

```text

v = excess Green transport beyond the extreme path.
```

Each fixed `(u,v)` channel is eventually 2-power-periodic.  Hence every
finite-band approximation remains nonperiodic, while hypothetical eventual
periodicity of the true center forces cancellation to escape every finite
rectangle in `(u,v)`.  This is a useful partial theorem: complexity is now
located at infinity in a precise filtration.

## Prize 1: attack cancellation, not the backbone

The nonperiodicity front now has a clean graded form.  Write formally

```text
C(z)=H(z)+sum_(u,v>=0) J_(u,v)(z).
```

Every finite truncation that includes `H` is nonperiodic because `H` is
nonperiodic and its finite correction is periodic.  A proof of center
nonperiodicity must prevent an infinite cancellation after the specialization
which forgets `(u,v)`.

Three plausible attacks survive the hostile checks:

1. **Lowest Cartier layer.**  Retain a radial variable and find a lowest
   `(u,v)` layer whose 2-adic Cartier species cannot be supplied by higher
   grades.  This is the lawful descendant of the factorial unique-channel
   strategy.  Literal unique collision events are already impossible.
2. **Escape tariff contradiction.**  Assume a center period `p`.  The theorem
   forces a cancelling channel outside every finite rectangle at carefully
   chosen times `2^M+1`.  Add a propagation-speed, support, or valuation bound
   strong enough to make those required escapes incompatible.
3. **Radial generating module.**  The shell recurrences make every `H_d`
   2-automatic.  Classify the module generated by the source series and show
   that the valuation-parity component survives specialization.  This needs a
   grading-sensitive invariant, not ordinary density or one Hankel minor.

The first cheap hostile for any proposal is now fixed: it must distinguish a
nonzero graded shell from a scalar cancellation between the `u=0` and `u=2`
boundary sources.

## Prize 2: a Haar theorem and a marked-point problem

The innovation depths give a compatible Boolean coordinate chart on the
2-adic phase space.  Haar phase becomes independent fair innovation bits, so
almost every phase is normal along those depths.  But the prize center is the
single codeword `Gamma(0)`.

At every noninnovation depth the spatial readout is an antiderivative of an
OR current.  Its exact bias is the alternating sum of gaps between current
events, and the missing Walsh statistic is the aligned simultaneous-zero
correlation of its two parents.  A lift motif `110` forces the next child into
the interval `[3/8,5/8]` spatially.

This suggests a two-step density program:

1. compute a blockwise Doob/Walsh decomposition of each noninnovation readout,
   retaining the aligned zero-intersection drift; then
2. control evaluation of those functions at the marked innovation address.

Neither step can be replaced by an average.  For every phase cylinder and
every `L`, some extended phase word in that cylinder has a depth block `0^q1`,
`q>=L`, physically realized on the ray at site `-q`; its agreeing shallow
prefix is periodic-extension data.  A residual set of extended phases has
arbitrarily long zero runs.  This Baire-typical statement coexists with
Haar-normal innovation coordinates.  It is the exact analogue of the
repository's repeated measure-versus-owner warning.

## Prize 3: three carrier models, three different invoices

The finite exact computations separate three notions of shortcut:

- ordinary Hankel rank invoices a homogeneous unary state `T^n`;
- binary-address flattening rank invoices automatic, tensor-train, or fixed
  digit-matrix evaluation;
- Mealy sections retain the correct observer, but require the address
  `0^(n-1)` of length `n`.

At eighteen input bits the rational digit tensor has maximal rank at every
cut; the middle bond dimension is `512`.  The `8192` center Hankel matrix is
nonsingular over `F_2`.  Exact padded-index decision trees have worst-case
depth `m` for `2<=m<=18`.  These are substantial fixed-seed finite barriers,
but none is a general time lower bound.

The factorial work supplies the decisive hostile: a sequence can have a huge
homogeneous Hankel carrier and still have a short time-varying recurrence.
The next Prize-3 probe should therefore seek a bounded **time-varying dyadic
cocycle** for collision-current summaries.  The Green transport already costs
only logarithmic digit work; nonlinear current production is the only honest
state-growth target.

## What not to do next

- Do not fit a recurrence to another finite center prefix; every prefix has a
  periodic-ring extension.
- Do not infer marked density from Haar phase normality.
- Do not call the three Green branches a Berggren tree after forgetting path
  order.
- Do not infer general time from Hankel, communication, tensor, or decision-
  query rank without a fixed machine and advice policy.
- Do not use a tournament: the faithful local object is a filtration DAG with
  a two-parent intersection hyperedge, not an intrinsic oriented pair relation.

The useful outside-the-box lesson is that the three prizes are not three
unrelated mysteries.  They are three views of one filtration defect:
nonperiodicity asks whether infinite grades cancel, density asks whether a
marked antiderivative is typical, and prediction asks how much state is needed
to retain the grades and the moving observer at once.
