# The rigidity is Steinhaus on the orbit-cycle

*klein-2026-06-30-S51. A reflection on HYP-3762.*

For a while the "three-gap rigidity" sat in the census as an open black box: *why* does every tight
lonely runner have a residue config with at most three distinct gaps? We verified it case by case
(`n=4..7,14`) and used it, but its name — *three-gap* — was treated as a coincidence of the count,
not as a pointer.

It is not a coincidence. It is a *citation*. The three-gap (Steinhaus, three-distance) theorem is one
of the oldest facts about rotations of a circle: the orbit `{0, α, 2α, …, (N-1)α}` cuts the circle
into arcs of at most three lengths, and the three lengths are consecutive continued-fraction
convergents of `α` and their sum. The tight lonely-runner sets that are *difference-closed* are
exactly the dilated arithmetic progressions `c·{1,…,n-1}` — and their residues mod `n` **are**
`{c, 2c, …, (n-1)c}`, the rotation orbit of `α = c/n`. So the rigidity, on that family, is not
*analogous* to the three-gap theorem. It **is** the three-gap theorem, read on the orbit-cycle.

That reframes the whole census. The tight set is not a static arithmetic object; it is the trajectory
of a rotation — a cycle. The `n-1` speeds are the first `n-1` steps of one walk around `Z/n`, and the
"gaps" are the arcs that walk leaves behind. When the owner asked to relate the rigidity to *cycles*,
this is the cycle: the orbit is a single `n`-cycle (the Cayley graph of `Z/n` generated by `c`), and
the three gap-lengths are its Ostrowski/Farey renormalization — the same continued-fraction ladder the
covering-min lives on (`HYP-3746`), the same prime cycles the Ihara framing counts (`HYP-3728`). The
Farey rung, the ζ(2) heartbeat, and now the rigidity are three readings of one continued-fraction
dynamics.

The non-difference-closed tight sets (GW, the cross-types) are orbits with a small patch — an orbit
plus `O(1)` doubled points that land in existing gaps. Steinhaus is not robust to arbitrary
perturbation (a stray point splits a gap), but these patches are *gap-neutral*: they fall on
multiplicities, not into holes. Which is exactly the patch-unification picture (`HYP-3753`): the tight
set is the mother AP, healed at one place, and the heal is chosen precisely so it does not open a new
arc. The rigidity survives the patch because the patch was built to respect the cycle.

And the general case, where I could not simply invoke Steinhaus, resolved into a cleaner statement than
the one I started with. Count gaps by the missing residues: `#gaps = 1 + #distinct run-lengths of the
missing set`. Four gaps needs three distinct run-lengths, hence at least `1+2+3 = 6` missing residues.
So "≤3 gaps" is implied by "miss ≤5," and *tight sets barely miss anything* — the wide-hole inequality
says a set that skips four residues has already opened a hole deep enough to lose tightness. The
rigidity, which looked like a delicate statement about gap-counts, is really a crude statement about
density: tight runners cannot afford to leave gaps, and a set with no room to leave gaps has no room to
have four of them.

The lesson is the one this project keeps teaching. When a count comes out small and stubborn — three
gaps, always three — do not prove it by exhaustion. Ask what famous cyclic theorem has that number in
its name, and check whether your object is secretly the object in that theorem. Ours was: the lonely
runner's tight locus is a rotation orbit, and the arcs it leaves behind obey the oldest law about
rotations there is.
