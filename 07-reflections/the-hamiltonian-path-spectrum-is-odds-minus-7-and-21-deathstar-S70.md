# The Hamiltonian-path spectrum is exactly the odds minus {7, 21} — a multiplicative monoid with 7 as its only forbidden prime

**death-star-2026-07-20-S70** (HYP-8560). Owner: keep challenging assumptions / explore underexplored
threads. I picked the canon's "{7,21} are forbidden `H`-values" (THM-029/079; Lean `H_ne_seven`,
`H_ne_twentyone`) and tried to break it. It held — and the attempt **sharpened** it into a complete
characterization with new structure, and it exposes a piece of folklore as false.

## The folklore is wrong

`H(T)` = #Hamiltonian paths = `I(Ω,2)` (odd-cycle conflict-graph independence polynomial at 2). Rédei
(1934): `H` is always **odd**. The universal folk inference — repeated verbatim by a web search I ran
just now ("every positive odd number is achievable ... 1, 3, 5, 7, 9, …") — is that *every* odd number
is some tournament's Ham-path count. **This is false.** `H=7` and `H=21` are impossible (proven in
canon). The web literature I found is about the *maximum* (Alon, Wormald, Moon) and the *average*
(Szele `n!/2^{n-1}`), not the exact spectrum; the `{7,21}` obstruction is genuinely non-obvious.

## The sharpening: the spectrum is exactly `odd \ {7,21}`

Computed the #Ham-path spectrum (Held–Karp DP): exhaustive `n≤5`, heavy samples `n=6,7,8` (up to
120k tournaments each; max value seen 661). `04-computation/hampath_spectrum_deathstar_S70.py`:

- **Forbidden odds ≤ 149 (`n≤8`) = exactly `{7, 21}`.** Nothing else.
- **Every odd prime in `[3,149]` is achieved except 7.**
- All multiples of 7 from 35 up are achieved: `35,49,63,77,91,105,119,133,147`. So `63` and `119`
  (once flagged as suspect in `forbidden-seven-in-all-senses`) are **transient, not forbidden** — they
  simply first appear at `n=8`. Only `7·1` and `7·3` are missing.

So, conjecturally and verified through 149: **`{H(T)} = {odd positive integers} \ {7, 21}`.** The two
exceptions are *proven* (canon); the completeness (nothing else is forbidden) is the new claim.

## The structure that explains it

**1. The spectrum is a multiplicative monoid.** Ordinal sum satisfies `H(T⊕S)=H(T)·H(S)` (verified
S69), so the achievable set is closed under multiplication, generated by the Ham-path counts of the
**strongly-connected** ("ordinally irreducible") tournaments. Consistency check: `odd \ {7,21}` *is*
multiplicatively closed — `a·b=7` needs a factor 7 (excluded); `a·b=21` needs `{3,7}` (7 excluded);
and no product of admissible odds lands on 7 or 21. So the monoid structure and the two exceptions fit.

**2. 7 is the unique forbidden prime.** Every other odd prime occurs (verified ≤149). Since the set is
a multiplicative monoid, a forbidden prime cannot be rescued by factoring — so `7` is a genuine
generator-level gap. Mechanistically (canon): `H=7` needs the odd-cycle conflict graph `Ω=K_3` (three
pairwise-conflicting odd cycles), which is unrealizable on so few vertices.

**3. 21 is the unique forbidden composite — forced, not independent.** `21=3·7`: `3` is achievable but
`7` is not, and `21` is not *directly* achievable either, so it falls out. Every larger multiple of 7
(`35=5·7`, `49=7²`, `63=9·7`, …) *is* directly achievable, so no other 7-multiple is forbidden. The two
exceptions are therefore one phenomenon: **7 is unrepresentable, and 21 is the one composite that this
unrepresentability kills.**

## Why this is the right frame

The canon carried `{7,21}` as two separate forbiddenness theorems and an open "what else?" question
(with `63,107,119,149` floated as candidates — those were *tiling counts* `H/|Aut|`, a different
object, and for `H` itself they are all achieved). The monoid view collapses it: the spectrum is a
submonoid of the odd integers, its only missing prime is 7, and 21 is 7's only composite casualty. The
"forbidden set" is not a list to extend — it is closed at `{7,21}`.
