# Even-graph minors are not the H obstruction

The Kuratowski/Wagner prompt sharpened a useful distinction.  The fixed-path
tournament cube and the even-graph cycle space really are the same labelled
cube: fixing a Hamiltonian path leaves `C(n-1,2)` free arcs, and the triangle
basis of the even cycle space has exactly `C(n-1,2)` coefficients.  That is a
real bridge, not just a numerology.

But the bridge is not a graph-minor closure for `H`.

The exact audit `tournament_even_minor_obstruction_codex_s96.py` takes a free
arc `(i,j)` and maps it to the triangle-basis bit `(0,i+1,j)`.  Then it tests
the two simplification moves in the prompt.

First, degree-2 suppression in the even-graph quotient is a GF(2) smoothing
operation.  Remove the two incident edges and toggle the chord.  That is the
right cycle-space move, but it is not topological-minor suppression: a triangle
can cancel to the empty graph.  Through `n=7`, the deterministic terminal
smoothing core `{empty}` contains `25414` rooted tournaments and `77` different
`H` values, from the transitive `H=1` row to an `H=189` row.  Whatever this
core remembers, it is not the Hamiltonian-path count.

Second, GF(2) contraction behaves better internally: it preserves even degree
in every checked case (`344064` one-edge contractions at `n=7`, zero parity
failures).  But it still does not preserve the `H` order.  The audit finds an
explicit increase:

```text
n=7, H=31, contract even edge (2,3)  ->  n=6, H=33.
```

and an explicit decrease:

```text
n=4, H=3, contract a triangle edge  ->  n=3, H=1.
```

So the `{7,21}` gap is not behaving like a finite forbidden-minor set of an
even-graph family.  The Robertson-Seymour analogy is a warning, not a proof
template: before invoking a finite obstruction theorem, identify the operation
under which the relevant predicate is closed.  Here the predicate is not closed
under the natural even-graph simplifications.

The surviving closure is older and sharper: strong-component condensation.
Every directed odd cycle lies inside one strong component, and every
Hamiltonian path traverses the tournament's strong components in condensation
order.  Therefore

```text
H(T) = product H(C_i).
```

This is the reduction that sees `{7,21}`.  `7` is a missing irreducible
strong-core value; `21=3*7` tries to import the missing factor.  The obstruction
is a semigroup/core obstruction, not a graph-minor obstruction.

For LRC work, the lesson is procedural.  Even-graph and tournament quotient
vertices are useful only after saying what they preserve.  Candidate vertex
sets considered here were free arcs, triangle-basis bits, even-graph smoothing
cores, contraction shadows, strong components, OCF conflict-graph components,
and proof obligations.  The quotient that preserves the relevant H predicate is
the strong-component/OCF packet quotient; the even-graph minor quotient
preserves parity and cycle-space membership but destroys too much H data.

This matches THM-520's Alcuin lesson: there can be a well-behaved minor-closed
skeleton and a small correction that escapes exactly through contraction.  For
`H`, the strong-component product is the skeleton; the `{7,21}` obstruction is
in the irreducible conflict-packet correction.

## Post-pull signal from mac-mini S33

After this audit, mac-mini S33 landed an idea batch proposing that the
permanently forbidden `H` values may form a multiplicative ideal generated by
forbidden strong primes, and that one should look for a tournament minor order.
This note supports the first point and qualifies the second.

The multiplicative-ideal language is the right positive abstraction:
strong-component values generate the achievable semigroup, so a missing strong
prime such as `7` propagates to `3*7`, `3^2*7`, and similar products unless a
new irreducible strong component realizes the value directly.

The minor-order language needs a preservation theorem.  Edge contraction in
the even-graph GF(2) shadow is not enough, because it can raise `H`.  If there
is a useful Wagner-style order here, its contraction should be
strong-component condensation or an OCF-packet reduction with an explicit
`H`-factor ledger, not arbitrary graph contraction in the even shadow.
