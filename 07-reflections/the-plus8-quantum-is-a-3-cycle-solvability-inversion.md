# The +8 quantum is a 3-cycle, and the solvability inversion

**The identity that reframes THM-866:** x(T) = Σd² = (n³−n)/3 − 8·c3(T), so the canonical
distance to transitivity k(T) = ((n³−n)/3 − x)/8 **equals the number of cyclic triangles**,
and each tie-splitting flip annihilates exactly one net 3-cycle. The "axis" of the
metagraph is a 3-cycle abacus; "no holes in the levels" is an intermediate-value theorem
for c3. Proof = Kendall–Babington Smith + two lines (draft §5).

**Why this transcends the theorem.** S703 located the quintic's unsolvability at "two
cyclic triangles sharing one vertex" — the perfectness of A₅ — with smallest carrier the
round tournament C₅. Now the SAME atom (the 3-cycle) is revealed as the exact quantum of
the +8 climb. So the two dynamics differ precisely in how atoms interact:

> In a tournament, 3-cycles are *counted* — a ℤ-graded mass that tie-splits decrement one
> at a time; every tournament sorts in exactly c3 moves. In a group, 3-cycles are
> *conjugated against each other* — two overlapping ones regenerate, the derived series
> stalls, the quintic has no formula. Same atoms, abelianized vs not.
> **The x-axis is what the derived series would be if 3-cycles commuted.**

**The parity divider (proved).** |Aut(tournament)| is odd, always (an involution would
reverse the arc inside a swapped pair) ⇒ by **Feit–Thompson every tournament's symmetry
group is solvable**. The unsolvability obstruction is structurally un-hostable in the
tournament category: A₅ (even order) acts on no 5-tournament, while the solvable-quintic
group F₂₀'s largest odd subgroup ℤ₅ IS Aut(C₅) — realized by the n=5 floor, where all 24
labelled floor tournaments are copies of C₅ with c3 = 5 = k_max. Solvable quintics have
tournament-sized symmetry; unsolvable ones exceed what tournaments can carry. This is the
categorical reading of "parity is the tamer" (S316): digit-0/Rédei tameness and the
always-sorting +8 walk are two faces of the same odd-order world.

**Three towers, one shape.** Radical/derived tower (halts at perfect A₅) — 2-adic OCF
digit tower (digit 1 dies at n=7, THM-865's locker refutation is its arithmetic-family
witness) — +8 level tower (never stalls; = c3 descent). The first two have irreducible
cores; the third is free. The interesting mathematics lives in the FIBERS of the third
(which tournaments share a level), where the first two towers' obstructions reappear.
