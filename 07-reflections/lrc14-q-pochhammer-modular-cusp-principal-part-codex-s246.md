Source: codex-2026-06-27-S246.

The useful part of the q-Pochhammer prompt is not just the product
`(q;q)_infty`, but the whole product/tail/derivative/cusp square:

- product: `(q;q)_infty = prod(1-q^n)`;
- recursive tail: `1/(q;q)_infty = sum p(n)q^n`;
- derivative: `q d/dq log((q;q)_infty) = -sum sigma_1(n)q^n`;
- modular guardrail: a modular function meromorphic at `i infinity` has only
  finitely many negative q-powers.

This gives a strong LRC14 interpretation.  A product quotient is allowed to
compress infinitely many tail coefficients only if its polar/principal part is
finite.  In packet language: every negative coefficient is proof debt, and every
unit of debt must have a name before the quotient is legal.

`q^-1+q^-2+...`, which is the clean modular image of the failure mode in
HYP-2963-style quotients: an infinite hidden residual ledger.
