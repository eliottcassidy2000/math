## THE GHOST THEOREM
Call a component of the asymptotic (Jelonek) set A(F) of a Keller map F: ℂⁿ→ℂⁿ a
**ghost** if the local monodromy around it is nontrivial. THEOREM: *there is no
Galois (regular-monodromy) Keller counterexample all of whose asymptotic
components are ghosts — in any dimension n ≥ 2.*

PROOF. F not an automorphism ⟹ F non-injective (injective polynomial maps are
automorphisms) ⟹ generic degree d ≥ 2, and A(F) ≠ ∅ (else F proper étale over
simply-connected ℂⁿ ⟹ d = 1). Over U = ℂⁿ∖A(F), F is a proper étale covering of
degree d with connected total space V = ℂⁿ∖F⁻¹(A(F)). (i) *Persisting sheets are
monodromy-fixed*: if p ∈ F⁻¹(c) for c a smooth point of a component, étaleness at
p gives a local section on a full ball around c, hence a single-valued sheet
around the meridian — fixed by the local monodromy g. (ii) Galois means the deck
action is regular, so every g ≠ e is fixed-point-free on the fiber. (iii) All
components ghosts ⟹ g ≠ e at each ⟹ no persisting sheets ⟹ the fiber over every
generic point of A(F) is EMPTY ⟹ F⁻¹(A(F)) is contained in finitely many
(étale-finite) fibers ⟹ finite. (iv) Then V = ℂⁿ minus a finite set (real
codimension 2n ≥ 4) is simply connected, so V→U is the universal covering and
π₁(U) has order d < ∞. (v) But H₁(U;ℤ) ≅ ℤʳ with r = #components ≥ 1 (Alexander
duality for hypersurface complements in ℂⁿ) — infinite. Contradiction. ∎

## Corollaries
1. **Abelian-monodromy counterexamples with all-ghost asymptotic sets are
   impossible** (abelian + transitive = regular).
2. **The dim-3 kernel's S₃ was forced**: a degree-3 counterexample has monodromy
   ℤ/3 (Galois) or S₃; by the theorem a ℤ/3 one needs a *silent* (trivial-
   monodromy) asymptotic component — and the meridians generate the monodromy
   (Zariski–van Kampen), so silent components contribute nothing: an all-meridian-
   generated ℤ/3 needs ghosts, which the theorem forbids in the all-ghost case.
   The kernel took the S₃ route: VERIFIED — its caustic carries a persisting
   sheet (fiber = 1 at the exact caustic point (0,1,1); transpositions fix a
   sheet), so it is not all-ghost.
3. Any degree-2 Keller counterexample (any n) must carry ≥ 1 ghost (to generate
   ℤ/2) AND evade (iii): impossible in the all-ghost case ⟹ it needs a silent
   component that its own monodromy does not see — a severe structural squeeze.
