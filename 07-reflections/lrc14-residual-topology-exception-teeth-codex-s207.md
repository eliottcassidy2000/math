# LRC14 Residual Topology-Exception Teeth - S207 Reflection

The hidden detail here is that "topology fails twice" was too vague.  S199
already showed arc topology separates `13` of the `15` coarse ET+unit
residual route fibers, and S200 showed the primitive `q<=13` deck splits all
residual route debt.  S207 overlays those two facts and finds that the two
topology failures are the same small object seen from two directions.

The incoming HYP-3040 hidden statement ledger and HYP-3039 hidden-coordinate
rule made the right reading clearer: compact topology is a legal quotient only
while its missing coordinate is named.  The unrelated-looking HYP-3038 q=23
drop/add square matches the pattern too, because scalar coincidence becomes
harmless only after endpoint-owner data is reattached.  Here the corresponding
reattached data is owner stalk plus primitive denominator deck.

HYP-3041's AP-tail puncture atlas is an even closer warning from the target
automatic fiber: a mod-14 owner strip can remember the local shape but forget
the exact-period puncture bit.  The two topology-exception collars should be
handled with the same discipline: topology first, then the missing
owner/period coordinate before route labels.

The failing topology buckets are exactly:

```text
9->99  covering  versus  9->155  q-witness
11->121 covering  versus 11->163 q-witness
```

All four rows are strict-open single-swap collars.  The covering rows have no
primitive safe mass for `2<=q<=13` and first appear at `q=14`; the Q-witness
rows have first primitive safe denominator equal to the drop speed (`9` or
`11`) and to their `q_threshold`.  The coarse largest-safe-component stalk
also splits both buckets.  This makes the exceptions owner-strip repairs, not
new boundary/open debt and not route-label debt.

The proof target becomes sharper: after status convergence, use arc topology
as the first tooth; if topology does not split, prove the row is one of these
single-swap collar forms and split it by owner stalk or the primitive
`q<=13` deck.  That is a much smaller statement than "classify all residual
routes."

Assumption challenged: the vertices were not runners or route labels.  They
were exception repair carriers: topology, owner stalk, primitive denominator
deck, exact stalk, and route-label sink.  The quotient preserves strict-open
status but destroys theorem route until the exception carrier is reattached.
