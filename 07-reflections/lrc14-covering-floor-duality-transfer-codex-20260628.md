# LRC14 Covering-Floor Duality Transfer

The useful connection from the older duality work is narrower than the slogans.
Even/odd was already recorded as "2-adic fold versus coordinate resurrection";
S259 says the covering floor binds on the even side.  So the floor should not
start from the odd/coprime witness.  That witness lives at `t=1/2`, exactly
where every even speed disappears.

The corrected picture is:

```text
even fold gives the floor-bearing window;
odd data is phase-cover debt on that window;
additive/multiplicative energy is useful only if it becomes SPEC penalty;
owner cuts are finite sidecars that expose the same 2-adic debt.
```

This resolves the apparent tension between S558 and S259.  S558 says the
even-good set `G` exists and the remaining question is whether odd danger arcs
cover it.  S259 says the actual optimum is pulled by even binders.  Both are
true: `G` is not a disposable solved half; it is the geometric object on which
the two-adic descent has to prove a floor, while the odd sector becomes a
positional blanket/phase obligation.

The additive/multiplicative bridge should also be demoted into a transfer rule.
HYP-2272 gives a real structural theorem: the additive pair-sum face is
multiplicative energy.  HYP-2128/HYP-2129 add the odd-square/parity shell
`8*C(14,2)+1=27^2`.  But for the covering floor, those facts only matter after
they are cashed out as low-mode SPEC mass or an odd phase-cover debt.  Otherwise
they remain in the off-path census/equality layer.

The finite owner-cut work now looks less like a separate side quest.  The
frontier current `{2:g2,11:g1,13:g1}` is one even-cover label plus two binding
labels.  That is exactly the finite combinatorial trace one would expect from a
floor whose analytic obstruction is two-adic but whose residual packets still
carry binding labels.

The incoming HYP-3422 two-adic relocation result makes that analytic branch
concrete.  It replaces "even fold" as a slogan by the interval obligation
`E_safe` intersecting one of the two odd half-lift filters.  In this reflection
that is the floor-side descent companion to the signed-SPEC branch.

The incoming HYP-3421 off-grid transparency/Rprime closure is the analytic
side of that same transfer.  It removes free-floating resonance anxiety only
after the full optimum is retained: danger is grid-local, the checked optima are
off-grid, and the resulting route feeds signed SPEC/Rprime rather than the
failed coprime-only witness.

The incoming HYP-3423 topology/arithmetic guardrail explains why the attractive
`C2`, `C3`, `C6`, and `Q(sqrt(-7))` language still cannot close the covering
floor by itself.  Those quotients preserve residue and equioscillation
structure, but the floor is q-specific magnitude data.  So every topological or
Galois packet must either resurrect arithmetic/floor sidecars or be marked as
off-path census.

The incoming HYP-3420 owner-cut chiral synthesis strengthens that branch: the
owner/chiral sidecar is exit-exact on the scanned expanded banks, with size-one
owner cuts for the current residue-mixed families.  In this reflection it lives
as the finite sidecar exit, not as the covering-floor theorem itself.

The next proof-facing obligation is therefore a transfer lemma, not another
scalar invariant:

```text
covering packet
-> two-adic even descent / signed SPEC floor
-> odd phase-cover debt, owner-current sidecar, add-mult energy penalty,
   HYP-3422 interval relocation, HYP-3421 off-grid/Rprime transparency,
   HYP-3423 topology-to-magnitude guardrail, or off-path 7-adic filter.
```

Tournament Analysis should keep the vertices as proof obligations and transfer
gates.  Runners, residues, parity sectors, Fourier modes, owner labels, and
additive-pair shells were considered, but any one of them alone destroys too
much of the covering-floor predicate; HYP-3423 is the warning label for that
destruction when the quotient is topological or Galois.
