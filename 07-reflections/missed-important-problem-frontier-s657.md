# S657 Reflection: Missed Problem Frontier Carrier Atlas

Prompt: search around the repo and web for possible important math problems we
have missed and could make strides on.

The useful correction was that "missed" should not mean "the repo never typed
the name."  It should mean "the repo has not yet built the finite carrier that
would let us make a small honest theorem or obstruction ledger."  Under that
definition, several world-famous problems are less actionable than quieter
finite/restricted shadows already adjacent to our machinery.

The best candidates are:

1. Hadwiger-Nelson finite unit-distance coloring, because the repo already has
   Moser/Sawin carriers, direction masks, Hamiltonian-spine questions, and
   `chi` side-channel prompts.
2. Graph reconstruction, because deletion decks are the external version of
   our endpoint-transfer and sufficient-statistic programs.
3. Kakeya/Falconer incidence geometry, because finite-field direction coverage
   is a clean toy model for direction support, distance fibers, Paley
   orientations, and unit-distance energy.
4. Sunflower/extractor set systems, because OCF odd-cycle supports are already
   hypergraphs but we are not retaining core/petal side channels.
5. Union-closed families, because closure and frequency witnesses are close to
   cauldron finite-sums, divisor carriers, and observer/source predicates.

The web/source anchors checked were Clay's Millennium list, MathWorld's
Hadwiger-Nelson page, IPAM's Kakeya overview, Open Problem Garden's
Erdos-Hajnal entry, the Annals sunflower bound, the Theory of Computing
robust-sunflower/extractor bridge, Nagel's union-closed frequency notes, the
Caccetta-Haggkvist survey, a SIAM Rota basis paper, and reconstruction-number
data from RIT.

Tournament Analysis used external problem clusters as vertices.  The pairwise
observable was

```text
(repo_leverage, near_term_actionability, undercoverage,
 external_importance, source_confidence, -raw_fame_chasing_risk).
```

Candidate vertex sets challenged first: named conjectures, proof techniques,
invariant families, finite toy models, and local carrier programs.  Clusters
won because the preserved predicate is "could this workspace make a concrete
stride soon?"  The quotient destroys detailed literature status, so every proof
claim still needs a focused literature audit.

The resulting tournament is transitive, with one Hamiltonian path and no
directed 3-cycles:

```text
hadwiger_nelson
> graph_reconstruction
> kakeya_falconer
> sunflower_extractors
> caccetta_haggkvist
> union_closed
> erdos_hajnal
> erdos_distinct_distances
> rota_ryser_rainbow

```

The most useful next sessions are small-first, not grand:

- **S657a:** color-critical fingerprints for finite unit-distance carriers.
- **S657b:** deck reconstruction atlas for tournament invariants.
- **S657c:** finite-field Kakeya/Falconer incidence ledger.
- **S657d:** sunflower-core scan inside OCF support hypergraphs.
- **S657e:** union-closed frequency tournaments on elements versus sets.

This also gives a sanity check for future brainstorming: fame is only a
coefficient.  The repo should prefer problems where a local carrier already
preserves the right predicate, or where a controlled failure would teach us
what side channel we are missing.
