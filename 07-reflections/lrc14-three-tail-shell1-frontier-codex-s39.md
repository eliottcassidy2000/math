# LRC14 Three-Tail Shell-1 Frontier

The temptation was to keep pushing the three-tail AP-tail scan by brute force: raise the bound from `35`, or run the unbounded comb script until it finishes. That was the wrong first quotient. The interrupted runs were useful precisely because they showed the proof was spending time on packets that the new KPS carry-conservation law already knows how to classify.

The shell-1 gate is the missing first cut. Among four-hole AP bases, the worst crude three-tail comb cutoffs are overwhelmingly bases that delete one of `{1,2,4,8}`. HYP-2661 says sub-second rows must keep that whole dyadic tower, so those worst packets should not enter the exact residue enumeration at all.

This feels like the right kind of progress: not a new scalar, but a better order of quotients. First preserve the Glaisher carrier. Then preserve the root packet. Then preserve mouth ownership. Only then ask the comb to do finite arithmetic.

The remaining shell-1-full frontier is still nontrivial. The worst base is `holes=(3,5,6,13)`, not one of the obvious top damaged packets. That gives the next concrete place to stare: what endpoint-owner or old-mouth feature makes that base expensive for the crude comb, and why should it still pay the second threshold after tails are added?

The general lesson matches the repo's newer invariant discipline. Replacement count is a scalar shadow. Tail bound is a scalar shadow. The proof carrier is the addressed packet: shell-1 tower, holes, tails, mouth survivor, endpoint owners, and then measure.
