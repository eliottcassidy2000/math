# LRC(14): the underlying object is a ramified sheet cover

*codex-2026-07-14-S3/S4/S5 — synthesis after the `q<=25` refutation,
binding-scale packet theorem, height-twelve owner census, and seven-exception
endpoint correction.*

## The failed shortcut exposed the missing coordinate

The proposed universal good-period bound `q<=25` looked plausible because it
was true in several carefully generated banks.  It is false.  The coherent
family

```text
26*{1,...,12} union {339}
```

first witnesses at `2/27`, while a gcd-incoherent residual first witnesses at
`3/26`.  The error was not merely that 25 was a little too small.  A bounded
denominator deck forgets the scale at which residue classes become coherent.
The denominator is an observer, not the object.

The same omission occurred in the old prime-13 rigidity slogan.  A tight
twelve-speed set with no 13-multiple is indeed forced onto the full nonzero
residue fibre.  But a 13-multiple blocks every shallow `a/13` point.  A global
maximum can then live at `p/(13s)`, and the extra integer `s` changes the
geometry completely.  Prime residues describe only the height-one fibre.

## What the historical viewpoints each saw

The repository has accumulated many honest projections of the same object.
Each sharpened the question, and each also forgot something.

1. **Danger-tooth circle covers.**  The functions `||vt||` turn the problem
   into a union of periodic open arcs.  This gives exact safe components,
   endpoint slopes, and component-tooth containment.  It sees the global
   quantifier in `t`, but raw component counts forget ownership and scale.

2. **Farey and pair-sum rulers.**  Rational maxima occur at pair-sum,
   pair-difference, and half-turn cusps.  This makes exact `M` computation
   possible and identifies the two active slopes at a maximum.  A list of
   candidate fractions forgets why all other times are blocked.

3. **Residue and signed-unit decks.**  At a fixed modulus, zero owners and
   signed unit pairs exactly decide the small-period witness problem.  This is
   the right replacement for the false `q<=25` claim through `q=28`.  It
   forgets how decks at different moduli are coupled by one integer family.

4. **Core/killer peeling.**  Removing the largest speed exposes a loose core
   whose safe components must all fit teeth of the last runner.  THM-759's
   ratio bound and THM-766's projective tooth cones make this quantitative.
   Scalar ratios and total safe measure forget simultaneous component
   alignment.

5. **Scale quotients and sheet lifting.**  Writing a coherent core as `cP`
   turns every core time into `c` lifts.  Exceptional runners burn subsets of
   these sheets.  THM-760/761 show why raw speed cutoffs cannot be uniform and
   close broad low-exception rays.  Capacity alone becomes sharp at seven
   exceptions, where overlaps and ramification matter.

6. **Endpoint ownership.**  `chi=kappa-P` records safe-component defect after
   protected end/start splices.  In the seven-owner sheet problem,
   THM-771's exact refinement is

   ```text
   free sheets = capacity slack + overlap debt - ramification surplus.
   ```

   Ownership repairs what component counts forgot.  Pairwise ownership still
   forgets multiple simultaneous incidences.

7. **Additive structure, three gaps, discrepancy, and Fourier energy.**  These
   viewpoints explain why arithmetic progressions are unusually efficient
   blockers and why detuning often creates room.  They are valuable stability
   coordinates, but no scalar energy presently retains the exact strict
   endpoint and all-times cover predicate.

8. **Tournaments and metagraphs.**  Tournaments compress comparisons, expose
   gauge dependence, and organize switching histories.  They are excellent
   diagnostics for which information a quotient has discarded.  They have
   not been faithful theorem carriers when vertices are chosen to be runners
   by default.

9. **Finite exact computation.**  Exact rational sweeps falsified the period
   shortcut, repaired the missing half-turn cusp, and proved enormous bounded
   classifications.  THM-770 represents `13^12` conceptual packets by a
   24,008-cell owner CSP and finds only thirteen zero-defect dilates.  The
   lesson is to enumerate obligations and incidences, not raw tuples.

These are not competing descriptions.  They are coordinate charts on a
single ramified incidence object.

## The scale-residue-sheet packet

At the inner threshold `1/13`, choose a reduced global maximizer of a putative
tight twelve-set:

```text
t*=p/(13s).
```

THM-769 proves that every multiplied residue lies in `[s,12s]`, both endpoints
occur, and endpoint owners are divisible by `s`.  Split

```text
E={v:s|v}=sU,               F=A\E.
```

For every quotient time `tau`, the map `t -> st=tau` has the `s` lifts

```text
t_j=(tau+j)/s,              j in Z/sZ.
```

Define the exact incidence stalk

```text
I(tau)={(w,j) in F x Z/sZ : ||w(tau+j)/s||<=1/13}.
```

The base is the loose set

```text
G_U={tau:min_(u in U)||u tau||>1/13}.
```

Then the deep packet is tight exactly when the projection

```text
I(tau) -> Z/sZ
```

is surjective for **every** `tau in G_U`.  Each exception has effective sheet
order

```text
D_w=s/gcd(w,s).
```

This object simultaneously retains:

- scale `s`;
- quotient-core geometry `G_U`;
- exception identity;
- sheet multiplicity and ramification;
- closed endpoint convention;
- persistent, not pointwise, coverage.

It is a finite fibre over a one-dimensional semialgebraic base.  Calling it a
ramified sheet cover is literal: gcds merge sheets into repeated grid points,
and endpoint events change the labelled incidence without changing the base.

The shallow branch is `s=1`.  Its stalk has one sheet, and global tightness at
all twelve prime-grid points forces the full nonzero residue system.  The deep
branch begins at `s=2`.  With two exceptions, primitivity and capacity force
exactly ten even speeds plus two odd speeds; the two odd owners must carry
opposite nearest-integer parity on every component of `G_U`.  With three
unramified exceptions, the equality edge is `s=3` and the owners must carry
all three colours everywhere.  The residue theorem has become a persistent
colour-cover theorem.

## The outer LRC(14) sheet wall is the same object one level up

For a thirteen-speed LRC(14) family written `cP union W`, a core-safe time `x`
again has lifts `(x+k)/c`.  The exceptional runners define an owner-by-sheet
incidence matrix.  At seven exceptions, THM-771 shows that the correct defect
is

```text
F=Q+Omega-sigma,
```

not a raw union bound and not a Kirchhoff handoff law.  Here `Q` is unused
owner capacity, `Omega` is multiple ownership, and `sigma` is capacity created
by ramification.  In the unramified layer an endpoint event tears every exact
strict cover; the event mesh is `gcd(w,c)/w`, so sufficiently high **reduced**
winding forces a lonely sheet.  In the ramified layer, surplus can be paid by
overlap, as the primitive `c=21` example shows.

Thus the inner n=12 rigidity problem and the outer seven-exception LRC(14)
wall are recursive instances of one schema:

```text
loose base set + finite sheet fibre + labelled danger incidences
    + endpoint monodromy + ramification defect.
```

The problem contains a smaller copy of itself at every coherent scale.

## What Tournament Analysis says after the carrier is corrected

The useful tournament vertices are proof obligations, sheets, endpoint
events, or owner runs—not automatically runners.

- In the small-period audit, changing the modulus gauge flips many pairwise
  orientations while the witness verdict is unchanged.
- In THM-770, all 66 pairwise runner comparisons tie on every zero-defect leaf;
  a tie Hamiltonian path can order them, but it cannot distinguish the thirteen
  dilates.  The endpoint-owner hypergraph does.
- In THM-771, owner-adjacency tournaments distinguish two example rows by
  cycle fingerprints, yet they discard `Q`, `Omega`, and `sigma`, the three
  fields in the exact theorem.

This is a productive negative result.  The tournament is a controlled
forgetful functor from the incidence deck.  Edge flips and SCCs measure how
unstable that forgetting is.  A tournament proof should therefore begin by
stating which LRC predicate survives the quotient.  If simultaneous sheet
coverage is the predicate, a hypergraph or labelled bipartite deck is usually
the minimal faithful carrier.

## New formulations of the remaining hard statements

The old statement “the sporadic n=12 branch is empty” is exact but opaque.  It
can now be split into two more structural conjectures.

### Shallow descent rigidity

> If a primitive full-nonzero-residue twelve-packet has zero endpoint defect,
> then either it lies in THM-770's height-twelve box or its owner deck admits a
> strict gcd/scale descent to a smaller primitive zero-defect packet.

THM-770 would then terminate the descent at `{1,...,12}`.  The missing
ingredient is not another residue sieve; it is a monotone height carried by
protected owner incidences.

### Deep persistent-colour obstruction

> No primitive hereditary twelve-packet satisfying the divisor pins and
> THM-759/766 tooth cones supports a surjective off-sheet incidence stalk over
> every component of its quotient loose set.

At two exceptions this asks for one `tau in G_U` where an odd owner is
ineligible or the two nearest-integer parities agree.  At three exceptions it
asks for a missing colour.  This is far more local than enumerating all tight
twelve-sets, but it retains the all-times quantifier that scalar capacity lost.

For the outer seven-exception wall the parallel target is:

> Classify the equality packets `Q+Omega=sigma` component by component and show
> that primitive ramified packets either meet a reduced-winding event or descend
> to a smaller sheet cover.

## A concrete next research program

1. Enumerate quotient cores `U` up to normalized tooth-cone type, not by raw
   height.  Compute the exact components of `G_U`.
2. Replace each exceptional runner by its componentwise eligibility interval
   and colour word.  Solve persistent surjectivity as an endpoint-owner CSP.
3. Record endpoint monodromy: when `tau` crosses an event, which sheet loses or
   gains which owner?  Seek a parity or winding invariant incompatible with a
   full cover on the circle.
4. Search for a descent potential built from primitive gcd, reduced sheet
   orders, owner-splice defect, and component width.  Raw maximum speed is not
   scale invariant and should not be the potential.
5. Use tournaments only after this deck is constructed: orient event pairs by
   private-sheet transfer, report ties/cycles/SCCs/edge flips, and test exactly
   which quotient fields are needed to reconstruct surjectivity.

The frontier has therefore moved in a meaningful way even though the uniform
n=12 theorem is not yet proved.  The unknown object is no longer “a strange
set of twelve integers.”  It is a primitive, recursively ramified cover of a
loose-set base by finite sheet fibres, with endpoint ownership as its local
state and simultaneous coverage as its global predicate.
