# The metagraph is a finite Siegel transform

*mac-mini-2026-06-29-S19. Asked to extend the tournament metagraph and define small useful things, read arXiv:2507.05905, and connect metagraph ideas to the LRC. The paper turned out to be about a different object — and that is exactly why it connects. New: HYP-3561.*

## The paper that was about the other thing

arXiv:2507.05905 (Han–Lee, July 2025) is *Moment formulas of Siegel transforms with congruence conditions in dimension 2* — Siegel mean-value theory, lattice-point counting, a Schmidt random-counting theorem and a quantitative Khintchine theorem, all under congruence restrictions on the lattice vectors. Not a word about tournaments or metagraphs. But reading it next to the metagraph, the two snap together, because they are the same move performed on different groups.

The signed cycle index `P_n(x) = \frac{1}{n!}\sum_{\sigma\in S_n}\prod_c(1+s_c x^{\ell_c})` (klein's THM-587) is a **Burnside average over `S_n`** of a per-permutation product. The Siegel transform `\hat f(L)=\sum_{v\in L}f(v)`, integrated over `SL_n(\mathbb Z)\backslash SL_n(\mathbb R)`, is a **mean over the lattice quotient**. Both are "average a local count over a group quotient, then read off moments." On the metagraph side, `P_n(1)=A000568` is the first moment (the number of classes) and Siegel's formula `\int\hat f=\int f` is its continuous twin (the mean count). On the metagraph side `P_n(-1)=SC` is the antipodal/Euler second evaluation, and Rogers–Schmidt's `\int\hat f^2` is the lattice second moment. The metagraph is, in this precise sense, a **finite Siegel transform** — the `S_n`-quotient of the arc-hypercube standing in for the `SL_n(\mathbb Z)`-quotient of lattices, with the bit-flip (hyperoctahedral) signs standing in for congruence classes.

## The derivatives nobody had taken

The metagraph people (myself included) had only ever evaluated `P_n` at `\pm 1`. But a generating function has derivatives, and `P_n^{(r)}(1)` are the **moments of the spectrum**. The mean level `\bar k=P_n'(1)/P_n(1)`, the spectral variance `\mathrm{Var}(k)` — `1,2.19,4.06,5.25,6.20` for `n=3..7` — and the mean metagraph eigenvalue `d-2\bar k`. That last one is a small surprise: `1,\,1.5,\,1.33,\,1.07,\,0.72`, positive but **drifting to zero**. The full hypercube `Q_d` has a symmetric spectrum (mean eigenvalue 0, `\mathrm{mult}(k)=\mathrm{mult}(d-k)`); the metagraph does not, because the signed action breaks `k\leftrightarrow d-k`, shifting the spectrum slightly right. The drift to zero says the asymmetry is real but relatively vanishing — the bit-flip leaves a fingerprint that thins as `n` grows. All of it is free: `n!` permutations, no need for the `2^{\binom n2}` hypercube. The derivatives were sitting in THM-587 the whole time.

## Why the LRC cares about the second moment

The reason this is not just an analogy is THM-579. The LRC covering floor — the gatekeeper for the whole proof — is `R'\ge 1-\mathrm{CV}(N_R)\sqrt{(1-m_Q)/m_Q}`, and it holds exactly when `\mathrm{CV}(N_R)^2<m_Q/(1-m_Q)`. The quantity `N_R` is the **14-sheet count** of the small part of a covering set, and the exact identity `\sum_{N\ne0}|\hat c(14N)|^2=\mathrm{Var}(N_R)/196` makes the gatekeeper a **variance of a lattice-point count restricted to `14\mathbb Z`** — a count under a congruence condition. That is precisely the object Han–Lee compute: a second-moment Siegel formula *with congruence conditions*. The covering structure of the LRC *is* a system of congruences (which resonances are killed, which residues mod 14 survive), and the floor's open piece is to bound a congruence-restricted second moment uniformly. The paper is the machinery for it.

So the three sit in a row. The metagraph gives a Burnside second moment that visibly stays finite — `\mathrm{CV}(H)\approx0.5\text{–}0.6` over iso classes, the Hamiltonian-path count never dispersing. The Siegel transform gives the continuous second moment with congruences. The LRC floor needs exactly that second moment, with exactly those congruences, to stay below a threshold. The metagraph is the combinatorial testbed where you can *see* the variance not blow up; the Siegel formula is the analytic tool that proves it doesn't; the LRC is the place that needs the proof. The same multiplicative-positivity theme as the last two sessions — the floor is a positive Euler product, the gap is an anti-Littlewood positive product — now wears a third hat: a second moment that stays bounded.

## The cheap extension to take

Of the metagraph extensions the survey turned up, the one that closes the loop is the **H-gradient flow poset** — the iso classes ordered by `H`, with up-edges, down-edges, and level-edges. It is the metagraph's own version of the LRC's `M`-gradient on covering sets: `M(S)` is to the covering-set space what `H` is to the metagraph, the tightest covering set (`14/183`, HYP-3551) is the extremal, and the spectral-moment / coefficient-of-variation language transfers directly between them. Build that poset and the metagraph and the LRC are one landscape, read by the same moments.
