# Flipping an Arc Is Not Independent: the Recursive Sub-Ranking Tree

A response to the observation that `2^C(n,2)` "independent arcs" miss the essence:
a tournament is a **ranking** (Hamiltonian path) built recursively from
sub-rankings that either align with the current (transitive) or run against it
(cyclic), and this hidden dependence is what the **tiling model** captures.
This is exactly right, it is quantified by `H`, and it is precisely why LRC
resists the pure-metagraph reframe (S519). Computed support below.

## The essence collapses: dependence layers

```
n :   2^C(n,2) (free arcs)   ->   A000568(n) (iso)   ->   circular / runner-realizable
3 :        8                        2                        2
4 :       64                        4                        2
5 :     1024                       12                        4
6 :    32768                       56                        6
7 :  2097152                      456                       10        (8 -> 16)
```

(The realizable counts are `2,2,4,6,10,16 = 2·Fib(m-2)`, S518.) The "independent
arc" count is astronomically larger than the *essence*. Two collapses happen:
`2^C(n,2) → A000568` (mod relabelling) and `A000568 → 2·Fib(m-2)` (the
runner-realizable / circular slice). The arcs are nowhere near free.

## A flip is not local: the realizable set is no subcube

If arcs were independent for the realizable family, that family would be a
coordinate **subcube** — closed under single-arc flips. It is not. Fraction of
single-arc flips of a circular (round, runner-realizable) tournament that *stay*
circular:

```
n=5:  20/40  = 0.500
n=6:  36/90  = 0.400
n=7:  70/210 = 0.333      (decreasing toward 0)
```

A single flip mostly **ejects** you from the realizable family. So the realizable
tournaments are a thin, curved, *dependent* slice of the hypercube, not an
axis-aligned subcube. "Flipping an arc is less independent than we think" is
literally true: a lone flip usually destroys the recursive-ranking coherence that
made the tournament realizable.

## What the dependence IS: `H` = #rankings sharing the arcs

The tiling model names the dependence. Fix the base Hamiltonian path
`n→…→1` (the main ranking); the non-base arcs are **tiles**, each recording
whether a sub-ranking aligns with or reverses the current — the GF(2)
**cut ⊕ cycle** split (base-path arcs span the *score/ranking* space, the
`C(n-1,2)` tiles span the *cyclic* space). The staircase recursion is your tree of
sub-rankings. The binding identity:

> **Tilings × |Aut| = H** — the arc-set jointly supports `H` Hamiltonian-path
> rankings, which **share arcs**; flipping one arc perturbs *all `H`* at once.

So `H` (the directed Ham-path count) is exactly the hidden coupling among the
arcs — and it is the very quantity that turned out to be the **loneliness meter**
(S26): low `H` = few rankings = bunched/lonely; high `H` = many interlocked
rankings = evenly spread. The recursive-ranking tree and the loneliness of a
runner configuration are the same structure.

## Why this is the crux of LRC (and why S519's reframe failed)

The runner clock's wall-crossings *are* arc-flips, but they are driven by a
**single parameter `t`** (and the speeds): every arc `(i,j)` flips at the times
`m/(2|v_i-v_j|)`, all slaved to one clock. So the realizable runner-walk is a
**1-parameter** path, the ultimate hidden dependence — not a free walk in the
`2^C(n,2)` cube nor even a free walk in the metagraph. This is precisely why the
S519 attempt to make LRC a pure graph property (lonely classes = feedback vertex
set) **could not work**: it implicitly treated flips as free moves, but the
realizable flips are recursively/arithmetically coupled. The coupling *is* the
conjecture.

The constructive consequence: **the right coordinates for the LRC walk are not the
arcs but the recursive sub-ranking tree (the tiling/cut-cycle basis).** In those
coordinates:
- the base path = the runners' instantaneous ranking by position;
- the tiles = which pairs are "anti" (out of position order within a semicircle);
- a wall-crossing = a single tile toggling, but constrained by the recursion to
  keep the configuration *round* (realizable);
- loneliness (observer source) = a specific recursive-tree shape.

Characterizing the realizable walks — the open problem behind LRC@14 — is then
"which tile-toggle sequences are generated by a one-parameter circular sweep,"
i.e. an **interval-exchange in the tiling basis**. That is the honest next object,
and it is the bounded-ansatz/wall-order program (S514) expressed in the
dependence-aware (tiling) coordinates rather than the misleading free-arc ones.

## Takeaway

The free-arc `2^C(n,2)` picture is a coordinate artifact; the essence is the
recursive sub-ranking tree, whose coupling is measured by `H` (= loneliness). The
runner walk is a 1-parameter sweep through this dependent structure, which is why
LRC is neither a free-arc nor a free-graph problem — and why progress needs the
tiling/recursive coordinates, not independent flips.
