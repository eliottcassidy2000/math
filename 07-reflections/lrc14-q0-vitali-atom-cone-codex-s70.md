# LRC14 Q0, Vitali Atoms, and the Generated Cone

The useful q0/Vitali connection is not that both involve "small" sets.  It is
that both are failures of a tempting low-observer quotient.

For the tournament Vitali atom, the visible quotient says the reversal is
harmless: lambda is preserved, and the low cycle channels do not move in the
right net way.  Then the hidden endpoint-completion channel exposes the real
effect on `H`.

For LRC14, the analogous trap is to look at raw cover mass, raw miss-zeta
coordinates, or a signed odd/even slogan.  THM-561 says the exact object is
the factorial atom transform.  A unit `W_j` is a finite-difference atom move
`B_j`; it preserves every lower factorial observer and still moves `Q_0`.
That is the q0 Vitali move.

This changes the proof order.

1. First select relation-support packets: support-3 Schur/additive triangles,
   support-size tail, low-height exact relations, and small nonrelation
   denominators.
2. Then keep the generated miss-zeta word.  The arbitrary cone is too large;
   the LRC cone is generated by coherent sector automata over slow `x`.
3. Only after compatibility is proved should the proof evaluate
   `Q_0=sum_j(-1)^jW_j`.

The cell-automaton analogy belongs in step 2.  A row does not merely have an
atom vector `Q_t`; it has a cyclic word of missed-sector states, and coherent
carriers act as deletion/OR automata on that word.  The scalar `Q_0` is the
boundary readout of this automaton, not the automaton itself.

The S70 scout gives a useful warning.  The non-origin atom cone is much larger
than the origin atom: aggregate origin share is about `0.154`, and non-origin
tax is about `5.49 * sum|Q0|`.  This means a full-law dominance theorem is
probably the wrong theorem.  But the high factorial tail is also much larger
than `Q_0`, with `tail2_L1/sum|Q0|` about `15.93`, so a low-moment theorem is
also too weak.  The proof has to retain the middle object: generated
relation-supported factorial packets.

The abstract LP makes the warning sharper.  If all signed atom moves on
`t=0..6` are allowed, high missed-count states can hide a unit `q0` move more
cheaply than the local finite-difference basis: preserving `W_1` costs only
`7/5` by using `q_1=-6/5,q_6=1/5`, not the `3` units paid by `B_2`.  The
canonical basis names the channel, but the generated LRC cone must exclude or
tax these cheap high-state escapes.  That is where THM-558/HYP-2696 belongs in
the q0/Vitali proof.

The next plausible lemma is:

```text
After finite low-support relation packets are removed, every generated
high-tail factorial packet has origin boundary contribution bounded by its
available cap slack.
```

This is exactly the Vitali lesson in LRC language: do not quotient away the
completion channel until it has paid for the target functional.
