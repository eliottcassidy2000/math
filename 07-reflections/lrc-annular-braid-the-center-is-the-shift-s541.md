# The annular braid group image of LRC: the center is the shift symmetry

**Prompt (user):** consider deeply the annular braid group image of LRC.

Building on the autonomous explorer `oracle-2026-06-01-S540o`, which read the runner
worldlines as a **disk pure braid** on the cylinder `S^1 × [0,1]` and recovered our
invariants (linking `lk(i,j)=v_i-v_j` = tension; word length `Σ|v_i-v_j|` =
holdback; loneliness = a fat tube around the observer strand). That picture treats
the observer as just another strand. The **annular** braid group is sharper: it puts
the observer at the **hole**, and that single move makes three things click —
including *why* LRC only depends on speed differences.

## The image: a homogeneous torus braid in the annular braid group

Put the observer at the **center hole** of an annulus; the `n-1` runners are strands
in the annulus, winding around the hole. Then the annulus' **radial coordinate is
exactly the LRC quantity**:

```
radius of runner i at time t  =  distance to the observer  =  ||v_i t||.
```

Runner `i` winds around the hole **`v_i` times** (its speed = its winding number).
So the LRC system's image in the annular braid group `Z_{n-1}` (= the affine braid
group of type `Ã_{n-2}`, = `π_1` of `Conf_{n-1}` of the annulus) is the
**homogeneous torus braid with winding vector `(v_1,…,v_{n-1})`** — a thin,
explicitly arithmetic slice of the whole group.

**Generators (verified, `lrc_annular_braid_center_s541.py`).** The annular braid
group has the *cyclic* generators `σ_0,…,σ_{m-1}` (one more than the disk's `σ_1…
σ_{m-1}`); the extra one, `σ_0`, is the crossing **at the cut = a runner lapping the
observer**. Counting the LRC braid word:

```
#σ_0  (observer-wrap)   =  Σ v_i        (runner i laps the hole v_i times)
runner-runner crossings =  Σ_{i<j} |v_i - v_j|   (= holdback, S25/S540o)
```

The `σ_0` count `Σ v_i` is invisible in the disk picture; it is the **level** of the
affine-symmetric-group image — the total winding around the hole.

## The point: the CENTER of the annular braid group = the LRC shift symmetry

The annular braid group has a **center** generated by the full **core rotation `ρ`**
— rotate the whole annulus once, i.e. **add `1` to every winding number = add `1` to
every speed**. `ρ` is central; the braid of `v + c·1` is `ρ^c · β_v`.

Loneliness depends only on the pairwise differences `v_i - v_j` (the radial
distances between runners), which `ρ` fixes. Verified directly: every runner's
clear-collar radius is **unchanged** under `v → v + c·1` for `c = 10, 100`. So:

> **The center of the annular braid group acts trivially on the LRC question.** LRC
> is a statement in the **annular braid group modulo its center** — which is the
> **affine symmetric group `S̃_{n-1}`**. This is the braid-theoretic reason LRC
> reduces to speed *differences* / why we normalise away the global shift.

That single fact reorganises a lot:
- the `ρ`-cosets = the global-shift classes of speed systems;
- the **affine symmetric group `S̃`** = the alcove-walk world of S525 (the runner
  clock is an alcove walk; the affine permutation is the winding vector);
- "which runner is the observer" = a choice of *which strand is the hole*, and the
  per-runner loneliness values (AP `{0,1,2,3,4}`: edge runners tight at exactly
  `1/n`, middle runners looser — the extremal AP again) are the collars of the
  re-centred systems.

## The deep reading

LRC, in the annular braid group, is: **the homogeneous torus braid with winding
vector `v`, projected to the affine symmetric group (forgetting the central core
rotation), has a height where the hole's collar is fat (`≥ 1/n`).** The conjecture
is a property of the **affine-symmetric-group shadow of a torus braid** — and the
center being exactly the shift symmetry explains why only the *differences* (the
finite, `S̃`-level data) can matter. The disk pure braid (S540o) sees the linking
(differences) but not the hole; the annulus adds the radial coordinate
(`= ||v_i t||`), the wrap generator (`= Σ v_i`), and the center (`=` the shift) — the
three things LRC is actually about.
