# Obstruction Carrier Atlas S639

The shared object is not a number.  It is a carrier:

```text
scalar quotient + hidden witness + side-channel ledger.
```

That sentence now covers a surprising amount of the repo.

`H=21` is not just an absent Hamiltonian-path count.  It is absent after the
strong-component and odd-cycle side channels are retained: `H>=1+2c3`, and the
`c3<=10` strong population vanishes at `m=13`.

LRC `n=14` is not just `n=14`.  It is `C=2n-1=27=3^3`, split into unit shells,
nonunit shells, owner/carry/lift data, and 64 self-converse round classes.  The
AP/V* distinction lives exactly where the nonunit shell ledger can see it.

Unit-distance `n=21` is not a raw echo of tournament `H=21`.  It is `57` unit
edges, split as a `20`-edge Hamiltonian spine plus `37=C_hex(3)` bulk.  The
number `21` is the vertex count; the useful proof packet is spine/bulk plus
traceability.

A000568 is not only the next hard sequence value.  It is Burnside strata,
self-converse fixed layers, q-shadows, merged/nonfixed companions, and
transporters.  The companion sequences are not decoration; they are the side
channels that make hard terms legible.

Schanuel/pi-e is not only "is `e+pi` rational?"  It is trace, norm,
discriminant, `log(pi)`, and transverse power sums.  S638's lonely-shadow
fallout says that if one scalar descended, the neighboring shadows become even
more important.

Twin primes and Goldbach sit naturally in the same language.  A twin prime is
not just a gap of `2`; it is a pair surviving every local residue channel.  A
Goldbach representation is not just an additive split; it is a local-prime
ledger plus a global representation count, with major/minor arc side channels.

The little S639 computation made this concrete.  For LRC `C=27`, the antipodal
shell strata are:

```text
gcd 1: 1,2,4,5,7,8,10,11,13
gcd 3: 3,6,12
gcd 9: 9
```

For twin primes, primorial filters keep fewer and fewer residue classes:

```text
mod 2:    1/2
mod 6:    1/6
mod 30:   3/30
mod 210:  15/210
mod 2310: 135/2310
```

For Goldbach at `N=42`, the same filters keep a different local packet because
the target sum interacts with each prime:

```text
mod 2:    1/2
mod 6:    2/6
mod 30:   6/30
mod 210:  36/210
mod 2310: 324/2310
```

The important part is not those tiny numbers; it is the habit.  Before asking a
global question, name the local channels and price their contribution.

The transfer-method tournament ranked:

```text
side-channel jackknife
> spine/bulk decomposition
> finite-window extinction
> local-prime ledger
> transverse-shadow fallout
> Burnside transporters
> quotient-collapse test
> Schanuel-style completion
> raw scalar numerology.
```

That ranking feels right.  Schanuel-style completions are beautiful but too
expensive as a first move.  Raw scalar numerology is inspirational but unsafe.
The workhorse is jackknifing: delete one side channel, observe what escapes,
then promote the escaping channel into a proof obligation.

This suggests a practical program:

- For twin primes, build a residue-channel jackknife table, not just a singular
  series value.
- For Goldbach, split each representation count into local skeleton and
  flexible bulk, then monitor where minor-arc damage enters.
- For unit distance, search same-edge-count scalar twins with different
  spine/bulk/direction-support packets.
- For A000568, treat every hard term as a family of companion shadows.
- For LRC, continue pricing owner/carry/lift/unit-shell channels at `n=14`.

The common creative move is to stop asking whether the scalar is true.  Ask
which side channel makes it true, false, or undecidable by that quotient.
