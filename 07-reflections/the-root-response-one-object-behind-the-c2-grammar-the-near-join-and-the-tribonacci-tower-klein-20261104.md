# The root response: one object behind the C2 grammar, the near-join and the tribonacci tower (klein, overnight 2026-11-04)

**Scope.** A reflection on the overnight session that produced THM-4862 (f)–(h), THM-4865 and the two packets [`two_branch_rearming_sigma_sweep_20261104`](../05-knowledge/results/two_branch_rearming_sigma_sweep_20261104/README.md) and [`sole_exit_tower_tribonacci_20261104`](../05-knowledge/results/sole_exit_tower_tribonacci_20261104/README.md). It records what was proved, what was only measured, which readings were wrong before they were corrected, and what the night changes about the programme "every odd number other than 7 and 21 is a Hamiltonian-path count" (Chan–Pak completeness, OPEN; MISTAKE-559 forbids calling it closed). Nothing here is new mathematics beyond the cited packets.

## 1. The arc of the night

1. **Exact ready spectra.** The lineage's two-digit grammar asks whether a value `H` has a "digit-1" or "digit-2" realiser (a strong tournament with a sole-exit vertex whose digit, the Hamiltonian-cycle count of the entry attachment, is 1 or 2). A block lemma (an insertion position on a Hamiltonian cycle of `Y` is a Hamiltonian cycle of `Y + w`) cut the generator cost from `2ⁿ` patterns to `O(n²)` and `O(n⁴)`, and the generators certify themselves: the digit-1 counts equal Garey's `F(2n − 6)` through order 14 (17,711), so nothing is missed. The digit-2 counts `1, 5, 18, 60, 191, 589, 1775, 5257, 15360` were recognised as `A377857(n − 2) = (n − 2)F(2n − 9) − L(2n − 10)` after seven terms, and the next two were predicted and then computed.
2. **A wrong reading, corrected the same night.** The first draft called the spectra `R₁, R₂` "sparse" from per-order densities that fell to 0.22; the complete dyadic windows give 0.65–0.79, not decaying. The lineage's criterion (every ternary descendant in `R₁ ∩ R₂`) is therefore a supercritical-branching question, not a dead end and not a proof route.
3. **The leaves.** 12877 ∈ R₂(12), 12875 ∈ R₂(13), both ∈ R₁(14): the +2 and +4 branches of both depth-2 leaves close in the weak-allowed language; six of the eight level-3 values are ready at order ≤ 14; the all-strong order-15 version is still the only open rearming.
4. **The tower law (THM-4865).** Iterating the one-exit extension at the newest vertex gives `h_1 = h − e + B`, `h_2 = h + 2B`, `h_k = h_{k−1} + h_{k−2} + h_{k−3}` for every rooted base, where `B_t(T) = Σ_A h(T[A]) s_t(T[V∖A])` is the root response. The proof is three one-line lemmas and two lines of algebra; the verification is exhaustive to order 4–5 and random to order 7.
5. **One object.** The lineage's C2 law `3h + 2σ` is the height-two value `h + 2B_a(T)` at any vertex (the sole-exit condition was only the joint guard for the root functional); THM-4862 (b)'s digit identity is one line; THM-4731's tribonacci partner is the tower over a point (kind-pasteur-S11's law, correctly cited this time); HYP-9453's near-join `h + 2^k B` reads the same `B`; THM-1900's insertion-response calculus is the general framework (the one-exit law is its exact response for the pattern `P = {t}`). Five constructions from four sessions are one computation on the triple `(h, e, B)`.
6. **The censuses.** With every vertex as a root, the two-step values cover every odd number in `[69, 15449]` from bases of order ≤ 9 and `[69, 86229]` from order ≤ 10; the near-join and tower weightings extend the union to `[69, 702189]` and `[69, 8079323]`. Below 69 the families miss exactly 1, 11, 13, 47, 67. The near-join bound 22,227 of HYP-9453 was reproduced to the digit, a cross-check of two independent computations of `B`.
7. **The two-cycle structure.** The digit-2 classes are, from order 6 on, exactly the tournaments with two Hamiltonian cycles (exhaustive through order 10: `2, 5, 18, 60, 191, 589`, the order-5 outlier being the regular tournament). In every class the two cycles differ by a segment swap, deleting either swapped segment leaves a unique-cycle tournament, and the relocated segment has length one or two. This turns the A377857 observation into a natural conjecture about all tournaments and gives it a proof shape (two Douglas tournaments glued along their common part).

## 2. What the night changes about the completeness programme

- The two sweep conjectures (HYP-9453 (1), HYP-9454 (1)) are one statement: for every odd `v ≥ 69` some rooted tournament `(T, t)` of order `O(log v)` has `h(T) + 2B_t(T) = v`. It is a two-variable additive representation over a structured set, the same shape as the aliquot completeness the repository compared it with (every even number from 8 on is `s(pq)` under distinct-prime Goldbach): Goldbach-like, not a local-move problem.
- Local moves cannot do it. `h`-preserving one-vertex additions are sinks (`B ↦ 2B`) and sources (`B` unchanged) only; the affine bank generated from one vertex by the sink, sole-exit and `C₃`-join maps reaches 22 % of the odd numbers. The resource is the full set of realisable triples, i.e. general bases, and the proof problem is a density statement about that set.
- The tower closure `(h, e, B) ↦ (h_k, h_{k−1} − e_{k−1}, B + h_0 + … + h_{k−1})` and the join scalings `(h(J)h, h(J)e, h(J)B)` (prefix) and `(h(J)h, 0, Q(J)B)` (suffix, `Q(J) = Σ_S h(J[S]) h(J[V∖S])`) are the explicit algebra on triples; the suffix by a transitive tournament of order `m` is the near-join's `2^m`.
- The honest status is unchanged: Chan–Pak is OPEN; the night added structure and evidence, not a proof.

## 3. Method notes

- The productive moves were (i) computing exact objects where the lineage had estimates (spectra, floors, memberships), (ii) asking what the digit *is* (the cycle count of an attachment) rather than what it does, and (iii) one noise round that led, through THE-ZOO's generative-engine seed, to THM-1900 — prior art that the tower packet now cites. The unproductive move was the attempt to numerate from a single seed (the bank), recorded as a negative control.
- Two process slips: the "sparse" reading (corrected within hours, with the correction written into the packet and the hypothesis), and a push that was rejected silently through `| tail -1` and then discarded by a chained `reset --hard` (recovered from the reflog; pushes are now verified with `merge-base --is-ancestor` before any reset, and `check_docs` runs on the rebased commit, after the hypotheses index exceeded its budget once through parallel landings).

## 4. Obligations left open

- Prove the two-cycle law (gluing of two Douglas tournaments; the segment-size split `s₂(n) = s₁(n − 1) + 2` and the palindromic pair row are the first constraints a proof must reproduce), or at least the lemma that a tournament with exactly two Hamiltonian cycles has a vertex of out-degree 1 for `n ≥ 6`.
- Reconstruct Douglas's characterisation in chord language (the end-insertion and suffix rules are necessary; sufficiency is not established) and use it for an explicit description of `R₁`.
- Order 14 for digit 2 (`A377857(12) = 44394`; 12871, 38633, 38635 ∈ R₂(14)?) and order 15 for digit 1 (`F(24) = 46368`) were running at the time of writing; the all-vertex census cannot go to order 11 (903 million tournaments) without a different method.
- The numbers of tournaments with exactly `k` Hamiltonian cycles for `k = 3, 4, 5` (`1, 11, 38, 124, 390`; `6, 25, 91, 315`; `5, 38, 138, 466` at orders 5–9) are not in OEIS either; the `k = 1, 2` columns are the only ones with a formula.
