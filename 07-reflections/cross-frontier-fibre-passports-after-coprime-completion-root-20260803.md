## Two factorial cells that were hiding behind symmetry

[THM-3304](../01-canon/theorems/THM-3304-fourier-dirichlet-kernel-and-alternating-quintic-hfc3-exclusion.md)

```text
sum_(r,s>=0) N_(r,s) u^r v^s/(r!s!)
  =1/(1-u^3-v^3-3uv).
```

Therefore `<a^r b^s>` vanishes exactly when `r-s` is nonzero modulo `3` and
is strictly positive in every balanced case.  This converts finite-character
selection from a mere vanishing rule into a positive residual kernel.

## A lawful HFC(3)-to-JC(2) bridge

[THM-3303](../01-canon/theorems/THM-3303-keller-simplex-null-moments-force-a-boundary-collision.md)
gives a narrow but predicate-preserving connection.  Let `g=p+iq` have all
positive triangle moments zero and suppose `det D(p,q)=c!=0` is constant.  If
`F=(p,q)` were injective on the triangle boundary, degree and change of
variables would turn its image into a one-node quadrature domain.  Its Cauchy
transform and Bergman kernel force that image to be a centered disk, while a
nonconstant polynomial edge cannot trace a circle.  Therefore the boundary
must collide.

Consequently, any HFC(3) counterexample in the constant-Jacobian sector is
already a real noninjective Keller map and hence a JC(2) counterexample.
Equivalently, JC(2) implies HFC(3) on this sector.  Boundary collision alone
does **not** imply multiplicity at least two: cuts or holes may carry
one-sheeted interiors.  The correct residual object is the full
boundary/multiplicity passport.

This complements THM-3289 rather than depending on it.  THM-3289 kills a
large candidate coordinate family by a residual critical scheme before any
mate exists.  THM-3303 starts after the Keller condition and forces a
boundary passport.  A concrete joint next test is to eliminate the collision
equations between the three polynomial edge restrictions of a constant-J
candidate, separated into same-edge loops, cross-edge collisions, vertex
identifications, and cut/hole winding data.  Any claimed exclusion must keep
those passports separate.
