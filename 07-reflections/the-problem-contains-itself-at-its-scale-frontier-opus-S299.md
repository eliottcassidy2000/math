## 2. The recursion, made literal

The sheet construction is not merely a trick that happens to work. Look at what the
residual problem IS. Fix the core optimum `t0` and the scale `c`. Sheet `k` is bad for
exception `w_a` iff

`|| (w_a t0)/c + (w_a/c)·k || < 1/14`  —  i.e.  `|| θ_a + s_a·k || < 1/14`,  `k ∈ Z_c`.

This is an **inhomogeneous lonely-runner problem on the discrete circle Z_c**: the
"time" is the sheet index `k`, the "runners" are the exceptions with speeds
`s_a = w_a mod c`, the offsets `θ_a` are the residue holonomy, and the loneliness
radius is the SAME 1/14. The free sheet is the lonely time. LRC(14)'s scale frontier
is a discretized, pointed copy of LRC itself, one level down — with the crucial gain
that the copy is FINITE (c sheets) and the core has become invisible (fiber-exact,
THM-761(i)).

And the recursion carries the tight structure with it. At radius 1/14 each exception
burns an open arc of length exactly `2/14 = 1/7` of the sheet cycle. Six runners burn
at most `6/7`; the seventh can exactly finish the circle. The tight case of the sheet
level — `r = 7` arcs of length `1/7` covering `Z_c` exactly — is a **cyclic tiling**,
and it is the same arithmetic as THM-754's 7-clock partition one level up (the six
cells `a/7 ± 1/14` plus the origin cell tiling the time circle: `7·(1/14) = 1/2` per
half). The factorization `14 = 2·7` acts at BOTH levels: on the time circle it makes
`k = 7` the unique self-dual clock whose half-cell is the origin band; on the sheet
cycle it makes `r = 7` the unique exception count whose arcs can exactly tile. Tight
instances are tilings, at every level of the recursion. (This is also exactly where
AP and Goddyn–Wong live one level up: on the tiling corners, THM-754.)

So the answer to "what are we missing in our view of the underlying object" is, I
believe, this:

> **The underlying object is not the speed set. It is a pointed circle with burned
> arcs — and it is self-similar under scale descent.** Every lens the project has used
> (torus orbit, forbidden-arc cover, Farey cells, pair-sum rulers, blocker hypergraph,
> Fourier envelope, sheets) is a chart on this one object at some level of the
> recursion. What the July sprint added is the DESCENT MAP between levels: dilation
> structure in the speeds ⟹ a c-cover of the circle ⟹ a finite pointed sub-circle
> (the sheet cycle) carrying a smaller lonely-runner instance whose runners are the
> exceptional residues. The observer-lens principle (the marked origin and its local
> view) survives descent: the free sheet is the next level's basepoint.

The owner's (n−1)² identity was the load-bearing decomposition WITHIN one level
(origin band / spokes / pair sector — the S270–S296 arc). The scale descent is the
decomposition BETWEEN levels. The two are perpendicular, and the covering endgame
needed both: the capped envelope (within-level, origin cap × spoke envelope) handles
unstructured cores; the sheet descent (between-level) handles structured ones. The
constant `c=43` separates the proved high-support sheet bulk from its finite sheet
failures. It does not compactify the scale-free lane. Their splice (HYP-6830) now
requires a peel-relative fragmentation coordinate rather than `c*` alone.

## 3. The Frank–Ivanisvili bridge (arXiv:2605.29035), read structurally

The owner's pattern with fresh arXiv papers is structural analogy, not subject
overlap. Frank–Ivanisvili settle the sharp log-Sobolev constant on the n-cycle for
all `n ≥ 4`: it equals half the spectral gap, and they deduce it from an optimal
CUBIC Sobolev inequality. Three structural readings against our frontier:

**(a) The discrete cycle is the right compactification of a sharp-constant problem.**
Their object is a functional inequality's optimal constant on `Z_n`; our scale
frontier just became a covering problem on `Z_c`. In both cases the continuous
problem's hard direction (sharpness; scale non-compactness) is captured by a FINITE
cycle carrying the full obstruction. We arrived by disintegration (the c-cover);
they by discretization. The lesson runs our way: theorems about the finite cycle —
tilings, spectral gaps, character sums — are theorems about the frontier.

**(b) Zeroth moment closes the bulk; the sharp/tight case is spectral.** THM-761 is a
zeroth-moment (measure/pigeonhole) argument on `Z_c`, and it closes everything except
the measure-exact configurations (`r = 7` tilings). Which rotated arcs can tile `Z_c`
is a question about vanishing of arc Fourier coefficients at subgroup frequencies —
Newman-style cyclic tiling conditions, i.e., the character theory of `Z_c`. FI's
result is the same shape one derivative deeper: the sharp constant sits exactly at
the spectral gap, and the bulk inequality is easy while the extremal case is pinned
by the second eigenvalue. Prediction worth testing (filed in HYP-6830): the `r = 7`
residual decomposes as [non-tiling residue profiles ⟹ free sheet by a signed/spectral
refinement of the union bound] ∪ [tiling profiles ⟹ rigid arithmetic (7 | c-type
divisibility + AP structure on the exceptional residues), classifiable exactly like
the AP/GW corners one level up].

**(c) Strengthen-then-deduce.** They could not prove the log-Sobolev inequality
directly at its sharp constant; they proved a STRONGER cubic inequality whose
extremals are more rigid, then specialized. The project keeps rediscovering this
move: the exact-ℚ referee (prove the identity, not the estimate), kps's detuning
target (`M ≥ 2/23` on covering sets — strictly stronger than the `1/14` needed, with
a rigid named extremal), the L=0 census (classify equality exactly rather than bound
near it). Where it has NOT yet been tried deliberately: the band protocol's exact-ℚ
fallbacks, which currently decide by direct evaluation whenever the QUADRATIC
certificate (disc/autocorrelation, THM-731/732) fails. FI suggests the right
replacement is one moment HIGHER, not one constant tighter: a third-moment/cubic
certificate on the band bodies, with the quadratic one recovered by specialization.
Concretely testable against the 8,260-family band bank: does an exact cubic
functional of the good-set indicator separate the 19 direct-`L` closures from the
4,011 certificate closures? If yes, the band protocol becomes one uniform inequality
— the FI shape exactly.
