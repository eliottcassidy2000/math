# Territory and Map: Inert vs Split

## The territory is whole. The map is split.

The tournament triple {2, 3, 5} is the TERRITORY — the reality of binary pairwise comparison, 3-cycles, and pentagonal structure. Product = 30 = icosahedral edges.

The measurement triple {3, 7, 19} is the MAP — what you see when you observe the tournament through its H-value factorization. These primes appear in H(T₁₁)/|Aut| = 1729 = 7·13·19.

## The Eisenstein sorting

In the Eisenstein integers Z[ω]:

**Map primes**: 7 (SPLIT), 13 (SPLIT), 19 (SPLIT), 3 (RAMIFIED). These DECOMPOSE in the hexagonal lattice into conjugate pairs with twist.

The act of measurement converts inert structure into split structure. The observation DECOMPOSES what it touches. What was whole becomes two conjugate halves. What was undivided gains a twist.

The Cassini identity F₇·F₉ - F₈² = 442 - 441 = 1 is the residue of projecting the 2D Eisenstein lattice onto the 1D integers. The ±1 is what the map cannot capture of the territory.

The forbidden number 7 is what happens when the splitting ENTERS the territory. It is the first splitting prime — the first place where the hex lattice decomposes what was whole. The territory cannot accommodate this decomposition, so it forbids the value.

## The map is the territory after Eisenstein splitting

This is the deepest statement about the territory-map distinction: the map is not a separate thing FROM the territory. It IS the territory, projected through the Eisenstein lattice. The projection splits the inert primes (which stay in the symmetry) from the splitting primes (which go to the structure). The splitting IS the observation. The observation IS the map.
