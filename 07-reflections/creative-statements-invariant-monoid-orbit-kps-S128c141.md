# Creative statements in the invariant / monoid / orbit frame

*kind-pasteur-2026-07-21-S128c141. Owner: think of everything as invariants, monoids, orbits; make
creative statements. Companion to THM-1945 (the five VERIFIED dictionary entries) and THM-1885 (the
`(object, monoid, action)` catalog). This note is the generative overflow — statements ranked by how
sure I am, tagged [V]erified / [C]onjecture / [R]eframing. The five [V] ones are in THM-1945; the
rest are new creative claims worth their own sessions.*

The grammar: pick a set `X`, a monoid `M ⟳ X`, an invariant `f` (constant on orbits). Then —
**theorem** = "`f` is `M`-equivariant", **census** = "count `M`-orbits", **hardness** = "how big is
`f`'s stabilizer", **nullcone** = "the degenerate orbit", **reduction** = "a generator steps `X`
down". Twelve statements.

### The verified five (THM-1945), one line each
1. **[V] Ryser = orbits.** Score = complete invariant of the 3-cycle-reversal monoid; orbits = score classes (38=38 n=4, 291=291 n=5).
2. **[V] Switching = cut-space orbits.** Two-graph = complete cut-space invariant; count `2^{C(n−1,2)}` = A002854 = `V(E_n)` = Seidel two-graphs.
3. **[V] Iso = `S_n`-orbits.** Census A000568 = Burnside; odd-cycle parity = the "odd" of OCC.
4. **[V] The invariant lattice.** score ⟂ spectrum from n=5; first cospectral-non-iso at n=4.
5. **[V] Hardness = stabilizer dimension.** `tr/det` continuous-stabilizer ⇒ P; `per/H` finite ⇒ #P (GCT); THM-1780 is the instance.

### The creative overflow

6. **[R] `H` is an orbit cardinality, which is *why* it is `#P`.** By LEM-003 (orbit–stabilizer on
   the tiling fibration), `H = #tilings × |Aut|` — literally the size of a monoid orbit. Spectral
   invariants are *coordinates* (polynomial); orbit *cardinalities* are counts (`#P`). The whole
   "H is #P, tr(A^k) is P" split is "cardinality vs coordinate." **Creative form:** *the easy
   invariants tell you where you are in an orbit; the hard ones tell you how big the orbit is.*

7. **[R] The spectral ladder is the ring of symmetric functions — nothing more.** `M` = GL-conjugation;
   its invariants are generated by `tr(A^k)`, `k≥1` (Newton). So "spectral" = "symmetric function of
   eigenvalues," and the ladder's ceiling is Newton's theorem: any conjugation-invariant is a
   polynomial in the first `n` power sums. `H` is off the ladder because it is not symmetric in the
   spectrum. **Creative form:** *the spectral ladder has exactly `n` rungs because a monic degree-`n`
   form has exactly `n` coefficients; H needs an `(n+1)`-th.*

8. **[R] GIT stability = closed orbit; transitive is the nullcone.** `M` = `SL₂` on `char_A ∈ Sym^n`
   (THM-1810). Transitive = `xⁿ` = the nullcone (orbit closure contains 0, *un*stable); Paley/regular
   = closed orbit (polystable, `var(λ²)=0`, THM-1880). **Creative form:** *a tournament's genericity
   is the GIT-stability of its characteristic binary form; the transitive tournament is the least
   generic possible — it is the origin.*

9. **[R] Cayley–Dickson is the free `×2` monoid; the lost axioms are a descending invariant
   filtration.** `M` = `⟨ doubling ⟩`, orbit of `ℝ` = `ℝ→ℂ→ℍ→𝕆→𝕊`. Each generator application kills
   exactly one axiom: order ⊃ commutativity ⊃ associativity ⊃ alternativity ⊃ power-associativity.
   The axioms are the invariants that survive `k` doublings. **Creative form:** *"how many properties
   you keep" is an invariant of the `×2`-orbit; it decreases by exactly one per step — the same `b`
   (halving/doubling) generator as everywhere else (THM-1885).*

10. **[C] The GMC nullcone is the orbit of 0 under the charge grading.** `M` = the charge-`Z`
    grading (`Z`-action, THM-1885); `f_m(P) = CT[P^m]` the toral moments. `P ∈ nullcone ⇔ f_m(P)=0
    ∀m ⇔ P` lies in the intersection of the kernels of all characters ⇔ `P` is in the *augmentation
    ideal* of the character monoid. **Creative form:** *TNC/GMC(2) says the only tournament-like `P`
    fixed by the whole charge torus is `0`; the nullcone is the torus's fixed cone.* (Would formalise
    the DvdK/toral picture as "fixed locus of a torus action.")

11. **[C] LRC(n) = every projective orbit meets a region.** `M` = `PSL(2,Z)`-type action on rational
    speed sets mod scaling; `f` = the loneliness gap (a projective / `PGL`-invariant of the speed
    vector). **LRC(n) ⇔ every orbit meets `{gap ≥ 1/2n}`**, and tightness at `(Z/n)*` = the orbit's
    stabilizer being the full unit group. **Creative form:** *the runners are lonely iff no
    `PSL(2,Z)`-orbit of speed sets is trapped below the wall; LRC's hardness is the non-amenability of
    `PSL(2,Z)` (THM-1885/1945 hardness law).* (The wall-quintic and `2/25` gap should have a
    continued-fraction = `S,T`-word address.)

12. **[C] The metagraph is a Schreier graph.** `X` = iso classes `Q_m/S_n`; generators = the wiggly
    tile-flips. **Is `G_n` the Schreier coset graph of `S_n` acting on the flip generators?** If so its
    spectral gap, diameter, and the spine/ribs/sea decomposition are Schreier-graph invariants, and
    the "principal line" is the Cayley geodesic from the transitive corner. **Creative form:** *the
    merged metagraph is a Cayley/Schreier graph of the relabeling monoid; its geometry is group
    geometry.* (Directly testable at n=5,6.)

### The meta-statement

> **A mathematical topic is understood when you know its `(X, M, f)`.** The invariant `f` says what
> you can measure, the monoid `M` says what you can change without changing `f`, and the orbits are
> the things that are "really" different. Everything else — census, hardness, nullcone, reduction,
> counterexample — is grammar on top of `(X, M, f)`. The project's open problems are exactly the
> topics whose `M` is non-amenable (LRC, `PSL(2,Z)`) or whose `f` has a finite stabilizer (`H`,
> `per`). *Find the monoid, and you know how hard the problem is before you start.*
