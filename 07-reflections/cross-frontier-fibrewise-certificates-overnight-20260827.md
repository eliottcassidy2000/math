## The shared mechanism

The common pattern is not “the same formula appears in several subjects.” It
is a typed transport problem:

| Source object | Operation | Preserved predicate | Lost information | Required sidecar |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| endpoint repair family | semantic pair selection | exact safe-mass inequality | which masks remain active | pairwise activation |
| primitive `(3,4)` wall | dilation by `g` | joint-wall combinatorics | absolute phase against the fixed pool | scale `g` and prefix integral |
| formal series | moving specialization | evaluation and finite jets | transverse normal coordinate | graph ideal / Hasse jet |
| finite factor | pushforward | measure of a target set | source mass distribution in fibres | normalized fibre weights |
| reset chain | migration or merging | survival event | freshness and dependence | conditional filtration |
| cyclic attachment word | visible projection | recurrence and zero observation | hidden eigenspace | projector relation / two-torsion word |

In each row the cheap but unsound move is to transport a certificate by its
name. The successful move is to transport the semantic object, restore the
coordinate that the operation discarded, and re-evaluate the certificate in
the target fibre.

This sharpens “controlled forgetting needs a sidecar” into a procedural rule:

1. choose the target fibre without inspecting the certificate outcome;
2. state the source, target, map, and preserved predicate;
3. identify the information destroyed by the map;
4. restore exactly that coordinate, not a cosmetic label;
5. recompute the certificate after the move;
6. test the first adjacent hostile fibre and an independently implemented
   positive control.

The rule has now produced distinct proved results in arithmetic covering,
formal specialization, finite-factor density, and cyclic attachment modules,
so it is promoted to `META-PATTERNS.md` with explicit counterindications.
