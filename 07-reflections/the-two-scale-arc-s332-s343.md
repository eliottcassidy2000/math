# The two-scale arc (opus S332–S343): from the corrected filter to the folded identity

Twelve sessions, one arc: the loose half of LRC(14) went from an unmapped
obligation to a wired machine whose last estimates are closed forms. The
shape of the arc is worth recording beyond its theorems, because the same
shape has now appeared three times in this project and will appear again.

  then sharpened to 7/3 by the nested-gap proof, A′), the DK second proof

  BONF5 certificate ({300..2208}, out-of-sample).
- **THM-932/933** — local-density block gluing (with codex's sharp

## The three patterns worth keeping

**1. Blocker → filter → certificate.** Every counterexample became the
next admissibility condition (13-multiples → quadruples → 3-APs/linear
forms → the weighted capped filter), and the corrected filter then FOUND
the certificate that the naive one proved couldn't exist. The instrument
and the object fail the same way (MISTAKE-151's quantization loophole =
the parallel-class escape): when your filter and your theorem break
identically, the shared structure is the real theorem.

**2. The two-scale shape recurses.** Exponential/accessible, global/local,
width-floor/liveCount-floor, per-window/positional — at every level the
resolution was the same: prove the coarse scale outright, certify the
fine scale exactly, and glue with an error that a scale gap kills. The
gluing theorem itself obeys the pattern (its own loss terms are a scale
gap's worth of components).

**3. The fold closes the circle.** fold(r) = r(M−r) — the staircase
triangle's leg product, the first object of the tournament-parity
project — is the closed form of the last wall's floor. The residue fold
(k mod 7)(6 − k mod 7) in boxeph's consecutive identity, the two folds
of THM-965, and the triangle's k(n−1−k) are one reflection. The project
did not need new geometry to finish; it needed to recognize its first
geometry at the bottom of its last estimate. When a program's endgame
keeps producing objects that look like its beginning, that is not
nostalgia — it is the sign the decomposition was right.
