## The reading

Three signed sums, three sign-strings. Read as connection-set indicators on ℤ/n they are the two
canonical characters plus the base:

- `+ + + − − −` = sign(sin 2πk/7) = interval `{1,2,3}` — the **additive/Dirichlet** character;
- `+ + − + − −` = Legendre `(k/7)` = QR `{1,2,4}` = Paley — the **multiplicative/Gauss** character;
- `+ + −` = ℤ/3 base = the 3-cycle.

And `Σ_k ε_k ω^{jk} = 2λ_j + 1` (j≠0) is literally `A+B+C−D−E−F = G`, the circulant eigenvalue up
to the affine `2·+1` (kps's `b·a`!). So the "recursion modes" are **character sums**, and the object
they build is a **circulant tournament**.

## The one clean fact I didn't expect

**Every ℤ/p circulant tournament lives on `Re(λ) = −1/2`.** Not just Paley — *all* of them, because
the pair `{k, p−k}` shares a cosine, so the real part is forced to `½·Σcos = −½` regardless of the
connection set. This *refines my own THM-1925(c)*: I had written the interval eigenvalue real part as
`(D_m − 1)/2`; it equals `−1/2` because the Dirichlet kernel `D_m` **vanishes at the roots of unity**.
The modes are therefore distinguished **only in the imaginary (sine) part**:

- Paley/Legendre → **flat**, `|2λ+1| = √p` (the Gauss sum `|g(χ)| = √p`);
- interval → **spread** (Dirichlet values).

This is kps's char_S spread-duality (transitive cot-ladder max-spread vs Paley Gauss zero-spread,
THM-1880) seen on the *adjacency* eigenvalues: the same flat/spread dichotomy, now as the choice of
**multiplicative vs additive character** on the ±k pairs. The `−1/2` line is kps's `b`; the Gauss-sum
concentration is what makes Paley the GIT-polystable pole.

## Which iso classes come from smaller ones — the reduction DAG

There are exactly two ways an iso class is built from smaller subtournament classes, and they are the
two "recursions":

1. **Order-join (literal subtournaments).** A *reducible* class `T = T₁ ▷ T₂` has `char_A, H, R, ζ`
   all factoring over its strong components — its invariants are **products of strictly-smaller
   strong-class invariants** (THM-1862/1926/1936). This is the deck/composition recursion.
2. **Character recursion (the modes).** A *circulant* strong class is generated by a ℤ/n character
   sum — the `A+B+C−D−E−F` mode.

Everything else — the strong, non-circulant tournaments — is **prime**. The census (n=3..7):

```
   reducible :   1,  3,  6,  21, 103     (order-join, the bulk)
   circulant :   1,  0,  1,   0,   2     (character modes, a thin thread)
   prime     :   0,  1,  5,  35, 351     (strong non-circulant, the genuinely new)
```

So the owner's recursions cover two thin/structured strata; the mass of tournaments (351 of 456 at
n=7) are prime atoms that come from *nothing smaller*. The reduction principles are powerful exactly
where they apply (they collapse the reducible bulk to products, and pin the circulant thread to
Gauss/Chebyshev characters) — but they do not reach the prime interior, which is why the general
theory is hard.

## The deck recursion, unsigned and signed

The literal "A+B+…+G" over the vertex-deleted subtournaments is, unsigned, the derivative:
`char_A'(x) = Σ_i char_{T−v_i}(x)`, and `Σ_i c₃(T−v_i) = (n−3)c₃(T)`. The owner's *signed* deck sums
`Σ_i ε_i char_{T−v_i}` are twisted functionals; on a vertex-transitive (circulant) tournament they
degenerate to `(Σε)·char_D`. The character modes (on ℤ/n) and the deck recursion (on the vertices)
are the two faces of the reduction DAG: one builds the circulant atoms, the other relates a class to
its subtournament deck. Both are the same reduction reflex — *factor a class over smaller structure*
— that runs through THM-1862 (join), THM-1926 (zeta Euler product), THM-1936 (signed R), and kps's
char_S monoid. The modes just name where the structured strata are.
