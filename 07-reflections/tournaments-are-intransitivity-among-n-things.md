# Tournaments are intransitivity among n things

*mac-mini-2026-06-30-S34. The owner asked to expand the odd cycle: stop seeing a tournament as a graph among n nodes, and see it as the possible intransitivity among n things. That one shift reorganizes everything, and it lands the LRC reading the same week klein-S16 showed the measure was the wrong thing to bound.*

## The shift

A tournament is usually drawn as n dots with arrows. That picture is bookkeeping. What a tournament *is* — among n things, n alternatives, n runners — is a complete set of pairwise verdicts: for every pair, which one wins. And the only thing that verdict-set can fail to be is **a ranking**. If the verdicts are consistent, there is a total order, a scale, a number you could have assigned to each thing; the tournament is then just that order, the same one up to relabeling, the unique transitive tournament. If the verdicts are inconsistent — A beats B, B beats C, C beats A — there is no order, no scale, no number. That failure is the whole content. A tournament is the **intransitivity among n things**: how, and how much, the pairwise verdicts refuse to be a ranking.

Once you say it that way, the objects we have been computing rename themselves. The transitive tournament is not "the boring one"; it is the **orderable** point, the rankable preference, the only one that comes from a potential — a function `f` on the things with `a → b ⟺ f(a) > f(b)`. It is a coboundary. Every other tournament is a relation that **no** potential explains: an intransitivity, a cohomology class. The cut space (the `n−1` score degrees of freedom) is exactly the orderable part — the best-fit ranking — and the cycle space (the `C(n−1,2)` degrees of freedom) is exactly the intransitivity, the part the ranking cannot reach. We proved this split was clean at odd `n` and called it Cut ⊕ Cycle; in the language of things it is **Order ⊕ Intransitivity**, and for large `n` the intransitivity dimension dwarfs the order dimension. A preference among many things is mostly inconsistency.

## The atom is the 3-cycle, and it is odd

The irreducible intransitivity is three things in a cycle — rock beats scissors beats paper beats rock. The **Condorcet paradox**. It is the smallest verdict-set with no ranking, and by Moon's theorem every intransitivity contains one: the 3-cycles generate. It has length three, which is to say it is **odd**, and that is not incidental. An odd cyclic verdict is the obstruction to laying the things on a line — you cannot two-color, cannot bipartition, cannot orient consistently around an odd loop. The atom of intransitivity is an odd cycle because odd is precisely what a linear order cannot absorb.

This is the odd cycle the whole project keeps rediscovering, now seen as what it always was: not a subgraph, a *paradox*. The Odd-Cycle Collection formula counts collections of these paradoxes. The metagraph's one surviving invariant, the cyclicity, is the count of them. And the metagraph itself is the **space of all intransitivity patterns among `n` things**, organized radially from the transitive cusp (zero paradoxes, the rational point) outward to the regular tournament (maximal balanced paradox). The number of essentially different intransitivity *shapes* — the cycle space up to relabeling — is the even-graph count `A002854 = 2, 3, 7, 16, 54`, and the larger tournament count is what you get by overlaying an order on each shape. Order times intransitivity.

## Intransitivity is the rule, not the exception

The reframe also tells you where you live. The fraction of preferences among `n` things that are fully rankable is `n! / 2^{C(n,2)}` — `0.75, 0.375, 0.12, 0.02, 0.002, …` — and the fraction with even a single overall winner is `n / 2^{n−1}`, also vanishing. As soon as there are more than a handful of things, a random preference is almost surely intransitive and has no winner. The Condorcet phenomenon. So the project has not been studying an exotic corner; it has been studying the **generic** case. Order is the measure-zero miracle; intransitivity is what there is. The transitive tournament — the one we keep calling the cusp — is the rare exception, and the whole rich landscape is the rule.

## The Lonely Runner is intransitivity among runners

Here the reframe pays its way, and it pays it precisely because of what klein found this same week. The Lonely Runner is a question about `n` things — runners — and their mutual danger relation `D(v,t) = [‖vt‖ < 1/14]`. A disproof would be a covering: the dangers fit together so tightly that no runner is ever alone, which is to say the danger relation can be **ordered** — separated, factored `f(v)g(t)`, made into a consistent scale with no irreducible cycle. A proof is the opposite: the danger relation is **intransitive**, it contains an irreducible cyclic resonance that no ordering dissolves, and that intransitivity forces a lonely time. The bilinear `vt` inside `‖vt‖` is exactly what closes the cycle and forbids the factoring — anti-Littlewood, the essentiality. So the LRC, read among its things, is: *the runners' danger relation is irreducibly intransitive.*

And the atom of that intransitivity is an odd cycle whose **length is the apex prime**. Generic tournaments bottom out at the 3-cycle because three is the fewest things that can cycle. The LRC14 apex resonance is among the seven residues of `Z₇`, so its irreducible cycle is `C₇` — seven things in a loop, still odd because seven is. The apex prime sets the cycle length; the genus counts how many such independent global cycles there are, and at `N = 14` it is exactly one. `LRC14` is the first case with a single genuinely global intransitivity.

klein-S16 sharpened why this is the right object and not the measure. The lonely *measure* over the full infinite family of coverings has infimum zero — the set of lonely times can shrink to nothing, so you cannot prove the conjecture by bounding a measure from below. But existence is not measure. An odd cycle either is present or is not; it is a counting fact, a topological fact, `σ`-odd, finite. The intransitivity exists even where its measure vanishes, and that existence is what produces the lonely point. THM-590 (klein-S15) is the certificate at the atom: the apex odd cycle's spectral gap, `4cos²(3π/7)`, is strictly positive — the intransitivity is non-degenerate, present, irreducible — and it is zero only at the full `Z₇`, the one covering-complete configuration that lies off the floor. The proof is not "the lonely set is large"; it is "the irreducible odd intransitivity is there."

## What it is good for

Seeing tournaments as intransitivity is not only an interpretation; it is a working method other people use. Aggregating pairwise comparisons — sports results, chess Elo, A/B preferences, search-relevance judgments, votes — is exactly the problem of extracting an order from a tournament and measuring what is left over. The Hodge / HodgeRank decomposition splits such data into a gradient (the best-fit ranking) plus a curl (local cyclic inconsistency) plus a harmonic part (global inconsistency) — the real-valued cousin of our Order ⊕ Intransitivity split, with the 3-cycle as the unit of curl. The cyclicity `c(T)` is a literal **irrationality index** for a comparison dataset: how far the judgments are from any ranking, measured in Condorcet paradoxes. Counting and bounding odd cycles is then not an abstraction about runners; it is the quantitative theory of when pairwise data can and cannot be ranked, and the LRC sits at the hardest edge of it — the first instance where a single global paradox among the apex prime's worth of things cannot be ordered away, and is certified, finitely, not to vanish.
