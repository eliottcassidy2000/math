\begin{center}
{\Large\bfseries Is ChatGPT Really Better Than Claude?\\A One-Number Test}
\end{center}

\medskip

The Chatbot Arena ranks language models by pairwise human preference --- a \emph{tournament}. The number of consistent total rankings is $H(T)$. For $n$ models compared randomly: $\mathbb{E}[H] = n!/2^{n-1}$, with $\mathrm{CV} = \sqrt{2/n}$. The Z-score
\[
Z = \frac{H(T) - n!/2^{n-1}}{n!/2^{n-1} \cdot \sqrt{2/n}}
\]
measures whether the ranking is real or noise. Low $H$ (negative $Z$) means a \emph{clear hierarchy}: the ranking is statistically supported. $Z \approx 0$ means the models are essentially interchangeable.

For $n = 10$ models: $\mathbb{E}[H] \approx 7{,}088$. If the arena's pairwise data gives $H < 1{,}000$, the ranking is \emph{very significant} ($Z < -4$). If $H \approx 7{,}000$, the leaderboard is noise.

Nobody is currently reporting $H(T)$ for chatbot tournaments. One number would tell you whether the ranking \emph{means anything at all}.
