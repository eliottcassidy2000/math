\noindent This problem set concerns Burnside's lemma applied to a concrete counting problem. You will implement a formula, optimize it, and then --- in the final problem --- discover something about its structure that reduces the computational complexity by many orders of magnitude. The final problem is open-ended and significantly harder than the others.

\section*{Background}

\begin{definition}
A \textbf{tournament} on $n$ vertices is a complete directed graph: for every pair $\{u,v\}$, exactly one of $u \to v$ or $v \to u$ holds. Two tournaments are \textbf{isomorphic} if one can be obtained from the other by relabeling vertices.
\end{definition}

\noindent Let $T(n)$ denote the number of non-isomorphic tournaments on $n$ vertices (OEIS~\href{https://oeis.org/A000568}{A000568}). The first few values are:
\[
T(0) = 1,\quad T(1) = 1,\quad T(2) = 1,\quad T(3) = 2,\quad T(4) = 4,\quad T(5) = 12,\quad T(6) = 56.
\]

\subsection*{Problem 1: Warm-up \hfill \normalfont [15 points]}

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
  \item Show that there are exactly $2^{\binom{n}{2}}$ labeled tournaments on $n$ vertices.
  \item List all non-isomorphic tournaments on 4 vertices. For each, give its score sequence (the sorted list of out-degrees). Verify $T(4) = 4$.
  \item Two of the four are self-complementary: reversing all arcs yields an isomorphic tournament. Which two?
  \item Show that the average number of Hamiltonian paths in a random labeled tournament on $n$ vertices is $n!/2^{n-1}$.
\end{enumerate}

\bigskip

%% ============================================================
%% PROBLEM 2
%% ============================================================

\subsection*{Problem 2: Implementing the formula \hfill \normalfont [25 points]}

The following result is due to Davis (1954).

\begin{theorem}[Davis]\label{thm:davis}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:davis}
T(n) = \sum_{\substack{\lambda \vdash n \\ \textup{all parts odd}}} \frac{2^{c(\lambda)}}{z_\lambda}
\end{equation}
where $\lambda = (l_1^{m_1}, l_2^{m_2}, \ldots)$ is a partition of $n$ in exponential notation, the sum runs over partitions into odd parts only, the cycle exponent is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:exponent}
c(\lambda) = \sum_i m_i \left\lfloor \frac{l_i}{2} \right\rfloor + \sum_i \binom{m_i}{2} l_i + \sum_{i < j} m_i m_j \gcd(l_i, l_j),
\end{equation}
and $z_\lambda = \prod_i l_i^{m_i} \cdot m_i!$ is the centralizer order.
\end{theorem}

\noindent This is a direct application of Burnside's lemma to the action of $S_n$ on the $2^{\binom{n}{2}}$ labeled tournaments. Only permutations with all odd-length cycles fix any tournament (even cycles force contradictory arc directions).

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
  \item Show that $c(\lambda)$ equals the number of orbits of unordered pairs under a permutation with cycle type $\lambda$.
  \item The exponent has a compact form: $c(\lambda) = \frac{1}{2}[\sum_{r,s} m_r m_s \gcd(r,s) - \sum_r m_r]$. Prove this is equivalent to~\eqref{eq:exponent}.
  \item Implement the formula in a language of your choice. Verify against the table in the appendix.
  \item Report the running time for $T(50)$.
  \item Profile your implementation. What fraction of time is partition generation vs.\ per-partition arithmetic? At what $n$ does it exceed 60 seconds?
\end{enumerate}

%% PROBLEM 3
%% ============================================================

\subsection*{Problem 3: Optimization \hfill \normalfont [25 points]}

The na\"ive implementation from Problem~2 slows around $n \approx 80$ because the number of odd-part partitions grows as $\exp(\pi\sqrt{n/3})$. Your task: compute $T(200)$ in under 60 seconds.

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
  \item \textbf{GCD table.} Precompute $\gcd(i,j)$ for $1 \leq i,j \leq n$.
  \item \textbf{LCD scaling.} Compute $L = \mathrm{lcm}\{z_\lambda\}$ over all partitions. Accumulate $(L/z_\lambda) \cdot 2^{c(\lambda)}$ as integers. Divide once at the end.
  \item \textbf{Bucket accumulation.} The values $2^{c(\lambda)}$ are large (up to $2^{19900}$ at $n = 200$). Instead, index an array by the exponent $c(\lambda)$, accumulate the integer prefactors, and multiply by powers of 2 once.
  \item \textbf{Parallelism.} Describe a strategy for distributing partitions across threads.
  \item \textbf{Language.} Compare Python (arbitrary-precision integers) against C with GMP.
\end{enumerate}

%% PROBLEM 4 --- THE CHALKBOARD PROBLEM
%% ============================================================

\subsection*{Problem 4 \hfill \normalfont [35 points]}

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
  \item Compute the ratio $r(\lambda) = (n!/z_\lambda) \cdot 2^{c(\lambda) - \binom{n}{2}}$ for every odd-part partition $\lambda$ of $n = 10$. Sort them in decreasing order. What do you notice?

  \item The identity partition $\lambda = (1^n)$ has $c((1^n)) = \binom{n}{2}$ and $z_{(1^n)} = n!$, contributing exactly $2^{\binom{n}{2}}/n!$ to the sum. What fraction of the total $T(n)$ does this single partition account for, at $n = 10$? At $n = 15$? At $n = 20$?

  \item Define the \textbf{deficit} of a partition as $\Delta(\lambda) = \binom{n}{2} - c(\lambda)$. Show that the partition with the smallest positive deficit is always $\lambda = (3, 1^{n-3})$, with $\Delta = 2n - 4$.

  \item Using (c), show that the second-largest contribution to the Burnside sum is
  \[
  \frac{n(n-1)(n-2)}{3} \cdot 2^{\binom{n}{2} - (2n-4)}.
  \]
  What is this as a fraction of the identity contribution?

  \item Suppose you sum only over partitions with deficit $\Delta(\lambda) \leq D$, and round the result to the nearest integer. For what value of $D$ is this guaranteed to give the exact answer? How many partitions survive? Compare with $p_{\mathrm{odd}}(n)$.

  \item Implement this truncated summation. Report the time for $T(500)$.
\end{enumerate}
