# Plain-Language Explainers: Tournament Math Research

These write-ups explain the mathematical work done in this project to a general audience — no math background needed. Each document covers one major topic area.

---

## The Explainers

1. **[What is a Tournament?](01-what-is-a-tournament.md)**
   The basic object we study. What it is, why it matters, and how to picture it.

2. **[The Core Question: Counting Paths](02-counting-paths.md)**
   Why we care about Hamiltonian paths, what Rédei's theorem says, and what makes the answer always odd.

3. **[The Odd-Cycle Formula](03-odd-cycle-formula.md)**
   The central discovery: how counting conflicts between small loops gives you the total path count.

4. **[Forbidden Numbers](04-forbidden-numbers.md)**
   Why 7 and 21 are impossible as path counts — forever — no matter how big the tournament gets.

5. **[Special Tournaments: Paley, Cyclic, and Self-Complementary](05-special-tournaments.md)**
   Three special families, why they're remarkable, and what they maximize.

6. **[Spectral Analysis: The Personality of a Tournament](06-spectral-analysis.md)**
   What "eigenvalues" are, why we compute them for tournaments, and what they reveal.

7. **[Path Homology: Counting Holes](07-path-homology.md)**
   How topology (the math of shapes and holes) applies to directed graphs and tournaments.

8. **[The Tiling Picture](08-tiling-picture.md)**
   How every tournament can be encoded as a black-and-white grid pattern — and why that helps.

9. **[The Map of All Tournaments](09-metagraph.md)**
   The graph whose nodes are tournament types and whose edges are single arc-flips — a universe map.

10. **[Connections to Other Fields](10-connections.md)**
    How this work touches physics, cryptography, error-correcting codes, and data analysis.

11. **[What's New: Novelty in Context of Prior Work](11-novelty-and-prior-work.md)**
    Honest accounting of what this project contributes vs. what was already known — Grinberg-Stanley, Alon, GLMY, and where the gaps are.

12. **[Number Theory Connections](12-number-theory-connections.md)**
    How quadratic residues, Gauss sums, the mod-4 condition, quadratic reciprocity, CRT, and the Krawtchouk transform all run through tournament math.

13. **[Speedups and Algorithms](13-speedups-and-algorithms.md)**
    Six concrete techniques — sparse matrices, CRT, block diagonalization, deletion-contraction, real-rootedness — that make large computations feasible.

14. **[David Bessis and Tournament Theory](14-bessis-connections.md)**
    How Bessis's dual braid monoid, K(π,1) theorem, noncrossing partitions, and cyclic sieving connect — in some cases exactly — to the tiling model, Hamiltonian paths as Weyl chambers, and the β₂=0 result.
