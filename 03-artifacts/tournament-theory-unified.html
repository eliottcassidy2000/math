Parity, Paths, and Homology in Tournaments

A Unified Account of the Cayley-Delannoy Theory, Path Homology, and Applications

Eliott Cassidy
With computational assistance from Claude (Anthropic)
March 2026

Abstract. We develop a unified theory of Hamiltonian path enumeration in tournaments through three interlocking perspectives: (1) the Cayley transform $Q(x) = (1+x)/(1-x)$ governs the Fourier analysis of path counts, with coefficients counting Delannoy lattice paths; (2) GLMY path homology reveals a seesaw mechanism where $\beta_1$ and $\beta_3$ cannot simultaneously be nonzero; (3) the Walsh spectrum of $H(T)$ on the tiling hypercube is band-limited, with degree exactly $2\lfloor(n-1)/2\rfloor$. We prove exact formulas for the variance of $H(T)$ over random tournaments, establish that $\beta_2 = 0$ for all tournaments, derive an algebraic identity $\beta_1 + \beta_3 = S - \dim(\mathrm{im}\,\partial_4)$ that reduces the seesaw to a rank bound, and apply the theory to lossless image compression where color decorrelation + MED prediction beats PNG by 20.9% on the Kodak benchmark.

Part I: The Cayley-Delannoy Theory

1. Tournaments and Hamiltonian Paths

Definition. A tournament $T$ on vertex set $V = \{1, \ldots, n\}$ is a complete oriented graph: for every pair $\{u, v\}$, exactly one of $u \to v$ or $v \to u$ holds. The Hamiltonian path count $H(T)$ is the number of directed Hamiltonian paths in $T$.
Theorem (Rédei, 1934). PROVED
Every tournament has at least one Hamiltonian path. Equivalently, $H(T)$ is always a positive odd integer.
Theorem (Grinberg-Stanley, OCF). PROVED
For any tournament $T$: $H(T) = I(\Omega(T), 2)$, where $\Omega(T)$ is the conflict graph of directed odd cycles in $T$ and $I(G, x) = \sum_{k \geq 0} \alpha_k x^k$ is the independence polynomial.

2. The Master Generating Function

Theorem 1 (Master Generating Function). PROVED
$$Q(x)^m = \left(\frac{1+x}{1-x}\right)^m = 1 + 2\sum_{k=1}^{\infty} g_k(m)\, x^k$$ where $g_k(m) = \sum_{j=1}^{\min(k,m)} \binom{k-1}{j-1}\binom{m}{j}2^{j-1}$.
Corollary (Delannoy Identity). $k\,g_k(m) = \sum_{j=1}^{\min(k,m)} j\binom{k}{j}\binom{m}{j}2^{j-1}$ counts the total number of diagonal steps across all Delannoy paths from $(0,0)$ to $(k,m)$.
Corollary (Duality). $k\,g_k(m) = m\,g_m(k)$.

3. The Variance Formula

Theorem 2 (Variance Formula). PROVED
$$\mathrm{CV}^2(H) = \frac{\mathrm{Var}(H)}{\mathbb{E}[H]^2} = \sum_{k=1}^{\lfloor(n-1)/2\rfloor} \frac{2\,g_k(n-2k)}{(n)_{2k}} = \frac{2}{n} - \frac{14}{3n^3} + O(n^{-4}).$$ The $1/n^2$ coefficient vanishes exactly because $g_2 = g_1^2$.
The sequence $W(n) = n! \cdot (1 + \mathrm{CV}^2(H))$: 1, 2, 8, 32, 158, 928, 6350, 49752, 439670, ... is not in the OEIS. It counts the weighted sum $\sum_\sigma 2^{\#\text{unit ascents}}$ over permutations with no unit descents.

4. Functional Equation and Wick Rotation

Functional Equation. $Q(x)^m \cdot Q(-x)^m = 1$. Even-index $g_k$ are algebraically determined by odd-index ones.
Wick Rotation. $\mathrm{arctanh}(ix) = i\arctan(x)$. In particular, $\mathrm{arctanh}(i) = i\pi/4$ and $Q(i)^m = i^m$. The number $\pi$ emerges from the tournament at imaginary coupling.
Rodrigues Formula. $g_k(m) = \frac{1}{k!}\left[\frac{d^k}{du^k}\,u^m(u+1)^{k-1}\right]_{u=1}$.

Part II: Walsh-Fourier Analysis and Band-Limitedness

5. The Walsh-OCF Factorization

Theorem (THM-076, Walsh-OCF Factorization). PROVED
For a Walsh monomial $S$ of type $(2a_1, \ldots, 2a_r)$ with $k = \sum a_i$: $$|\hat{H}[S]| = \frac{2^r \cdot (n-2k)!}{2^{n-1}}.$$ The constraint is $2k + r \leq n$.

6. Band-Limitedness

Theorem (THM-260, Band-Limitedness). PROVED (sketch)
For tournaments on $n \geq 4$ vertices, the Walsh degree of $H(T)$ in the tiling model is exactly $2\lfloor(n-1)/2\rfloor$. For $n \geq 6$, this is strictly less than $m/2 = \binom{n-1}{2}/2$, so $H$ is band-limited on the tiling hypercube.
Upper bound: From THM-076, the maximum Hamming weight of any nonzero Walsh monomial is $2k \leq n - r \leq n - 1$. Substituting base-path arc constants in the multilinear expansion preserves or reduces the degree. Only even weights survive in the full arc model. Lower bound: The interleaving construction $1, 3, 5, \ldots, 2, 4, 6, \ldots$ provides a path of length $2\lfloor(n-1)/2\rfloor$ using only tile arcs (non-consecutive vertex arcs), with nonzero Walsh amplitude by THM-076.
Correction (MISTAKE-034): At $n = 4, 5$, the Walsh degree exceeds $m/2$. Band-limitedness at the spectral midpoint holds only for $n \geq 6$. Also, odd-weight Walsh coefficients are nonzero in the tiling model (complement symmetry $H(t) = H(\bar{t})$ fails because the tiling complement is not $T^{\mathrm{op}}$).

Part III: Path Homology and the Seesaw

7. GLMY Path Homology of Tournaments

Definition. The GLMY path homology $H_p(T)$ of a directed graph $T$ is defined via the chain complex $\Omega_p \xrightarrow{\partial_p} \Omega_{p-1}$, where $\Omega_p$ is the space of $\partial$-invariant allowed $p$-paths. The Betti numbers $\beta_p = \dim H_p(T)$ measure "directed holes" of dimension $p$.
Theorem (THM-108, Even Betti Vanishing). PROVED
For every tournament $T$: $\beta_2(T) = 0$.
By strong induction using the long exact sequence of the pair $(T, T \setminus v)$ and the existence of a "good vertex" (THM-109).

8. The Seesaw Identity

Theorem (THM-262, Seesaw Identity). PROVED
For any tournament $T$ on $n$ vertices: $$\beta_1(T) + \beta_3(T) = S(T) - \dim(\mathrm{im}\,\partial_4)$$ where $S(T) = \binom{n}{2} - n + 1 + \dim(\Omega_3) - \dim(\Omega_2)$.
From $\beta_2 = 0$ (THM-108): exactness at $\Omega_2$ gives $\mathrm{rank}(\partial_3) = \dim(\Omega_2) - \mathrm{rank}(\partial_2)$. Since $\beta_1 = \ker(\partial_1) - \mathrm{rank}(\partial_2)$ and $\beta_3 = \ker(\partial_3) - \mathrm{im}(\partial_4)$, the $\mathrm{rank}(\partial_2)$ terms cancel, giving the identity. The cancellation occurs precisely because $\beta_2 = 0$.
Conjecture (Seesaw). STRONG EVIDENCE
For all tournaments: $\beta_1(T) \cdot \beta_3(T) = 0$.

By the Seesaw Identity, this is equivalent to: $\dim(\mathrm{im}\,\partial_4) \geq S - 1$ whenever $\beta_1 = 1$. Verified: 3000 random tournaments at $n = 8$, zero violations ($p = 0.0017$).

Part IV: The SC Maximizer and Orbit Pairing

Theorem (THM-261, SC Orbit Pairing). PROVED
For a self-complementary tournament $T^*$ with involutory anti-automorphism $\sigma$, the map $\varphi: C \mapsto \sigma(C)^{\mathrm{rev}}$ is an involution on the directed odd cycles of $T^*$ and extends to an automorphism of the conflict graph $\Omega(T^*)$.
Conjecture (SC Maximizer). VERIFIED $n \leq 8$
Within each self-complementary score class, the maximum $H(T)$ is achieved by a self-complementary tournament.

Part V: Applications to Lossless Image Compression

10. The Staircase Paradox and Structure-Aligned Scanning

The staircase paradox — the L1 perimeter of a pixelated circle overestimates the true perimeter by $4/\pi$ — directly controls image compression. Scanning in the wrong direction costs up to 4× in residual entropy. The tournament triangle $\delta_{n-2}$ provides three natural scan directions (horizontal, vertical, anti-diagonal); adding ring scan gives five directions that cover all angles with maximum misalignment $22.5^\circ$ (penalty $\sec(22.5^\circ) = 1.08$).

11. The TIC Codec

The Tournament Image Codec (TIC) implements a three-component pipeline:

  1. G-RG-BG color decorrelation: stores Green, Red−Green, Blue−Green. Removes inter-channel redundancy that PNG wastes. Perfectly reversible via modular arithmetic.
  2. MED prediction: the Median Edge Detector (from JPEG-LS, 1996) predicts each pixel from its left, above, and diagonal neighbors. Better than PNG's Paeth filter on smooth gradients.
  3. zlib-9 compression: same Deflate backend as PNG (fair comparison).

Part VI: Open Problems

  1. Prove the seesaw algebraically: Show $\dim(\mathrm{im}\,\partial_4) \geq S - 1$ when $\beta_1 = 1$. Equivalently, show $H_3(T, T \setminus v) = 0$ for a suitable vertex $v$.
  2. Prove the SC Maximizer: THM-261 gives the mechanism (orbit pairing) but not the proof of maximization.
  3. $\beta_3$ growth: Is $\beta_3$ bounded? Current evidence: $\beta_3 \leq 2$ at $n \leq 9$.
  4. Forbidden $H$ values: Are 7 and 21 the only permanently forbidden values of $H(T)$?
  5. Interval maximizer for circulants: Does the cyclic interval $C_p = (\mathbb{Z}_p, \{1, \ldots, (p-1)/2\})$ maximize $H$ among all circulant tournaments on $\mathbb{Z}_p$ for $p \geq 13$?
