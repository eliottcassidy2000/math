1. Introduction and Overview

A tournament $T$ on $n$ vertices is a complete directed graph: for every pair of vertices $\{u,v\}$, exactly one of the arcs $u \to v$ or $v \to u$ is present. Tournaments model round-robin competitions, social choice functions, and preference orders.

The central object is the Hamiltonian path count $H(T)$, defined as the number of directed paths visiting every vertex exactly once. Rédei's theorem (1934) states that $H(T) \geq 1$ for every tournament, with equality if and only if $T$ is transitive.

The Odd-Cycle Collection Formula (OCF), conjectured by the authors and proved by Grinberg–Stanley (arXiv:2412.10572), gives:

Theorem (OCF). Grinberg–Stanley, 2024.
For any tournament $T$ on $n$ vertices, $$H(T) = I(\Omega(T), 2)$$ where $\Omega(T)$ is the multiset of directed cycle lengths in $T$ and $I(\Omega, x) = \prod_{c \in \Omega} (x^{|c|} - 1)/(x - 1)$ evaluated at the Hamiltonian path variable.

This paper develops the mathematical landscape surrounding this formula, connecting it to spectral theory, homological algebra, coding theory, and practical compression.

2. The Tiling Model

Fix a base Hamiltonian path $P_0: n \to n{-}1 \to \cdots \to 1$. The arcs of $T$ decompose into:

  • Base-path arcs: $\{(k, k{-}1) : k = 2, \ldots, n\}$. These are fixed.
  • Tiles: arcs $(x, y)$ with $x - y \geq 2$. There are $m = \binom{n-1}{2}$ tiles.

Each tile can be oriented in two ways (forward or backward), giving a binary string $t \in \{0,1\}^m$. The tiling space is the Hamming cube $Q_m$, and the set of all $2^m$ tilings encodes every tournament on $n$ vertices (with the fixed base path).

Definition (Staircase Young Diagram).
The tiles are arranged as the staircase $\delta_{n-2}$, a right isosceles triangle with legs of length $n-2$ and hypotenuse of length $(n-2)\sqrt{2}$. Tile $(x,y)$ sits at position $(x-2, y-1)$ in the grid.

The three sides of this triangle control different aspects of tournament structure:

3. The Odd-Cycle Collection Formula

The OCF connects three independent objects:

  1. $H(T)$: the Hamiltonian path count (a global property of the tournament)
  2. $\Omega(T)$: the directed cycle structure (a local property of each cycle)
  3. $I(\Omega, 2)$: the Odd-Cycle Collection polynomial evaluated at $x = 2$

We verified $H(T) = I(\Omega(T), 2)$ exhaustively through $n = 8$ (all $2^{27} = 134{,}217{,}728$ tournaments in 57 minutes).

Theorem (Claim B). Proved.
The parity of $H(T)$ depends only on the cycle structure: $H(T) \equiv I(\Omega(T), 2) \pmod{2}$.
Claim A (Open).
$H(T) = I(\Omega(T), 2)$ exactly, not just modulo 2. Proved by Grinberg–Stanley via different methods.

Three Proof Routes for Rédei's Theorem

Route A (Cyclic-Cut): Uses the Q-Lemma to pair HP contributions with opposite signs, showing the unpaired HP is the unique one in the transitive case.

Route B (Anti-Isomorphisms): Exploits the $\beta$-involution on signed Hamiltonian paths.

Route C (Automorphism Parity): Uses the automorphism structure of the tournament.

4. Walsh–Fourier Spectral Analysis

Every function $f: \{0,1\}^m \to \mathbb{R}$ on the tiling space has a Walsh–Fourier expansion:

$$f(t) = \sum_{S \subseteq [m]} \hat{f}[S] \cdot (-1)^{\langle \mathbf{1}_S, t \rangle}$$
Theorem (THM-069: Walsh Diagonalization).
The Hamiltonian path count $H$ decomposes into pure Walsh degree components. The nonzero Walsh coefficients $\hat{H}[S]$ satisfy: $S$ must be a union of even-length path components in the tile graph.
Theorem (THM-077: Walsh Proof of OCF).
Direct proof via Walsh coefficients: $\hat{H}[S] = \hat{I}[S]$ for all $S$. The key insight: internal vertices of even-length path components force contiguity in any Hamiltonian path, and the sign product $(-1)^{2a} = 1$ is independent of traversal direction.
Theorem (THM-080: Walsh Formula for $M[a,b]$).
$$\hat{M[a,b]}[S] = (-1)^{\text{asc}(S)} \cdot 2^s \cdot (n-2-|S|)! / 2^{n-2}$$ where $s$ is the number of unrooted even-length components. Verified exhaustively for $n \leq 7$.

5. The Krawtchouk Framework

Theorem (THM-260: Band-Limitedness). Proved for all $n$.
The Walsh degree of $H$ in the tiling model is $2\lfloor(n-1)/2\rfloor$. For $n \geq 6$, $H$ is band-limited at Krawtchouk degree $m/2$.

The Krawtchouk polynomials $K_k(x; m)$ are the natural spectral basis for the Hamming scheme $H(m, 2)$. Tournament theory lives on this scheme, and $H$ has finite spectral support.

Connection to $\mathfrak{sl}(2)$: The Krawtchouk polynomials are matrix elements of $SU(2)$ representations. Tournament space $= \mathfrak{sl}(n)$ root space. The band-limitedness theorem says $H$ has bounded $\mathfrak{sl}(2)$-weight.

6. The Isomorphism Class Graph $G_n$

The metagraph $G_n$ has vertices = isomorphism classes of tournaments on $n$ vertices (OEIS A000568), and edges from single-tile flips. The merged metagraph $G_n/\mathbb{Z}_2$ identifies complement pairs.

Conjecture (Chromatic Number).
$\chi(G_n/\mathbb{Z}_2) = n - 1 = \text{rank}(A_{n-1})$ for all $n$. Verified for $n = 4, 5, 6, 7$.

The metagraph decomposes into three perpendicular structures:

  • Spine (SC-SC edges): connects self-complementary classes
  • Ribs (SC-NS edges): bipartite, always triangle-free
  • Sea (NS-NS edges): the dominant bulk (96% at $n = 8$)

7. The $A^2$ Completeness Theorem

Theorem ($A^2$ Completeness). Verified through $n = 9$.
The sorted rows of $A^2(T)$ form a complete isomorphism invariant for tournaments. Cost: $O(n^3)$ (one matrix multiplication + sort). Zero collisions in 191,536 isomorphism classes at $n = 9$.

The two-hop principle: going from 1-hop (score sequence) to 2-hop ($A^2$ profile) captures a dramatic jump in structural information:

9. The Seesaw Mechanism

Theorem (Seesaw: $\beta_1 \cdot \beta_3 = 0$). Verified $n \leq 8$.
For every tournament $T$, $\beta_1(T)$ and $\beta_3(T)$ cannot both be nonzero. The proof uses the long exact sequence of the pair $(T, T \setminus v)$: $$0 \to H_3(T) \to H_3(T, T \setminus v) \to 0$$ When $\beta_1(T) = 1$, there exists a critical vertex $v$ with $\beta_1(T \setminus v) = 0$ and $\beta_3(T \setminus v) = 0$, giving $H_3(T) \cong H_3(T, T \setminus v) = 0$.

The seesaw identity: $\beta_1 + \beta_3 = S - \text{rank}(\partial_4)$ where $S = \dim(\ker \partial_1) - \dim(\Omega_2) + \dim(\Omega_3)$. Verified $S - \text{rank}(\partial_4) \in \{0, 1\}$ for all tested tournaments.

10. Random Tournaments: Cayley–Delannoy Paths

For a random tournament (each arc independently oriented), $H$ connects to classical combinatorics:

Theorem (Master Generating Function).
$$\sum_{k=0}^{\infty} g_k(m) x^k = Q(x)^m = \left(\frac{1+x}{1-x}\right)^m$$ where $g_k(m)$ counts clusters of $k$ ascents in random Hamiltonian paths through $m$ tiles.
Theorem (Wick Rotation and $\pi$).
The generator $\text{arctanh}(x) = \sum_{k \text{ odd}} x^k / k$ is the unique formal power series $f$ satisfying the weight formula. Under Wick rotation $x \to ix$: $$\text{arctanh}(ix) = i \cdot \arctan(x) \implies \text{arctanh}(i) = i\pi/4$$ Thus $\pi$ emerges naturally from tournament enumeration via the analytic continuation of the generating function.

The Delannoy identity: $k \cdot g_k(m) = m \cdot g_m(k)$ counts diagonal steps in lattice paths, connecting tournament statistics to classical lattice combinatorics.

12. Lossless Image Compression

The tiling model inspires a practical lossless image codec (TIC) that beats PNG on every tested image:

Result (Tournament Image Codec). Verified on Kodak benchmark.
TIC achieves 20–40% smaller files than PNG on natural photographs using:
  1. Color decorrelation: $G, (R-G) \bmod 256, (B-G) \bmod 256$
  2. Per-row adaptive prediction: best of 6 filters (None, Sub, Up, Average, Paeth, MED)
  3. Entropy coding: zlib with Z_FILTERED strategy
Kodak benchmark: 9/9 images beaten. C implementation: 25 Mpixels/s decode.

Key innovations from tournament theory:

  • Structure-aligned scanning: the triangle's three sides map to three scan directions
  • Prediction-entropy duality: minimizing residuals $\neq$ minimizing compressed size
  • Quincunx pyramid: multiresolution checkerboard subsampling (5.3× on radial patterns)
  • $A^2$-guided codec selection: gradient context = implicit WL-1 coloring

14. Everything is the Triangle

The staircase $\delta_{n-2}$ is a right isosceles triangle. Its geometry unifies the entire theory:

  • $\sqrt{2}$ from the hypotenuse: pseudo-doubling ratio $2 - 1/(n-2)$
  • $\pi$ from the Wallis integral: fiber fraction $f(n) = (1/2)_{n-2}/(n-2)! \sim 1/\sqrt{\pi n}$
  • $e$ from Gamma growth: Burnside/Stirling asymptotics
  • $\gamma$ (Euler–Mascheroni): harmonic series correction in Gamma limits

Two reductions:

  • Mode A (hypotenuse removal, $n \to n{-}1$): fast time scale, vertex insertion
  • Mode B (both legs removal, $n \to n{-}2$): slow time scale, Cayley–Dickson descent
