Notation (THM-5070, base x = 2).  Ported tournament (T, u), state s(T, u) = (h, E, O): h = H(T); E (resp. O) = sum of
H(T - V(P)) over the directed paths P of T starting at u with |V(P)| even (resp. odd), H(empty) = 1.  Gluing a marked
block (B, w, u2): T (.) B = ordinal sum T => B with the one arc between u and w reversed (w -> u), new port u2.
THM-5070 (1) (PROVED there): s(T (.) B, u2) = N(B, w, u2) s(T, u), N a nonnegative integer 3 x 3 matrix with first
row (H(B), 2 q_o, 2 q_e).  The semigroup S_k is generated by the N of the blocks of order <= k; orbit = S_k (1,0,1).


LEMMA P (parity lemma).  STATUS: PROVED (all block orders); cross-checked FINITE-EXACT on the 1,884 distinct
matrices of order <= 6 (structure_scan_k6.out (1); finish_parity_lemma_check_k6.out: the formula of the proof, 1884/1884).

Statement.  For every marked block (B, w, u2),
        N(B, w, u2) = [[1, 0, 0], [e, A0, A1], [o, A1, A0]]   (mod 2),
where e / o = number of directed paths of B starting at u2 with an even / odd number of vertices, and A0 / A1 =
number of pairs (Q, R), Q a directed path of B from u2 ending at w, R a directed path of B - V(Q) or the empty
path, with |V(Q)| + |V(R)| even / odd.

Proof.  (a) By Redei's theorem H is odd for every tournament (and H(empty) = 1).  Hence for every ported tournament
(h, E, O) = (1, e_T(u), o_T(u)) (mod 2), with e_T(u), o_T(u) the numbers of directed paths of T starting at u of even
and odd order.
(b) In T' = T (.) B the only arc from B to T is w -> u, and every arc t -> b (t in T, b in B) is present except
u -> w.  So a directed path of T' starting at u2 either stays in B, or has the unique form Q P R: Q a path of B from
u2 to w; then (through w -> u) a path P of T starting at u; then (optionally, through an arc T -> B, which exists
because the first vertex of R is not w) a path R of B - V(Q); it cannot return to T because w is used.  Conversely
every such triple is a path.  Counting by the parity of |V(Q)| + |V(P)| + |V(R)|:
        e_T'(u2) = e + A0 e_T(u) + A1 o_T(u),      o_T'(u2) = o + A1 e_T(u) + A0 o_T(u).
(c) So with M = [[1,0,0],[e,A0,A1],[o,A1,A0]] we have s(T', u2) = M s(T, u) (mod 2) for EVERY ported T, while
s(T', u2) = N s(T, u) exactly.  The states of the single vertex, of the arc with port at its source and of the
3-cycle are (1,0,1), (1,1,1), (3,1,2), of determinant -1, hence independent mod 2.  Therefore N = M (mod 2).  QED

Corollaries (all PROVED).
(P1) The 16 matrices [[1,0,0],[p,x,y],[q,y,x]] over F_2 are closed under multiplication
     ((x,y)(x',y') = (xx'+yy', xy'+yx')), so EVERY element of every S_k has this form mod 2; the invertible ones
     (x + y = 1) form a group P_8 of order 8.  [group_probe.out (2): the monoid of the 56 generators mod 2 has exactly
     16 elements, 8 invertible.]
(P2) det g = x + y and trace g = 1 (mod 2) for every g in S_k.  First coordinates of orbit states are odd.
(P3) The characteristic polynomial of g in S_k is (t+1)(t+x+y)^2 mod 2.  If g is singular then x + y = 0 and
     charpoly(g) = t (t^2 - a t + b) with a odd and b EVEN.  When ker g has rank 1, the map induced by g on the
     rank-2 lattice Z^3 / (ker g) has characteristic polynomial t^2 - a t + b, so determinant b (when the rank of g
     is <= 1 there is no 2 x 2 action at all).  Hence: NO singular element of the port-chain semigroup (any block order, any
     word) induces a unimodular 2 x 2 action; in particular the autonomous (h, R)-system, R = E + O, of the blocks
     with N (0,1,-1)^T = 0 consists of 2 x 2 matrices of even determinant and is never a continuant system.
     [structure_scan_k4/k5/k6.out (2): a odd, b even for all rank-2 blocks; min |b| = 4, 2, 2.]


LEMMA D (dominating cycle).  STATUS: PROVED from the classical pancyclicity theorem (Harary-Moser 1966; Moon 1966,
vertex-pancyclic form; CITED); cross-checked FINITE-EXACT on all labelled tournaments with n <= 7
(omega_dominating_check.out: 0 failures among 2,097,152 at n = 7).

Statement.  Let Omega(T) be the conflict graph of the directed odd cycles of a tournament T (adjacent = sharing a
vertex).  The connected components of Omega(T) are exactly the sets of odd cycles contained in one strong component
W of T with |W| >= 3, and each contains a cycle C* on at least |W| - 1 vertices that meets every odd cycle of W.
Hence every component of Omega(T) has a dominating vertex and diameter <= 2.

Proof.  A directed cycle lies in one strong component, and cycles of different strong components are disjoint.  Let
W be a strong component, m = |W| >= 3.  T[W] is a strong tournament, so it has a cycle of every length 3, ..., m.
Take C* of length m if m is odd and m - 1 if m is even (then m >= 4 and m - 1 >= 3 is odd).  An odd cycle has at
least 3 vertices and at most one vertex of W is off C*, so every odd cycle of T[W] meets C*.  So the odd cycles of
T[W] form one component, dominated by C*.  QED

Corollary (negative result for "chains of cliques").  If a component of Omega(T) is a chain of cliques
C_1 - C_2 - ... - C_k (nonempty classes, edges only inside a class and between consecutive classes) then k <= 3,
because such a chain has diameter k - 1.  So the continuant-type recurrence I_k = I_(k-1) + 2 m_k I_(k-2) for
I(Omega, 2) along a chain of cliques cannot be iterated: no tournament realises a chain with k >= 4, and for a
non-strong tournament I(Omega, 2) is just the product over the strong components.  (Chains with exactly three classes
exist: 10,800 labelled tournaments at n = 6 and 334,880 at n = 7.)


PROPOSITION Z (Zariski closure).  STATUS: PROVED, using the exact computations of analyze_gens_k4.out.

Statement.  Let T0 = [[1,2,0],[0,0,1],[1,1,0]] (the one-vertex block, the tower) and g = [[1,4,4],[2,3,2],[2,4,3]]
(the transitive triple with w = sink, u2 = source); both are generators of S_3 with determinant 1.  The Zariski
closure of the semigroup generated by T0 and g is SL_3.  Consequently the semigroup of the 8 unimodular generators of
order <= 4 is Zariski dense in SL_3, and the semigroup of the 21 invertible generators of order <= 4 is Zariski dense
in GL_3.

Proof.  The Zariski closure G of a sub-semigroup of GL_3 is an algebraic group.
(1) charpoly(T0) = t^3 - t^2 - t - 1 is irreducible over Q (no root +-1) with discriminant -44, so its Galois group
is S_3 and it has one real root l1 = 1.839... and two complex roots l2, l3 of modulus l1^(-1/2).  If
l1^a l2^b l3^c = 1, applying the transposition (l1 l2) and dividing gives (l1/l2)^(a-b) = 1, so a = b since
|l1| != |l2|; likewise a = c.  So the only multiplicative relations are the powers of l1 l2 l3 = 1, and the Zariski
closure of {T0^n} is the whole maximal torus S = centraliser of T0 in SL_3 (2-dimensional, connected, defined over Q).
(2) Let H be the algebraic group generated by S and g S g^(-1); it is closed, connected, defined over Q, contained in
G, and contains the maximal torus S.  So Lie(H) = Lie(S) + sum of the root spaces of a set Psi of roots of
(SL_3, S), and Psi is stable under Gal(Q-bar/Q).  The Galois group acts on the three eigenvalue characters of S as
S_3, hence transitively on the six roots.  So Psi is empty or everything.
(3) If Psi is empty then H = S, so g S g^(-1) = S and g T0 g^(-1) commutes with T0.  But
g T0 g^(-1) T0 != T0 g T0 g^(-1) (exact computation: generator 18 is in the witness list of analyze_gens_k4.out).
So Psi is everything, Lie(H) = sl_3, H = SL_3, and G = SL_3 because T0, g lie in SL_3(Z).
(4) The 21 invertible generators include matrices of determinant 4, 5, -2, ...; an algebraic group containing SL_3
and an element whose determinant is not a root of unity is GL_3.  QED

Complements (FINITE-EXACT / VERIFIED, analyze_gens_k4.out, group_probe*.out).
- The algebra generated by the 8 unimodular generators (and by all 56) is all of M_3(Q) (dimension 9): absolutely
  irreducible; no common invariant line or plane; no invariant quadratic form.
- The 8 unimodular generators generate SL_3(F_p) mod p for p = 3, 5, 7 (orders 5616, 372000, 5630688).
- 2-adic image of the group generated by the 8 unimodular generators (all of determinant 1): orders 4, 128, 4096,
  2^20 mod 2, 4, 8, 16.  The step 8 -> 16 has the full ratio 2^8 = |sl_3(F_2)|, i.e. the image mod 16 contains
  Gamma(8)/Gamma(16).  Standard lemma: if a closed subgroup of SL_n(Z_2) contains Gamma(2^k)/Gamma(2^(k+1)) mod
  2^(k+1) for some k >= 2, it contains Gamma(2^k) (square X = 1 + 2^k A: X^2 = 1 + 2^(k+1) A mod 2^(k+2)).  So the
  2-adic closure is open: the full preimage of its image mod 8, of index 168 * 2^16 / 2^12 = 2688 in SL_3(Z_2).
- For the 46 unimodular generators of order <= 6 (determinants +-1) the images mod 4 and mod 8 have orders 2^12 and
  2^20 = the full preimage of P_8 inside {det = +-1}: by the same lemma (k = 2) the 2-adic closure is EXACTLY
  {g in GL_3(Z_2): det g = +-1, g mod 2 in P_8}.  The parity lemma is the only 2-adic constraint.
- Orbit of (1,0,1) mod q under the 56 generators (and under the 21 invertible ones): every (h,E,O) with h odd for
  q = 2^k, k <= 6; every residue of h for q = 3, 9, 27, 5, 25, 7, 49, 11, 13, 15, 21, 35, 45, 63, 105.  No congruence
  obstruction other than "h odd" was found.
- The group generated by the 8 unimodular generators contains non-trivial unipotent elements (20 among the words of
  length 2 in the generators and their inverses).  Whether it has finite index in SL_3(Z) is OPEN (not needed).


PROPOSITION C (the only 2 x 2 unimodular system inside port chains of block order <= 6).  STATUS: FINITE-EXACT
(structure_scan_k6.out (3)-(5), finish_plane_scan_k6.out).

(C1) Singular blocks: 2 x 2 quotient systems exist (267 blocks of order <= 6 kill (0,1,-1) and act on (h, R),
     R = E + O), but their determinants are even (Corollary P3): never unimodular.
(C2) Invertible blocks: among the 1,252 invertible matrices of order <= 6 the only invariant plane through the start
     state shared by two or more of them is h = O (normal (1,0,-1)); it is preserved by 4 invertible blocks, and 77
     singular blocks map everything into it.  On the plane, in the coordinates (h, E), the induced matrices have
     determinants -1, 1, 5, -21 (invertible blocks) and 0 or even (singular blocks).  The unimodular ones are
            A = [[5,4],[4,3]] = M(1) M(3) M(1)       (transitive triple, w = sink, u2 = source; M(a) = [[a,1],[1,0]])
     and A^2 (the order-6 block that is two such triples).  So the unimodular part is the cyclic semigroup <A>:
            H = K(1,3,1, 1,3,1, ..., 1,3,1) = 1, 5, 41, 333, ...,    H_(k+1) = 8 H_k + H_(k-1),
     a continuant with a FIXED word; there is no free alphabet.
(C3) No invertible block of order <= 6 has (0,1,-1) as an eigenvector with eigenvalue +-1, and no two invertible
     blocks share an invariant line killed by the readout.
Conclusion: within port chains of block order <= 6 there is no family H = K(a_1, ..., a_k) with the a_i free in an
alphabet; the theorems on Zaremba-type semigroups (which need the full continued-fraction semigroup of an alphabet of
dimension > 0.7748) have nothing to act on.


SPINE FAMILIES ARE TRIANGULAR (two_apex_algebra.out).  STATUS: FINITE-EXACT for the triangularisation of the four
explicit 36 x 36 letter matrices of the two-apex family G(2,0) and of the two 4 x 4 digit matrices of the one-apex
family; the transfer itself is VERIFIED against direct counts (460 words of length <= 6; all h < 2^8 for one apex).
In an explicit basis the four letters are upper triangular with the same diagonal {0 (x8), 1 (x16), 2 (x10), 3 (x2)};
the one-apex digit matrices are upper triangular with diagonal (1, 0, 2, 1).  So these semigroups lie in a Borel
subgroup (solvable Zariski closure): they are digit systems (regular sequences), not thin orbits of a semisimple
group, and expansion-based local-global machinery does not apply to them.
