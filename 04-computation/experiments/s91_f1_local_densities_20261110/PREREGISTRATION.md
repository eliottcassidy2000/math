## The claim being tested

For the F1 family `N = x1 x2 + 2^s y1 y2 - [s = 0]` (`x(h) = 2 psi(h) + 1`, `y(h) = 2h + 1`, ordered triples):

1. *Mechanism.* At an odd prime `p` exactly one residue class is biased on each sheet (`N = -1` for `s = 0`,
   `N = 0` for `s >= 1`), and to first order the bias is carried by the p-divisible rooted states, the `h` with
   `p | gcd(x(h), y(h))`: with `f` the share of the triples whose first state is p-divisible,
   `E = p D / (1 - D)`, `D = 2f - 1/p^2`, where `E` is the excess of the distinguished class over the mean of the
   others. After removing the p-divisible triples the distinguished class is deficient,
   `E_nd = p (f^2 - 1/p^2) / (1 - D)` (about `-1/p`).
2. *Weights.* The number of triples with first state `h1` and padding `s` is
   `W(h1, s) = (1/x1) int_{f0}^{f1} w(f) df` (`f0 = x1/2 + 2^s y1`, `f1 = x1 + 2^s y1`, `w(f) = (B - A)/f` below
   `A`, `B/f` on `[A, B)`), because `x/y` is uniformly distributed on `[1/2, 1]`.
3. *New primes.* The Fermat prime 65537 (`h = 2^15` has `x = y = 65537`) shows an excess of about `+0.18` (s = 0)
   and `+0.21` (s >= 1); the Wagstaff primes 2731 and 43691 about `+0.06` and `+0.07`; 257 and 683 as listed; the
   neighbouring primes 65521, 65539, 2729, 2741, 251, 263, 677, 691 and the Mersenne prime 8191 show none.
   None of 2731, 43691, 65537, 65521, 65539, 2729, 2741, 8191 was used in any earlier statement about F1; 65537
   and 43691 are below the noise on the seen windows.

All predicted values and all test intervals are in `prereg_window_27_24.json` (generated by
`python3 window_split.py 27 24 write-prereg prereg_window_27_24.json`, which does not enumerate the window; its
console output is `prereg_window_27_24.predictions.out`). The inputs of the predictions are the list of
p-divisible states below 2^26 (arithmetic of `psi`, not window data) and the two formulas above.

## Tests (intervals fixed in the json; rules in the docstring of window_split.py)

- A total mass (3 items): all triples within 0.2 %, each sheet within 2 % of the predicted number.
- B weights (18): the count of triples with `h1 = h` within 0.5 % of `W(h)` for `h = 0, 1, 2, 3, 8, 16, 64, 128,
  1024`, each sheet.
- C divisible fraction (68): 31 primes up to 131 and 241, 257, 683; relative error 3 % (first divisible state below
  2^12) or 15 %, plus `4 / sqrt(count)`.
- D excess at first order (64): the 26 fast primes up to 131 and 241, 257, 683, 2731, 43691, 65537;
  `|E - E_pred| <= 0.008 + 0.08 |E_pred| + 4 se`, `se = sqrt(0.75 p / #N)`.
- E excess at the slow primes (10): `|E - E_pred| <= 0.08` (p = 3), `0.15` (p = 7), `0.05` (p = 31, 73, 127).
  These are the primes where the second-order term is visible (rate at least 0.85 per binary digit).
- F controls (18): 251, 263, 677, 691, 2729, 2741, 8191, 65521, 65539; `|E - E_pred| <= 0.008 + 4 se`.
- G non-divisible deficiency (52): fast primes up to 131; `|E_nd - E_nd_pred| <= 0.012 + 4 se`.
- H signs (46): `E >= 0.10` for 3, 5, 7, 11, 17, 257, 65537; `-0.05 <= E <= 0.02` for 29, 31, 37, 61, 67, 71, 73,
  79, 83, 97, 101, 103, 109, 113, 127, 131.

## Calibration (in-sample, not evidence)

The same rules evaluated on the two seen windows: all eight hold on [2^18, 2^21); on [2^21, 2^23) all hold except
one item of H (65537 on the sheet s = 0: `E = +0.066` with standard error `0.10`; the window is too short for
that prime). The tolerances of E and the constant 0.75 were chosen on those windows.

## Outcome rule

A test is CONFIRMED only if every item is inside its interval. Any REFUTED test is reported with the failing items
and recorded in HYP-9660; the tolerances are not adjusted after the run.
