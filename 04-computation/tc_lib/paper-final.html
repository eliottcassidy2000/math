A tournament on $n$ vertices is a complete directed graph. Fixing a Hamiltonian path as base, the remaining $m = \binom{n-1}{2}$ arcs form a binary string on the staircase Young diagram $\delta_{n-2}$. We develop a theory connecting: (I) the Cayley transform $Q(x) = (1+x)/(1-x)$ whose Taylor coefficients count Delannoy lattice paths and govern the variance of Hamiltonian path counts (proved); (II) Walsh–Fourier spectral analysis showing $H(T)$ has degree $2\lfloor(n-1)/2\rfloor$ and is band-limited for $n \geq 6$ (proved); (III) GLMY path homology with $\beta_2 = 0$ for all tournaments (proved) and a seesaw identity $\beta_1 + \beta_3 = S - \dim(\mathrm{im}\,\partial_4)$ (proved); (IV) the conjecture that sorted rows of $A^2$ form a complete isomorphism invariant, verified on 191,536 classes; and (V) a lossless image codec achieving 20.9% smaller files than PNG on the Kodak benchmark using channel decorrelation and adaptive filtering.

Part I: Foundations

1. Tournaments and the Tiling Model

Definition.
A tournament $T$ on $n$ vertices is a complete oriented graph. The Hamiltonian path count $H(T)$ is the number of directed paths visiting every vertex exactly once. Rédei's theorem (1934): $H(T)$ is always a positive odd integer.

Fix a base path $P_0: n \to n{-}1 \to \cdots \to 1$. The remaining $m = \binom{n-1}{2}$ arcs (tiles) each have two orientations, giving a binary string $t \in \{0,1\}^m$. The tiles form the staircase Young diagram $\delta_{n-2}$ — a right isosceles triangle whose three sides control tournament structure:

2. The Odd-Cycle Collection Formula

Theorem (OCF, Grinberg–Stanley 2024). Proved.
For any tournament $T$: $H(T) = I(\Omega(T), 2)$, where $\Omega(T)$ is the conflict graph of directed odd cycles and $I(G, x) = \sum_{k \geq 0} \alpha_k x^k$ is the independence polynomial.

Verified exhaustively through $n = 8$ (all $2^{27}$ tournaments). The OCF connects a global property ($H$) to a local property ($\Omega$) via an algebraic evaluation ($x = 2$).

Part II: The Cayley–Delannoy Theory

3. Master Generating Function

Theorem 1 (Master GF). Proved.
$$Q(x)^m = \left(\frac{1+x}{1-x}\right)^m = 1 + 2\sum_{k=1}^{\infty} g_k(m)\, x^k, \quad g_k(m) = \sum_{j=1}^{\min(k,m)} \binom{k-1}{j-1}\binom{m}{j}2^{j-1}.$$
Corollary (Delannoy Identity).
$k\,g_k(m) = \sum_{j} j\binom{k}{j}\binom{m}{j}2^{j-1}$ counts total diagonal steps in all Delannoy paths from $(0,0)$ to $(k,m)$.
Corollary (Duality).
$k\,g_k(m) = m\,g_m(k)$. The matrix $T(k,m)$ is symmetric.
Corollary (Rodrigues Formula).
$g_k(m) = \frac{1}{k!}\left[\frac{d^k}{du^k}\,u^m(u+1)^{k-1}\right]_{u=1}$.
Corollary (Functional Equation).
$Q(x)^m \cdot Q(-x)^m = 1$. Even-index $g_k$ are algebraically determined by odd-index ones.

4. Variance Formula and Delannoy Paths

Theorem 2 (Variance Formula). Proved.
$$\mathrm{CV}^2(H) = \sum_{k=1}^{\lfloor(n-1)/2\rfloor} \frac{2\,g_k(n-2k)}{(n)_{2k}} = \frac{2}{n} - \frac{14}{3n^3} + O(n^{-4}).$$ The $1/n^2$ coefficient vanishes exactly because $g_2 = g_1^2$.

The sequence $W(n) = n! \cdot (1 + \mathrm{CV}^2(H))$: 1, 2, 8, 32, 158, 928, 6350, 49752, ... is not in the OEIS.

5. Wick Rotation and $\pi$

Theorem (Wick Rotation). Proved.
$\mathrm{arctanh}(ix) = i\arctan(x)$. In particular, $\mathrm{arctanh}(i) = i\pi/4$ and $Q(i)^m = i^m$. The number $\pi$ emerges from tournament enumeration via analytic continuation.

Uniqueness: $\mathrm{arctanh}$ is the unique odd formal power series with rational exponential (degree 1,1).

Part III: Spectral Analysis

6. Walsh–Fourier Diagonalization

Theorem (Walsh-OCF Factorization).
For a Walsh monomial $S$ of type $(2a_1, \ldots, 2a_r)$ with $k = \sum a_i$: $$|\hat{H}[S]| = \frac{2^r \cdot (n-2k)!}{2^{n-1}}, \quad \text{provided } 2k + r \leq n.$$

7. Band-Limitedness (THM-260)

Theorem (Band-Limitedness).
The Walsh degree of $H(T)$ in the tiling model is $2\lfloor(n-1)/2\rfloor$. For $n \geq 6$, this is $< m/2$, so $H$ is band-limited on the Hamming scheme.

Note: At $n = 4, 5$, the Walsh degree $2\lfloor(n-1)/2\rfloor$ exceeds $m/2$, so band-limitedness at the spectral midpoint holds only for $n \geq 6$.

Part IV: Path Homology

9. $\beta_2 = 0$ for All Tournaments

Theorem ($\beta_2$ Vanishing).
For every tournament $T$: $\beta_2(T) = 0$.
By strong induction using the long exact sequence of $(T, T \setminus v)$ and existence of a "good vertex" (THM-109). The Extreme Score Lemma gives the algebraic step: a vertex with score 0 or $n-1$ is in no 3-cycle, blocking other bad vertices.

10. The Seesaw Identity (THM-262)

Theorem (Seesaw Identity).
For any tournament $T$ with $\beta_2 = 0$: $$\beta_1(T) + \beta_3(T) = S(T) - \dim(\mathrm{im}\,\partial_4)$$ where $S(T) = \binom{n}{2} - n + 1 + \dim(\Omega_3) - \dim(\Omega_2)$.
From $\beta_2 = 0$: exactness at $\Omega_2$ gives $\mathrm{rank}(\partial_3) = \dim(\Omega_2) - \mathrm{rank}(\partial_2)$. Since $\beta_1 = \ker(\partial_1) - \mathrm{rank}(\partial_2)$ and $\beta_3 = \ker(\partial_3) - \mathrm{im}(\partial_4)$, the $\mathrm{rank}(\partial_2)$ terms cancel. This cancellation occurs precisely because $\beta_2 = 0$.
Conjecture (Seesaw).
$\beta_1(T) \cdot \beta_3(T) = 0$ for all tournaments $T$. By the seesaw identity, this reduces to showing $\dim(\mathrm{im}\,\partial_4) \geq S(T) - 1$ whenever $\beta_1(T) = 1$. Verified exhaustively at $n \leq 6$ and by sampling (3000 tournaments) at $n = 7, 8, 9$ with zero counterexamples.

Part V: Structure and Symmetry

12. The Metagraph $G_n$

The metagraph $G_n$ has vertices = isomorphism classes (OEIS A000568) and edges from single-tile flips. The merged graph $G_n/\mathbb{Z}_2$ decomposes into spine (SC-SC), ribs (SC-NS, bipartite), and sea (NS-NS, dominant at large $n$).

13. SC Orbit Pairing (THM-261)

Theorem (SC Orbit Pairing).
For an SC tournament $T^*$ with involutory anti-automorphism $\sigma$, the map $\varphi: C \mapsto \sigma(C)^{\mathrm{rev}}$ is an involution on directed odd cycles and an automorphism of $\Omega(T^*)$. When $\sigma$ is fixed-point-free (even $n$), cycles using one vertex per orbit pair as vertex-disjoint partners.

The orbit pairing provides a structural mechanism for large independence numbers in $\Omega(T^*)$, but does not by itself prove that SC tournaments maximize $H$.

Part VII: Everything is the Triangle

The staircase $\delta_{n-2}$ is a right isosceles triangle. Four constants emerge from its geometry:

  • $\sqrt{2}$: hypotenuse/leg ratio $\to$ pseudo-doubling $2 - 1/(n-2)$
  • $\pi$: Wallis integral from fiber fraction $f(n) \sim 1/\sqrt{\pi n}$
  • $e$: Gamma function growth (Burnside/Stirling)
  • $\gamma$: Euler–Mascheroni correction in $\Gamma(1/b)^b$

Two reductions: Mode A (hypotenuse, $n \to n{-}1$), Mode B (both legs, $n \to n{-}2$).

Part VIII: Open Problems

  1. Seesaw proof: Show $H_3(T, T \setminus v) = 0$ when $\beta_1(T) = 1$ (completes the LES induction).
  2. $A^2$ completeness: Prove sorted $A^2$ rows are a complete invariant for all $n$.
  3. SC Maximizer: Prove max $H$ in each SC score class is achieved by an SC tournament.
  4. $\chi(G_n/\mathbb{Z}_2) = n - 1$: Prove the chromatic number conjecture.
  5. $\beta_3$ growth: Is $\beta_3$ bounded? Current evidence: $\leq 2$ at $n \leq 9$.
  6. Only $H = 7, 21$ forbidden: Prove no other permanent gaps in the $H$-spectrum.
  7. Interval maximizer: Does the cyclic interval maximize $H$ among circulants for $p \geq 13$?
