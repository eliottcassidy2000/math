How It Works

The Problem

Industry compressors (gzip, zstd, lzma) use byte-pattern matching. They look for repeated byte sequences and replace them with references.

This works great for text. But for numerical data — sensor readings, gradients, waveforms — the bytes change smoothly, and there are few exact repeats.

The Solution

TC applies a mathematical transform before compression. Delta encoding (byte[i] -= byte[i-1]) converts smooth signals into near-constant streams.

A gradient [0,1,2,3,...] becomes [0,1,1,1,...] — trivial to compress. TC tries 10 transforms and picks the best. Cost: 1 byte overhead.

Pipeline:

Input → try 10 transforms → pick smallest → zlib compress → Output

Transforms: raw | delta | xor | delta² | stride(2,4,8)+delta | Gray+delta | word-delta
