## 2. Digital formulas and boundary conditions

For f(m)=g(m+1), the two recurrence formulas are correct, including m=0. In the even branch the increment is m+(m mod 2), not merely m. Iterating along the least-significant bits gives the first weighted formula. ## 3. 2-regularity and its exact meaning

Both five-by-five matrices are correct. Their action gives the entire vector V(2n) or V(2n+1), not just its first coordinate. The extension g(0)=1 is consistent with both transitions; T0 fixes V(0), so leading zero digits cause no change. It does not create an index-zero physical source.

For each e,r, composing the digit matrices expresses g(2^e n+r) as an integer linear combination of the five coordinate sequences. Thus the module generated by the 2-kernel is contained in a finitely generated integer module and is itself finitely generated. This proves 2-regularity. The independent checker verifies 2555 exact kernel-row controls, with n=0 included, in addition to full vector transition controls.

Reducing the vector modulo a fixed positive M indeed gives a finite automaton, with at most M^5 possible vector states. That result concerns residue outputs, not an automaton for the exact unbounded graph. Tests include M=1 and leading zeros.

## 4. MSB-first graph separation and exact intersection

Its input has sparse-bit parameters a=k+1 and b=m+1, so the actual deficit is 2^k while its proposed output has deficit 2^m. Equality holds if and only if k=m.

The alternative intersection statement is also exact. For a general word

the input's true deficit is 2^k, whereas the encoded input-output deficit is 2^(s+1). Hence membership is precisely s=k-1, with no restriction on r beyond positivity. The independent checks cover 9216 distinguishing pairs and 14400 intersection controls, including s=0.

## 5. Signed finite-carry closure and projection

The fixed-integer relation t=alpha*y+beta*v+gamma*(v mod 2)+delta is regular on padded nonnegative integer tuples. An explicit construction makes the signed-coefficient point precise. Read least-significant digits first, initialize

    carry_0=delta+gamma*(v mod 2),

and at each digit require an even numerator and set

    carry_next=(carry+alpha*y_i+beta*v_i-t_i)/2.

A valid transition keeps the carry within the fixed bound

    C=max(|delta|+|gamma|, |alpha|+|beta|+1).

After the supplied digits, acceptance at carry zero is equivalent to the exact integer relation. The first input bit determines the parity contribution once; extra high zero padding does not alter the accepted relation. This is a finite-state construction even with negative coefficients and negative intermediate carries. Reversal gives the most-significant-first relation. The independent checker compares it with exact arithmetic on 57344 padded triples for several positive, negative, zero-coefficient, and signed-constant cases.

For the graph-recovery argument, alpha!=0 is essential. If the nonnegative-output graph of h were regular, lift it to tuples, intersect with the fixed linear relation and v>=2, and project t away. The resulting numeric pairs satisfy alpha*y=alpha*g(v), hence y=g(v). Nonnegativity of h ensures that its output can be carried by the unsigned t track; uniqueness uses the nonzero coefficient, not its sign.

A longer t track may introduce extra common leading zero pairs into the projected encoding. Removing them uses a left quotient by the regular language of common zero pairs, followed by intersection with the regular canonical-encoding language. These operations preserve regularity and recover precisely the canonical graph. Equivalently, one may use arbitrary common padding throughout. Thus padding cannot invalidate the contradiction.

Adding or deleting finitely many initial indices also cannot repair the graph: each such index contributes a regular zero-padded word language. This is regular-language closure, not an assumption that padded encodings of finitely many numbers contain only finitely many words.

Let A=g(u), p=u mod 2. The independent matrix reconstruction gives the following exact coefficients of g(v):

Every expression is strictly positive for A>=1, u>=2, and p in {0,1}. Each also equals the d coordinate of the actual adjointed source, as independently checked. The row outputs are nonnegative because they are physical target readouts.

This concerns synchronized tuple encodings and each labeled template separately. It does not rule out algorithms with unbounded integer state, alternate encodings outside the theorem, regularity of value support, or regularity of an unlabeled union of witness relations. Projecting a nonregular relation may yield a regular set. No conclusion about eventual occupancy is imported from the graph obstruction.
