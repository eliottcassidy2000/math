## Main argument and surrounding architecture

1. **A model is not its ordinary values (Sections 3 and 11).** The proposed integer model is an infinite cyclic group of point codes on an elliptic curve over Q(sqrt(2)). Membership and addition operate on those codes. The final target is the ordinary embedded integers inside Q, so an additional ordinary-value relation is needed. This closely matches our distinction between a source word or numerical profile and an independently prescribed Hamilton-path count. A successful grammar or recognizable encoding is not by itself scalar coverage.

2. **Conjunction and witness ownership (Sections 2 and 10; Appendix C).** The manuscript uses sums of squares for conjunction, products for disjunction, guarded inverses, and distinct private witness blocks for template calls. Fixed scalar equations stay outside inactive branches. The actual saved polynomial, rather than an unspecified equivalent formula, is the intended object. The tournament counterpart is retaining an actual shared pair of physical source recipes, not choosing unrelated modular profiles or splicing coordinates from different realizations.

3. **Height plus contact information (Section 4).** A proposed six-contact estimate bounds rational height by a fixed constant times a product of contact primes to power24. The constants, support set, and order of choices matter because later local tests depend on them. In our independent theorem, the elementary substitute is the physical bound K(v,w)<=U_vU_w. This is enough to prove an exact-value bridge with an explicit growing height threshold.

4. **Approximate correctness followed by a finite guard (Section 6).** Local logarithm tests force high powers of contact primes to divide a multiplication error. The exponent advantage in the height argument bounds every false accepted triple in one finite box. A finite product excludes that box. The useful general pattern is an all-order bound first, then a finite exceptional guard, not extrapolation of finite success. A tournament analogue would be a genuinely subcritical bound for actual source witnesses outside a fixed exceptional target set. That bound remains a construction problem.

5. **Actual coefficient objects before normalization (Sections 7-8; Appendices E-G).** The manuscript's restricted converse argument constructs even and odd coefficient systems, uses omitted-prime, contraction, and isolation identities, arranges a common address family, and postpones odd normalization until a lower bound and nonzero witness exist. Its finite realization lemma retains fixed original data and adds new compatible primes. These are potentially useful ideas about preserving source identity across auxiliary constructions, but no elliptic or theta coefficient theorem is transferred to tournaments here.

6. **One functional and one finite diagram (Sections 8-9).** The same class-functional coordinate is transported through localizations, switches, finite models, and determinant clearing. Varying-field and fixed-field constants are kept separate. The relevant lesson is that separately achievable local conditions need not come from one common witness. Our exactness criterion consequently requires the congruence and product-height inequality on the same actual source pair.

7. **Boolean coefficient parity (Section 9.9, pages 75-76).** A low-degree indicator on a Boolean cube cannot have a unique supported address when its degree is smaller than the cube dimension. We independently apply this elementary mechanism to the exact source-word polynomial. The novel all-order result is stronger than a mere application of the degree bound: at the critical source length, the top coefficient is the perfect-matching parity of a squared path, giving sharpness at every dyadic precision.

8. **The final ordinary-value bridge (Section 11, pages 81-83).** One shared shift kappa enters both Close and Test. Test supplies h(t)<=B(n,kappa); Close supplies |t-n|<exp(-B). The elementary separation |t-n|>=exp(-h(t)) for a different rational forces equality. Its tournament counterpart is exact and independent: odd K congruent to odd n modulo m, with U_vU_w<n+lcm(m,2) and lcm(m,2)>n, must equal n. The same pair supplies both statements. The strict threshold is attained by a concrete false alias already in a primitive zero-root-change balanced family.

## What the new packet contributes

`PROOF.md` proves:

- At exactly 2^r-2 retained-root births, every odd residue modulo2^r has an odd number of indexed physical C1 constructions, for every actual source prefix and either ordinary or balanced closure.
- At every larger depth, every odd-residue fiber is nonempty and even. This gives an exact no-singleton theorem on modular labelled-word fibers, not on exact scalar or unlabelled graph fibers.
- The threshold is sharp at every r because the relevant perfect-matching count is Fibonacci F_(2^(r-1)), always odd.
- A precise, growing-height exactness criterion and its sharp physical boundary.
- A quantified source-pair lemma which would imply the existing four-block coverage conjecture if proved for all required targets.

The packet also proves why the first result does not settle the second: at the parity theorem's critical depth the actual counts are already exponentially large in the modulus. Their residues have the desired multiplicity while their ordinary values escape. A physical operation lowering the modular quotient without changing its residue, or a much more economical shared-witness constructor, remains the substantive next step.

## Carmichael connection, with the fiber type retained

Carmichael's conjecture concerns the multiplicity of an exact totient value. Our theorem concerns residues of Hamilton-path counts on an indexed family of labelled source words. What transfers is the method of detecting a singleton fiber by parity and forcing a second witness. What does not transfer is the scalar map, exact equality, or quotient by graph isomorphism.

The sharp critical level is particularly useful: every odd residue has odd positive multiplicity there, and the next level has positive even multiplicity. This gives a complete mechanism for the parity transition rather than a numerical analogy between two multiplicity questions. It is an actual family of tournaments because every bit chooses a physical O or Z birth.

## Number-field extension: the concrete obstacle and a safe replacement

The attachment's polynomial over Q does not automatically define the same set after replacing rational witnesses by K-valued witnesses. The witness domain enlarges. A model of integer indices over a field is also different from a definition of the ordinary integers or its ring of integers.

There is an additional concrete compiler issue: sums of squares encode conjunction over Q, but not over an arbitrary number field. If i lies in K, f^2+g^2=0 can have f,g nonzero. For a fixed number field K, an unconditional replacement for a finite conjunction is an anisotropic field norm. Choose a finite field extension L/K of degree at least the number of equations and linearly independent e_i in L. Then

    N_(L/K)(sum_i e_i f_i)=0  if and only if every f_i=0.

The norm vanishes only at zero, and K-linear independence separates the f_i. This repairs the finite logical compiler, but it does not repair the witness-domain or ordinary-value problem. If the ordinary Z were already Diophantine in K, a fixed integral basis would then give O_K as the existential Z-linear span of that basis. A result only about Z in Q does not, by itself, provide that hypothesis.

No claim of a solution of the general ring-of-integers question is made here. The useful outcome is a precise list of which mechanisms survive base change and which require new arithmetic input.
