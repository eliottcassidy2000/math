\noindent\textbf{Problem 12592} (proposed by Elliot Glazer)\textbf{.}
\emph{Consider a biased coin $C$ with sides labeled $0$ and $1$. When $C$ is
flipped, it shows $0$ with unknown probability $p$ and $1$ with probability
$1-p$, where $0<p<1$. Let the \emph{critical value} of a nonconstant bit
sequence be the number of bits in the maximal constant string starting at the
beginning.}

\emph{(a) Show how to use $C$ to simulate a fair coin by a method that assigns
to any bit sequence with critical value $n$ a result after at most $2n$ flips
of $C$. For example, if the first five flips of $C$ are $00001$, then $n=4$,
and the method must declare heads or tails after at most three more flips.}

\emph{(b) Find a simulation method as in part (a) that always gives a result
after $\max(2,\,2n-1)$ flips of $C$, where $n$ is the critical value.}

\section*{Notation and the exactness lemma}

Write the flips as independent bits $X_1,X_2,\dots$ with $\Pr(X_i=0)=p$ and
$\Pr(X_i=1)=q:=1-p$. For a nonconstant sequence, the critical value is
$n=\min\{k\ge 1: X_{k+1}\ne X_1\}$. A \emph{simulation method} is an online
rule: after each flip it either stops and declares \head{} or \tail{}, or
flips again, and the decision taken at time $k$ depends only on
$X_1,\dots,X_k$. The method is \emph{exactly fair} if
$\Pr(\head)=\Pr(\tail)=\tfrac12$ for every $p\in(0,1)$.

Both solutions below, and the further construction in the closing note, fit
one frame. For a sequence of critical value $n$, let $m$ be the unique power
of two with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:shell}
m\le n\le 2m-1 ,
\end{equation}
so that the first $m$ flips are constant while the first $2m$ are not. The
word $w=X_1\cdots X_{2m}$ then lies in the \emph{shell}
\[
S_m=\{w\in\{0,1\}^{2m}:\ w_1=\dots=w_m,\ w \text{ nonconstant}\},
\]
and distinct $m$ give disjoint cylinder events that exhaust the nonconstant
sequences.

\begin{lemma}[Exactness]\label{lem:exact}
Suppose a method decides every sequence of critical value $n$ by flip $2m$,
with $m$ as in \eqref{eq:shell}, so that on each shell the outcome is a
function $F_m\colon S_m\to\{\head,\tail\}$. If for every $m$ and every $k$
the words of $S_m$ containing exactly $k$ ones split equally between \head{}
and \tail{}, then the method is exactly fair.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
All words of $S_m$ with $k$ ones have the same probability
$p^{\,2m-k}q^{\,k}$, so the equal split makes the \head{} and \tail{}
probabilities agree shell by shell. The shells are disjoint events whose
union is the event that the sequence is nonconstant, and the two constant
sequences have probability $\lim_N p^N=\lim_N q^N=0$. Summing over shells,
$\Pr(\head)=\Pr(\tail)$, and the two add to $1$.
\end{proof}

Note that a method may reach its verdict before flip $2m$; the lemma applies
as long as the verdict is determined by the first $2m$ flips.

\section*{Solution to (a): dyadic block compression}

\emph{The rule.} Flip until the first disagreement $X_{n+1}\ne X_1$ reveals
$n$, let $m$ be as in \eqref{eq:shell}, and flip on through flip
$2m\ (\le 2n)$. Now compress the word $w=X_1\cdots X_{2m}$: group consecutive
symbols into pairs $(w_1w_2)(w_3w_4)\cdots$. If some pair is unequal, the
\emph{leftmost} unequal pair decides: declare \head{} if it is $01$ and
\tail{} if it is $10$. If all pairs are equal, replace each pair by its
repeated symbol (halving the length) and repeat. A nonconstant word of
power-of-two length can never be compressed to a single symbol, so after some
number $r\ge 0$ of halvings a deciding pair appears. The deciding pair at
depth $r$ is a pair of adjacent constant blocks of $w$ of length $2^r$,
jointly occupying an \emph{aligned} interval of length $2^{r+1}$ (positions
$(k-1)2^{r+1}+1$ through $k2^{r+1}$ for some $k$); the compression reaches
depth $r$ precisely because every aligned block of length $2^r$ is constant
and, at depth $r$, every pair to the left of the deciding one is equal.

\emph{Fairness.} Let $\sigma(w)$ be the word obtained from $w$ by swapping
the two constant blocks of its deciding pair. We claim $\sigma$ is a
fixed-point-free involution of $S_m$ that preserves the number of ones and
reverses the verdict; by Lemma~\ref{lem:exact} the method is then exactly
fair, since $\sigma$ matches the \head{} and \tail{} words of each
composition class in pairs.

The deciding pair cannot lie inside the constant first half, so its aligned
interval, having power-of-two length, either lies inside the second half or
is the whole word; in the latter case $w=b^m\bar b^m$ and
$\sigma(w)=\bar b^m b^m$. In both cases the first half of $\sigma(w)$ is
constant, and $\sigma(w)$ is nonconstant, so $\sigma(w)\in S_m$. Swapping two
constant blocks keeps every aligned block of length $2^r$ constant, so the
compression of $\sigma(w)$ again reaches depth $r$; the pairs strictly to the
left of the deciding interval are untouched and still equal, and the deciding
pair itself is still unequal but reversed. Hence the deciding pair of
$\sigma(w)$ is the same interval with the opposite orientation: the verdict
flips. Finally $\sigma$ permutes the letters of $w$, so it preserves
composition, and $\sigma\circ\sigma=\mathrm{id}$ with no fixed points.

\emph{Deadline.} The verdict comes at flip $2m\le 2n$ by \eqref{eq:shell}.
For the sample $00001$ we have $n=4$, $m=4$: at most three further flips.
\qed

\section*{Solution to (b): the cyclic checksum}

\emph{The rule.} If the first two flips disagree, stop at flip $2$: declare
\head{} on $01$ and \tail{} on $10$. Otherwise $m\ge 2$ in
\eqref{eq:shell}. Write the second half of the shell word as
$y_i=X_{m+i}$ for $1\le i\le m$ and set
\[
s(y)\;=\;\sum_{i=1}^{m} i\,y_i \bmod m,\qquad s(y)\in\{0,1,\dots,m-1\}.
\]
Declare \head{} if $s(y)<m/2$ and \tail{} if $s(y)\ge m/2$.

\emph{Deadline.} The coefficient of $y_m$ in $s$ is $m\equiv 0\pmod m$, so
$s$ is determined by flips $m+1,\dots,2m-1$ together with the value of $m$
--- and $m$ is known as soon as the first disagreement appears. If
$n\le 2m-2$, the disagreement occurs by flip $2m-1$, so the method stops at
flip $2m-1\le 2n-1$ (using $m\le n$). If $n=2m-1$, the first $2m-1$ flips
are constant and flip $2m$ itself is needed, but then
$2m=n+1\le 2n-1$ because $n\ge 3$. With the base case this gives
termination within $\max(2,\,2n-1)$ flips. (For $00001$: $n=4$, $m=4$, and
$s=y_1+2y_2+3y_3 \bmod 4$ with $y_1=1$ already seen, so at most two further
flips are used, one fewer than part (a) requires.)

\emph{Fairness.} By Lemma~\ref{lem:exact} (the verdict is a function of the
first $2m$ flips) it suffices to split every composition class of every shell
evenly. Fix the shell of $m\ge 2$ and a class with $k$ ones, $1\le k\le
2m-1$.

If $k\ne m$, the constant first half is forced ($0^m$ if $k<m$, $1^m$ if
$k>m$), so the class is a copy of the set of tails $y$ of fixed weight
$j\in\{1,\dots,m-1\}$, $j\equiv k\pmod m$. Write $j=2^au$ with $u$ odd and
put $\delta=m/2^{a+1}$, a positive integer since $2^a\le j<m$. Let $\rho$
rotate the tail cyclically, $(\rho y)_{i+1}=y_i$ (indices mod $m$). Then
\[
s(\rho y)\;=\;\sum_{i=1}^{m} (i+1)\,y_i - m\,y_m \;\equiv\; s(y)+j
\pmod m ,
\]
so $s(\rho^{\delta}y)\equiv s(y)+\delta j = s(y)+u\cdot\tfrac m2\equiv
s(y)+\tfrac m2 \pmod m$, as $u$ is odd. Therefore the map sending $y$ to
$\rho^{\delta}y$ when $s(y)<m/2$ and to $\rho^{-\delta}y$ when
$s(y)\ge m/2$ is a fixed-point-free, weight-preserving involution of the
class that exchanges \head{} and \tail{}: the class splits evenly.

If $k=m$, the class consists of exactly two words, $0^m1^m$ and $1^m0^m$,
with checksums
\[
s(1^m)=\tfrac{m(m+1)}2\equiv \tfrac m2, \qquad s(0^m)=0 \pmod m ,
\]
so they receive opposite verdicts. (Stopping at flip $2m-1$ when possible is
harmless: both one-flip extensions of such a stopping word carry the same
verdict, and $p+q=1$.) By Lemma~\ref{lem:exact} the method is exactly fair.
\qed

\section*{Note to the editor: a part (c), and the linear-deadline frontier}

The mechanisms above can be pushed further. We record here a sharper
envelope with full proof, the values of $n$ at which it is provably optimal,
and what we can and cannot say about the proposer's linear-deadline question
(minimize $C$ such that, for some constant $D$, a method always terminates
within $Cn+D$ flips). Write $T(n)$ for the worst-case number of flips a given
method uses over all sequences of critical value $n$.

\begin{lemma}[Floor]\label{lem:floor}
For every exactly fair method, no stopping word is constant; consequently
$T(n)\ge n+1$ for every $n$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
If the method could stop at the constant word $b^k$ with verdict $v$, then
$\Pr(v)\ge \Pr(X_1=\dots=X_k=b)$, which is $p^k$ or $q^k$ and exceeds
$\tfrac12$ for $p$ near $1$ or near $0$, contradicting exact fairness. Since
the first $n$ flips of a critical-value-$n$ sequence are constant, no verdict
can be reached before flip $n+1$.
\end{proof}

So the envelope $\max(2,2n-1)$ of part (b) is already optimal at $n=1$ and
$n=2$, and \emph{no} method achieves the uniform shift $\max(2,2n-2)$: that
would require deciding critical value $2$ by flip $2$, i.e.\ stopping at the
constant words $00$ and $11$. In particular an envelope of the shape
$\max(n,2n-2)$ is not attainable either; the correct sharpening carries
exceptions at $n\le 3$, where it meets the floor:

\begin{theorem}[a part (c)]\label{thm:c}
There is an exactly fair method whose deadline is $T(1)=2$ and, for
$m\le n\le 2m-1$ with $m=2^{r}\ge 2$ as in \eqref{eq:shell},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cdeadline}
T(n)=
\begin{cases}
\tfrac32 m, & n=m,\\[2pt]
2m-1, & m<n\le 2m-2 \quad (\text{so } m\ge4),\\[2pt]
2m=n+1, & n=2m-1 .
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
Consequently
\[
T(n)\le\max(n+1,\,2n-2)\ \text{ for all } n\ge1,
\qquad
T(n)\le\max(n+1,\,2n-3)\ \text{ for all } n\ge5 .
\]
The floor $T(n)=n+1$ of Lemma~\ref{lem:floor} is attained at $n=1$ and at
$n=2^{r+1}-2$ and $n=2^{r+1}-1$ for every $r\ge1$; in particular this method
is pointwise optimal at $n=1,2,3,6,7,14,15,30,31,\dots$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For $n=1$ use flips one and two as in part (b). Fix a shell
\eqref{eq:shell} with $m\ge2$, put $t=m/2$, and set $b=X_1$ and
\[
z_i=X_{m+i}\oplus b\qquad(1\le i\le m),
\]
the tail relative to the run bit. Membership in the shell says exactly that
$z\ne0$, and $z_1=1$ if and only if $n=m$. The verdict will be a function of
$b$ and $z$; on the branch $b=1$ we always give the relative word $z$ the
verdict opposite to the one it receives on the branch $b=0$, so it is enough
to describe the branch $b=0$, i.e.\ a set $H\subseteq\{0,1\}^m\setminus\{0\}$
of \head{} words.

\emph{What has to be checked.} A composition class of $S_m$ with $k$ ones is:
for $1\le k\le m-1$, the branch-$0$ words with $|z|=k$; for
$m+1\le k\le 2m-1$, the branch-$1$ words with $|z|=2m-k$; and for $k=m$, the
pair $\{0^m1^m,1^m0^m\}$, which is the \emph{same} relative word $z=1^m$ on
the two branches and therefore automatically receives opposite verdicts.
Since branch $1$ carries the complementary labels, Lemma~\ref{lem:exact}
reduces exact fairness to the single requirement
\begin{equation}\label{eq:layer}
\#\{z\in H:\ |z|=j\}=\tfrac12\tbinom mj \qquad (1\le j\le m-1),
\end{equation}
with $z=1^m$ free.

\emph{The rule.} It has two strata.

\emph{(i) If $z_1=1$}, so $n=m$: declare \head{} iff
$z_2+\dots+z_t\equiv0\pmod2$, and stop at flip $m+t=\tfrac32m$. (For $m=2$
the sum is empty, so $00\,1{*}\mapsto\head$ and $11\,0{*}\mapsto\tail$ at
flip $3$.)

\emph{(ii) If $z_1=0$}, so $n\ge m+1$: declare by a fixed table on
$(z_2,\dots,z_m)$ built below, and stop at flip $2m-1$ if $m\ge4$ and
$n\le 2m-2$, and at flip $2m$ otherwise.

\emph{Step 1: the counts the completion must realize.} Let $A_j$ be the
number of weight-$j$ words already put in $H$ by stratum (i). Choosing $z_1=1$,
an even number of ones among $z_2,\dots,z_t$, and anything among
$z_{t+1},\dots,z_m$ gives
\[
\sum_j A_j u^j
= u\,\frac{(1+u)^{t-1}+(1-u)^{t-1}}2\,(1+u)^{t}
= \tfrac12\Bigl[u(1+u)^{m-1}+u(1+u)(1-u^2)^{t-1}\Bigr].
\]
Write $e_j:=[u^j]\,u(1+u)(1-u^2)^{t-1}$, so that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:e}
e_{2a+1}=e_{2a+2}=(-1)^a\tbinom{t-1}a \qquad (0\le a\le t-1)
\end{equation}
and all other $e_j$ vanish. Then $A_j=\tfrac12[\binom{m-1}{j-1}+e_j]$, so by
\eqref{eq:layer} and Pascal's identity stratum (ii) must contribute exactly
\begin{equation}\label{eq:R}
R_j:=\tfrac12\tbinom mj-A_j=\tfrac12\Bigl[\tbinom{m-1}{j}-e_j\Bigr]
\qquad(1\le j\le m-1)
\end{equation}
\head{} words of weight $j$, out of the $\binom{m-1}j$ available. Both
$m-1=2^r-1$ and $t-1=2^{r-1}-1$ have all binary digits equal to $1$, so by
Lucas's theorem $\binom{m-1}j$ and $\binom{t-1}a$ are odd and $R_j$ is an
integer. For the range, write $m-1=(t-1)+t$ and use Vandermonde's identity:
$\binom{m-1}{2a+1}\ge\binom{t-1}a\binom{t}{a+1}$ and
$\binom{m-1}{2a+2}\ge\binom{t-1}a\binom{t}{a+2}$, and the second factors are
at least $1$ in the relevant ranges ($j=2a+2\le m-1$ forces $a\le t-2$); with
\eqref{eq:e} this says $|e_j|\le\binom{m-1}j$, i.e.\
$0\le R_j\le\binom{m-1}j$. Taking the first $R_j$ words of each layer in
lexicographic order already proves the theorem with $2m$ in place of $2m-1$
in \eqref{eq:cdeadline}.

\emph{Step 2: the completion can ignore the last flip.} This step is what
makes the method dominate part (b) rather than merely cross it: without it
the rule needs $2m$ flips throughout stratum (ii), which is worse than the
checksum's $2m-1$ at every $n$ with $m<n\le2m-2$. Assume $m\ge4$. We
choose the stratum-(ii) table to be independent of $z_m$, so that it is
determined by $y:=(z_2,\dots,z_{m-1})\in\{0,1\}^{m-2}$; then the whole rule
ignores flip $2m$, since stratum (i) ignores $z_{t+1},\dots,z_m$ as well.
Such a table is a set $S\subseteq\{0,1\}^{m-2}$, and the chosen $y$
contributes one \head{} word of weight $|y|$ (namely $z_m=0$) and one of
weight $|y|+1$ (namely $z_m=1$) --- except for $y=0^{m-2}$, whose weight-$0$
partner is $z=0^m$, not a shell word. Writing $s_j=\#\{y\in S:|y|=j\}$ and
$S(u)=\sum_j s_ju^j$, requirement \eqref{eq:R} becomes
\[
R_j=s_j+s_{j-1}\ \ (1\le j\le m-1),
\qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad
(1+u)\,S(u)=s_0+R(u),\quad R(u)=\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}R_ju^j
\]
(there is no requirement at $j=0$). By \eqref{eq:R} and $e_0=0$,
\[
2R(u)=\bigl[(1+u)^{m-1}-1\bigr]-\bigl[u(1+u)(1-u^2)^{t-1}-e_mu^m\bigr],
\qquad e_m=(-1)^{t-1}=-1 ,
\]
the last because $t=m/2$ is a power of two with $t\ge2$, hence even. Taking
$s_0=1$ we get
\[
2(1+u)S(u)=(1-u^{m})+(1+u)^{m-1}-u(1+u)(1-u^2)^{t-1} ,
\]
and since $m$ is even we may divide by
$1+u$ using $1-u^m=(1+u)\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}(-u)^i$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:S}
2\,S(u)=\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}(-u)^i+(1+u)^{m-2}-u(1-u^2)^{t-1} .
\end{equation}
Reading off coefficients, and using $[u^{2a+1}]u(1-u^2)^{t-1}=(-1)^a\binom{t-1}a$,
\[
s_j=\tfrac12\Bigl[\tbinom{m-2}{j}+1\Bigr]\ (j \text{ even}),
\qquad
s_{2a+1}=\tfrac12\Bigl[\tbinom{m-2}{2a+1}-1-(-1)^a\tbinom{t-1}a\Bigr].
\]
As $m-2=2^r-2$ has binary expansion $1\cdots10$, Lucas's theorem makes
$\binom{m-2}j$ odd for even $j\le m-2$ and even for odd $j$, so both
expressions are integers. They also satisfy $0\le s_j\le\binom{m-2}j$. For
even $j$ this says $\binom{m-2}j\ge1$. For $j=2a+1$ the two inequalities read
\[
\binom{m-2}{2a+1}\ \ge\ 1+\binom{t-1}a \ \ (a \text{ even}),
\qquad
\binom{m-2}{2a+1}\ \ge\ \binom{t-1}a-1 \ \ (a \text{ odd}),
\]
and the first implies the second. Since $t$ is even, $a=t-1$ is odd, and
there the requirement is $\binom{m-2}{m-1}=0\ge\binom{t-1}{t-1}-1=0$. For
$a\le t-2$, writing $m-2=(t-1)+(t-1)$, Vandermonde gives
$\binom{m-2}{2a+1}\ge\binom{t-1}a\binom{t-1}{a+1}$, which is at least
$2\binom{t-1}a\ge1+\binom{t-1}a$ unless $\binom{t-1}{a+1}=1$, i.e.\ unless
$a=t-2$; and for $a=t-2$ one has
$\binom{m-2}{m-3}=m-2=2t-2\ge1+(t-1)=\binom{t-1}{t-2}+1$. So $S$ exists;
take the first $s_j$ words of each layer of $\{0,1\}^{m-2}$ in lexicographic
order.

\emph{Conclusion.} Requirement \eqref{eq:layer} holds by construction, so the
method is exactly fair by Lemma~\ref{lem:exact}. For the deadline: stratum
(i) reads flips $1,\dots,m+t$, giving $\tfrac32m$; stratum (ii) reads
$b,z_2,\dots,z_{m-1}$, i.e.\ flips $1,\dots,2m-1$, and the verdict is known
as soon as the first disagreement has also been seen --- which happens by
flip $2m-1$ exactly when $n\le2m-2$, and otherwise ($n=2m-1$) at flip
$2m=n+1$. This is \eqref{eq:cdeadline}. Finally $\tfrac32m\le2m-2$ for
$m\ge4$ and $\tfrac32m=3=n+1$ for $m=2$; $2m-1\le2n-3$ for $n\ge m+1$; and
$2m=n+1$ for $n=2m-1$. The floor is met at $n=1$; at $n=2m-2=2^{r+1}-2$,
where $T=2m-1=n+1$ for $m\ge4$ and $T=3=n+1$ for $m=2$; and at
$n=2m-1=2^{r+1}-1$, where $T=2m=n+1$.
\end{proof}

For the sample $00001$ ($n=m=4$) the method stops after \emph{one} further
flip, declaring \head{} iff $X_6=0$.

\begin{remark}
The one flip saved in Step 2 is all that the shell structure permits. Encode
a branch-$0$ verdict rule by $f\colon\{0,1\}^m\to\{\pm1\}$. If $f$ ignores a
set of $c\ge1$ coordinates, then $f$ extends canonically to $z=0$ and
$F(u):=\sum_zf(z)u^{|z|}=(1+u)^cG(u)$ for some $G\in\mathbb Z[u]$. But
\eqref{eq:layer} kills the coefficients of $u^1,\dots,u^{m-1}$, so
$F(u)=\varepsilon_0+\varepsilon_mu^m$ with $\varepsilon_j=\pm1$; divisibility
by $1+u$ at even $m$ forces $\varepsilon_m=-\varepsilon_0$ and hence
$F(u)=\varepsilon_0(1-u^m)$, whose root at $u=-1$ is simple. Thus $c\le1$:
no exactly fair shell rule can ignore two of the tail flips
$X_{m+1},\dots,X_{2m}$. Our rule ignores flip $2m$. Within the scheme of
Theorem~\ref{thm:c} the same conclusion is visible directly: making the
stratum-(ii) table ignore $z_{m-1}$ as well requires
$(1+u)^2\mid s_0+R(u)$, i.e.\ $S(-1)=0$, whereas \eqref{eq:S} gives
$S(-1)=m/2$.
\end{remark}

We verified all three constructions by machine --- exact composition balance
in every shell, hence exact fairness for every $p$, together with the stated
deadlines and the causality of every early stop --- exhaustively through the
shell $2m=32$. Call a method \emph{shell-balanced} if it decides every
sequence of critical value $n$ by flip $2m$ and splits every composition
class of every shell evenly; all the methods above are of this kind, and by
Lemma~\ref{lem:exact} any such method is exactly fair. On the first
nontrivial shell $m=4$ there are exactly $1\,036\,800$ shell-balanced rules;
enumerating all of them shows that the achievable deadline vectors
$(T(4),T(5),T(6),T(7))$ are precisely
\[
T(4),T(5)\in\{6,7,8\},\qquad T(6)\in\{7,8\},\qquad T(7)=8,
\qquad\text{subject to } \ (T(4),T(5))\ne(6,6).
\]
So $T(4)\ge6$, and the values $T(6)=7$, $T(7)=8$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:c} meet
the floor. There are exactly two Pareto-minimal vectors, $(6,7,7,8)$ ---
attained by Theorem~\ref{thm:c} --- and $(7,6,7,8)$: within a shell one may
buy $T(5)=6$, but only by paying a flip at $n=4$. (The dyadic-contrast method
of remark 2 below realizes $(8,6,8,8)$ here, which is not minimal.) Two
consequences worth recording: the componentwise minimum $(6,6,7,8)$ is
attained by no single rule, and any method with $T(4)=5$ must abandon shell
balance altogether.

\noindent\textbf{A cross-shell addendum: every pointwise floor is attained.}
The shell obstruction does not survive global compensation.  Fix a power of
two $M$.  There is one exactly fair method with
\[
 T_M(1)=M,\qquad T_M(n)=n+1\quad(2\le n<M),                 \tag{*}
\]
after which the old cyclic-checksum continuation is left unchanged.  On
$2\le n<M$, stop at the first disagreement, declaring \head{} on both
initial-bit branches when $n$ is even and \tail{} on both when $n$ is odd.

Here is the complete capacity argument for the delayed $n=1$ branch.  In the
weight-$w$ layer of $\{0,1\}^M$, put
\[
 A_w=\binom{M-2}{w-1},qquad
 E_w=\#\{n\ge2:n\text{ even}\},\qquad
 O_w=\#\{n\ge3:n\text{ odd}\},
\]
where the last two symbols count words, not indices.  There are $2A_w$ words
with $n=1$.  Left cyclic rotation is a weight-preserving bijection from the
odd-$n$ words to the even-$n$ words whose last bit equals their initial bit.
Thus $E_w-O_w$ counts the remaining even-$n$ words.  If such a word has
critical value $n$, rotating it left by $n-1$ places gives an $n=1$ word.
This map is injective: the image ends in a run of exactly $n-1$ copies of its
initial bit, which recovers $n$ and the inverse rotation.  Consequently
\[
 0\le E_w-O_w\le2A_w.                                      \tag{**}
\]
All interior binomial coefficients $\binom Mw$ are even because $M$ is a
power of two.  Hence $E_w-O_w$ is even, and
\[
 r_w:=A_w-\frac{E_w-O_w}{2}
     =\frac12\binom Mw-E_w
\]
is an integer in $[0,A_w]$ by (**).  On the $01$ branch with $n=1$, read to
flip $M$ and label any $r_w$ of its $A_w$ weight-$w$ words \head{}; label the
$10$ branch \tail{}.  Each layer then has $E_w+r_w=\binom Mw/2$ heads, so its
head probability on the nonconstant length-$M$ prefixes is
$(1-p^M-q^M)/2$.  Splicing this prefix into the checksum method preserves
exact fairness and proves (*).

For any fixed $n\ge2$, choosing a dyadic $M>n$ meets the unavoidable floor
$T(n)\ge n+1$; $M=2$ handles $n=1$.  Therefore the pointwise optimum is
\[
                         T_{\rm opt}(n)=n+1\qquad(n\ge1).    \tag{***}
\]
The quantifiers in (***) are important: $M$, hence the method, may depend on
the target $n$.  This does not produce one method attaining all floors and
does not determine the asymptotic constant $C^*$ below.

There is nevertheless a single method which attains almost all of these
floors.  Partition the streams into the dyadic annuli in which the first
$d=2^r$ bits are constant and the first $M=2d$ bits are not.  Put
$H=M-1$.  On a nonconstant prefix of length $H$, label both orientations

\[
 n-d\text{ odd as \head,}\qquad n-d\text{ even as \tail}
 \quad(d<n<H).
\]

In a prefix weight layer let $A_w$ count the critical-$d$ words and let
$D_w=E_w-O_w$ be the signed count already prescribed.  The same rotation as
above gives $0\le D_w\le A_w$.  Moreover every interior layer of this odd
prefix shell has odd size, since over $\mathbf F_2$
\[
 (1+x)^{d-1}(1+x^d)=(1+x)^{2d-1},
\]
and all binomial coefficients in row $2d-1$ are odd.  Therefore
$A_w-D_w$ is positive and odd.  Select
\[
 q_w={A_w-D_w+(-1)^w\over2}
\]
critical-$d$ prefixes of weight $w$ as \head.  This is feasible and leaves
signed prefix count $(-1)^w$ in layer $w$.  Finally label
$0^H1$ as \head{} and $1^H0$ as \tail.  The exact cancellation is
\[
 (1+x)\sum_{w=1}^{H-1}(-1)^w x^w+x-x^H=0.
\]
Thus every full $M$-bit composition layer is bisected.  Applying the rule on
all dyadic annuli gives one fair method with
\[
 T(1)=2,\qquad
 T(n)=\begin{cases}2n-1,&n\ge2\text{ a power of two},\\
                    n+1,&\text{otherwise}.
       \end{cases}                                             \tag{****}
\]
So a single method meets the pointwise floor away from a zero-density set of
critical values.  The one-bit donor saving in (****) is sharp if every
non-donor row is required to stop at its floor: the donor enumerator can be
made divisible by $1+x$, but its derivative at $-1$ receives magnitude
$M-2$ from the top row and at most $2$ from the penultimate row, so it is
never divisible by $(1+x)^2$.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{The linear-deadline frame.}
The proposer's commentary poses the problem of minimizing $C$ such that, for
some constant $D$, a method always terminates within $Cn+D$ flips. Five
remarks, in increasing order of openness. The one we would most like to
report is remark~4: slope $2$ is \emph{not} optimal.

\emph{1. Taken literally, the pair $(C,D)$ is pinned at $n=1$ and cannot see
any of these improvements.} By Lemma~\ref{lem:floor}, $T(1)\ge2$ forces
$C+D\ge2$; so once $C=2$ the smallest admissible constant is $D=0$, and
$2n+0$ is already attained by part (a). The three methods are in fact totally
ordered pointwise: the exact worst-case profiles are $T(n)=2m$ for part (a);
$T(n)=2m-1$, except $2m$ at $n=2m-1$ and $2$ at $n=1$, for part (b); and
\eqref{eq:cdeadline} for part (c). So part (b) beats part (a) at every $n$
outside $\{1\}\cup\{2m-1\}$, and part (c) beats part (b) exactly at the powers
of two $n=2^r$, $r\ge2$, where it saves $2^{r-1}-1$ flips. Yet all three leave
the optimal pair $(2,0)$ untouched. The quantity a linear envelope \emph{can} see is
asymptotic, so the question should be read as being about
$C^{*}:=\inf\{C:\ T(n)\le Cn+D$ for some method and some $D\}$, with the
finitely many small $n$ absorbed into $D$. The finer object, and the one our
constructions actually move, is the exact envelope $T(\cdot)$ ordered
pointwise. A useful normalization is the \emph{excess} over the floor,
$d_n:=T(n)-n-1\ge0$; then $C=1+{}$(growth rate of $d_n$), and
$C^{*}=1+\gamma^{*}$ where $\gamma^{*}$ is the least $\gamma$ for which
$d_n=\gamma n+O(1)$ is feasible.

\emph{2. Which $n$ are hard is a $2$-adic question, not a linear one.}
There is a second exactly fair method, of a different design, with
\[
T(n)\;\le\;n+2^{\nu_2(n)}\qquad\text{for every } n ,
\]
where $2^{\nu_2(n)}$ is the largest power of two dividing $n$: stop at the
first \emph{aligned dyadic interval} $[2Lk+1,2L(k+1)]$ ($L$ a power of two)
whose two halves are constant and distinct, and declare \head{} iff the left
half is the $0$-block. Exchanging the two constant halves of the stopping
interval is again a probability-preserving, verdict-reversing involution on
stopped prefixes (no earlier stopping interval is created or destroyed), so
the method is exactly fair; and if $L=2^{\nu_2(n)}$, the blocks
$[n-L+1,n]$ and $[n+1,n+L]$ are the two halves of an aligned interval, the
left one inside the initial run, so a stop occurs by flip $n+L$. This method
therefore meets the floor $n+1$ at \emph{every odd} $n$ and stays within
$\tfrac43n$ unless $n$ is a power of two --- but pays $2n$ at $n=2^k$.
Theorem~\ref{thm:c}'s method has the complementary profile: fast \emph{at}
the powers of two ($\tfrac32n$) and at the top of each shell (the floor
$n+1$), slowest just above a power of two, where every even $n$ is costly.
The two are Pareto-incomparable. Both, and every classical construction,
pay slope close to $2$ somewhere near the powers of two --- though remark~4
below shows that this is avoidable. The difficulty concentrates
$2$-adically.

\emph{3. The dyadic structure is forced.} Call $[N,N+\ell)$ a \emph{block} if
a method decides every critical value in it by flip $N+\ell$, and
\emph{balanced} if every composition class of its terminal set
$\{0^Ny: y\ne 0^\ell\}\cup\{1^Ny: y\ne 1^\ell\}$ has even size --- the
hypothesis that makes Lemma~\ref{lem:exact} apply block by block, and which
all the methods above satisfy. Counting by weight, the class sizes are the
coefficients of $(1+u^N)(1+u)^\ell-1-u^{N+\ell}$, so the block is balanced
iff $(1+u^N)(1+u)^\ell=1+u^{N+\ell}$ over $\mathbb F_2$. That happens
\emph{if and only if $N$ and $N+\ell$ are both powers of two.} Indeed
sufficiency is $(1+u)^{2^g}=1+u^{2^g}$; for necessity, comparing coefficients
in degrees $1,\dots,N$ forces $\binom{\ell}{k}$ even for $k<N$ and
$\binom{\ell}{N}$ odd, so $N=\min\{k\ge1:\binom{\ell}{k}\text{ odd}\}
=2^{\nu_2(\ell)}$ by Lucas, whence $1+u^N=(1+u)^N$ and the criterion becomes
$(1+u)^{N+\ell}=1+u^{N+\ell}$. So every balanced block is a dyadic interval
$[2^a,2^g)$: the shells of parts (a)--(c) are not a design choice. In
particular $\ell\ge N$, so no balanced block has ratio $(N+\ell)/N$ below
two.

\emph{4. Nevertheless, slope $2$ is not optimal.} Block ratio and deadline
slope are different quantities, and the second is much smaller. Fix the shell
$[m,2m)$ and let a rule decide $n=m+k$ by flip $T(m+k)=m+k+1+a_k$; on the
branch $X_1=0$ the relative tail is $z=0^k1w$, and the rule reads exactly
$a_k$ coordinates of $w$, so its heads set on that stratum is
$S_k\times\{0,1\}^{L_k}$ with $L_k=m-1-k-a_k$. Writing
$p_{k,i}=\#\{y\in S_k:|y|=i\}$, balance of the shell says precisely
\[
\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\sum_{i=0}^{a_k}p_{k,i}\binom{L_k}{\,j-1-i\,}
=\tfrac12\binom mj,\qquad 1\le j\le m-1,
\]
to be solved in integers with $0\le p_{k,i}\le\binom{a_k}i$; every such
solution is realized by a rule. (Equivalently
$u\sum_kP_k(u)(1+u)^{L_k}=\tfrac12[(1+u)^m-1]+cu^m$. Note
$p_{k,i}=\tfrac12\binom{a_k}i$ solves it identically, so the \emph{only}
obstruction is that $\binom{a_k}i$ is odd for $i$ a binary submask of $a_k$
--- the corner deficits again, now confined to one shell.) Feasibility is
monotone in each $a_k$, so the least achievable
$\rho(m)=\min\max_kT(m+k)/(m+k)$ is computable. We find
\[
\rho(4)=\tfrac32,\quad \rho(8)=\tfrac{14}9,\quad
\rho(16)=\tfrac{25}{16},\quad \rho(32)\le\tfrac{11}7,
\]
with explicit rules --- e.g.\ on $[16,32)$ the deadlines
$25,26,28,29,31,32,\dots,32$. Since balanced blocks stitch, this gives an
exactly fair method with
\[
T(n)\;\le\;\tfrac{11}{7}\,n \qquad\text{for } 4\le n<64,
\]
against $2n-2$ for Theorem~\ref{thm:c}. So the slope-$2$ envelope of the
classical constructions is an artifact of never optimizing the within-shell
profile, not a barrier. (These are verified: the $m=4$ row reproduces the
exhaustive $1\,036\,800$-rule enumeration above, the rules for $m\le16$ were
rebuilt and brute-force checked, and the $m=32$ witness was re-verified in
exact integer arithmetic.)

\emph{5. Nor is slope $3/2$ achievable: a floor near $1.598$.} Put
$p_{k,i}=\tfrac12(\binom{a_k}i+e_{k,i})$, legitimate because every level is
exactly centred; the system becomes homogeneous,
$\sum_kE_k(u)(1+u)^{L_k}=\pm u^{m-1}$ with
$|[u^i]E_k|\le\binom{a_k}i$ and $[u^i]E_k\equiv\binom{a_k}i\ (2)$. Modulo
$2$ there is \emph{no} obstruction: the forced-odd slots are the binary
submasks of $a_k$ and $\sum_{i\subseteq a}u^i=(1+u)^a$ over $\mathbb F_2$,
so the pattern collapses to $\sum_k(1+u)^{m-1-k}=[(1+u)^m-1]/u=u^{m-1}$,
the last step exactly because $m$ is a power of two --- every coefficient
below the top vanishes identically, for every profile. The obstruction is
archimedean, and appears at $u=-1$: writing $u=-1+v$ turns $(1+u)^{L_k}$
into $v^{L_k}$, and $|[v^r]E_k(-1+v)|\le\binom{a_k}r2^{a_k-r}$ forces
\[
\binom{m-1}{d}\;\le\!\!\sum_{k\,:\,0\le d-L_k\le a_k}\!\!
\binom{a_k}{d-L_k}2^{\,a_k-d+L_k}\qquad\text{for all }d .
\]
This certifies $\rho(m)>1.4000,1.5000,1.5556,1.5610,1.5753,1.5828,1.5887,
1.5925,1.5949,1.5962$ for $m=4,\dots,2048$, hence $C^{*}>1.596$ --- well
above the $3/2$ of the classical argument. Rescaling $k=xm$, $d=\delta m$,
the condition survives iff $H(\delta)\le\max_x[\alpha H(r/\alpha)+\alpha-r]$,
whose least admissible slope is $C_{\mathrm{arch}}=1.5979874\ldots$ at
$\delta^{*}=0.6179\ldots$; the finite-$m$ bounds converge to it.

\emph{6. The frontier.}
So $C^{*}$ is neither $2$ nor $1$: for balanced block schemes it lies in
$(1.596,2]$, and both the exact small-$m$ values and the near-tightness of
remark 5 point at $C^{*}=C_{\mathrm{arch}}\approx1.598$. What is missing is
the matching construction. We also
do not know whether $C=1$ (that is, $T(n)\le n+D$) is impossible, though we
expect it is. The question has a
concrete equivalent form. A method with deadline $T$ never stops before the
first disagreement (Lemma~\ref{lem:floor}), and after the disagreement at
$0^{n}1$ or $1^{n}0$ its stopping words form a complete prefix code of depth
at most $d_n:=T(n)-n-1$; padding to exact depth $d_n$ and counting \head{}
leaves by weight, one checks that such a method exists \emph{if and only if}
there are integers $0\le w_{n,k},v_{n,k}\le\binom{d_n}k$ for which the
polynomials $W_n(p)=\sum_{k}w_{n,k}p^{\,d_n-k}q^{\,k}$ and
$V_n(p)=\sum_{k}v_{n,k}q^{\,d_n-k}p^{\,k}$ satisfy
\[
\sum_{n\ge1}p^{n}q\,W_n(p)\;+\;\sum_{n\ge1}q^{n}p\,V_n(p)\;=\;\frac12
\qquad\text{for all } p\in(0,1).
\]
(The two sums run over the $0$-spine and the $1$-spine; the coefficients
$p^nq$ and $q^np$ are the probabilities of the two disagreement patterns and
sum to $1$, so the identity says exactly that the conditional heads-share
averages to $\tfrac12$.) Part (a) solves this with $d_n=n-1$, part (b) with
$d_n=n-2$, and Theorem~\ref{thm:c} with $d_n$ dropping to $\tfrac n2-1$ at
$n=2^r$ and to $0$ at $n=2^{r+1}-2$ and $n=2^{r+1}-1$. Whether it is solvable
with $d_n=\gamma n+O(1)$ for some $\gamma<1$ is exactly the proposer's
question, with $C^{*}=1+\inf\gamma$. For $C=1$ the $W_n$ range over a fixed finite set
of polynomials, which is why we expect a rigidity argument to refute that
case.  The individual pointwise values are all settled by (***) above; the
finite questions that remain concern simultaneous Pareto profiles and their
compatibility with a uniform asymptotic envelope. We would be glad to
learn what value of $C$ the proposer has in mind, and whether the intended
reading of $Cn+D$ is uniform or asymptotic.
