THE IDENTIFICATION.
Lambda = R(u) u^{-M} with R(0) != 0 and deg R = d = M+N is a ONE-VARIABLE Laurent polynomial whose exponents run from -M to N. So for every M >= 1, N >= 1 it is TWO-SIDED -- neither a polynomial in u nor in u^{-1}.
Duistermaat and van der Kallen, 'Constant terms in powers of a Laurent polynomial', Indag. Math. (N.S.) 9 (1998) 221-231, prove EXACTLY THIS CASE FIRST AND SEPARATELY as their THEOREM 2, in about one page, using only residues and Liouville. Their own words: 'We start with the case l = 1 ... as it is much more elementary and yet illustrates the method.'
    DvdK Theorem 2: if f in C[z,z^{-1}] is neither a polynomial in z nor in z^{-1}, then f has a critical value v != 0 with limsup |Cst(f^n)|^{1/n} = |v| > 0.
    DvdK Remark 3: hence if CT(f^n) = 0 for every n >= 1, then f is in C[z] or C[z^{-1}], without constant term.
Applying Remark 3 to Lambda, the two allowed conclusions are:
    Lambda in C[u^{-1}] with no constant term  <=>  all exponents < 0  <=>  deg R < M -- VERBATIM the degenerate case THM-1610(B) identified;
    Lambda in C[u] with no constant term  <=>  all exponents > 0  <=>  -M > 0, IMPOSSIBLE for M >= 1.
=> TNC(M,N) IS DvdK THEOREM 2 + REMARK 3, FOR EVERY (M,N). It has been a theorem since 1998.
I read the primary text (van der Kallen posts it free at webspace.science.uu.nl/~kalle101/powers.pdf); this is not from a search snippet. A second, purely algebraic proof is Monsky's (arXiv:0906.1836), written out in van den Essen-Schoone arXiv:2305.10062 SS5.

WHAT SURVIVES, AND IT IS THE GOOD PART.
THE EFFECTIVE QUESTION IS OPEN. Sturmfels asked for an effective DvdK; the sharp form is Erman-Smith-Varilly-Alvarado Conjecture 1 (JCTA 118 (2011) 396-402, arXiv:0908.2609): for doubly-monic f = z^{-m} + ... + z^{n}, the constant terms [[f^1]],...,[[f^{m+n}]] generate the unit ideal -- i.e. THE FIRST NONZERO CONSTANT TERM OCCURS BY INDEX m+n. Open as of 2011; no resolution found in a 2026 sweep. It is TIGHT: f = z^{-N} + z first hits at exactly N+1, so no universal constant bound can exist.
AND THE REPO'S MACHINERY IS ALREADY POINTED AT IT. The branching ladder at (2,2) produced the non-degenerate branch R = 1 + u + u^3 - u^4, i.e. Lambda = u^{-2} + u^{-1} + u - u^2 with exponents -2..2, and found its first nonzero constant term at EXACTLY m = 4 -- and m+n = 4. The computation lands on the ESV bound, tightly, from a completely independent direction. The tooling was aimed at the right open problem and merely mislabelled.
