        SESSION S285: RARE DUAL EDGES

DEFINITION: A DUAL EDGE connects two merged classes via BOTH
a wiggly line (tile flip) AND a complement line (flip all tiles).

FREQUENCY: 67% → 39% → 26% → 8.2% at n=4,5,6,7 (shrinking)

THREE KEY PATTERNS:

1. DUAL EDGES CONNECT NEARBY CLASSES:
   Mean |ΔH|: dual=21, complement-only=30, wiggly-only=21 (at n=7)
   Dual edges are "short-range" — they connect classes with similar H.

2. W/B WEIGHT RATIO GROWS EXPONENTIALLY:
   n=4: 1.5, n=5: 8.4, n=6: 14.7, n=7: 59.7
   Dual edges carry MUCH more wiggly weight than complement weight.
   The wiggly signal overwhelms the complement signal at these edges.

3. DUAL EDGES LIVE IN THE SEA:
   At n=7: all 621 dual edges are NS-NS (sea).
   Zero dual edges on the spine or ribs.
   The skeleton (SC structure) is completely non-dual.

INTEGRATION WITH KIND-PASTEUR (S20ez, S20fa):
   The even rarer "collapsed edges" (class adjacent to own complement)
   follow sequence 0,0,5,0 at n=4..7 (even-n spikes, odd-n zeros).
   These are a SUBSET of dual edges — the most extreme case where
   C is adjacent to C^c in the unmerged graph.
   At n=6: 5 collapsed out of 66 dual edges (7.6%).

PHYSICAL MEANING:
   Dual edges are "resonances" — places where local (single tile)
   and global (all tiles) operations connect the same classes.
   They become rarer because the two operations become orthogonal
   as n grows: changing 1 of m tiles vs changing all m tiles
   have less and less in common.
