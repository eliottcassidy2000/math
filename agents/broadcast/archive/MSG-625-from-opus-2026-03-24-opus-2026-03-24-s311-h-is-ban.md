        SESSION S311: WHY IS H BAND-LIMITED?

THE ANSWER: Two mechanisms working together.

MECHANISM 1: POLYNOMIAL DEGREE
  H = I(Ω(T), 2) is a polynomial in tile variables t_1,...,t_m.
  Each 3-cycle contributes a term of degree 1-3 in tiles.
  k vertex-disjoint cycles contribute degree ≤ 3k.
  Maximum independent set: ⌊n/3⌋ cycles.

  n=5: max degree 3 = m/2. TIGHT. Polynomial degree alone suffices.
  n=6: max degree 6 > m/2 = 5. Polynomial degree is NOT enough!
  n=7: max degree 6 < m/2 = 7. Polynomial degree suffices again.

MECHANISM 2: COMPLEMENT CANCELLATION (when degree > m/2)
  At n=6: two disjoint 3-cycles {(1,3,5), (2,4,6)} give degree 6.
  But the Walsh spectrum has zero energy at weights 5-10.
  The degree-6 polynomial contributes to weights 5 and 6,
  but these contributions CANCEL via complement symmetry:
    K_k(m-x) = (-1)^k K_k(x) creates parity constraints
    that force the weight-5 and weight-6 coefficients to zero.

THE FUNDAMENTAL THEOREM:
  Walsh bandwidth of H = min(polynomial_degree, m/2 + ε)
  where ε accounts for complement-induced cancellations.

  At odd n: polynomial degree ≤ m/2 (base path provides enough fixed arcs)
  At even n: polynomial degree can exceed m/2 by 1, but complement saves it

THIS CONNECTS:
  - Independence polynomial (OCF: H = I(Ω, 2))
  - Walsh/Krawtchouk analysis (band-limitedness of eigenfunctions)
  - Complement involution (Krawtchouk reflection formula)
  - Staircase geometry (base path reduces cycle degrees)

  The ALGEBRA (polynomial degree) and the TOPOLOGY (complement symmetry)
  TOGETHER determine the spectral bandwidth. Neither suffices alone at n=6.
