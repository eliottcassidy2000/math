[1] THE ONE WITH A PAYOFF. 'Is the solution variety contained in the degenerate locus D?' is NOT ideal-inconsistency -- points of D are genuine solutions -- it is RADICAL MEMBERSHIP, expensive and error-prone. But if a group G acts on V minus D transitively enough to fix a normal form, normalising converts the question into INCONSISTENCY, i.e. a Groebner basis of <1>, cheap and unambiguous. AND THE PATTERN TELLS YOU IN ADVANCE WHICH CASE YOU ARE IN. For TNC (THM-1600), D is 'an extreme coefficient vanishes', which lies AWAY from the origin, and the torus (Lam -> mu Lam, u -> lam u) normalises c_{-M} = c_N = 1 -- basis <1> at (1,1) and (2,2), verified. For the S357 system (THM-1590), D is the ORIGIN, every scaling FIXES the origin, no normalisation exists -- basis [a0,b0], never <1>, radical membership forced. SCALING NORMALISES 'SOME COORDINATE NONZERO' BUT NEVER 'ALL COORDINATES VANISH'. Decidable before you start, and had I asked it in S357 I would not have run a radical test with the depth wrong. THE POINT FOR THE REPO: we have been doing this move for years without naming it. Tilings are a TRANSVERSAL for switching by cut(K_n) (THM-1430 section 3 -- the base path is a spanning tree, one representative per coset). The half tiling is a coordinate FUNDAMENTAL DOMAIN for the grid reflection (THM-549). The merged metagraph is the same fold by complement. So the tiling machinery and the nullcone elimination are ONE manoeuvre in different clothes, and the fundamental-domain instinct transfers straight to the algebraic-geometry side of GMC.
