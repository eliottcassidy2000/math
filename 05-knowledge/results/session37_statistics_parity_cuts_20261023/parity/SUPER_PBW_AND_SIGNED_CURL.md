# Vertex-parity PBW and the signed curl quotient

Status: PROVED as a formal superalgebra construction and a signed refinement of the inherited Session36 curl classification; standard super-Lyndon/PBW theorems are CITED. FINITE-EXACT controls are separate. No physical interpretation or novelty claim for free Lie superalgebras is made.

This note's parity is VERTEX COUNT modulo two. The companion `EXTERIOR_TOURNAMENT_FOCK.md` uses EDGE COUNT parity of a different family of graph components. These are two valid but different constructions and must not be identified.

## 1. Recovery and the chosen parity

Let V be the rational vector space with basis nonempty strongly connected tournament isomorphism types, a singleton included. The inherited SCC decomposition identifies the ordered-join type algebra with T(V), with Hilbert series

    A(z)=1/(1−S(z)),

where S counts strong types by vertex count. Let e_n count connected Royle automorphism-even graph types. The old SCC/Lyndon codec and Session36 recover

    A(z)=∏_(n≥1)(1−z^n)^(−e_n),

and e_n is the number of primitive cyclic SCC words of total vertex weight n. “Primitive” here means not an r-th power for any integer r>1; it does not mean strong, indecomposable under every operation, or prime tournament.

Give a strong generator S parity p(S)=|V(S)| mod 2 and extend additively to words. This is a specified supergrading compatible with ordered join, not a conclusion forced by the fact that a tournament automorphism group has odd order. The latter group-order fact is a different parity statement.

There is a geometric justification for retaining this particular supergrading: exchanging vertex blocks of sizes m and n changes their top exterior orientation by (−1)^(mn). Its cyclic form will become the actual vertex-sign character of curl stabilizers in section 4.

## 2. The precise PBW factorization, including the missing odd squares

Use the supercommutator

    [x,y]_s = xy − (−1)^(p(x)p(y)) yx.

The Lie subsuperalgebra L generated by V inside T(V) is the free Lie superalgebra, and U(L)=T(V). Standard super-PBW says that the associated graded enveloping algebra, for the PBW filtration, is

    gr U(L) ≅ Sym(L_even) ⊗ Λ(L_odd).

This is not generally an isomorphism of associative algebras U(L)≅Sym⊗Λ. The latter is the associated graded statement; ordered PBW monomials also give an isomorphism of graded vector spaces after a basis is chosen.

The standard super-Lyndon basis consists of ordinary Lyndon brackets, plus [u,u]_s for each odd Lyndon bracket u. For odd u,

    [u,u]_s=2u²,

which is nonzero in the free tensor algebra. Thus assigning a fermionic PBW occupation to u DOES NOT discard repeated u from the original tournament-word algebra: its pair is stored by an additional even Lie generator. In PBW degree this square has fallen from the product of two Lie generators to one even Lie generator; this is why it disappears only in the associated graded odd square.

Primary source: Bokut–Kang–Lee–Malcolmson, [Gröbner–Shirshov Bases for Lie Superalgebras and Their Universal Enveloping Algebras](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/9809024), section 2 definition of super-Lyndon words, Theorem 2.2 and Proposition 2.11; published J. Algebra 217 (1999), 461–495, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1006/jabr.1998.7810). This note imports only the basis/PBW facts, not any tournament claim. We work over Q, so the required characteristic restrictions are automatic.

Every degree-n Lie monomial has parity n mod 2. Hence, if β_n and φ_n denote even and odd Lie dimensions,

    φ_n = e_n                        if n odd, and 0 otherwise;

    β_n = e_n + 1_(n≡2 mod 4)e_(n/2) if n even, and 0 otherwise.       (10)

The first summand comes from primitive words of weight n. The extra even summand is the square of an odd primitive word of weight n/2. PBW gives

    A(z)
      = ∏_(n even)(1−z^n)^(−e_n−1_(n≡2 mod4)e_(n/2))
        · ∏_(n odd)(1+z^n)^(e_n).                                  (11)

An independent scalar check pairs each odd m factor with its square:

    (1+z^m)/(1−z^{2m}) = 1/(1−z^m).

This recovers the inherited ordinary Euler product exactly. In particular, omitting the extra even generators is already false at n=2: for the singleton generator a, the word aa is a nonzero two-vertex transitive tournament, while Λ(a) has no degree-two vector. The free Lie superalgebra contains [a,a]_s=2aa, restoring that coefficient.

The dimensions at n=1,…,8 are

    dim L_n: 1,1,1,2,8,44,398,6413;

with parity n mod 2. These are not the companion note's connected b_n/f_n. Both Hilbert products count tournaments, but they retain different structures and use different generators.

The total connected-space dimensions of the two constructions agree at vertex orders one and three through seven, but already differ at order two: the exterior graph model has b_2+f_2=0, whereas L_2 has dimension1 from the odd singleton square. They differ again at eight: the exterior graph model has b_8+f_8=6414 connected dimensions, whereas L_8 has dimension6413. Even before that, their parity splittings disagree at order one: the singleton graph has zero edges and is even in the graph model, while the singleton tournament generator is odd in the vertex model. Matching small total dimensions is not a grading-preserving identification.

## 3. Super-cocenter: a necklace can vanish rather than just lose its initial gap

Define the super-cocenter

    Q_s=T(V)/span{xy−(−1)^(p(x)p(y))yx : x,y homogeneous}.

The denominator is a linear span, not a two-sided ideal. A word uv is identified with its rotation vu, multiplied by (−1)^(wt(u)wt(v)). Thus each ordinary necklace contributes either a one-dimensional orientation line or zero.

Let w=u^r, where u is its unique primitive cyclic root, of total vertex weight d. Rotating by one complete copy of u fixes the word and acts by

    (−1)^(d·d(r−1)) = (−1)^(d(r−1)).

Every stabilizing rotation is a power of this primitive rotation. Therefore:

    the necklace u^r vanishes in Q_s
      iff d is odd and r is even.                                  (12)

Otherwise it contributes exactly one dimension. Indeed, if no stabilizer has sign −1, one can choose a representative, transport its coefficient along rotations with their signs, and obtain a consistent nonzero linear functional on that orbit. If a stabilizer has sign −1, the relation identifies the representative with its negative and kills it in characteristic zero.

Primitive necklaces never vanish. But the natural map L→Q_s still kills EVERY Lie superbracket, including the extra odd squares. Its image is precisely the one-letter strong-generator space V. A basis count connecting e_n to primitive necklaces does not make this quotient map a primitive-necklace realization of L.

Let c_n be the ordinary curl-necklace count from Session36 and let d_n=dim(Q_s)_n. Since c_n=Σ_(j|n)e_j, equation (12) gives

    d_n=c_n                                  if n odd;
    d_n=c_n−Σ_(j|n, j odd)e_j                if n even;

equivalently,

    d_n=c_n−1_(2|n)c_(oddpart(n)).                                  (13)

At n=1,…,8:

    c_n: 1,1,2,3,9,45,399,6416;
    d_n: 1,0,2,2,9,43,399,6415.

There is also a signed Burnside formula. A rotation with cycles of length k contributes a factor (−1)^(k−1) on each odd generator. Hence

    Σ_(n≥1)d_n z^n
      = Σ_(k≥1) (φ(k)/k) log A((−1)^(k−1)z^k).                     (14)

Here φ is Euler's totient, unrelated to the odd Lie dimension φ_n above. Formula (14) is an ordinary trace/dimension of the signed rotation representation; it is not the superdimension obtained by evaluating an already constructed series at −z.

## 4. Why the sign is actually visible in tournament curl symmetry

Let K_n be the finite set of labelled realizable tournament curls on [n], as in Session36. Its S_n orbits are cyclic SCC-type words. The stabilizer of a curl with primitive word root u of weight d and repetition r fits the inherited sequence

    internal strong-component automorphisms → Stab(curl) → C_r.

Every internal tournament automorphism has odd order, hence even vertex-permutation sign. For a generator rotating the r copies of u, choose compatible vertex identifications around the copies. It permutes r blocks of d vertices and has sign

    (−1)^(d(r−1)).                                                (15)

Any other lift differs by internal automorphisms, so the sign is the same. Equations (12) and (15) are identical.

Consequently,

    d_n = dim (Q[K_n] ⊗ sgn)^{S_n},

and a curl orbit survives the vertex-sign twist exactly when its stabilizer lies in A_n. This is an actual refinement of tournament curl symmetry, not just an abstract necklace sign convention. At the line-space level, take the determinant orientation line of every strong component; its automorphisms act trivially, and exchanging the lines gives the vertex-weight Koszul sign. Choosing a nonzero vector in each such line recovers the formal generator convention used above.

For n≥2, S_n/A_n has order two. A symmetric-group orbit splits into two alternating-group orbits iff its stabilizer has no odd permutation. Therefore

    number of A_n-orbits on K_n = c_n+d_n
      = 2c_n−1_(2|n)c_(oddpart(n)).                               (16)

The restriction n≥2 is necessary: A_1=S_1, so no index-two splitting exists there. Direct labelled curl enumeration gives respectively 1,4,5,18 alternating-group orbits at n=2,3,4,5.

Keep three parity statements distinct:

- A curl stabilizer has even ORDER exactly when r is even (Session36).
- It contains an odd VERTEX PERMUTATION exactly when r is even AND d is odd.
- The super-cocenter kills precisely the second class, not every even-order stabilizer.

For example, let a be the singleton and b the directed triangle. The word ab is primitive of even weight four. The necklace abab has an even-order block symmetry but survives the vertex-sign twist. This is the missing primitive-block parity sidecar.

## 5. Hamiltonian counts cannot be supertraces under this grading

The usual Hamiltonian-path count H is a positive multiplicative character under ordered join and factors through the ORDINARY cocenter. It does not factor through Q_s. The two-vertex witness is decisive:

    [a,a]_s=2aa,        H(2aa)=2.

A linear functional on Q_s would have to vanish on this supercommutator. Thus neither H nor the full unmodified path-cover character can be called a supertrace for this vertex grading. A signed or parity-enriched replacement would be a genuinely different observable and needs its own definition and preservation proof.

Likewise, the super-cocenter is not a supercommutative algebra quotient. The two-sided ideal generated by supercommutators gives Sym(V_even)⊗Λ(V_odd), a much coarser object that kills odd generator squares everywhere and allows all signed reorderings, not just cyclic rotation.
