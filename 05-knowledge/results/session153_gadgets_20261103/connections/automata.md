# Twin-tower recognizability: exact power sums and limits

Fix a tournament B on n vertices and an interface s(v) in {0,1}, with s(v)=1 meaning v beats each vertex of a transitive twin set U_k. Let F(k) be the number of Hamiltonian paths in the resulting tournament, k>=0. This is a fixed-template result only, not a classification of the full spectrum.

For each ordering pi=(v_1,...,v_n), define gap indicators (a_i,b_i) as follows. A zero-length internal gap is legal iff v_i -> v_(i+1); a positive gap is legal iff v_i -> U -> v_(i+1). A zero-length end gap is always legal; a positive initial gap is legal iff U -> v_1; a positive terminal gap iff v_n -> U. Thus a_i,b_i are their respective truth values. For fixed positive gap sizes, each run of U is forced into increasing order, but the assignment of labeled U vertices among the gaps contributes a multinomial coefficient. Hence the exponential generating function is

  sum_{k>=0} F(k) z^k/k! = P(e^z),
  P(t) = sum_pi product_{i=0}^n [a_i + b_i(t-1)] in Z[t].

If P(t)=sum_{j=0}^{n+1} c_j t^j, then F(k)=sum_j c_j j^k, using 0^0=1. In particular its ordinary generating function is sum_j c_j/(1-jx), with the j=0 summand interpreted as the constant c_0. The diagonal linear representation c^T diag(0,...,n+1)^k 1 is a recognizable unary weighted series; the general recognizable/rational correspondence is classical, e.g. Berstel and Reutenauer, Noncommutative Rational Series with Applications, Theorem 7.1, https://www-igm.univ-mlv.fr/~perrin/Livres/RationalSeries9April2010.pdf . This elementary representation is enough; no general automata theorem is required.

**Exact finite residue check.** For m>=2 write m=product p^e and put K=max e and L=lcm lambda(p^e), where lambda is the Carmichael exponent. For k>=K, j^k mod p^e is zero if p divides j, and is L-periodic otherwise. CRT proves F(k+L)=F(k) mod m for all k>=K. Therefore the exact set of residues attained by the template is obtained by checking the finite range 0<=k<K+L. This supplies a finite certificate of any modular obstruction for a fixed template. The exponent may be improved to the lcm of the actual unit orders of bases with nonzero coefficients.

**Density obstruction, not merely a computational one.** If the largest j>=2 with c_j != 0 exists, its coefficient is positive because F(k)>=0 for all k, and F(k) is asymptotic to c_j j^k. It follows that only O(log X) indices give F(k)<=X, except possibly finitely many initial terms. If no such j exists, F(k) is constant for k>=1. Consequently any finite union of these fixed-interface towers realizes O(log X) distinct positive integers up to X; it cannot cover all sufficiently large odd numbers. Modular surjectivity, even for many separately checked moduli, cannot overcome this fixed-template density obstruction. To prove cofinite spectrum by gadgets, one must vary the template, iterate operations with an unbounded state, or use a genuinely different family.

**Automata design target.** Recognizable series alone do not force sparsity: e.g. the nonnegative 2x2 matrix [[1,2],[0,1]] produces 2k+1 from a linear functional. Its unipotent Jordan block is the precise algebraic feature missing from the diagonal representation above. To use such a recurrence for tournaments, one must provide a tournament construction and an exact closed finite-dimensional boundary state for the HP counts, including a proof that each new gadget updates the state by that matrix (or another useful matrix). An abstract matrix realization does not establish that those states are tournament-realizable. This is a constructive proof target, not a claimed gadget.

**Check.** Take B as a directed 3-cycle and make one cycle vertex beat U while the other two lose to U. The formula gives P(t)=t+2t^2 and F(k)=1+2^(k+1). The adjacent check.py computes P from the gap formula and independently enumerates all paths for 0<=k<=5, obtaining 3,5,9,17,33,65. The small check is a sanity test, not a proof of generality.

References: Berstel–Reutenauer above, theorem on rational/recognizable series; Jason Bell, A generalised Skolem–Mahler–Lech theorem for affine varieties, https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0501309, for the much more general characteristic-zero recurrence zero-set theorem. Neither reference proves the tournament mapping here. The explicit modular proof above is stronger and effective in this special diagonal case.
