# A finite-state inverse obstruction to a one-feedback-vertex prime-lane proof

Status: proved elementary lemma; finite-exact example. No claim of the full spectrum.

Let F(0)=1 and, for b>=0, define F(2b+1)=F(b), and for b>=1 define F(2b)=F(b)+b+(b mod 2). F(a) is the Hamiltonian-path count of the tournament obtained by adjoining one vertex x to a transitive chain, with the binary representation of a describing the directions from x to the chain (1 means x points to that chain vertex). This is exactly the insertion response of the binary word from session25_spectrum_wiring_20261021/session25-spectrum/PROOF.md, §2; the p sidecar is the final bit of a.

**Lemma (exact inverse and bound).** The set of counts of strong tournaments of order at least three whose deletion of one vertex is transitive is exactly {F(b)+b+(b mod 2): b>=1}. In particular, to test whether an integer N>=3 belongs to this class it suffices to check 1<=b<=N-1. Proof: a tournament with x removed transitive is represented by a binary word. It is strong iff the first bit is 1 and the last bit 0. The first condition can be assumed by removing leading zeros, which have no effect on F; the final zero means the integer word a=2b with b>=1. Conversely the word for 2b begins in 1 and ends in 0, so the resulting tournament is strong. Since F(b)>=1, any representation N=F(b)+b+(b mod 2) has b<=N-1. Exact recurrence follows from the established word update (H,A,p) on appending a 0 or 1. The one-vertex tournament and arbitrary nonstrong transitive paddings add count 1 but no new count to this class.

Taking b=1,...,12 yields counts respectively 3,5,5,9,9,11,9,17,15,19,15,23. Thus 13 is not in this class, an obstruction already proved in session 149; the inverse recurrence restates and sharpens the algorithmic form of that result. This is an all-order obstruction, not merely a finite length search. It also excludes 7 and 21, consistent with the global theorem. However 13 is realized in the full tournament spectrum by inherited finite-prefix results, so adding arbitrary length within the one-feedback class cannot by itself solve even the ordinary prime lane.

Precise alternate target: find a finite collection of explicitly specified *interface classes*, each with a provable exact update and finite witnesses for every initial state, and prove a descending cover of both THM-4094 prime lanes: for every sufficiently large target in {p : p odd prime, p!=7} union {7p : p odd prime, p!=3}, one transition gives a smaller target and a realizable parent interface; all terminal targets must have certified witnesses. It is essential that descent preserve an interface, not merely the scalar H, and that the reduction applies to every lane target, not just residues or positive density. This statement is noncircular provided transitions and tests are explicit and do not invoke the desired spectrum theorem. No such descent is established here.

Known guardrails: the order-join product gives the two-prime-lane reduction (THM-4094), not a construction of either lane; modular surjectivity of F or a family says nothing about exact coverage, as shown by the sparse modular-universal block family in the cited session25 proof. A random construction provides a proof only after a rigorous positive lower bound on exact-hit probability for every target; empirical frequency or expected total hits is not sufficient. An interval-tiling induction requires explicit parent-interface compatibility and overlap inequalities, not only scalar intervals at successive orders.
