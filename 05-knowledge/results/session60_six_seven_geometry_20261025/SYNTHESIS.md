# Session60: why six and seven recur—and when they do not mean the same thing

## 1. The adjunction balance, not a numerical coincidence

For the smooth simultaneous r-th-root cover of n hyperplanes in general position in P^d, the complete-intersection canonical coefficient is (r−1)n−r(d+1). In the square-root surface case it is n−6. Thus six is the zero-canonical threshold and seven is the next canonical embedding, not a free-standing numerological constant. The smooth six-root K3 polarization has degree8 and genus5; its root count is not its polarized genus.

For surfaces, uniform-root zero-canonical cases are (r,n)=(2,6),(4,4). Canonical coefficient1 occurs at (2,7),(3,5),(5,4), giving canonical geometric genera7,5,4 and canonical self-intersections16,9,5. These are classical generalized-Fermat comparison families with proved scope, not claimed new classifications.

Multiple concurrence is essential. Four or more concurrent square-root lines can destroy the K3/general-type conclusion. Nevertheless the non-torification conclusion survives for every distinct spanning complex line arrangement with at least six roots: without higher concurrence the canonical-form argument applies; with higher concurrence, root-coordinate projection maps onto a positive-genus curve, obstructing rationality. A positive dualizing class on a singular model alone does not prove its resolution is general type.

## 2. Seven canonical forms come from seven K3 quotients

The projective sign group of an n-root cover is (C2)^(n−1), and its characters are even subsets of the branch labels. A size-six character contributes one holomorphic two-form on the surface. Consequently six roots give one such character, while seven roots give seven, indexed by the omitted root.

Each coordinate-flip quotient of the hourglass has the corresponding six-root K3 resolution. Its invariant two-form is exactly the associated character sector. On the resolved level, exceptional curves can be contracted; do not describe every comparison as a finite morphism between minimal resolutions.

A single simultaneous pair flip is not the subgroup generated by two independent flips: the former quotient has invariant geometric genus5, whereas the latter leads to a five-root quotient with geometric genus0. This is a concrete hostile control against forgetting the subgroup.

The rational transcendental Hodge structure of the hourglass splits through its seven K3 quotients. In particular rho(Y7)=Σ rho(S_j)−76, with the precisely specified resolutions and rational correspondences retained.

## 3. The six outer lines form a genuine hexagon

The distinguished central line is intrinsically recognized by its three triple crossings. The six remaining dual line points form an A2 root hexagon; the full arrangement symmetry group is the order-twelve dihedral group. Literal coordinate permutations lift a subgroup (C2)^6⋊D12, not asserted to be the full automorphism group.

The seven six-root deletions form a1+6 orbit decomposition. Deleting the central line gives a smooth six-root model; deleting any outer line gives eight nodes, with all six outer models isomorphic under these realized symmetries. The canonical representation has a determinant twist, not merely the seven-point permutation action.

## 4. A genus-two and CM connection, with its arithmetic twist retained

The six outer branch lines are tangent to a conic. Their branch parameters are {∞,−1,0,1/2,1,2}; over C the associated genus-two curve is y²=u⁶−1. Its two independent elliptic quotients have j=0, so its Jacobian is isogenous to a square of the j=0 elliptic curve.

The double-plane quotient is therefore a Kummer surface of Picard rank20. An explicit Gale transform separately identifies the original central six-root K3 with the classical diagonal Kummer model; this is not inferred merely from having a finite map to a Kummer surface. The rational Shioda equation contains a factor27, so the complex identification must not be silently promoted to an untwisted Q-isomorphism.
