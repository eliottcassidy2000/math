# What eleven tournament vertices and the first 25 square-sum labels encode

Write \(H(T)\) for the number of directed Hamilton paths, with the vertices of a path read in order. The odd-precedence tournament directs \(u\to v\) when an odd number of those words put \(u\) before \(v\). The sharp order eleven applies to the family with **transitive odd precedence**. The earlier nine-vertex phenomenon concerns [switching all arcs across a cut in one switching class, THM-5313](../../../01-canon/theorems/THM-5313-switching-derivatives-of-hamiltonian-paths-and-cycles.md). ## One exact rule for all orders

**PROVED.** Twelve fixed marked eleven-vertex tournaments suffice. At any prescribed order \(n\ge11\) replace the same marked vertex of each by an ordered chain of length \(n-10\). The resulting twelve strongly connected rigid tournaments, all with transitive precedence, realize the twelve odd residues modulo 24 exactly once. Both the initial order and the bank size are smallest possible for this rule. The choice for a given residue depends only on whether the chain has length one, even length, or odd length at least three. On the twelve residue names the even and later odd phases generate a dihedral group of order 24, with orbits of sizes 4, 6 and 2. The six inert residues form one orbit. The proof counts the ways that a Hamilton word leaves the marked vertex and returns. [Twelve templates and exact formulas](EXTENSIONS.md)

The first step admits its own sharp inverse. Exactly three strong ten-vertex parents are needed to obtain all twelve residues by adjoining a vertex while preserving strong connectivity and transitive precedence. Every one of the 280 optimal triples uses the two converse-pair nodes of heights 1243 and 1563. Looking back one more size, every minimum bank of nine-vertex starts includes strong parents of heights 591 and 751. Starting as far back as eight and insisting on those properties at every intervening order leaves just seven residues at eleven. [Parent and ancestry classification](STRONG11.md)

The [six rigid cores of heights 5, 17, 37, 77, 129, 751](../chanpak_tiling_viewpoint_20261123/PRECEDENCE-FIBER.md) already select every odd residue at simultaneous powers of 2, 3 and 11 by ordered joins. Our fixed six-vertex outer tournament gives a strong rigid version using the exact formula \(5H(B)+6Q_2(B)\), with \(Q_2\) counting ordered pairs of paths covering the filler. For each prescribed residue at sufficiently large order, exponentially many distinct ordinary isomorphism classes even have the **same integer height**. The generating function for the words used in that bound is \(1/(1-z-z^4-2z^6-z^9)\). [Substitution law, finite order bound and multiplicity proof](STRONG-SELECTOR.md)

## The square-sum graph at 25 gives a construction at arbitrarily large scales

The earlier [microcosm at 32](../microcosm_squaresum32_metagraph_selector_20261116/MICROCOSM.md) highlighted the need to preserve which edges of a spanning path remain available. On the first 25 vertices of the square-sum graph, take for each \(1\le v\le25\) the two heights \(4^k v-2\pm1\). From \(k=2\) onward all fifty have exact strong witnesses; connecting two whenever their shifted labels sum to a square gives precisely two Hamilton cycles. At the first scale deleting heights 7 and 21 leaves three vertices of degree one and no spanning path. Identified edges in the two cycles permit arbitrarily many copies to join into one cycle on \(50r\) distinct, actually attained heights. A second pair of shifts makes a 100-height graph covering all twelve odd residues with exactly two components. [Constructions, exact graph counts and physical flips](SQUARE25.md)

For the perfect-number target, take any strong template on at most ten vertices and replace two vertices by ordered chains with independently chosen positive widths. Suppose every Hamilton path visits each of the two chains in at most two separate runs at all widths. Among **all** such templates and widths, \(2^{p-1}(2^p-1)-1\) is attained exactly for integer exponents \(2\le p\le12\), and the upper neighbor exactly for \(3\le p\le13\). The proof shows why unrestricted width choices eventually reduce to finitely many fixed exponent shifts. Thus at exponent 61 neither endpoint lies in this entire class. [Two-port rigidity, census and exact boundary](TWO-PORT-RIGIDITY.md)
