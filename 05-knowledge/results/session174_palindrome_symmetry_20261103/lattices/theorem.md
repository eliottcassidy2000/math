# Balanced tournament-score shells: finite reflection closure exactly in dimensions 2,4,8

Status: PROVED, with exact finite verification. Independently derived extension of THM-868-e8-bridge-score-lattice.md; no historical novelty claim. Reconstructed after ephemeral-workspace reset.

For even n>=2 define H_n={h in {−1/2,1/2}^n:sum(h)=0}, and let W_n be generated by s_h(x)=x−2<x,h>h/<h,h>.

**Theorem.** H_n is precisely the set of centered near-regular labelled tournament scores. W_n is finite iff n is 2,4,8. The closures under reflections in those cases are A1, three orthogonal root pairs A1^3, and the 126 E7 roots, respectively. For n=8 the 70 original vectors acquire exactly the 56 vectors e_i−e_j, all after one reflection step. Their integral span L has Gram determinant 2. In every other even dimension the reflection closure is infinite.

**Proof.** Delete one vertex of the cyclic regular tournament on n+1 vertices. Half the remaining vertices have score n/2 and half score n/2−1. Relabelling realizes every balanced pattern; conversely total score sum forces equal multiplicities. Centering scores by (n−1)/2 gives H_n.

The squared norm is n/4. Adjacent positive supports differ by one exchange, so their inner product is n/4−1 and angle theta satisfies cos(theta)=1−4/n. If the product of their reflections had finite order, exp(2i theta) and hence exp(i theta) would be roots of unity. Therefore 2cos(theta)=2−8/n would be a rational algebraic integer, hence an integer. For even n>=6 this lies in [2/3,2), whose only integer is1, forcing n=8. Thus all other such dimensions already have an infinite subgroup from this pair. At n=2 the shell is one antipodal pair; at n=4 it is three mutually orthogonal antipodal pairs, so both groups are finite.

At n=8 set A7={z in Z^8:sum(z)=0}. All differences of shell vectors lie in A7. For any i!=j choose a common three-element positive support disjoint from i,j; the two resulting shell vectors differ by e_i−e_j. Hence ZH_8=A7+Zh for any fixed h in H_8. Because 2h is in A7 but h is not, the index over A7 is2, so the determinant is det(A7)/4=8/4=2.

The norm-2 vectors in L are precisely the 56 integer roots e_i−e_j and 70 balanced half-integer vectors: the integer sum of squares2 forces one+1 and one−1, while each of eight half-integer squares is at least1/4. L is integral because <z,h> is integral for z in A7 and <h,h>=2. Consequently reflection in any norm-2 vector preserves L and its norm-2 set R, proving finiteness. Adjacent shell vectors have inner product1, so s_h(k)=k−h=e_i−e_j, giving the entire 126-root closure in one step.

An explicit E7 basis is a_i=e_i−e_(i+1), i=1,...,6, together with h=(-1,-1,-1,1,1,1,1,-1)/2. Its Gram matrix has diagonal2, chain edges a1−a2−a3−a4−a5−a6, and branch h−a3, all with inner product−1, and other off-diagonal entries0. Its determinant is2. This identifies R as E7, also exactly the sum-zero root slice inside E8's240 roots. The original70 omit170 E8 roots; the reflected126 omit114.

Finally H_n spans the sum-zero hyperplane because its differences span all coordinate differences. A finite reflection closure would give a faithful action of W_n on a finite spanning set, while W_n fixes the orthogonal complement pointwise. This would make W_n finite. Thus the excluded dimensions have infinite closure. QED.
