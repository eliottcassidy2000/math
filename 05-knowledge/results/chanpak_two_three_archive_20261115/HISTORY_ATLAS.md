# The 2–3 archive: follow the mechanisms, not the numeral matches

This is a curated citation walk, not a count of every 2 or 3 in the
repository. The research notes distinguish a theorem, a model with
additional assumptions, an analogy, and an open transfer. Consult the
linked inventories for their full lists, rather than interpreting their
overlapping entries as independent open problems.

## The working classification

- **Alphabet or group action:** what may be chosen, conjugated, or
  colored locally. Examples: Rule 30's free input, a ternary branch,
  the three nonzero points of F₂², fixed points in Burnside.
- **Growth and normalization:** how number scale changes when the
  local decision is repeated. Examples: multiplicative birth,
  slot-count padding, growth with no loss of bits, balanced carries.
- **Actual return:** exact equality, admissible endpoints, and a
  marked state that survives the move. Examples: one arithmetic
  unit on a fixed integer fiber; not a residue hit, first
  moment, return after forgetting the height, or alternate
  parallel branch with no route between them.

A suggested analogy is useful only if it says which of these
coordinates it transports and what can get lost. This is why the
problem list is relevant to proving Chan–Pak, not just to filing
coincidences.
