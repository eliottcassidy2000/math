# THM-3652 -- Wright's elementary-Jacobian criterion

**CITED + INDEPENDENTLY REPROVED + INDEPENDENTLY HOSTILE-AUDITED.**

Let `k` be a characteristic-zero field and put

```text
E_+(f)=[1 f;0 1],             E_-(f)=[1 0;f 1].        (1)
```

Write `E_2(k[x,y])` for the subgroup generated by `(1)` and
`GE_2(k[x,y])` for the group generated by elementary and invertible diagonal
matrices.

## 1. Statement and attribution

Let `F=(P,Q) in k[x,y]^2` satisfy

```text
Jac(F)=lambda in k*.                                   (2)
```

Then

```text
DF in GE_2(k[x,y])    iff    F is a polynomial automorphism.  (3)
```

The implication from left to right is Wright's weak Jacobian theorem.  It is
stated over `C` as Theorem 4.1 of Druzkowski's survey and over an arbitrary
characteristic-zero field in Drensky--Yu, both citing Wright's 1978 paper.
The proof below is an independent reduced-word reconstruction of the forward
direction, included to pin every constant and chain-rule boundary used by the
current search.  The converse uses the cited Jung--van der Kulk tameness
theorem and the chain rule.

## 2. Determinant normalization

All units of `k[x,y]` are constants.  Diagonal matrices normalize the
elementary subgroup, and every determinant-one constant diagonal matrix lies
in `E_2(k)`.  Consequently

```text
ker(det:GE_2(k[x,y])->k*)=E_2(k[x,y]).                 (4)
```

Postcompose `F` by `diag(lambda^-1,1)`.  This is an affine target change, and
its new Jacobian matrix has determinant one.  Equations `(2)--(4)` therefore
reduce the forward implication to

```text
DF in E_2(k[x,y]),             det DF=1.               (5)
```

## 3. The nonconstant reduced word

Merge adjacent elementary factors of the same sign.  First suppose the
resulting alternating word has only nonconstant parameters and ends on the
right in `E_+(f_1)`.  Its rows are consecutive vectors in the recurrence

```text
v_0=(0,1),       v_1=(1,f_1),
v_j=v_(j-2)+f_j v_(j-1).                               (6)
```

With lexicographic monomial order `x>y`, nonconstancy makes the product term
in `(6)` strictly dominate the skipped term.  Hence, for `j>=2`,

```text
LM(v_j,1)=product_(i=2)^j LM(f_i),
LM(v_j,2)=product_(i=1)^j LM(f_i).                     (7)
```

The same ratio is immediate for `v_1`.  If `(f_1)_x!=0`, write

```text
LM(P)=x^a y^b,       LM(f_1)=x^e y^r,       e>=1       (8)
```

for any nonconstant row `(P,Q)` in `(6)`.  Equation `(7)` says
`LM(Q)=LM(P)LM(f_1)`.  The leading monomial of `Q_x` strictly exceeds every
term of `P_y`: if `e>1` its `x` exponent is larger; if `e=1`, the `x`
exponents tie and its `y` exponent is larger.  If `b=0`, the leading term of
`P` disappears under `partial_y`, which only strengthens the comparison.
Thus the row cannot be closed.  Since a Jacobian row is a closed polynomial
one-form, one must have

```text
f_1 in k[y].                                            (9)
```

Choose `A'(y)=f_1` and set `T(x,y)=(x+A(y),y)`.  If
`DF=N E_+(f_1)`, the correctly typed chain rule is

```text
D(F o T^-1)(z)=N(T^-1 z).                              (10)
```

This is an integrable elementary word one factor shorter; polynomial
substitution preserves nonconstancy.  If the rightmost factor is lower, use
the simultaneous exchange of source and target coordinates and lexicographic
order `y>x`.

## 4. Constants shorten by Bruhat reduction

Zero parameters delete, equal adjacent signs merge, and endpoint constants
are affine source or target factors.  For an internal `c in k*`, direct
multiplication gives

```text
E_+(a)E_-(c)E_+(b)
 =E_+(a+c^-1) w_c E_+(b+c^-1),
w_c=[0 -c^-1;c 0].                                    (11)
```

Moreover

```text
w_c E_+(h) w_c^-1=E_-(-c^2h),
w_c E_-(h) w_c^-1=E_+(-h/c^2).                        (12)
```

Push `w_c` to the source end, absorb it by a linear precomposition, and
obtain one fewer elementary factor after a polynomial parameter substitution.
Induction on word length ends at a constant Jacobian matrix.  Every step was
affine or triangular, so the normalized map, and hence `F`, is tame.

## 5. Converse and failure boundary

Every planar polynomial automorphism over `k` is tame by Jung--van der Kulk.
The derivative of an affine automorphism is a constant `GE_2` matrix, while a
triangular shear has elementary derivative.  The chain rule substitutes
polynomial coordinates into later factors and therefore keeps the derivative
in `GE_2`.  This proves the converse in `(3)`.

Characteristic zero is load-bearing.  In characteristic `p`,

```text
F=(x-x^p,y)
```

has `DF=I` but is not a polynomial automorphism.  The two-variable matrix
size is also load-bearing: Cohn's determinant-one matrix

```text
[1+xy x^2;-y^2 1-xy]
```

does not lie in `E_2(k[x,y])`.  It is not itself a Jacobian matrix because
its row curls are `(-x,-y)`.  Thus `(3)` does not prove `JC(2)`; it proves
that every normalized planar counterexample must carry a nontrivial
`SL_2/E_2` class.  **QED.**
