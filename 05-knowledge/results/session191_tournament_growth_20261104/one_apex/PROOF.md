# Exact order windows and sharp boundary gaps for one-feedback-vertex tournaments

**PROVED, with finite exact controls.** Session 191, 2026-11-04. No literature-priority claim. This packet determines the first two and last two Hamiltonian-path counts at every sufficiently large order among strong tournaments whose deletion of one vertex is transitive. It also gives a strict local rearrangement law, a sharp lower bound on its increments, a complete arithmetic classification of every single-run affine family, and an all-order exclusion of 13 from this construction class. It does not prove the global `{7,21}` spectrum.

## 1. Inheritance and target

Write H(T) for the number of directed Hamiltonian vertex words, with H(empty)=1. The inherited insertion-word recurrence in `session25-spectrum/PROOF.md` constructs a family with every parity-admissible residue modulo every integer. Congruence completeness is not exact integer coverage. The S190 four-arc patch preserves one endpoint-cover response and produces infinitely many +2 sibling pairs, but does not permit arbitrary-target or repeated +2 closure.

Here the underused coordinate is the ordered placement of the apex incidences at fixed weight. The canonical hostile is the actual value 13: it is attained by a five-vertex tournament but, as proved below, by no tournament with feedback vertex number at most one. The near miss corrected here is promoting modular coverage, or a monotone rearrangement of incidences, to an interval-filling mechanism.

Live concepts: ordered apex incidences; binary affine state; majorization by adjacent swaps; order-dependent growth; exact versus congruence coverage. The all-order extrema turn the last distinction into explicit growing forbidden bands at every fixed order in this class.

Let w=b_1...b_n be a binary word. Define T(w) on x,1,...,n by i->j for i<j, and x->i iff b_i=1. Thus T(w)-x is transitive. Every tournament with a specified transitive one-vertex deletion has this form, since a transitive tournament has a unique linear order. A nontrivial T(w) is strong iff b_1=1 and b_n=0. Necessity follows because otherwise vertex 1 is a source or vertex n is a sink. For sufficiency, x reaches every chain vertex through vertex 1, and every chain vertex reaches x through vertex n.

Throughout, n is the **chain length**, so the tournament order is N=n+1. This distinction matters in every exponent below.

## 2. Counting and the exact local rearrangement

For any prefix u, use the state (h,A,p), where h=H(T(u)), A is the binary integer represented by u, and p its last bit; the empty word has (1,0,0). The inherited recurrence, also justified directly here, is

    append 1: (h,A,p) -> (h,2A+1,1),
    append 0: (h,A,p) -> (h+A+p,2A,0).                   (1)

One direct count is

    H(T(w)) = b_1+(1-b_n)+#{i:b_i b_(i+1)=01}
              + sum_(j<i) b_j(1-b_i) 2^(i-j-1)         (2)

for a nonempty word. Paths starting or ending at x give the first two terms. For an internal x, let i be its predecessor and j its successor in the chain. When i<j, the chain is covered only if j=i+1, giving an adjacent 01. When j<i, each intervening chain vertex independently belongs before or after x, giving 2^(i-j-1). This partitions all Hamilton words and proves (2). Comparing prefixes in (2) proves (1), including the p term.

For a suffix v=c_1...c_q, put

    C(v)=sum_(j:c_j=0) 2^(j-1),
    epsilon(v)=[q>=1 and c_1=0].

The dependence of the eventual count on an initial state (h,A,p) is h+A C(v)+p epsilon(v), plus a term independent of that initial state. Apply this to the two middle words 01 and 10. If u has state (h,A,p), then

    H(T(u10v))-H(T(u01v))
      = A+2-p+C(v)-epsilon(v)
      = 2[1+floor(A/2)+sum_(j>=2:c_j=0) 2^(j-2)].       (3)

In particular, this difference is always a strictly positive even integer. This is an exact physical two-arc edit: only x's incidences with the two adjacent chain names change. It is not a scalar-H update law, because the prefix and suffix coordinates occur explicitly.

Consequently H is strictly increasing on the fixed-weight binary-word order generated by 01->10. With strong endpoints held fixed, the unique minimum and maximum at each fixed pair of positive weights (o,z), o+z=n, are

    minimum word: 1 0^(z-1) 1^(o-1) 0,
    maximum word: 1^o 0^z.                              (4)

The words are unique in this specified apex/chain presentation, not claimed unique as unrooted tournament classes.

## 3. Exact fixed-weight extrema

When o,z>=2, evaluating (1) on the words in (4) gives

    L(o,z)=(2^(o-1)+1)(2^(z-1)+1),
    U(o,z)=(2^o-1)(2^z-1)+2.                            (5)

When min(o,z)=1, there is only one strong word of that weight and its count is

    L(o,z)=U(o,z)=2^(n-1)+1.                            (6)

The exception in (6) must not be replaced by the first formula in (5): the two forced endpoint bits would overlap one of its runs. The minimum construction in (5) is the familiar touching-interval/Moon chain product, already recorded in `THM-4748-two-reversed-arcs-exact-path-counts-the-seven-mod-eight-lines-and-the-mersenne-side-three-arcs-from-transitivity.md`; no novelty is claimed for that product construction. The addition here is its extremality within **all** fixed-weight one-apex words, and the sharp neighboring gaps.

Since the product term 2^(n-2) in L and 2^n in U are fixed, balancing o,z minimizes L and maximizes U. For n>=4 the global minimum is

    L_n=(2^floor((n-2)/2)+1)(2^ceil((n-2)/2)+1),         (7)

while for every n>=2 the global maximum is

    U_n=(2^floor(n/2)-1)(2^ceil(n/2)-1)+2.              (8)

At n=2,3, the sole counts are 3,5. At n=4 the minimum 9 also occurs at the two extreme weights, besides the balanced minimum word. At n>=5 only balanced weights attain the minimum; only balanced weights attain the maximum for every n>=2.

The lower and upper endpoints are asymptotic to 2^(n-2) and 2^n respectively. These are class-specific bounds, not universal strong-tournament bounds.

## 4. The sharp size-dependent cost of an internal adjacent swap

Assume n>=4, u starts with 1, v ends with 0, and p_len=|u|>=1, q_len=|v|>=1, p_len+q_len=n-2. To avoid confusion with the state bit p, denote these lengths a,b. Formula (3) gives the sharp lower bound

    delta >= 2+g(a)+g(b),
    g(1)=0,   g(t)=2^(t-1) for t>=2.                    (9)

For a>=2, the smallest binary prefix beginning with 1 is 10^(a-1), and floor(A/2)=2^(a-2). For b>=2 the last zero contributes 2^(b-2) inside (3), with every other suffix bit after the first chosen as 1. The first suffix bit does not affect the difference. Thus equality is attained, for example, by u=10^(a-1), v=1^(b-1)0.

Minimizing over a+b=n-2 gives the exact minimum increment J_n among all such strong internal swaps:

    J_4=2, J_5=4,
    J_n=2+2^(floor((n-2)/2)-1)+2^(ceil((n-2)/2)-1)
        for n>=6.                                     (10)

For a,b>=2 the ordinary convexity/balancing of powers of two proves the minimum. If one length is 1, its candidate is 2+2^(n-4), which ties at n=6 and is larger for n>=7. The endpoint cases n=4,5 give the stated exceptions.

Therefore the only chain length supporting a +2 internal adjacent 01->10 edit is n=4. At n>=5 no path consisting solely of forward internal swaps can have total increment +2. More strongly, every nonempty such path costs at least J_n. This does not forbid cancellations along mixed-direction paths, changing weights, changing chain arcs, or changing the chosen apex.

## 5. First two minima and the growing lower missing band

Let L_n^(2) be the next **distinct** strong one-apex value after L_n. For n>=6,

    n=2m:   L_n^(2)=L_n+2^(m-2),
    n=2m+1: L_n^(2)=L_n+3*2^(m-2).                     (11)

For n=4,5 the respective first two values are (9,11) and (15,17).

Proof. Among the fixed-weight minima, the next weight beyond balance is attained by

    (o,z)=(m-1,m+1)       if n=2m,
    (o,z)=(m-1,m+2)       if n=2m+1,

and their converses. Formula (5) gives the exact differences in (11). More unbalanced weights increase 2^(o-1)+2^(z-1). The exceptional extreme weights have still larger count for n>=6. Every non-minimum word at any fixed weight is obtained from its weight-minimum word by a nonempty sequence of forward internal swaps. Its excess is at least J_n, and (10) gives

    J_(2m)=2+2^(m-1) > 2^(m-2),
    J_(2m+1)=2+3*2^(m-2) > 3*2^(m-2).

Hence a non-minimum word cannot enter the band before the next-weight minimum. This proves (11), with literal witnesses supplied by (4).

Thus each odd integer strictly between the endpoints in (11) is absent at that order in this class. The lower missing band has width proportional to the square root of L_n, although the class collectively realizes every parity-admissible fixed residue by S25.

## 6. Last two maxima and the upper missing band

Let U_n^(2) be the next **distinct** value below U_n. For n>=4,

    n=2m:   U_n^(2)=U_n-2^(m-1),
    n=2m+1: U_n^(2)=U_n-[3*2^(m-1)-2].                 (12)

For fixed weights o,z>=2, the sorted maximum word has only one possible first internal descent 10->01, namely its block boundary. Formula (3), with u=1^(o-1) and v=0^(z-1), gives its cost

    D(o,z)=2^(o-1)+2^(z-1)-2.                          (13)

Every other word at those weights is obtained by a sequence of downward swaps from the maximum, so the unique immediate boundary-swap word is the second largest at that fixed weight. No assertion of uniqueness across rooted presentations is needed.

At n=2m the next unbalanced maximum drops by 2^(m-1), whereas the balanced first boundary swap drops by 2^m-2. The former is smaller (equal at n=4), proving the first formula. At n=2m+1 the next unbalanced maximum drops by 3*2^(m-1), whereas the balanced boundary swap drops by 3*2^(m-1)-2. The latter wins. Every word at a nonbalanced weight is bounded by that weight's smaller maximum. This proves the second formula, including n=5. The n=4 boundary is checked directly.

The lower boundary is governed by changing the weight, whereas at odd chain lengths the upper boundary is governed by a local internal swap. This is a structural difference, not just four fitted exponential sequences.

## 6.1 A single expanding run cannot generate the Mersenne side

Fix finite words u,v and a bit b, and require T(u b^k v) to be strong for every k>=1. There are explicit constants a>0,c with

    H(T(u b^k v))=a*2^k+c.                            (14)

Write (h,A,p) for the state of u, and use C(v),epsilon(v) from section 2. Direct iteration of (1) gives

    b=0: a=A(1+C(v)),
         c=h-A+p+H(T(v))-1;
    b=1: a=(A+1)C(v),
         c=h+H(T(v))-1-C(v)+epsilon(v).                (15)

Both slopes are positive under strongness: for b=0, u is nonempty and starts with 1; for b=1, v is nonempty and ends with 0.

**Dyadic-slope rigidity.** If a is a power of two, then c=1, and each resulting tournament is a nearly transitive tournament with just one minority apex incidence. Consequently no such single expanding run produces 2^(k+d)-1, or 2^(k+d)+c for fixed c!=1, uniformly in k.

For b=0, the factors A and 1+C(v) must both be powers of two. Since u starts with 1, its binary integer A being a power of two forces u=10^r. Since v is empty or ends with 0, the binary support equation C(v)=2^s-1 forces v=0^s. Hence the whole word is 1 followed by zeros and c=1. For b=1, A+1 being a power of two forces u to consist entirely of ones, possibly empty. The equation C(v)=2^s forces v=1^s0, since v ends in 0. Thus the whole word consists of ones followed by one zero, and again c=1.

The known three-back-arc Fibonacci fan of THM-4748 does realize every 2^k-1 for k>=4, within this same one-feedback-vertex class. It therefore necessarily uses more than one changing run or a changing placement; the rigid single-clock subclass is the obstruction, not feedback vertex number one itself. This is a direct structural reason why one may not turn a one-clock exponential formula into an arbitrary binary-digit construction.

## 6.2 Complete arithmetic classification of single-run affine families

There is a stronger finite classification, not just the dyadic-slope special case. For a nonnegative integer B, let v(B) be the binary word whose j-th bit is zero exactly when the (j-1)-st binary bit of B is one, with length equal to the bit length of B. Put v(0)=empty. Thus v(B) ends in zero for B>0. For an odd positive integer m and every positive divisor d of m, define

    gamma_m(d) = H(T(bits(d))) - d + H(T(v(m/d-1))).     (16)

Here bits(d) is the usual binary representation, starting and ending in 1.

**Single-run classification theorem.** Let a be any positive integer, and let m be its odd part. The set of constants c for which a strong family T(u b^k v), with fixed u,v,b, has

    H(T(u b^k v))=a*2^k+c for every k>=1

is exactly

    {gamma_m(d): d divides m}.                         (17)

Repeated constants are deduplicated; this is a classification of affine count functions, not rooted-isomorphism classes.

Proof for the expanding zero run. Strongness forces u to start in 1 and v to end in 0 or be empty. Formula (15) gives a=A(C(v)+1). Write

    A=2^r d,       C(v)+1=2^s e,
    d,e odd,       de=m,       r+s=nu_2(a).

The word u is bits(d) followed by r zeros. Appending a zero leaves the quantity H(T(u))-A+p unchanged, by (1). Since the final bit of bits(d) is 1, this prefix contribution is H(T(bits(d)))-d+1.

The equation C(v)+1=2^s e forces v to consist of s leading zeros followed by v(e-1): a leading zero changes C to 2C+1. Leading zeros are source padding, so they do not change H. Substituting in (15) gives exactly gamma_m(d).

Conversely, choose any factorization m=de and any r,s>=0 with r+s=nu_2(a). Set u=bits(d)0^r and v=0^s v(e-1). The family u0^k v is strong for every k>=1, including e=1 and v empty, and equations (15)-(16) give its claimed slope and constant. This proves both directions for zero runs.

For one runs, take reverse complement of the entire binary word. This replaces a one run by a zero run and gives the converse tournament, which has the same Hamiltonian-path count by reversal of every path. Therefore no additional constants occur. QED.

The constant set depends only on the odd part of the slope, so shifting the integer clock k by any fixed integer (where the resulting run length is positive) does not change the available constants. In particular (17) also decides whether such an affine family can be realized after a fixed clock shift.

This directly tests the two exact ternary-parent rays identified in S190 section 5.2:

    slope 1273 = 19*67: constants {-789,-35,37,1119};
    slope 5303 (prime): constants {-4909,3045}.           (18)

Neither 191 nor 201 is present. Hence neither 191+1273*2^k nor 201+5303*2^k is realized uniformly by a fixed one-apex word with one expanding constant run, even with a fixed clock shift or with either run bit. The result allows arbitrarily long fixed prefix and suffix words: their integer encoding and the factorization in the proof make the test finite. The inherited S190 exclusion concerns a different architecture (a fixed rooted core under its wrapper); the present test rules out this additional candidate replacement architecture, not every clock-dependent parent.

The verifier explicitly constructs every divisor witness for odd slopes through 255 and binary scale through 2^4, tests both run-bit formulas on all prefix/suffix words of lengths at most four, and records the exact target certificates (18). These are controls of an all-order theorem, not a finite-search extrapolation.

## 7. All-order class exclusions and a concrete global-spectrum hostile

For an arbitrary word w, remove its initial zeros and final ones. Those vertices are respectively source and sink chains, and do not change H. If the remaining word is nonempty, it begins with 1 and ends with 0, and is a strong core. Otherwise T(w) is transitive and H=1.

Consequently the **entire** feedback-vertex-number-at-most-one spectrum is {1} together with the union of the strong one-apex spectra at chain lengths n>=2. This is an all-order reduction with no unbounded padding loophole.

In particular, 13 is impossible in this class. At chain lengths <=4 the possible strong counts are {3,5,9,11}; at n>=5, (7) gives H>=15. Nevertheless the actual tournament with outgoing bit rows

    (18,8,3,5,14)

has H=13, as checked independently by permutations and subset DP. Deleting names 0,1 leaves the transitive triple with rows inherited on {2,3,4}, so its feedback vertex number is exactly two: at most two by the exhibited deletion, and not at most one by the all-order exclusion above. [The verifier checks the deletion rather than relying on the listed names if they are changed.]

The same exact order cutoff computes every class membership through any finite X: enumerate strong words until L_n>X, where L_n is strictly increasing. The generated receipt gives the complete one-apex spectrum through 500 and all its missing odd values. These are absences from this class only. In particular, such an exclusion never proves a new global forbidden H.
