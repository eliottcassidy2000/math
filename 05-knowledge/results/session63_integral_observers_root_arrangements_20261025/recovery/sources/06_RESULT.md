# Graph tori, shared-component gluing, and a flat multiplicative-to-additive magic family

**Status:** PROVED elementary functorial constructions, with classical ingredients explicitly attributed below. FINITE-EXACT tests are separate. This is not a claim that the square-root variety descends to F1, not a q→1 degeneration of fields, and not a solution of the integer magic-square-of-squares problem. No external novelty claim.

## 0. Inheritance, concept board, and the actual gain

The closest proved mechanisms are Session57's integral additive graph-potential inverse in `two-path-frame/THEOREM.md`, its AP-K3,3 carrier, and Session58's spanning-tree hourglass overlap and root fiber product. Their numerical AP and square predicates remain indispensable. The canonical hostile is two locally consistent path labelings that agree on shared edges but have inconsistent potential gauges on different overlap components. The least-used sidecar is the integral incidence lattice: it contains more structure than the bare path counts.

The live board is: (1) graph incidence and spanning-tree coordinates; (2) number of overlap components; (3) monomial tori over F1; (4) additive magic equations; (5) ordinary square roots. The positive connection is an explicit torus whose integral tangent space is the additive carrier, strengthened to a smooth flat family preserving graph gluing and magic completion. Square-root covers can be pulled through the family without pretending their arithmetic becomes monomial.

## 1. A graph-potential functor over every abelian group

Let G=(V,E) be a finite oriented graph; loops and parallel edges are allowed. Let A be an abelian group, written additively. Define

    C_G(A) = { (p_head(e)−p_tail(e))_e : p∈A^V } ⊂ A^E.

Isolated vertices count among the c(G) connected components. A loop has label zero. Fix a spanning forest T, and choose one root per component.

**Theorem 1 (integral forest coordinates).** Restriction to T gives a natural group isomorphism

    C_G(A) → A^T ≅ A^(|V|−c(G)).

Its inverse propagates potentials from value zero at each chosen root, adding or subtracting the edge label according to its orientation. An arbitrary edge vector belongs to C_G(A) iff its signed sum around every fundamental cycle of T is zero. These conditions hold over EVERY abelian group, not only vector spaces or groups without torsion.

**Proof.** Every tree edge determines the next potential uniquely. For a nontree edge e, the recovered endpoint difference is the signed sum along the unique tree path. Its agreement with the supplied e-label is precisely the fundamental-cycle equation. Necessity follows by telescoping. The potential kernel consists exactly of independently constant values on the connected components. All operations use only sums and inverses, so all maps are integral and natural. ∎

For a bipartite graph with shores L,R, orient each edge from R to L and put p_i=a_i on L and p_j=−b_j on R. Its label becomes a_i+b_j. This is exactly the square-sum potential carrier used in Sessions57–58; no arithmetic condition on a_i+b_j is supplied by Theorem1.

For A=R^×, using multiplicative notation, the same functor is the embedded graph torus

    T_G(R) = { (s_head(e)/s_tail(e))_e } ⊂ (R^×)^E.

It is a split torus of rank |V|−c(G). The bipartite switch gives t_ij=x_i y_j. Forest restriction is an isomorphism of group schemes, not merely a point count.

Let M_G be the subgroup of Z^V generated by the incidence vectors e_head−e_tail. Then M_G is the lattice of vectors whose coordinate sum is zero in each component; a forest gives a Z-basis. The pointed group monoid M_G∪{0} defines an explicit affine monoid scheme over F1, whose Z-base extension is Spec Z[M_G]=T_G. This is genuine monoidal descent for the **multiplicative** carrier.

The abstract torus remembers only its rank. The graph/graphic-matroid data reside in its indexed edge characters. A rank-sized edge subset is a coordinate basis iff it is a spanning forest. Thus do not call the number of spanning trees the literal number of F1-points of the torus.

## 2. Exactly which seam data permit gluing?

Let connected subgraphs H1,H2 cover a connected graph G, and suppose their common vertex set is nonempty. Let K=H1∩H2 include ALL common vertices, including those isolated in the common-edge graph. Write k=c(K). Let

    P(A)=C_H1(A) ×_(A^E(K)) C_H2(A)

be the compatible local edge-label pairs.

**Theorem 2 (shared-component obstruction).** There is a natural exact sequence

    0 → C_G(A) → P(A) → A^k/diag(A) → 0.

It splits after choosing potentials and one overlap component; equivalently its last group is noncanonically A^(k−1). The obstruction of a compatible pair is the vector of differences between its two potential lifts, one constant on each connected component of K, modulo a common constant. A compatible pair glues globally iff these k constants are equal. The global EDGE vector is then unique; global potential lifts still have their usual one-parameter constant gauge.

**Proof.** Choose local lifts p1,p2. On every shared edge their differences telescope to zero, so p1−p2 is constant on each component of K. Changing either local lift changes all k constants by the same amount. Vanishing in A^k/diag(A) lets us change one local gauge to make the lifts agree on all shared vertices, after which they glue. Conversely a global potential makes the obstruction vanish. To realize any vector of component constants, set p1=0, give p2 the chosen constants on K's components, and arbitrarily extend p2 to the other vertices of H2; the common-edge labels agree. Taking zero off K and normalizing one component gives a group-homomorphic section. ∎

For finite A, precisely a fraction |A|^(−(k−1)) of compatible local pairs are globally consistent. This is an exact uniform group count, not an assertion that square-valued samples behave independently.

**Recovered cases.**

* Session58's two seven-edge hourglasses share the central five-edge spanning tree of K3,3: k=1, hence no missing graph-consistency equations. The two central AP guards remain additional decoration, reducing five tree coordinates to three magic coordinates.
* Session57's frozen two Hamilton paths on eight vertices share a forest with component sizes 2,3,3: k=3, hence exactly two independent cross-cycle equations. These are its two displayed alternating cycle identities.
* A two-Hamilton-path cover of K3,3 with one common edge has, on its SIX common vertices, one two-vertex component and four isolates: k=5, hence FOUR missing equations. Ignoring isolated common vertices would give the false answer zero. Each path has five edges, their union nine, and the cycle rank is9−6+1=4.

The seam theorem is the elementary graph-cohomology/Mayer–Vietoris mechanism in explicit potential coordinates; it is not claimed as a new general cohomology theorem.

## 3. An honest additive-to-multiplicative interpolation

Put B=Z[t] and define the smooth commutative B-group scheme

    G_t = Spec B[u,(1+tu)^−1],
    u ⊕_t v = u+v+tuv,
    ⊖_t u = −u/(1+tu).

This is the classical group scheme G^(λ), with λ=t (Suwa2009, §1.2, printed p118). The map u↦1+tu is a group homomorphism to G_m, an isomorphism after t is inverted. At t=0 the group is G_a. No invention of this classical interpolation is claimed.

**Theorem 3 (flat graph family).** Apply the graph-potential construction to G_t. The resulting closed subgroup scheme C_G,t⊂G_t^E is isomorphic, by forest restriction, to G_t^(|V|−c(G)). It is smooth and flat over Z[t]. Its t=0 fiber is the additive graph-potential carrier, and over Z[t,t^−1] it is the graph torus. The exact seam sequence of Theorem2 and its chosen splitting hold over Z[t]; in particular all these restrictions and gluings commute with every base change, including t=0 and characteristic2.

**Equations and the necessary saturation.** For a cycle with positive and negative oriented edge sets C+,C−, put

    F_C(t,z) = [ ∏_(e∈C+)(1+tz_e) − ∏_(e∈C−)(1+tz_e) ] / t.

The numerator has zero constant coefficient in t, so this denotes an INTEGER POLYNOMIAL obtained before specialization. It does not mean division by the element t inside an arbitrary ring. The deformed cycle equation is F_C=0. Fundamental cycles suffice after all1+tz_e are inverted. Indeed its equation is the group-word equality in G_t, multiplied by a unit, and forest propagation proves sufficiency. The special fiber is the signed additive cycle equation.

Using the undivided numerator is wrong: its t=0 equation is0=0 and loses every cycle constraint. It defines a generally nonflat extra component over t=0. The divided equations are the t-saturated closure of the generic graph torus in this coordinate neighborhood: their quotient is the displayed polynomial localization in tree coordinates, hence t-torsion-free, and agrees with the generic fiber.

For a four-cycle the correct equation is

    z1+z3−z2−z4 + t(z1z3−z2z4)=0.

It interpolates the additive parallelogram identity and the multiplicative cross-product identity.

**Tangent interpretation.** At the multiplicative identity, substitute t_e=1+εz_e with ε²=0. The monomial map x_i y_j gives z_ij=a_i+b_j, and cycle-binomial equations give the alternating linear cycle equations. Thus Lie(T_G) over Z is exactly the inherited additive carrier. This is a map of explicitly related objects, not a q→1 field limit.

## 4. Magic arrays are functorially three parameters

Let A be any abelian group. A3×3 array is magic if its three row sums, three column sums and two diagonal sums agree. Its common total is3E where E is its central entry. Every magic array is uniquely

    E+x       E−x−z       E+z
    E−x+z     E           E+x−z
    E−z       E+x+z       E−x.

Here x is top-left minus center, and z is top-right minus center. This is the universal additive-group normal form already established in Session58. Its derivation sums/cancels lines and never divides by2 or3: the three center lines and outer rows give T=3E, opposite pairs give2E, and the first row determines its remaining entry. Substitution gives the converse.

**Theorem 4 (magic torus and flat magic family).** Apply this normal form to the commutative group schemes G_m and G_t. Multiplicative magic arrays of units form the monoidal split torus

    M_× ≅ G_m^3,

with entries

    ex       e/(xz)       ez
    ez/x     e            ex/z
    e/z      exz          e/x,

and common line product e³. This embedding and its inverse e=middle, x=top-left/middle, z=top-right/middle are monomial, so the **entire multiplicative magic construction** is defined over F1 in the stated pointed-group-monoid convention. Its integral Lie algebra is exactly the ordinary additive magic plane.

Similarly, the functor of magic arrays for the operation ⊕_t is represented by M_t≅G_t³. It is smooth and flat over Z[t], specializes to the ordinary three-dimensional additive magic scheme at0, and becomes the multiplicative magic torus after t is inverted and every entry w is changed to1+tw. Its entries are obtained from the displayed additive normal form by replacing +,− with ⊕_t,⊖_t. No denominators other than the already inverted1+tu occur.

An explicit equation for equality of a line(a,b,c) with a reference line(d,e,f) is

    a+b+c−d−e−f
      +t(ab+ac+bc−de−df−ef)
      +t²(abc−def)=0.

All seven equalities against the top row cut out M_t inside G_t^9. Their universal group interpretation proves the scheme statement even over nonreduced rings. Characteristic2 and characteristic3 require no special exclusions unless one additionally asks for distinct entries.

## 5. The square-root cover also has a flat family—but is not monomial

Let R be the coordinate ring of M_t and let z_1,...,z_9∈R be its nine deformed entry functions. Define

    X_t = Spec R[r_1,...,r_9]/(r_i²−z_i :1≤i≤9).

**Theorem 5 (finite-free arithmetic pullback).** X_t→M_t is finite locally free of rank2^9=512, with basis ∏r_i^ε_i, ε_i∈{0,1}. Consequently X_t is flat over Z[t]. At t=0 it is exactly the affine nine-square magic scheme, with no nonzero/distinctness assumptions inserted. At t invertible its line equations are equality of products

    ∏_(i in line)(1+t r_i²),

not equality of products of r_i². The unit conditions1+t r_i² are retained. Flatness neither supplies rational points with distinct positive values nor transfers such points between fibers.

**Proof.** Successive quotients by monic polynomials r_i²−z_i are free of rank2, independently of zero divisors or characteristic. The base M_t is flat, giving the conclusion. Specialization substitutes t=0 in its entry functions and in the displayed line equations. ∎

**Hourglass compatibility throughout the family.** Let S_t be the cover of M_t adjoining roots for the five shared corner/center cells. It is finite free of rank32 over M_t. Adjoin the two horizontal-only roots to obtain H_h,t and the two vertical-only roots to obtain H_v,t. Each is finite free of rank4 over S_t, and

    X_t = H_h,t ×_(S_t) H_v,t

as schemes over Z[t]. Hence X_t is finite free of rank16 over S_t, consistent with512=32·4·4. These are tensor products of the explicitly disjoint sets of monic root equations. They recover Session58's root-fiber-product construction at t=0 and show that its compatibility is not an accident of linear equations. They do NOT make a rational-point count the product of two unconditioned marginal counts. In characteristic2 these covers need not be étale or reduced.

Ordinary squaring r↦r² here is not the group doubling map [2]_t(r)=2r+tr². Substituting group doubling would destroy the original square arithmetic in the additive fiber.

## 6. Graph operations remain visible

The coordinate-decorated group carrier, unlike its bare rank, retains graph minors. In any of the group functors above:

* Deleting a nonbridge edge gives an isomorphism C_G→C_(G−e): its label is recovered from a remaining path.
* Deleting a bridge gives a split exact sequence with kernel one copy of the coefficient group: the relative potential gauge of the two new components becomes invisible.
* For a nonloop e, contracting e identifies C_(G/e) with the closed identity-label locus z_e=0 (or t_e=1 multiplicatively) in C_G. Loops after contraction carry the identity label; parallel edges remain indexed.

Proofs are direct potential restriction and identification. They commute with the G_t-family and with tangent passage. This is a concrete graphic-matroid connection; it does not identify the Tutte polynomial with the point count of one torus.

## 9. Reproduction and scope

Run `python3 check.py` and `python3 -O check.py` in this directory (Python3 and SymPy required). The normal and optimized stdout and certificates are byte-identical. The checker exhausts all512 labelled subgraphs of K3,3 for Z/2 and Z/3 by two independent descriptions of potential edge images: all vertex assignments versus integrated edge assignments. This covers19,683 and262,144 edge vectors respectively. It checks nine seam/group cases, all35 deformed-group carriers Z/m with2≤m≤8 and0≤t<m, plus C2×C2, and independently compares every magic normal-form image with five-coordinate elimination. Symbolic checks include all eight deformed magic lines, specialization, cycle saturation, the unit Smith factors, and finite-free degree consistency. `certificate.json` retains the full universes and results; `SHA256SUMS` retains the source/output hashes. The discriminant arithmetic recorded by the checker is auxiliary; its geometric consequences are treated separately in a later addendum.

Proofs establish all-group and all-ring statements; finite tests do not replace those proofs. The former arithmetic problems remain open. This family is useful because it transports **relations, gluings, tangent structure, and finite-free covers**, not because it makes difficult square constraints disappear.
