# Exact gluing of the two magic hourglasses

## Scope and provenance

This is a scheme-theoretic sidecar to the session58 overlapping-hourglass calculation. Its shared-label identification is supplied by that calculation; the square-root construction and multiquadratic valuation argument are the same classical constructions used in session57 `geometry.md`, Sections 1–2 and 5. The full nine-root surface is the already-studied magic-square-of-squares surface, not a newly discovered surface. Session57 identifies Asher Auel and Benjamin Singer, *The algebraic geometry of 3-by-3 magic squares of squares*, arXiv:2609.09351v1, https://arxiv.org/html/2609.09351v1, as a primary source for that surface; that source is inherited attribution here, not a fresh source check. The exact coordinate gluing, uniform degree proof, and coupled count below are explicit deductions supplied in this note. They are not claimed to be historically novel. No rational point, distinct positive integer solution, F1 model, RH statement, or new Weil theorem follows.

Throughout k is any field of characteristic different from 2. All schemes and fiber products below are over k.

## 1. Equations and global maps

Write the five shared roots as (c,a,b,d,e), with squared labels

(c²,a²,b²,d²,e²) = (E,E+x,E−x,E+z,E−z).

Let

A = k[c,a,b,d,e]/(a²+b²−2c², d²+e²−2c²),  B = Proj(A).

Put F=a²+d²−c², G=3c²−a²−d², J=c²+a²−d², K=c²−a²+d². Define graded rings, all root variables having degree 1,

A+ = A[u,v]/(u²−F,v²−G),
A− = A[s,t]/(s²−J,t²−K),
D  = A[u,v,s,t]/(u²−F,v²−G,s²−J,t²−K).

Thus H+=Proj(A+) adds E±(x+z), H−=Proj(A−) adds E±(x−z), and M=Proj(D) is the nine-root surface. B, H±, M are surfaces cut out respectively by 2,4,6 quadrics in P4,P6,P8. Their regular-sequence assertion is elementary: use c,a,d as free variables and successively adjoin b,e and whichever added roots occur by monic quadratics. The affine rings are free of ranks 4,16,64 over k[c,a,d]. The embedding degrees are 4,16,64, distinct from the relative cover degrees below. Their dualizing sheaves are O(−1), O(1), O(3), respectively, by complete-intersection adjunction; this does not classify resolutions.

**There is no projective base locus in these specific schemes.** A general coordinate-forgetting map can have one, but here setting c=a=b=d=e=0 forces u²=v²=s²=t²=0. Scheme-theoretically the irrelevant ideal of D lies in the radical of the ideal generated by the shared coordinates. Consequently those five standard opens cover Proj(D), and similarly cover Proj(A±). This includes the needed nilpotent check: the remaining quotient is Artinian with nilpotent irrelevant ideal, so its Proj is empty. Thus the forgetful morphisms H±→B and M→B exist globally, not merely on E≠0.

## 2. Scheme-theoretic fiber product and flatness

On affine cones, D=A+ tensor_A A− exactly, with no saturation or reduction imposed.

To prove the projective statement without a generally false blanket rule about Proj and tensor products, choose any shared coordinate q from {c,a,b,d,e}. On the open q≠0 normalize every root by q. The coordinate rings of the H± charts are obtained from the B chart by adjoining u/q,v/q and s/q,t/q, respectively, with their displayed monic square equations divided by q². Their tensor product is exactly the corresponding M chart. These identifications agree on chart overlaps because both use the same root ratios. The charts cover all schemes as proved above. Hence

M ≅ H+ ×_B H−

as schemes globally.

Each chart extension has free basis {1,u/q,v/q,uv/q²} for H+, the analogous four elements for H−, and the sixteen squarefree monomials in the four new normalized roots for M. Therefore the maps are finite locally free of ranks 4,4,16, including over zeros and singular points of B. A rank-16 scheme fiber need not have sixteen rational or geometric points: zeros can produce nonreduced fibers.

## 3. Independence, generic degrees, branch loci

The chart c≠0 admits X=x/E, Z=z/E. Its shared roots a/c,b/c,d/c,e/c square to 1+X,1−X,1+Z,1−Z. In k(X,Z), the eight polynomials

1±X, 1±Z, 1±(X+Z), 1±(X−Z)

are nonzero pairwise nonassociate irreducible linear polynomials in every odd characteristic. Their square classes are independent: a nonempty product has odd valuation at each selected line. The elementary multiquadratic lemma then gives degree 2^8 for adjoining all eight roots, and degree 2^4 for adjoining just the shared four. Equivalently the four new square classes remain independent over k(B). This proves geometric integrality of B,H±,M (their rings are free over the parameter polynomial rings and embed in their generic multiquadratic fields). It also proves the relative generic degrees are exactly 4,4,16, not just upper bounds. After extending constants to an algebraic closure the same proof works.

The generic extension k(M)/k(B) is Galois with group (Z/2)^4, generated by independent signs of u,v,s,t; each hourglass subgroup gives (Z/2)^2. On the complement of F G J K=0, M→B is a finite étale torsor under this constant group. The relative non-étale locus is exactly the union where at least one new root vanishes: the relative differentials have relations 2u du=2v dv=2s ds=2t dt=0 in normalized charts. The corresponding branch support on B is the union of the four radicand-zero loci. This support statement is safe on the singular base; no assertion about a smooth normal-crossing compactification is intended. Each branch line is distinct from the five shared parameter lines, so generic ramification along its pullback is quadratic. Calling the generic sign group geometric monodromy is justified by the geometric integrality proof, but no stronger topological monodromy computation is needed.

## 4. Exact coupled finite-field counts

Let q be odd, χ the quadratic character of F_q with χ(0)=0, and r(y)=1+χ(y). Then r(y) is the number of roots of T²=y, including r(0)=1. For α=(E,x,z) define

w(α)=r(E)r(E+x)r(E−x)r(E+z)r(E−z),
h+(α)=r(E+x+z)r(E−x−z),
h−(α)=r(E+x−z)r(E−x+z).

Writing N for affine-cone rational-point counts, the exact formulas are

N_B=Σα w(α),
N_H+=Σα w(α)h+(α),  N_H−=Σα w(α)h−(α),
N_M=Σα w(α)h+(α)h−(α).

These formulas incorporate zeros and all sign multiplicities. For any of the four schemes, its projective count is (N−1)/(q−1), since every nonzero root tuple has a free simultaneous F_q^* scaling orbit. One must subtract the common cone vertex before dividing.

There is also an intrinsic projective formula. For b∈B(F_q), choose a rational root representative and normalize by any nonzero shared coordinate. Let n±(b) be the number of F_q-points in its hourglass fiber, computed as the product of the two corresponding r-values. This is independent of representative since rescaling changes the radicands by a nonzero square. Then

#M(F_q)=Σ_b n+(b)n−(b).

If b is uniformly distributed on B(F_q), this says

#M/#B = (#H+/#B)(#H−/#B) + Cov(n+,n−).

Thus multiplying the marginal counts, even with the natural division by #B, silently discards a covariance. Generic field-extension independence does NOT imply independence of these finite-field fiber-count functions.

Concrete independently checked projective counts:

q=5: (#B,#H+,#H−,#M)=(36,88,88,320), covariance 236/81.
q=7: (64,160,160,384), covariance −1/4.

So covariance can have either sign. At q=3 it vanishes accidentally: (16,64,64,256). These examples disprove an unqualified multiplication rule, not any claimed asymptotic estimate.

### C2 spectral refinement

The involution σ:[c:a:b:d:e]↦[c:a:b:e:d] of B sends z to −z and interchanges the two added pairs. Thus if f(b)=n+(b), then n−(b)=f(σb). This assertion holds on every shared-coordinate chart, including c=0, since exchanging d and e gives E+z↔E−z and preserves the shared equations. Define real-valued functions f_even=(f+f∘σ)/2 and f_odd=(f−f∘σ)/2 on the finite set B(F_q). The invariant uniform measure makes these two functions orthogonal, and E[f_odd]=0. Consequently, with unnormalized squared norm ||g||²=Σ_b g(b)²,

#M = Σ_b f(b)f(σb) = ||f_even||²−||f_odd||²,
Cov(n+,n−) = Var(f_even)−E[f_odd²].

This is the elementary decomposition into the two characters of C2, not a quantum or cohomological assertion. The covariance sign is governed by a competition between symmetric fluctuation and antisymmetric energy; it need not be nonnegative. The executable check gives energies (324,4) at q=5, hence 320, and (448,64) at q=7, hence 384. The means remain 88/36 and 160/64, respectively. Cone sums compute these projective energies by removing the vertex contribution (1 for even energy, 0 for odd energy) and dividing by q−1.

**Entry counts are different.** The number of parameter triples whose nine labels are squares uses the indicator 1_{r(y)>0}, not r(y). A label tuple having m nonzero square labels has 2^m affine root lifts, not uniformly 2^9. For q=5 the affine admissible-parameter counts (base,plus,minus,full) are (11,9,9,7), whereas the affine root counts are (145,353,353,1281). When an actual magic square is required to have distinct nonzero entries, insert that common admissibility restriction before either count. None of the displayed counts imposes it. Do not discard zeros or diagonal degeneracies after multiplying unrelated marginals.

The formulas are valid over every odd prime power. The supplied execution checks only the listed prime fields; extension-field execution is not claimed. The coefficient matrix of the six square relations has a 6×6 minor equal to 1, so equation rank does not drop modulo any prime. However Jacobian ranks involve 2C diag(roots), and finite-field dependencies of subsets of the parameter forms must be computed in that field. The characteristic-zero incidence matroid must not be assumed unchanged modulo small primes. Characteristic 2 is excluded from all separability and character arguments.

## 5. Spanning-tree interpretation and classical-recovery guard

Place the nine entry labels in the additive 3×3 array E+i x+j z, with i,j∈{−1,0,1}. The middle row and middle column give the five shared labels and are a spanning tree in K3,3. A general additive K3,3 tree has five free edge labels. Imposing that the two endpoint values in each of the middle row and column average to the center gives precisely the two AP equations above, leaving the three parameters E,x,z. The four non-tree edges are the corners. One diagonal pair adds E±(x+z), and the other adds E±(x−z). Each hourglass therefore adds two chords, and gluing adds all four.

The raw additive array is not itself asserted to be a magic square: the usual magic-square arrangement permutes these nine labels. That distinction is necessary. Shared-edge root assignments glue exactly when their five root coordinates agree (or their projective points agree, with the unique common scaling fixed in a chart); agreeing only in squares forgets sign data and changes counts.

General incidence gluing via tensor products and character expansions is classical. What is recovered here is a precise model-specific translation and an auditable coupled-count obstruction to treating overlapping hourglasses as independent. It adds no F1 descent and supplies no shortcut to Weil bounds or RH. Those require independently specified schemes, cohomological inputs, and hypotheses.
