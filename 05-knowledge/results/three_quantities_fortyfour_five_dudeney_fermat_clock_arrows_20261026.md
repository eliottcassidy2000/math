**Theorem A (incidence-cycle law).** Let a finite protocol attach to every state `x` two terminal responses `a(x) in A`, `b(x) in B` (here the source class and the target class), and let `E` be the set of attained pairs, `V` the attained responses, `c` the number of connected components of the bipartite graph `(V, E)`. A joint law on the paired cells (a vector indexed by `E`) is observed through its two marginals; that observation has rank `|V| - c`, and exactly `|E| - |V| + c` directions of the joint law are invisible, namely the cycle space of the bipartite graph. *Proof.* The marginal map is the transpose of the vertex-edge incidence map of `(V, E)`, whose rank is `|V| - c` and whose kernel on edge space is the cycle space. QED. At order 5 this is 50 cells, 24 attained responses, one component: 23 independent responses, 27 lost directions; the order-6 and order-7 rows are new. The same law in converse-merged coordinates is boundary_support's Proposition 0 (`r - c - b`) and the session33 continuation note's `2r - c - b`; the repository did not state it as a cycle space before session72's announcement. The bipartite graph is connected at every order tested (prediction 3 of the pre-registration, confirmed at order 7).

**Theorem B (degree bound on restored tests).** On `X_n`, `g_p` is the indicator of the subcube of states whose free pairs along `p` (at most `n - 1` of them) are oriented as `p` requires. Hence a product `g_(p_1) ... g_(p_k)` is the indicator of a subcube fixing at most `k(n - 1)` free pairs, a Boolean polynomial of degree `<= k(n - 1)` in the `M` coordinates, and the span of all `k`-fold products has dimension at most `sum_(j <= k(n-1)) binom(M, j)`. So full recovery of the `2^M` presentation-law coordinates by `k`-fold restored tests requires `k(n - 1) >= M`, i.e. `k >= ceil((n - 2)/2)`: single guards suffice only at order 4, pairs can suffice only through order 6, and at order 7 pairs span at most `32768 - 105 - 15 - 1 = 32647` directions. *Proof.* Monomial counting. QED. FINITE-EXACT: pairs do span at orders 4, 5, 6 (the summary's "pairs recover all 64" is the order-5 case of a phenomenon that ends at order 6). The bound is not tight for single guards (`44 < 57`, `273 < 638`).

**Cover depth (FINITE-EXACT, with the dark-pair obstruction).** The point indicator of a state `x` is itself a product of its own guards iff `k` of its Hamiltonian paths cover all `M` free pairs; a state with a *dark* free pair (on no Hamiltonian path, THM-4806 / THM-4807) is never such a cube:

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4 | 1 | 2 | 5 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 1 = 1 |
| 5 | 13 | 0 | 21 | 28 | 2 | 0 | 2 = 2 |
| 6 | 68 | 0 | 32 | 593 | 264 | 67 | 2 = 2 |
| 7 | 492 | 0 | 0 | 6,806 | 18,690 | 6,780 | 3 = 3 |
| 8 (10,000 sampled) | 21 | 0 | 0 | 109 | 3,934 | 5,936 | 3 = 3 |

So at order 5 only 21 of the 64 point indicators are two-path cubes, yet the pair products span everything: recovery is a linear-combination phenomenon, not a point-indicator one. The pre-registered predictions 1 and 4 ("every state is covered by `ceil((n-2)/2)` paths") were ill-posed and are refuted as written (uncoverable states exist at every order from 4, the transitive state first); the refined statement that holds is "the least cover depth among covered states equals the degree bound" (orders 4-7).

**Reading.** Three quantities: distinguishable states (`2^M`, or the `E` paired cells), independent linear responses (`V - c`, or the guard ranks), realizable positive responses (the cone over the attained cells; its extreme rays are the cells, so it can have more rays than its dimension, session72's U2 observation). ## 3. Fermat's two-square theorem and Dudeney's optimality

What the paper proves and uses (session71's dependency audit, confirmed here against the v3 HTML): no dissection of the equal-area square (side `sigma = 3^(1/4)`) and equilateral triangle (side `tau = 2`) into at most three polygonal pieces under translations and rotations; two pieces die by the diameter inequality `sqrt(2 sqrt 3) < 2`; three pieces die by a case analysis of coupled edge and vertex matching diagrams whose contradictions are linear (`3 sigma = 3 tau`, or a sum forced to equal both `sigma` and `tau`). Fermat's theorem, Gaussian integers, lattices and rational independence do not occur in the proof.

How Fermat does relate, in the form the facts allow:
1. **Norms govern coordinates, not piece counts** (session71, unnamed-carrier sec. 4; CITED, PROVED there). A square of integer area `N` has all vertices in `Q^2` iff `N` is a sum of two squares (a Gaussian norm). Over `K = Q(sqrt 3)` the equal-area square has no coordinate realization in any orientation, because `a^2 + b^2 = sqrt 3` would force a negative conjugate; `Q(3^(1/4))` removes the obstruction; the minimal real field has degree 4 and the minimal complex one degree 8 with Galois group `D_4`. The analogous golden statement obstructs equal-area similar kite/dart copies in abelian fields. None of this separates three pieces from four: scaling the pair to area 5 or 44 leaves the minimum at four.
2. **The congruence filter the paper does use** is the angle charge (session71, PROVED there): in each component of the vertex matching diagram, the corner counts satisfy `2a/3 - 2b/4 in Z`, so a component takes all or none of the triangle's corners and an even number of the square's; the charge group is `Z/6 = Z/2 x Z/3`. This is the same *shape* as Fermat's local condition (a prime is a Gaussian norm iff `p = 1 mod 4`): a cyclic-group residue condition that must be met before any metric realization is attempted. The paper then needs the metric step (the matching equations); Fermat's theorem needs no metric step because the norm form is definite over `Z[i]`.
3. **The Waring-type reading (ANALOGY).** Two pieces impossible, three impossible, four suffice; two squares suffice exactly for Fermat's numbers, three exactly outside Legendre's class `4^a(8b + 7)`, four always (Lagrange). In both ladders the small counts are decided by congruence-plus-definiteness and the top count by a construction (Dudeney's hinged four pieces; Lagrange's identity). The analogy predicts nothing about other polygon pairs and is typed as such.
4. **A question, not a claim (HYP-9419 (3)).** Whether there is a local obstruction theory for three-piece dissections between regular polygons, i.e. whether the angle-charge congruence plus the field obstruction decide the three-piece question for the pairs where the paper's metric case analysis is needed, is open; session71's pentagon-square corollary (all-or-none pentagon corners, even square corners) is the first test case.

## 6. Tournaments and even graphs

The thread is settled canon and session72's programme; one paragraph suffices. Labelled: tournaments on `n` vertices, all graphs on `n` vertices and even-degree graphs on `n + 1` vertices are equinumerous (`2^binom(n,2)`; a graph on `n` vertices extends uniquely to an even graph on `n + 1` by joining the new vertex to the odd vertices; orient an edge `{i < j}` as `i -> j` when present), and labelled switching classes of tournaments match labelled even graphs on `n` vertices (THM-474, `2^binom(n-1,2)`). ## 7. Clock arrows: the pattern of a sequence as a circulant (THM-4809, Theorem C)

Three readings of "arrows on the clock" are now on record: the concurrent branch's trichotomy (index arrows `n mod m -> a_n mod m`, successor arrows `a_n -> a_(n+1)`, and the reduction of an iteration `x -> f(x)`, each a functional graph only under a fibre-constancy condition, with Fibonacci modulo 5 as the hostile and with precision-changing maps `Z/(qm) -> Z/m` for division), THM-4532's clocks (a circulant-type digraph `Clk(G, C)`, a tournament iff `C` and `-C` partition `G \ {0}`), and the reading used here, the *pattern as connection set*: the pattern of `(a_k)` modulo `m` is the set `S_m` of nonzero residues attained, and its arrows are `i -> i + s` for `s in S_m`, the circulant `Clk(Z/m, S_m)`. This is the "partial tournament-like structure" of the directive made exact:

**Theorem C.** `Clk(Z/m, S_m)` is an oriented graph (at most one arc per pair, no loops) iff `S_m` and `-S_m` are disjoint, and a tournament iff moreover `|S_m| = (m - 1)/2`. For an odd prime `p`: the pattern of the squares is a tournament iff `p = 3 mod 4`, and then it is the Paley tournament; the pattern of the powers `a^k` is an oriented graph iff `-1` is not in `<a>` and a tournament iff moreover `<a>` has index 2, i.e. `a` is a quadratic residue of order `(p - 1)/2` (for `a = 2`: `p = 7 mod 8` with `ord_p(2) = (p - 1)/2`, the Mersenne-type primes of THM-126 and THM-4644). *Proof.* The first sentence is THM-4532's criterion; `-1 in <a>` iff the pattern meets its negative; index 2 iff half-set. QED

FINITE-EXACT census to modulus 120 ([`clock_arrows_circulant_20261026.out`](../../04-computation/experiments/clock_arrows_circulant_20261026.out)): squares give tournaments exactly at `3, 7, 11, 19, 23, 31, 43, 47, 59, 67, 71, 79, 83, 103, 107` (the primes `3 mod 4` below 120) and oriented graphs also at the composite moduli `4, 9, 12, 16, 21, 27, 28, 33, 36, 44, 48, 49, 57, 63, 64, 69, 76, 77, 81, 84, 92, 93, 99, 108, 112` (those whose unit group makes `-1` a non-square); powers of 2 give tournaments exactly at `7, 23, 47, 71, 79, 103`; powers of 3 at `3, 11, 23, 47, 59, 71, 83, 107`; the triangular numbers at `3` and `5` only (`{1, 3}` modulo 5 is a skew half-set); the factorials are oriented at `6, 20, 24, 40, 45, 60, 90, 112, 120` and never tournaments; the cubes, the Fibonacci numbers, the primes and the odd Collatz successors `(3k + 1)/2^v` are never oriented (each pattern contains a residue and its negative at every modulus tried). So the Paley tournaments and the `<2>`-tournaments of the repository's circulant thread are exactly the clocks of the two most classical sequences, and the Collatz successor pattern is never tournament-like: the directive's "partial tournament" is the oriented-graph case, realised by the squares at composite moduli and by the factorials.

## 9. A step back: unifying-theorem candidates (HYP-9419)

The repository's advances of the last fortnight share one pattern. An object (tournament, dissection, triple, tiling, sequence, machine) is observed through a finite family of named tests; the observation forgets a set of alignment bits; the forgotten bits are counted by a cohomological quantity of an incidence structure (the cycle rank of a bipartite graph, the number of conjugate prime pairs minus one, the number of independent attachment sites); recovery needs tests of growing depth (products, restored tests, full paths), with an exact rank law (Boolean zeta ranks `2^k - d_t`, Theorem B's degree bound); and realization adds a predicate that linear rank does not see (positive cones, legality, metric identities, norms). The candidates, stated so that they can fail:
1. **Three-layer composition (PROPOSED).** For a finite response theory with incidence, transport and realization data retained, responses compose (session69 P3, rooted-lines R1, the branch's convolution are instances); dropping any layer produces one of exactly four failure types (joint-root collision, phase collision, metric contradiction, insufficient test depth), each already witnessed. Falsified by a composition failure of a fifth kind, or by a lawful compression that drops a layer (U2's reset lemma is the known lawful case, which must be classified inside the frame, not against it).
2. **Rank laws (PROVED in part).** Theorem A (incidence) and Theorem B (realization depth) are the exact linear content; the conjectured completion is that the rank of `k`-fold restored tests on `X_n` equals the dimension of the span of the monomials `x_S` over the free-pair sets `S` that are unions of at most `k` path supports, so that it is computable without elimination; this predicts the single-guard ranks `8, 44, 273, ...` from path supports alone. Testable at order 7 (the GF(2) rank 1,934 is a lower bound for the rational rank).
3. **Local obstructions for dissections (QUESTION).** Section 3 (4).
4. **Pattern clocks (PROVED criterion; census).** The clocks of classical sequences are tournaments only for the squares at Paley primes and for `a^k` at index-2 primes without `-1`; conjecture: no sequence given by a polynomial of degree `>= 3`, nor the Fibonacci numbers, nor the primes, has a tournament pattern at any modulus (true to 120 for the cubes, Fibonacci and primes).
