# Squarefree path addresses, divisor balance, and Pythagorean triples

Status: proved as a direct specialization of the existing endpoint/PPT codec; finite example checked independently. The F₂-vector-space law is an address operation, not claimed to be a geometric symmetry of the triples or an automorphism of the tournament.

Let N=p²qr for three pairwise distinct primes, all 1 mod 4. Order the primes p,q,r as address coordinates, taking q<r (the repeated prime is distinguished). Set R=pqr. The proper, nontrivial divisor counts are F=10, S=7, U=3, as defined in arithmetic_braids_20260917_divisors.md. The nonsquarefree divisors, including N, are p²,p²q,p²r,N.

The nonexceptional Hamiltonian paths of E_5 are P_A=(A increasing,4,0,A^c increasing), where A is a subset of {p,q,r}, identified in that order with {1,2,3}. Complementing the subset defines an involution on these eight paths, independent of whether this involution comes from any tournament automorphism (it does not). Its four orbits form V=F₂³/<(1,1,1)>. Each orbit has a unique representative A of cardinality zero or one.

Map its zero element [∅] to N, and the three nonzero elements [{p}],[{q}],[{r}] respectively to p²,p²q,p²r. This gives a canonical bijection between V and the four nonsquarefree divisors, taking V\{0} bijectively to the three nonsquarefree proper divisors and hence to the three prime-divisor directions. The action exchanging q and r fixes 0 and [{p}] and swaps [{q}],[{r}]; on proper nonsquarefree divisors it realizes an actual 1+2 involution, albeit as an abstract coordinate relabeling, not as an involution exchanging the exponent shapes p³ and p²qr.

To obtain primitive Pythagorean triples, select for each prime t a Gaussian factor π_t of norm t; let e_p=2, e_q=e_r=1. For A⊂{p,q,r}, let α_A=product of π_t^{e_t} for t∈A times the conjugate factors for t∉A. Write x+iy=α_A, n=max(|x|,|y|), m=min(|x|,|y|). The associated primitive triple is (n²−m²,2mn,N), with legs parity-ordered. Complements give the same triple and exhaust the four leg-swap classes, by the previously proved marked Gaussian factor-choice codec (endpoint_pythagorean_fibers_20261019.md). The choice of Gaussian factors is part of the decoder; no numerical symmetry of the legs is implied by relabeling q and r.

For p³ with p≡1 mod 4, r=1, and the same codec gives H(E_3)=3 and one primitive PPT class. In both nonsquarefree balance cases, H(E_{U+2})=1+2^U=S+2=F−U+2, and the number of PPT classes is 2^{U−1}=(S+1)/2. The prime case has U(p)=0 but ω(p)=1 and is not covered by these formulas.

Finite example using π_5=2+i, π_13=3+2i, π_17=4+i, N=5525. Root: (5133,2044,5525); p: (5427,1036,5525); q: (2163,5084,5525); r: (4557,3124,5525). For 5³=125 the unique class is (117,44,125). See check.py. All three integer sides have unique canonical Zeckendorf codes; this lossless post-encoding is not claimed to turn the quadratic equation into an independent digitwise law, because Zeckendorf carries remain essential.

## A composed guarded ternary return

The address/Pythagorean codec can be composed with, but is not itself, the separate ternary map of ternary_bridge_20260925.md. The latter uses positive odd coprime s,t and requires a choice of sign of the even leg: (A,B,C) recovers s=√(C+B), t=√(C−B). For t=3^k, its plus map reaches the integer boundary t=1 in exactly k steps, because one factor 3 cancels in each step. For general t=3^k q with 3∤q it reaches denominator q after k steps; q=1 is necessary and sufficient for reaching the integer boundary. Discarding B's sign can change t and hence changes the actual state.

Take N=5²·13·113=36725, π5=2+i, π13=3+2i, π113=8+7i. The singleton {113} class yields (A,B,C)=(813,36716,36725) and s=271, t=3. The plus numerator is 3s+t=816=2^4·3·17; canceling 2^4 and then the common 3 gives (s',t')=(17,1), hence (A',B',C')=(17,144,145). This is exactly one normalization step. It supplies a concrete bridge between the p²qr balance, endpoint path complement class, a primitive Pythagorean triple, and a ternary normalization to the integer stratum of the rational Collatz triple chart. It is not a tournament path-spectrum return or an equivalence of the two dynamics. The target 145=5·29 has F=2, S=2, U=2 and is not a divisor-balance solution. Thus this return does not preserve F=S+U, and it says nothing about convergence of the subsequent odd-integer Collatz dynamics.
