# Small forbidden patterns and full-correlation reconstruction

19 October 2026. Follow-on to the proved finite-group identities in
[the prime-pairs structural note](prime-pairs-structural-note.md), Section 1.

The question is now purely additive-combinatorial. It does not require a
conjecture about primes: how small can a symmetric forbidden pattern be if
its fourth-moment data fail to determine the sign of its internal-target bias?

There are three answers, depending on what structure is retained:

- In general cyclic groups, two forbidden residues suffice, already modulo 6.
- For connected loopless cyclic Cayley graphs, degree four is necessary;
  an exact small-order census finds the first example modulo 12.
- In prime-order groups, four forbidden residues are necessary and sufficient;
  among four-residue examples the first prime modulus is 13, and an explicit
  family exists at every prime >=13.

The distinction is important. A claim that four residues are always minimal
would be false because of the disconnected modulo-6 example. A further
reconstruction theorem below shows that full correlation, unlike a fourth
moment, exactly recovers every symmetric set at a known center on an
odd-order abelian group.

## 1. The two invariants

Let G have order q, let B=-B be a proper subset with 0 not in B, put s=|B|,
and set A=G\B. Define

  J_B(t)=#{b in B : b-t is in B},
  E(B)=sum_t J_B(t)^2,
  T(B)=#{(b1,b2) in B^2 : b1+b2 is in B}.

The normalized pair kernel is

  gamma_A(t)=q #{a in A : a-t is in A}/(q-s)^2.

The earlier exact identities are

  variance(gamma_A)=[q E(B)-s^4]/(q-s)^4,
  average_(t in A) gamma_A(t)=1+[s^3-q T(B)]/(q-s)^3.  (1)

Thus equal q,s,E means equal variance, but the sign of the conditional bias
is the sign of s^3-qT. For the simple undirected Cayley graph generated by B,
qT is its third adjacency trace and qE its fourth trace.

## 2. Complete four-element classification over prime fields

Let p>=11 be prime and a in F_p\{0,1,-1}. Set

  B_a={1,-1,a,-a}.

Every symmetric four-element subset of F_p not containing 0 is a nonzero
scalar multiple of some B_a. Scaling preserves both E and T.

### Theorem 1. PROVED.

Define the two exceptional ratio sets

  R_3={2,-2,1/2,-1/2},
  R_4={3,-3,1/3,-1/3}.

Then

  T(B_a)=6 if a belongs to R_3, and 0 otherwise;
  E(B_a)=44 if a belongs to R_4, and 36 otherwise.     (2)

Each exceptional set has four elements, and the sets are disjoint for p>=11.
There are consequently exactly three cases in this normalized parameter:

- Four ratios with T=6 and E=36.
- Four ratios with T=0 and E=44.
- The remaining p-11 ratios with T=0 and E=36.

This counts normalized ratios a, not isomorphism classes of graphs or sets.
Different a can represent the same set after scaling or changing generators.

Proof of the triple formula. If two summands are opposites, their sum is 0
and is not in B_a. Enumerating the remaining sums from {+/-1,+/-a} shows that
one can land in B_a exactly when one generator is twice the other, up to sign.
These are precisely a=+/-2,+/-1/2. When a=2, the six ordered solutions are

  (1,1), (-1,-1), (2,-1), (-1,2), (-2,1), (1,-2).

The other exceptional ratios give scalar-equivalent sets. No additional
coincidence occurs for p>=11. This proves the T formula.

Proof of the energy formula. Before merging equal nonzero difference values,
the difference multiset has

  0 with multiplicity 4;
  +/-2 and +/-2a, each with multiplicity 1;
  +/-(a-1) and +/-(a+1), each with multiplicity 2.      (3)

If all eight displayed nonzero values are distinct, the energy is
4^2+4*1^2+4*2^2=36. Equality between values in (3), after excluding
0 and a=+/-1, occurs exactly when a=+/-3 or a=+/-1/3. For such a ratio,
there are exactly two symmetric collisions, each merging multiplicities
1 and 2. Each increases the energy by 3^2-1^2-2^2=4, so the energy is 44.
The possible exceptional ratios do not overlap for p>=11; overlap would
force p to divide one of 2,3,5,7. QED.

### Corollary 2. An infinite opposite-bias family. PROVED.

For every prime p>=13 there are symmetric four-element forbidden sets with
identical energy 36 and opposite conditional-bias signs.

Proof. Compare B_2 with any B_a whose ratio avoids

  {0,+/-1,+/-2,+/-1/2,+/-3,+/-1/3}.

This excluded set has 11 elements, leaving p-11>=2 choices. Both sets have
energy 36. Their conditional-surplus numerators are respectively

  64-6p < 0,   and   64 > 0.                         (4)

Equation (1) proves the assertion. QED.

Both Cayley graphs are connected because their generating set contains 1.
They are four-regular and each has exactly p four-cycles, since

  number of four-cycles = [pE-p*4*(2*4-1)]/8 = p.

The B_2 graph has p triangles, whereas the generic B_a graph is triangle-free.
Thus even exact degree, vertex count, and four-cycle count fail to determine
the sign. The missing coordinate is genuinely cubic.

### Corollary 3. Prime-order minimality. PROVED.

Among nonempty symmetric forbidden subsets of prime-order groups that do
not contain 0, cardinality four is the smallest permitting equal energy and
opposite conditional-bias signs. The smallest prime order admitting such a
four-element pair is 13.

Proof. At an odd prime, a symmetric loopless set has even size. Size two is
{+/-a}; for p>=5 it has no additive triple and hence has positive surplus 8.
At p=3, T=2 and its surplus is still positive, namely 8-3*2=2. At p=2 the
only nonempty proper loopless choice has size one and positive surplus.

For size four at p=5 there is only the full set of nonzero elements, so no
pair exists. At p=7 all three choices up to the literal symmetric pair
selection are scalar-equivalent, with E=44,T=6, and positive surplus 22.
At p=11, Theorem 1 has no generic ratios: the four ratios with energy 36
have T=6, while the four with energy 44 have T=0. Equal energy cannot have
opposite signs. At p=13, choose a=5 and compare with a=2. QED.

## 3. The smaller disconnected example

In Z/6Z let

  B_1={1,5},   B_2={2,4}.

Both sets have size two and energy six. The first has T=0, the second T=2.
Their normalized conditional means are

  1+8/4^3=9/8,   1+(8-6*2)/4^3=15/16.               (5)

The first Cayley graph is a six-cycle. The second is the disjoint union of
two triangles. Their fourth traces agree, but their third traces do not. More strongly,
their entire labelled autocorrelation vectors agree:

  J_(B_1)=J_(B_2)=(2,0,1,0,1,0).

The sets differ by translation by 3, an element of order two. That translation
preserves both symmetry about 0 and the correlation, but changes which
targets are being conditioned on. Thus the full kernel itself loses the
conditioning set at this even-order boundary.

Size one cannot produce an opposite-bias pair: a loopless symmetric
singleton is a nonzero involution and has T=0. Thus size two is genuinely
minimal without a connectivity condition.

Order six is also the smallest cyclic order for any opposite-sign pair.
For orders below six, list the symmetric loopless possibilities: in odd
orders they have even size, and in order four one may additionally use the
single element of order two. Every proper possibility has positive
conditional-surplus numerator. Explicitly, the only nonzero triple-count
cases are the full nonzero set modulo 3 (surplus 2), the full nonzero set
modulo 4 (surplus 3), and the full nonzero set modulo 5 (surplus 4).

## 4. What connectivity changes

### Proposition 4. Degree at most three cannot produce negative bias in a
connected loopless cyclic Cayley graph. PROVED.

Proof. Degree one forces the connected graph on two vertices and has T=0.
At degree two, B={+/-a} with a generating the group, so the graph is a cycle.
Its triple count is zero unless the group has order three, where the surplus
is 2; it is always positive.

At degree three the group has even order q=2h and
B={h,+/-a}, with gcd(a,h)=1 for connectivity. A triangle using h requires
2a=h modulo q. Then q is divisible by four, a=+/-q/4, and connectivity forces
q=4. This is the complete graph on four vertices, with surplus 27-4*6=3.
A triangle not using h requires 3a=0. Connectivity then forces q=6; here
T=2 and the surplus is 27-6*2=15. In every other case T=0. Thus the surplus
is positive in all cases. QED.

There is a degree-four example at q=12:

  B_1={1,4,8,11}={+/-1,+/-4},
  B_2={1,2,10,11}={+/-1,+/-2}.

Both graphs are connected. Both energies are 36. Their triple counts are 2
and 6, giving surplus numerators 40 and -8. Their conditional means are
69/64 and 63/64, respectively. They have the same twelve four-cycles, while
their triangle counts are four and twelve.

FINITE-EXACT minimal-order statement: exhaustive enumeration of every
symmetric loopless subset of Z/qZ of every size, for 2<=q<=12, finds
no connected equal-energy/opposite-sign pair before q=12. The companion
script records all connected patterns and all (energy, triple count,
surplus) possibilities for size four. Combined with Proposition 4, this is
an exact finite certificate for the smallest cyclic order and degree.

This statement is explicitly about cyclic Cayley graphs. It is not a
classification of arbitrary regular graphs or of every finite abelian group.

## 5. Full correlation is lossless on odd-order groups

### Theorem 5. Odd-order parity reconstruction. PROVED.

Let G be any finite abelian group of odd order, and let B=-B be any subset,
including the empty set or a set containing 0. Then for every x in G,

  1_B(x) = J_B(2x) modulo 2.                          (6)

In particular the complete labelled autocorrelation determines B uniquely.
The theorem is not restricted to prime order or to cyclic groups. More
generally, the parity of the sum-correlation recovers ANY subset of an
odd-order abelian group; symmetry is needed only to identify sum-correlation
with autocorrelation.

Proof. Symmetry identifies J_B(t) with the number of ordered pairs
(b1,b2) in B^2 satisfying b1+b2=t. Swap the two entries. Every off-diagonal
pair belongs to a two-element orbit, so contributes zero modulo 2. For t=2x,
a diagonal pair satisfies 2b1=2x. Doubling is a bijection on an odd-order
abelian group, so b1=x is the unique possible diagonal. It occurs exactly
when x belongs to B. QED.

This is the finite-group version of the ordered-parent parity identity in
THM-2443, whose exact repository path is recorded in the
[inheritance ledger](literature-and-inheritance.md). It is also the identity
(sum_x 1_B(x) X^x)^2=sum_x 1_B(x) X^(2x) in the group algebra over F_2.
The indicator values are binary, so recovering them modulo 2 recovers them
completely.

Known center is essential. If B is symmetric about c rather than 0, the
reconstruction becomes

  1_B(x)=J_B(2(x-c)) modulo 2.

If c is not supplied, autocorrelation remains translation-invariant and
cannot determine the absolute location of the set. For the twin-center
wheels the forbidden set is already centered at 0, so there is no such
ambiguity.

For even-order groups the general statement is instead

  J_B(t) modulo 2 = #{x in B : 2x=t} modulo 2.         (7)

It only reveals the parity of occupancy in a doubling fiber. On the full
binary vector space F_2^G, this pushforward has rank |2G| and kernel dimension
|G|-|2G|: every nonempty fiber supplies one independent parity bit, and the
fibers are disjoint. This is the exact loss kernel of the parity measurement,
not a claim about the kernel of the full integer autocorrelation. The
order-six example shows that genuine full-correlation ambiguity can occur
too.

This gives a precise hierarchy:

- The full odd-wheel correlation retains the actual symmetric residue set.
- Its fourth-moment compression retains energy but can lose the conditional
  bias sign, as Theorem 1 shows.
- An even wheel can lose the symmetric set even before moment compression.

Count parity under swapping is not the sieve parity obstruction concerning
the parity of the number of prime factors. The exact reconstruction theorem
does not give a prime-factorization statistic or establish prime pairs.
It identifies what information the finite residue kernel itself contains.

## 6. Why this matters to the prime-pair programme

The twin-center forbidden pair is sum-free at every sieve prime >=5.
That explains its positive local conditional surplus. The classification
shows both how special this mechanism is and exactly how it fails for a
slightly richer symmetric pattern.

- A fourth moment can measure the size of local fluctuations while missing
  the sign of target conditioning.
- Connectivity prevents the simplest two-residue counterfeit, but does not
  repair the implication at four residues.
- Prime-order assumptions eliminate subgroup artifacts but still leave the
  degree-four obstruction at every prime >=13.
- Replacing a symmetric relation by a tournament would hide the relevant
  cubic and quartic self-adjoint spectral data rather than strengthen them.

The broader research question is now sharper: which additional constraints
on a forbidden pattern force the nontrivial cubic spectral sum to have one
sign? Sum-freeness is one sufficient condition; energy by itself is not.
All results above concern finite residue models and graph structure. None
converts these local models into an actual-prime correlation theorem.

## Reproduction

Run `python forbidden_pattern_checks.py` in this directory. The output
`forbidden_pattern_checks.json` contains the exhaustive small cyclic census,
the explicit order-six and order-twelve witnesses, and verification of (2)
for every prime 11<=p<=499 and every allowed ratio a. Formula proofs above
are unrestricted in p. The script additionally checks the reconstruction identity for every
symmetric subset, with or without 0, of every odd cyclic group of order
3 through 27. The finite checks are supporting controls, not the basis of
an all-prime inference.
