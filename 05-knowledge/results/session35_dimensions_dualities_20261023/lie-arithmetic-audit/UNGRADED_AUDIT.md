## Independently checked argument

Let s be complex simple, V nontrivial irreducible with invariant nondegenerate alternating form, n=dim V, a=dim s, and g=s⋉(V⊕Cz) with the original nonzero Heisenberg bracket. Put d0=a+2n+2. Consider an arbitrary finite-dimensional complex quadratic Lie algebra L containing g, with no grading assumptions.

First V is not the adjoint s-module: the adjoint has only scalar multiples of the symmetric Killing form as invariant bilinear forms, so has no invariant symplectic form. The usual invariant-form argument on g therefore generalizes. Its restriction is scalar Killing on s and zero on H=V⊕Cz. If that scalar vanishes, g is totally isotropic, so dim L≥2dim g=d0+a>d0+1. In either dimension d0 or d0+1 the scalar is consequently nonzero.

The equivariant surjection L→H* from the ambient metric has kernel H-perp. The kernel contains g and has dimension dim g+e, where e=dim L−d0∈{0,1}. Complete reducibility of s-modules then gives L≅s⊕(V⊗C²)⊕T, with T trivial of dimension2+e. The three isotypic summands are mutually orthogonal and individually nondegenerate. In particular T=centralizer_L(s) is itself a quadratic Lie subalgebra. The multiplicity-space metric in the V-isotypic component is alternating because the V-factor's invariant form is alternating while the ambient form is symmetric.

Let R be the solvable radical. As an s-submodule it cannot contain the unique adjoint copy, since that copy is s. Hence B(R,s)=0. For r∈R,l∈L,x∈s, invariance gives B(r,[l,x])=B([r,l],x)=0, as [r,l]∈R. Thus R is orthogonal to [L,s]=s⊕(V⊗C²), so R⊂T. This excludes arbitrary mixed or one-copy V components; irreducibility of the whole multiplicity-two component is not assumed.

A quadratic Lie algebra of dimension2 is abelian: its bracket tensor B([x,y],z) is alternating of degree3 and therefore zero. In dimension3, the tensor is either zero (abelian) or a nonzero volume form. Over C choose an orthonormal metric basis; the latter bracket is a nonzero multiple of the standard cross-product bracket, yielding so3(C)≅sl2(C). Thus T is abelian or, only in dimension3, sl2. If T=sl2, its solvable ideal R must vanish. If T is abelian, R commutes with T and s. Also [R,V⊗C²]⊂R⊂T, but s-equivariance admits no nonzero map from V to a trivial module. Thus R is central.

For central R, Levi decomposition yields L=S⊕R with S semisimple. Perfectness of S implies B(S,R)=0, and nondegeneracy makes B|S nondegenerate. Perfectness of g gives g⊂[L,L]=S. At dimension d0, a nonzero R would produce a metric completion below the basic lower bound d0. At dimension d0+1, once dimension d0 has been excluded, any nonzero R likewise gives a smaller forbidden completion. Therefore in either stage L is semisimple.

The unique adjoint s-copy puts s wholly into one simple ideal I: two nonzero projections would supply two adjoint copies. Other simple ideals commute with s, so all V-isotypic summands lie in I; the original Heisenberg bracket puts z in I. Thus g⊂I. Distinct simple ideals are metric-orthogonal, so I is itself a quadratic completion. A proper I contradicts the lower bound at d0, and the already excluded dimensions at d0+1. Hence L is simple.

The centralizer action T→End_s(V⊗C²)=End(C²) preserves the alternating multiplicity form, so lands in sp2=sl2. Faithfulness requires a sentence: the subalgebra generated by s and the full V-isotypic component is stable under T and hence an ideal of L, so simplicity makes it all of L. A centralizer element acting trivially on V then centralizes these generators and is zero.

At d0, T would be a two-dimensional abelian subalgebra of sl2, impossible: the centralizer in sl2 of any nonzero element has dimension1. Hence dim L≥d0+1 without grading. At equality, the same reasoning proves simplicity and injects three-dimensional T into sl2, forcing T≅sl2. For s=E7,V56 the lower bound248 is realized by E8; the simple248 classification then identifies every equality completion with E8 as an abstract complex Lie algebra.

This argument does not require the embedded g to be an ideal, a grading, or a homogeneous metric. It does require an injective Lie embedding retaining the original Heisenberg bracket, characteristic zero, finite dimension, and a nondegenerate invariant symmetric ambient form.


## Final-note refinements and automatic grading

The final producer proof is slightly shorter than the mechanism above. At dimension N, T is abelian, so ker(rho) is central even before simplicity is proved. Semisimplicity alone gives injectivity, excluding abelian dimension2 inside sl2. At equality, after T=sl2, the unique simple ideal containing s and W also contains original z. All other ideals then have combined dimension at most2, impossible for a nonzero semisimple summand. Both refinements are valid.

The action rho at equality is nonzero: otherwise invariance makes B([W,W],T)=0, contradicting nonzero original z in [W,W] intersect T and nondegeneracy of B|T. Hence rho is an isomorphism sl2→sl2. The original V corresponds to a multiplicity line ell. The nonzero matrix rho(z) kills ell, so it is rank-one nilpotent with image and kernel ell. Completing it to an sl2 triple produces h with weights+1 on ell,−1 on a complement, and [h,z]=2z. The eigenspace grading extends original g degrees0,1,2, and invariance ensures metric homogeneity. This is an automatic grading at equality, not a recovered hypothesis imposed on L.

The constructive symplectic family in §10 also passes. With Omega(e+,e−)=1, N_u(e−)=u and N_u(v)=omega(u,v)e+ give symplectic endomorphisms. Their bracket on e− is2omega(u,v)e+, exactly omega(u,v)Z under the specified factor2 normalization. All other bracket coordinates agree. The dimension identity realizes equality for s=sp(2m), standard V, every m≥1; this does not assert equality for arbitrary s,V. The H57 control dimensions1596,1653,1711 are correct.

Independent executable ungraded_independent_checks.py verifies symbolic sl2 centralizer rank using minors−4a²,2b²,2c², so every nonzero traceless matrix has centralizer dimension1. It also independently builds all classical N_u matrices for m1..5 and verifies every pair bracket and symplectic identity. These exact controls are not substitutes for the complete module/radical proof.

Verdict: the grading-free minimum and equality rigidity are supported under the exact complex finite-dimensional Lie-embedding hypotheses. The initial graded results remain valid special cases. A nonisomorphic248-dimensional contraction retaining the embedded original g and a nondegenerate invariant metric is excluded even if its original grading is forgotten.
