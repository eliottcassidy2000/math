## 3. Exact fixed-k asymptotic morphology

Write A(u)=sum_(L>=2)c_L u^L and

F_n(k)=sum_(r=1,...,floor(k/2)) [u^k]A(u)^r * binom(n-k,r),

for k>=2 and n>=k+1. This is a finite exact formula. With k fixed it is eventually a polynomial in n of degree floor(k/2), with positive leading coefficient. For k=0 the count is1; k=1 is impossible.

If k=2r, the maximal-run term is exactly binom(n-2r,r): all r runs have width2 and each has weight1. Therefore

F_n(2r) ~ n^r/r!.

If k=2r+1, the maximal-run term is exactly5r binom(n-2r-1,r): one run has width3 and the other r-1 runs width2; its position has r choices and its internal cover has5 choices. Therefore

F_n(2r+1) ~ 5n^r/(r-1)!.

Under the **uniform distribution conditioned on exactly k good cuts**, maximal-run morphology has probability1-O_k(1/n). All profiles with fewer runs have one lower polynomial degree or less. For k2 and k3 the maximal morphology is already certain for every admissible n. For odd k, conditional on the maximal morphology each ordered location of the width3 run has the same weight, and each of its five interval-cover choices is equiprobable. These are fixed-k conditional statements, not claims about an unconditioned random tournament.

Under the uniform distribution on all path-fixed tilings, P(exactly k good cuts)=F_n(k)/2^binom(n-1,2). Fixed-k configurations are exponentially rare; conditional fragmentation must not be conflated with a typical unconditioned sample. No uniformity in growing k follows from these asymptotics.

The placement binomial can be checked without generating functions: r good runs use k cuts and need r-1 separating absent cuts. Distributing the remaining n-k-r absent cuts among r+1 gaps gives binom(n-k,r).
