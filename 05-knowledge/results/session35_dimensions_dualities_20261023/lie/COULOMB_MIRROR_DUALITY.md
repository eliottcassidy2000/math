Define, for t∈C×,

    c(t)=13−6(t+t^(−1))=1−6(t−1)^2/t.

This is the usual central-charge parametrization normalized so t=p/q gives c_(p,q)=1−6(p−q)^2/(pq). Everything involving the function itself is proved below, independent of the representation-theoretic interpretation.

## 2. Exact arithmetic classification

The parameter obeys

    6t^2+(c−13)t+6=0,
    t=(13−c ± sqrt((c−1)(c−25)))/12.

Therefore:

1. t is algebraic over Q if and only if c(t) is algebraic over Q.
2. t is transcendental if and only if c(t) is transcendental.
3. For c∈Q, both reciprocal parameters are rational exactly when D=(c−1)(c−25) is a square in Q; otherwise they have algebraic degree2 over Q.
4. More generally [Q(t):Q(c)]≤2 whenever the field-degree expression is interpreted over the subfield generated by c.

Proof: rational-function evaluation preserves algebraicity in one direction; the quadratic equation and transitivity of algebraicity give the other. The quadratic formula proves the rational criterion. Thus rationality can be lost under the inverse coordinate map, but algebraicity cannot.

Examples:

- c=−5: D=180, t=(3±sqrt5)/2, irrational positive quadratic parameters.
- c=1/2: t=3/4 or4/3, the Ising minimal-model parameter.
- c=13: t=±i, nonreal algebraic parameters despite real rational c.
- t=2: c=−2, showing rational t does not by itself guarantee rationality of the simple Virasoro VOA.

The words real and transcendental are not complementary: there are real algebraic numbers and real transcendental numbers, as well as nonreal examples of both arithmetic types.

## 3. Real locus and branch geometry

For t=x+iy≠0,

    Im(c(t))=−6y(1−1/|t|^2).

Consequently c(t) is real if and only if t is real or |t|=1. More precisely:

- t>0 gives c≤1, with equality only t=1.
- |t|=1 gives c=13−12cosθ∈[1,25].
- t<0 gives c≥25, with equality only t=−1.

Conversely these three ranges describe the complete inverse image of the real c-axis, with overlaps only at the endpoints.

This is the Joukowski quotient, followed by an affine change of coordinate. The involution is t↔1/t. It has fixed points±1, mapping to branch values1 and25, as seen directly from

    c−1=−6(t−1)^2/t,   c−25=−6(t+1)^2/t.

The map extends to a degree-two map P1→P1, with t=0 and∞ both mapping to∞ and unramified there. On C× its invariant Laurent-polynomial algebra is C[t+t^−1]: every symmetric Laurent polynomial is a polynomial in u=t+t^−1, using S_n=t^n+t^−n and S_n=uS_(n−1)−S_(n−2). Thus c is a genuine quotient coordinate, not just a numerical symmetry.

## 4. Rational VOA is a different predicate

CITED: Wang's Virasoro rationality classification is explicitly restated as Theorem2.1 in Huang, *Virasoro vertex operator algebras, the (nonmeromorphic) operator product expansion and the tensor product theory*, pp15–16: L(c,0) is rational precisely at the minimal-model values for coprime p,q>1 (including the trivial c=0 case p,q=2,3). The theorem also supplies the minimal-model fusion rules. Huang notes rationality of finite tensor products immediately afterward. [Primary paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/q-alg/9505020); [Wang publication record](https://academic.oup.com/imrn/article/1993/7/197/759466).

NEW APPLICATION: L(c(t),0) is rational exactly when t is a positive rational with reduced numerator and denominator both at least2. The reciprocal choice makes no difference. The boundary ratios1 and integer/reciprocal-integer ratios fail this test. In particular L(−5,0), L(−2,0), L(1,0), and L(57,0) are not rational.

This statement concerns the **simple Virasoro VOA**, not all VOAs with that central charge. Explicit hostile controls:

- Let Y=L(−22/5,0) be the Lee–Yang minimal model and I=L(1/2,0) the Ising minimal model. The rational tensor-product VOA Y^⊗5⊗I^⊗34 has central charge−5.
- I^⊗114 is rational and has central charge57, while L(57,0) is not rational.

Both follow by central-charge additivity and the cited tensor-product fact. Hence even fixing the exact central charge does not determine rationality when the chiral algebra is allowed to change.

A further elementary consequence: the only rational **simple Virasoro** VOA with integer central charge is the trivial c=0 model. Indeed integer c_(p,q) implies pq divides6(p−q)^2. Coprimality gives gcd(pq,(p−q)^2)=1, hence pq divides6. With coprime p,q≥2 this forces {p,q}={2,3}.

Finally, rationality of a VOA does not require rational quantum dimensions. In the unitary Ising model, σ⊗σ=1⊕ψ and ψ⊗ψ=1. Positive quantum dimensions satisfy dψ=1 and dσ^2=2, so dσ=sqrt2. The irrational number is an algebraic dimension of a fusion object, not an infinite vector-space dimension or failure of semisimplicity. These fusion rules are the p=3,q=4 specialization of the cited theorem.

## 5. The actual mirror bridge, not merely an analogous involution

Let 1<x<e<y be a distinct mirror pair and set t=y/x>1. The equality of logarithmic levels gives

    t log x = log x + log t,
    x=X(t)=t^(1/(t−1)),
    y=Y(t)=t^(t/(t−1))=tX(t).

Conversely these formulas give a mirror pair for every t>1. Positivity and the inequalities log t<t−1 and log t>1−1/t prove 1<X(t)<e<Y(t). The reciprocal substitution exchanges the bases exactly:

    X(1/t)=Y(t),   Y(1/t)=X(t).

Thus the Coulomb reciprocal coordinate really parametrizes the mirror involution. Since c'(t)=−6(1−t^−2)<0 for t>1, c is a bijective real coordinate from unordered distinct mirror pairs to (−∞,1). The coalescence t→1 gives X,Y→e and c→1.

Source→target ledger:

- Source: unordered positive distinct mirror pair {x,y}.
- Intermediate coordinate: reciprocal pair {y/x,x/y}.
- Target: c=13−6(y/x+x/y).
- Preserved: unordered mirror pair, via the inverse formulas and positive-real branch.
- Lost: which base was designated the original input.
- Sidecar: branch/input orientation when an ordered pair is needed.
- Outside scope: this change of coordinate is not a functor between VOA representation categories and mirror forests.

## 6. New arithmetic dichotomy for rational quotient coordinate

**Theorem.** Let c<1 be rational. Let t>1 be its reciprocal-root parameter, and form X(t),Y(t) as above. Then:

- If D=(c−1)(c−25) is a rational square, both mirror bases are algebraic.
- If D is not a rational square, both mirror bases are transcendental.

Proof. In the square case t is a positive rational other than1 and 1/(t−1) is rational; the positive real rational power of a positive rational is algebraic. In the nonsquare case t is algebraic irrational; then 1/(t−1) is also algebraic irrational. The Gelfond–Schneider theorem makes X=t^(1/(t−1)) transcendental. Since t is nonzero algebraic, Y=tX is also transcendental. QED.

CITED input: the classical Gelfond–Schneider theorem, already used with exact scope in the session31 mirror note. The inherited primary record is [Gelfond1934](https://www.mathnet.ru/rus/im4924); direct retrieval timed out again during this pass, so this is use of the standard theorem, not a new audit of its original proof.

Concrete cases:

- c=−2 gives t=2 and the integer mirror pair(2,4).
- c=−7 gives t=3 and the algebraic irrational pair(sqrt3,3sqrt3).
- c=−5 gives t=(3+sqrt5)/2 and a provably transcendental mirror pair.
- c=1/2 gives t=4/3 and the rational pair(64/27,256/81).
- The excluded endpoint c=1 has limiting pair(e,e), transcendental despite rational t=1. The formula's exponent is singular there and must not be substituted directly.

Thus the same rational c=−5 simultaneously labels a nonrational simple Virasoro algebra, an explicitly constructible rational extended VOA, a quadratic irrational Coulomb parameter, and a transcendental mirror pair. The maps and predicates are different, so these are consistent statements rather than a paradox.

## 7. Limits and next question

No assertion is made that arbitrary transcendental t forces transcendental mirror bases. In fact, the inherited integer-input theorem gives pairs with one integer base and one transcendental base, whose ratio and c-coordinate are transcendental. The algebraicity equivalence holds for the rational map t↦c, not for the transcendental power map t↦X(t).

A well-posed extension would ask what additional arithmetic or categorical data survives the exact quotient map, rather than infer Langlands duality from t↔1/t. The present result is already an explicit bridge between the prior mirror involution and the central-charge parameter, with a complete rational-c arithmetic classification and its sharp branch-point exception.
