# The exact noncontracting Collatz language has sharp entropy at an irrational boundary, but is not regular

Let T be the ordinary plus shortcut map on the 2-adic integers: even x maps to x/2 and odd x to (3x+1)/2. Put p=log(2)/log(3) and let h₂ denote binary entropy (base 2). For a parity word w, write S_j for the number of odd letters in its first j positions. Let L be the finite words all of whose prefixes have S_j≥pj, and X the points in the 2-adic integers all of whose parity prefixes lie in L. These are exactly the points whose prefix affine slopes never contract, not an assertion that a slope-contracting prefix descends at every integer on its cylinder.

## 1. The exact Hausdorff dimension is h₂(p)

The map sending a 2-adic integer to its sequence of shortcut parities is a bijective isometry for the cylinder metric (distance 2^(−k) at common-prefix length k). The proof is recursive: on a common length-k cylinder the first k steps are affine of slope 3^S_k/2^k, so the two lifts modulo 2^(k+1) have opposite parities at step k. This also ensures that every infinite parity sequence is realized by exactly one 2-adic point.

For the upper bound, level k needs at most sum_{s≥pk} binom(k,s) cylinders of diameter 2^(−k). The logarithm of this bound divided by k tends to h₂(p), by the elementary binomial entropy bound and Stirling's formula.

For the lower bound take m≥1 and let b=ceil(pm). Among words of length m and weight b, at least 1/m have every prefix on or above the line of slope b/m: rotate at a minimum of S_j−bj/m. This works for periodic words as well, since each orbit has size at most m and at least one good rotation. Concatenating any sequence of such good blocks stays in the survivor set: completed blocks have nonnegative drift relative to p, and each partial block does as well. The equal-weight product measure on the block alphabet of size M≥binom(m,b)/m gives dimension log₂(M)/m. Indeed a length-k cylinder has mass at most M^(−floor(k/m)), which is at most M times its diameter to that exponent. The mass bound yields the lower bound by summing over covers. Letting m grow gives h₂(p). In particular this is a measure-zero 2-adic set of positive dimension, not a finite set of exceptional trajectories.

## 2. No exact finite-state isolator for L, and any regular sublanguage loses entropy

For an infinite regular language, trim a finite deterministic automaton to vertices reachable from its initial state and able to reach an accepting state. There is a cycle. Give edges weight 0 or 1 according to their label. The infimum limiting fraction of ones over arbitrarily long accepted words equals the smallest mean weight among the finitely many simple cycles of this trimmed graph. For the lower bound, decompose an accepting walk into cycles and a residual walk of bounded length; for the upper bound, pump a minimum cycle between a fixed entry and accepting exit. Thus this asymptotic minimum density is rational. The language L has minimum density p: the lower bound follows by its definition, and the mechanical word with cumulative counts ceil(pj) shows equality in the limit. Since p is irrational (unique prime factorization), L is not regular.

More strongly, every infinite regular sublanguage of L has minimum cycle mean q>p. In that automaton each accepted length-k word has at least qk−O(1) ones by the same decomposition. Its exponential counting rate is therefore at most h₂(q)<h₂(p), including the case q=1. Finite-state safe underapproximations can approach h₂(p) via the good-block languages above but can never attain the full rate. This is a theorem about exact prefix-slope language recognition. It does not rule out finite-state approximate tests, a transducer with auxiliary counters, or any ordinary Collatz proof.

## 3. Periodic shadows are dense and cannot be positive counterexamples

Every admitted prefix extends by 1 forever. The resulting 2-adic point is rational and negative: after the prefix its value is the all-ones fixed point −1, and inverting the prefix affine map subtracts a nonnegative carry before dividing by a positive slope. More generally, every eventually periodic parity vector in X describes a strictly negative rational. For a periodic tail with period m and b ones the tail slope must be ≥1, otherwise its repetitions would eventually consume the finite prefix surplus. Equality is impossible for m>0, so the tail is the negative rational fixed point of its affine map, whose carry is positive since b≥1. Inverting the prefix again leaves a negative rational. This explains how a compact infinite survivor can contain periodic witnesses densely while those witnesses cannot supply a positive cycle or counterexample. Every individual finite cylinder also contains arbitrarily large positive integers; taking the intersection does not preserve that statement.

## 4. Even a single-stack grammar cannot capture the exact observer

The Parikh image (counts of zeros and ones) of L is exactly the integer points (z,o) with o≥p(z+o). Necessity follows from the final prefix; sufficiency is realized by all ones before all zeros. This set is not semilinear. Indeed, if it were a finite union of linear sets, every nonzero generator of any of their linear parts would have ratio o/(z+o) at least p, since its multiples within that part can be arbitrarily large. As those ratios are rational, their finite minimum would be strictly greater than p. Finite offsets cannot lower the limiting ratio below that minimum. The mechanical prefixes contradict this. By [Parikh's theorem](https://doi.org/10.1145/321356.321364) the language is not context-free either. This sharpens the finite-state obstruction: even an exact context-free production system over the two emitted parity symbols does not suffice. It does not restrict more powerful arithmetic grammars with guards.

## 5. A necessary boundary for a genuine ordinary Collatz escape

If a positive ordinary shortcut orbit never repeats, its integer values x_i tend to infinity: every bounded set is finite, so it is visited only finitely many times. The exact accumulated identity is

    log(x_k/x_0) = S_k log 3 − k log 2 + ∑_{i<k, x_i odd} log(1+1/(3x_i)).

The sum is o(k), by Cesàro averaging, so such an orbit necessarily satisfies liminf S_k/k≥p. It need not lie in X, which imposes an inequality at each prefix, not just an asymptotic lower bound. This is why the entropy computation cannot by itself rule out escape: it describes an exact slope language and gives a limiting necessary arithmetic feature, not an integral orbit existence or exclusion theorem.
