# Sharp visit-depth hierarchy for context-safe block substitution

## 1. Which semantic states can be merged? Exact rank hierarchy

For nonempty tournaments B let F_j(B) be the number of ordered spanning covers by j nonempty directed paths. Let Q be an arbitrary fixed tournament with a distinguished vertex v; substitute B for v, retaining the other vertices as singletons. The exterior budget is |Q|−1. Define the normalized response of B as H(Q[v←B])/F_1(B), where H=F_1. The following equivalence holds for every integer m≥0:

> Two blocks have identical normalized response in **every** context with at most m exterior vertices if and only if their ratios F_j/F_1 agree for j≤⌊m/2⌋+1.

Indeed, collapse each maximal consecutive B-run in a Hamilton path to v. Every other vertex occurs once, and between two runs there must be at least **two** exterior vertices: with uniform incidence a single x cannot both receive from and send into B. For a fixed reduced word with j v occurrences, its fillings are exactly the ordered F_j covers, because external joins are uniform. Therefore H=Σ a_j(Q)F_j(B), with a_j independent of B and a_j=0 above the claimed bound. This is the one-variable Hamiltonian slice of the general quotient walk-content kernel [THM-3121](../../../01-canon/theorems/THM-3121-path-cover-walk-content-substitution-kernel.md); the novelty here is the sharp converse on a *budgeted observational equivalence*.

For the converse use the probe Q whose substitution is the cyclic join C3[B,TT_(j−1),TT_(j−1)] for j≥2. Its exterior budget is exactly 2(j−1). The word (vab)^(j−1)v is legal, with a and b vertices taken in their respective transitive orders, so a_j>0. The lower coordinates are already known by induction, and higher coordinates cannot occur by the bound. Thus the probe recovers the jth ratio. For j=1 the ratio is tautologically 1. These probes are minimal-size *universal linear detectors* of each new coordinate, whether or not a particular pair realizes the lower-coordinate coincidence.

There is also an **exact support law for each context**. Let μ be the size of a maximum matching in the bipartite arc graph from N⁺(v) to N⁻(v). Each excursion from B back to B chooses an arc from the first to the second side somewhere along its exterior segment, and disjoint segments force disjoint endpoints, giving a_j=0 for j>μ+1. Conversely, choose j−1 disjoint arcs x_i→y_i of such a matching. Replace v temporarily by j transitive twins b_0,…,b_(j−1); the path b_0,x_1,y_1,b_1,…,x_(j−1),y_(j−1),b_(j−1) uses them in separate runs. Insert each unused exterior vertex using the tournament path insertion lemma. Inserting a vertex into a directed path preserves the order of all vertices already present. Therefore each separator x_i,y_i remains between its two twins and all j twin runs remain distinct. Compressing back to v gives a legal reduced word with j occurrences. Hence a_j>0 exactly for 1≤j≤μ+1. This is a profile-support refinement of the two-visit guard in Session 303, not a new claim about the full substitution kernel.

**Consequence for a reverse-block compiler.** Treating a block as its scalar count is exact only while every certified context sees a single visit; treating it by the S305 normalized two-cover clock is exact for contexts seeing at most two visits. A purported neutral repair that can later expose an additional visit must either verify the next coordinate or prove a structural bound on the available excursions. Counting more small tournaments does not repair this logical gap.

## 2. Explicit fixed-order collision

Session 305 gave order-seven code 245600 and 168491, with (H,F₂,F₃)=(29,522,3402) and (33,594,3714) respectively. Both normalized F₂ are 18, but the normalized F₃ differ. Hence these are indistinguishable with ≤3 arbitrary exterior vertices, not merely in the original one-apex family, and the four-vertex detector is sharp. For four outsiders in two transitive groups of size two, the probe has (a₁,a₂,a₃)=(11,17,4). To see this without data, enumerate the three possible starting letters for a cyclic word; the transitive side blocks have first two ordered-cover counts 1 and 2. The equality or failure is about normalized observation, not isomorphism of the blocks.
