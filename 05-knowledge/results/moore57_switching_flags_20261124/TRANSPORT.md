# Moore(57,2): the sheet transport cannot have a solvable grammar

**PROVED implications, conditional on existence.** A degree-57 diameter-two Moore graph would have 3250 vertices. No construction or nonexistence proof is claimed.

## Rooted transport and the lifted tournament gauge

Fix a vertex o. Its 57 neighbors are independent. Their second-neighborhood fibers each have 56 vertices. Between any two different fibers there is exactly one perfect matching: a leaf needs 56 other leaf neighbors, at most one in each other fiber by absence of 4-cycles. Let P_ij be the square permutation matrix for that matching, with P_ji=P_ij^t and P_ii=0. Then

    P_ij + sum_{k != i,j} P_ik P_kj = J_56,   i != j.                 (1)

Indeed any distinct leaves from different fibers have exactly one route of length at most two. If (1) holds, the matching blocks and the prescribed root/parent edges give a degree-57 graph with A²+A=56I+J, so (1) is also sufficient. This formulation is in [Smith–Montemanni, cyclic derangements](https://doi.org/10.3390/axioms15050332) and see also [Less is Moore](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.04815v1) for related root deletion and search.

Independent relabelings of the 56 sheets send P_ij to g_i P_ij g_j^(-1). Normalize P_0j=I for all j by choosing labels via the matching to fiber 0. The group K generated by all resulting blocks is the group of sheet transports around closed walks based at fiber 0, determined up to conjugacy. The normalized P_ij, i,j != 0, are triangle holonomies. This is the exact analogue of [tournament switching gauges](../chanpak_tournament_sl2_20261124/PROJECTIVE-SWITCHING.md): edge labels are not intrinsic but transport around a closed word is. The analogy ends there: here (1) is entrywise over the integers, not a character sum or a modular reachability test.

## A dimension obstruction which precedes search

For every K-invariant subspace U of the 55-dimensional centered permutation module over Q, or over R, we have

    5 divides dim U.                                                   (2)

Let B be the 57-by-57 block matrix of all P. The direct sum U^57 is B-invariant. By (1), B²+B equals 56I on U^57, so its eigenvalues there are 7 and -8. Its trace is zero because every diagonal block is zero. Writing d=dim U, their multiplicities are 152d/5 and 133d/5. This proves (2).

The holonomy group is transitive: the 56 permutations in (1) send each fixed sheet to every sheet exactly once. If there is a nontrivial K-invariant partition with b blocks, transitivity gives equal sizes and b divides 56; its centered block-constant subspace has dimension b-1. The only divisors of 56 congruent to 1 mod 5 are 1 and 56. Hence K is **primitive**.

In fact K is **nonsolvable**. In a faithful solvable primitive permutation group a minimal nontrivial normal subgroup is elementary abelian and transitive: its orbits are a block system, and singleton orbits would contradict faithfulness. A transitive abelian subgroup acts regularly, since all its stabilizers agree, so the permutation degree would be a prime power. The degree 56 is not.

Consequences:
- This excludes *every* regular group-voltage assignment on 56 sheets, cyclic or not. The May 2026 [cyclic-derangement paper](https://doi.org/10.3390/axioms15050332) rules out the cyclic case and in §7 leaves the other semiregular group cases as a separate possibility. It also excludes arbitrary imprimitive (possibly nonsolvable) hierarchical seam models.
- It excludes transports in an automorphism group of a tournament, since that group has odd order and cannot act transitively on 56 sheets. Even augmenting by global converse yields a group of order with 2-adic valuation at most one, still too small for transitivity on 56.
- It excludes PGL(2,7) on the 56 ordered distinct projective pairs, not by its order but because the projection to the first coordinate gives eight blocks of seven. The *unordered* 3-subset action of A8 or S8 is primitive, but also fails: the point-incidence map sends x in Q^8 of sum 0 to (sum of x_i over each triple). This injective map (compare triples differing in one point) gives a forbidden invariant subspace of dimension seven.
- By the [GAP PrimGrp catalogue](https://gap-packages.github.io/primgrp/doc/chap1.html), the remaining degree-56 options are A56, S56, or one of the five primitive Gewirtz-group actions with socle PSL(3,4). The 20- and 35-dimensional Gewirtz eigenspaces satisfy (2); this test alone does not exclude that action. The [PG(2,4) incidence filter](GEWIRTZ.md) does exclude its collineation-only subgroup and further constrains mixed transports. GAP replay: `List([1..NrPrimitiveGroups(56)], i -> [ i, Size(PrimitiveGroup(56,i)), StructureDescription(Socle(PrimitiveGroup(56,i))) ]);` (StructureDescription for the giant groups can be replaced by checking Contains/IsAlternatingGroup or by comparing orders 56!/2 and 56!.)
- The graph neighborhood pointwise stabilizer is trivial by a separate short check. An automorphism fixing o and all its neighbors, if it fixes a sheet in any one fiber, fixes every sheet in every other fiber by the 56 routes in (1), and then all sheets. Otherwise it fixes exactly 58 vertices and moves each moved vertex within an independent fiber, so tr(gA)=0. If t_7,t_-8 are its traces on the rational eigenspaces of A, then t_7+t_-8=57 and 57+7t_7-8t_-8=0, giving t_7=399/15, not an integer. Each t is an integer because it is rational and an algebraic integer. This stabilizer statement concerns the graph, not the holonomy group: the latter is not presumed to act on the graph.

## Edge-rooted second chart: the row and column ports

Mark the root edge rs. Delete both endpoints and all their first neighbors. The remaining 3136 vertices are the cells (i,a) of a 56 by 56 array: i is the unique neighbor of r on the distance-two route; a is the unique neighbor of s on the distance-two route. In the normalized chart above choose s as parent 0, so a is the matching label via fiber 0; there is precisely one vertex per cell. Let C be the interior adjacency. It has degree 55, and every row and every column is independent. Every vertex has exactly one interior neighbor in each *other* row and each *other* column. The two coordinate projections therefore hold simultaneously, not just after changing labels between tests.

An exact equation for C is

    C²+C = 55 I + (J_56-I_56) ⊗ (J_56-I_56).                         (3)

Off the diagonal, a pair sharing either coordinate already has its unique two-step path via that port; for two different coordinates any required route is interior. On the diagonal C²=55. Conversely a symmetric zero-diagonal 0/1 array matrix satisfying (3) has degree 55 and no edges in a row/column from the literal zeros of (3); it has at most one neighbor in any given other row/column since two such neighbors would yield a forbidden two-step pair in that row/column. The degree forces one in every other row/column. Reattach the two sets of port parents and the edge rs, and these conditions supply all pairs at distance at most two without duplicates. This is an exact constructive search formulation, not a relaxed target.

Small computational filters on (3): the constant has eigenvalue 55, the centered row- and column-constant spaces (dimension 110 together) have eigenvalue -1, and on the tensor product of two centered 55-spaces the equation is C²+C=56I. Taking the trace shows 7 with multiplicity 1617 and -8 with multiplicity 1408 on the last space. The connection to the repo’s two-port work is that preserving each marginal separately is easy; the entrywise off-diagonal in (3) is the exact joint check, analogous to preserving both endpoint registers under the same tournament contacts. It is not an implication about Hamilton-path counts.

## Two additional tests before a full search

The same 5-divisibility applies to **every invariant centered subspace over F_5** and therefore to its composition factors. Indeed (B−2I)²=0 on U^57 over F_5, so 0=tr(B)=2·57·dim(U) modulo 5. This does not say the construction is modular: it is a necessary filter on a candidate transport group.

There is also a fixed-fiber pentagon identity. Write F_j for the matrix of transport over nonbacktracking length-j walks in the complete graph on the 57 fibers (i.e. a walk may not return immediately along the edge it just used). Then F_1=B, F_2=B²−56I and F_j=B F_(j−1)−55F_(j−2) for j≥3. This follows by canceling the inverse matching at the initial backtrack. Hence (F_5)_ii=(B⁵−221B³)_ii. The only such closed walks of length five with a repeated vertex apart from closure have a root tail i,j,k,l,j,i; classify possible repeats by their separations 2 and 3, and use nonbacktracking to leave only the repeat at positions 1 and 4. Their transport sum is 54(B³)_ii: all 56 choices of j conjugate the complete triangle sum at j, then remove the walks in which i occurs in the triangle (each rooted oriented triangle at i counted twice). Here (B³)_jj=−56 I+56 J is central, by the equations on constants and contrasts. On the same two spaces compute

    sum of transport over simple rooted fiber pentagons
       = (B⁵−275B³)_ii
       = 9072 I + 157248 J.

On contrasts use B³=57B−56I and B⁵=3305B−6328I and B_ii=0. On constants use the row sum 56·55·54·53. Thus from any sheet there are 166320 oriented simple base-pentagon walks taking it back to itself and 157248 to each other sheet. The exact numerical identity is auxiliary to (1), but it gives a target for character constraints in a proposed catalogue action.
