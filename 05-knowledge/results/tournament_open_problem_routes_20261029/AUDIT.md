## Proof P1: adversarial checks

- **Domain:** BOTH large-sieve indices are odd positive squarefree integers;
  prime support satisfies this. Complex coefficients are allowed. Enlarging
  the outer sum preserves the upper bound, not a reverse inequality.
- **Matrix convention:** the restricted large-sieve matrix is K^t; singular
  values agree with K. Quadratic reciprocity is used only for the tournament
  orientation, not for an independence assumption.
- **Clock:** X is numerical prime height, N the number of admitted primes.
  N~X/(2logX) is needed; the abstract CRT extension property is insufficient.
- **Counting:** W has diagonal1/2 solely for the iid homomorphism calculation.
  Colliding vertices are removed with an explicit O_k(1/N) error. With all
  other vertices fixed, one telescoping edge leaves a product f(x)g(y), so
  the operator bound really applies. There is only one arc for each pair.
- **Orbit normalization:** labelled frequencies are2^{-binom(k,2)};
  unlabelled ones are k!/Aut times that. The81/27 frequency ratio is3.
- **Quantifiers:** k is fixed while X grows. The robust subset extension
  requires size at least X^(1/2+eta) for a fixed eta>0. No assertion about
  arbitrary tiny neighborhoods, growing k, exact counts or named ports.
- **Inputs:** classical large sieve and fixed-modulus PNT, both CITED.
  Published input proofs are not independently reverified by our finite code.

## Proof P2: adversarial checks

- Open OUT-neighbourhoods, with all vertex rows allowed, are essential.
  Switching to external witnesses only changes the definition and proof.
- The pair a->b already has patterns{b} and empty from a and b. Two other
  rows supply{a} and{a,b}; they are necessarily distinct.
- An outside vertex beating exactly one triangle vertex is the easy-to-miss
  case: it creates a new triangle dominated on an arc by the old third vertex.
  This is why mere domination of the original triangle is too weak.
- No triangle outside the chosen C3 is possible, because each triangle would
  have to dominate the other. This proves the entire remainder transitive.
- n=1,2 are treated separately; n=3 gives6+2=8 labelled members. At n>=4
  converse changes the second family's VC, and cyclic substitution exits it.
- The finite implementation uses direct row shattering versus an independent
  source-peeling classifier. It does not assume the triangle proof in its
  transitivity routine. All33867 labelled tournaments through six agree.
