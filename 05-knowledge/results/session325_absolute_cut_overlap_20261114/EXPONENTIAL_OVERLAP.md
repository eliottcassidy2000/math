# Exact intersections of exponential cut charts

A cut chart is specified by actual tournaments and a parameter domain on which its formula is valid. The theorem here decides the form of **absolute equality of outputs**, not equivalence of the output tournaments. Any extra requirements on root states must also be imposed.

## 1. A finite affine-lattice cover of the exact equality set

Let the two chart outputs be finite sums of monomials with rational coefficients and positive rational bases raised to integer affine functions of their separate integer parameters. Parameters are nonnegative integers, or are restricted by an explicit semilinear guard (a finite union of translates of finitely generated additive monoids). Then the exact equality pairs form a semilinear set. In particular, the exponents on a fixed single-parameter ray at which it coincides with a fixed finite-exponential chart form an eventually periodic subset of the nonnegative integers. This includes affine binary rails, arbitrary independent lengths at fixed acyclic marked modules, and a fixed finite ordinal SCC bank with arbitrary multiplicities. It does not include arbitrarily many marked variables or arbitrary word-position-dependent coefficients.

**Proof.** Move all terms to the same side, discard zeros, and write the relation as Σ c_i M_i(t)=0. All positive monomial values are in the finitely generated group of rational S-units for a single finite set S of primes. At every solution, partition the term indices into inclusion-minimal zero-sum blocks. There are finitely many partitions. For a given block B choose j∈B and divide by −c_j M_j. The resulting inhomogeneous unit equation is nondegenerate: a proper vanishing subsum would contradict minimality. The [Evertse–Schlickewei–Schmidt theorem](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/2002/155-3/p04) gives finitely many normalized ratio tuples for that block.

For each retained tuple, equality of a monomial ratio with its specified rational value means that all its prime valuations equal specified integers. These are integer affine equations in the chart parameters. Conversely, any parameter tuple satisfying those equations has the same normalized blocks and therefore satisfies the original equality. This is a finite union of affine-lattice constraints on a nonnegative orthant. If a semilinear guard is present, substitute its linear generators as new nonnegative variables first.

For completeness, a set {x∈N^d:Ax=b} is a finite union of translates of {x∈N^d:Ax=0}: take its coordinatewise minimal solutions, finite by Dickson's lemma, and subtract one from any given solution. The homogeneous monoid is generated by its finitely many minimal nonzero elements, by repeated coordinatewise subtraction. Projection preserves finite translates and generating sets. In one dimension a nonzero finitely generated additive monoid contains every sufficiently large multiple of the gcd of its generators: modulo any positive generator its residues form a finite subgroup, and an attained representative in each residue can be padded by that generator. This gives eventual periodicity.

The general finite cover is an existence theorem; the proof does not provide an effective bound on the magnitude of all unit ratios. In particular a failure to find an overlap numerically is not a disjointness certificate.

## 2. An effective version for dyadic monomials

For terms c_i·2^(l_i(t)), clear rational coefficient denominators. In each minimal vanishing block B put C=Σ_(i∈B)|c_i|. If the exponents in the block, sorted at the solution, contain a gap of at least the bit length of C, split at that gap. The high part is an integer multiple of its minimum power of two, while the low part has strictly smaller absolute magnitude. Thus both parts must vanish, contrary to minimality. This argument is unchanged if some exponents are negative: first divide by the block's lowest power of two.

Hence, after choosing a lowest-exponent term j, every offset d_i=l_i−l_j lies in [0,(|B|−1)(bit_length(C)−1)]. Enumerate these finite offsets, keep exactly those with Σ c_i 2^d_i=0, and impose l_i−l_j=d_i for the whole block. Taking the union over partitions gives an effective list of affine systems. One can now test compatibility with any explicit semilinear guard by ordinary integer linear feasibility, or retain the systems as checkable overlap certificates. The elementary bound depends on coefficients and the number of terms, not the largest exponent being tested.

## 3. Two elementary diagnostic examples

**Different fixed offsets have at most two meetings.** Normalize two affine dyadic rays to c+a·2^x and d+b·2^y with a,b odd positive and x,y restricted to their allowed tails. If c=d, they are disjoint unless a=b, in which case the common exponents give the overlap. Otherwise put Δ=d−c, t=v₂(|Δ|), Δ'=Δ/2^t. If x<y then x=t and Δ'=a−b·2^(y−t)≤a−2b. If x>y then y=t and Δ'=a·2^(x−t)−b≥2a−b. Thus only one unequal branch can occur, and its other exponent is found by a single power-of-two test. If x=y there is one separate power-of-two test Δ/(a−b), with no solution if a=b. Both meetings can happen (a=3,b=1,Δ=2 and (x,y)=(0,0),(1,2)). This applies to the S324 fixed-source Busch rails H+L·2^j. A different source count cannot persist merely by retiming a single fixed rail.

**The two fully transitive selective charts never meet.** In S321's two charts take source TT_u and reservoir TT_v. Then N+1=A_gamma(u)·2^v, A_gamma(u)=gamma·3^u+6·2^u−3 odd, gamma=2 or 6. An equality fixes v by its two-adic valuation. Across gamma values it would require 3^u+3·2^u=3^(w+1)+3·2^w. The right side lies strictly between the left expression at u=w and u=w+1; monotonicity excludes all u. Thus apparent agreement in arbitrarily many low dyadic bits does not give even one absolute equality. Changing the source profile, not merely adding transitive vertices on the two sides, is necessary.

## 4. Scope at the Chan–Pak frontier

Every fixed finite-dimensional strong chart whose only choices are lengths of finitely many growing blocks has only O((1+log X)^d) possible absolute counts up to X, by the strong-order bound of Busch. The finite-affine result is about exactly which sparse charts overlap, not an argument that their union is dense. To cover a positive-density lane, the number of useful independently selectable digits must grow, or the remaining targets must be assigned to a genuinely different construction with a proof of entry. The fixed-root word grammars have growing digit count and are not settled by this fixed-dimensional obstruction.
