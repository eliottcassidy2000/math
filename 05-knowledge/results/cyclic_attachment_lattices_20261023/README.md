## 1. Actual comparison relations are cosets

Let the two named cells have supplied internal automorphism groups G_i,G_j,
including any required pointwise marks. After choosing reference internal
isomorphisms from source cells to target cells, let A and B be their actual
binary cross blocks. Define

    R(A,B) = {(g,h): B(g(u),h(v))=A(u,v) for every u,v}.

The equality uses the same whole-cell maps on all incidences. If R is
nonempty and r is one solution, then

    R(A,B) = r H_A,
    H_A = {(g,h): A(g(u),h(v))=A(u,v) for every u,v}.       (1)

Indeed composing r on the right with a source stabilizer preserves the
equation; composing any other solution with r^-1 recovers a source
stabilizer. Thus arbitrary binary CSP relations are a strictly larger
carrier than these actual block-comparison relations.

Put P_i=pr_i(H_A), P_j=pr_j(H_A), and

    K_i={g:(g,1) in H_A},  K_j={h:(1,h) in H_A}.

The kernels are normal in their respective projections. The rule
gK_i -> hK_j for (g,h) in H_A is a well-defined group isomorphism
P_i/K_i -> P_j/K_j. Its full inverse image is H_A. This follows by
multiplying two pairs with the same first or second component and then
lifting a matching quotient class. A nonfunctional relation therefore
consists of independent kernel choices coupled through one common quotient.
It is not an unstructured list of pairs.

Different edges can have DIFFERENT kernels at the same vertex. Their
quotient states cannot be chosen independently: they must arise from one
element of G_i. General nonabelian synchronization is not solved merely by
writing (1). In the cyclic case it becomes the following exact arithmetic.

## 2. Two congruences retain every cyclic block relation

Suppose the allowed cell charts are rotations Z/m and Z/n. Use the convention

    B(u+a,v+b)=A(u,v),                                  (2)

with indices reduced in their named cells. If R is nonempty choose one
offset (r_a,r_b). Its stabilizer H is a subgroup of Z/m x Z/n. The inverse
image L of H in Z^2 contains (m,0) and (0,n). It has a unique basis

    L = <(h,0),(s,v)>,  h,v>0, 0<=s<h,                 (3)
    h|m,  v|n,  h divides s*(n/v).

**Proof.** Let h be the least positive horizontal element and v the least
positive second coordinate of an element of L. Reduce the first coordinate
of an element at height v modulo h to obtain s. Subtracting its multiple
from any element of L leaves a horizontal element. This proves generation
and uniqueness; the period vectors give the displayed divisibilities.

An actual rotation pair belongs to R if and only if

    b = r_b                         (mod v),
    v*a - s*b = v*r_a - s*r_b        (mod h*v).          (4)

The first congruence makes t=(b-r_b)/v integral; the second says
a-r_a-s*t is divisible by h. This proves both directions. Each congruence
is well-defined on the original residue domains because of (3).
The relation has exactly mn/(hv) pairs. Empty relations are explicitly
retained as rejection, not assigned an arbitrary offset.

Three lattice parameters and two offset coordinates describe the relation,
regardless of its fibre sizes. They do not reconstruct A or B: the blocks
must be supplied or independently authenticated. Constructing the record
can still require reading those blocks.

## 3. All-size network theorem

Give cell i its own rotation a_i modulo m_i. For every attachment insert
the two rows (4) using these SAME variables. An actual marked vertex fixes
its rotation after reference identification; insert that congruence too.

**Theorem.** The complete compatible chart set is either empty or

    (a_0 + Lambda) / D Z^t,
    D=diag(m_1,...,m_t),  D Z^t subset Lambda subset Z^t. (5)

It is obtained by intersecting the periodic integer congruences, without
assuming a tree, functional relations, or small cycle rank. A column-Hermite
basis B of Lambda and one a_0 certify the entire set. Its exact size is

    product_i(m_i) / det(B).                            (6)

**Proof.** Each row is a coset of the kernel of a homomorphism from Z^t to
a finite cyclic group. A nonempty intersection is the coset of the
intersection of these kernels. Periodicity gives D Z^t subset Lambda.
The index formula proves (6). An elementary constructive algorithm starts
with a_0=0,B=I. For a new congruence c*a=d mod q, substitute a=a_0+Bz.
Unimodular column reduction turns cB into (g,0,...,0). The one remaining
congruence is soluble iff gcd(g,q) divides d-c*a_0. A Bezout solution updates
the offset; multiplying the first parameter column by q/gcd(g,q) gives
the new kernel lattice. Hermite normalization changes no lattice. This
induction proves soundness, completeness, and detection of empty fibres.

The supplied cyclic chart groups and complete blocks are the hypotheses.
This is compact linear-congruence compilation, not a theorem that every
tournament automorphism group is cyclic or that a few motifs recover its
blocks. General supplied finite abelian chart groups work by choosing cyclic
coordinates and the same lattice argument; the two-row bound is specific
to one cyclic coordinate at each endpoint.

This gives a compact certificate, not merely an implicit solution set.
In column-Hermite form B is upper triangular, with0<=B_ij<B_ii for j>i.
Since m_i e_i belongs to Lambda, back substitution gives B_ii|m_i.
Thus every entry in row i is bounded by m_i, and the whole solution
certificate uses O(t^2 log(max m_i)) bits, plus the edge records and the
retained source data. This does not bound the size of that source data.

For named cells internally equal to the consecutive cyclic tournament R_m^con,
where m is odd and u->u+d for1<=d<=(m-1)/2, rotations ARE the whole internal
automorphism group. An arc of step d has (m-1)/2-d common outneighbors.
The unique maximal-common-neighbor outgoing arc is therefore u->u+1.
Every automorphism preserves this directed cycle and is a rotation.
Thus (5) gives ALL named-cell isomorphisms in this family, not just a chosen
subgroup of them. A Paley seven-cell has a larger group and requires a
different carrier.

## 4. Why the first genuinely nonfunctional cyclic cases are composite

For a nonuniform p-by-p cross block with constant row and column sums,
where p is prime, its rotational stabilizer H<=F_p^2 is either trivial or
a line with both projections injective. A subgroup containing a nonzero
horizontal element makes every row equal. Constant column sums then force
the block to be all zero or all one. The vertical case is identical.
A two-dimensional subgroup also forces uniformity. These exhaust the
subgroups, proving that a nonempty comparison relation is functional.

The equitable hypothesis matters: an arbitrary column-constant binary
block may have a horizontal kernel. At differing coprime cell sizes,
constant row and column sums already force uniformity by counting edges.

At m=n=9 take

    A(u,v)=1 iff u=v (mod3).

It is nonuniform with row and column sums3. Its stabilizer is

    H={(a,b):a=b (mod3)},  |H|=27,
    (h,s,v)=(3,1,1).                                  (7)

Each first chart has three compatible second charts. For three such cells,
give all three edges this relation: there are81 joint choices. Translating
only the final block changes that edge to a_2-a_0=1 mod3, while the other
two enforce a_1=a_0 and a_2=a_1 mod3. Every local relation still has27
pairs, but the global comparison is empty.

These are actual tournament blocks. Put R_9^con inside each cell, and orient
cross arcs in the direction of the displayed 0/1 block (reverse every zero).
The three cell outdegrees are10,13,16, so even an unmarked isomorphism must
preserve the cells. The same construction works at every odd m divisible
by3: the flat comparison has m^3/9 maps, the twisted one none. This is an
unbounded native nonfunctional hostile and a positive family for (5).
It is not a Hamiltonian-port inventory or a metagraph wiring theorem.

### An explicit all-size lift family and its minimum owner cost

Let d>=3 be odd, let every odd m_i be divisible by d, and use R_(m_i)^con
inside the named cells of a connected support graph. On an oriented edge
ij let the block be1 exactly when v-u=c_ij modulo d. Uniform blocks on
nonedges are fixed. A global rotation changes c_ij by a_j-a_i modulo d.

Choose a spanning tree. Its phase differences can all be removed by cell
rotations modulo d. Each remaining chord retains one cycle sum in Z/d,
and those sums are a COMPLETE invariant for the named-cell family. With
beta=|E|-|V|+1, there are exactly d^beta joint classes, while every source
has exactly

    d * product_i(m_i/d)                               (9)

cell-preserving automorphisms. Indeed the common rotation modulo d has d
choices, and each cell has m_i/d independent lifts. This proves both counts
without assuming d prime. The smallest actual-owner base has size

    max(1, #{i:m_i>d}).                               (10)

Every nontrivial cell kernel needs an owner in its own cell. If at least
one such owner is fixed, the common quotient rotation is also fixed; one
owner in each kernel-bearing cell then suffices. If there are no kernels,
the common free C_d action needs exactly one owner. This counts pointwise
marking, not isomorphism classes, and retains all original named cells.

## 5. Gluing displacement necklaces without recycling a rotation

For a matching permutation p on Z/n, retain the ordered word

    d_p(u)=p(u+1)-p(u) mod n.

Incoming [Session40](../session40_marked_ports_square_sum_20261023/PRIME_CELL_BURNSIDE_FORMULAS.md)
already proves the necklace is complete for its two-cell rotation orbit.
For actual source matching p and target matching q, the full comparison
relation is more informative than the bare necklace:

    d_q(u+a)=d_p(u) for every u,
    b=q(a)-p(0).                                      (8)

Necessity follows by differencing q(u+a)=p(u)+b. Conversely the difference
is constant around the cycle, and evaluation at u=0 gives b. This proves
that every necklace alignment has a unique owned lift to a rotation pair.
Use all these pairs in (3)-(5), retaining a_i across its incident edges.

More generally, above fixed local translation-orbits the incoming alignment
quotient extends from vector spaces to finite abelian groups:

    W = direct-sum_e (Z/m_i x Z/m_j)/H_e,
    D: product_i Z/m_i -> W,
    joint classes = W/image(D).                       (11)

If k is the homogeneous solution count from (6), the exact number of
joint classes is product_e(h_e v_e) * k / product_i(m_i). This follows
from the homomorphism theorem, including nonfunctional kernels. The integer
lattice test decides membership in image(D); it is the composite-modulus
extension of the already published prime-field alignment rule, not a new
claim that independent necklace classes alone suffice.

For prime p, an affine matching has p self-comparison rotations; a
nonaffine matching has only the identity. A nonzero translation period of
its difference word would force the whole word constant, proving this
claim. It does not justify replacing arbitrary permutations by affine maps.

Even three identical local necklaces can fail globally: take identity
matchings on all source edges of a three-cell triangle, and in the target
translate only the last matching by1. All difference words remain the
constant1 word. The first two relations force equal chart rotations; the
third forces their difference to be1. This is the functional shadow of
the composite hostile (7), and explains exactly what the necklace quotient
forgets: how its phase is shared with another attachment.
