# Session 331: exact returns, sections, and switch surfaces

Different ways to split a target can agree in value while exposing different ports. This session treats that as a proof problem: determine the interface states, find physical moves that return them, and prove what those moves can and cannot cover.

## A genuine primitive return beyond the old two ports

The [full Fermat-port rule](FERMAT_INTERIOR_PORTS.md) gives a four-coordinate transfer for every new vertex of the block. Its endpoint correction at the **first** interior vertex is essential. For prime-like targets, interior roots can escape the factors imposed by terminal-only returns. Literal controls give the prime 109 and \(623=7\cdot89\).

The [fixed-size return](PRIMITIVE_BINARY_RETURN.md) uses only the three genuine Fermat lengths \(1,2,4\). At a primitive ready state, two rationally invertible prefixes share a size and have incompatible bad lines modulo five. Next come two orders of the same pair of penultimate operations. A ratio readout distinguishes the order, so the entire return can be read *backwards* from the new two-coordinate state. After \(m\) returns it produces exactly \(2p3^{m-1}\) different primitive ready profiles at one order \(n+13m\), where \(p\) is the initial number of safe prefixes. Under a guarded variant the same number all have counts prime to three, spread over only \(m+1\) orders. A sublanguage stays prime to 3,5 and 11 at every fixed-order return, and a separate local theorem reaches every primitive pair modulo every \(7^k\) at the same sufficiently large orders. These are genuine repeatable physical states, not just residues of some unbuilt tournament. They still do not select an arbitrary absolute odd target.

## Two sums, a product, and what survives a section

The count \(713=9+704=23\cdot31\) has three literal order-twelve constructions: one block of size seven, a three-block split \(2+2+3\), and an ordinal product of two six-cores. The equal-total rooted seams have different returned ports. [Rooted split rigidity](ROOTED_SPLIT_RIGIDITY.md) proves at most one such one-versus-three collision per input; a backwards binary reading recovers an entire fixed-size split from its marked response. It also proves a nonzero mixed difference for *any* two genuine edits in separated breakpoint windows, so they cannot provide additive independent digits at a fixed old root.

A different [directed gate](DIRECTED_GATE_PRODUCT.md) really does multiply endpoint counts. It gives order-thirteen strong graphs whose marked counts are \(6=1\cdot6=2\cdot3\) from 4+7 and 5+6 cores, but their unmarked Hamiltonian totals differ. This distinguishes what a measurement preserves from what it hides.

The exact [Lefschetz-inspired section theorem](MATCHING_SECTIONS.md) uses uniform external incidence with a tournament module: a crossing matching, not the whole exterior size, decides the highest observable path-cover layer. Same-size strong probes recover the permitted layers. At the opening layer their output differences obey a factorial divisibility law; the leading coefficient is a permanent, with a whole binary interval of admissible settings at fixed auxiliary size. In addition, [strong graphs generate the entire signed slot lattice](PHYSICAL_LATTICE.md), not just a rational subspace. Neither assertion turns signed combinations into positive sources. The [frontier phase tests](PHASE_AT_THE_FRONTIER.md) provide actual small contexts keeping a 99-unit source unit, with and without third-slot visibility.

## Gauss–Bonnet made into a switch test

The [square-switch surfaces](SQUARE_SWITCH_SURFACES.md) give a literal disk-and-band construction. The Euler characteristic charges the number of proposed switches, but the boundary count is one plus the nullity of their interlacement form. In the same square-sum graph at size 99, the same norm identity \(130=3^2+11^2=7^2+9^2\), even the same local switch, behaves differently in two cyclic orders. The Gaussian factorization explains the matching identity; the intersection form explains whether it closes. The two exact palettes grow under the 25-fold gluing into families with circuit polynomials \(x(3+x)^k\) and \(x(2+2x)^k\), \(k=25^d\). This reconciles the local additive identities with the true product law when blocks are independent.

## Remaining Chan–Pak gap

The new return is injective on *marked primitive profiles*, the sections have exact visibility and divisibility, and the product gates really seal the interfaces they claim. The missing implication is an inverse from **every prescribed absolute odd count above a finite threshold** into the allowed ready states. The explicit sparsity results and fixed-slot granularity explain why another larger finite scan or another unconstrained residue cycle is not that inverse.
