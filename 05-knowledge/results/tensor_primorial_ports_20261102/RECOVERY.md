## Requested arithmetic patterns as collision tests

The statement54-19=35 supplies a precise small residue experiment: in the
offset set{0,19,54}, the last two ports collide modulo5 and7, the first
and last collide modulo2 and3, and the first two collide modulo19.
These are exactly the prime divisors of19*54*35, the product of its
pairwise differences. At an even wheel the original triple is inadmissible
because it contains both parities. Doubling to{0,38,108} removes that
obstruction; the exact unit-translate count modulo2310 is120.

For the literal pattern suggested by(3,5,16+-1), take
S={0,3,5,15,17}. Again parity blocks an all-unit translate at even modulus.
For2S={0,6,10,30,34}, the CRT count modulo2310 is

    (2-1)(3-2)(5-3)(7-4)(11-5)=36.

Thus3,5,7 cause different collision partitions, whereas11 is a
collision-free prime for this pattern. This is an exact interpretation
of the supplied integers, not a recovered historical theorem about that
particular tuple. The general rule(W) explains how the actual collision
blocks, not their names or mere cardinalities, control later contractions.
