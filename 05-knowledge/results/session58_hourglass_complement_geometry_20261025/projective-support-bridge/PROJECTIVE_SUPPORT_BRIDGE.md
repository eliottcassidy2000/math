# Squarefree gap support and the toric projective carrier

Status: PROVED from the audited Gaussian allocation/valuation results named below; FINITE-EXACT checks separately reported. Standard projective and monoid geometry is not claimed as new. No descent of the integer hourglass equations, no new hourglass exclusion, and no RH consequence is asserted.

## 1. Exact arithmetic source

Use `session58-hourglass-prime-support/RESULT.md`, sections 3, 4, 9. Let e=product_{i=1}^k p_i be squarefree, with distinct primes p_i=1 mod 4 and k>=1. Fix their order and Gaussian factors pi_i with pi_i conjugate(pi_i)=p_i. For a nonzero j in {-1,0,1}^k set

z(j)=product_i pi_i^(1+j_i) conjugate(pi_i)^(1-j_i),
d(j)=|2 Re(z(j)) Im(z(j))|.

The inherited theorem proves that j modulo simultaneous negation maps bijectively onto D_e, the positive d with e²-d and e²+d squares. Its proof uses Gaussian unique factorization, and the fact that fixed norm and absolute coordinate product determine the unordered absolute coordinate pair. Units and conjugation produce exactly the relevant equivalence. Reduction modulo 3 identifies this quotient with P^{k-1}(F_3). The bijection depends on prime order and conjugate choices; changes produce coordinate permutations/signs, not arbitrary arithmetic-preserving projective transformations.

For S={i:j_i!=0}, define e_S=product_{i in S}p_i and t=e/e_S. Exact valuation at an active coordinate gives v_{p_i}(d)=0; at an inactive coordinate it gives v_{p_i}(d)>=2. Since e is squarefree,

gcd(e²,d)=t².

Excess balanced valuation is allowed: d=3000 at e=65 has v_5(d)=3, but gcd(65²,3000)=25. The truncation in the gcd is indispensable.

For each fixed nonempty S there are 2^|S| signed vectors and negation acts freely. Thus exactly 2^(|S|-1) gaps have this support, equivalently have gcd(e²,d)=(e/e_S)². In particular the gaps coprime to e are precisely the full-support stratum, of size 2^(k-1).

## 2. Division, not merely a counting coincidence

If T is disjoint from S and u=product_{i in T}p_i, the corresponding Gaussian factors are p_i, so z_e(j)=u z_{e/u}(j with T deleted). Therefore

d_e(j)=u² d_{e/u}(j with T deleted).

This is an exact integer identity preserving the remaining indices. More generally, the entire coordinate subspace j_i=0 for i in T maps bijectively onto D_{e/u} by d -> d/u². It includes every gap in the slice u²|d, by the valuation criterion above. Any actual Schur relation among gaps in this slice survives division, as do positivity and distinctness. This says nothing about whether a Schur relation exists.

The nonempty support subsets form a Boolean incidence poset. In projective space the closure of the exact-support stratum S is the coordinate projective subspace on S, containing all strata R with nonempty R subset S. Arithmetically its gap set is t² D_{e_S}. Thus this entire coordinate-support containment/deletion diagram, not just the total point count, is transported by the indexing bijection. A general projective hyperplane has no such proved divisibility interpretation.

## 3. Genuine standard projective geometry

For any finite field F_q, the exact-support stratum of P^{k-1}(F_q) at nonempty S is (F_q^*)^S/F_q^*. Normalizing one selected nonzero coordinate to 1 identifies it with (F_q^*)^{|S|-1}. The corresponding scheme stratum is the split torus G_m^{|S|-1}. Hence

#stratum_S(F_q)=(q-1)^(|S|-1),
#P^{k-1}(F_q)=sum_{nonempty S}(q-1)^(|S|-1)=1+q+...+q^(k-1).

The first equality counts explicit coordinates; the second follows either by counting all nonzero vectors modulo nonzero scalars or by the binomial identity. At q=3 it recovers the arithmetic support strata exactly. At formal q=1 only singleton supports contribute, giving k coordinate-axis strata. This is a genuine, established toric/F1-adjacent combinatorial carrier: the geometry P^{k-1}_Z and its standard toric model exist independently of the hourglass problem.

It is NOT an interpolation of integer gaps by varying q. The arithmetic bijection is with the single fiber F_3; p_i are varying integer prime labels, not the variable q. We have not supplied a family of hourglass equations whose base change is projective space, or whose F_q-solutions agree with these points for arbitrary q. In particular replacing 3 by 1 in the indexing count is not a limit of integer solutions.

## 4. Three different meanings of points

The unambiguous count k is (i) the counting polynomial evaluated at 1, (ii) the number of zero-dimensional torus strata, and (iii) the number of closed coordinate points in the standard pointed-monoid projective model. Do not identify it automatically with Hom(Spec F1,P).

Here is the explicit pointed-monoid convention: F1={0,1}; form projective space from the free homogeneous monoid on x_1,...,x_k. Its homogeneous prime ideals are generated by subsets of the variables. Removing the irrelevant ideal leaves proper variable subsets, equivalently nonempty complementary supports S. There are 2^k-1 underlying topological points, with specialization toward smaller supports. Singleton supports give the k closed points. A point with support S has residual multiplicative group of rank |S|-1.

In this convention, the unique map from that residual group to {1}, with zero sent to zero, gives a morphism Spec F1 into the point. Equivalently its homogeneous coordinates are a nonzero 0/1 vector; each nonempty support gives exactly one. Thus literal Hom-points here number 2^k-1, not k. For the projective line this distinguishes two closed/rank-zero points from three topological (and literal Hom-)points. The formal q->1 count is a rank-zero/Weyl-style notion, not this literal functor.

Primary framework check: Lorscheid, *Lectures on F1*, Lecture 1 §3 Examples 3.3–3.6 and Lecture 3 §6, uses this pointed-monoid convention, constructs projective models, and explicitly distinguishes expected closed-point sets from Hom(Spec F1,-). His rank-space/Tits framework addresses that mismatch. We do not transfer this terminology universally to other F1 approaches. Deitmar's early paper uses a different initial monoid convention, so mixing its raw Hom functor into this paragraph would be inappropriate.

## 5. Minimal hostile and lost information

At e=65, indices (1,0),(0,1),(1,1) are linearly dependent over F_3 and map respectively to gaps 4056,3000,3696. No one is the sum of the other two. Indeed D_65={2016,3000,3696,4056} contains no Schur triple.

Source: integer Gaussian allocation vectors. Target: projective F_3 points, then support strata. Map: mod-3 coordinate reduction and simultaneous-sign quotient, followed by support. Preserved: index identity, coordinate zeros, divisibility slices, exact support-stratum cardinality, and integer division along coordinate subspaces. Lost under the projective incidence interpretation: integer phase, Gaussian multiplication, and numerical gap addition. Lost under support alone: the 2^(|S|-1) sign patterns and exact d. Required sidecar: actual integer gaps or Gaussian phase values. Cheapest decisive test: the e=65 dependent triple above.

This is a constructive counterpart to the separate negative frame/base-change audit: an actual standard geometric carrier can exist while the integer equations of interest are not transported to it. The positive result is the support/divisibility diagram, not a theorem produced by a numerical analogy.

## 6. Hypotheses and exact tests

H1: The Gaussian projective bijection refines to torus support strata with arithmetic gcd labels. PROVED in §1–3; checked independently by endpoint-square enumeration for e=1,5,13,65,85,1105,32045.

H2: Coordinate-subspace deletion transports gaps by exact square division, retaining indices. PROVED in §2; every eligible deleted subset checked for every enumerated gap.

H3: Projective dependence implies a gap Schur relation. REFUTED at e=65. Strongest survivor: coordinate incidence encodes divisibility, not addition.

H4: The formal count at q=1 equals every reasonable notion of F1-rational points. REFUTED as a universal statement by the explicit pointed-monoid Hom count; k and 2^k-1 already differ at k=2. The rank-zero interpretation survives.

Reproduce with `python3 verify_support.py` and `python3 -O verify_support.py`. Both produced identical results and stdout. The script uses only integer arithmetic and explicit exception gates. Independent direct projective-vector enumeration checks k=1,...,5 over prime fields q=2,3,5,7, including all support strata. The proof covers all finite prime powers; the test scope is prime fields only. k=0 is separately represented by an empty gap set, not an ordinary nonempty projective space.
