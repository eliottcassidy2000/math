# Component products for observer rank and its invisible modes

Status: PROVED formal-series identities and all-size lower bound; FINITE-EXACT coefficients through n=6. This is a structural continuation of the frozen Session58 observer-profile algebra packet, not a closed numerical formula for A000568 and not a statement that individual tournament profiles collide.

## 1. Inherited definitions and the precise kernel

Let X be a finite simple graph, with isolated vertices allowed. In an ordered vertex gauge define

    alpha_X = (1/|V(X)|!) sum_p (-1)^{number of edges whose endpoint order p reverses}.

Call X admissible, or Royle-even, if every graph automorphism reverses an even number of edges. This condition is independent of the chosen gauge. The value alpha_X may change sign under relabelling, but its zero/nonzero status is intrinsic. “Even-edge” means |E(X)| is even; “Royle-even” does not mean all vertex degrees are even.

The inherited signed-Walsh projection theorem gives one converse-invariant basis vector S_X for each admissible graph type with even total edge count. The graph-pair observer's adjoint sends S_X to alpha_X U_X, in compatible unnormalized orbit-sum conventions. Therefore, if c_n is the number of tournament converse classes and R_n is the rank of the observer matrix, the **converse-side adjoint kernel** has dimension

    k_n = c_n - R_n,

with basis indexed by admissible even-edge graph types X having alpha_X=0. This is not the kernel dimension on the larger graph-pair-domain side. Individual-profile separation is a separate question; at n=6 all 34 individual profiles differ although R_6=32.

The empty graph on zero vertices is included: its automorphism group is trivial, empty sign product is one, alpha_empty=1, and t_0=c_0=R_0=1, k_0=0.

## 2. Disjoint unions: coefficient multiplicativity

Give every vertex an independent continuous uniform label in [0,1]. Its rank is a uniform random permutation, and the product of edge comparison signs has expectation alpha_X. For disjoint graphs G,H there are no cross-component edges. The two products use independent random labels, so, in the inherited component gauges,

    alpha_(G disjoint union H) = alpha_G alpha_H.

Equivalently, for signed permutation sums Sigma_X=|V(X)|! alpha_X,

    Sigma_(G disjoint union H)
      = binomial(|V(G)|+|V(H)|, |V(G)|) Sigma_G Sigma_H.

The binomial coefficient counts interleavings of their internal orders. This proves the exact normalization; summing signs without the interleaving factor would be wrong. The product law also holds for any number of components.

If a connected component C has an odd number of edges, reversing its entire internal order negates its edge product and pairs all orders without a fixed point. Hence alpha_C=0. (The case of one vertex has no edges and is not subject to that argument.) Consequently alpha_X can be nonzero only when EVERY component has even edge count, not just their total.

## 3. Admissibility is componentwise, including swaps

**Lemma.** A graph is Royle-even iff every connected component is Royle-even.

An odd automorphism of one component extends by the identity and is an odd automorphism of the union, proving necessity. For sufficiency, choose one reference model and one consistent orientation/order gauge for every connected isomorphism type, identify each copy with its reference, and label components in consecutive blocks. Every automorphism is a product of automorphisms inside the copies and permutations of isomorphic copies. The internal factors have sign +1 by assumption. A coherent swap of two copies sends each oriented edge to the corresponding edge in the other copy with the same within-copy order, so it reverses no edges in this block gauge. Copy permutations are generated by such swaps, hence also have sign +1. Thus every automorphism of the union is even. Admissibility is gauge-independent, so this proves the claim for any labels.

There is no extra sign (-1)^{|E(C)|} when two equal components are swapped: these Walsh edge variables commute, and the swap is not an exterior-algebra exchange. In particular two copies of the odd-edge admissible claw K1,3 form an admissible even-edge graph. Its alpha is nevertheless zero by Section 2. This is a useful hostile against conflating global edge parity, component edge parity, and signed automorphism parity.

The component lemma is RECOVERED from THM-4721, Lemma E (and that file's credited earlier component work). The coherent-copy proof above is supplied to make this application self-contained; no new component theorem priority is asserted.

## 4. Exact formal Euler products

All products below are in Q[[z]]. They are well-defined formally because only finitely many graph types have any fixed vertex count. Every graph type is uniquely a multiset of connected types, and admissibility is componentwise. These are ordinary generating series of UNLABELLED graph types, not exponential series of labelled graphs.

Let A be the set of connected admissible graph types, and divide it by edge parity into A_even and A_odd. Define

    T(z) = product_{C in A} (1-z^{|V(C)|})^{-1},
    D(z) = product_{C in A_even} (1-z^{|V(C)|})^{-1}
           product_{C in A_odd} (1+z^{|V(C)|})^{-1}.

The coefficient [z^n]T is the number t_n of tournament types, by the recovered signed-Walsh graph-type basis. The sign-weighted coefficient [z^n]D is the number of admissible even-edge graph types minus odd-edge graph types. Therefore

    C(z) := sum_n c_n z^n = (T(z)+D(z))/2.

The converse action on a signed Walsh basis vector S_X is (-1)^{|E(X)|}; hence D is also its trace series. Equivalently, [z^n]D counts self-converse tournament classes, explaining why these coefficients are nonnegative although some individual Euler factors alternate. This equality follows by evaluating the trace also on the tournament orbit-indicator basis; no fixed-point-free converse assumption is made.

Now let A_good be the connected admissible types with alpha_C !=0. Every member has even edge count by Section 2. Multiplicativity and unique component decomposition prove the desired rank product:

    R(z) := sum_n R_n z^n
          = product_{C in A_good} (1-z^{|V(C)|})^{-1}.

Equivalently, writing g_j for the number of connected admissible j-vertex types with nonzero alpha,

    R(z) = product_{j>=1} (1-z^j)^{-g_j}.

This is an exact structural identity. The exponents g_j encode nontrivial signed-order cancellations; this formula does not claim an easy closed computation from n or from A000568 alone.

### Exact decomposition of the missing information

Let A_zero_even be the connected admissible even-edge types with alpha=0, and put

    Z(z) = product_{C in A_zero_even} (1-z^{|V(C)|})^{-1},
    O_+(z) = product_{C in A_odd} (1-z^{|V(C)|})^{-1},
    O_-(z) = product_{C in A_odd} (1+z^{|V(C)|})^{-1},
    O_even(z) = (O_+(z)+O_-(z))/2.

O_even counts multisets containing an EVEN NUMBER of odd-edge components, with multiplicity; it does not mean each component has an even number of edges. Every globally even-edge admissible graph uniquely decomposes into good even-edge components, zero-alpha even-edge components, and an even number of odd-edge components. Thus

    C(z) = R(z) Z(z) O_even(z),
    K(z) := sum_n k_n z^n = C(z)-R(z)
          = R(z)[Z(z) O_even(z)-1].

This expresses two distinct mechanisms for invisible modes: one connected even-edge cancellation, or a nonempty even collection of odd-edge components. All coefficients of Z, O_even, and K are nonnegative by their combinatorial meanings. It is not valid to discard odd-edge connected types before forming the converse-invariant space: pairs of such components contribute genuine converse-even kernel modes.

## 5. A uniform all-size dimension lower bound

Let F be the connected six-vertex graph with edges

    01,02,03,04,05,12,13,14.

The frozen prior packet proves that F has eight edges, Aut(F)=S_3 permuting 2,3,4, is admissible, and alpha_F=0. The proof integrates its order signs to 2 integral_0^1 d(1-d)(1-2d)^3 dd=0. Thus F is one generator of Z(z).

For EVERY admissible even-edge graph type H on n-6 vertices, map

    H -> F disjoint union H.

The image is admissible and even-edge, while alpha vanishes by multiplicativity. Its signed Walsh vector is nonzero, so it indexes a kernel basis vector. The map on graph types is injective: equality of component multisets permits deletion of one occurrence of the fixed connected type F, even when H already contains copies of F. Consequently

    k_n >= c_(n-6)  for all n>=6,
    equivalently R_n <= c_n - c_(n-6).

At n=6 the empty H gives one kernel mode; the actual defect is two. In generating-series language K(z) >= z^6 C(z) coefficientwise. This is a graded injection of basis INDEX sets, not an assertion that products S_F*S_H in a fixed Boolean function space implement disjoint union.

### Sharper bound from both order-six connected zero types

The exact six-vertex certificates also verify a second connected admissible even-edge zero-alpha type F' with mask 2015 and edges

    01,02,03,04,05,13,14,15,23,24.

It has ten edges and is therefore not isomorphic to the eight-edge F. Its admissibility and vanishing signed sum are FINITE-EXACT base facts, checked over all 720 vertex permutations; connectedness is checked directly. The following propagation is then PROVED for all sizes.

Graphs containing F as a component are counted by c_(n-6), as are those containing F'. Their intersection consists exactly of graphs containing BOTH distinct component types, counted by c_(n-12). Inclusion-exclusion on these component-multiset ideals yields

    k_n >= 2 c_(n-6) - c_(n-12),

where c_j=0 for j<0 and c_0=1. Equivalently K(z) >= (2z^6-z^12)C(z) coefficientwise. The right side counts a union despite the displayed negative term. This is sharp at n=6, where it gives k_6>=2 and the exact defect is 2. Existing extra copies of either component cause no overcounting: deleting one specified TYPE occurrence is a unique operation on a multiset. This sharper bound uses no new enumeration beyond the same two certified order-six types.

The lower bound propagates a known obstruction to arbitrary size and retains its exact parity. It does not produce two individual tournament classes with the same profile, nor does it obstruct the earlier same-latent multi-order nonlinear repairs.
