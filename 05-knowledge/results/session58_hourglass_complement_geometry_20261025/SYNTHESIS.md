# Concept board: which bridges preserve the mathematical object?

Session 58, third and final research checkpoint. All planned research lanes are audited; publication verification is separate. The organizing principle is a shared labelled carrier with an explicit consumer, not a visual or numerical analogy.

## 1. Seven cells, five shared labels, three free values

For the usual A,...,I magic placement, the horizontal support is ABC/ E /GHI and the vertical support is ADG/ E /CFI. Their intersection is A,C,E,G,I. Put A=E+x and C=E+z. The seven entries are E, E±x, E±z, E±(x+z); the unique missing values are E±(x−z).

Source → target: compatible labelled hourglasses → their union magic array. This is an equivalence over any additive abelian group; shared totals agree automatically. The five shared values reduce to E,x,z, but this does not permit arbitrary unordered matching. Two compatible injective copies imply injectivity of the union. One injective copy does not.

Square-membership sidecar: E is square and x,z,x+z lie in D_E={t:E±t are squares}; completion requires x−z in D_E. Keep positivity and collision exclusions separately when working over the rationals/integers. The finite-field F13 countercontrol refutes automatic residue-square completion, not an integer distinct-hourglass implication.

## 2. The AP tree is not merely a seven-vertex drawing

Source → target: the nine values → indexed edges L_ij=E+i x+j z of K3,3. The five common edges i=0 or j=0 form a spanning tree with two AP guards. Each hourglass adds two of the four remaining chords. The inverse map permutes the additive-grid labels into magic placement.

Preserved: every value, address, signed gap and square predicate. Lost if labels are dropped: all arithmetic. Session 57's two Hamilton paths instead share one edge and cover nine edges; they are a different decomposition. Likewise the unused-pair K3,3-minus-matching in the seven-cell incidence argument has **cells** as vertices, while this AP K3,3 has **potentials** as vertices.

## 3. Mitre → affine plane, but not Fano and not integer arithmetic

The seven-cell five-line hypergraph is Brouwer's classical mitre. Its uncovered pairs form a six-cycle, so no sixth block can be added on the same seven points. It cannot be five lines of the Fano plane. Nine is the minimum order of a Steiner host; deleting two points of AG(2,3) gives the mitre.

There are 36 mitres, four rooted at each of nine centers. Choosing two distinct directions at a center gives 54 unordered same-center pairs; each yields an eight-line magic pattern and eight D4-related layouts, hence 432 layouts. The argument reconstructs the pair from the union, not just the matching count.

Lost arithmetic: the four additional affine triples AFH,BDI,BFG,CDH have defects +3x,−3x,−3z,+3z from total 3E. They vanish in characteristic three; in a torsion-free target they all vanish only for x=z=0. The affine host does not manufacture square values for missing cells.

## 4. Shared roots → a real fiber product, with a coupled count

Over characteristic different from two, retain the five shared roots, not only their squares. H+ and H− each adjoin two roots and are finite locally free of rank four over the shared-root surface B. The nine-root surface M is globally H+ ×_B H−. The proof uses the shared-coordinate charts and absence of a projective base locus, not a blanket Proj/tensor-product rule. The relative rank over B is sixteen; it is not a claim of sixteen distinct rational points in every fiber.

For b in B(F_q), let f(b) count the H+ fiber, and let σ exchange the roots for E+z and E−z. Then H− has fiber count f(σb), and

    #M(F_q) = Σ_b f(b)f(σb) = ||f_even||² − ||f_odd||².

The covariance is not forced positive: at q=5 the projective counts are (36,88,88,320), energies (324,4), whereas at q=7 they are (64,160,160,384), energies (448,64). The C2 coupling operator is self-adjoint, but not positive semidefinite on all functions. Generic independence of square classes is not statistical independence of finite-field fibers. Cone/projective conversion subtracts the vertex and divides by q−1. Square-entry indicators are not root multiplicities.

The corrected Keller inverse-fiber 3,1,3 pattern is a genuine recovered historical result, but a separate object with a nonproper escaping-sheet mechanism. The two hourglass-to-shared-root maps are finite locally free of rank four, and their joint map M→B has rank sixteen; all are proper. This is a contrast of map types only, not a bridge between their solution sets or an endorsement of broader Keller claims.

## 5. Two complement charts and one safe observer average

Same-size chart: with a total order, backward arcs encode graph edges, so graph complement is tournament converse. Transporting vertices also requires transporting the order; dropping it discards an inversion cocycle. At three vertices, isomorphic one-edge graphs can decode to a transitive triple or a cycle. Thus the chosen chart does not descend to ordinary unlabelled graph pairs.

Marked-path chart: freeze a directed Hamilton path on n vertices and encode its remaining chords on n−1 positions. Here graph complement reverses chords only, K_P(T)=T^op XOR E(P). Converse with reversed path instead reflects the position graph. The two operations cannot be substituted for one another.

Repair: retain the histogram of complementary graph-pair types over all n! orders. It is converse invariant, has mass n!, and has even coefficients for n≥2. It distinguishes converse types through n=5 in exact enumeration; no all-order completeness theorem is claimed. Overlapping pieces must use restrictions of one global order, with compatible pair bits and separately supplied cross-pairs. Independent averaging loses this correlation.

## 6. Counting → geometry requires fixed base and Frobenius sidecars

Inherited odd-prime frame count: C_p=(p+1)p^(p−1)−p(p+1)/2, with disjoint frames C_p(q−p²). Freeze k=F_Q containing the affine F_p-plane. Chosen gauge representatives give C_p copies of A¹_k minus p² rational points and extension counts C_p(Q^m−p²). This is a chosen parameter space, not a canonical moduli functor, and square restrictions are not built in.

The sequence C(p^n−p²) for all n≥2 cannot be the extension counts of one finite-type F_p-scheme when C>0: zeta rationality forces the missing n=1 count to be negative. A correct F_p deletion of F_(p²) points has counts p^n−p^gcd(n,2); the Frobenius permutation of punctures is essential. Freezing dimension d instead gives a different d-dimensional F_p model with counts p^(dm)−p².

Converse-type census → finite étale scheme: fixed classes become Spec F_Q and two-cycles become Spec F_(Q²). This preserves exactly the involution cycle data and extension traces, not tournament arcs, marked paths, automorphism groups or adjacency. A finite permutation with cycle counts a_j has zeta Π_j(1−t^j)^(-a_j).

Formal counting polynomial C(T−r) → absolute rational function s^(Cr)/(s−1)^C is a normalized formal transform with r fixed. Negative value at T=1 excludes a literal finite-set interpretation, not every F1 framework. No integral all-prime descent, compatible Frobenius lifts, completed-Riemann-zeta trace identity or positivity theorem is supplied.

## 7. Gaussian indexing and hereditary arithmetic: a bijection, not a homomorphism

For a positive integer center root e, define D_e={d>0:e²−d and e²+d are integer squares}. Positive gaps correspond bijectively to unordered Pythagorean coordinate pairs u²+v²=e² via d=2uv. A seven-distinct-square hourglass exists precisely when three distinct positive gaps satisfy x+y=z. Merely having three gaps is insufficient.

Strip powers of two and primes congruent to three modulo four: if e=mn with n containing only split primes, D_e=m²D_n. For every divisor t of e, D_e∩t²Z=t²D_(e/t). At n=∏p_i^a_i, Gaussian allocations j_i∈[−a_i,a_i], modulo one simultaneous sign and excluding zero, index the gaps bijectively. Thus |D_e|=(∏(2a_i+1)−1)/2.

When j_i≠0, v_(p_i)(d)=2(a_i−|j_i|); balanced j_i=0 guarantees only v_(p_i)(d)≥2a_i. Every signed three-gap relation therefore has each coordinate's absolute maximum attained at least twice. A single split-prime center cannot satisfy it, even after inert/2 scaling. The one-prime obstruction is recovered from the explicitly cited preprint, not a novelty claim; its separate semiprime argument is not imported as audited.

For squarefree support, the indexing set is PG(k−1,3). Coordinate-zero subspaces exactly encode square-divisibility and hereditary deletion of center primes. This is an index bijection, not an additive homomorphism: at e=65 the dependent vectors (1,0),(0,1),(1,1) correspond to gaps 4056,3000,3696, with no Schur relation. Integer phase addition cannot be reduced modulo three without losing phase. Arbitrary projective incidence does not supply numerical addition.

## 8. Exact necessary-candidate counts and their primitive ancestry

For bounds a_i≥1, let N(a) count distinct unordered sign-orbit triples satisfying the repeated-maximum filter. Define L(a)=12a²+8a+1, U(a)=4a+1, V(a)=2a²+4a+1, W(a)=2a+1. The exact all-exponent formula is

    N(a) = [∏L(a_i) − 3∏U(a_i) − 6∏V(a_i) + 14∏W(a_i) − 6]/48.

The denominator is the free action of six permutations of the three columns and eight independent whole-column sign choices, giving 48. These are sign classes in an integer box, not proportionality classes in a larger projective field. The empty box gives zero.

Primitive means every coordinate maximum reaches its bound. For k≥1 the count is

    M(a) = [∏(24a_i−4) − 3·4^k − 6∏(4a_i+2) + 14·2^k]/48,
    M(()) = 0.

Every candidate has a unique maximum vector b. Deleting zero coordinates and dividing all associated roots by ∏p_i^(a_i−b_i) gives its primitive ancestor. Consequently N(a)=Σ_(0≤b≤a) M(nonzero coordinates of b) is a disjoint reversible partition, not only a formal identity. For an actual Schur triple the hereditary map preserves the relation; it does not turn an arbitrary candidate into one.

In the squarefree case,

    N_k=(21^k−6·7^k−3·5^k+14·3^k−6)/48,
    M_k=(20^k−6·6^k−3·4^k+14·2^k)/48 for k≥1, M_0=0.

The prefixes are N: 0,0,4,150,3736; M: 0,0,4,138,3160. The binomial transform is N_k=Σ_j binom(k,j)M_j. The primitive OGF is

    Σ M_k z^k = (4z²+10z³−120z⁴)/(1−32z+284z²−928z³+960z⁴).

Its minimal eventual constant-coefficient recurrence has order four but starts at k=5, NOT k=4. The failed k=4 application has residual −120. This exception is essential because the uncorrected exponential formula at k=0 would give 1/8. The total sequence instead has five distinct noncancelled exponential bases and minimal recurrence order five.

Among all unordered squarefree index triples, N_k has density asymptotic to (7/9)^k, and M_k to (20/27)^k; among balanced candidates, M_k/N_k~(20/21)^k. These denominators differ. None of these densities counts actual hourglasses without the missing numerical Schur test.

## 9. Observer profile linear loss versus individual separation

The first checkpoint's n≤5 separation check now extends exactly through n=6: all 34 converse classes still have different individual profiles. Nevertheless their 34-by-78 first-moment matrix has rational rank 32. The loss is mixture identifiability, not an individual-profile collision. Explicit disjoint mixtures supported on 9 and 12 classes have the same one-observer law; both arise from a signed kernel certificate with positive/negative mass 122880.

In the inherited signed Walsh basis, ordinary and tournament averaging map between two different invariant spaces. For a Royle-even graph orbit X, the two maps have coefficients α_X; only their composition is an endomorphism with eigenvalue α_X². Complement-evenness requires an even edge count and is distinct from Royle-evenness. At n=6 precisely two relevant modes vanish, graph masks 255 and 2015. A connected cancellation family, padded with isolates, supplies a nonzero linear kernel for every n≥6. This proves all-size linear failure, not all-size failure of point separation.

Retain one latent tournament class and sample two observer orders independently conditional on it. The law then contains E[p_T(q)p_T(r)], not the product of separately averaged laws. At n=6, 32 independent linear columns plus the squares of graph-pair columns 3 and 15 have exact rank 34, with determinant 211991592542375584355844096000000. These events correspond to the complementary pairs containing K1,2 plus three isolates and K1,4 plus one isolate. The displayed ambiguous mixtures differ by probabilities −1/57600 and +1/230400 on the two diagonal events. Thus two shared-latent orders are sufficient and one is insufficient for mixture identification here.

Freshly resampling the tournament for each observation loses this repair. Squaring an already averaged probability also loses it. No all-n degree-two sufficiency follows. On any finite set with distinct profile vectors, polynomial interpolation eventually spans all functions; a genuine profile collision would block every polynomial degree. These are different questions.

## 10. Dirichlet ancestry and Delange asymptotics for necessary candidates

Write N(e) for the repeated-maximum candidate count at center root e, and P(e) for its primitive count. The unique ancestry partition gives N(e)=Σ_(d|e)P(d). Thus their absolutely convergent Dirichlet series satisfy F(s)=ζ(s)G(s) for Re(s)>1. Each is a finite linear combination of explicit Euler products, NOT itself an asserted Euler product; N(1)=P(1)=0 already prevents ordinary multiplicativity. The primitive unit correction is a constant Dirichlet term, not another ζ term.

For the leading component, let χ_4 be the character modulo four and define

    H(s)=(1−2^(−s))^10
         × ∏_(p≡1 mod4)(1+18p^(−s)+5p^(−2s))(1−p^(−s))^18
         × ∏_(p≡3 mod4)(1−p^(−2s))^10.

Cancellation of every linear local term makes H holomorphic by locally uniform absolute convergence on Re(s)>1/2. Finite factors may vanish; a global nonvanishing claim is neither needed nor made. At s=1 its factors are positive and H(1)>0. Positive integer powers of ζ and L(s,χ_4) give meromorphic continuation with the sole possible pole at 1, without a zero-free-region or RH hypothesis. The leading pole orders of F,G are 11,10, with common leading Laurent coefficient

    C=H(1)(π/4)^10/48 > 0.

Applying Delange's original theorem to the ordinary Laplace transforms F(s)/s and G(s)/s of the nonnegative nondecreasing summatory functions proves

    Σ_(e≤x)N(e) ~ C x(log x)^10/10!,
    Σ_(e≤x)P(e) ~ C x(log x)^9/9!.

The bound x is on the center ROOT, not its square. No error term or complete lower-order polynomial is claimed. The aggregate primitive-to-total ratio is asymptotic to 10/log x. Comparing to all gap triples gives candidate survival ratios of order (log x)^−3 with the explicit Euler-product/factorial constants in the packet; these are ratios of aggregate counts, not averages of per-center fractions.

For actual signed gap relations A(e), the same hereditary partition still gives A(e)=Σ_(d|e)A_prim(d) and domination 0≤A≤N, 0≤A_prim≤P. Hence their Dirichlet series converge for Re(s)>1 and obey the same ζ convolution there. No continuation, pole, or asymptotic for these actual-relation series follows. This is the exact boundary between arithmetic ancestry and the explicitly countable necessary filter.

## 11. A positive toric carrier, with its point convention stated

At squarefree split center e=∏p_i, the ternary projective indexing refines to exact supports S. It preserves

    gcd(e²,d)=(e/∏_(i∈S)p_i)²,
    #gaps of exact support S=2^(|S|−1).

Higher balanced valuation is truncated by the gcd; it must not be silently set equal to two. Inactive-coordinate deletion is exact square division and preserves any existing additive relation. The closure/containment diagram of coordinate strata transports this divisibility structure, not arbitrary projective hyperplanes or numerical addition.

Standard projective space over Z supplies a genuine geometric carrier: its support-S stratum is G_m^(|S|−1), with (q−1)^(|S|−1) points over F_q. Summing gives 1+q+...+q^(k−1). At q=3 this matches the arithmetic strata; varying q is not an interpolation of integer gaps. This positive carrier coexists with the earlier no-descent warnings because the hourglass equations themselves were not transported.

At formal q=1 the polynomial gives k, also the number of zero-dimensional strata and closed coordinate points. In the explicitly specified free pointed-monoid model F1={0,1}, however, underlying topological points and literal Hom(Spec F1,−) are indexed by ALL nonempty supports and number 2^k−1. The residue multiplicative rank at S is |S|−1. Thus the rank-zero/Weyl-style count k differs from raw Hom already on the projective line (two versus three). This is a convention-specific boundary, not a universal claim about all F1 models.

## 12. Why uniform higher exponents do not give the same projective bijection

Fix an auxiliary odd prime ℓ, put a=(ℓ−1)/2, and take integer center e=∏p_i^a with k≥1. Balanced-representative reduction identifies Gaussian gap indices with (F_ℓ^k\{0})/{±1}, not generally with projective space. Passing to full scalar classes maps onto P^(k−1)(F_ℓ) with constant fiber a. The projective map is bijective precisely when ℓ=3.

Support survives this quotient, but absolute integer index magnitudes need not. The exact formula is

    gcd(e²,d(j))=∏p_i^(2(a−|j_i|)).

At ℓ=5,e=25, the signed index classes j=1,2 give gaps 600,336 with gcd(625,d)=25,1. Both collapse to the sole point of P^0(F_5). Thus projectivization loses actual valuation data even with one coordinate. Keep the scalar class modulo signs, or the original signed index, as a sidecar. Neither reduction nor projectivization turns integer exponent addition, Gaussian phase multiplication, or numerical gap addition into field addition.

## 13. Component products locate the observer's invisible modes

For the order-sign coefficient α_X, disjoint unions satisfy α_(G⊔H)=α_G α_H; signed permutation sums acquire the binomial interleaving factor. Royle-even admissibility is componentwise, including permutations of equal components. No exterior-algebra sign appears when equal components are exchanged. Odd-edge components have α=0, even when paired into a globally even-edge graph.

Let A be the connected admissible unlabelled graph types, A_even/A_odd their edge-parity subclasses, and A_good those with α≠0 (necessarily even-edge). The ordinary formal Euler products are

    T(z)=∏_(C∈A)(1−z^|C|)^−1,
    D(z)=∏_(C∈A_even)(1−z^|C|)^−1 ∏_(C∈A_odd)(1+z^|C|)^−1,
    C(z)=(T(z)+D(z))/2,
    R(z)=∏_(C∈A_good)(1−z^|C|)^−1.

Here coefficients of T count tournament types, D self-converse types, C converse types and R observer rank, by the inherited signed-Walsh basis. These are formal unlabelled-type generating functions, not labelled exponential series or simple numerical formulas for all ranks.

Let Z count multisets of connected even-edge zero-α types, and O_even count multisets containing an even number of odd-edge admissible components. Then C=R Z O_even and K=C−R=R(Z O_even−1). This separates two kernel mechanisms: a connected even-edge cancellation and a nonempty even collection of odd-edge components. The kernel here is the converse-side ADJOINT kernel, not the larger graph-pair-domain kernel.

The two distinct connected six-vertex zero types with masks 255 and 2015 give a component-multiset injection and inclusion-exclusion bound

    k_n ≥ 2c_(n−6) − c_(n−12),

with c_0=1 and c_j=0 for j<0. The bound is sharp at n=6, where c_6=34 and rank is32. It propagates certified basis modes to all sizes; it does not exhibit individual tournament-profile collisions or rule out nonlinear shared-latent repair. Connected-component exponents remain nontrivial information, not data recoverable from the tournament count alone.
