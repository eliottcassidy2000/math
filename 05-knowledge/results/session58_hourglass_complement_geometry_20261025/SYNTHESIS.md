# Concept board: which bridges preserve the mathematical object?

Session 58, first checkpoint. Session ongoing. The organizing principle is a shared labelled carrier with an explicit consumer, not a visual or numerical analogy.

## 1. Seven cells, five shared labels, three free values

For the usual A,...,I magic placement, the horizontal support is ABC/ E /GHI and the vertical support is ADG/ E /CFI. Their intersection is A,C,E,G,I. Put A=E+x and C=E+z. The seven entries are E, E±x, E±z, E±(x+z); the unique missing values are E±(x−z).

Source → target: compatible labelled hourglasses → their union magic array. This is an equivalence over any additive abelian group; shared totals agree automatically. The five shared values reduce to E,x,z, but this does not permit arbitrary unordered matching. Two compatible injective copies imply injectivity of the union. One injective copy does not.

Square-membership sidecar: E is square and x,z,x+z lie in D_E={t:E±t are squares}; completion requires x−z in D_E. Keep positivity and collision exclusions separately when working over the rationals/integers. The finite-field F13 countercontrol refutes automatic residue-square completion, not an integer distinct-hourglass implication.

## 2. The AP tree is not merely a seven-vertex drawing

Source → target: the nine values → indexed edges L_ij=E+i x+j z of K3,3. The five common edges i=0 or j=0 form a spanning tree with two AP guards. Each hourglass adds two of the four remaining chords. The inverse map permutes the additive-grid labels into magic placement.

Preserved: every value, address, signed gap and square predicate. Lost if labels are dropped: all arithmetic. Session 57's two Hamilton paths instead share one edge and cover nine edges; they are a different decomposition. Likewise the unused-pair K3,3-minus-matching in the seven-cell incidence argument has **cells** as vertices, while this AP K3,3 has **potentials** as vertices.

## 3. Mitre → affine plane, but not Fano and not integer arithmetic

The seven-cell five-line hypergraph is Brouwer's classical mitre. Its uncovered pairs form a six-cycle, so no sixth block can be added on the same seven points. It cannot be five lines of the Fano plane. Nine is the minimum order of a Steiner host; deleting two points of AG(2,3) gives the mitre.

There are 36 mitres, four rooted at each of nine centers. Choosing two distinct directions at a center gives 54 unordered same-center pairs; each yields an eight-line magic pattern and eight D4-related layouts, hence 432 layouts. The argument reconstructs the pair from the union, not just the matching count.

Lost arithmetic: the four additional affine triples AFH,BDI,BFG,CDH have defects +3x,−3x,−3z,+3z from total 3E. They vanish in characteristic three; in a torsion-free target they all vanish only for x=z=0. The affine host does not manufacture square values for missing cells.

## 4. Shared roots → a real fiber product, with a coupled count

Over characteristic different from two, retain the five shared roots, not only their squares. H+ and H− each adjoin two roots and are finite locally free of rank four over the shared-root surface B. The nine-root surface M is globally H+ ×_B H−. The proof uses the shared-coordinate charts and absence of a projective base locus, not a blanket Proj/tensor-product rule. The relative rank over B is sixteen; it is not a claim of sixteen distinct rational points in every fiber.

For b in B(F_q), let f(b) count the H+ fiber, and let σ exchange the roots for E+z and E−z. Then H− has fiber count f(σb), and

    #M(F_q) = Σ_b f(b)f(σb) = ||f_even||² − ||f_odd||².

The covariance is not forced positive: at q=5 the projective counts are (36,88,88,320), energies (324,4), whereas at q=7 they are (64,160,160,384), energies (448,64). The C2 coupling operator is self-adjoint, but not positive semidefinite on all functions. Generic independence of square classes is not statistical independence of finite-field fibers. Cone/projective conversion subtracts the vertex and divides by q−1. Square-entry indicators are not root multiplicities.

The corrected Keller inverse-fiber 3,1,3 pattern is a genuine recovered historical result, but a separate object with a nonproper escaping-sheet mechanism. The two hourglass-to-shared-root maps are finite locally free of rank four, and their joint map M→B has rank sixteen; all are proper. This is a contrast of map types only, not a bridge between their solution sets or an endorsement of broader Keller claims.

## 5. Two complement charts and one safe observer average

Same-size chart: with a total order, backward arcs encode graph edges, so graph complement is tournament converse. Transporting vertices also requires transporting the order; dropping it discards an inversion cocycle. At three vertices, isomorphic one-edge graphs can decode to a transitive triple or a cycle. Thus the chosen chart does not descend to ordinary unlabelled graph pairs.

Marked-path chart: freeze a directed Hamilton path on n vertices and encode its remaining chords on n−1 positions. Here graph complement reverses chords only, K_P(T)=T^op XOR E(P). Converse with reversed path instead reflects the position graph. The two operations cannot be substituted for one another.

Repair: retain the histogram of complementary graph-pair types over all n! orders. It is converse invariant, has mass n!, and has even coefficients for n≥2. It distinguishes converse types through n=5 in exact enumeration; no all-order completeness theorem is claimed. Overlapping pieces must use restrictions of one global order, with compatible pair bits and separately supplied cross-pairs. Independent averaging loses this correlation.

## 6. Counting → geometry requires fixed base and Frobenius sidecars

Inherited odd-prime frame count: C_p=(p+1)p^(p−1)−p(p+1)/2, with disjoint frames C_p(q−p²). Freeze k=F_Q containing the affine F_p-plane. Chosen gauge representatives give C_p copies of A¹_k minus p² rational points and extension counts C_p(Q^m−p²). This is a chosen parameter space, not a canonical moduli functor, and square restrictions are not built in.

The sequence C(p^n−p²) for all n≥2 cannot be the extension counts of one finite-type F_p-scheme when C>0: zeta rationality forces the missing n=1 count to be negative. A correct F_p deletion of F_(p²) points has counts p^n−p^gcd(n,2); the Frobenius permutation of punctures is essential. Freezing dimension d instead gives a different d-dimensional F_p model with counts p^(dm)−p².

Converse-type census → finite étale scheme: fixed classes become Spec F_Q and two-cycles become Spec F_(Q²). This preserves exactly the involution cycle data and extension traces, not tournament arcs, marked paths, automorphism groups or adjacency. A finite permutation with cycle counts a_j has zeta Π_j(1−t^j)^(-a_j).

Formal counting polynomial C(T−r) → absolute rational function s^(Cr)/(s−1)^C is a normalized formal transform with r fixed. Negative value at T=1 excludes a literal finite-set interpretation, not every F1 framework. No integral all-prime descent, compatible Frobenius lifts, completed-Riemann-zeta trace identity or positivity theorem is supplied.
