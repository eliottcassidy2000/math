## Proof audit

1. Give all vertices independent identically distributed continuous coordinates, for example uniform coordinates in `[0,1]`. The induced total order is uniform. For a fixed labelled graph, its sign is the product of its edge-order signs. On a disjoint union, this product is a product of functions of disjoint independent coordinate sets. Therefore the expectation, and hence alpha, factors exactly. Components use their inherited vertex-order gauges. The word "identically" matters: independent variables with different distributions need not give a uniform order.

2. Royle-evenness is equivalent to Royle-evenness of every component. An odd component automorphism extends by the identity. Conversely choose one model for each connected isomorphism type, and fixed identifications of all its copies with that model. Automorphisms of the union are generated by component-internal automorphisms and coherent copy swaps. A coherent swap maps every edge in one copy to a corresponding edge in the other copy and back. The reversal signs of the two corresponding edges are equal, so their product is `+1`. Every coherent swap therefore has sign `+1`, including when the components' labels are interleaved. Internal automorphisms have sign `+1` by hypothesis. This directly recovers THM-4721, Lemma E; it does not require treating a vertex permutation's ordinary sign as the graph sign.

3. Reversing the entire induced order within a component multiplies its edge-order product by `(-1)^e`. This is a measure-preserving fixed-point-free pairing of orders for any nonempty-edge component of odd edge count, so its alpha is zero. The order reversal is not asserted to be a graph automorphism.

4. A Royle-even graph survives ordinary/twisted averaging if and only if every connected component has nonzero alpha. Every such component has even edge count by item 3. Consequently its component multiset gives exactly one factor `(1-z^v)^(-1)` per connected surviving isomorphism type in the rank OGF.

5. The OGF of all Royle-even graphs is the component-multiset product `T`. Weighting each graph by `(-1)^e` gives `D`, whose factor for an odd-edge component is `(1+z^v)^(-1)`, not `(1-z^v)^(-1)`. Projecting onto globally even edge parity yields `C=(T+D)/2`. Pairs of odd-edge components belong to `C`, even though they never survive in the rank series. Thus replacing `C` by a product over only even-edge components would be incorrect; the proposed formula correctly avoids this error.

6. These are ordinary formal power series. Each connected component is nonempty, and only finitely many types have at most any fixed number of vertices. Thus every coefficient depends on finitely many factors and is well-defined, without an analytic-convergence claim. The empty multiset gives `T_0=D_0=C_0=R_0=1`, and the order-zero kernel has dimension zero.

7. With `L: V_G^+ -> V_T^+`, the dimension `c_n-R_n` is the dimension of `ker(L*)`, the converse-side kernel. It is not generally the dimension of `ker(L)`, whose ambient dimension is the graph-complement-pair count. This distinction should remain explicit.

8. `F_3` has degree classes `5,4,2,2,2,1`, so its automorphisms are exactly the permutations of its three common neighbors. In the stated gauge these reverse no edges. It is connected, Royle-even, and has eight edges. The frozen integral proof gives alpha zero; the independent enumeration gives automorphism order six and signed-order sum zero. For every globally even-edge Royle-even graph `H`, the graph `F_3 disjoint-union H` is another globally even-edge Royle-even graph and has alpha zero. Distinct `H` give distinct graph types by cancellation of the fixed connected-component multiplicity of `F_3`. Their nonzero signed Walsh sums have disjoint supports and are independent. Therefore `dim ker(L*_n) >= c_(n-6)` for every `n>=6`, including `n=6` where `H` may be empty. This is a linear-information statement only.

9. Relabelling the chosen graph representative can negate alpha and its associated signed orbit sum. Royle-evenness, alpha being zero, and alpha squared are independent of that choice. The rank series uses only zero/nonzero, so no hidden canonical-sign convention is needed.

10. The target packet additionally identifies `D` as the self-converse tournament trace series. This is correct: complement acts diagonally on signed Walsh vectors with eigenvalue `(-1)^e`, while on tournament orbit indicators it is a permutation involution. Its trace in the latter basis counts fixed, self-converse classes. The decomposition `C=R Z O_even` is also correct. Its three unique component categories are good even-edge components, zero-alpha even-edge components, and an even total multiplicity of odd-edge components. Subtracting the all-good multisets gives `K=R[Z O_even-1]`. This does not identify products of fixed-space Walsh functions with disjoint union.

11. The final packet strengthens the lower bound using the second connected six-vertex zero-alpha graph, mask 2015. Its ten edges distinguish it from the eight-edge mask 255, and both the target verifier and this independent audit verify connectedness, Royle-evenness, and exact zero alpha. Among globally even-edge admissible graphs, those containing either fixed component type are each counted by `c_(n-6)`; those containing both are counted by `c_(n-12)`. Removing one occurrence of each distinct type is a bijection on component multisets and preserves the required parity. Inclusion-exclusion therefore proves `k_n >= 2c_(n-6)-c_(n-12)`, with `c_j=0` for negative j. The negative term in the equivalent coefficientwise series bound causes no problem: the full expression counts a union of two sets. This sharpened proof is valid for all n and is sharp at n=6.
