## 2. The Legendre-type placement theorem, in three tiers (S3)

**S3 (PROVED).** Let `K, L >= 1`, `Delta = 2^K-3^L`, `e = Delta/3^L`, so `2^K = 3^L(1+e)` and `K - L alpha = log_2(1+e)`.

*Lemma (elementary).* For `|e| < 1`, `|log_2(1+e)| <= |e|/((1-|e|) ln 2)`. Proof: for `e >= 0`, `ln(1+e) <= e <= e/(1-e)`; for `e < 0`, `-ln(1-|e|) = int_0^{|e|} dt/(1-t) <= |e|/(1-|e|)`. (Grid check in the `.out`, max ratio `0.9999`.)

*Tier A.* If `|Delta| <= 3^L/(4L)` then `|e| <= 1/(4L) <= 1/4`, so `|K - L alpha| <= (1/(4L)) / ((3/4) ln 2) = 1/(3 L ln 2) < 1/(2L)` because `3 ln 2 > 2`. Hence `|alpha - K/L| < 1/(2L^2)`, and if `K/L = K'/L'` in lowest terms then also `|alpha - K'/L'| < 1/(2L'^2)`. By Legendre's theorem (CITED: Hardy-Wright, *Introduction to the Theory of Numbers*, Thm 184) `K'/L'` is a convergent of `alpha`. QED.

*Tier B.* If `|Delta| <= 3^L/(2L)` and `L >= 2` then `|e| <= 1/4` again and `|K - L alpha| <= (1/(2L))/((3/4) ln 2) = 2/(3 L ln 2) < 1/L` (as `3 ln 2 > 2`), so `|alpha - K'/L'| < 1/L'^2`. By the Fatou-Grace theorem (CITED: Fatou 1904, Grace 1918; the statement "`|alpha - p/q| < 1/q^2` implies `p/q` is a convergent or an intermediate fraction with `j=1` or `j=a_{n+1}-1`" is standard, e.g. in Bugeaud's *Approximation by algebraic numbers*, but the exact page is UNCITED-RECOLLECTION), `K'/L'` is a convergent or an extreme intermediate fraction. For `L=1` the hypothesis is `|Delta| <= 1`, i.e. `K in {1,2}`, both convergents. QED.

*Tier C (the task's hypothesis).* If `|Delta| < 3^L/L`, `L >= 2`, the lemma gives only `|K - L alpha| < (1/L)/((1/2) ln 2) < 2.9/L`, i.e. `|alpha - K/L| < 2.9/L^2`. No placement theorem holds at this constant, and the implication "tier C forces a convergent" is **REFUTED** by the minimal witness `5/3` (`Delta = 5`, `5*3 = 15 < 27`, an intermediate fraction) and by the seven-cycle clock `11/7` (`139*7 = 973 < 2187`, an intermediate fraction; it fails tier A since `4*7*139 = 3892 > 2187` but passes tier B since `2*7*139 = 1946 < 2187`, consistent with Fatou-Grace: `j=1` extreme).

**S3 (FINITE-EXACT, `L <= 400`).** Every `(K,L)` with `|Delta|*L < 3^L` was found (only `K = round(L alpha) +- 2` can occur, and for `L >= 6` at most one `K`, since `2/(L ln 2) < 1/2`; the only `L` with two hits is `L = 1`, with `1/1` and `2/1`) and classified. ## 3. The Eliahou bridge: a cycle's minimum forces its clock onto a convergent (S4)

**S4 (PROVED mechanism; CITED as Eliahou 1993, *The 3x+1 problem: new lower bounds on nontrivial cycle lengths*, Discrete Math. 118).** Let `n_1, ..., n_L` be a signed odd cycle of `T_b(n) = (3n+b)/2^{v_2(3n+b)}`, `b = +-1`, with halving word `w` and `min |n_i| = N`. Multiplying `3n_i + b = 2^{k_i} n_{i+1}` around the cycle gives `2^K/3^L = prod_i (1 + b/(3 n_i))`. If every `b/n_i > 0` (nodes of the sign of `b`) then `0 < Delta/3^L <= (1+1/(3N))^L - 1`; if every `b/n_i < 0` then `0 < -Delta/3^L <= 1 - (1-1/(3N))^L`. (Mixed-sign cycles cannot occur for `b=+-1` since `3n+b` has the sign of `n` for every odd `n`, as `|3n| >= 3 > |b|`; the clock bound (C8) of the catalan lane is the same product with the crude bounds `2 <= |3n+b|/|n| <= 4`.) Since `(1+x)^L - 1 <= exp(Lx) - 1`, tier A holds as soon as `exp(L/(3N)) - 1 <= 1/(4L)`, i.e. roughly `N >= 4L^2/3`. Consequently:

> **Corollary (PROVED given the CITED input).** If every positive `3n+1` cycle other than `{1}` has minimum element `N >= 2^68` (Barina 2020, CITED: *Convergence verification of the Collatz problem*, J. Supercomputing; volume and year UNCITED-RECOLLECTION), then every such cycle with `L <= 14878195707` odd terms (about `sqrt(3*2^68/4) = 14878203147`; the lane's `L` is a sufficient bound found with a `1e-6` float margin, the exact threshold of the `exp`-form condition being `14878203146`, audit `.out`) has reduced `K/L` equal to a convergent of `log_2 3`; and since the only tier-A hits with `L <= 400` are `3/2`, `19/12`, `84/53`, all with `Delta < 0`, there is no positive `3n+1` cycle with `2 <= L <= 400` at all.

The second clause is much weaker than the literature (Steiner 1977, CITED, no nontrivial 1-cycles; Simons-de Weger 2005, CITED, *Theoretical and computational bounds for m-cycles of the 3n+1 problem*, Acta Arith. 117, no `m`-cycles with `m <= 68`; Hercher 2023, J. Integer Seq., `m <= 91`, UNCITED-RECOLLECTION for the exact bound) and is recorded only to show that the S3 tiers and the S4 product identity compose correctly. The eight known signed cycles satisfy the product bound exactly as predicted (table in the `.out`): `{1}` and `{-1}` at both signs sit on `2/1` and `1/1` with `rel = bound = 0.3333`; `{-5,-7}` / `{5,7}` sit on `3/2` (`rel 0.1111`, `bound 0.1289`, tier A); the seven-cycle sits on `11/7` (`rel 0.0636`, `bound 0.1294`, tier B, extreme intermediate). Legendre thresholds `N_min(L)` (smallest `N` with `(1+1/(3N))^L - 1 <= 1/(4L)`) for the convergent denominators: `L=5: 3.3993e+01`, `L=12: 1.9383e+02`, `L=41: 2.2480e+03`, `L=53: 3.7540e+03`, `L=306: 1.2490e+05`, `L=665: 5.8974e+05`, `L=15601: 3.2452e+08`, `L=31867: 1.3540e+09`, `L=79335: 8.3921e+09`, each within a fraction of a percent of `4L^2/3` (`3.3333e+01, 1.9200e+02, 2.2413e+03, 3.7453e+03, 1.2485e+05, 5.8963e+05, 3.2452e+08, 1.3540e+09, 8.3921e+09`).

**Pillai and Catalan (CITED; dates UNCITED-RECOLLECTION).** Pillai's conjecture (the general perfect-power form is usually dated Pillai 1945; the 1931 and 1936 papers concern `a^x - b^y = c` for fixed bases, where finiteness for each fixed `c` follows from Siegel's theorem; the audit could not verify which paper states which and downgrades the attribution) states that for fixed `c >= 1` the equation `x^p - y^q = c` in perfect powers has only finitely many solutions; it is OPEN in general (Bennett 2001 gives at most two solutions for fixed bases). ## 7. The honest bridge: what Baker / Pillai lower bounds do and do not bound (S10)

**S10 (CITED mechanism; typed analogy).** Baker's theory (linear forms in logarithms) gives effective lower bounds `|2^K - 3^L| > 2^K * K^{-c}` for an absolute `c`, and for these two bases explicit versions exist (Ellison 1971, quoted as `|2^K - 3^L| > 2^K e^{-K/10}` for `K >= 27` in Simons-de Weger and in Lagarias's annotated bibliography; the constants are UNCITED-RECOLLECTION). Composed with S4 this bounds cycle lengths from below in terms of the verified minimum `N`: a cycle gives `|Delta| <= 3^L ((1+1/(3N))^L - 1)`, roughly `3^L L/(3N)`, while Baker gives `|Delta| > 3^L L^{-c}` on the relevant clocks, so `N < L^{c+1}/3` — a long verification forces a long cycle, which is exactly how Eliahou, Simons-de Weger and Hercher obtain their bounds (with much sharper Diophantine input than S3).

*Analogy, typed.* Source: Pillai/Baker lower bounds on `|2^K - 3^L|`. Target: Collatz cycle lengths. Map: the product identity `2^K/3^L = prod(1 + b/(3n_i))` (S4). Preserved: the clock `(K,L)` and the relative gap. Lost: the carry `B` and hence the actual cycle (Baker sees only that `|Delta|` is not tiny, never whether some carry cancels it; `11/7` shows a non-tiny gap being cancelled). Sidecar: the continued fraction of `log_2 3`, which says *where* small gaps can sit (S3). Test: the S4 corollary and the eight known cycles (all pass).

*What it does not bound.* A divergent trajectory has no closing identity: its partial words satisfy the ordered carry `2^{K_L} T^L(n) = 3^L n + B_L` (blueprint / guards lanes) with no relation between `2^{K_L}` and `3^L` beyond the density statements of those lanes, so `Delta` never appears and no Diophantine lower bound applies. The power-gap machinery is a cycle tool; it is silent on divergence (SCOPE, agreeing with [collatz_blueprint_20260921_energy.md](collatz_blueprint_20260921_energy.md) and [catalan_elliptic_20260921_catalan.md](catalan_elliptic_20260921_catalan.md) sec.7).

## Stopping boundary / next question

The continued fraction tells where `|2^K-3^L|` is relatively small (S3, PROVED in tiers A and B), the product identity tells that a cycle with a large minimum must sit there (S4), and the gate census tells that on those clocks a carry cancels the gap only at the unit gaps and at `139` for `L <= 13` (S5), with no new signed cycle through period 11 (S6).
