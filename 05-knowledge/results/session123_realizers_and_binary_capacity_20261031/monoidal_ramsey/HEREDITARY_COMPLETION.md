# Hereditary completion preserves the exact capacity

**PROVED corollary, independently audited.** This deliberately enlarges the
class; it is not a silent change in the original theorem's definition.

Let D be the singleton/join/union/full-nonempty-C5 class. Let Dbar contain every
induced subgraph of a graph in D, including the empty graph. Equivalently,
Dbar is generated by singleton and empty graphs, joins, disjoint unions, and
C5 substitution with possibly empty slots.

To see the equivalence, restrict the singleton leaves of a D construction to
the selected induced vertices; removed leaves become empty. Conversely, fill
every empty leaf of an empty-enabled construction by a singleton. The resulting
full construction lies in D and its selected original vertices induce the
original empty-enabled graph. Empty-enabled operations are also preserved
under induced restriction.

For empty graphs put omega=alpha=0. For possibly empty C5 children with
statistics(a_i,b_i), the root formulas remain exactly

    omega=max_i(a_i+a_(i+1)), alpha=max_i(b_i+b_(i+2)).

Every allowed singleton support is contained in one of the displayed pairs,
so zeros do not invalidate either maximum. The all-budget weighted pentagon
inequality already allows zero ports. Together with its superadditivity
specializations, structural induction gives order<=G(omega,alpha) throughout
Dbar, with the zero/empty base handled separately.

The original D balanced witnesses remain in Dbar. Therefore for every positive
A,B the exact Dbar capacity equals

    F_Dbar(A,B)=F_D(A,B)=sum_(i<A,j<B)2^popcount(i & j).

The object classes differ: deleting one vertex of C5 gives P4, which is not
in D. Equality of their capacity profiles does not imply equality or equivalence
of the graph categories, nor classify all optimizing graphs. This completion
is hereditary graph closure, not the exact completion defining a realizability
topos. No bound for unrestricted graphs follows.
