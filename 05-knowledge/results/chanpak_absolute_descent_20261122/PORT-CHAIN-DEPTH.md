# Logarithmic completeness and a slope-two barrier for single-backarc port chains

This uses the physical [THM-5070 single-backarc blocks](../kps_s18_ternary_chains_digraph_weights_20261109/PROOF.md), at ordinary weight two. A block \(B\) is appended ordinally except for one return edge from its connector \(w\) to the old marked port, and a vertex of \(B\) is marked as the new port.

Write \((h,E,O)\) for the count and the two path/complement masses for even and odd paths starting at the mark. The [signed identity](../chanpak_branching_inverse_20261122/TWO-FLANKS.md) gives \(O-E=e\ge0\), the full paths ending there. Moreover \(E+O\ge h\): split a full path just before its visit to the port, and use the suffix as the port-start path. Thus \(O\ge h/2\). If the new port is a sink its state is exactly \((h',0,h')\), since the only start from it is the singleton and it is forced to end every full path.

The inherited transfer is \(h'=\eta h+2q_o E+2q_e O\), where \(\eta=H(B)\), and \(q_o,q_e\) are the analogous masses of paths ending at \(w\) inside \(B\). In particular \(q_o\ge1\). If the new port is not a sink, then \(E'\ge h/2\): an outgoing neighbor inside \(B\) gives a two-vertex path whose complement contains all of the old tournament; if no such neighbor exists the port must be \(w\), and every old odd path prepended by \(w\) gives a new even path with at least its previous complement weight.

**Absolute depth.** Any reachable height \(N\) has a word using at most \(3\lfloor\log_2 N\rfloor+2\) blocks, and when \(N\) is prime at most \(2\lfloor\log_2 N\rfloor+1\) blocks. At a sink the next height is an odd multiple of its old height. Erase any step preserving the same sink state. In any three remaining steps, if the first ends at a nonsink the second adds at least the height before the first. If the first ends at a sink and the second increases the height, it at least triples it. Otherwise the unchanged-height second step must leave the sink for a nonsink, and the third adds at least the height before the second. Thus every three steps double the start height. For a prime output, stop at its first attainment and discard the prefix through the last height-one sink (which has precisely the singleton's state). No intermediate sink of height between one and the prime is possible, since the gcd of the state entries divides every later height by the integer transfer. Every two steps now double. For maximum block order \(m\), the corresponding absolute order bound is one plus \(m\) times the block bound. This proves completeness of the bounded target search **within** the grammar, not existence for every target.

**No slope-two return, even on specially prepared states.** Suppose a fixed nonempty word maps an affine family of physical states of unbounded height to the same affine family with height slope two. Its leading state direction is an eigenvector of the word with eigenvalue two. All intermediate height ratios are at least one, and the direction cycle repeats. Any block with \(\eta>1\) or \(q_e>0\) would at least triple height (use \(E+O\ge h\)). So every block must be transitive with connector \(w\) its source, giving \(q_o=1\) and \(h'=h+2E\).

If the new port is distinct from \(w\) and not a sink, its two-vertex internal path avoids \(w\); the complement retains the same slow block formula, so \(E'\ge h'\). The following step triples. If the new port is \(w\) in a nonsingleton block, a two-vertex path inside the block and the paths through the old port give disjoint contributions \(E'\ge h+O\). The two steps then give at least \(h+2E+2(h+O)\ge5h\). Both are impossible on the repeating direction cycle (even when the two-step gain spans two repeats of a one-step word). A sink direction cannot occur either: if it never leaves the sink it has multiplier one, and if it leaves by the only remaining possibility, the singleton block, it takes \((h,0,h)\) to \((h,h,h)\), forcing a triple on the next step. Thus all blocks in a doubling word must be singletons. Their transfer has the tribonacci Perron multiplier \(\tau\in(\sqrt{2},2)\); a nonnegative nonzero direction for a power must have eigenvalue \(\tau^k\), never two. The barrier concerns a fixed word returning to one affine interface, not higher slopes or transitions between interfaces.
