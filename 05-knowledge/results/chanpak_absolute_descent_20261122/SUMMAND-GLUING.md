# Why physical addition needs more than a shared root

Let \(H(T)\) be the ordinary Hamiltonian path count. Interpret it by the [Grinberg–Stanley odd-cycle packing formula](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.05569), with weight \(2\) for each selected cycle. Pairing compatible packings gives sharp lower bounds that do not depend on how the unfilled cross arcs are oriented.

## Disjoint operands force multiplication

If a completion contains induced \(A\) and \(B\) on disjoint vertex sets, then
\[
H(T)\ge H(A)H(B).
\]
The reason is that every pair of internal weighted packings combines, regardless of any cross arcs. If \(T\) has exactly the union of their vertices, equality holds precisely when each strong component of \(T\) stays inside one side. Indeed a mixed odd cycle adds a positive term. Conversely, if there is no mixed directed triangle, then between a fixed vertex of one side and any strong component of the other side all arcs must be uniform: otherwise strong reachability inside that component gives an arc forming a mixed triangle with the fixed vertex. It follows that between any strong component of \(A\) and any strong component of \(B\) the arcs are uniform. The quotient of these components has no directed triangle, so it is transitive. This proves the equality criterion and gives the opposite construction by ordinal product.

In particular preserving two disjoint nontrivial count witnesses can never give \(H(A)+H(B)-1\), the addition law for \((H-1)/2\).

## A shared root still cannot give an exact reduced sum

Now suppose \(V(A)\cap V(B)=\{v\}\). Put \(a=H(A), b=H(B)\), \(a_0=H(A-v), b_0=H(B-v)\), with \(H(\emptyset)=1\). A pair of internal packings is incompatible exactly when both cover \(v\). Since no directed cycle is internal to both sides, distinct compatible pairs give distinct packings. Thus
\[
H(T)\ge ab-(a-a_0)(b-b_0)=ab_0+ba_0-a_0b_0.
\]
This lower bound is at least \(a+b-1\). To see this, write \(a=a_0+\alpha\), \(b=b_0+\beta\) with \(\alpha,\beta\ge0\), and subtract \(a+b-1\) to obtain \((a_0-1)(b_0-1)+\alpha(b_0-1)+\beta(a_0-1)\). If one side has no cycle through \(v\), the bound is \(ab\), which already exceeds \(a+b-1\) for \(a,b>1\).

If both sides have a cycle through \(v\), each has a directed triangle through \(v\): a shortest directed cycle through \(v\) is a triangle, by a chord from \(v\). The induced union of these two triangles on five vertices is strong regardless of the unfilled cross arcs. Its Hamiltonian path count is at least 9 by [Busch's sharp bound](https://www.combinatorics.org/ojs/index.php/eljc/article/view/v13i1n3) (or by the 16 literal completions below). The pure internal packings on those two triangles account for only \(1+2+2=5\) because they intersect. At least four more weighted packings on their union are therefore mixed; all remain available in \(T\), and none was included in the pure internal term above. Thus in this case
\[
H(T)\ge ab_0+ba_0-a_0b_0+4.
\]

In particular two nontrivial operands meeting in at most one vertex always overshoot the reduced sum by at least four. The bound is sharp already for two triangles. A physical summand compiler using ordinary tournament counts needs a larger overlap, a state read other than the full operand counts, or changes to existing operand edges. This does not prohibit more general arithmetic circuits.

## A finite target for a different, stateful addition architecture

If a reusable marked class at \(H=15\) supports both repeatable transitions \(H\mapsto H+4\) and \(H\mapsto H+10\), it supplies every odd \(H\ge23\): the additive monoid generated by \(2,5\) contains all integers at least 4, so \(15+2\langle2,5\rangle\) includes every required odd. This is a sufficient gate, not one constructed here. No fixed append-only implementation that adds a positive number of active strongly connected vertices at each step can supply even one of these translations indefinitely: order would grow linearly in the number of iterations while strong height grows exponentially in order. Rewiring, carries, or dropping the old core are therefore necessary for such a constant-step gate.

## Two shared vertices can remove the first toll, but not all carries

For arbitrary common induced anchor \(W=V(A)\cap V(B)\), put
\(a_0=H(A-W)\), \(b_0=H(B-W)\), \(w=H(T[W])\). A general bound is
\[
 H(T)\ \ge\ H(A)b_0+H(B)a_0-w a_0b_0.
\]
Indeed allow arbitrary internal packings on one side, but only
packings avoiding \(W\) on the other. In the intersection of the
two resulting collections, all cycles touching \(W\) must lie
entirely in \(W\), so the weighted intersection is \(w a_0b_0\).
Consequently, when the two deletions are transitive, the pure
lower bound is just \(H(A)+H(B)-w\); larger overlap changes the
proper additive baseline.

The first exact nontrivial reduced sum is possible on an overlap
of size two: glue two triangles on the common arc \(a\to b\),
orient both extra vertices \(u\) by \(b\to u\to a\), and choose
either of their mutual orientations. This is a strong
four-vertex tournament of height 5, exactly \(3+3-1\).
However, retaining the same arc and gluing \(k\) triangles
with their \(k\) third vertices ordered transitively gives
\(1+2^k\), not \(1+2k\). Every odd cycle uses \(a\to b\) and
chooses an odd subset of the third vertices. Already the third
triangle forces a carry term and the count is 9 rather than 7.
This is a literal transition from reduced addition to a power
digit, obtained solely by completing the free arcs among leaves.
