# Three-cusp D4 monodromy cannot be involutive, even with partially retained sheets

**Status: PROVED / FINITE-EXACT / INDEPENDENTLY AUDITED, with the stated geometric suppliers.**
The finite theorem below concerns the literal signed permutation group.
The geometric conclusion consumes actual common-access cusp and node
marking, transitivity, retained-page specialization and Euler integration.
It does not assert that arbitrary Artin D4 monodromy is involutive.

## 1. Precise statement and inheritance

Let W be the 192 even signed permutations on four coordinates and use
the marked generators

    a=s12, b=s23, c=s34, d=s3,-4, e=bcb^-1.

Retain the six pairs

    (b,c), (b,d), (a,e), (e^-1ae,b), (a,d), (c,d).       (1)

For every transitive W-set of degree D>1, let a0>0 be the number of
fixed points of b and let f1,...,f6 be the joint fixed counts in (1).
For every integer 1<=k<=min(a0,D-1), define

    Lc_i=max(0,k-a0+f_i,ceil((3k-D)/2)), i=1,2,3,
    Ln_j=max(0,D-2k,D-2a0+f_j),         j=4,5,6,
    B=-2k+Lc_1+Lc_2+Lc_3+Ln_4+Ln_5+Ln_6.              (2)

**Finite theorem.** B<=1 forces D=4, a0=k=2, B=1.
The cusp fixed counts are (1,1,1), and the node fixed counts are a
permutation of (2,0,0). There are six labelled stabilizers with these
properties in the fixed normalization used below.

**Geometric consequence.** Suppose a nonautomorphic planar
polynomial Keller map has whole nonproperness set one irreducible curve
normalized by A1, with exactly three ordinary cusps, N>=3 ordinary
nodes, and no other affine singularities. Suppose its complement is
generated by four positive meridians satisfying the marked Artin D4
relations of the [three-cusp passport](planar_jc48_sep08_three_cusp_passport.md),
with its three cusp pairs and three distinct node pairs paid by actual
local access. Then its positive meridians cannot act as involutions
on the generic sheets. Consequently their common cycle type contains
at least one cycle of length at least three.

An inside-pair change of Hurwitz basis is allowed only with the actual
count-transport proof in
[the new braid supplier, Section 4](planar_jc48_sep08_three_cusp_braid.md).
Its joint fixed counts are unchanged because the local subgroup is
unchanged; the actual retained intersection counts and deleted overlap
counts are preserved by that separate proof. No unsupported replacement
of retained sets by abstract D4 generators is used.

The closest mechanism is
[the full-fixed D4 census](planar_jc48_sep08_d4_involution.md).
Its finite theorem remains valid and frozen. Its full-fixed hypothesis
was an unpaid supplier for more general geometric applications; this
note removes that hypothesis with two elementary lower bounds.
The canonical hostile is the degree-four S4 action, which attains
Euler one. The corrected near miss is treating inertia-fixed sheets as
automatically retained. The least-used operation is to bound actual
set intersections by the number of omitted fixed sheets.

The live concepts are actual retained sets, their fixed-set envelopes,
local access, point stabilizers, and the global Euler sum. Passing to
fixed sets discards deletion data; the deficit a0-k retains exactly
enough information for a valid lower bound. No equality is inferred.

## 2. Two bounds which preserve actual retained information

At an actual cusp let sigma,tau be its two meridians, with

    A subset Fix(sigma), B=(sigma*tau)A subset Fix(tau),
    |A|=|B|=k, n=|A intersect B|.

Write C=Fix(sigma) intersect Fix(tau), |C|=f. Every point of C is
fixed by sigma*tau. Thus A intersect C is contained in B, and

    n>=|A intersect C|>=k-(a0-f).                        (3)

Together with n>=0 and the inherited actual cusp injection
2n>=3k-D, this is the cusp bound in (2). This argument does not
require involutivity; in particular no assumed saturation is hidden
in it. It uses the actual reaccess formula only at the actual cusp.

At an actual node, A and B are k-element subsets of their meridian
fixed sets. Both full fixed sets have size a0 and intersect in f
points. Deleting at most a0-k points from each full fixed set gives

    |A intersect B|>=max(0,f-2(a0-k)).                   (4)

The deleted-set overlap is exactly

    omega=D-2k+|A intersect B|.

It is nonnegative, so (4) yields the node bound in (2). No relation
between choices of retained subsets at different nodes is assumed.
Allowing independent choices only weakens the necessary bound.

For three cusps and N nodes, the inherited exact Euler identity is

    1=-2k+n1+n2+n3+sum_(all N nodes) omega.              (5)

Every additional node contributes a nonnegative summand. Applying
(3)--(4) to the three declared nodes therefore gives 1>=B for every
N>=3. This is the reason the finite test extends beyond exactly
three nodes. It does not reuse the earlier equality formula with an
unrecorded extra node.

## 3. The exhaustive finite universe and computation

Choose a point fixed by b. Its stabilizer H contains b, and the
transitive action is exactly the left-coset action W/H. There is no
change of meridian labels when choosing this point. Unlike the earlier
finite census, H is not required to contain the central element -I.
Actual retained sets need not be invariant under that element, so its
former parity argument is deliberately not an inherited filter.

Starting with <b>, adjoin every element outside every subgroup reached
and take its generated closure. This procedure reaches every overgroup
of <b>: a larger subgroup is generated by adjoining its elements one
at a time. The construction uses one representative per set gH, since
<H,gh>=<H,g>. The verification then separately checks every one-element
extension for every subgroup, without the representative shortcut.
Exactly 53 subgroups occur.

For each subgroup, construct every coset, the complete marked action,
the common meridian fixed count and all six pair counts. Check every
integer 1<=k<=min(a0,D-1). There are 211 such retention trials, with
no bound on D beyond the finite group itself. Only six labelled
subgroups have B<=1; all have D=4, a0=k=2 and the profile stated in
Section 1. All other trials have the integer B>=2.

This proves the finite statement by exhaustive exact computation with
an analytic completeness argument. It does not enumerate hypothetical
polynomial maps or mistake a representation for such a map.

## 4. Paying the involutive group supplier

The marked Artin D4 presentation has the three leaf commutations and
the three length-three braid relations. If its four generator images
are involutions, the action factors through its Coxeter quotient.
The standard identification of that quotient with the even signed
permutations is **CITED**, rather than asserted from a one-way map:
Brandon Crain and Josh Clement, *Lecture 2: Coxeter Groups*, January 7,
2009, Definition 2 and Examples 1--3,
[UC Davis course notes](https://www.math.ucdavis.edu/~anne/WQ2009/MAT280-Lecture2.pdf).
Reverse the four coordinate indices in their standard generators to
obtain the exact marking a=s12,b=s23,c=s34,d=s3,-4 used here. Equivalently
their simple-root marking is e1-e2,e2-e3,e3-e4,e3+e4. This classical
presentation identification supplies a homomorphism FROM the literal
W to the sheet permutation group, even when the representation has a
kernel. Faithfulness is neither assumed nor needed.

All positive meridians of the irreducible curve are conjugate. A
nontrivial transitive cover therefore cannot have an identity meridian;
if every nontrivial cycle had length two, all four images would be
involutions. Actual retained sets give 1<=k<=a0 and k<D, as supplied in
the inherited passport. Sections 2--3 then force D=4. The classical
geometric degree-four exclusion, **CITED** with its original sources
in the inherited passport, rules out this remaining case. It is an
essential geometric input, since the S4 finite action survives.

The proof thus excludes all involutive meridian monodromy under the
stated geometric suppliers, with arbitrary retained subsets and every
N>=3. It leaves monodromy with longer cycles open. In particular cusp
injection saturation by itself does not imply involutivity; longer
even cycles remain a named hostile to that proposed shortcut.
