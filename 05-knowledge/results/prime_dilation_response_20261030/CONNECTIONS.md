## A precise unifying framework

For a specified operation system, keep four objects separate:

1. The algebra or monoid of permitted continuations, including source guards.
2. The response pairing between past histories and future contexts.
3. The integral lattice and its precision-dependent invisible submodule.
4. The subset of states attainable from actual objects, with transported marks.

D5 proves the algebraic version for any finite free commutative algebra and
a scalar functional: the invisible pairing radical is an ideal, and quotienting
by it gives the smallest continuation-preserving algebra. D1--D4 solve this
construction explicitly for an arbitrarily large prime alphabet. D6 then
counts the realized subset under a unit-core hypothesis. D8 proves that
enlarging the allowed alphabet can invalidate the quotient completely.

This supplies a complete example of **small symbolic description, large
linear memory, smaller realized orbit, and operation-sensitive closure**.
It explains why seeking one universal numerical invariant has repeatedly
failed in the repository. The appropriate object is the response together
with its action and realization law. This formulation itself inherits the
repository's guarded-observer and joint-port program; the explicit all-size
integer solution is the advance here.

## Strong next targets

**OPEN A: realize the nonunit-core Smith layers.** D4 computes the complete
linear module for every n,P,M, but actual source trajectories need not fill
that module. Derive exact joint clock stabilizers when C is divisible by ell.
Source57 at P=(7,11,13) is a cheap boundary: its sigma contribution is invisible
through modulus16 and reappears at32, where the actual state count is32 while
the linear module has2048 elements. Its rank-one dark regime is already proved
in D7; the later layers are the target, not another replay of the unit case.

**OPEN B: close under aliquot as well as named multiplication.** The full
dilation trace quotient fails by D8. Determine which source sidecars permit
one, two, or arbitrarily many aliquot operations at a fixed precision, and
prove a hierarchy or finite bound. Exact n is sufficient but expensive;
retaining only a source residue is not automatically enough. This is an
observer problem, not a claim that arbitrary aliquot orbits terminate.

**OPEN C: find a native tournament response algebra with a finite presentation.**
The target is an actual attachment family with legal complement and re-marking
operators, ordered ports, and an invariant integral kernel. D5 transfers only
after closure and its commutativity hypothesis (or an explicitly proved
noncommutative replacement) are established. The existing interval-family
complement exit and the 81/27 pair must be replayed. A compact formula for one
Hamilton-path count is insufficient.

**OPEN D: carry precision and realization into bank indexing.** Incoming113
already solves named-group prime-bank rank/unrank using original periods and
ideal-cut masks. Test whether its completion tables admit smaller exact
observable quotients at a fixed precision without identifying distinct valid
rank intervals. The lower bound must concern its actual consumer; replacing
exact rank by a zero/nonzero predicate changes the problem.
