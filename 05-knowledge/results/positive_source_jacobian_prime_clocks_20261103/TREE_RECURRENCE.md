# Uniform named-port births: an exact recurrence and a complete variance obstruction

Status: **PROVED**, with **FINITE-EXACT** independent path-count controls.
This closes the uniform-port positive-law test in the inherited
[handoff](../primegame_port_spectrum_20261103/HANDOFF.md), not the full
Hamiltonian spectrum conjecture. A source is an actual labelled tournament.
Its birth word retains each parent port and the chronological vertex labels.

## 1. Path covers close after averaging the named ports

Let c_r(T) count unordered collections of r nonempty directed paths covering
all n vertices. Singletons are paths; c_1=H and c_n=1. Set c_0=0 for n>0
and c_r=0 outside 1..n. Let B_x append a vertex z beating precisely old x;
all other old vertices beat z. Let S append a universal sink. Then, for
every tournament and every integer r,

    sum_(x in T) c_r(B_x T)
      = n c_(r-1)(T) + nr c_r(T) + r(r+1)c_(r+1)(T).        (1)
    c_r(S T) = c_(r-1)(T) + r c_r(T).                      (2)

Proof of (1): delete z from a child cover. If z was a singleton, there
are n choices of x. If z ended an old path, its last vertex cannot be x,
giving r(n-1) choices. If z began a path, x is its next vertex, giving r
choices. If z was internal, deletion leaves r+1 paths: choose the ordered
pair to join in r(r+1) ways, and x must be the first vertex of the second
path. Its predecessor belongs to the other path and automatically differs
from x. These disjoint cases reconstruct both the cover and its parent port.
For a sink only the singleton and terminal cases survive, proving (2).

For C_T(t)=sum_r c_r(T)t^r the uniform-port conditional expectation is

    E[C_(B_X T)(t) | T] = t(C_T + C_T') + (t/n) C_T''.    (3)

This quotient is exact for uniform port choice and sink attachment. It is
not closed for an individually selected port, adaptive selection, native
path complement, or second moments. The inherited endpoint-block carrier
retains the extra information for fixed named attachments.

## 2. An all-order integer recurrence and real-rootedness

Start at the one-vertex tournament and choose a parent for every subsequent
birth. There are (n-1)! words at order n; count words, not isomorphism classes.
Let A_(n,r) be the sum of c_r over these words and P_n(t)=sum_r A_(n,r)t^r.
Equation (1) gives

    A_(1,1)=1,
    A_(n+1,r)=n A_(n,r-1)+nr A_(n,r)+r(r+1) A_(n,r+1),   (4)
    P_1=t,  P_(n+1)=t(P_n''+nP_n'+nP_n).                (5)

The first Hamiltonian totals A_(n,1) are

    1,1,4,24,216,2664,42912,871488,21721536,650479680.

Every P_n has only real, nonpositive roots. Explicitly

    P_1=t, P_2=t(t+1), P_3=2t(t+1)(t+2),
    P_4=6t(t+2)(t^2+4t+2).

For n>=4, D^2+nD+n=(D+lambda_+)(D+lambda_-), with
lambda_+/-=(n+/-sqrt(n^2-4n))/2 positive. If a polynomial has real
nonpositive roots, so does f'+lambda f for lambda>0: the logarithmic
derivative sum_i 1/(t-r_i) is strictly decreasing between its poles,
placing a root between consecutive distinct roots and one to the left;
repeated roots persist with multiplicity reduced by one. There are no
positive roots (all terms of f'/f are positive there). Multiplication by
t adds a zero root. This proves the induction, including n=4's repeated
operator factor. Newton's inequalities therefore give log-concavity of
A_(n,r)/binom(n,r). This assertion is about the aggregate P_n, not every
individual tournament's cover polynomial.

## 3. Why this actual-source law cannot finish the positive certificate

Let T_n be generated by independent uniform choices among the currently
available old ports. Uniformity here is on birth words. The six order-four
words have Hamiltonian counts 1,3,5,5,5,5, hence

    E H(T_4)=4,   Var H(T_4)=7/3.                        (6)

Every order-four tournament satisfies

    (c_1,c_2,c_3,c_4)=(H,H+6,6,1).                     (7)

Indeed c_3=6 and c_4=1. Writing q for the number of cyclic triples,
c_2=(4+2q)+3=7+2q: the terms are singleton+triple and two pairs.
The four isomorphism types have (q,H)=(0,1),(1,3),(1,3),(2,5),
or one can enumerate the 24 orders directly, giving H=1+2q.

The linear nonnegative transfer (3) implies for every n>=4

    E[H(T_n) | T_4] = alpha_n H(T_4)+beta_n.             (8)

To calculate alpha, start d_4=(0,1,1,0,0) in c_0..c_4 coordinates and
apply

    d_(n+1,r)=d_(n,r-1)+r d_(n,r)+r(r+1)d_(n,r+1)/n.

Then alpha_n=d_(n,1); alpha_4=1, alpha_5=3/2, alpha_6=27/10.
All d coordinates are nonnegative and alpha_(n+1)>=alpha_n. The law of
total variance now proves the all-order obstruction

    Var H(T_n) >= (7/3)alpha_n^2 >= 21/4 > 4  (n>=5).  (9)

For any real h, E[(H-h)^2]>=Var H. Thus this uniform-port law never
achieves the inherited sufficient certificate E[(H-h)^2]<4 for n>=5.
This remains true even if its mean happens to equal the target exactly.

The small odd-target cases are completely classified: n=1,2 certify only
h=1; n=3 certifies h=1,3 with error2; n=4 certifies h=3,5 with error10/3.
All other odd h fail the strict threshold in this family. At n=5 the actual
variance is35/3, so the propagated-prefix bound is deliberately conservative.

This does not exclude biased or adaptive port laws, different source
families, or deterministic constructors. An unrestricted optimized law is
not a shortcut: a point mass at a target already assumes the desired witness.
The remaining constructive problem is to specify lawful weights from a
smaller certificate and prove the bound uniformly without target conditioning.
