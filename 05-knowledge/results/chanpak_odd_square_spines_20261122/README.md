# The odd-square bracket and the two remaining Chan–Pak spines

Let \(E=2^{60}(2^{61}-1)\). **FINITE-EXACT.** On canonical Farey spine four, two strong 118-vertex tournaments admit a single marked arc reversal with endpoints \(E+d-1\) and \(E+d+1\). The offsets are \(d=-844{,}710\) and \(d=+184{,}814{,}588\). Both endpoints of each edge were counted on its reconstructed vertex rows. For the closer edge the left and right binary addresses are 735435556549430 and 1575929833923500. **OPEN.** The offset zero, the unrestricted search on spines four and eight, and Chan–Pak remain open. [Witnesses and replay](NEAR-WITNESSES.md)

Chan and Pak record the spectrum question as Conjecture 6.2 and credit the March 2016 MathOverflow discussion by bof and Gordon Royle. The single-arc condition studied here asks for a pair of neighboring tournaments in addition to the two prescribed counts. [Chan and Pak, §6.5](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tcs.2024.114776)

**FINITE-EXACT metagraph barrier.** Each pair forms an isolated two-class component of the graph of 118-vertex tournaments under one arc reversal when restricted to a band around \(N=E-1\). For the nearer pair, every route to either target must first visit a tournament with \(\lvert H-N\rvert\ge9{,}860{,}704{,}255{,}316{,}668{,}080\). All 27,612 labelled neighbors of the four hosts were counted exactly. The 51 neutral flips on each host simply transpose twins. [Sharp barriers and all-arc calculation](NEIGHBOR-FLIPS.md)

**FINITE-EXACT repair test.** At either of those near witnesses, change both high words arbitrarily and at most two of the 80 low bits (40 on each flank), in any positions on either tape. No such pair reaches the exact center. A hit must change at least three of those low bits relative to **each** witness, however many high bits are available. [Search and integer certificate](RATIONAL-CARRIES.md#repairing-the-near-witnesses)

At fixed exterior words, allowing **every** replacement of the three interior tournaments of orders 4, 6, 6 and every interior apex tag gives only the original two endpoint counts within \(10^{10}\) of \(E-1\) for each witness. Changing the interior alone cannot supply the missing 844,710. [Core replacement search](CORE-REPAIRS.md)

The primes sharing a bracket between consecutive odd squares with their doubles are exactly \(2,3,11\). Their doubled neighbors include \(7=2\cdot3+1\) and \(21=2\cdot11-1\). Under the shortened \(3x-1\) map on positive integers, \(2,3,11\) all reach 1, 7 belongs to the cycle through 5, and 21 reaches the cycle through 17. The consecutive states 7 and 10 on the middle cycle suggested the normal that proved optimal. Its optimality comes from the projected tournament congruence, including the two physical parities. The exclusion of Hamilton counts 7 and 21 comes from the decomposition into strong tournament components and the bounds on those components. The related prime 11 supplies two distinct clocks. The congruence \(3^5=1\pmod{121}\) slows the ternary clock by one digit at 11, while \(2^{10}-1\) has valuation one. In the inherited varying-spine theorem the binary coefficient at precision \(11^r\) determines the ternary coefficient at precision \(11^{r+1}\). On fixed spines 4 and 8 all finite target congruences can already be met. For these two spines the new search therefore retains the absolute sum, divisor pair, or input row.
