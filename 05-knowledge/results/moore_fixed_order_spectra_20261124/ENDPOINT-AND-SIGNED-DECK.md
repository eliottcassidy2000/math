# Exact endpoint steering and signed Moore-deck separation

Two related uses of an interface. In a tournament the two endpoint transfers can be selected independently and their determinant can be prescribed. In a valid degree-57 graph the positive and negative changed contacts must balance everywhere outside the edit support, which forces many actual edits.

## 1. One physical flip reads an exact determinant

For a nonempty block \(B\) pointed by a common apex \(a\), retain the [four guard coordinates](GUARDS-AND-DECK-DISTANCE.md): \(h=H(B)\), the total ordered two-path covers \(Q\), the covers \(R\) with right path empty or starting at an apex outcontact, and those \(J\) with nonempty left path ending at an apex incontact. Also record \(C=H(B+a)\), the number \(e\) of full paths of \(B\) ending at an incontact, and the number \(s\) of full paths starting at an outcontact. The exact six-site scan now checks that all 48 marked guarded moves have \(\Delta(C,e,s)=(2,2,2)\).

For an ordinal join \(B_1\to B_2\) under the same apex, \(h=h_1h_2,\ Q=Q_1Q_2\), and
\[
 R=(R_1-h_1)Q_2+h_1R_2,\quad
 J=Q_1J_2+J_1h_2,\quad
 e=h_1e_2,\quad s=s_1h_2 .
\]
The reading is
\[
 C=h_1C_2+h_2C_1+(R_1-h_1)J_2-h_1s_2-h_2e_1+e_1s_2 .
\]
Indeed split each two-path cover at the ordinal boundary. According as the right lane visits \(B_1\) and the left lane visits \(B_2\), both, just the second, just the first, or neither, the contributions are \((R_1-h_1)J_2,\ h_1(C_2-s_2),\ h_2(C_1-e_1),\ e_1s_2\). The other coordinate formulas follow by inspecting only the pertinent extremal lane. Consequently in \(U\to Y\to V\), where \(Y\) is the fixed six-site fork, its **one internal arc** changes the common-apex reading by the signed amount
\[
 2\left[e_U h_V-h_U(h_V-s_V)\right].
\]
The four guards of the combined block still agree at the two endpoints. With only the right flank \(V\) the gap is \(2s_V\). Example: take a cyclic triangle \(U\) with \(e_U=2\), and the four-site one-apex word \(100\) as \(V\), with \(h_V=5\) and \(s_V=2\). Exact subset DP verifies the actual 14-site counts \(33575\to33577\).

### The endpoint sums on each strong one-apex flank are complete

Normalize a nontransitive one-apex flank by trimming dominating and dominated padding, obtaining a binary word \(w_1=1,w_L=0\) of the same height \(h\). The inner apex starts one full path. For chain vertex \(i\ge2\) its start count is
\[
 p_i=(1-w_i)+\sum_{j>i,\ w_j=0}2^{j-i-1}
     =(1-w_i)+\sum_{j>i}p_j.
\]
A path starting at \(i\) must use the inner apex after it and exit to chain vertex 1. The part before the apex is increasing, begins at \(i\), ends at a zero \(j\), and the intermediate sites are freely assigned before or after. Reading the \(p_i\) backwards gives all sub-sums from 0 to \(S=\sum_{i\ge2}p_i\), since each new weight is at most one more than the current total. The remaining path-start count at site 1 is \(h-1-S\). By the inversion formula, \(h\le1+2S\): each zero at \(j\) contributes \(2^{j-2}\) to \(S\), at least its possible sum of half-inversion weights from earlier ones. Thus \(h-1-S\le S\); appending the apex weight 1 and the site-1 weight leaves a complete interval of all start sums \(0,\ldots,h\). The analogous end sums follow by converse. Height 1 can be implemented by a singleton.

### Complete attainable signed gap set for fixed flank heights

For arbitrary realizable one-apex heights \(A=h_U,\ B=h_V\), the possible half-gaps are exactly
\[
 d=eB-bA,\qquad 0\le e\le A,\quad0\le b\le B.
\]
In particular \(0\le d\le AB\) occurs if and only if the reflected deficit \(AB-d\) is in the additive numerical semigroup generated by \(A,B\). The coefficients automatically satisfy the bounds: \(AB-d=(A-e)B+bA\le AB\). Negative gaps are obtained by complementing both selected counts.

For coprime \(A,B>1\) the first positive missing half-gap is **\(A+B\)**, so this supplies every even physical gap through \(2(A+B-1)\) on the very same two flank heights. To see it, choose \(1\le e\le A\) by the inverse of \(B\bmod A\). For \(1\le d<A+B\), the positive number \(d\) cannot exceed \(eB\) by a positive multiple of \(A\); also \(eB-d<AB\), so \(b=(eB-d)/A\) is allowed. At \(d=A+B\) the only representative of \(e\) is 1, and it fails. There are exactly \(((A-1)(B-1))/2\) positive missing values in \([1,AB]\): the only repetition of the coprime rectangular map is the two zero corners, since any equality has coordinate differences proportional to \((A,B)\). If a flank has height 1 then all half-gaps up to the product occur. For noncoprime heights a necessary condition is divisibility by the gcd, and the semigroup criterion still applies.

In particular every \(2d>0\) is realized by **one actual arc reversal** with \(O(\log(d+1))\) vertices and height \(O(d^{\log_2 3})\): use only the right flank with word \(1\,0^L\), where \(L\ge1\) is smallest with \(2^L+1\ge d\), and select apex outcontacts of start sum \(d\). Its height is \(2^L+1\). A two-path cover of this flank has at most \(2\cdot3^{L+1}\) descriptions: specify which lane contains the inner apex, then send each chain vertex to the other lane, or to either side of the inner apex in its lane. The full augmented height is at most \(Q(Y)Q(V)=320Q(V)\) because every path yields a two-lane cover. This is a theorem about **prescribing differences**, not prescribing either absolute height.

## 2. From endpoint balance to a 1410-arc Moore-deck exclusion

**Conditional on two valid, distinct normalized degree-57 decks in the same labels.** Continue from the [frozen-probe result](GUARDS-AND-DECK-DISTANCE.md#2-frozen-probes-give-a-minimum-moore-deck-edit-distance). Now the symmetric edge difference is at least **1410** and at least **2826** if every changed vertex loses at least three neighbors. For two decks on the same complement spine these are literal reversed tournament arc positions. No Moore graph is asserted to exist.

Write \(A,A'\) for their adjacencies, \(D=A'-A\), \(X\) for the active vertices, \(x=|X|\), and \(r_v\) for the number of old neighbors lost at \(v\) (equal to the number gained). By the normalization to the hub branch, each satellite sees each other label exactly once among the other nonhub branches: any two distinct labels in the hub comparison must have exactly one common neighboring route, and the equal-label pair is already adjacent. Thus the contact rows are permutations of all labels except the satellite's own. A changed satellite cannot switch just one contact, so \(r=\min_{v\in X}r_v\ge2\). Also \(r\le56\). Write \(M=\sum_{v\in X} r_v\) for the size of the edge symmetric difference; this is even.

Subtract the two Moore identities:
\[
 AD+DA'+D=0.
\]
For active \(v\), the column \(D\mathbf e_v\) has \(r_v\) plus and \(r_v\) minus entries, and \(AD\mathbf e_v\) vanishes outside \(X\), as the corresponding rows of \(D\) vanish. Two consequences are useful.

1. Choose \(v\) with \(r_v=r\), and let \(p,q\) be counts of plus/minus support points seen by a given vertex. Outside \(X\), \(p=q\), so its \(p+q\) incidences are at most \(2pq\). At a vertex in \(X\) seeing at least one support point, beyond its first incidence the excess is bounded by \(\binom p2+\binom q2+2pq\). Since each pair of support points has at most one common neighbor, summing waste over vertices gives at most \(2\binom r2+2r^2=3r^2-r\). There are \(114r\) incidences in total, so \(x\ge r(115-3r)\). Also \(x\ge2r+1\) from the support and \(v\) itself.
2. Every active \(u\) adjacent to \(v\) by a deleted old edge has at most \(r_v\) old neighbors outside \(X\). Each such external neighbor sees \(u\) as a minus support point of \(D\mathbf e_v\) and must see at least one opposite support point. Those plus points are distinct choices across external neighbors: two points \(u\) and that plus point could not share two common neighbors. This is the local balance constraint on the cut boundary.

For integer \(j\) let \(b_j\) count \(u\in X\) with at least \(j\) neighbors outside, and \(a_j\) count \(v\in X\) with \(r_v\ge j\). By (2), each of those \(b_j\) vertices has all its deleted neighbors in that \(a_j\)-set, using at least \(\binom r2\) unordered pairs. Two distinct vertices cannot use the same pair, by square-freeness of the **old** graph. Hence
\[
 b_j\le\min\!\left(x,\left\lfloor\frac{a_j(a_j-1)}{r(r-1)}\right\rfloor\right)
       \le c_x a_j,\qquad
 c_x=\max_{1\le a\le x}\frac{\min(x,\lfloor a(a-1)/(r(r-1))\rfloor)}{a}.
\]
It is important to charge all thresholds to the **same** surplus, not independently to budget \(M\) each time. Indeed \(\sum_{j>r} a_j=M-rx\), and \(b_j\le x\) for \(j\le r\). Combining the degree sum with the centered Moore eigenvalue bound \(7\) gives
\[
 \left\lceil50x(3250-x)/3250\right\rceil
 \ \le e(X,\overline X)=\sum_j b_j\ \le rx+c_x(M-rx).
\]
Evaluate the maximum \(c_x\) by integer comparisons. The replay excludes every candidate \(r,x\) for \(M\le1408\) (639 size checks); with \(r\ge3\) it excludes all \(M\le2824\) (984 checks). In each case test the largest allowed budget because the right side increases with \(M\), restricting \(x\le M/r\) and imposing (1). Evenness gives the stated exclusions. This is stronger than a support-size estimate alone because a small trade would force many large changed degrees simultaneously to balance its unchanged boundary.
