# The double-voltage pentagon and a 540-site order-11 chart

**Conditional on the degree-57 Moore graph having an order-11 symmetry.** By the fixed-point classification this branch fixes a pentagon. There are five fixed sites, 25 port orbits (five size-11 orbits attached to each fixed vertex), and 270 remote orbits. This extends [the 1,120-site difference-basis chart](DIFFERENCE-BASIS-CHARTS.md) and [the voltage row test](COUPLED-AXES-AND-VOLTAGE-BRANCHES.md). All decoders here know the marked indexing of the tournament contacts; we do not claim an unmarked-isomorphism decoder.

## 1. A pentagon on the gap classes excludes the 250-cycle stratum

Write `[d]` for the class of a nonzero phase difference modulo sign in F_11, with representatives 1 through 5. If an orbit has internal edges, it has just one step class `[s]`: two distinct internal step pairs would give a square. Call such an orbit cyclic. The old row equations give three doubled neighbors or one tripled neighbor for a cyclic remote orbit; an independent remote orbit has five doubled neighbors or one triple and two doubles.

A doubled connection with shift gap `[d]` uses that gap at **both** ends: e.g., two vertices of one orbit at that difference already have a common neighbor in the opposite orbit. No orbit can use the same gap twice. If the first orbit is internally cyclic with step `[s]`, it has already spent its gaps `[s]` and `[2s]`, respectively on adjacency and on an internal two-step path. The remaining three gaps must each be used by exactly one pair of cross shifts. Port connections are simple and consume none.

- **No triple touches a cyclic orbit.** Scale its step to 1. The three differences of a triple would need to be the three distinct classes 3, 4 and 5. Translate and, if necessary, negate to make the pair at distance 3 be 0 and 3. The third point must be 4, 7, 5 or 6. Its difference to 3 is then 1, 4, 2 or 3, respectively, never the missing complementary class.
- **Two cyclic orbits of the same step cannot be joined even by a single shift.** Internal parallel edges and the chosen cross matching would produce a square.
- **For unequal cyclic steps**, one doubled connection has to avoid all classes among `s, 2s, t, 2t, s+t, s-t`. The first four exclusions come from internal edges and internal two-step paths; the last two come from a square using one internal edge in each orbit and two cross edges. These are also sufficient for a *two-orbit induced graph* of girth at least five: all short cycles use internal paths, two cross edges as just classified, or four cross edges. The latter would repeat the ordered difference of two shifts, impossible for two distinct shifts over F_11.

| Cyclic step pair | Allowed doubled gap |
|---|---:|
| 1, 2 | 5 |
| 2, 4 | 1 |
| 4, 3 | 2 |
| 3, 5 | 4 |
| 5, 1 | 3 |

All other unequal cyclic pairs can have at most a single connection. In particular the compatibility pentagon `1–2–4–3–5–1` is **exactly the doubling orbit on F_11^× / {±1}**. At a cyclic orbit of step `[s]`, at most one double goes to each neighboring cyclic type on this pentagon. Its remaining gap, `[4s]`, **must** go by a double to an independent orbit. The cyclic-to-cyclic double graph is therefore a collection of paths and cycles; each cycle winds around the type pentagon and has length divisible by five. This is a search constraint, not a construction of any completed quotient.

For completeness, restrict the graph adjacency to a nontrivial Fourier character of the 11-action. Its dimension is 295 and its eigenvalues are 7 and −8. With ζ a primitive eleventh root and c_s cyclic orbits of step `[s]`, its trace is the integer sum of c_s(ζ^s+ζ^-s). The eleventh cyclotomic polynomial implies c_1=...=c_5=t and that the trace is −t. Hence t ≡ 5 (mod 15), from the trace 7a−8(295−a), and 5t≤270. This recovers the possibilities **25, 100, 175, 250** for the cyclic orbit count c. Equivalently the old displacement possibilities are 55, 220, 385, 550 for a generator, as in [Mačaj–Širáň, Lemma 12](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0024379509003735); the generator sees one of the five step types. The extra inequality is new to this chart: there are at least c cyclic-to-independent doubles, but at most 5(270−c) independent double slots. Thus **c is 25, 100 or 175**, with 5, 20 or 35 cycles of each step. At c=175 there are only 95 independent orbits; at least 175 of their slots are occupied by the forced cross-type doubles. Check the five labeled gap channels separately, not merely this total.

The contrast with the [order-3 Petersen branch](COUPLED-AXES-AND-VOLTAGE-BRANCHES.md) is exact: a 3-orbit cannot cycle internally without giving a triangle, so its only repeated connection is a double that lifts to a 6-cycle. At 11, doubling yields the type-pentagon, and a triple cannot touch an internally cyclic orbit. These are genuine roles for 2, 3 and 11. The fact that they are also the only primes for which p and 2p are in the same [odd-square bracket](../procgen_numerology_20260923_odd_square_brackets.md) is a parallel finite-exception problem, not a proof of the voltage constraint.

## 2. From 1,120 to 1,100 literal sites or 540 coded sites

**1,100 literal sites.** In the old chart keep the same 20 moving rows and remove the fixed column as well. For each observed row pair a perfect matching between its 56 columns loses either one edge (fixed to fixed) or two edges (one at each fixed endpoint). The 55-by-55 restriction is respectively perfect or has exactly one unmatched endpoint on each side, so the full matching is restored uniquely. Extend by the four-phase difference basis and then use [the fixed row decoder](MOORE-ROUTE-MACHINE.md). Same marked induced-graph orientation convention, after choosing complement-spine boundaries. We do not reuse the 1,120-site height window for this new chart.

**540 coded sites.** This is a voltage encoding by tournament orientations, not an induced subgraph chart. Use two labelled sites per remote orbit. For each unordered pair of remote orbits the four inter-orbit contacts form a four-bit word relative to the lexicographic baseline. Use codes 1 through 11 for a single shift, 12 for a double and 13 for a triple. Both 0 and 14 mean *no graph edge*. They carry a free bit without changing that answer. Assign each pair to its cyclic owner on the 270 labels (134 forward offsets, ties on offset 135 split by index), so each row owns at least 134 pairs. With remote degree at most 52, at least 82 owned pairs are blank. Read these in lexicographic order, using:

- 3 bits for the internal step (0 or 1 through 5);
- 30 bits for the five port contacts (each chooses amongst 5·11 phase-labeled options);
- 30 bits for the ranked shift subsets of the owned doubles and triples (6 bits per double, 8 per triple, in the same pair order). The row test caps the cost at 30; one triple and two doubles cost only 20.
- On the first 45 rows, 3 more bits each for the global port data.

The fixed pentagon itself is prescribed. Between ports belonging to adjacent fixed points there are no edges (would make a square through the fixed points). A port vertex sees exactly one port vertex over each fixed point at distance two from its own, by the unique two-step route to that point. Thus each of the five distance-two color pairs is an equivariant perfect matching. Store that matching in 27 bits: 7 for the permutation of the five orbit labels, 20 for the five four-bit shifts. This is all 135 bits of global port data. The total row budget is at most 66 bits, below 82. Decoding the nibble categories first fixes the locations of the free bits, so no external guide to the blank contacts is needed. Together these data reconstruct the entire labelled Moore graph. The replay exercises the compressor on a full-size synthetic input; no degree-57 graph is supplied.

The [old 1,120 statistic](../../../07-reflections/metagraph-summand-recursive.md) was the number of labelled unoriented single flips with zero Hamilton-count displacement at order five; it is not the new sample size's origin. The shared operation is to ask what counts as a null move for the **chosen observable**. Here toggling a blank code does not change the quotient's support, but freely carries shift information. This is also the actionable version of the [THM-1120 essential-region idea](../../../01-canon/theorems/THM-1142-essential-region-criterion-proved.md): after fixing the already-covered region, calculate the actual leftover capacity. In this chart the capacity is certified row by row; the old theorem concerns single runner substitutions, not the size of a Moore chart.

## 3. Exact count probes at 11, 25 and 36

An 11-site pulse reads a single internally cyclic orbit. Begin with the Paley orientation (forward differences 1,3,4,5,9). Reverse the eleven contacts of the undirected internal cycle, if present. Multiplication by quadratic residues acts transitively on the five unoriented step classes, so the Paley read detects cyclic **status** and not the step. The subset-endpoint recurrence gives:

| 11-site reading | Exact H | Factorization |
|---|---:|---|
| Paley, no internal cycle | 95,095 | 5·7·11·13·19 |
| Paley, any internal cycle | 93,027 | 3·11·2819 |

All 32 cyclic-invariant orientations on 11 sites give exactly four heights: 92,411; 93,027; 93,467; 95,095. The order-11 action explains divisibility by 11, not by 121 or 3. The computed heights all have exactly one factor 11, and only 93,027 has a factor 3. If an isolated ordered join uses one Paley read for each of the 270 remote orbits, the **census-only** tournament has exact count 95095^(270−c)·93027^c for c in {25,100,175}, so v_11=270 and v_3=c. These are specific odd values, not asserted to be counts of the native graph chart. A compact *fully decodable* 551-vertex alternative joins the 540-site coded chart to just one cyclic pulse. Its count is 93027·H_code, where H_code is odd and unknown but 1≤H_code≤540!<2^4129 (and the tagged total is <2^4146). An ordered join cannot bridge the difference between selecting a residue and prescribing H_code, the same obstruction emphasized by the [prior exact vs. modular 2–3–11 selector](../chanpak_square25_dilation_20261123/README.md).

There is also a smaller **partial** voltage chart where 25 and 36 arise without choosing a target height. Use one site per port orbit (25). On each of the five distance-two color pairs, reverse the actual matching contacts relative to a fixed total order. Its five shifts use 20 ordinary bits in the 25 contacts of a separate unused color pair; biject distance-two color pairs to distance-one color pairs beforehand. The 25-site chart decodes the entire port-to-port lift, not the remote quotient. In the identity-matching, zero-shift control, order the sites by port label first, cycle color second. The tournament is the ordered join of five 5-site charts whose exact count is 11, so it has **161,051 = 11^5** paths. Append one 11-site cyclic-invariant pulse by an ordered join and get a 36-site control with exact count amongst **14,882,883,961; 14,982,091,377; 15,052,953,817; 15,315,144,845**, according to the pulse. In particular the Paley cyclic pulse gives 14,982,091,377. All these 36-site counts have v_11=6. The control is synthetic; the real port matching and its shifts are not known. This 36 is not the earlier [Hoffman–Singleton 6×6 satellite chart](COUPLED-AXES-AND-VOLTAGE-BRANCHES.md) or the [first 36 square-sum labels](../square36_signed_collatz_20261123/CHARACTER.md). The useful shared idea is to preserve the *complete* small local signature before testing the resulting Hamilton count; in the square-sum case the three characters are tied to a bounded label interval, not to these voltages.
