# Coupled row/column actions, exact small heights, and voltage tests for degree 57

[Follow-up: 1120-site order-11 chart and two exact symmetry-based Hoffman–Singleton counts](DIFFERENCE-BASIS-CHARTS.md).

This follows the [edge-rooted grid](EDGE-ROOTED-GRID-AND-NATIVE-WINDOW.md), [current height windows](GRID-INSERTIONS-AND-UNMARKED-ROWS.md), and [route machine](MOORE-ROUTE-MACHINE.md). No degree-57 graph is assumed to exist. The new constraints below concern explicit symmetry branches, not all candidates.

## What the smaller graphs actually do

Fix an **ordered** graph edge and take its edge-rooted satellite grid. We enumerated automorphisms of the full graph by refinement and checked the same pairs directly on the grid: for each row permutation \(\sigma\) and one guessed image under the column permutation \(\tau\), the sharp first-row transports determine all other values of \(\tau\). Test the resulting maps on every transport. All directed edges of each small graph are in one orbit, verified separately.

|---|---|---|---|
| \(C_5\) | \(1\times1\) | trivial | Grid \(1,1\); full marked graph spine \(9,11\); cyclic orientation \(15\) |
| Petersen | \(2\times2\) | \(S_2\times S_2\), independent flips | Grid \(5,5\); full marked graph spine \(5213,6961\). Across complement spines the exact ranges are 4927–13837 and 5571–15571, with holes |
| Hoffman–Singleton | \(6\times6\) | \(720\) paired actions, each projection \(S_6\), coupled by the exceptional outer automorphism | New 8-site labelled phase slice: \(317,499\) in the standard case; packet ranges 39–481 and 201–577. The full 36-site grid still has only the certified window \(2^{53}<H,H^\ast<2^{107}\) for the displayed control, not evaluated counts |

The cycle types swapped by Hoffman–Singleton's outer coupling are \(1^4 2\leftrightarrow2^3\), \(1^3 3\leftrightarrow3^2\), and \(1\,2\,3\leftrightarrow6\), with respectively 15, 40 and 120 elements in each direction; the other cycle types are preserved. The graph also has 720 edge-swappers (36 involutions), versus four swappers (two involutions) in Petersen. These are symmetries of the graph/layout, not necessarily automorphisms of the oriented tournament. This geometric construction of outer \(S_6\) is known; see [Junker, The Hoffman–Singleton graph and outer automorphisms of \(S_6\)](https://home.mathematik.uni-freiburg.de/junker/preprints/mj-hs.pdf). Both replays retain the actual axis labels, not just group orders.

### Exact 8-site Hoffman–Singleton atlas

In the [rank-one affine phase model](EXACT-PHASE-CHARTS.md), normalize \(v_1=1\) and let \(u,v\) permute the nonzero field labels in their other slots, with zero first. Cross incidences are \(y=x+u_iv_j\) modulo 5. Use sites \(U_{1,0},U_{2,0},U_{3,0}\) followed by \(W_{1,1},W_{2,1},W_{1,2},W_{3,1},W_{1,3}\). For each of the three first-cohort \(W\) probes the unique incident head gives its indicated \(u_i\); no hit means omitted head 4. The remaining value of \(u\) is forced. The two singleton probes determine \(v_2,v_3\), and therefore \(v_4\). Preserve the site tags supplied by the displayed order. If \(U_{3,0}\) touches \(W_{1,1}\), instead order the suffix \(W_{1,2},W_{1,1},W_{2,1},W_{3,1},W_{1,3}\). This is also a legal complement spine: \(U_{3,0}\) cannot touch both first-cohort probes, and the adjacent \(W_{1,2},W_{1,1}\) pair is a nonedge. All other consecutive \(W\) pairs are from different cohorts.

Orient forward except on the actual induced graph edges. Subset DP gives **144 distinct ordered count pairs for 144 labelled phase tables**, using this deterministic order rule; the source alone has 101 distinct values and the mate alone 90. Precisely, the standard 8-site masks \(2247684,93950842\) give \(317,499\). Mask \(69356548\) changes just one nonpayload arc of the source and gives 319 with the same recovered table and spine. More generally, allow either displayed order when legal and decorate only the seven nonconsecutive same-side arcs. The resulting exact bank of 170 different lower counts admits an assignment of **144 distinct literal +2 pairs**, one per labelled table, by bipartite matching. The assignment is a finite decoder in this bank; no arbitrary odd height is promised. The compact representation does not supply an exact count for the 36-site grid or the native 50-site graph-spine chart. For comparison the older 14-site phase code gives standard \(5119,65647\), while the 50-site phase **code** gives \(356498466438421,129545487780991322017\); neither is the native 50-site chart.

## General constraints on moving both axes

A coupled action with \(a\) fixed rows and \(b\) fixed columns fixes exactly \(2+a+b+ab\) sites of the full graph. If \(a,b>0\), a fixed satellite has exactly \(a-1\) fixed satellite neighbors by the row matchings and exactly \(b-1\) by the columns. Hence \(a=b\). The fixed graph is then Moore of degree \(a+1\), since unique common neighbors of fixed nonadjacent pairs are fixed. If an element fixes a coordinate on **both** sides, so do all its powers; their equal fixed counts on the two axes force **equal full cycle types**, by inversion of the power-fixed counts. The genuinely off-class couplings in the table all have a derangement on at least one side.

For degree 57, a nontrivial odd prime-order action fixing the root edge pointwise has these candidates: order 3 with \(a=b=2\) (Petersen); order 5 with \(a=b=1\) or 6 (\(C_5\) or Hoffman–Singleton); order 11 with \(a=b=1\); or order 7 with one fixed count zero (a fixed star). These follow from the congruence \(a,b\equiv56\bmod p\) and the fixed-subgraph classification, and agree with [Mačaj and Širáň, Lemma 4](https://www.academia.edu/178604545/Search_for_properties_of_the_missing_Moore_graph). [Ishida's no-involution preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.29183) excludes order 2, hence any swap of the two ends by an automorphism. In particular do not copy the smaller edge-swap constructions literally.

A stronger matching of axes holds for any \(p\)-group fixing an edge, \(p=3,5,11\). The fixed coordinate counts for **each subgroup** are positive (orbit counting modulo \(p\)) and equal by the same neighbor argument. Equal subgroup fixed counts determine a finite group-set: decompose into coset orbits \(H/K\) and compare the marks in descending stabilizer order, where the conjugate diagonal marks are nonzero. Thus the two axes are isomorphic as permutation \(H\)-sets, not just elementwise equal in cycle type. For these \(p\)-groups a simultaneous labeling makes the group permutations identical, but this does **not** make the matching tables identical or symmetric in their two coordinates.

## New voltage constraints: what lies beyond the fixed core

Take a prime-order symmetry of a hypothetical degree-57 graph, fixing a Moore core of \(f\) vertices. Call a nonfixed orbit a *port* if its vertices are adjacent to the core, and *remote* otherwise. No nonfixed vertex has two fixed neighbors, since its orbit would supply multiple common neighbors. Each fixed vertex has \(57-d_{\rm core}\) of moved neighbors, where \(d_{\rm core}\) is its degree in the fixed core. These are whole \(p\)-orbits with no internal edges (they share their fixed neighbor). Every remote vertex has exactly one neighbor in the port orbits of each fixed core vertex, by unique two-step routing. Therefore each remote orbit has \(f\) edges per vertex into ports, distributed \(0/1\) by orbit, and \(57-f\) to other remote vertices. Let \(F\) be the matrix whose entries are the numbers of neighbors in the corresponding remote orbit. Equal orbit sizes make it symmetric, including the diagonal internal-orbit degree.

The full equitable quotient of \(A^2+A=56I+J\) has right side \(56I+\mathbf1\cdot(\text{orbit sizes})^{\mathsf T}\). Its remote diagonals now give the following *integer* row filter:
\[
                         \sum_j F_{ij}=57-f,\qquad
                         \sum_j F_{ij}^2+F_{ii}=56+p-f.              \tag{1}
\]
In an odd cyclic regular orbit the internal degree is 0 or 2: two non-opposite step pairs would create a 4-cycle. A block between two moving orbits is given by the subset of shifts in \(\mathbb Z/p\) that connect them.

* **Order 3 fixing Petersen:** there are 180 port orbits, 900 remote orbits. A remote orbit has no internal arcs in the graph (three would be a triangle). With \(f=10,p=3\), (1) forces exactly one doubled orbit connection and 45 single connections at every remote orbit. Hence the doubled connections form a perfect matching on the 900 remote orbits. Each doubled pair lifts to a 6-cycle: the two distinct shifts form two perfect matchings whose relative nonzero translation on three sites is transitive. A search must therefore place **450 disjoint induced six-cycles** on the 2700 remote vertices, before placing the other remote edges.
* **Order 5 fixing Hoffman–Singleton:** there are 500 port orbits, 140 remote orbits. By (1), every remote orbit is either an internal pentagon with five simple remote connections, or an independent orbit with two double and three simple connections. Double edges among the independent orbits make a degree-two graph. The two shift differences incident at any such orbit must be of opposite types \(\{\pm1\}\), \(\{\pm2\}\), otherwise two vertices of that orbit have two common neighbors. The type is the same at both ends of a double edge. So these cycles are even. Length four is impossible: from four two-element shift sets around the cycle one can select shifts summing to zero modulo 5 (the sumset of four two-element subsets is the entire field). This would lift a 4-cycle. Thus the double-edge cycles have even length **at least six**, a constraint not contained in the row degree/square counts alone. If \(c\) of the remote orbits are pentagons, the 690-orbit quotient has trace \(2c\). Its eigenvalues are 57 once and otherwise \(7,-8\), so \(2c\equiv5\bmod15\). Since the independent cycles have even lengths, \(c\) is even. The candidate values are \(10,40,70,100,130\).
* **Order 11 fixing \(C_5\):** there are 25 port and 270 remote orbits. The same test allows five doubles or one triple plus two doubles if there is no internal cycle. With internal degree 2 it allows one triple or three doubles. Fill each remaining slot with single connections to give the total degree. This is a necessary row test, not an exclusion.

**Search bets, not conclusions.** I would test the order-5 / fixed-Hoffman–Singleton case first among the geometrically seeded models. At the extremal \(c=130\), its independent part must be a **single doubled decagon**, with alternating shift types; this is a concrete small motif to try to extend or rule out. For maximal permutation reduction, compare order 11 / fixed pentagon: its 56 rows give 140 unordered row-pair orbits, versus 320 (15 already fixed in the core) for the order-5 / Hoffman–Singleton seed. Neither count measures the hardness of the whole search. In the order-3 case I would seed the 450 paired lifts before exploring unrestricted matchings. An asymmetric graph remains possible, and no native degree-57 Hamilton odd value can yet be inferred from the small chart values.

The lack of a large \(S_6\)-style action does not rule out *equivariant* tournament charts: an odd-order symmetry group never reverses an unordered pair within its orbit, so one can choose an invariant base orientation on each pair orbit and reverse the represented graph edges. Any retained group acts freely on ordered Hamilton paths, so its order divides the chart's path count. This predicts a divisibility condition **only for deliberately equivariant charts**, not the ordinary monotone or cyclic row orders.
