# Converse is not orientation reversal in the native linking construction

## No antiisometry

The native literal complement-line graph has a nondegenerate orthogonal
2-primary linking summand generated by g with

    ord(g)=256,       b(g,g)=1/256,
    K(G)[2^∞] = <g> orthogonal-sum H,       exponent(H)≤4.

This is the frozen Session39 independent replay of the Session38 group
decomposition, reconciled with the incoming Hall/Bott packet's generator
105/256. The normalized generator used here has self-link 1/256.

Every element x of order 256 has a unique expression x=a g+h, with a odd
and h in H. Orthogonality gives

    256 b(x,x) = a² + 256 b(h,h) ≡1 modulo eight.       (1)

Indeed b(h,h) has denominator dividing four, so its contribution is a
multiple of 64; every odd square is one modulo eight. The expression on
the left is a residue modulo 256 and its reduction modulo eight is
well-defined.

An antiisometry would be a group automorphism f satisfying
b(f(x),f(y))=-b(x,y) for every x,y. It would send g to an order-256 element
with self-link -1/256, whose numerator is seven modulo eight, contradicting
(1). Therefore the native 2-primary linking form has **no antiisometry**.
An antiisometry of the full critical pairing would restrict to its
characteristic 2-primary subgroup, so it is impossible too, regardless of
the still-uncomputed odd-primary factors.

The proof uses the unique highest cyclic summand and the lower exponent
bound. It does not extrapolate to a repeated highest factor, a smaller
exponent gap, or other tournament orders. The elementary positive control
<1/2> is self-negative, so there is no general claim that discriminant
pairings can never be self-reversing.

## What tournament converse actually does

Converse sends T to T^op. A merged vertex is the unordered converse orbit
{[T],[T^op]}, so converse fixes every merged vertex. On fixed-path
presentations it is represented by reversal of the path labels, the
reflection R of free cells. Reflection commutes with complement C.

There is an important distinction between three levels:

1. Every merged vertex and every aggregated edge multiplicity is fixed.
2. Individual literal edges need not be fixed: each black edge is exchanged
   with its reflected twin, while blue edges are fixed. After quotienting
   those black twin pairs, the fundamental edge objects are fixed too.
3. On the vertex-divisor presentation of the critical group, the induced
   map is the identity. It therefore preserves b, not -b. Edge permutations
   can act on cycle coordinates without changing this conclusion.

For item 2, the closing free cell is reflection-fixed but complement flips
it. Thus R(x) cannot equal C(x). A complement edge {x,C(x)} is reflection-
fixed exactly when R(x)=x, which is the definition of its blue color.
This is why black edges have two-element reflection orbits, including
black loops at a merged vertex.

## The precise negative connection

In the formal integral-matrix construction K=A, reversing the sign gives
K=-A. The underlying cokernel is the same, while its finite linking form
is negated because (-A)^(-1)=-A^(-1). An internal identification of the
native finite linking data with its orientation-reversed version would
therefore require an antiisometry. None exists at order eight.

Consequently tournament converse in this converse-merged construction
cannot be identified with orientation reversal, or with time reversal
when that phrase means the formal operation K→-K. It acts as the identity
on the critical-group data instead. No change of anyon/divisor labels can
repair the proposed identification, since the obstruction excludes every
antiisometry, not merely the identity map.

This says nothing about central charges, physical realizability, a Hall
fluid, or other ways to associate a physical model to tournaments. It is
exactly a typed obstruction to one proposed bridge, with its source,
target and operation specified.
