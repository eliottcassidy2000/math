## 1. Integral lattice and algebra generation

Let `R=Z[P]/(P^5-1)` and `A=P+P^2`. In the ordered basis
`1,P,P^2,P^3,P^4`, the columns `1,A,A^2,A^3,A^4` form

```
1 0 0 3 4
0 1 0 1 6
0 1 1 0 4
0 0 2 1 1
0 0 1 3 1
```

Independent cyclic convolution produces this matrix. A separate Leibniz
calculation gives determinant -11. The gcds of all minors of sizes 1 through
5 are `1,1,1,1,11`, so Smith invariants are `1,1,1,1,11`. This checks all
251 nonempty minors, not a numerical determinant approximation.

The identity `A^5=5A^2+5A+2` proves that **every** integer polynomial in A is
in the displayed five-column lattice. Thus the lattice is exactly Z[A], not
merely a subset generated by some low powers. Its additive index in R is 11.
The constructive rational inverse also passes independently:

`11P=5A^4-A^3-2A^2-18A-17`.

Consequently `Q[A]=Q[P]`, while the integral rings differ.

## 2. The defect is between factors, not within the cyclotomic order

In `O=Z[zeta_5]`, put `lambda=zeta_5+zeta_5^2`. Exact reduction modulo Phi5
verifies

`zeta_5=-lambda^3-2lambda^2-3lambda-2`.

Both inclusions are integral polynomial expressions, so `Z[lambda]=Z[zeta_5]`.
There is no extra index eleven in this cyclotomic factor. Independently checked trace discriminants are 3125 for R, 378125 for Z[A], and 125 for either cyclotomic power basis; the full-order ratio is exactly 11 squared. In the full rank-five
ring the same inverse expression equals `P-5J`, where `J=1+P+...+P^4`;
therefore confusing the cyclotomic relation `J=0` with the full ring would be
an actual error.

The factorization `lambda-2=(zeta_5-1)(zeta_5+2)` has norm factors 5 and 11,
independently computed as four-dimensional multiplication determinants.
Equivalently the full-ring index is described without any terminology about
orders: evaluation at P=1 and P=9 defines two ring maps from R to F11, and

`Z[A] = { r in R : r(1)=r(9) mod 11 }`.

Indeed both evaluations send A to 2, so Z[A] is contained in this equalizer.
The difference of evaluations is an additive surjection to F11, since it
sends P to 8. Its kernel has index 11 and therefore equals Z[A]. This is a
precise additional congruence tying together the augmentation component and
a cyclotomic residue component. The older norm-five congruence is already
present in Z[C5]; the new norm-eleven congruence explains the extra index.

**Type boundary:** the difference of two evaluations is an additive map, not
in general a ring homomorphism. `R/Z[A]` here means an additive module cokernel,
not a quotient ring. A proper unital subring cannot be an ideal of R.

## 3. Reduction modulo an arbitrary positive integer

For every positive m, the generated subalgebra in R/mR is the image of Z[A].
The additive cokernel is

`R/(Z[A]+mR) = (R/Z[A])/m(R/Z[A]) ~= Z/gcd(m,11)Z`.

This follows either from the Smith form or from right exactness of quotienting
modules; no flatness assumption is used. Therefore full algebra generation
holds exactly when `gcd(m,11)=1`, equivalently `11` does not divide m. If 11
divides m, the additive cokernel has order **11**, even when m is divisible by
121 or 1331. The defect does not grow as `11^v_11(m)`.

Modulo 11, the five P-eigenvalues are `1,3,4,5,9`, and A takes the respective
values `2,1,9,8,2`. Thus the split semisimple algebra has one collision, with

`chi_A(x)=(x-2)^2(x-1)(x-8)(x-9)`.

The generated subalgebra has dimension four and identifies precisely the two
coordinates at P=1 and P=9.

Modulo 5, by contrast, all eigenvalues coalesce, yet A still generates the
entire five-dimensional nonsemisimple algebra: the displayed denominator 11
is invertible modulo 5. The characteristic polynomial is `(x-2)^5`, and P-1
is nonzero nilpotent. Eigenvalue-support counting is therefore not a valid
universal test for algebra generation outside the split semisimple case.

## 4. Sharp boundary: full labeled adjacency recovers the generator

The integral obstruction applies only to ordinary polynomial functional
calculus. It does not imply loss of P from the full labeled adjacency matrix.
Independent integer matrix calculations verify, for all four orientations,

`J=I+A+A^T`,

`P=5J-A^3-2A^2-3A-2I`,

`P=A-A circ A^2`.

For the last identity, A squared has shift coefficients `(0,0,1,2,1)`;
entrywise multiplication with A keeps exactly the P-squared shift. Hence
subtracting that Hadamard product recovers P. Integer polynomial identities
and disjoint shift supports make these formulas valid over every commutative
coefficient ring, including characteristic 11. Eleven explicit residue rings
are also checked. Thus adjoining J or transpose restores R, and allowing the
specified entrywise operation also recovers the generator. The producer's
sharp distinction between restricted functional calculus and graph data is
correct and essential.
