## 1. The numerical assignment, not the picture, controls magic

A 3-by-3 magic square has mean equal to its center and four opposite pairs
summing to twice that mean. For `f(k)=Ak^2+Bk+C`, `k=-4,...,4`, the third
centered moment is `(44/3)A(65A^2+63B^2)`. Over the reals it vanishes only
when `A=0`. This is the mechanism behind the signed-root arithmetic-
progression no-go, not a large bounded search. Its general odd-packet version
holds on at least five equally spaced arguments; the three-point boundary
has real exceptions and is recorded.

The positive continuation is exact and sharp through degree3. For
`Ak^3+Bk^2+Ck+D`, the complete criterion is
`B=0 and (A=0 or C=-15A)`. The 945 center/pair systems cover every real
coefficient vector, not a coefficient-height box. Their ranks are914 at3,
30 at2, and1 at1. Only the odd plane and four nonodd rays survive central
symmetry; all four nonodd rays fail the magic-quartet constraint. On the
odd plane, all384 signed matchings leave only `C/A=-15` when `A!=0`.
Independent elimination and matching methods reproduce the classification.

The unique nonlinear family supplies an ordinary magic array. Turning all
nine values into distinct integer squares is a separate requirement, not a
consequence of the classification. Conversely, an arbitrary nonlinear
root assignment lies outside the excluded arithmetic-progression class.

## 2. Cycles need carries; octonions need signs

The user's nine-state sequence is the `+4` clock on `Z/9`. Its two ternary
digits require carry, and the absence of an order9 affine permutation of
`F3^2` rules out an affine relabeling. Odd-cycle adjacency also rules out a
hypercube embedding. In the eight-label octonion model, every homomorphism
`C9 -> F2^3` is trivial, and a permutation action on eight labels has only
one- or three-cycles. These are target-specific obstructions, not a ban on
all eight-dimensional representations of `C9`.

Octonion labels alone forget the sign cochain and its determinant
associator. Reinstating that data yields two actual bridges: a selected
Boolean inclusion lattice of coordinate subalgebras, and the diagonal
character action shared with multiquadratic Galois theory. A linear
transport to the rational octonion algebra preserves that action and the
fixed-subobject dimensions, not multiplication. The 16-element full
subspace lattice and its28 selected eight-corner cubes must stay distinct.
The repository's Lucas cube at index5 has11 vertices and15 edges, so it is
not silently the same eight-corner object either.

## 3. One direct magic/octonion bridge preserves the actual equations

With the declared clockwise perimeter labels, left multiplication by `e4`
swaps opposite root coordinates with signs and has square `-I` on the
perimeter. Squaring roots erases those signs, leaving exactly the grid
half-turn. This map preserves all rows, columns and diagonals as a family,
hence the full eight-line magic predicate and the squared-entry multiset.
It is a chosen signed lift of a known symmetry, not a solver, a new squared
multiset, or an octonion algebra automorphism.

This bridge also identifies the missing condition in the weaker norm
analogy. Nine distinct positive integer roots can have the correct norm and
all opposite squared pairs equal, while four outer line sums still fail.
The actual line-incidence predicate is indispensable. Six of the other
nontrivial XOR translations fail it on explicit controls.

## Explicit projective-point / puzzle / tournament construction

A further **PROVED / FINITE-EXACT** bridge starts with explicitly normalized
points of `PG(2,3)` and the legal Conway-puzzle rule: a hole move swaps its
endpoints and the other two points on their four-point line.

The closed word `[0,1,3,2,4,0]` induces an order-five permutation `g`.
Its selected orbit is `(3,1,4,2,12)`. The closed word
`[0,1,7,12,9,4,0]` induces an order-four permutation `h` with
`hgh^-1=g^2`. They generate an explicit order20 subgroup acting faithfully
on that orbit. Ordering it by `g` and taking forward steps1 and2 gives the
regular five-tournament; `h` rotates its four orientation choices.

This is a concrete projective-points → legal puzzle words → cyclic orbit →
tournament construction, with every word and permutation verified. It is
**not a projective collineation**: `g` sends one named line to a nonline.
It is not an action of all puzzle permutations on this fixed five-set, not
an individual graph's `5:4` automorphism group, and not a Baby Monster
intertwiner. The source choices, unused eight positions, line incidence and
word history are not hidden by the graph output.
