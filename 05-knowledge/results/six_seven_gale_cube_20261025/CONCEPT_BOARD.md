## Leads allowed to run, and what they produced

1. **Six/six duality.** The equality of parameter and relation dimensions
   led to an actual Gale matrix calculation. It returns the six-line
   configuration by an explicit invertible matrix, permutation, and six
   scalars. Changing square roots to fourth roots separates that balance
   from the K3 condition, locating the scope.
2. **Seven K3 contributions to p_g=7.** Character decomposition explains the
   seven-dimensional canonical space using six-line double planes. This
   does not identify the seven canonical characters with a Fano plane:
   their binary rank is six and they have no three-term relation.
3. **Seven and the Fano plane anyway.** Changing the carrier to quotient
   inertia succeeds. The simplex/Hamming code gives Campedelli quotients;
   a geometric concurrent triple that is also a Fano line is exactly a
   fixed-point obstruction.
4. **Six and Enriques; seven and Campedelli.** The same support/inertia
   test gives free involutions on the six-root K3 and free groups of order
   eight on the seven-root resolution. This is an operation-level bridge
   between classical surface types, not a claim that they are isomorphic.
5. **A cube inside the hourglass.** The central deletion produces eight
   conics with the cube adjacency graph, two geometric intersections per
   edge. The graph arises from three independent sign choices and is
   checked against actual equations. It is not imposed as a drawing.
6. **Sixteen equals sixteen.** K^2=16 and the hourglass's sixteen maximal
   free kernels initially agree. A second seven-root subset with the same
   K^2,p_g and node count has seventeen kernels. The strongest survivor is
   an incidence-controlled quotient count, not a formula from K^2.
7. **Twenty-four equals twenty-four.** The K3 Euler number and the hourglass
   node count coincide, but the latter varies among seven-root subsets.
   A meaningful replacement is the actual double-cover Euler calculation:
   resolving the cube branch nodes gives e=2(24+24)-16=80. The outer-root
   description gives e=2(24+8)+16=80 by a different branch divisor.
8. **F1 seven-support reflection.** Incoming `reflection_bridge_20261025`
   gives a real seven-point set map that loses four Fano lines. Under an arbitrary good inertia labeling, it creates a dependent
   geometric triple and destroys the free action. With an incidence-adapted
   labeling it instead realizes the geometric half-turn and preserves
   quotient type. The marking is an essential sidecar; see the corrected
   distinction in `COMBINATORIAL_BRIDGES.md`, §7.
9. **Other sevens in LRC and tournaments.** THM-2523,
   `THM-2523-chi7-hamilton-energy-split-form-and-fano-boundary.md`, has six
