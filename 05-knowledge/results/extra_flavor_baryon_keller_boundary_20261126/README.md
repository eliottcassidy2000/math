# One extra color flavor: Keller lifts and the scheme over the zero minor vector

This extends [the single-determinant calculation](../null_boundary_baryon_radial_jacobian_20261126/). It uses the same distinction between retaining boundary data and projecting away intermediate charged states that motivated the comparison with [Chandrasekaran, §§3.6–4](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34924v1#S3.SS6). No gravity or QCD-dynamics hypothesis is needed. The new model has independent baryons, a full shift normal form, and a nonreduced classical fiber that detects the origin. It does not establish Keller invertibility.

## Coordinates, Wick law, and radial moments

Let $Z$ be a $d$ by $m=d+1$ matrix, $d\ge2$, with independent variance-one standard complex Gaussian entries. Color acts on the left by $SU(d)$, and $u_i$ is the signed minor omitting column $i$. The holomorphic invariant ring is $\mathbb C[u_1,\ldots,u_m]$. Indeed invariance extends to $SL(d,\mathbb C)$, and the wedge of the rows determines the full-rank orbit. Every nonzero $d$-wedge in dimension $d+1$ is decomposable. On $u_i\ne0$, choose the corresponding square block diagonal of determinant equal to the required unsigned minor; the omitted column is given by Cramer's rule. Thus an invariant polynomial is regular in each $\mathbb C[u,1/u_i]$, whose intersection is the polynomial ring.

The flavor action on signed minors is $Q\mapsto\det(Q)Q^{-T}$, surjective on $U(m)$: for any unitary $V$, take $Q=aV^{-T}$ with $a^{m-1}=\det V$. A single minor, conditioned on its first $d-1$ Gaussian columns, has the law of a circular Gaussian of variance their squared volume. Gaussian Gram–Schmidt makes this volume a product $W$ of independent gamma variables with shapes $d,d-1,\ldots,2$. Flavor unitary invariance identifies all real linear projections with those of $\sqrt W\,G$, for an independent standard complex Gaussian vector $G$ of length $m$. Hence these vectors have the same joint law. In particular the monomials $u^\alpha$ are orthogonal, with squared norms
\[
\alpha!H(n),\qquad H(n)=\prod_{j=1}^{d-1}(n+j)!/j!,\qquad n=|\alpha|.
\]
For every total-degree homogeneous polynomial $P(u,\bar u)$ of degree $k$, each moment equals the corresponding independent-Gaussian moment of $P$ times the strictly positive $\mathbb E W^{kn/2}$ (for moment order $n$). This is an exact reduction, not a claim that the several-variable Gaussian problem is solved.

There is also an inhomogeneous exact sector. Put $q=u^a\bar u^b$ with $a\ne b$, $a,b$ nonnegative multiindices, and $C=|a|+|b|$. Torus phases kill $q^i\bar q^j$ for $i\ne j$; on the diagonal the weight is
\[
\mathbb E |q|^{2n} = H(Cn)\prod_i ((a_i+b_i)n)!.
\]
Omitting zero-slope factors gives exactly the monotone affine factorial weights treated in the single-determinant note. Therefore a nonzero polynomial in $(q,\bar q)$ has all positive moments zero iff its exponents lie strictly on one side of $i=j$. On that side any fixed ambient polynomial multiplier also has eventual zero moments: the character weights of powers lie in a fixed strict cone on the ray $a-b$, whereas the multiplier has finite character support under the minor flavor torus. This extends the rank-one criterion to channels involving several different baryons without asserting the general inhomogeneous several-variable criterion.

## Quantum normal form and the marked Keller condition

Write $U_i$ for multiplication, $E_{ij}=u_i\partial_j$, $N=\sum E_{ii}$, and $g(T)=\prod_{j=1}^{d-1}(T+j)$. The minor constant-coefficient derivative restricts to $D_i=\partial_i g(N)$ by the Wick adjoint. The flavor infinitesimal action $A\mapsto\operatorname{tr}(A)I-A^T$ is invertible on $\mathfrak{gl}_m$, so all $E_{ij}$ occur as restrictions too.

Let $W_s$ denote the total-degree shift $s$ subspace of the ordinary Weyl algebra. The restriction algebra $\mathcal A$ of color-invariant ambient polynomial differential operators is
\[
\mathcal A_s=W_s\quad(s\ge0),\qquad
\mathcal A_{-r}=W_{-r}\prod_{j=0}^{r-1}g(N-j)\quad(r\ge1),
\]
where the product acts on the right. Restriction stays polynomial differential: ambient order at most $k$ implies the $(k+1)$-fold commutator with invariant multiplications vanishes, which is the order test on a smooth polynomial ring. Separate shifts using the ambient polynomial grading. Every normally ordered Weyl monomial of nonnegative shift is in the algebra generated by $U,E$, by pairing multipliers with derivatives and subtracting lower-order commutator terms. Wick adjoints preserve the ambient equivariance and transport $W_r$ bijectively onto the stated negative part: compared with ordinary Gaussian adjunction the factor on input degree $n$ is $H(n)/H(n-r)$. This gives equality and proves generation by $U,E,D$.

Let $F$ be a polynomial self-map of $\mathbb C^m$ with nonzero constant Jacobian. The polynomial commuting fields $X_j$ defined by $X_j F_k=\delta_{jk}$ give the ordinary Weyl lift. This induces an endomorphism of $\mathcal A$ precisely when $F(0)=0$, and every endomorphism of $\mathcal A$ with $U\mapsto F(U)$ is this one followed by $\exp(\operatorname{ad}h(U))$ for a polynomial $h$ (unique modulo constants).

* If $F(0)=0$, the fields $F_iX_j$ have no negative shift, and $N'=\sum_i F_iX_i$ has degree-zero shift $N$. The only negative shift of $X_jg(N')$ is its shift minus one, the constant linear combination of $\partial_i g(N)$ coming from the constant coefficients of $X_j$.
* In any lift the image of $E_{ij}$ differs from $F_iX_j$ by a multiplication $f_{ij}$: the difference commutes with all $F_k$. Differentiating a nonzero positive-order principal symbol in the momentum variables against the invertible Jacobian would contradict such commutation. If some $F_i(0)\ne0$, a nonzero constant-coefficient first derivative occurs at shift minus one, forbidden by the normal form.
* The relations $U_a E_{ij}=U_i E_{aj}$ force $f_{ij}=F_i h_j$. When $i\ne a$ the two $F$ components are coprime since otherwise their Jacobian rows would be dependent at a zero of a common irreducible factor. The commutators of the $E$ imply $X_jh_k=X_kh_j$: for $j\ne k$ use the two operators with common first index $i$, then the zeroth-order difference is $F_i^2(X_jh_k-X_kh_j)$. Thus $\sum h_j\,dF_j$ is closed and polynomially exact (integrate each homogeneous piece along radial lines). Choose $h$ with $X_jh=-h_j$. The exponential is finite on each operator because commutation with a multiplication lowers differential order, and it and its inverse preserve $\mathcal A$. Finally $U_a D_j=E_{aj}g(N)$ fixes the image of $D_j$ uniquely.
* A lift is invertible exactly when $F$ is polynomially invertible. Indeed it preserves Weyl differential order exactly by the leading symbol formula and algebraic independence of $F$. Surjectivity of the lift therefore implies surjectivity on order zero, where the map is substitution by $F$. Conversely $F^{-1}$ also fixes the origin and gives the inverse of the untwisted lift.

## Classical boundary: the entire special fiber, including nilpotents

For the weight $d$ on $U,D$ and $2$ on $E$, the associated graded embeds in $\mathbb C[u,\xi]$ as the algebra $R$ generated by
\[
u_i,\qquad e_{ij}=u_i\xi_j,\qquad w_j=\xi_j s^{d-1},\qquad s=\sum_i u_i\xi_i.
\]
In detail, combine shifts with differential order: an operator of shift $s_0$ and order $k$ has weight $d s_0+2k$. For nonnegative shift the normal form yields all monomial leading symbols in that range. For shift $-r$ it gives precisely the normally ordered symbols multiplied by $s^{(d-1)r}$; the remaining symbol has momentum degree at least $r$. These are generated by the displayed $w,e$. The filtration is nonnegative on $\mathcal A$: negative shift $-r$ requires order at least $dr$. It induces the usual Poisson bracket on the subalgebra, of weight minus two. Over each $u_i\ne0$, recovering $\xi_j=e_{ij}/u_i$ gives the ordinary cotangent chart.

Let $B$ be the rank-at-most-one matrix coordinate ring on the $(m+1)$ by $m$ matrix whose first $m$ rows are $e$ and whose last row is $w$ (all $2$ by $2$ minors are zero). Put $\sigma=\sum_i e_{ii}$ in $B$. The scheme-theoretic fiber over $u=0$ is
\[
R/(u_1,\ldots,u_m)\ \cong\ B/(\sigma^{d-1}e_{ij}:1\le i,j\le m).
\]
Here is the elimination proof. In bidegree $(a,b)=(\# e,\# w)$ the ring $B$ has the ordinary rank-one parameterization $e_{ij}=v_i\xi_j,\ w_j=t\xi_j$. It spans the whole space $t^b\mathbb C[v,\xi]_{(a,a+b)}$ since the factors can be paired arbitrarily. Substitution $v=u,\ t=s^{d-1}$ is injective: each component is multiplied by a nonzero polynomial, and distinct $(a,b)$ give distinct ambient bidegrees $(a+(d-1)b,a+db)$. Thus no extra relations exist in $B$ before quotienting by the base ideal.

In that ambient bidegree, a monomial in $(u)R$ must use $b+h$ factors $w$ if it uses $h\ge1$ naked $u$ factors. Its count of $e$ is then $a-dh$. Hence there are none if $a<d$. For $a\ge d$ already $h=1$ spans exactly
\[
s^{(d-1)(b+1)}\,\mathbb C[u,\xi]_{(a-d+1,a+b-d+1)};
\]
products with $h>1$ lie in that same space. Back in $B$ this is precisely the span of $\sigma^{d-1}e_{ij}$ times the component of bidegree $(a-d,b)$. This identifies the full ideal, not just its zero set.

In particular $\sigma^d=0$ in this fiber by taking the trace of the relations, but $\sigma^{d-1}\ne0$ since its $e$-degree is below $d$. The nilradical is exactly $(\sigma)$: on the rank-one parameter space $v\cdot \xi=0$ gives a domain; its contraction to $B$ is $(\sigma)$ by the bidegree description. Equivalently the reduced boundary consists of rank-one matrices whose $e$ block is traceless. It has dimension $2m-1$ (one equation in the irreducible rank-one variety of dimension $2m$), versus dimension $m$ for all other fibers. Thus the origin is intrinsically the unique exceptional point of this projection, and the scheme there retains the color parameter $d$ as the exact nilpotency index of the trace. This is a classical explanation for distinguishing the zero minor vector; the quantum shift minus one condition is the exact operator obstruction, not a deduction from the classical dimension count.

The ordinary cotangent formula gives the parallel Poisson lift of origin-fixed Keller maps on $R$, without requiring the quantum lift to preserve the weight filtration for nonlinear $F$. It replaces $\xi_j$ by the symbol of $X_j$. For $e$ the new coefficient vanishes at $u=0$; for $w$ the lowest $u$-degree $d-1$ term is a constant combination of $w$, since the linear part of $\sum_iF_iX_i$ is the Euler field. Higher $u$-degree terms have at least $d$ multiplier degrees and momentum degree $d$, so are made from $u,e$. If $F(0)\ne0$, a constant coefficient momentum term in some transformed $e$ is outside $R$. This adds an exact classical counterpart but does not assert that all unmarked Poisson maps are of this form.
