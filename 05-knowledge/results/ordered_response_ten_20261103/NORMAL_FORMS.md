## N1. Ten rewrite schemas and a unique normal form

Write a=3, c=49, d=343, e=63; for each prime p>=5, p!=7, write p for the ordinary atom and b_p=7p. In a commutative factorization use these ten schemas:

| Left | Right | Condition |
|---|---|---|
| b_p b_q | c p q | p=q allowed |
| b_p c | d p | |

| b_p e | c a^2 p | |
| b_p a^2 | e p | |

| e^2 | c a^4 | |
| e c | d a^2 | |
| e d | c^2 a^2 | |

Every rule preserves integer value and strictly decreases the nonnegative integer

    R = sum_p (p+3) nu(b_p) + 2 nu(e) + nu(d).

Therefore **every** choice of an applicable rule terminates in at most R steps; priority is unnecessary for termination. For

    n = 7^A 3^B product_p p^C_p,

the unique terminal factorization is:

- A even: `c^(A/2) a^B product p^C_p`;
- A odd>=3: `d c^((A-3)/2) a^B product p^C_p`;
- A=1, B>=2: `e a^(B-2) product p^C_p`;
- A=1, B<2: choose the least p with C_p>0, use one b_p and remove one ordinary p.

Proof of uniqueness: a terminal word has at most one b, at most one e, and at most one d. A b cannot coexist with c,d,e or two a's; exchange forces its prime to be the least remaining prime. An e cannot coexist with c or d. These conditions force exactly the listed cases. The only excluded exponent vectors are n=7 and21. Thus termination plus the explicit terminal classification proves confluence and completeness, without an assumed critical-pair theorem. Any two equal-value factorizations are joined through this normal form.

The numerical rank depends on actual primes; it is not asserted to be a bit-complexity bound. Ten means ten schemas, not ten literal rules over the infinite alphabet. Restricting to p in{5,11,13} gives exactly ten symbols, the actual bank used below.
