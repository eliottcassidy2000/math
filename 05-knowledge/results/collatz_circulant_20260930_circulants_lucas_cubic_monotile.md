## 1. The level operator is a Gauss-twisted circulant, and its spectrum is the half-circle (THM-4520, PROVED)

`T_n = (S/2)(I - S/2)^{-1} diag(omega_n)` on `C^{L_n}`, `L_n = 2 3^{n-1}`, `omega_n(k) = e(2^k/3^n)`. Because `{2^k mod 3^n}` is the whole unit group, `{omega_n(k)}` is the set of primitive `3^n`-th roots of unity, and the eigenvalue condition `(2 lambda)^L = prod_k (lambda + omega_n(k)) = Phi_{3^n}(-lambda) = lambda^L - lambda^{L/2} + 1` gives the characteristic equation

`(2^L - 1) lambda^L + lambda^{L/2} - 1 = 0`:

all eigenvalues lie on two circles of radii `(1/2)(1 ∓ 2^{-L/2}/L)`; the spectral radius is `1/2 + 2^{-L/2}/(2L)`; the eigenvectors are `w(k-1) = 2 lambda/(omega_n(k) + lambda) w(k)`, `v = S w/(2 lambda)`. The singular values are those of the circulant, `1/|2 e(xi/L) - 1| in [1/3, 1]`, whose eigenvalues `1/(2 e(xi/L) - 1)` fill the circle `|w - 1/3| = 2/3`. The gauge `omega_n` has two-level autocorrelation (`L`, `-L/2` at `± L/3`, `0` elsewhere — the exponential analogue of a Legendre sequence, from the Gauss-sum flatness of S23's Theorem 1), and `(1/L) sum_k |G_n(2^k)|^2 = 1/3 + O(2^{-L/3})`: the rms one-step Gauss sum over the units is exactly the Parseval rate. Verified for `n <= 7` to machine precision (`..._spectrum_exact.out`, `..._circulant_spectrum_n7.out`).

**What it says.** The Parseval-rate contraction `3^{-1/2}` per level of the Syracuse law (S20/S22; my part 2) is not a spectral quantity: the one-level operator has spectral radius `1/2` but `sigma_max = 1`, and iterating it contracts at `0.56–0.58` per step for a dozen steps before the spectral `1/2` takes over (`..._transient.out`: `||T_n^8||^{1/8} = 0.61, 0.66, 0.70, 0.72` at `n = 3..6`, lengthening with `L`). The true recursion changes the operator at every level (`T_n` on `Z/L_n`, then `T_{n+1}` on `Z/(3 L_n)`), so every level is a fresh transient, and the cocycle reproduces the S20 profile exactly (`max |f_n| = M(n) = 0.37792, 0.25224, 0.17700, 0.12927, 0.09611, 0.07587`, `n = 2..7`; rms `0.836 3^{-n/2}`). H1 (HYP-9166) is a cocycle statement; the spectral radius `1/2 < 3^{-1/2} < 0.585` is what H1 would trivially follow from if the recursion were spectral, and it is not. Circulant-graph reading: the Syracuse step is a weighted jump `k -> k - a` (weight `2^{-a}`) on the cycle graph `Cay(Z/L_n, {-1, -2, ...})` with the Gauss-sum gauge on the vertices; the Dalfó–Fiol–Reyes Moore count (S20) is the number of distinct vertices reachable in `d` jumps — the same-length spread lemma.

## 2. The Lucas identities, the Fibonacci-monodromy tower, and the finite Fibonacci groups as Pillai clocks

`phi^8 + 1 = 7 phi^4` and `phi^10 = 1 + 11 phi^5` are `phi^4 + phi^{-4} = L_4 = 7` and `phi^5 - phi^{-5} = L_5 = 11` (exact; `..._lucas_pillai.out` and the mpmath check to `10^{-29}`). In the monotile paper these are the levels `j = 4, 5` of the tower: `O_j = Z[phi]/(phi^j - 1) ≅ F(2,j)^{ab}` has order `|L_j - 1 - (-1)^j|` (its Proposition 7.3), i.e. `N(phi^4 - 1) = N(3 phi + 1) = 5 = |O_4|` and `|O_5| = 11`; `O_5 = F_11` is the Fibonacci group `F(2,5) = Z/11` (Conway's problem), `O_4 = F_5`. The paper's own `lambda` is `4 + sqrt 15`, the fundamental unit of `Q(sqrt 15)`, with boundary dimension `delta = 6 log phi/log(4 + sqrt 15) = 1.39925`; the owner's `lambda = 1/(5 (2 phi^4)^{1/6}) = 0.129280563` is a different number (`1/(4 + sqrt 15) = 0.12702`).

**Collatz reading (FINITE-EXACT, `..._lucas_pillai.out`).** The Pillai gaps `2^K - 3^X` that are Lucas numbers, Fibonacci numbers or Fibonacci-group orders, for `K <= 400`, are exactly the sixteen with `K <= 8`: `L_1 = 1` (Gersonides, four shapes), `L_2 = 3 = 2^2 - 1`, `L_4 = 7 = 2^3 - 1 = 2^4 - 3^2`, `L_5 = 11 = 3^3 - 2^4`, `L_7 = 29 = 2^5 - 3`, `L_8 = 47 = 2^7 - 3^4`; `F_5 = 5 = 2^3 - 3 = 3^2 - 2^2 = 2^5 - 3^3`, `F_7 = 13 = 2^4 - 3 = 2^8 - 3^5`, `F_10 = 55 = 2^6 - 3^2`. By THM-4484 each is a free clock: `3y + 7` has the free shape `(4,2)` (its cycle through `5`, verified in part 1), `3y - 11` the shape `(4,3)`, `3y + 29` the shape `(5,1)`, `3y + 47` the shape `(7,4)`, `3y ± 5` the shapes `(3,1), (5,3)` and `(2,2)`. ## 4. The cubic surface (FINITE-EXACT + CITED)

`(x, y, z) = (723809206820, -631758385864, -245500254229)` satisfies `x^3 + 2x + y^3 + 2y + z^3 + 2z = xyz + 1` exactly (`x + y + z = -153449433273`, `xyz + 1 = 112260523882682460072980422639797921`). Structure: in the elementary symmetric functions `e_1, e_2, e_3` the equation reads `2 e_3 = 1 - e_1^3 - 2 e_1 + 3 e_1 e_2`, so a solution is a triple of integer roots of `t^3 - e_1 t^2 + e_2 t - e_3` with `e_3` determined by `(e_1, e_2)`, `e_1` odd and `e_2` even; modulo `3`, `t^3 + 2t ≡ 0` for every `t`, so `xyz ≡ 2 (mod 3)`. There are no Vieta involutions (each variable enters cubically; the quadratic in the two companion roots has discriminant `4yz - 3x^2 - 8`, negative at the solution in all three variables). The affine surface is smooth, and its curve at infinity `x^3 + y^3 + z^3 = xyz` (the Hesse pencil `x^3 + y^3 + z^3 = 3k xyz` at `k = 1/3`; the circulant determinant `x^3 + y^3 + z^3 - 3xyz` is the singular member `k = 1`) is the elliptic curve `26b1`: `y^2 + xy + y = x^3 - 5x - 8`, `j = -10218313/17576 = -217^3/26^3`, conductor `26`, rank `0`, torsion `Z/3` (PARI `ellfromeqn`, `ellglobalred`, `elltors`, `ellrank`; `..._cubic_curve.gp`). Search: no integer solutions with `|x|, |y| <= 6000` and any `z` (`..._cubic_search_B6000.out`; the cubic in `z` solved exactly), and none on the diagonal family `x = y` with `|x| <= 10^6`. So the owner's twelve-digit point is isolated far above the range of naive search on a smooth log-K3-type surface whose boundary curve has rank zero — the sums-of-three-cubes phenomenon on a surface with no descent. Its provenance is not known to this session (no literature hit for the numbers), and no connection to `phi` or to `2`/`3`-smoothness was found (`x = 2^2 5 7 5170065763`, `y = -2^3 17^3 16073641`, `z = -2539 96691711`).

## 5. The two papers as mirrors (typed; no theorem)

* **Reis–Song.** Discrepancy of `±1` matrices: `D_n < sqrt(3 arsinh 10) + 4/sqrt n < 3` by minimising a reciprocal-`sinh` potential and rounding, and `D_n^± > 1.0000002` by a Hadamard matrix with a `2^{-22}` fraction of random columns (Bandeira's conjecture).
