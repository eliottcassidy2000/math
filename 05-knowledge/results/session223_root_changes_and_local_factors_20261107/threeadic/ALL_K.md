# Exact all-precision 3-adic factor for fixed-length two-J words

Status: PROVED analytically below; exact finite graph controls at q=3,9,27,81. The height-truncated factor of M_2 is NOT claimed.

## Theorem

Fix k>=1, q=3^k. Uniformly sample length L words with exactly two J. Put v=v_3(r) for nonzero residues r modulo q (so 0<=v<k). Then

Pr(F=r mod q)=f(v)/q+O_k(1/L),

where

f(0)=47/54,
f(v)=157/108 - 3^(-v)*(v/6+85/144) for v>=1.

For r=0 the factor is

C_k=157/108 - 3^(-k)*(k/8+11/24),

so Pr(q|F)=C_k/q+O_k(1/L).

The factors strictly increase from v=0 and tend to 157/108. Their limiting ratio to the unit factor is 157/94, about 1.67021. This is a fixed-length law. It neither agrees with nor contradicts the exploratory height-window ratio about three, because the ensembles differ.

At precisions q=3,9,27,81, the valuation factors (last entry is the zero-residue tail) are respectively:

- 47/54; 34/27.
- 47/54, 173/144; 11/8.
- 47/54, 173/144, 1751/1296; 461/324.
- 47/54, 173/144, 1751/1296, 5495/3888; 2803/1944.

## 1. O/Z communicating classes at q=3^k

After one O/Z letter E belongs to {0,d}. Put g=gcd(d,U,q). For fixed d,g and c modulo g, define C(d,g,c) by

D=c mod g, gcd(d,U,q)=g, E in {0,d}.

Repeated E values when d=0 are counted once. These sets partition the normalized state space. Both operators preserve each class. Each vertex has two incoming O/Z edges, counting multiplicity: for an O-target, solve U_old=U_new/2 and D_old=D_new with the two possible E_old; for a Z-target solve U_old=(U_new+d)/2 and D_old=D_new+E_old-U_old. When d=0 the two maps are permutations of the E=0 slice instead. Hence uniform measure on each class is stationary.

We prove each class is strongly connected. Subtract c from D and divide D-c,E,U,d by g, reducing to gcd(d,U,q)=1 (with smaller q); the singleton q=1 case is immediate. It suffices to connect the E=0 slice, since one O reaches it and a Z maps it bijectively onto the E=d slice.

On E=0, O acts by S(D,U)=(D,2U). If ell is the multiplicative order of 2 modulo q, the word Z O^(ell-1) acts by

A(D,U)=(D+U,U+h), h=-d/2.

Both maps are permutations of the finite slice, so their inverses are realizable by positive powers. The elementary fact that 2 is primitive modulo 3^j follows from ord_3(2)=2 and v_3(4^(3^b)-1)=b+1 (binomial induction). In particular R=S^(3^(k-1)) is the reflection (D,U) -> (D,-U). Direct composition gives

(R A)^2(D,U)=(D-h,U).

Thus the generated group contains all D-translations by the ideal (d).

If d is a unit, these D-translations are arbitrary. Apply powers of A to set U arbitrarily, then correct D. This gives transitivity.

If d is not a unit, U is a unit. Powers of S reach any specified unit U=u. From U=u, apply A^m, giving

D_new=D+m*u+h*m*(m-1)/2, U_new=u+m*h.

The new U is still a unit, so a power of S restores it exactly to u without changing D. Modulo the ideal (d), the change in D is m*u, so choose m to set D to any desired residue modulo that ideal. Then a pure D-translation in (d) corrects D exactly. When d=0, A itself is the independent D-translation by the unit U, so the same conclusion holds. This proves transitivity.

For an arbitrary pre-normalized state (D,E,U,d), g is unchanged by the first O or Z, and its projected D-coset is respectively c=D or c=D-E modulo g. Hence its Cesaro projection is the equal mixture of the uniform measures on those two classes.

The initial state lies in d=1, whose full class is aperiodic because O fixes E=U=0. Therefore the resolvent argument in PROOF.md section 5 applies at every fixed q: only the z=1 pole can have order three. All other peripheral poles have order at most two. This proves an O_k(1/L) limit given by pi_1 R Pi R Pi, even though other classes may have nontrivial periods.

## 2. First-J projected law as an explicit probability density

In the initial stationary class, D and U are independent uniform residues and E is an independent equal coin in {0,1}. Apply J, then the O/Z projection. The new d is the old D; the new U has gcd with d equal to gcd(old D,old U,q). Conditional on d and g, the projection makes U uniform among the residues with gcd(d,U,q)=g. Thus the new pair (d,U) is again independent uniform modulo q.

The new D-coset modulo g is 0 with probability 3/4 and -1 with probability 1/4: after J, the first O preserves D_old-E_old+U_old, while the first Z gives D_old+2U_old. The stationary E is an independent equal coin in {0,d}; this convention remains valid when d=0, where the two values coincide.

Consequently the first-J stationary distribution can be generated by:

1. sample d,U independently uniformly;
2. let g=gcd(d,U,q);
3. sample D uniformly in the coset 0 modulo g with probability 3/4, or -1 modulo g with probability 1/4;
4. sample E independently from {0,d} with equal probabilities.

Its density relative to independent uniform (d,U,D) is

w=g*((3/4)*[g|D]+(1/4)*[g|D+1]).

For the second J, put h=gcd(D,U,q). After projecting, the output F is uniform in the coset 0 modulo h with probability 3/4, and the coset -d modulo h with probability 1/4.

These two formulas already give an exact finite-q local factor, using finite sums only. They explain why the two-J result becomes radial in v_3(r), despite the first-J result being nonradial.

## 3. Evaluating the factor, with all tails retained

For clean evaluation use independent Haar-uniform d,U,D in Z_3 and write a=v_3(d), b=v_3(U), c=v_3(D), j=min(b,c). Infinite valuations occur with probability zero. The finite-q projected laws are compatible under reduction, since finite Markov Cesaro projections commute with reduction. Thus this Haar calculation computes their common cylinder probabilities. Equivalently one may truncate every valuation at k and sum the same finite geometric series before taking the compatible limit. More explicitly, the truncated integrands are dominated by g*h, where g=3^min(a,b) and h=3^min(c,b). Conditional on b, independence gives E[g*h | b]=(1+2*b/3)^2, and b has a geometric distribution. Thus g*h is integrable, and dominated convergence rigorously justifies passage from the finite quotients to the displayed Haar expectations. Cylinder probabilities are then recovered by integration over the output cylinder; the zero 3-adic point has Haar measure zero and creates no extra atom, since its nested-cylinder probabilities tend to zero.

For fixed d of valuation a define T_j(a) to be the integral of the first-J density w over U,D with min(v_3(U),v_3(D))=j. For j>=1, put

p_j=(8/9)*9^(-j),
w_0=1, and w_a=(3/4)*3^a for a>=1.

Then

T_j(a)=p_j*w_a if a<=j,
T_j(a)=(2/3)*9^(-j)*w_j if a>j.

To verify: on j>=1, D is divisible by 3, so the D+1 term in w vanishes unless g=1. If a<=j then g=3^a and the formula is immediate. If a>j, the density is nonzero precisely when b=j and c>=j, which has probability (2/3)*9^(-j).

Integrating d gives the mass m_j of this h-valuation under the first-J law:

m_j=(24*j+41)/(54*9^j), j>=1.

Indeed sum_(a=0)^j Pr(v_3(d)=a)*w_a=2/3+j/2, and the a>j contribution is (1/6)*9^(-j). Hence

m_0=1-sum_(j>=1)m_j=91/108.

Let Z_j be the joint mass with h-valuation j and d divisible by 3^j. The a=j contribution is (4/9)*9^(-j), and the a>j contribution is (1/6)*9^(-j), so

Z_j=(11/18)*9^(-j).

Fix an output N with valuation v. For j<=v, the zero-coset branch contributes (3/4)*3^j*m_j and the -d-coset branch contributes (1/4)*3^j*Z_j. Their sum is

(6*j+13)/(18*3^j).

For j>v, only the -d branch contributes. The congruence d=-N modulo 3^j has Haar probability 3^(-j), and T_j depends only on a=v, so its factor is (1/4)*T_j(v). Therefore

f(0)=m_0+(1/4)*sum_(j>=1)p_j=47/54,

and, for v>=1,

f(v)=91/108 + sum_(j=1)^v (6*j+13)/(18*3^j) + 1/(48*3^v)
    =157/108 - 3^(-v)*(v/6+85/144).

All series are absolutely convergent and nonnegative before the final algebraic simplifications, so interchanging their sums and integrals is valid.

Finally, the zero-residue factor at precision k is the conditional Haar average over v>=k:

C_k=sum_(v>=k) (2/3)*3^(-(v-k))*f(v)
   =157/108 - 3^(-k)*(k/8+11/24).

This proves every claimed constant without fitting any numerical data.

## 4. Scope, controls, and open bridge

`exact_higher.py` constructs the full normalized finite-state universe, verifies two-in/two-out regularity, independently verifies forward and reverse component connectivity, checks initial aperiodicity, and computes the exact rational two-J law. `HIGHER.txt` covers q=3,9,27. `exact_81.py` and `HIGHER81.txt` cover q=81 (1,056,321 normalized states, 405 classes). None of these computations is required for the analytic group proof; they are independent finite controls.

The factors here describe the distribution of F for uniformly sampled length-L prefixes, not the average of M_2(N) on a height interval. In particular the saturation ratio is 157/94, not three. A joint real-height and residue kernel is needed to pass to the height-truncated ensemble. The bridge audit also finds that conditioning on the last O/Z before the second J changes the limiting mod-3 law, so generic independence from real-history side information is false. No claim of independence from final scalar height, either positive or negative, is made here.

## 5. Corollary for length-resolved M_2 multiplicity

For each source-prefix length L, let B_(j,L)(r) count prefixes with exactly j Js and F=r modulo q. The actual terminal non-O words of length L+1 with at most two Js have residue counts

    M_(2,L)(r)=2 B_(0,L)(r)+2 B_(1,L)(r)+B_(2,L)(r).

The total masses of the three prefix layers are 2^L, L*2^(L-1), and binomial(L,2)*2^(L-2). The two lower layers therefore contribute only O(1/L) of the total weighted mass. Consequently the same factors f(v), C_k and O_k(1/L) error hold for uniformly sampled counted terminal words of that fixed length. This is a direct length-resolved multiplicity corollary, still not the height-truncated M_2(N) law.
