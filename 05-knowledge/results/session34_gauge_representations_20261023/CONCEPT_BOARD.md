# Session34 concept board

## 1. Averaging and representation channels

Source: finite group action on states or fields. Map: Reynolds projector and character decomposition. Preserved: declared invariant subspace and multiplicities. Lost under a scalar orbit count: all nontrivial representation channels. Cheapest test: explicit S3 tensor decomposition or a negative character coefficient. Irreducibles are additive atoms in the semisimple category, not multiplicative primes.

## 2. Tournament signs and simplicial curvature

Source: an ordered tournament edge assignment, with a fixed baseline converting skew orientation to symmetric Z2 transport signs. Operation: vertex switching. Preserved: Wilson loops, tetrahedral Bianchi data. Lost: ordinary Hamiltonian-path count, which is not gauge invariant globally. Repair in the Fano family: the precise rainbow Wilson-loop extension and its constrained domain, not a claim that every tournament invariant is gauge invariant.

## 3. Fourth-order observables versus spectral gaps

Source: finite quartic code or edge action. Operation: specified transverse flips and specified representation projection. Preserved: each exact sector spectrum. Lost by projecting: other sectors and potentially slower splittings. The line and vertex-gauge-projected edge models share a cubic gap coefficient but have different fourth-order coefficients. The physical state space and the parameter limit must be named before interpreting any gap.

## 4. Simplex coherence and vertex algebras

Source: tournament winner algebra. Imposing constant-field locality/associativity collapses SCCs. A genuine positive model instead retains a3-cocycle and braiding satisfying pentagon and hexagon identities. The rank-one even lattice VOA realizes that semion category. Fusion labels alone omit the associator, twist and central-charge normalization.

## 5. Closed state sums versus boundaries

Source: positive central S3 face densities. Map: closed triangulated-surface partition function. Lost: one sign of a character coefficient, because the number of triangular faces is even. Two models agree on every closed orientable triangular mesh but differ in reflected-cylinder positivity and continuous-time refinability. Boundary operators are necessary data, not decorative details.

## 6. Continuum and scope

A finite simplex model, a character sum, a fourth-order Taylor identity and a finite-volume matrix gap are different achievements. The four-dimensional Yang–Mills target still requires the appropriate limiting quantum theory and a physical vacuum gap with controlled limits. The separate attached-paper audits remain outside this public packet pending publication approval.

All bridges in this board have named maps, explicit losses and finite hostile controls. No numerical coincidence or arbitrary identification of gauge groups is used as proof.
