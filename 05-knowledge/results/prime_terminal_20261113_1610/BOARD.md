Anchor: actual prime and7p targets via3h+2/4. Niche: changing the division
between fixed core and uniform module. Wildcard: Collatz-style alternate

## Typed connections

1. **Cover algebra to commuting ordinal words.** Source: ordered q-bin path
   covers. Target: uniform-frame full counts. Map: ordinal sum multiplies
   P(q), then binomial inversion recovers every nonempty-run response.
   Preserved: all unmarked cover coefficients and every uniform consumer.
   Lost: order of the SCC atoms. Needed sidecar for nonuniform consumers:
   marked incidences/positions. Decisive test: compare literal H after a
   nonuniform attachment, not the number of formal reorderings.

2. **Real response cone to integer module realizability.** Source: the H25
   bivariate run polynomial. Target: count-neutral module substitution.
   Map: insert physical K_j values into that polynomial. Preserved: full H
   difference. Lost by rational relaxation: parity and factorial divisibility.
   Needed sidecar: K2=2 mod4. Decisive test: the four mixed port pairs, where
   a rational positive zero exists but every nontrivial physical point fails.

3. **Exponential sums to finite correction opportunities.** Source: padded
   M0=>TT_t and ordinal powers. Target: constant-digit frame differences.
   Map: integer binomial transform to the positive bases1,2,... or P_M0(j).
   Preserved: exact full counts. Lost if only the dominant term is kept:
   the fixed digit. Sidecar: the base1 coefficient is divisible by H(M0).
   Decisive test: isolated signed Bezout matches versus the all-length
   constant identity. The Rolle zero bound is proved in MODULES, not assumed.

4. **Collatz inverse choices to tournament proof forests.** Source: the idea
   of different inverse branches. Target: certified digit gates and products.
   Map: choose a legal arithmetic parent and factor it into smaller atoms.
   Preserved: the scalar target equation and a decreasing proof potential.
   Destroyed: the original tournament witness and every marked preparation.
   Needed sidecar: a new physical source certificate. Decisive test:
   reducible H1919 versus the strong source actually used for H5761.

5. **Prime-count capacity to approximate witness coverage.** Source:
   elementary lower bound pi(X)>=cX/logX. Target: finite frame/atom banks
   with bounded terminal correction. Map: count distinct base values and
   the union of all granted correction intervals. Preserved: an upper bound
   on possible target supply. Lost: overlap details, intentionally making
   the bound generous. Sidecar: distinct scalar values, not graph multiplicity.
   Decisive test: the explicit primitive families with O(S^epsilon) debt
   still have too few placements to cover either critical lane.

## Outside sources and retained limits

Chan--Pak [Conjecture6.2](https://www.math.ucla.edu/~pak/papers/DominoPermanent16.pdf)
states the odd spectrum assertion and attributes its earlier formulation
to bof and Gordon Royle. Their comparison of counting functions is useful
here as a demand for actual output coverage, beyond many witnesses per value.
No external novelty claim is made for the conjecture or the prime reduction.

Lagarias's [Collatz overview](https://arxiv.org/html/2111.02635v1)
discusses inverse-iterate trees. That motivates branching source selection;
its results do not transfer a tournament count or solve the reconstruction
problem. The present strict proof-forest potentials are elementary arguments
in the declared tournament arithmetic language, not Collatz Lyapunov functions.

Busch's [strong minimum theorem](https://www.combinatorics.org/ojs/index.php/eljc/article/download/v13i1n3/pdf)
is the cited quantitative bridge from a small H to a finite strong core.
It makes the H11/H13 full fibres and the two unshifted response exclusions
all-order statements. It does not justify extending any other finite search.
