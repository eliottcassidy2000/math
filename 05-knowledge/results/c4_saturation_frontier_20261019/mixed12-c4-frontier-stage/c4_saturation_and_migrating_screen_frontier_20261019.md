# Seven-thin c=4: saturation pruning and migration beyond the old four comparisons

Date: 2026-10-19. The zero-increment lemma is proved; the sample is exploratory; the restricted-screen obstruction and its repair are exactly verified. None of these establishes the full c=4 case or resolves the 1/3–2/3 conjecture.

## 1. Exact structural pruning

See [the saturation increment lemma](seven_thin_saturation_increment_lemma_20261019.md). A core vertex already having seven incomparable core neighbors must satisfy A<v<B for the entire lower/upper exterior. Every prefix increment whose word contains v is zero, including all increments after its first occurrence. Dually, every suffix increment containing v is zero. Thus every generator with a saturated vertex in either boundary word can be removed.

An independent standard-library audit checks 80 actual seven-thin posets through order 15, on four core shapes. All 2,760 tested saturated prefix increments and 22,000 saturated suffix increments vanish. Full and pruned fibre expansions agree exactly with whole-poset subset-DP counts. This checks the analytic lemma; it does not replace its proof.

At c=4, all-vertex core degree≤7 removes only common vertices isolated from the whole core. The 96,428 naturally labeled allowed cores reduce to 79,121. This is an exact finite census, not an isomorphism count or a majority-order theorem.

## 2. Bounded application, kept separate from proof

A first 20,000-order exploration covered 46 retained cores (the final core only partially); all tested orders had an exact one- or two-comparison certificate in the unrestricted generator cone.

A targeted deterministic sample selected 400 of the 18,060 degree-valid cores extending the exceptional six-point morphology. Across their 3,290 tested compatible maximum-span orders, every order originally needed two rows in this screen. Saturation pruning reduced the combined generator count from 457,344 to 200,099. After pruning, 1,775 tested orders had one-row certificates and the remaining 1,515 had two-row certificates. These figures are sample evidence only.

Among 581 embedded exceptional four-comparison patterns with a compatible order, the original weight vector (3,4,1,1) passed on 434. Thus the old inequality does not extend uniformly by simply ignoring the additional common neighbors.

## 3. An exact limitation of the old four-comparison screen

Use labels

    (x,y,b,z1,z2,z3,z4,a1,a2)=(0,1,2,3,4,5,6,7,8)

and predecessor masks

    (0,4,0,0,4,0,60,29,45).

Its relations are generated by

    b<y, b<z2,
    z1,z2,z3<z4,
    x,z1,z2<a1,
    x,b,z1,z3<a2.

It has 3,184 extensions. Its core incomparability degrees are

    (6,7,3,5,5,6,4,4,4).

The candidate order

    b,x,z1,z2,z3,z4,y,a2,a1

is a legal core extension with maximum root span. Only y is saturated, and roots never occur in the boundary words; hence saturation pruning changes no generator here.

Consider just the four comparisons inherited from the old exception:

    x<z2, z2<y, y<a2, a2<a1.

The following convex mixture of conditional generator columns makes all four probabilities equal to

    1458653/2181763 > 2/3.

| Prefix | Suffix | E | Four comparison numerators | Convex-weight numerator |
|---|---|---:|---|---:|
| empty | a2,a1 | 364 | 190,224,364,364 | 374647 |
| z1 | a2 | 162 | 93,114,162,0 | 21897 |
| z1,z3 | empty | 136 | 88,100,81,79 | 631448 |
| z1,z3 | z4 | 37 | 27,24,22,23 | 1153771 |

Every convex weight has denominator 2181763, and the numerators sum to it. The unnormalized generator coefficient is that convex weight divided by E. The common strict margin 3p−2 is 12433/2181763.

Consequently no nonzero nonnegative weighted ≤2/3 certificate supported only on those four comparisons can hold coefficientwise across the retained cone. This is a **restricted-screen limitation**. The mixture is not claimed to come from an actual exterior, and it does not make all incomparable comparisons follow the proposed order.

The last conditional column literally recovers the old 37-word exceptional core after conditioning away z1,z3,z4; this explains why carrying only its four comparisons loses information when the other common neighbors are restored.

## 4. The full screen repairs this case by moving to another common neighbor

For this same nine-point core, every actual exterior satisfies

    p(x<z1)+p(z1<y) ≤ 203/158 < 4/3.

The standard-library verifier checks the inequality against all 1,204 compatible prefix/suffix generators. The largest cone value 203/158 occurs at prefix z3 and suffix z4, with E=158 and comparison counts 79,124. This is an upper bound for all actual exteriors; no claim of actual attainment is made.

Both comparisons are forward in the candidate order, so the inequality rules out simultaneous >2/3 probabilities. Thus the earlier rational mixture is not a surviving full-comparison cone or a counterexample to the conjecture. It shows why a useful migration certificate must be allowed to select a different common endpoint.
