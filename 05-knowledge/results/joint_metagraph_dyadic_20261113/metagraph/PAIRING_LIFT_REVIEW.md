## Precisely scoped statement and proof

Let X be finite, R:X→X an involution, and Y=X/R. Partition Y into singleton fibres Y_s and double fibres Y_c. Let G be **any subgroup** of Sym(Y) preserving these two sectors. For the application, G is the weighted merged-graph vertex automorphism group, optionally restricted to preserve blue/black matrices or labels on Y. A label such as H must be constant on R-orbits to be a label on Y; tournament H has this property.

Let W consist of all permutations J of X which commute with R and whose induced permutation on Y belongs to G. There is a canonical exact sequence

    1 → C2^(|Y_c|) → W → G → 1.

It is surjective: any prescribed size-preserving permutation of the fibres has equivariant bijections between its source and target fibres. Choose labels (y,0),(y,1) in each double fibre, with R(y,e)=(y,e+1), the addition being modulo2. Sending (y,e) to (g(y),e) defines a group-theoretic section. Thus

    W ≅ C2^(|Y_c|) ⋊ G,

where G permutes the double-fibre coordinates. The splitting is noncanonical because it depends on the selected labels in the double fibres. The kernel and exact sequence themselves do not require those choices.

A general lift of g takes (y,e) to (g(y),e+b_y). It is an involution precisely when

    g²=1 and b_y=b_(g(y)) for every y in Y_c.

For an involution g, let f_s(g),f_c(g) count its fixed singleton and double fibres, and let p_c(g) count its **unordered two-cycles** in Y_c. Then:

- Each fixed singleton contributes one fixed ordinary element, hence x.
- Each exchanged singleton pair has its unique lift and no fixed ordinary elements.
- Each fixed double fibre has two lifts: identity with two fixed elements, or flip with none, hence 1+x².
- Each exchanged double-fibre pair has two compatible transport choices, because its two transport bits must agree. Neither choice fixes an ordinary element, hence the factor2.

These independent factors give exactly

    I_G(x) = sum_(g∈G, g²=1) 2^p_c(g) x^f_s(g) (1+x²)^f_c(g).

There is no overcount: different g have disjoint lift sets, and the local choices determine the permutation on every fibre. There is no missing multiplicity: every compatible lift is one of those choices. The identity is included among the involutions.

For each g, choose the flip on every fixed double fibre; this realizes exactly f_s(g) fixed elements. Consequently

    min_J |Fix(J)| = min_(g∈G, g²=1) f_s(g).

Equivalently, a free compatible ordinary involution exists iff G contains an involution acting freely on the singleton-fibre sector. No freeness condition on its double-fibre action is needed. If G is trivial, the polynomial is x^|Y_s|(1+x²)^|Y_c| and the unique minimizing ordinary permutation is R itself. These conclusions include empty sectors and empty X.

The result is conditional on knowing G. It does not reconstruct the actual graph, classify its automorphism group at arbitrary order, or supply preferred physical marked-path lifts for arbitrary independent fibre flips. The separate global-converse reflection lift remains valid.

## Finite consequences at orders 3,4,5

Applying the formula to the independently computed weighted merged-node automorphism groups gives:

| n | no H guard | with H guard |
|---:|---|---|
|3|1+x²|x²|
|4|x²+x⁴|x²+x⁴|
|5|x⁸+2x¹⁰+x¹²|x⁸+2x¹⁰+x¹²|

Thus the free diagram symmetry at order3 changes H; the graph is vertex-rigid at orders4,5; and the order-five minimum is8 even without an H guard. With the graph guard removed but H retained, the order-five polynomial is

    x⁴(1+x²)^4 = x⁴+4x⁶+6x⁸+4x¹⁰+x¹²,

agreeing with all16 previously enumerated H-preserving converse-equivariant involutions. Here the allowable quotient group independently permutes the two H9 singleton fibres and the two H15 singleton fibres, while the two chiral fibres each have a distinct H label.

The exchanged-double-fibre factor has an independent hostile/positive control: take two double fibres and let G swap them. The complete polynomial is

    (1+x²)^2 + 2 = 3+2x²+x⁴.

Using one lift rather than two over that quotient transposition would give an incorrect constant coefficient. A restricted diagonal subgroup on two singleton fibres and two double fibres, generated by swapping both pairs simultaneously, gives

    x²(1+x²)^2+2 = 2+x²+2x⁴+x⁶.

This checks that the formula uses actual allowed quotient involutions, not independent permutations of all sectors when G couples them.
