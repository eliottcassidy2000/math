## S1 The free coordinate over every finite field of characteristic two

For q=2^m, the same isotropic Plucker coordinates satisfy

\[
t^2=ad+bc,\qquad t=(ad+bc)^{2^{m-1}}.
\]

Consequently the Lagrangians of Fq^4 correspond bijectively to projective
points [a:b:c:d] in PG3(Fq). Scaling the four coordinates by lambda scales
t by lambda, so the inverse is well-defined projectively. The count is

\[
(q+1)(q^2+1)=q^3+q^2+q+1.
\]

This proof uses the bijectivity of squaring in the field. It does not apply
to an arbitrary characteristic-two ring with nilpotents or to odd fields
where a square root can have two signs or fail to exist.

The field degree enters the inverse through an actual Frobenius power;
it is not a label inferred from the number of objects.

## S2 Transport and its classical square identity

Let N(g) be the four-by-four matrix on (a,b,c,d) obtained from exterior
square transport. Its entries are two-by-two minors of g, indexed by
(01,03,12,23). The omitted t-column is zero because the nucleus is fixed.
Swap coordinates c,d to restore the original symplectic basis convention,
and call the resulting group map Psi. This is the exceptional
characteristic-two symplectic endomorphism.

The classical identity is

\[
\boxed{\Psi^2(g)=g^{[2]},}
\]

where the right side squares every field entry. The same exterior-square
quotient and basis are described in [Muller, Introduction to finite simple
groups, section 6.1, PDF page 41](https://www.icts.res.in/sites/default/files/1285648885MuellerWshopGAC2010_0.pdf).
That source explains the resulting Suzuki twist for odd extension degree.
Our matrix implementation recomputes this identity from minors in the
explicit finite test universes below; it does not substitute a finite test
for the classical all-field theorem.

Because the minor formulas have coefficients in F2, Psi commutes with
Frobenius phi:g->g^[2]. Thus

\[
(\Psi\phi^k)^2=\phi^{2k+1}. \tag{6}
\]

On F_(2^m), an involutive correction of this specific form exists exactly
when 2k+1=0 mod m. Hence it exists precisely for odd m, and its unique
residue is k=(m-1)/2. For even m, no Frobenius power flattens this transport
to an involution. This is an obstruction for the declared correction
family, not for every imaginable re-encoding or auxiliary extension.
