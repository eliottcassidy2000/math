## 1. The board, exactly

Squares `(i,j)`, `0 <= i,j < N = 2m`; `ring(i,j) = max(|i - c|, |j - c|) - 1/2` with `c = (N-1)/2`; colour `= i + j mod 2`. Script `04-computation/experiments/chessboard_weave_20261006_census.py` (8x8) and `..._general_boards.py` (`N = 2..16`), outputs alongside.

**Proposition 1.1 (rings and diagonals; PROVED, checked `N <= 16`).**
(a) Ring `k` has `(2k+2)^2 - (2k)^2 = 4(2k+1)` squares and is a cycle `C_{4(2k+1)}` of the orthogonal-adjacency grid graph; the rings form a 2-factor `C_4 + C_12 + C_20 + C_28` of the 8x8 grid (112 edges = 64 ring edges + 48 spokes, `8(k+1)` spokes between rings `k, k+1`).
(b) The diagonal-adjacency graph has exactly two components, the colour classes. In the colour class `i+j` even the anti-diagonals have lengths `1,3,5,7,7,5,3,1` and the diagonals `2,4,6,8,6,4,2`; in the other class the roles swap (the owner's scaffolds, verbatim).
(c) The odd-length diagonals (white anti-diagonals, black diagonals: sixteen lines, four of each length `1,3,5,7`) partition the board; so do the even-length ones (fourteen lines). Ring `k` and the four odd lines of length `2k+1` have the same size `4(2k+1)`; the odd lines of length `2k+1` are not ring `k` (the length-1 lines are the four corners, in ring 3) — the bijection is in size only.
(d) The board's `D_4` has `T_m = m(m+1)/2` square orbits, `k+1` of them in ring `k` (one diagonal orbit of size 4 and `k` of size 8): `1+2+3+4 = 10` on 8x8.

**Proposition 1.2 (the rings are what the bishop sees; PROVED).** On the `N x N` board the number of squares a bishop reaches from `(i,j)` on the empty board is `2N - 3 - 2 ring(i,j)` (13, 11, 9, 7 on 8x8), the queen's is `4N - 5 - 2 ring`, the rook's is the constant `2(N-1)`. *Proof.* With `a = min(i, N-1-i)` and `b = min(j, N-1-j)` (distance to the nearest edge), and by symmetry `i, j <= (N-1)/2`, the four diagonal rays have lengths `min(i,j)`, `i`, `j`, `N - 1 - max(i,j)`, summing to `N - 1 + 2 min(a,b)`, and `min(a,b) = N/2 - 1 - ring`. ∎ So the horizontal/vertical rings are exactly the level sets of the diagonal sliding range: the two structures the owner names are welded by the bishop.

**Proposition 1.3 (the knight's ring law; PROVED, checked `N <= 16`).** On the `2m x 2m` board the `4(2m-1)(2m-2)` knight edges split as
`8(m-1)` inside a ring (exactly 8 in every ring `k >= 1`, none in ring 0), `16 C(m,2)` between adjacent rings, `16 C(m-1,2)` jumping two rings, none jumping three. Every knight edge joins the two colours. On 8x8: `24 + 96 + 48 = 168`, and by ring pair `(0,1):16, (0,2):16, (1,1):8, (1,2):32, (1,3):32, (2,2):8, (2,3):48, (3,3):8`.
*Proof.* Use centred half-integer coordinates `(x,y)`, radius `r = max(|x|,|y|)`. A knight vector changes one coordinate by 2 and the other by 1, so `|Delta r| <= 2`. (i) Inside ring `k`: a knight move changes both coordinates, so it cannot stay on one side of the square of radius `k + 1/2`; opposite sides are `2k+1 >= 3` apart; so the two ends lie on adjacent sides near a corner, and there are exactly two such moves per corner for `k >= 1` (none for `k = 0`, whose four squares are pairwise a king move apart). (ii) `Delta r = +2` for the vector `(1,2)` forces `y = r >= |x|` and `y + 2 <= m - 1/2`; for ring `k <= m-3` this leaves the `2k+2` squares of the top side, so `sum_{k=0}^{m-3} (2k+2) = (m-1)(m-2)` ordered pairs per vector, `8(m-1)(m-2) = 16 C(m-1,2)` edges. (iii) The rest is `Delta r = 1`. ∎ Asymptotically half the knight moves cross one ring and half cross two. The within-ring edges sit at the corners: in ring 1 they form two 4-cycles (the "square" and "diamond" circuits of the central 4x4), in every ring `k >= 2` a matching of 16 of its squares (knight lane, `..._knight_rings.out`).

## 2. The lonely runner as a sliding piece (anchor)

**Dictionary (map / preserved / lost).** A speed vector `v = (v_1, ..., v_n)` is a rider direction; the time flow `t -> t v (mod 1)` is the rider sliding on the torus board `R^n/Z^n` from the corner (all runners at the start). Loneliness at `t` is `lambda(t) = min_i ||t v_i||`, the `L^infinity` distance from `t v` to the boundary of the unit cell, so **the Chebyshev rings of a subdivided cell are the level sets of the lonely-runner function**: on the `N x N` subdivision, the centre of a square in ring `k` has `lambda = (N - 1 - 2k)/(2N)`, and Proposition 1.2 becomes the exact affine identity

`bishop reach = (N - 2) + 2N lambda(centre).`

"Unlimited amounts until an obstruction is reached" is Cusick's view obstruction (THM-386): LRC with `n` speeds says every positive rider reaches the closed central cube `[1/(n+1), n/(n+1)]^n`, i.e. on the `(n+1)^n` subdivision it does not stay in the outer ring forever. "One step at a time" is discrete time `t in (1/q)Z`, the repository's good-period question (THM-762/764: no uniform `q <= 25`). Preserved: the predicate, exactly. Lost: nothing at fixed `n`; the board picture forgets only the integrality of `v` when one passes to general lines (irrational directions are dense, hence lonely). Sidecar: none needed. The billiard reading: `x_i(t) = ||t v_i||` is the billiard flow in the box `[0, 1/2]^n` with velocity `v` (unfold each coordinate), so LRC says every positive billiard from the corner enters `[1/(n+1), 1/2]^n`; a bishop billiard (`v = (1,1)`) stays on one colour, a knight billiard alternates.

**Theorem 2.1 (two speeds: loneliness lives on the anti-diagonal; PROVED).** For coprime positive integers `a, b` with `s = a + b`,

`delta(a,b) := max_t min(||ta||, ||tb||) = floor(s/2)/s`,

i.e. `1/2` when `s` is even (the `(a,b)`-leaper keeps its colour) and `1/2 - 1/(2s)` when `s` is odd (it changes colour). The minimum over all riders is `1/3`, attained only by the knight `{1,2}`.
*Proof.* Because `gcd(a,b) = 1`, the closed orbit `{t(a,b)}` is the connected subgroup `L = {(x,y) : bx - ay in Z}` of the torus. `lambda >= c` (`0 <= c < 1/2`) at a point iff it lies in the square `[c, 1-c]^2`. The linear form `f = bx - ay` maps this convex square onto the closed interval of centre `(b-a)/2` and half-length `s(1/2 - c)`. So `delta >= c` iff that interval contains an integer. If `s` is even the centre is an integer and `c` may be any value below `1/2` (and `t = 1/2` gives `1/2`). If `s` is odd the centre is a half-integer and we need `s(1/2 - c) >= 1/2`. ∎ Verified for all 489 coprime pairs `a < b <= 40` by exact evaluation over the candidate times (`..._rider_rings.out`). The colour of the leaper is the parity of `s`; the loneliness deficit `1/2 - delta` is `0` on one scaffold and `1/(2s)` on the other, largest (`1/6`) for the knight. In the space of directions the level sets of `delta` are the bishop's lines `a + b = s`.

**Theorem 2.2 (the two scaffolds are the two extremes, and their weave is the tight progression; PROVED).** For `m >= 1`: `delta(1, 3, ..., 2m-1) = 1/2`; `delta(2, 4, ..., 2m) = 1/(m+1)`, tight for `m` speeds; `delta(1, 2, ..., 2m) = 1/(2m+1)`, tight for `2m` speeds. On the 8x8 board: the odd lengths `{1,3,5,7}` (and the ring sizes `{4,12,20,28} = 4{1,3,5,7}`) are lonely at `1/2`, the even lengths `{2,4,6,8}` exactly at `1/5`, and all eight lengths together exactly at `1/9`. *Proof.* Odd speeds: `t = 1/2`. Progressions: `t = 1/(n+1)` gives `||k/(n+1)|| >= 1/(n+1)`, and for every `t` two of the `n+1` points `0, t, ..., nt` lie within `1/(n+1)` on the circle, so some `||jt|| <= 1/(n+1)` with `1 <= j <= n` (the even set is `2(1, ..., m)` and `delta` is invariant under scaling). ∎ The owner's picture is literal here: each scaffold alone is extremal in a harmless way (maximally lonely, or tight at its own small size), and interleaving them is the canonical hostile of the whole LRC program. The knight `{1,2}` is the first weave (`{1}` and `{2}` are each lonely at `1/2`).

**Corollary 2.3 (the 8x8 board; PROVED).** Subdivide the unit cell into the 8x8 board. Every coprime rider line `t(a,b)` meets the closed central four squares (ring 0, `lambda >= 3/8`) except the knight's, which reaches only ring 1. On the `2m x 2m` board the riders that miss the central 2x2 block are exactly those with `a + b` odd and `< m`; so 4x4 and 6x6 have none (on 6x6 the knight touches the block at its corners), 8x8 is the smallest board with an exceptional rider, and that rider is the knight. *Proof.* `delta >= (m-1)/(2m)` iff `s` even or `s >= m`, by Theorem 2.1. ∎ (`..._rider_rings.out` lists the exceptions for `2m = 4..16`.)

**What does not transfer (guardrails 5-7).** Two-dimensional projections of an `n`-speed set are lonely at `>= 1/3`, far above `1/(n+1)`, so no pair argument reaches LRC(14); the AP defeats counting; the board picture adds no averaging power. What it adds is vocabulary with exact content: rings = loneliness levels, colour = parity of the anti-diagonal, knight = the tight direction, AP = the `n`-dimensional knight. It is tight exactly as the knight is, and its lonely times at level `1/(n+1)` are exactly `t = k/(n+1)` with `gcd(k, n+1) = 1` (if `min_{j <= n} ||jt|| >= 1/(n+1)`, the `n+1` points `0, t, ..., nt` have all gaps `>= 1/(n+1)`, hence equal gaps, hence `t in (1/(n+1))Z`). The rider then sits at the permutation points `(sigma(1), ..., sigma(n))/(n+1)` on the boundary of the central cube of the `(n+1)^n` board (one coordinate on each of two opposite faces); for `n = 2` these are the corners `(1/3, 2/3), (2/3, 1/3)` of the centre square of the 3x3 board, which the knight's line grazes and never enters.
