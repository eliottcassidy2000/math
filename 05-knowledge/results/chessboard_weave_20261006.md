## 2. The lonely runner as a sliding piece (anchor)

**Dictionary (map / preserved / lost).** A speed vector `v = (v_1, ..., v_n)` is a rider direction; the time flow `t -> t v (mod 1)` is the rider sliding on the torus board `R^n/Z^n` from the corner (all runners at the start). Loneliness at `t` is `lambda(t) = min_i ||t v_i||`, the `L^infinity` distance from `t v` to the boundary of the unit cell, so **the Chebyshev rings of a subdivided cell are the level sets of the lonely-runner function**: on the `N x N` subdivision, the centre of a square in ring `k` has `lambda = (N - 1 - 2k)/(2N)`, and Proposition 1.2 becomes the exact affine identity

`bishop reach = (N - 2) + 2N lambda(centre).`

"Unlimited amounts until an obstruction is reached" is Cusick's view obstruction (THM-386): LRC with `n` speeds says every positive rider reaches the closed central cube `[1/(n+1), n/(n+1)]^n`, i.e. on the `(n+1)^n` subdivision it does not stay in the outer ring forever. **Theorem 2.1 (two speeds: loneliness lives on the anti-diagonal; PROVED).** For coprime positive integers `a, b` with `s = a + b`,

`delta(a,b) := max_t min(||ta||, ||tb||) = floor(s/2)/s`,

i.e. `1/2` when `s` is even (the `(a,b)`-leaper keeps its colour) and `1/2 - 1/(2s)` when `s` is odd (it changes colour). The minimum over all riders is `1/3`, attained only by the knight `{1,2}`.
*Proof.* Because `gcd(a,b) = 1`, the closed orbit `{t(a,b)}` is the connected subgroup `L = {(x,y) : bx - ay in Z}` of the torus. `lambda >= c` (`0 <= c < 1/2`) at a point iff it lies in the square `[c, 1-c]^2`. The linear form `f = bx - ay` maps this convex square onto the closed interval of centre `(b-a)/2` and half-length `s(1/2 - c)`. So `delta >= c` iff that interval contains an integer. If `s` is even the centre is an integer and `c` may be any value below `1/2` (and `t = 1/2` gives `1/2`). If `s` is odd the centre is a half-integer and we need `s(1/2 - c) >= 1/2`. ∎ Verified for all 489 coprime pairs `a < b <= 40` by exact evaluation over the candidate times (`..._rider_rings.out`). ## 5. Knight tours over the rings and scaffolds (niche; lane `knight` and the orchestrator)

**Theorem 5.1 (the weave law; PROVED).** In every closed knight's tour of the 8x8 board let `k` be the number of moves that stay inside the outer ring (ring 3). Then `k` also equals the number of moves that stay inside the middle annulus (rings 1 and 2), and `k >= 1`. Exactly `1 <= k <= 8` occur (CP-SAT optimal, both ends attained).
*Proof.* The owner's ring sizes are an arithmetic progression, so `|ring 0| + |ring 3| = 4 + 28 = 12 + 20 = |ring 1| + |ring 2| = 32`. For any vertex set `S` and any 2-regular spanning subgraph, `2|S| = 2 e(S) + e(S, S^c)`; with `|S| = |S^c|` this gives `e(S) = e(S^c)`. Take `S = ring 0 ∪ ring 3`: there is no knight move inside ring 0 and none between rings 0 and 3 (Proposition 1.3), so `e(S)` counts exactly the tour's moves inside ring 3, and `e(S^c)` its moves inside rings 1-2. If `k = 0`, every move of the tour leaves its side (`S` or `S^c`) and, being a knight move, also changes colour; so colour + side (mod 2) is constant along the tour, hence on the whole board — but `S` contains squares of both colours. ∎
**Hall form of the same obstruction.** Delete the 24 within-ring moves. For the colour class `W`, the 16 squares `X = W ∩ (ring 0 ∪ ring 3)` then have exactly the 16 neighbours `B ∩ (ring 1 ∪ ring 2)`, while a Hamiltonian cycle of a balanced bipartite graph needs `|N(X)| >= |X| + 1` for every non-empty proper `X` of one colour. Found independently by an exact CP-SAT minimisation of `|N(X)| - |X|` (value `0`, both colours; `..._nowithin_witness.out`) and confirmed by a second solver path (CaDiCaL with lazy subtour cuts: UNSAT after 3 cuts, positive control found a tour; `..._nowithin_check.out`). In the owner's words: a knight's tour cannot weave the scaffolds and the ring pairs perfectly; it must stall inside the outer ring at least once, and every stall there is paid by a stall in the middle annulus. Checked on 60 random closed tours (`k` from 4 to 8 seen; `..._weave_law.out`).
