## 2. Every finite tail itinerary occurs among the terminal powers

Use the shortcut map `T(n)=n/2` for even n and `(3n+1)/2` for odd n.
One T-step counts one halving, with an odd multiplication bundled in when

\[

\]

\[

\]

\[
j\bmod2^L\longmapsto
\bigl(Z(j)\bmod2,TZ(j)\bmod2,\ldots,T^{L-1}Z(j)\bmod2\bigr)  \tag{2.2}

arbitrarily large **positive integer** exponents in its residue class.

Proof. `125^j=1 mod4` supplies the actual initial parities `10`. Since
`v2(125-1)=2`, the elementary lifting identity gives
`v2(125^j-125^k)=2+v2(j-k)`. Dividing the difference by four and multiplying
by three proves (2.1). Hence Z permutes each finite residue ring. For the
second map, two sources differing by exactly `2^h` times an odd number have
the same first h parities. Following these common branches divides their
difference by `2^h` and multiplies it by an odd power of three; their next
parities differ. Thus the length-L parity map also permutes residues.
Composition proves the assertion and positive representatives are obtained

\[

## 6. Three observations suffice in the old 4/105 transfer

Use exactly the notation of

\quad\alpha_i=2(i+1)(2i+1)vd,\quad\beta_i=i(i+1)\Delta d.
\]

`C_r=alpha_(n+r) C_(r-1)+d beta_(n+r) C_(r-2)` for r>=2.

\[
E_{L+1}=u_{n+L}E_L+c_{n+L}C_{L-1}.                          \tag{6.1}
\]

**P6 (PROVED).** For every positive integer n, in every field of
characteristic zero, and all d!=0 and v, the three coefficients
`E_3,E_4,E_5` cannot vanish simultaneously. This closes the larger-n and
extension-field questions about this triple left in THM-3210 section4.
It is not a claim about every shifted triple of depths.

Proof. The inherited explicit expression is

\[
E_3=(n+2)[-(d-n-2)(n+3)\Delta+2(2n+3)vd(d-n-3)].             \tag{6.2}
\]

If Delta=0, then v=1/(4d); E3=0 forces d=n+3. The continuants C1,C2
are then nonzero and E4=-C2, a contradiction.

Suppose Delta!=0. Equation (6.1) and triple vanishing imply

\[
(d-n-4)C_2=0,\qquad(d-n-5)C_3=0.                           \tag{6.3}
\]

If d is neither n+4 nor n+5, then C2=C3=0. The invertible second-order
recurrence forces C1=0 and then C0=0, contradicting C0=1.

For d=n+4, (6.2) uniquely gives
`v=(n+3)/(3(n+4)(2n+5))`, the known double-cancellation ray. Delta is
`(2n+3)/(3(2n+5))`; C1,C2,C3 are positive rationals. Therefore E5=-C3!=0.
For d=n+5, (6.2) uniquely gives
`v=3(n+3)/(4(n+5)(5n+12))` and `Delta=(2n+3)/(5n+12)`.
Here C2 is a positive rational and E4=C2!=0. Positivity only establishes
nonzero rational values in these two specializations; those values remain
nonzero in every characteristic-zero extension. This exhausts the cases.

**P6a (PROVED, stronger real classification).** For every n>=1, the only
real solution of `E3=E4=0` with d!=0 is the displayed ray d=n+4. This

If d!=n+4, (6.1) would force C2=0. Write A=(2n+3)(2n+5). Explicitly

\[
C_2=d^2(n+2)(n+3)(4Av^2-4dv+1).
\]

Therefore `dv=(1+4Av^2)/4>0`. Substituting Delta=-4Av^2 into E3=0,

\[
2(d-n-2)(n+3)(2n+5)dv+d^2(d-n-3)=0.
\]

The positive coefficients force n+2<d<n+3. But in that interval
`d^2<(n+3)^2<A`, since `A-(n+3)^2=3n^2+10n+6>0`.
The quadratic `4Av^2-4dv+1` then has negative discriminant and no real
root. This contradicts C2=0. The d=n+4 case is already determined by E3.

\[
\max_{L=3,4,5}|E_L|\ge q^{-3}.
