# Both Collatz signs together still admit a global 3-edge-colouring

[Kohl's primary group construction](https://stefan-kohl.github.io/preprints/collatzgroups.pdf) and the [archived September Tait/Kempe analysis](../procgen_kuratowski_20260925_tait_kempe_triples.md) concern the one-sheet Schreier graph on the integers outside 0 mod 6, with fixed-generator half-edges. Its three involutions exchange odd n and 3n+1, lower m≡1 mod3 and 2m, or lower m≡2 mod3 and 2m, respectively. Negation swaps the doubling colours and transports the odd-up colour to the other sheet's odd-up colour. The closed {-1,−2} digon is the only closed **two-colour** chain in that archived classification, not a claim that there are no other Collatz cycles. The single-sheet undirected graph is a pseudoforest since the underlying directed graph is functional, and has no Petersen, K₅ or K₃,₃ minor. The archive gives window-specific first minor certificates 52,68,104 for K₃,₃,K₅,Petersen respectively when the two signs are overlaid.

## New: a perfect matching with acyclic complement in the whole overlay

Let U_q be the simple graph on all positive integers with doubling edges {m,2m}, and, for odd n, edges from n to qn±1, with q odd and q≥3. The matching M takes the doubling edges whose lower endpoint m has even v₂(m). This is a perfect matching: a vertex at even valuation is matched above, one at odd valuation below. Its complement has maximum degree two and no cycles, hence can be two-coloured by alternating along paths. Colouring M with a third colour gives a single global proper edge colouring, not separate finite-window colourings.

**Proof.** The remaining doubling edges disjointly pair even integers as {x,2x}, with v₂(x) odd. An even integer has at most one odd predecessor across both signs, since q does not divide 2. One such pair joined to both odd predecessors can be regarded as a single edge between the two odd sources; if just one odd edge arrives it is a dead end. Double-free even vertices need no colour beyond the matching and this residual graph.

* For q≥5 the two ends cannot both have odd predecessors: that would require x≡±1 and 2x≡±1 mod q. Thus a component of U_q−M has at most one odd vertex and at most four edges. Odd vertices have at most two incident residual edges.
* For q=3, pairs with 3|x have no odd predecessor; others have two unless one odd-up edge was the already removed doubling edge {1,2}. For x=3n+1, its doubled endpoint is 3(2n+1)−1. For x=3n−1, it is 3(2n−1)+1. The compressed edges among odd sources are therefore n↔2n±1. Apart from the exceptional loop candidate n=1, each goes from a smaller parent to a larger child, and the child has at most one possible odd parent: only one of (child−1)/2,(child+1)/2 is odd, and parent edges are a subset. Any finite cycle would have a greatest vertex with two smaller parents. The loop candidate uses 1→2 as its minus edge, but this edge is in M and contributes nothing to U₃−M, leaving just the path 1–4–2 at that boundary. For n>1, both up edges lead to pairs of even units with two distinct sources, so the compressed graph there is 2-regular and acyclic, a union of double rays. U₃−M is obtained by subdividing their edges into three, plus the boundary path and separate doubling edges on multiples of 3.

The compressed double ray with minimum 3 has fork 5–3–7: the paired even vertices {8,16} have sources 3 and 5, and {10,20} have sources 3 and 7. This gives a second explicit structural role for {3,5,7}, independent of the two missing counts. The graph can be properly three-coloured on its edges even though it has those minors; edge colourability of this graph does not assert edge colourability of graphs obtained by arbitrary contraction or suppression. Nor does colourability prove convergence for either sheet. What distinguishes 3 among odd multipliers here is the exact congruence 2≡−1 mod3, the source of long interactions in the complement.

One need not find a component minimum to colour the double rays. At odd n≥3 put k=(n−1)/2. Give the plus edge one of the two residual colours according to the parity of the binary length of k, and its minus edge the other. The two up edges for any paired even anchors have opposite signs and source labels k and 2k or 2k+1, so their colours agree. The doubling edge between the anchors gets the opposite colour. Give the boundary path and isolated edges any alternating colouring. This produces a finite rule per edge. The map k→2k or 2k+1 also explains why h=2k+1 conjugates their binary addresses to h→2h−1 or 2h+1, without identifying those compressed edges with ordinary Collatz arrows.
