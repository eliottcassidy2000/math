# An explicit prime-circulant tournament / sign-even graph bijection

20 October 2026. **PROVED elementary construction, pending independent proof audit.** The map is a bijection of isomorphism types for every odd prime. Its forward realization on translation-invariant labelled objects is an affine-equivariant **surjection inducing that type bijection**, not a labelled bijection. The algorithm uses cyclic differentiation, integration, and a specified local choice between two predecessor necklaces. It does not rank whole catalogues or invoke Hall's theorem. Historical novelty is not claimed.

## 1. Inheritance and precise target

Closest mechanism: THM-4721, `twisted-burnside-on-odd-fixed-point-sets-sylow-refinement-of-even-graphs-and-tournaments-components-walsh-basis`, Corollary C, proves prime-circulant type equinumerosity and an existence result through THM-4531's stabilizer-containment criterion. Its full note is `even_graphs_20261019_sylow_refinement_components_walsh_duality.md`. The prior `tournament_even_bijection_20261011c.md` gives an all-order SCC/Lyndon codec with ranking at irreducible atoms; the present map removes such ranking in the prime-circulant sector only. The least-used sidecar is the cyclic derivative's nilpotent/invertible decomposition from `binary_lfsr_tournament_clocks_20261020/`.

The live concepts are negacyclic words, primitive-root parity, two-preimage differentiation, path cutting, and odd multiplier stabilizers. A graph is **sign-even** when every graph automorphism reverses an even number of its edges relative to a reference orientation. This is not even vertex degree. Every undirected prime circulant has even degree, while many fail sign-evenness.

### Two inherited wording corrections

1. An `AGL(1,p)`-equivariant **labelled bijection** is impossible already at p=3: the two translation-invariant labelled directed triangles have one type, whereas the empty graph is the sole sign-even translation-invariant labelled graph. Their affine stabilizers have orders 3 and 6. At p=5 the corresponding labelled counts are 4 and 1. What Lemma N1 establishes, and this packet constructs, is an equivariant map inducing a bijection of types. A genuine bijection between finite G-sets would require conjugate stabilizers, not merely containment.
2. The statement that every tournament invariant under the maximal odd multiplier subgroup must map to the empty graph is not true for all primes. At p=17 that subgroup is trivial and there are 16 tournament types. More generally, if p−1=2^r q with q odd, its fixed sector has 2^(2^(r−1))/2^r types. This is one only when r=1 or r=2. The inherited forcing argument needs that restriction.

Neither correction refutes the underlying prime-circulant type equality.

## 2. The necklace dictionary

Fix p=2m+1 prime and a generator g of the cyclic group F_p^*. One may uniformly choose the least positive primitive root. A circulant tournament is specified by a skew selector S⊂F_p^*, exactly one of x,−x lying in S. Write

    f_i = [g^i∈S],    i modulo 2m.

Then f_(i+m)=1+f_i over F_2. Multiplication by g shifts the word; translations fix the circulant. Thus tournament multiplier types are rotation orbits of these antiperiodic words.

An undirected circulant has symmetric connection set A=−A. Write its indicator as a word w of length m, repeated twice in the exponent coordinates. Its multiplier types are ordinary binary necklaces of length m. Let d be the least period of w and v=w_0...w_(d−1) its primitive root.

**Sign criterion.** The graph is sign-even if and only if wt(v) is even.

*Proof.* The multiplier stabilizer is generated by a=g^d and has order r=2m/d, which is even. Under x↦ax, its self-paired edge orbits are the antipodal pairs {x,−x} in the nonzero vertex cycles. The condition that this is an edge is −2x∈A. There are |A|/r such cycles; hence the generator has edge-reversal sign (−1)^(|A|/r)=(−1)^wt(v). The sign character is trivial on the full affine stabilizer exactly when this is +1, since translations have odd order.

For completeness this affine test detects the full automorphism sign, without an assumption that the full automorphism group is affine. The translation group P=C_p is Sylow in S_p. If X has an odd-sign automorphism, its sign kernel B is normal of index two in Aut(X) and contains P. For any odd-sign a, P and aPa^−1 are Sylow subgroups of B, so some b∈B conjugates the latter to P. Then ba is an odd-sign automorphism in N(P)=AGL(1,p). Conversely any odd affine automorphism already witnesses failure. This is THM-4721's Frattini step. It also handles empty and complete graphs. ∎

Isomorphism types meeting this circulant family equal multiplier types: if an isomorphism s sends X to Y, then P and sPs^−1 are Sylow subgroups of Aut(Y), hence conjugate there. Composing s with such an automorphism produces an element of N(P). This applies to tournaments and graphs. No prime-circulant automorphism classification is needed.

The desired target is therefore

    B_m = {binary necklaces of length m with even-weight primitive root}.

## 3. One derivative turns tournaments into source necklaces

For a cyclic binary word define D=I+R, so (Dw)_i=w_i+w_(i+1). Given antiperiodic f of length 2m, Df is m-periodic and its first m bits have odd weight, since their sum telescopes to f_0+f_m=1.

Conversely any length-m word u of odd weight integrates uniquely after choosing f_0: recursively set f_(i+1)=f_i+u_i. The two choices give complements, and each satisfies f_(i+m)=1+f_i. Those two integrations differ by a rotation through m places, so they define one tournament type. Rotation commutes with D. Consequently D gives a bijection

    antiperiodic rotation classes on 2m positions ↔ O_m,
    O_m = {ordinary length-m necklaces with odd total weight}.

This is only the intermediate map: its output has odd primitive-root weight and is **never** the desired sign-even graph.

## 4. The indegree trichotomy

Regard D as a function on all length-m binary necklaces. The indegree of a necklace w is:

- zero if wt(w) is odd;
- two if its primitive root has even weight;
- one if its primitive root has odd weight and its repetition count is even.

These alternatives exhaust the possibilities.

*Proof.* The equations Dz=w have solutions of length m precisely when wt(w) is even, and then there are exactly two word solutions z and z+1. If w has primitive period d, integration gives

    z_(i+d)=z_i+wt(w_0...w_(d−1)) modulo 2.

When the primitive-root weight is even, both solutions have exact period d and cannot be rotation-equivalent: a rotation interchanging them would stabilize w, hence be a multiple of d, which fixes z. Thus there are two predecessor necklaces.

When that weight is odd, the repetition count must be even for solutions to exist. Both solutions have exact period 2d and are interchanged by rotation through d places, hence form one predecessor necklace. ∎

In particular the sources are O_m, and the indegree-two branches are B_m. This establishes the relevant objects structurally rather than only by comparing Burnside formulas.

A useful extra consequence is that differentiation changes the primitive period only from d to d or d/2. Indeed if Dz has period e then R^e z+z is constant, so R^(2e)z=z. Since the period of Dz divides the period of z, the latter is e or 2e. The odd part of the primitive period is constant on every derivative edge.

## 5. Cutting one incoming edge at each branch

The following elementary finite-functional-graph lemma makes the bijection explicit.

**Path lemma.** Suppose a finite function graph has indegrees only 0,1,2. At every indegree-two vertex b choose exactly one predecessor c(b) and delete the edge c(b)→b. The resulting graph has indegree and outdegree at most one. Its sources are exactly the original indegree-zero vertices, and its terminals are exactly the c(b). Every source path ends at one terminal: it cannot enter a cycle without creating two retained incoming edges. Reversing the unique retained edges from a terminal likewise reaches one source. Thus following a source to c(b), then restoring its last edge, gives a bijection between sources and branches, with explicit inverse.

For necklaces, both predecessors are obtained by cumulative XOR and canonicalized by taking their least cyclic rotations. A fully specified elementary rule is to cut the lexicographically larger predecessor. This alone gives an all-m bijection. The following stable-subspace rule improves the running-time bound and is the rule used in the final implementation.

Write m=e q, where e=2^v2(m) and q is odd. Define

    U = {w: XOR_(j=0,...,q−1) w_(r+je)=0 for each r=0,...,e−1}.

This is rotation-invariant. D preserves U and is injective on U: its only nonzero kernel word is the all-one word, which does not lie in U because q is odd. Therefore D permutes U. Also D^e=I+R^e, so every word enters U after at most e derivatives. The periodic necklace vertices are precisely the necklaces in U: if D^k w is a rotation of w, a power gives D^l w=w, and invertibility on U together with eventual entry forces w∈U.

For a target in U its two word preimages differ by the all-one word, so exactly one lies in U; since U is rotation-invariant they are two distinct predecessor necklaces.

**Final cut rule.**

1. At a branch in U, cut its unique predecessor outside U.
2. At every other branch, cut the lexicographically larger canonical predecessor.

A source has odd total weight, hence is outside U. Its path stops before entering U, so it crosses at most e derivative edges. The inverse has the same bound. The companion `bounded_fitting_routing_20261020/BOUNDED_FITTING_ROUTING.md` proves the more general commuting-group/Fitting version.

## 6. Forward algorithm, inverse, and complexity

**Forward, on actual words.**

1. Given the antiperiodic tournament word f, form u=(Df)_0..._(m−1).
2. Put x=u. Compute y=Dx and both predecessor necklace classes of y.
3. If [x] is the designated cut predecessor of [y], return the graph word y.
4. Otherwise put x=y and repeat.

The number k of repetitions is between 1 and e. Thus the graph word is the first half of D^(k+1)f. The stopping rule depends only on rotation classes, while actual derivatives retain the phase. The resulting map is therefore equivariant under every multiplier and translation.

**Inverse, on graph types.**

1. Start at an even-root graph necklace y and choose its designated cut predecessor x=c(y).
2. If x has odd total weight, stop. Otherwise take its unique retained predecessor: its sole predecessor if indegree one, or the uncut predecessor if indegree two.
3. Repeat until reaching an odd-total necklace u; at most e predecessor steps are needed in total.
4. Integrate u antiperiodically and take its rotation class.

The path lemma and the integration lemma prove both inverse identities. No graph catalogue, tournament catalogue, matching, or ranking by type is used. Least rotation is only a comparison between two local predecessor classes. With a simple quadratic-time least-rotation routine, both directions take O(e m^2) bit operations and O(m) working storage, apart from the initial field/connection-set encoding. Finding the chosen primitive root is a separate elementary finite computation.

### Preserved symmetry and unavoidable labelled fibers

If the source necklace u has period d, then m/d is odd and f has period 2d. If the final graph word has period h, every derivative edge shows h=d/2^j for some j≥0. The tournament multiplier stabilizer has order m/d; the graph multiplier stabilizer has order 2m/h=2^(j+1)m/d. Their odd-order subgroups are identical. Hence the map preserves, in both directions, invariance under each specified odd multiplier subgroup.

On the corresponding labelled orbit the forward map has constant fiber size 2^(j+1). This is precisely why it is a surjection, not a labelled bijection. The inverse is naturally an inverse on types. This statement concerns multiplier stabilizers; it does not claim equality of the odd parts of the full graph automorphism groups.

## 7. Closed formula for an infinite prime family

When p≡3 mod 4, m is odd and e=1. The final rule cuts the unique odd-weight predecessor, so the map always stops at the first ordinary derivative:

    F(f)=D^2 f=(I+R^2)f,
    A={x∈F_p^*: [x∈S] ≠ [g^2 x∈S]}.

Here g^2 generates the odd-order subgroup of quadratic residues. Every sign-even circulant graph is recovered by integrating along that subgroup, with the two complementary choices equivalent by multiplication by −1. The forward map is exactly two-to-one on labelled circulants and bijective on types. This applies to every prime congruent to 3 modulo 4, an infinite family.

For p≡5 mod8 the same uniform algorithm needs at most two ordinary derivative steps, choosing between D^2 f and D^3 f according to the local rule. Larger dyadic factors are handled without a catalogue search.

## 8. Hostiles and scope

- **Wrong graph predicate:** 0101 has even total weight but primitive root 01 of odd weight. At p=17, the word 01010101 gives an Eulerian circulant of degree eight that is sign-odd. All prime circulants are Eulerian, so degree parity alone distinguishes none of the target sector.
- **One derivative:** Df always has odd total weight and misses sign-evenness.
- **Uniformly two derivatives:** at p=5, f=0011 gives first-half Df=01 and first-half D^2f=11, the complete graph, which is sign-odd. A third derivative gives 00, the required empty graph.
- **Flip the final bit of the least rotation:** at m=6, odd-weight primitive necklaces 000111 and 001101 both land in the necklace 000011. A pointwise parity repair need not be injective on necklaces.
- **Why phase matters:** canonicalizing the output before returning it would destroy labelled affine equivariance. Canonicalization is used for the stopping decision and the type-valued inverse only.
- **Prime boundary:** the word correspondence is proved for every m, but its identification with all graph-isomorphism types in a circulant family uses p prime through the Sylow argument. No composite-order analogue, all-order tournament/even-graph bijection, or S_n-equivariant type map is asserted.
- **Choice boundary:** changing the primitive root or the off-cycle branch ordering can change the pairing. The construction is uniform and explicit, not independent of every arithmetic choice.

## 9. Primary literature and overlap

The operator is classical: Abraham Lempel, *On a Homomorphism of the de Bruijn Graph and Its Applications to the Design of Feedback Shift Registers*, IEEE Transactions on Computers C-19 (1970), 1204–1209, [DOI 10.1109/T-C.1970.222859](https://doi.org/10.1109/T-C.1970.222859); verified through the [Technion institutional record](https://cris.technion.ac.il/en/publications/on-a-homomorphism-of-the-de-bruijn-graph-and-its-applications-to-/). Melvyn Nathanson, *Derivatives of Binary Sequences*, SIAM J. Applied Math. 21 (1971), 407–412, [DOI 10.1137/0121043](https://doi.org/10.1137/0121043), also studies the same XOR derivative. No priority is claimed for differentiation, integration, or its parity behavior.

A close antecedent is Baker–Chen–Li–Qian, *Necklaces, permutations, and periodic critical orbits for quadratic polynomials*: [arXiv v2, September 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.12924v2), published in [European J. Combinatorics 134 (2026), 104352](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejc.2026.104352). Theorem A constructs a bijection from odd-weight primitive necklaces of length n to even-weight primitive necklaces of length n plus odd-weight primitive necklaces of length n/2. Iteration gives a different route to our necklace correspondence. Explicit parity-necklace bijections therefore predate this packet. Our self-contained derivative paths and bounded routing are not claimed historically new.

The sign-even target and original unlabelled question are from Royle–Praeger–Glasby–Freedman–Devillers, [*Tournaments and Even Graphs are Equinumerous*](https://arxiv.org/html/2204.01947v2), J. Algebraic Combinatorics 57 (2023), 515–524. This packet independently proves its restricted prime-circulant construction, while the general equinumerosity theorem and general natural-bijection question retain their own scope.

### A precise quadratic-dynamics connection

Let H_d be the real parameters c for which 0 has exact period d under z↦z²+c. Baker–Chen–Li–Qian's Theorem A also gives an explicit bijection from odd-weight primitive length-d necklaces to H_d. Partitioning our source necklaces by their primitive period therefore gives a composition bijection

    circulant tournament types of prime order p ↔ disjoint union of H_d,
    d divides m=(p−1)/2 and m/d is odd.

In particular t_p=Σ |H_d| over those d. The conventions include H_1={0} and H_2={−1}. For p=7 the two types correspond to exact critical periods 1 and 3. These are real critical parameters, not rational periodic cycles; the correspondence uses the cited kneading construction, not merely equal numerical counts.
