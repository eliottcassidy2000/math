## 2. Reset/action theorem: mathematical verification

Let q be onto, M the permitted finite deterministic transformation monoid, and every reset have its entire full-state row determined by q(x). For a finite X, the signature e(x)=(q(mx)) over M is exactly the coarsest stable partition refining q. Necessity follows from separately available deterministic words; sufficiency follows because signature coordinates are permuted/reindexed under composition and reset rows agree before any quotient.

The terminal experiment protocol must be fixed in advance and read only the final q. For this protocol the rowspace W is the span of the indicators of q(mx). Deterministic pullbacks preserve W; every reset pullback of every function lands already among functions of q. Conversely every generating row is an allowed terminal experiment. Thus W is exactly, not merely an upper bound for, the full terminal response space. This proves the minimum rank for **all input signed laws**. A fixed-initial scalar Hankel matrix may have a smaller rank and is not being identified with this map.

The products of the signature coordinate indicators recover every point indicator on E. This is valid over any field, including finite characteristic. It does not mean terminal linear experiments can multiply their responses on a common hidden sample: those products are an enlarged observer menu.

For an involution, the observation matrix is the unsigned incidence matrix of the realized bipartite pair graph. Multiplying one shore by −1 gives an oriented matrix; in characteristic two the same argument still holds. A row dependence is constant on each connected component, so rank is 2|Q|−c in every field. A spanning forest has independent columns and each chord gives one alternating cycle kernel vector. The kernel dimension on pair laws is β=|E|−2|Q|+c, and chord masses form an optimal β-coordinate repair.

The real-positive cone argument is also exact. Every column has one nonzero coordinate in each observation block. A nonnegative combination equal to one column can use no column with support outside it, so all |E| columns are extreme rays. The probability section has exactly those |E| vertices and dimension rank−1. Hence the cone is linearly isomorphic, within its span, to a coordinate orthant precisely when the columns are independent; in the involution case this is precisely the forest condition. This does **not** say the displayed marginal coordinates themselves form an orthant. The report's Hall inequalities correctly characterize nonnegative feasibility.

## 4. Cyclic all-arity theorem and sparse pair tests

Equality of all pair differences gives pairwise compatible common-shift congruences, hence one simultaneous shift by generalized CRT.

Antisymmetry and triangle congruences modulo the triple gcd are sufficient, not merely necessary. At each prime choose a factor with maximal valuation. Its edge residues determine all coordinates modulo their respective prime powers; the triangle relation makes every other edge agree. Ordinary CRT then combines the primes. This covers d_i=1 and arity1. Therefore any claim that complete labelled cyclic pair phases inherently miss a deterministic third-order invariant would be false.
