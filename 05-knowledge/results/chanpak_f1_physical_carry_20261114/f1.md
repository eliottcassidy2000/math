# Closing the normalized carry with literal finite sets

This is a partial result toward absolute-target closure. It gives a physical
unbroken interval of carries, and a distinct bank in which all residues
are also physical. The residue and interval choices are not yet aligned
at an arbitrary absolute target.

## The field-with-one-element lens

Use finite sets with disjoint union and Cartesian product, not a literal
field of size one. An apex can belong to at most one cycle in a
directed odd-cycle packing. A matched core replacement changes
only the packings whose apex cycle is wholly inside that core;
the other cores contribute independent Cartesian factors of size
\(h_i\). Consequently local increments \(2g_i\) contribute
\[
 2g_i\prod_{j\ne i}h_j
\]
separately, as in the earlier common-apex formula. More explicitly,
the two local choices at each position form a genuine two-element
finite set. Label all repeated positions, and read a tuple by its two
subset sizes. There are \(\binom ai\binom bj\) physical tuples
over a pair of ranks \((i,j)\). This is a Boolean-subset (rather
than a formal coefficient or a signed subtraction) model for carry
multiplicity. The binomial coefficient is the usual \(q=1\)
evaluation of the finite-field subspace counts; no field operations
are invoked.

We use the same profile convention as the
[earlier two-wing transfer](https://github.com/eliottcassidy2000/math/blob/1abd4acb779e3cb2eb8de8066da4cb171613b7f4/05-knowledge/results/chanpak_movable_reader_returns_20261114/bilateral.md):
\((h,f,P,Q,S,T)\) has base count, two-slot count, unsigned two
contact sums, and the negatives of the signed sums (full Hamiltonian
end and start counts in the contact subsets). \(K\) is the count of
the core together with the apex alone. Here are literal rows (out-neighbor
masks) and apex out-contact masks \(U\):

| h | out-neighbor masks | U | K | (h,f,P,Q,S,T) |
|---:|---|---:|---:|---|
| 15 | (18,20,9,3,12) | 3 | 41 | (15,120,42,63,6,9) |
| 15 | (18,20,9,3,12) | 5 | 43 | same |
| 29 | (10,44,57,48,35,1) | 24 | 91 | (29,320,93,198,9,20) |

The middle pair switches the actual core rather than just the contact
mask. The last pair permits a different prime-friendly family.

## Consecutive weights close an exact window

Take \(a\) cores of type 15 and \(b\) cores of type 29, in any fixed
order, with arbitrary fixed transitive wings. Let \(N_0\) be the
baseline with all cores low. Put \(L=15^{a-1}29^{b-1}\)
(for \(a,b\ge1\)). The exact count set is
\[
  \{N_0+2L(29i+30j):0\le i\le a,\ 0\le j\le b\}.
\]
In particular one core of each type affords a **unit** change by
switching them in opposite directions: \(29-30=-1\). This works
with all other contacts fixed and all those wings arbitrary.
For a literal example, put type 29 before type 15 with wing addresses
\((x,y)=(1,17)\). The two independent opposite choices give the
18-vertex counts \(898427\) (prime) and \(898429=7\cdot128347\)
(with prime cofactor).

If \(a\ge29,\ b\ge28\), every integer carry from \(812\) through
\(29a+30b-812\), inclusive, is physical. Neither adjacent carry
811 nor \(29a+30b-811\) is possible. To see this, at rank \(k=i+j\)
the carries are the integer interval
\[
 [29k+\max(0,k-a),\ 29k+\min(b,k)].
\]
Neighboring intervals join for all \(28\le k\le a+b-29\), since the
distance between one maximum and the next minimum is at most one.
\(I_{28}\) starts at 812 and \(I_{a+b-28}\) ends at the claimed
upper limit. The number 811 is \(28\) modulo 29 and would need
\(j\ge28\), but \(30\cdot28>811\); complementing both subsets gives
the upper failure. The literal solution for a given carry \(d\) is
obtained by taking \(j\equiv d\pmod {29}\) in
\[
 [\max(0,\lceil(d-29a)/30\rceil),\ \min(b,\lfloor d/30\rfloor)]
\]
and setting \(i=(d-30j)/29\). Choosing actual subsets gives the source.

This particular saturated bank does not touch large primes. For
each type-15 core every exposed coordinate is divisible by 3
while \(K\) need not be. In every term of the common-apex expansion,
at least \(a-1\) type-15 factors are exposed rather than the exceptional
\(K\). Thus \(3^{a-1}\mid N\), regardless of reader, other core blocks,
or the chosen switches.

## Residue surjection without the factor-three barrier

Use \(a\) type-77 cores and \(b\) type-29 cores instead. Then
\(M=77^a29^b\), \(L=M/(77\cdot29)\), and the gain is
\(2L(29i+154j)\). In general for coprime coin weights \(A,B\)
and bounds \(a\ge B-1,\ b\ge A-1\), the attainable carries contain
the exact central interval
\[
 [(A-1)(B-1), Aa+Bb-(A-1)(B-1)].
\]
For each residue modulo \(A\), the possible second digits are
\(j_0+At\), where \(0\le j_0<A\) and \(t\) runs through a consecutive
initial interval. After subtracting \(Bj_0\) and dividing by \(A\),
these give \([Bt,Bt+a]\), which join. The previous missing integer of
any residue is at most \(B(A-1)-A\); complementing gives the upper
bound. The Frobenius endpoint \(AB-A-B\) is indeed missing by the
same residue calculation. Here the conductor is \(4284\).

Moreover **every residue modulo \(M\)** has an actual baseline
in this same bank. Combine the centers into one ordinal-sum core;
its two-slot number is \(F=2096^a320^b\) by independent color choices,
and \(\gcd(F,M)=1\). Use even binary addresses \(x,y\) in the two-wing
formula. If \(P_C,Q_C\) are the unsigned exposed sums of this
combined core and \(K_C\) the low-option baseline without wings,
then
\[
 N_0(x,y) \equiv K_C+Q_Cx+(P_C+Fx)y\pmod M .
\]
Here the signed contacts vanish by even parity, and the wing-only
counts multiply \(M\). To make \(P_C+Fx\) invertible, avoid exactly
one residue for \(x\) modulo each prime dividing \(M\), by CRT;
choose such \(x>0\) even. The displayed equation then chooses
positive even \(y\) for any prescribed absolute residue. The two wings
ensure that these graphs are strong. At that baseline all central
carried values in the stated interval are literal as well.

What is still missing is an *absolute* bound on the gap between the
selected low baseline and the target. Choosing \(y\bmod M\) fixes a
residue, not the location of the window, and the window does not
automatically bridge the jumps to the next eligible \(y\).
Thus neither this unbroken carry interval nor its residue surjection
proves Chan–Pak.

There is also a simple capacity obstruction to the strategy of fixing
the CRT-valid \(x>0\) once and using only the other wing. For prescribed
\(a,b,x\), at height \(X\) only \(y\le X/(Fx)\) can contribute, since the
cross term \(Fxy\) is nonnegative and all terms of the positive-profile
readout are nonnegative. Among even \(y\) there are therefore at most
\[
  \left(1+\frac{X}{2Fx}\right)(29a+154b+1)
\]
distinct counts, using every switch option, not just the central window.
Since \(F=2096^a320^b\), this is strictly insufficient for all odd values
even before imposing a prescribed target residue. A genuine two-sided
baseline choice, or a different multiplicative geometry, is necessary
for this route.

Run `python3 replay_f1.py` for literal profile verification, the
two-wing unit switch, and the two exact bounded-coin carry inverses.

## Sharpened carry window from the padded five-path core

The companion minimal-unit-switch construction gives an even stronger
pair. The two literal punctures below have the same
\((h,f,P,Q,S,T)=(5,1024,716,810,0,0)\) and augmented counts
579 and 581. The low puncture rows are
\((510,496,482,486,492,448,384,256,0)\) and apex mask 105.
The high rows are \((510,508,504,496,384,272,304,352,0)\)
and apex mask 45. Pair this with the earlier \(h=17\) double switch
of profile \((17,224,72,135,6,11)\), with \(K=65,69\).
With \(a\) five-path cores and \(b\) seventeen-path cores, the
gain is
\[
 2\cdot5^{a-1}17^{b-1}(17i+10j),\quad 0\le i\le a,\quad 0\le j\le b.
\]
Already \(a\ge9,\ b\ge16\) suffices for the exact central carry
interval \([144,17a+10b-144]\), with adjacent carries absent.
The same all-residue argument applies modulo \(5^a17^b\), since
\(\gcd(1024\cdot224,5\cdot17)=1\). This improves the construction's
width and conductor, but does not remove the absolute alignment
obstruction.

## A stronger mixed bank: consecutive 10 and 11 with conductor 90

There is a matched eleven-path double switch with profile
\((11,3200,671,929,11,11)\) and \(K=209,213\). Its exact choices
are the following punctures, with their apex masks:
\[
\begin{array}{c|l|r}
K & \text{puncture rows} & U\\\hline
209&(1022,1020,1016,704,520,536,560,624,760,0)&812\\
213&(1022,996,896,902,910,924,956,768,512,0)&678 .
\end{array}
\]
Put \(a\) five-path cores **before** \(b\) eleven-path cores.
The gain is \(2\cdot 5^{a-1}11^{b-1}(11i+10j)\), so already
\(a\ge9,\ b\ge10\) gives an exact normalized interval
\[
 [90,\ 11a+10b-90] .
\]
There is also joint surjectivity modulo \(M=5^a11^b\), despite
the total split coefficient \(F=1024^a3200^b\) being divisible by 5.
For even wing addresses, fix positive \(y\) first and solve for \(x\).
The coefficient of \(x\) modulo the product \(M\) is \(Q_C+Fy\).
The complete ordinal sum has
\[
 Q_C=\sum_i Q_i\prod_{j<i}f_j\prod_{j>i}h_j
\]
by splitting at the last selected endpoint. Modulo 5, only the last
five-path core and the first eleven-path core survive: all earlier
five-path choices leave a factor \(5\) on the right, and all later
eleven-path choices introduce a factor \(3200\) on the left.
Consequently
\[
 Q_C\equiv 4^{a-1}(810+4\cdot929)\equiv4^{a-1}\not\equiv0\pmod5 .
\]
On the other hand \(F\) is invertible modulo 11, so choose \(y\)
even positive avoiding exactly one residue modulo 11. Then solving
for even positive \(x\) gives any desired residue of the absolute
baseline modulo \(M\). With that chosen baseline the normalized
carry interval above is exact. Again no assertion is made that the
appropriate interval reaches every target in its residue.
