## 2. Necessary presentation equations and normalization

Let the labelled Hamilton cycle have successor `R(i)=i+1 modulo n`. Its automorphic rotation subgroup is generated by `R^q`, with odd order `m=n/q`. The cutoff leaves the stated seven pairs `(n,q)`.

The blue-cut lemma is used correctly:

- For q=3, zero blue cut orbits would leave three black cut orbits, impossible at deficit two. There is therefore a blue cut, and the exact blue count is gcd(2,3)=1.
- For q=4, zero blue cut orbits would leave four black orbits. There is a blue cut, and the exact blue count is gcd(2,4)=2.
- Translating the cycle coordinates so that any such blue cut starts at zero makes its positional reversal `r(i)=n-1-i` an anti-automorphism. This translation preserves the successor-rotation presentation.
- For q=2, no blue cut need exist. Neither the producer nor the independent reconstruction imposes anti-reflection. This distinction is essential.

The order-7 orbit refinement is also used exhaustively. An outdegree-four vertex has three internal outneighbors in its regular seven-orbit, and its Hamilton successor is an external outneighbor. It therefore loses to every other external vertex. Rotation propagates this exact condition throughout its residue orbit. After blue-cut normalization the low orbit can have any residue, so all q residues are tried.

For n=21, low residue 1 is inconsistent. There is a direct explanation in addition to the linear-system contradiction: standard anti-reflection preserves residue 1 modulo 3, while rotation makes degrees constant on that orbit. Anti-reflection would therefore force its common outdegree to equal its indegree, hence be 10 rather than 4. Residues 0 and 2 remain. All four residues remain for n=28. The q=2 order-14 space is deliberately left completely unfiltered; using a necessary condition only in the larger spaces cannot discard a candidate.

No assumption that the actual rotation subgroup is exactly the imposed subgroup is needed in the enumeration. A presentation with extra symmetry is harmless. Likewise duplicates and multiple low-degree orbits are harmless: these are covering spaces of labelled presentations, not an isomorphism-class census.

## 3. Independent full-pair affine reconstruction

`reconstruct_spaces.py` imports no producer code. Rather than propagating signed pair orbits, it introduces a Boolean variable `x_ij` for every unordered pair i<j, with value one for the arc i→j. For ordered endpoints write

    A(i,j) = x_min(i,j),max(i,j) XOR [i>j].

It imposes the literal linear equations

    A(i,j) = A(i+q,j+q),
    A(i,j) XOR A(n-1-i,n-1-j) = 1  (only q=3 or4),
    A(i,i+1) = 1,

with indices modulo n. At the restricted endpoint it additionally fixes all external arcs of the representative low vertex, retaining its successor as its unique external outneighbor.

Ordinary GF(2) Gaussian elimination on these full-pair equations yields one particular solution and a basis indexed by free pair coordinates. Each solution and each homogeneous basis vector is checked directly against every original equation. Distinct free coordinates give linearly independent directions. This constructs the entire affine solution space, not a sampled subset.

## 4. Independent exhaustive search and certificate replay

`independent_scan.cpp` uses the independent affine basis and ordinary binary assignment order, not the producer's pair-orbit basis or Gray traversal. It independently searches every assignment.

For every tournament, not just a sample, it verifies:

- exactly one arc per unordered pair, with no loops or high bits;
- every prescribed Hamilton-cycle edge;
- rotation by q preserving each arc;
- standard reflection reversing each arc only for q=3 and4;
- outdegree four for every vertex in the designated low orbit, when present.

Its obstruction predicate differs from the producer in two important ways:

1. It explicitly tries all six directed four-cycles rooted at the smallest of the four vertices. It does not use the producer's four-vertex degree shortcut.
2. It checks the complement with bit-parallel Warshall transitive closure, not forward/backward graph searches from one root.

The terminal condition is checked by counting the four arcs individually. Every output witness is checked again as a five-vertex certificate. All 2,121,984 assignments have a valid witness; there are **zero survivors**. Independent complete results are in `independent_full_scan.jsonl`. The longest lexicographic search position is 41 for the large n=20 family and 50 for the n=28 low-2 family, matching the producer without depending on those search limits.

Separately, every one of the producer's 2,121,984 five-byte records was reconstructed in its documented Gray order and checked by the independent cycle/closure predicate. All succeed, with exact end-of-file checks. Results are in `producer_certificate_replay.jsonl`.

For every certificate, I checked both raw and gzip SHA-256 against the producer index, decompressed equality, exact byte length `5*presentations`, and stated record count. The replayed output streams are identical to the source streams. Neither hash validation nor a partial sample substitutes for the full replay or the independent full search.

## 5. Hostile controls and implementation checks

The q=2 spaces genuinely include presentations with no standard anti-reflection:

- n=10: 32 presentations have standard anti-reflection and **96 do not**;
- n=14: 256 have it and **1,792 do not**.

All of the latter presentations are included and excluded by the independent scan. Explicit non-reflection examples are saved in `algebra_and_boundaries.json`. An accidental anti-reflection restriction would therefore have been detected.

`predicate_controls.cpp` checks Warshall strongness against an independent exhaustive dominating-cut predicate for every labelled tournament of each order 1 through 6. It also checks, on all 64 labelled four-vertex tournaments, equivalence of strongness, presence of a directed four-cycle, and the producer's internal-degree test. Counts of strong tournaments are 1, 0, 2, 24, 544, and 22,320 respectively.

The scanner uses unsigned 32-bit masks only at orders at most 28, and 64-bit assignment counts at dimensions at most 21. Both shift ranges are safe. Its binary-counter update toggles precisely the affine directions in `index XOR (index-1)`; the separate producer replay uses the documented single Gray-bit change. All essential checks throw errors; they do not depend on C++ assertions.

The full independent scan was additionally rerun under AddressSanitizer and UndefinedBehaviorSanitizer. Leak detection alone is disabled because this execution environment runs under ptrace, which LeakSanitizer explicitly does not support. The actual address/undefined-behavior checks remain enabled. Results and stream-equivalence checks are recorded in the final validation summary.

## 6. Why this completes the mathematical proof

Every putative previously unknown deficit-two center has a presentation in at least one of the reconstructed spaces. Every presentation has a strong four-set S, a vertex v outside S beaten by at least three vertices of S, and a strong complement T−S. The complement has a Hamilton path starting at v, obtained by cutting its Hamilton cycle. Since all residual orders are at least 10, the four-prefix and its reflected support are disjoint.

The already-audited half-supported clique argument therefore gives deficit at least four for every candidate that is self-converse, contradicting deficit two. In the q=2 families some presentations may not be self-converse; that does not matter, because the argument is applied only to the putative center contained in the covering space.

All seven residual coefficients vanish. The inherited census through nine, independently reinforced by the explicit positive S4 count, now gives D=z^4. There is no remaining coefficient, unbounded-order, or reflection-normalization gap in this proof.
