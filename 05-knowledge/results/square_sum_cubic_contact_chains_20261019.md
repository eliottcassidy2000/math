# Cubic same-sign contacts: one infinite rational chain and nested finite-prefix scalings

**Status: PROVED, pending independent audit.** Companion controls are **FINITE-EXACT**. This improves the earlier degree-11 growing-chain construction: initialization depends only on the prescribed seed, and each subsequent petal costs one fixed cubic map. It gives exponential-in-length bit bounds, not polynomial-in-length bit bounds.

## 1. Main theorem and conventions

An admissible primitive petal is an unordered primitive Pythagorean triple `T=(a,b,C)` with `C²>4ab`. Its spread is `f_T=2ab/C²`. At common scale `C|H`, its two outer labels and the common center are

\[
 v_T^\pm=2H^2(1\pm2f_T),\qquad M=2H^2.                 \tag{1}
\]

A contact between ports `sigma,tau` is equivalent to
`1+sigma f_T+tau f_U` being a rational square. The existing AP classification supplies the triangle edges and the path-to-cycle transfer.

**Theorem 1.** Given an admissible primitive petal `T_*=(a,b,C_*)` and prescribed outgoing port `epsilon`, there is a deterministic algorithm, polynomial in `L=1+ceil(log_2 C_*)`, which constructs a rational parameter `t_0` and an infinite sequence of pairwise distinct admissible primitive petals `U_0,U_1,...`, all different from `T_*`, with

\[
 1+\varepsilon f_* -\varepsilon f_{U_0}\in\mathbb Q^2,
 \qquad
 1+\sigma_i f_{U_i}+\sigma_i f_{U_{i+1}}\in\mathbb Q^2,
 \quad \sigma_i=(-1)^i\varepsilon.                      \tag{2}
\]

The rational sequence is generated by alternating two fixed degree-three maps. Its primitive hypotenuses satisfy

\[
 C_{i+1}=C_i^2R_i,\qquad (C_i,R_i)=1,
 \qquad \frac78 C_i^3<C_{i+1}<\frac98 C_i^3.             \tag{3}
\]

For every `k>=1`, the seed and first `k` new petals yield a square-sum Hamiltonian cycle on `2k+3` distinct positive integer labels, with nested common scales

\[
 H_k=\operatorname{lcm}(C_*,C_{k-1}).                   \tag{4}
\]

Every label has

\[
 \boxed{\ O\!\left(3^k L^3\log^4(L+1)\right)\ }        \tag{5}
\]

bits. Initialization is independent of `k`; generating and writing a finite prefix takes `exp(O(k)) poly(L)` bit operations. For each fixed initialized seed orbit, the maximum label bit length is `Theta(3^k)`. More precisely it is comparable, with absolute constants, to

\[
 \log C_*+3^{k-1}\log C_0.                             \tag{6}
\]

The infinite object is rational petal/contact data. There is no single finite integer scale serving the entire infinite sequence: (3) already forces the required divisors to grow without bound, consistent with the common-center capacity theorem. Each finite prefix has its own scale (4).

Throughout this note **`t=n/m>1`, with `n` the numerator**. For coprime positive `n,m`, put

\[
 \delta=\begin{cases}2&n,m\text{ both odd},\\1&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}
 \quad C(t)=\frac{n^2+m^2}{\delta},\quad
 f(t)=\frac{4t(t^2-1)}{(t^2+1)^2}.                       \tag{7}
\]

The two legs are `(n²−m²)/delta,2nm/delta`, taken unordered. This also handles the both-odd parameter chart without a silent change of Euclidean convention.

## 2. Exact cubic contact maps

Define

\[
 F(t)=-\frac{t(t^2-4t+1)}{2t^3+t^2-2t+1},\qquad
 g_+(t)=\frac1{F(t)},\qquad g_-(t)=F(1/t)
       =-\frac{t^2-4t+1}{t^3-2t^2+t+2}.               \tag{8}
\]

The map `F` comes from the negative odd section `-Q-2P` of the inherited plus contact surface. The proof below needs only the displayed rational identities, not that provenance. Write

\[
\begin{aligned}
 P_+(t)&=t^6+24t^5-13t^4-16t^3+19t^2-8t+1,\\
 P_-(t)&=t^6-8t^5+19t^4-16t^3-13t^2+24t+1.
\end{aligned}
\]

Direct polynomial expansion gives

\[
 1+f(t)+f(g_+(t))=
 \left[\frac{P_+(t)}{(t^2+1)^2(5t^2-4t+1)}\right]^2,
                                                               \tag{9}
\]
\[
 1-f(t)-f(g_-(t))=
 \left[\frac{P_-(t)}{(t^2+1)^2(t^2-4t+5)}\right]^2.     \tag{10}
\]

Thus `g_+` gives `++` contacts and `g_-` gives `--` contacts. Alternating them makes the incoming and outgoing ports opposite at every interior petal. Requiring every individual contact to be mixed-sign had excluded this smaller-degree construction.

## 3. A fixed invariant chart, with no length-dependent cutoff

Set

\[
 I=(1,65/64],\qquad J=(1,17/16].                       \tag{11}
\]

Let `A=t²−4t+1<0` on `J` and `B=t³−2t²+t+2=t(t−1)²+2>0`. Exact identities give

\[
\begin{array}{ll}
 g_+-1=-\dfrac{(t-1)(3t^2-1)}{tA},&
 g_--1=\dfrac{(t-1)(3-t^2)}B,\\[4pt]
 g_+-t=-\dfrac{(t-1)^2(t^2+1)}{tA},&
 g_--t=-\dfrac{(t-1)^2(t^2+1)}B,\\[4pt]
 g_+'=\dfrac{(t^2+1)(9t^2-8t+1)}{t^2A^2},&
 g_-'=\dfrac{(t^2+1)(t^2-8t+9)}{B^2}.
\end{array}                                                    \tag{12}
\]

Consequently both maps are strictly increasing on `J`, and
`1<g_-(t)<t<g_+(t)` there. Also `g_+(1+h)<1+2h` for `0<h<=1/16`: after multiplication by the negative denominator `tA`, this is the negativity of
`2t³−5t²+2t−1=-2−2h+h²+2h³`. Therefore `g_+(I)` lies in `(1,33/32]`, a subset of `J`.

The plus-first two-step map is `H_+=g_- o g_+=F o F`. Its exact displacement is

\[
 H_+(t)-t=
 -\frac{16t^3(t-1)^3(t^2+1)(t^2+2t-1)}{D_9(t)},        \tag{13}
\]

where

\[
 D_9=16t^9-3t^8-132t^7+248t^6-124t^5-6t^4+20t^3-4t+1.
\]

In the variable `h=t−1`, its coefficients are all positive:

\[
 D_9(1+h)=16+96h+224h^2+272h^3+280h^4+440h^5
          +584h^6+420h^7+141h^8+16h^9.                \tag{14}
\]

Hence `1<H_+(t)<t` on `I`; the lower inequality follows from `g_+(t) in J` and (12).

For the minus-first map `H_-=g_+ o g_-`, let `u in (1,t)` be the unique solution of `g_+(u)=t`. It exists since `g_+(1)=1` and `g_+(t)>t`. The plus-first inequality gives `g_-(t)=H_+(u)<u`; applying the increasing `g_+` gives `H_-(t)<t`. Positivity follows from (12). Thus both two-step maps send `I` into itself, strictly downward. Every alternating orbit initialized in `I` stays in `J` forever. Every finite prefix map, as a function of its initial value in `I`, is strictly increasing.

Finally `f` is strictly increasing on `J` and `f(17/16)=35904/297025<1/2`, so every orbit petal is admissible. No estimate here depends on the number of iterations.

## 4. Primitive normalization and exact Gaussian factor allocation

For `t=n/m`, homogenize `F=N/D` as

\[
 N=-n(n^2-4nm+m^2),\quad
 D=2n^3+n^2m-2nm^2+m^3.
\]

The exact norm identity is

\[
 N^2+D^2=(n^2+m^2)^2(5n^2-4nm+m^2).                   \tag{15}
\]

The numerator/denominator resultant in the affine variable is `-2^6`, so coprime input integers have no odd common output prime. For opposite-parity inputs the output pair has opposite parity. For both-odd inputs, `v_2(N)=v_2(D)=1`, and after dividing by two both coordinates remain odd. Therefore `gcd(N,D)=delta`, and the output parity divisor is again `delta`. The same statements hold with `n,m` interchanged, and reciprocation only swaps the coordinates. Thus both maps satisfy the **exact primitive** formulas

\[
 C(g_+(t))=C(t)^2\frac{5n^2-4nm+m^2}{\delta},\qquad
 C(g_-(t))=C(t)^2\frac{n^2-4nm+5m^2}{\delta}.            \tag{16}
\]

For any odd prime `p|n²+m²`, both `n,m` are units modulo `p`. The first new quadratic differs from `n²+m²` by `4n(n−m)`, the second by `4m(m−n)`. Neither vanishes modulo `p`: `n=m` would imply `p=2`. Primitive hypotenuses are odd, so

\[
 (C(t),R_+(t))=(C(t),R_-(t))=1.                        \tag{17}
\]

Every old hypotenuse-prime valuation is doubled exactly; all new factors are coprime to the old hypotenuse. In particular hypotenuses strictly increase, so all orbit petals are distinct without a delicate comparison of alternating real subsequences.

There is also a direct Gaussian explanation:

\[
 N+iD=(n-im)^2[-n+i(2n-m)]
      =i\,\overline{(n+im)}^{\,2}[(2n-m)+in].           \tag{18}
\]

The `++` new factor is the primitive norm of the Euclidean pair `(2n−m,n)`, the projective map `L(t)=2−1/t`. The `--` new factor is the primitive norm of `(m,2m−n)`, the inverse projective map `L^{-1}(t)=1/(2−t)`, valid in our chart `t<2`. These pairs remain coprime and retain the same parity divisor. This is an explicit connection to the outer Euclidean/Berggren parameter transformations in the inherited ternary-tree analysis. In the both-odd chart, putting the odd leg first conjugates this presentation to the other outer branch, so a fixed named branch should not be asserted without specifying that convention. Equation (18), rather than a dynamical analogy alone, gives the precise arithmetic connection.

On `J`, (16) gives

\[
 \frac{C(g_+(t))}{C(t)^3}=\frac{5t^2-4t+1}{t^2+1},\qquad
 \frac{C(g_-(t))}{C(t)^3}=\frac{t^2-4t+5}{t^2+1}.        \tag{19}
\]

The first ratio increases from `1` to `613/545<9/8`; the second decreases from `1` to `481/545>7/8`. This proves (3), including `C_{i+1}>C_i²` because `C_i>=5`.

Put `b_i=log C_i`. Equation (3) implies

\[
 |b_i-3^ib_0|\le\tfrac12(3^i-1)\log(8/7).             \tag{20}
\]

Since `b_0>=log5`, this proves `b_i=Theta(3^i b_0)` with absolute constants. The growth statement is about the actual primitive output, not just the unreduced degree-three formulas.

## 5. One polynomial-height initialization avoids the seed forever

Use Lemma 2 of [the boundary-start recurrence note](square_sum_growing_chain_bit_bound_20261019.md). It constructs `r` distinct roots with

\[
 0<f(t_0)<\eta,\qquad
 1+\varepsilon f_*-\varepsilon f(t_0)\in\mathbb Q^2,
\]

using the rational zero-spread boundary point on the seed's fixed elliptic curve. Its signed even-translation budget is `Q=O(r² L/eta)` and its output height is `O(Q²[L+log(Q+1)])` bits. The lemma is uniform in the prescribed seed and port. On the low chart `f(1+h)>=h` for `0<h<=1/3`; hence taking `eta=1/64` puts every candidate root in `I`.

Let `j_*` be the least nonnegative integer such that

\[
 5^{2^{j_*}}>C_* .                                    \tag{21}
\]

Then `j_*=O(log(L+1))`. Take `r=j_*+1` candidate roots. For every orbit and every `i>=j_*`, repeated `C_{i+1}>C_i²` gives `C_i>=5^{2^i}>C_*`; equality with the seed is impossible. For each of the finitely many indices `0<=i<j_*`, the corresponding parameter prefix map is strictly increasing on `I`, and so is its spread. Hence at most one root candidate can give the seed's spread at that index. At most `j_*` roots are excluded in total; at least one of the `j_*+1` candidates avoids the seed **at every future index**.

This choice is effective: test the first `j_*` petals of every candidate and retain a survivor. Those tests use degree at most `3^{j_*}=poly(L)` and polynomially many bits. With fixed `eta`,

\[
 Q=O(L\log^2(L+1)),\qquad
 \operatorname{bits}(t_0)=O(L^3\log^4(L+1)).           \tag{22}
\]

The entire initialization is polynomial in `L` and independent of the later requested prefix length. Combining (20), (22), and the nested divisor identity (4) proves (5) and (6).

For the cycle itself, list the seed's two outer labels from `-epsilon` to `epsilon`. List `U_i` from its incoming port to its opposite outgoing port; (2) guarantees opposite incident ports at all interior petals. Prepend the center `M` and close the last outer label back to `M`. Internal petal edges, contacts, and endpoint-to-center edges are all squares by (1), (2), and the inherited triangle identities. Distinct petals have distinct positive spreads; the minus labels lie below `M`, the plus labels above `M`, so all `2k+3` vertices are distinct. Their maximum is between `2H_k²` and `4H_k²`, establishing the bit comparisons.

## 6. Slow real convergence alongside rapid arithmetic growth

Both two-step maps have the local expansion

\[
 H_\pm(1+h)=1+h-4h^3+O(h^4).                          \tag{23}
\]

The even subsequence decreases to `1`: otherwise its positive limit would be a fixed point in `I`, contradicting the strict inequalities of Section 3. If `x_j=t_{2j}−1`, then

\[
 \frac1{x_{j+1}^2}-\frac1{x_j^2}=8+O(x_j)\longrightarrow8.
\]

Averaging these increments proves

\[
 t_{2j}-1\sim\frac1{\sqrt{8j}}.                        \tag{24}
\]

Since `g_\pm(1+h)=1+h+O(h²)`, odd and even terms have the same first-order asymptotic. In particular

\[
 (t_i-1)\sqrt i\longrightarrow\tfrac12,
 \qquad f(t_i)\sqrt i\longrightarrow1.                 \tag{25}
\]

Meanwhile the exact primitive bit lengths grow as `Theta(3^i)` for the fixed orbit. Real cusp convergence therefore supplies no arithmetic compression by itself. In fact (19) tends to one, so the positive limit `lambda=lim 3^{-i}log C_i` exists and `log C_i=lambda 3^i+o(1)`.
