# No disjoint degree-two contact map with opposite real-zero allocation

**Status: PROVED by exact rational polynomial elimination; independently checkable ideal-membership certificate supplied.** This addresses one complete disjoint-pole case, to be combined with the adjacent allocations and shared/repeated-pole exclusions.

## 1. Scope and normalization

Write `D=t²+1`, `T=t(t²−1)`, and `f=4T/D²`. Suppose a rational degree-two map `h` satisfies

\[
y^2=1+\varepsilon f(t)+\eta f(h(t)),\qquad y\in\mathbb Q(t).
\]

Replacing `h` by `eta h` reduces the output sign to `eta=+1`, since `k_eta(h)=k_+(eta h)`. Changing `t` to `−t` if needed reduces to `epsilon=+1`; it preserves the opposite pair `{0,infinity}` of real zeros of `f`. Put `z=k_eta(h)`, so `y²−z²=f`. We assume here that the poles of `z` consist of two distinct Gaussian-rational conjugate pairs, disjoint from `±i`, and that the real zeros allocated to one of `y−z,y+z` are `{0,infinity}`. The complementary pair is `{−1,1}`.

The divisor classification gives, after possibly swapping the two factors and negating `z`, monic quadratics `qA,qB` and a nonzero rational `m` such that

\[
r=y-z=\frac{m t q_A}{Dq_B},\qquad
s=y+z=\frac{4(t^2-1)q_B}{mDq_A}.                     \tag{1}
\]

Here each quadratic is the norm of one Gaussian linear factor. Choose signed rational `b,d` to match the roots of the same factor `P+iQ` (or its conjugate) of `h=P/Q`, and write

\[
q_A=t^2-2at+q,\quad q=a^2+b^2,\qquad
q_B=t^2-2ct+\rho,\quad\rho=c^2+d^2.
\]

Thus, with

\[
\begin{aligned}
L&=(t-a-bi)(t-c-di)=X+iY,\\
X&=t^2-(a+c)t+ac-bd,\\
Y&=-(b+d)t+ad+bc,
\end{aligned}                                      \tag{2}
\]

we have `X²+Y²=qA qB`. Every such composition satisfies

\[
z=\frac{U(X^2-Y^2)-2VXY}{q_Aq_B},\qquad U^2+V^2=2,
\quad U,V\in\mathbb Q.                              \tag{3}
\]

This follows from the Gaussian quadratic identity explained in the shared-pole proof. Since (1) gives `z(infinity)=2/m`, it forces `U=2/m`. Set `v=−mV`; then

\[
v^2-2m^2+4=0,\qquad
z=\frac{2}{m}\frac{X^2-Y^2+vXY}{q_Aq_B}.              \tag{4}
\]

## 2. Cancellation at the input poles removes two parameters

From (1), the numerator before removing `D` is

\[
N=4(t^2-1)q_B^2-m^2tq_A^2.
\]

The function `z` is regular at `i`, so `N(i)=0`. Since `qA(i)!=0`, this gives

\[
\left(\frac{q_B(i)}{q_A(i)}\right)^2=-\frac{im^2}{8},
\qquad
\frac{q_B(i)}{q_A(i)}=\pm\frac{m(1-i)}4.
\]

Simultaneously negating `r,s,y,z` changes `m` to `−m` and leaves `v` unchanged, so the plus choice covers both signs. Comparing real and imaginary parts yields the exact formulas

\[
c=\frac{m(q-1+2a)}8,\qquad
\rho=1+\frac{m(q-1-2a)}4.                            \tag{5}
\]

No parameter denominator was introduced here other than the fixed nonzero integers `4,8`. The use of `qA(i)!=0` is justified by the disjoint-pole hypothesis.

For these expressions, `N` is identically divisible by `D`; write `J=N/D`. The leading term of `J` is `4t⁴`. Equations (1), (3), and (4) therefore imply that the polynomial

\[
E=J-4(X^2-Y^2+vXY)                                   \tag{6}
\]

has all four coefficients zero (its degree is at most three), together with

\[
64(d^2-\rho+c^2)=0,\qquad v^2-2m^2+4=0.              \tag{7}
\]

In (5)–(7), substitute `q=a²+b²`. Multiplying the four coefficients of (6) by `16` clears all denominators. This is an explicit system of six polynomials in `Q[a,b,d,v,m]`.

## 3. Exact certificate

For the six equations above, the reduced grevlex basis, up to nonzero rational scalars, in variable order `(a,b,d,v,m)` is

\[
\begin{aligned}
8d^2+m,&\qquad 4dv+3m+12,\\
v^2+36,&\qquad 3dm-12d+2v,\\
-48d+mv+4v,&\qquad m^2+16,\\
8a+m,&\qquad 12b+12d-v.
\end{aligned}                                      \tag{8}
\]

In particular `m²+16` belongs to the ideal generated by (6)–(7), which has no real solutions and hence no rational solutions. This excludes the entire stated allocation.

This is an exact algebraic exclusion, not a bounded search. The model used for the certificate is deliberately broader than the original maps: no inequalities, `m!=0`, `b*d!=0`, distinctness, or resultant constraints are imposed during elimination. Thus there are no unsaturated or degenerate branches omitted by (8).
