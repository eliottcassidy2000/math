# Migrating a majority witness into the upper slaves

Date: 2026-10-19. Status: exact boundary-cone proof for a specified rooted family, with a short sharp exceptional inequality. No claim of resolving all seven-thin posets or of priority over existing local-configuration methods.

## 1. The structural result

Let (x,y) be an ordered critical incomparable pair in a finite poset P, with exactly one lower slave b, exactly two upper slaves a1,a2, and c common incomparable neighbors Z. Thus

    b<y, b∥x;       x<a1,a2, y∥a1,a2;
    Z=I(x)∩I(y);
    D(x)⊆D(y), U(y)⊆U(x).

The exterior, the internal common-neighbor order, and the order between the two upper slaves are otherwise unrestricted.

**Theorem 1.** If c≤3, this pair cannot be a maximum-index-span incomparable pair in the >2/3 majority order of a poset having no balanced pair. The order-dual conclusion also holds.

The proof supplies positive weighted sums of actual ambient comparison probabilities. These include comparisons with upper-slave endpoints; the root/common comparisons alone are insufficient. In seven-thin notation this excludes the selected-root branch with slave multiplicities (1,2), or (2,1), and c=0,1,2,3. The next unhandled common count in this morphology is c=4, where the root degrees are6 and7.

The finite core calculation has no exterior-size or exterior-degree cutoff. It is not an enumeration of all posets of bounded order: the reductions retain the exact root morphology and eliminate arbitrary exterior analytically.

## 2. Why the old root-only aggregate cannot suffice

For the disjoint union of a chain x<a1<a2, a chain b<y, and any c-point common poset, uniform shuffles give

    Σ[z∈Z] [p(x<z)+p(z<y)] = 3c/4+2c/3=17c/12.

This exceeds4c/3 when c>0. Consequently the all-size mixed-one-lower/one-upper aggregate cannot simply be carried over. A certificate must use other endpoints or different information.

The following sharp lemma exhibits that migration explicitly.

## 3. A sharp six-point inequality

Let the roots' incomparability union consist of Q={x,y,b,z,a1,a2}, with relations generated by

    b<y,       b<z<a1,       b<a2,
    x<a1,      x<a2.                               (1)

No further core relation is imposed. Every exterior relation must preserve the stated root neighborhoods.

**Theorem 2.** For every such actual exterior,

    3p(x<z)+4p(z<y)+p(y<a2)+p(a2<a1) ≤ 6.          (2)

The four positive weights total9, and all four pairs are incomparable. Thus the candidate majority order

    b, x, z, y, a2, a1                              (3)

cannot have probability>2/3 on every one of its forward comparisons. This is the exceptional pattern requiring more than three unit-weight terms in the finite proof of Theorem1.

The bare core has37 extensions. The four comparison numerators in (2) are27,24,22,23. Hence its weighted numerator is222=6·37: the inequality is sharp.

### An explicit exterior certificate

Write A for the exterior below both roots and B for the exterior above both roots. For a fixed core word o, its fibre has size

    e(A)e(B)L(pre(o))R(suf(o)),                     (4)

where pre precedes the first root, suf follows the last root, and the two features are normalized interleaving counts. Their empty values are1.

Define the prefix increments U_u=L(u)−L(u with its last letter removed), and suffix increments V_v=R(v)−R(v with its first letter removed). Appending or prepending the removed letter proves U_u,V_v≥0. Let both empty increments be1.

For (1), the prefix-tree words are

    empty, b, bz,

and the suffix-tree words, in order, are

    empty, a1, a2, za1, a1a2, a2a1,
    za1a2, za2a1, a2za1.

The coefficient matrix of 6E0 minus the weighted numerator in (2), where E0=e(P)/(e(A)e(B)), is

    [ 0, 1, 6, 7, 9, 1, 6, 3, 3 ]
    [10, 4, 8, 4, 9, 3, 4, 2, 2 ]
    [ 7, 1, 3, 0, 2, 0, 0, 0, 0 ].                (5)

Each entry is obtained by summing the corresponding wordwise deficit over core extensions beginning with the chosen prefix and ending with the chosen suffix. All entries are nonnegative. Multiplying them by U_uV_v and summing proves (2) for arbitrary actual exterior. No independence or arbitrary realizability of the increments is assumed.

The separate independent verifier derives (5), cross-checks the37 core words by two enumeration methods, and checks the complete fibre evaluation against whole-poset subset DP on200 actual exteriors through order12.

## 4. Complete allowed core directions

For the general mixed(1,2) core, the only optional relations are

    b<z                  for z∈Z,
    b<aj                 for j=1,2,
    z<aj                 for z∈Z, j=1,2,
    relations inside Z,
    a relation between a1,a2.                       (6)

For example z<b would imply z<y, aj<z would imply x<z, and aj<b would imply x<y. These violate the prescribed neighborhoods. The other reverse directions are ruled out similarly.

Choose independently a natural labeling of the common poset and a natural labeling of the upper-slave poset. This is only a choice of labels; it adds no relations. Therefore increasing-index internal edges suffice for exhaustive generation. Enumerate all subsets of (6), add the mandatory root edges, close transitively, and deduplicate. This covers every required core up to the two independent relabelings. Closure accounts for every forced b-to-upper relation.

For c=0,1,2,3 this gives respectively7,40,357,4824 cores.

## 5. Projected majority orders and exact generator columns

If no balanced pair exists, the >2/3 comparison tournament is transitive: three cyclic comparison indicators cannot sum to more than2 in any extension. Let M be its total order, which extends the poset. An incomparable pair of maximum index span is critical.

Every point outside Q is below both roots or above both roots. In particular, no exterior point lies between x and y in M. Projecting M to Q preserves the root span exactly and can only shorten every other core-pair span. Hence the projected order must satisfy

    x before y,
    span_M(u,v)≤span_M(x,y) for every u∥v in Q.     (7)

We enumerate only legal core words satisfying (7). They may include orders that no actual exterior can realize as its majority order; that enlargement is safe.

The fibre factorization (4) remains valid for all the cores in (6). Expanding it into nonnegative tree increments gives one generator column for each compatible initial word u and terminal word v. Its E-coordinate counts core extensions beginning with u and ending with v. Its coordinate N_{ab} also requires a before b. Incompatible endpoint choices contribute zero and may be omitted.

For a proposed order M, orient every incomparable core pair forward. If positive integer weights w_i on some of those pairs satisfy

    Σ_i w_i [3N_i(u,v)−2E(u,v)]≤0                  (8)

for every generator, then the actual ambient probabilities satisfy

    Σ_i w_i p_i ≤ (2/3) Σ_i w_i.                  (9)

Every p_i would exceed2/3 in a no-balanced majority order M, contradicting (9). This proves that this projected order is impossible. The proof uses only the nonnegative actual increment products U_uV_v; an arbitrary-cone relaxation is used in a sufficient direction.

The standalone verifier uses integer arithmetic throughout. For each allowed core it recursively generates its extensions, constructs all compatible prefix/suffix columns, and checks every projected order in (7). It first tries one, two, or three unit-weight comparison rows. The only two cases not handled that way are the equivalent labelings of (1)–(3), for which it inserts the verified weights3,4,1,1.

The core-generation and lifting proofs, together with this exhaustive integer certificate for the indicated finite root family, prove Theorem1.

## 7. Relation to earlier mechanisms and limitations

The single/pair/triple rows in the table overlap the general idea of balanced-pair and balanced-triple local screens. This packet does not claim to have recovered or extended Peczarski's original complete six-thin configuration table. The primary reference for those methods remains Peczarski, *Comments on the Golden Partition Conjecture* (2017):

https://cdm.ucalgary.ca/article/download/62349/46785/178291

The contribution established here is the precise arbitrary-exterior tree-cone certificate for the stated morphology and its explicit sharp slave-endpoint inequality(2). A literature priority claim would need a separate comparison with the original configuration lists.

Theorem1 does not cover c≥4, mixed(2,2), or larger slave multiplicities. The seven-thin problem remains open in this work. The c=4 mixed(1,2) case is now an especially concrete next target: root degrees6 and7, four common neighbors, one lower slave, and two upper slaves, with actual transitivity and degree budgets retained.

For completeness, “balanced” means a comparison probability in the closed interval [1/3,2/3]. The assumed absence of such a pair therefore gives a strict >2/3 direction for each unordered pair. The root orientation used in (7) is necessary: if y preceded x in the majority order, then b<y would put b before y before x, and b∥x would have a strictly larger span than the root pair. Equivalently, the critical-pair swapping injection gives p(x<y)≥1/2, hence p(x<y)>2/3 under the no-balanced assumption.

The exterior increment monotonicity uses a structural fact: no core element can lie below an element of A unless it already lies below both roots, and no element of B can lie below a core element unless that core element already lies above both roots. For letters that can occur in the pre-root prefix or post-root suffix, these reverse comparisons are impossible. Appending the removed prefix letter after all A elements, or prepending the removed suffix letter before all B elements, therefore gives the required count-preserving injection.
