# Grounded child ports from an exact terminal phase

**PROVED:** the constructed ROOT families, simultaneous alternative ports, exact source-membership test, and inverse-word closure below. **FINITE-EXACT:** the declared literal and symbolic checks. **OPEN:** grounding the original billion-bit Mersenne sources in the parameter chart, including the new paid child at parameter 23. This factory does not replace a requested source by a convenient constructed member.

The useful change is from a paid implication with an unknown child suffix to an explicit terminal valuation that proves both sides reach ROOT. This constructs genuinely grounded members of the ordinary native source families. It does not prove that the fixed Mersenne member belongs to them.

## 1. Inheritance and the missing terminal

The current [parameter-complement synthesis](collatz_parameter_complement_synthesis_20261007e.md) distinguishes paid guard coverage from child grounding. The closest constructive mechanisms are [finite-seed receipts](finite_seed_receipt_compiler_20261005.md), [grounded child closure](collatz_grounded_child_closure_20261007.md), and [marked completion](collatz_marked_completion_20261007c.md). The inverse ROOT grammar is inherited; this note specializes it to the newly emitted signed child ports, preserves their alternatives, and makes fixed-source membership an explicit separate test.

The canonical hostile is an inverse child whose actual return word reaches the original ungrounded source. Replaying that return supplies no ROOT suffix. If \(F_w(h)=n>1\), every first-hit ROOT proof at \(h\) must begin with that actual prefix \(w\) and then contain a ROOT proof at \(n\). A size decrease can support well-founded induction; it cannot manufacture the missing suffix.

The anchor is actual grounding, the niche is an exact terminal exponent phase, and the wildcard is sharing one authenticated terminal across several child ports. The retained coordinates are original source, cofactor, ordered carry, terminal exponent, alternative child label, and ROOT status.

## 2. Constructing an explicit ROOT source

Write \(F_a(x)=(3x+1)/2^a\). Let \(u\) be empty or begin with a valuation at least 2, and put

\[
\ell=|u|,\quad A=\sum u,\quad P_u=3^\ell,\quad Q_u=2^A,
\quad F_u(x)=\frac{P_ux+B_u}{Q_u},
\]

\[
I_u=3B_u-3P_u+Q_u.
\]

Then \(I_u\equiv2\pmod4\) and \(I_u\equiv2^A\pmod3\). The first claim follows from \(I_\varnothing=-2\) and the recurrence \(I_{wa}=3I_w+2^{A_w+a}\); the first letter at least 2 and every later cumulative cost preserve valuation 1 at 2.

Fix a run \(e\ge0\), set \(L=e+\ell+1\), and choose an even integer \(c\ge4\) satisfying

\[
\boxed{2^{A+c}\equiv I_u\pmod{3^L}.}                 \tag{1}
\]

There is exactly one such exponent class modulo \(2\cdot3^{L-1}\). Indeed 2 generates the units modulo \(3^L\), and \(I_u2^{-A}\equiv1\pmod3\), so the terminal phase is even. Define

\[
q(c)=\frac{2^{A+c}-I_u}{2\cdot3^L},\qquad
N(c)=2^{e+1}q(c)-1.                                  \tag{2}
\]

**Grounding theorem.** Every sufficiently large member of the phase in (1) has positive odd \(q(c)\), and \(N(c)\) has the explicit first-hit ROOT word

\[
\boxed{1^e\,u\,c.}                                  \tag{3}
\]

In fact positivity is automatic on the exact native phase with \(c\ge4\). To keep a direct independent arithmetic boundary, the implementation checks \(2^{A+c}>I_u\), and its factory chooses the stronger sufficient cut \(A+c\ge\operatorname{bitlength}(|I_u|)+2\). This removes at most finitely many terminal exponents and is not an empirical coverage hypothesis.

To prove (3), put \(R=2^c\). Direct composition gives

\[
F_{1^e u}(N(c))=\frac{R-1}{3}.
\]

The phase ensures integral source and backward intermediate states. A nonintegral denominator 3 cannot disappear under further inverse divisions by 3, because every power of2 is a unit modulo 3. Starting at the positive odd integer \((R-1)/3>1\), each integral inverse step \((2^a y-1)/3\) is again positive and odd. Thus the whole actual prefix is legal. An earlier ROOT state could not later leave 1 to reach the displayed endpoint greater than 1. The final valuation \(c\) is exact and reaches 1. Finally \(v_2(I_u)=1\) proves \(q(c)\) odd, so \(v_2(N(c)+1)=e+1\): the prescribed initial run is retained.

This proof checks a family algebraically; it does not consult a ROOT orbit oracle.

## 3. One terminal grounds several alternative children

An authenticated signed port has heads \(u,v\), deletion \(D>0\), and integer gap \(g\), with

\[
|v|-|u|=D,\qquad A_u-A_v=2g,\qquad I_v=I_u.
\]

These retain the full ordered carrier identity, including the sibling direction. Suppose \(e\ge D\) and \(c+2g\ge4\). The same cofactor in (2) gives

\[
H_D(c)=2^{e+1-D}q(c)-1<N(c).
\]

Its explicit first-hit ROOT word is

\[
\boxed{1^{e-D}\,v\,(c+2g).}                          \tag{4}
\]

The partner prefix ends at \((2^{c+2g}-1)/3\). The same integrality, positivity, and first-hit argument applies. Both words have the same odd length; their valuation costs differ by \(D\). Every compatible alternative port on the same source head consumes the **same** \(c\) and \(q(c)\), rather than choosing an unrelated member independently for each child.

The smallest illustrative control uses the inherited reset port \(u=\varnothing,v=(2),D=1,g=-1\). With \(e=2,c=10\), formula (2) gives \(q=19\), and

\[
151\xrightarrow{(1,1,10)}1,
\qquad75\xrightarrow{(1,2,8)}1.
\]

Both words are supplied by the formula and independently replayed. The positive-gap port \(u=(2,6),v=(4,1,1),D=1,g=1\) is a second literal control.

For the new coarse parameter 1 rule, the ordinary native source head is the inherited 15-letter head followed by 6, of cost 38. Its two partner choices have deletions 3 and 4 and the same gap 1. Fixing \(e=76\) gives one exact completion family grounding both ports simultaneously. The first computed terminal exponent has 145 bits. The constructed source has between

\[
26703240540826344508365944166701290990894838
\quad\text{and}\quad
26703240540826344508365944166701290990894842
\]

binary digits. It is never expanded. Its short structured record stores the head, run, and exact terminal exponent; the checker verifies both coefficients of the affine identity in the formal variable \(R=2^c\).

This is the **native head** of the parameter 1 rule, instantiated at a constructed ordinary source. It is not the original \(2^{924745897+2^{32}}-1\).

The first-hit boundary matters. If a partner terminal were 2, its preceding endpoint would already be 1; the displayed word could contain ROOT padding. The present port API requires \(c+2g\ge4\) and explicitly rejects that boundary. For example the seed source 3 would have child 1 under the reset port and must be represented by an empty child proof, not by `(2,2)`. The factory retains this rejection rather than exporting a padded word.

## 4. Exact source membership is a separate obligation

For a supplied positive odd source \(n\), `recognizes_source` checks its actual identity with (2), using an exact power-of-two test. It never searches for a nearby source.

In particular the fixed Mersenne \(M_{e+1}=2^{e+1}-1\) requires \(q(c)=1\), or

\[
\boxed{2^{A+c}=I_u+2\cdot3^{e+\ell+1}.}              \tag{5}
\]

The congruence (1) alone does not prove (5). `is_mersenne_member` tests (5) without expanding the constructed source. `try_mersenne_terminal` checks this exact one-terminal equation within its finite precision domain, and returns a proof only for that fixed source. It succeeds for head \((2,3)\), run 2, producing the actual word \((1,1,2,3,4)\) at 7. The nearby completion 151 has the same run 2 but fails the cofactor-one test for 7.

The companion [fixed-head finiteness theorem](collatz_terminal_head_finiteness_20261007f.md) applies a cited S-unit bound to (5): a fixed middle head can represent only finitely many Mersenne exponents in this terminal form. The obstruction concerns a finite fixed-head library, not variable heads, recursive receipt composition, or the infinite ordinary-source families in (2).

For the actual [new parameter 23 head](collatz_residual_t23_20261007f.md), \(\ell=3317,A=6552,e=99708993704\), so the full congruence has ternary depth \(99708997022\). The bounded factory refuses that full computation. `terminal_equation` records its exact unexpanded equation, and `terminal_phase_prefix` computes any allowed finite compatible prefix of its unique terminal phase. Such prefixes remain an **OPEN** obligation, not a ROOT certificate.

An independent modular calculation of the actual source shows that its next valuation after that head is 1. It fails the ROOT terminal phase already modulo 3: \(2^{A+1}\equiv2\), while \(I_u\equiv1\). Thus this particular frontier is not itself a direct ROOT exit. This does not rule out its later ROOT suffix; the newly emitted child \(M_{99708993677}\) remains ungrounded here.

## 5. Grounded inverse closure without changing a supplied index

Choose a base terminal \(c_0\) and write

\[
c_j=c_0+2\cdot3^{L-1}j,\qquad N_j=N(c_j),\quad j\ge0.
\]

For distinct indices,

\[
\boxed{v_3(N_i-N_j)=v_3(i-j).}                       \tag{6}
\]

Factoring their difference leaves \(4^{3^{L-1}(i-j)}-1\), whose valuation is \(L+v_3(i-j)\); division by \(3^L\) gives (6). This is the inherited ternary-isometry closure mechanism applied to a newly constructed, explicitly grounded terminal family.

For any positive return word \(w\) with carrier \((p,q,b)\) and \(q<p\), exactly one class of \(j\pmod{3^{|w|}}\) makes

\[
Z_j=(qN_j-b)/p
\]

integral. It is positive, odd, and smaller than \(N_j\); its first-hit proof is \(w\) followed by (3). `inverse_phase` computes the family phase. By contrast `inverse_root_word` consumes a supplied index and returns `None` if it fails the guard. It never substitutes the successful phase representative for that index.

All residue readers compute a numerator modulo the **full denominator times the requested modulus**, then divide. They remain exact when the requested modulus contains 2 or 3. The stored terminal may represent an enormous binary exponent, but modular powers use that finite exponent as an integer and never expand the source.

These closure statements describe a proper constructive basin. They do not imply that every selector obligation lies in it, that a ternary limit is a positive integer source, or that every Mersenne passes (5).
