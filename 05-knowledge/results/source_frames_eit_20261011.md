## 2. Any Keller pair gives the same three-test criterion

Let A=K[p,y], char K=0, D=p^3-y^2, u=y^2/D and

\[
 \{p,y\}=-D/p,\qquad G_H=-u/2+H.
\]

Choose H_1,H_2 in A with constant nonzero Jacobian

\[
 J=H_{1,p}H_{2,y}-H_{1,y}H_{2,p}\in K^\times.
\]

**Proposition F1.** For any rational S in K(p,y), the following are
equivalent:

1. All three responses {G_0,S},{G_(H_1),S},{G_(H_2),S} belong to A.
2. Every response {G_H,S}, H in A, belongs to A.
3. S belongs to K+p^2 D A.

**Proof.** Put delta={-,S}. Subtracting the baseline response from the
other two yields delta H_1,delta H_2 in A. The derivation identity gives

\[
 \begin{pmatrix}\delta H_1\\\delta H_2\end{pmatrix}
 =\begin{pmatrix}H_{1,p}&H_{1,y}\\H_{2,p}&H_{2,y}\end{pmatrix}
   \begin{pmatrix}\delta p\\\delta y\end{pmatrix}.
\]

The matrix inverse is its polynomial adjugate divided by the unit J.
Hence both coordinate responses are polynomial. The inherited squarefree
pole lemma P1 forces S polynomial; the inherited P2 classification then
gives (3). Conversely (3) preserves every source by P2. QED.

No inverse polynomial map for (H_1,H_2) has been assumed or constructed.
Thus F1 is valid for a hypothetical noninvertible Keller pair as well;
it does not smuggle in the Jacobian conjecture. It extends the choice of
test functions, not the already settled Hamiltonian carrier.

**Localization consequence.** If J is any nonzero polynomial and the
three new responses are polynomial, every denominator prime of S must
divide J. Indeed the same adjugate calculation makes the coordinate
responses regular away from J. At a denominator prime h not dividing J,
the P1 pole argument still applies in the local ring. Differentiation
increases a genuine pole order by one, while the squarefree numerator
D cancels at most one order. This is a pole-support bound, not a converse.

Two exact controls explain the failure boundary.

* H_1=p^3,H_2=y^2 have J=6p^2y. S=D passes the three new tests:
  {G_0,D}=-3py and {p^3,D}={y^2,D}=6pyD. It fails the original p test,
  since {p,D}=2yD/p. Algebraic independence has hidden the p-divisor.
* H_1=D,H_2=pD have J=2yD. The genuinely rational S=u passes:
  {G_0,u}=0, {D,u}=-6py, {pD,u}=-8p^2y. Its original coordinate responses
  retain D poles. This is exactly the divisor the observer ramifies over.

The second control has the previously recorded rational flow
x -> x/(1-lambda x), t -> t(1-lambda x)^2. It supplies a real surviving
construction after changing the tests, with the lost original source
obligation explicitly retained.

## 3. What transfers from p^2qr: the exponent box, not the embedded curve

For N with distinct prime factors and exponent tuple (a_i), its divisor
lattice is the product of chains [0,a_i]. Divisibility, the squarefree
Boolean sublattice, and prime atoms depend only on that labelled tuple.
The same construction works in the UFD K[p,y], for pairwise nonassociate
irreducible factors; nonzero scalar units are ignored.

Thus the map from numerical factors P,Q,R to the polynomial factors
p,y,D sends P^2QR to p^2yD and preserves exactly the predicates used by
F=S+U. Here F counts proper nontrivial divisors, S the squarefree members
of that set, and U its prime members. The inherited classification says
F=S+U precisely for N=P, P^3, P^2QR; its k-free strengthening remains the
stronger arithmetic result.

For beta different from 0 and 1, put E_beta=p^3-beta y^2. The two
polynomials p^2yD and p^2D E_beta have the same exponent profile (2,1,1),
so both have (F,S,U)=(10,7,3). But their generic-fibre genera are 6 and
11, respectively. The divisor lattice has forgotten the weighted
embedding and branch locations.

The inherited Kummer formula makes the lost coordinate explicit. For
I=p^a y^b product_i(p^3-beta_i y^2)^{c_i}, with distinct nonzero beta_i,
strip the common gcd of a,b,c_i, set C=sum c_i, w=2a+3b+6C, k=a+b+3C,
and use v=y/p, z=y^2/p^3. The connected generic cover is

\[
 v^w=I\,z^k\prod_i(1-\beta_i z)^{-c_i},\qquad
 2g-2=s w-\gcd(w,k)-\gcd(w,k-C)-\sum_i\gcd(w,c_i).     \tag{10}
\]

This formula was proved in
[the triangle-carrier note, mixed weighted factors](planar_triangle_carrier_20261011.md).
It is being specialized, not reproved as a new nonrationality theorem.

## 4. Genus 15 has a genuine two-boundary degeneration to genus 5

For I_1=p^2D and I_2=p^2yD the leading commutator is

\[
 \{I_1,I_2\}=-p^2D^2(5p^3-2y^2).
\]

Its family, up to nonzero scalar, is I_beta=p^2D^2 E_beta. For beta not
0 or 1, the Kummer degree is 22 and the orders at the four branch
locations are

\[
 (11,-2,-1,-8),\qquad
 \text{numbers of permutation cycles }(11,2,1,2).
\]

Riemann--Hurwitz therefore gives

\[
 2g-2=-44+[(22-11)+(22-2)+(22-1)+(22-2)]=28,
 \qquad g=15.
\]

At beta=1 two cusp factors collide, giving p^2D^3. Its three orders
are (11,-3,-8), with cycle counts (11,1,2), hence genus5.
At beta=0 the extra factor becomes p^3, giving p^5D^2. The orders
(11,-2,-9), with cycle counts (11,2,1), again give genus5.
All three covers are connected, so no component normalization is hidden.
These are geometric genera of generic level curves at the specified
parameter, not a claim that every special level is smooth.

This is a structural explanation of a 15-to-5 relation: the degree stays
22, and collision changes the ramification deficit by20, lowering genus
by10. The arithmetic identities 15=T_5=4^2-1=3*5 also hold, whereas
3*5+1=16. There is a second exact 16 attached to this same commutator:
its factor profile (2,2,1) has 3*3*2-2=16 proper nontrivial divisors.
It does not follow that genus is always F-1:

| Carrier | Exponent profile | F | Generic genus |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| p^2 y^2 D | (2,2,1) | 16 | 7 |
| p^2 y D^2 | (2,2,1) | 16 | 9 |
| p^2 D^2 E_beta | (2,2,1) | 16 | 15 |

The incoming [boundary-palette theorem](boundary_palette_compression_20261011.md)
has another 15: S(6,5)=choose(6,2), the choice of a merged pair of named
ports. This equals T_5 by an explicit pair-counting bijection. No map
from those port pairs to this curve's holomorphic differentials has been
constructed. The general single-flow obstruction is already stronger
than these genus calculations; full flow commutators also contain higher
terms and are not excluded merely by their leading bracket.

## 5. The EIT source and the triangle/circle distinction

We inspected the `eit` README and TeX at octonion/mathematics commit
`5faca206b8cff76188154ae726fc8a24582f9d89` (2026-10-07).
Long's [Algebraic Exponential Integrals, Theorems1.2--1.4](https://github.com/octonion/mathematics/blob/5faca206b8cff76188154ae726fc8a24582f9d89/eit/algebraic_exponential_integrals_simplified.tex)
asserts algebraic-value rigidity for a nonconstant algebraic polynomial
phase integrated over a finite algebraic chain: at a nonzero algebraic
parameter, an algebraic value occurs exactly when the whole function
vanishes. It also transfers linear value relations to functional ones.
The README labels this a preprint. We use the claim as a cited external
input, not as a new internal proof. Its phase-moment argument uses a
finite tree in the phase plane, edge densities, and a Cauchy transform;
the tree has connected complement.

The following elementary control isolates what that last condition does.
Let Gamma be an oriented triangle boundary or a circle, and P any
polynomial. Then for every m>=0,

\[
 \oint_\Gamma z^mP(z)\,dz=0,
\]

although the current P(z)dz is nonzero if P is nonzero. Its Cauchy transform
with the convention 1/(s-z) is

\[
 \oint_\Gamma\frac{P(z)}{s-z}\,dz
 =\begin{cases}0,&s\text{ outside},\\-2\pi iP(s),&s\text{ inside}.
 \end{cases}                                             \tag{11}
\]

The proof is polynomial exactness for the moments and the residue theorem
for (11). Thus even all exterior holomorphic moments can forget a whole
interior analytic function. One interior sample is not enough: choose P
vanishing there. Three samples are enough within degree<=2, but not for
unbounded degree. An a priori dimension bound is part of a finite
observer's certificate.

A triangle boundary and a circle are alike here: both enclose a face.
A tree does not. Three noncollinear points determine a circle only after
the Euclidean circumcircle construction has been fixed; they are not
three identities of rational functions. In F1 the three source functions
are tested as identities, and the derivative matrix, not interpolation at
three points, is the information-recovery mechanism.

For area, the missing observer is already retained in the stronger
THM-3203 argument. On the triangle with vertices 0,1,i,

\[
 \oint z^m\,dz=0\ (m\ge0),\qquad
 \oint\overline z\,dz=2i\operatorname{area}=i.             \tag{12}
\]

The conjugate-coordinate factor retains two-dimensional area when plain
holomorphic boundary moments lose it. This suggests a disciplined route
around an obstruction: identify the kernel of the proposed readout, then
retain an observable that actually detects that kernel. In Collatz the
needed added observer must concern the actual source and its ROOT score;
a formal analogy to (12) is not that observer.

## 6. Real zeta(7), p-adic values, and the paid denominator boundary

The EIT source does not assert irrationality of the real number zeta(7).
The linked author also has a distinct
[p-adic zeta repository](https://github.com/octonion/p-adic-zeta-irrationality),
whose listed values include zeta_7(3) and zeta_2(7). The prime subscript
specifies a different completion; neither is the ordinary real zeta(7).
The local audit and THM-4089 above are the applicable stronger records.

There is nevertheless an exact zeta(7) interface in our work: W4 of the
companion note specializes the already proved alias-tail theorem at r=6.
It explains a power-seven decay and 6*zeta(7) without requiring zeta(7)
to be irrational. For a period comparison, the standard integral is

\[
 \zeta(7)=\frac1{720}\int_0^\infty\frac{t^6}{e^t-1}\,dt
 =\frac1{720}\int_0^1\frac{(-\log v)^6}{1-v}\,dv.
\]

Expanding the first denominator as sum_{n>=1}exp(-nt) proves the first
identity by positive integration; the substitution v=exp(-t) proves the
second. These are not the finite-chain polynomial-phase EIT input.
The infinite sum, logarithmic amplitude, and endpoint singularity cannot
be discarded. A valid reduction would need to represent or control them,
including the limiting arithmetic height; none is supplied here.

The [credit/zeta note](collatz_credit_zeta_bridge_20261005.md) already shows
why integer polynomial coefficients do not imply an integral infinite
readout, and why the raw localized moment series has uncancelled poles.
Reproving a smaller finite nonvanishing statement would not pay either
missing interface.
