# The +2 unit wheel for modulus 2pq

**Theorem.** Let p and q be distinct odd primes and m=2pq. Take the vertices to be the φ(m)=(p−1)(q−1) units mod m, with a directed edge r→r+2 whenever both endpoints are units. The underlying graph is a disjoint union of p+q−3 nonempty paths. It has (p−2)(q−2) directed edges, and if p<q, exactly two paths have each length 1,2,…,p−2, and exactly q−p+1 paths have length p−1. In particular the maximum path length is p−1. These are exact local residue statements, not primality or twin-prime assertions.

**Proof.** The odd residues form one cycle of length pq under +2. Label them r=pq+2t (mod 2pq), t∈Z/pq; +2 increases t by one. The deleted odd nonunits are exactly the points t divisible by p or q. There are q+p−1 such holes, by inclusion-exclusion. Since p,q are odd, neither p nor q can divide two consecutive t's. Consecutive holes must therefore have t divisible by one prime and t+1 divisible by the other. CRT gives exactly one solution to t≡0 (mod p), t≡−1 (mod q), and exactly one to t≡0 (mod q), t≡−1 (mod p). These two solutions are distinct: otherwise t and t+1 would both be divisible by pq. Hence exactly two +1 edges of the odd cycle have two holes as endpoints. There are also surviving vertices, e.g. t=1. On any finite cycle with both holes and surviving points, the number of surviving paths equals the number of runs of holes, which equals the number of holes minus the number of hole-to-hole edges. Therefore the number of paths is (p+q−1)−2=p+q−3. Subtracting this from the vertex count gives (p−1)(q−1)−(p+q−3)=(p−2)(q−2) directed edges. To obtain the complete length distribution, suppose p<q. First delete multiples of p: q length-(p−1) paths remain. The p−1 nonzero multiples kq, 1≤k≤p−1, are separated cyclically by q>p, so they lie in distinct paths. Their positions within those paths are the residues kq mod p, which run through 1,…,p−1 exactly once. A deletion at position j splits its original path into lengths j−1 and p−1−j; zero-length pieces are omitted. Thus each length ℓ=1,…,p−2 occurs exactly twice, while the q−p+1 untouched paths have length p−1. ∎

Examples, exact path-length histograms: p=3,q=5 gives {1:2, 2:3}; p=3,q=7 gives {1:2, 2:5}; p=5,q=29 gives {1:2, 2:2, 3:2, 4:25}. The last has 112 vertices, 81 edges and 31 components. A graph edge imposes only local coprimality and need not come from primes: mod 290, the edge (7,9) is a counterexample.

Run `python3 wheel_general.py` and `python3 -O wheel_general.py`, and compare outputs. The independent finite enumeration tests every unordered distinct odd-prime pair below 50, in addition to the explicit examples. The theorem is proved for all distinct odd primes; tests are not the proof.
