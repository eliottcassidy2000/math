## G1. An all-weight lifting theorem from a b-fold coloring

Suppose a finite simple graph H has alpha(H)<=q and a proper b-fold
a-coloring: each vertex receives b labels from{1,...,a}, and adjacent
vertices receive disjoint label sets. Assume a>=2b and put lambda=a/b.
Define W(0)=0,W(1)=1 and

    W(2h)=lambda W(h),
    W(2h+1)=(lambda-1)W(h)+W(h+1).                          (G1)

For every integer K>=0 and nonnegative integer weights x_v satisfying
both x_v<=K and x_u+x_v<=K on every edge,

    sum_v W(x_v) <= q W(K).                                (G2)

The singleton inequalities are necessary when H has isolated vertices.
Without them even a one-vertex graph refutes the statement.

**Proof.** The K=0 case is zero. At K=1 the nonzero coordinates form
an independent set of at most q vertices. For larger K write
x_v=2y_v+e_v and K=2h+epsilon. If epsilon=0, make a layers, each with
base y; an odd coordinate increments exactly in its b coloring labels.
Adjacent odd coordinates never increment in the same layer. Checking
zero, one, or two odd endpoints proves all layer edge budgets<=h;
the singleton budgets follow too.

If epsilon=1, use a-b unincremented layers and b layers incrementing
EVERY odd coordinate. The former have budget h and the latter h+1.
For two odd adjacent coordinates, y_u+y_v<=h-1, so the two simultaneous
increments are allowed. For one odd endpoint the base sum is<=h.

In either case the sum of layer masses at a coordinate is exactly

    a W(y_v)                       if e_v=0,
    (a-b)W(y_v)+b W(y_v+1)         if e_v=1,

which equals b W(x_v). Apply induction to the layers and divide by b;
(G1) gives q W(K). All layer budgets are smaller than K. QED.

This is a sufficient theorem for every graph supplied with the stated
coloring and independence bound. The source is actual incidence; the
output is an all-integer-weight inequality. It does not say that merely
knowing the chromatic number constructs a minimum coloring.

## G2. A uniform exact slope for the five-cycle composition class

Let D be generated from a singleton by joins, disjoint unions, and
C5 substitution with **all five children nonempty**. It is closed under
complement. We do not claim it is hereditary: deleting a vertex of C5
produces P4, which is not generated by joins and unions of singletons.
Write F_D(A,B) for its maximum order with omega<=A and alpha<=B,
for positive integer A,B.

Use lambda=5/2 in (G1), and call the resulting function w. The first
values are

    w(1..8)=1, 5/2, 4, 25/4, 31/4, 10, 49/4, 125/8.

Then, for ALL A,B,

    F_D(A,B) <= min(w(A)B, w(B)A).                          (G3)

Moreover equality F_D(A,B)=w(A)B holds whenever

    2^ceil(log_2 A) divides B.                              (G4)

In particular lim_(B->infinity) F_D(A,B)/B=w(A) for every fixed A.
Thus an infinite family of exact slopes has a binary formula, not a
finite fitted table. This does not compute F_D at every residue class.

**Upper bound.** C5 has independence number2 and a proper two-fold
five-coloring: vertex i receives{i,i+2} modulo5. G1 gives

    sum_(i=0)^4 w(a_i)<=2w(A) whenever a_i+a_(i+1)<=A.       (G5)

K2 also has such a coloring and alpha=1; hence
w(x)+w(y)<=w(x+y). This proves superadditivity and monotonicity of w.

Induct on the construction of D. Joins and disjoint unions respect
the bound n<=w(A)B by superadditivity and monotonicity. At a C5 root
let a_i,b_i be the actual child clique and independence numbers. They
satisfy a_i+a_(i+1)<=A and b_i+b_(i+2)<=B. Induction bounds the order
by sum_i w(a_i)b_i. The polytope b>=0, b_i+b_(i+2)<=B has vertices
equal to B times the eleven independent-set indicators of the constraint
five-cycle, plus the uniform vector B/2. One can recover this list by
the inherited elementary active-constraint proof for the C5 edge
polytope: a proper support is a union of paths and has integral vertices;
full support forces the five equations and yields the half vector.

An indicator vertex has support a clique of the original C5, so its
objective is at most B w(A) by superadditivity. The half vertex has
objective at most B w(A) by (G5). Complementation gives the other
half of (G3).

**Sharp construction.** For A=1 use B isolated vertices. If A=2h,
substitute five identical constructions with parameters(h,B/2). If
A=2h+1, put parameters(h+1,B/2) at two nonadjacent vertices of C5
and(h,B/2) at the other three. Their actual omega is A and alpha is B.
The order recurrence is respectively

    5 w(h)B/2,    [2w(h+1)+3w(h)]B/2,

which is w(A)B. Condition(G4) pays for every division by two along
the deepest child. For arbitrary B, take disjoint copies of the
attainer at L=2^ceil(log_2 A), then add B mod L isolated vertices.
This gives w(A)B-O_A(1), proving the limit. No empty slots are used.

**Classical recurrence credit.** The binary maximum law with
coefficients(3/2,1) is within
[Hwang--Janson--Tsai, 2210.10968v1, Proposition C.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2210.10968v1),
which credits earlier Knuth--Lossers work. We independently prove the
graph lifting and realization statements above; the recurrence family
itself is not presented as a new sequence. A query for w(A) takes
O(log(A+1)) rational arithmetic operations using the pair(w(m),w(m+1)).
The two bit updates are ((5/2)a,(3/2)a+b) and((3/2)a+b,(5/2)b).

## G3. Complete first four capacity rows

For every positive B,

    F_D(1,B)=B,
    F_D(2,B)=floor(5B/2),
    F_D(3,B)=4B-[4 does not divide B],
    F_D(4,B)=25 floor(B/4)+h_(B mod4),  h=(0,4,10,16).       (G6)

Complementation supplies the first four columns as well. These are
exact statements for the defined composition class, not unrestricted
two-color Ramsey numbers.

**Row1.** A graph with omega<=1 is edgeless.

**Row2.** In a full C5 root every child has a_i=1 and order<=b_i.
Summing its five nonedge constraints gives sum b_i<=floor(5B/2).
Joins give at most2B; disjoint union preserves the floor bound. For
B=2m take every b_i=m. For B=2m+1>=3 take b_i=m+1 at two adjacent
vertices of the original C5, and m elsewhere. These are nonadjacent
vertices of the constraint cycle. Each child is edgeless. For B=1
use K2. All upper bounds are attained.

**Row3.** Child clique budgets at a full C5 root lie in{1,2}, and
the slots with budget2 form an independent set of the original C5.
There are at most two. If there are two, label them0,2. The constraint
cycle on the b_i is ordered0,2,4,1,3. The objective coefficients
w(a_i) in this order are(5/2,5/2,1,1,1). Weight its first constraint
edge by2 and its other four edges by1/2. Each coordinate receives
exactly the required coefficient; the sum of these positive dual
weights is4. Thus n<=4B, with equality only if EVERY constraint is
tight, giving b_i=B/2. At a heavy child, Row2 attains(5/2)b_i only
if b_i is even. Equality therefore requires4|B. With fewer than two
heavy children, increasing budgets to two nonadjacent heavy slots
strictly increases the objective since all b_i>0, so equality is
impossible there too. The integer upper bound is4B-1 otherwise.
Joins split at most1+2 and cannot improve this bound. For unions,
4B-[4 does not divide B] is superadditive: if the total is not divisible
by4, at least one summand is not divisible by4.

Attainment at B=2m uses b_i=m with heavy slots0,2, giving
2 floor(5m/2)+3m=4B-[m odd]. At B=2m+1>=3 give the two nonadjacent
constraint positions0,2 the value m+1 and the others m. Exactly one
heavy child is high and one is low. The objective is
floor(5m/2)+floor(5(m+1)/2)+3m+1=4B-1. B=1 uses K3.

**Row4.** Write H(B)=25 floor(B/4)+h_(B mod4). First consider a full
C5 root with all a_i=2. Its exact objective is sum floor(5b_i/2).
Put S=sum b_i and O=number of odd b_i. The objective is(5S-O)/2,
and S<=floor(5B/2).

For B=2m, equality S=5m forces b_i=m on all five slots by summing
the constraint slacks. This gives5 floor(5m/2)=H(B). If S<=5m-1,
floor(5S/2)<=5 floor(5m/2), so it cannot improve the answer.
For B=2m+1, the largest possible S is5m+2. If it is attained, the
five nonnegative integer edge slacks have total1, so precisely one
is1. Solving the five equations gives two nonadjacent constraint
vertices with b_i=m+1 and three with b_i=m. Their objective is H(B).
If S<=5m+1, floor(5S/2)<H(B). These constructions are legal for
B>=2, and use Row2 attainers as children.

For a nonuniform positive clique-budget pattern a_i in{1,2,3},
the edge constraints imply sum w(a_i)<=11 and every adjacent pair
has w-sum<=5. To see the first claim: without a3, one1 reduces the
all2 sum from25/2 to at most11. With a3, both neighbors are1, and
the remaining adjacent pair contributes at most5, again giving11.
The same polytope argument as in G2 now bounds the order by
floor(11B/2)<=H(B) for B>=3. At B=2 all b_i=1, so the children are
cliques and sum a_i<=10=H(2). At B=1 a full C5 root is impossible.
Joins give at most5B<=H(B) for B>=2; B=1 uses K4. Finally H is
superadditive: h_r+h_s<=25 floor((r+s)/4)+h_((r+s) mod4) is the
complete sixteen-case residue check for r,s in{0,1,2,3}. This handles
all unions and finishes the inductive upper bound. The constructions
above and K4 attain it.

## G4. A precise obstruction to free realization

The bound w(3)B=4B is integral for EVERY B, but actual equality is
possible exactly when4 divides B. In particular F_D(3,2)=7, not8.
The missing information is not a denominator of the scalar value:
equality forces all five common port constraints tight, then each heavy
child imposes another parity condition. This is an explicit two-level
realization guard.

Compare the incoming
[odd-descent face/Smith analysis](../odd_descent_local_relaxations_20261030/README.md):
its denominator criterion is exact for its stated linear/affine
feasibility problem. Here actual recursive graph realization is an
additional predicate. There is no contradiction; dropping that predicate
would be the first invalid implication. The source graph, common root
constraints, and child feasibility must travel together.

**Next target, OPEN.** For fixed A, does F_D(A,B) eventually satisfy
an exact translation law F_D(A,B+L)=F_D(A,B)+w(A)L with a computable
power-of-two L? G2 proves sharp slopes and one infinite equality
progression, but neither eventual periodicity nor a minimal period.
The first four rows supply exact positive controls and nontrivial
residue penalties for a finite-state approach.
