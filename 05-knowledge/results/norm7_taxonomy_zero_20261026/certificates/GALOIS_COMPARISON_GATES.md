# Finite Frobenius comparisons require a represented object and a comparison theorem

**Status: CITED comparison theorems; PROVED explicit obstruction examples and Newton identities; FINITE-EXACT executable controls.** The TT7 raw sums are not promoted to pure rank-four motives. This note is a certificate interface, not a claim to prove or disprove their eventual motivic identification.

## 1. Precisely what all local traces identify

For two continuous finite-dimensional Q_l representations of G_(K,S), agreement of Frobenius traces outside S implies isomorphic **semisimplifications**, by Chebotarev density and Brauer–Nesbitt. Kedlaya's author notes give the actual statement as Proposition9.4.5 (the next chapter refers to an older numbering). For abelian varieties, additional semisimplicity and Faltings' theorem turn this into isogeny, not isomorphism of varieties or integral lattices. Source: https://kskedlaya.org/weil-cohom/chapter-9.html .

Kedlaya's Remark10.1.3 supplies the central application boundary: both trace lists must already arise from Galois representations with controlled ramification. A computed abstract sequence plus a guessed conductor does not satisfy this premise. The first comparison step controls residual representations by finite extensions with prescribed ramification; the next lifts the comparison. Source: https://kskedlaya.org/weil-cohom/chapter-10.html . We do not use that chapter's abbreviated Theorem10.2.4 as a dimension-free theorem; §3 gives an exact obstruction.

A fixed ramification set does not itself give a finite candidate list: powers of the l-adic cyclotomic character are infinitely many distinct continuous rank-one representations unramified away from l. This does not refute finite determining-set theorems with their full hypotheses. It refutes the shortcut “a ramification bound is already an enumerated finite list with a certified test bank.”

## 2. One precise Livné theorem, with its scope intact

Dieulefait–Guerberoff–Pacetti, *Proving modularity for a given elliptic curve over an imaginary quadratic field*, Theorem4.1, states Livné's criterion in this scope. Let K be imaginary quadratic, E/Q₂ finite with maximal ideal P₂, and ρ₁,ρ₂:G_K→GL₂(E) continuous and unramified outside a finite set S. Require both residual trace functions to be zero, residual determinants equal, and a finite prime set T outside S whose Frobenius images are noncubic in the Galois group of the compositum of quadratic extensions unramified outside S. If exact traces **and determinants** agree on T, the semisimplifications are isomorphic. The source also treats other residual images separately. It does not say that arbitrary equal residual traces or a noncubic set alone suffice in every dimension. Primary research source: https://arxiv.org/pdf/0804.2302 , printed p.8, Theorem4.1; definition on printed p.4.

This displayed theorem is not applied to TT7: no rank-two representation has been identified for its four residual directions. Even a proved rank-four target would require an appropriate higher-rank theorem, not substitution of “four” into this criterion. Exact determinant, coefficient field, stable integral model, residual comparison, ramification control and the test-set construction all belong to the certificate.

## 3. An actual rank-eight hostile to a dimension-free noncubic shortcut

Let V=F₂⁴, with characters χ_j(v)=(-1)^(v_j). Define two semisimple eight-dimensional representations

    ρ_even = ⊕_(|A| even) ∏_(j∈A) χ_j,
    ρ_odd  = ⊕_(|A| odd)  ∏_(j∈A) χ_j.

Their determinant characters are both1, because each χ_j occurs four times in each representation. Their traces are even, and

    tr ρ_even(v) − tr ρ_odd(v) = ∏_(j=1)^4 (1−χ_j(v)).

This vanishes at every v except w=(1,1,1,1), where it is16. In particular they agree at zero and at

    T=V \ {0,w},

but disagree at w (traces8 and−8).

Nevertheless T is noncubic. Homogeneous cubic polynomials over F₂, regarded as functions, are spanned by the fourteen square-free monomials of degrees1,2,3: x_i³=x_i and x_i²x_j=x_ix_j as functions. A function in that span vanishing on T also vanishes at0. If nonzero, it would be the indicator of w, whose unique square-free polynomial is x₁x₂x₃x₄, of degree4. Uniqueness follows by evaluating successively on subsets, or the invertibility of the Boolean subset-zeta matrix. Thus no nonzero cubic function vanishes on T. The checker independently verifies that the14×14 evaluation matrix is invertible over F₂.

This example occurs for actual Galois representations over Q. Take

    K_S=Q(i,√2,√3,√5),    S={2,3,5}.

It is the full elementary quadratic extension unramified outside S: quadratic squareclasses are generated by −1,2,3,5. Use Legendre symbols in that order to label Frobenius, with a negative symbol recorded as1. The following primes represent T, in binary-mask order1,...,14:

    71,61,11,41,31,29,19,73,23,13,83,17,7,53.

Prime43 has mask15 and detects the disagreement. Hence both representations have even residual traces and equal determinants, and agree on an actual noncubic Frobenius set, yet are not isomorphic. The GL₂ rank restriction cannot be discarded. No contradiction to Livné's stated rank-two theorem is involved.

**Source-quality control.** Kedlaya's Remark10.2.5 says noncubic sets cannot exist in dimension≤2, but with the definition used there and in the research paper this is false: V itself is noncubic by definition, and V\{0} works in dimensions1 and2. The checker gives explicit invertible evaluation matrices. We therefore use that lecture for its representation-existence warning, and the research theorem for precise comparison hypotheses.

## 4. Even all Euler factors can forget decisive data

### A nonsplit extension versus its split form

On the profinite group Z-hat, let c be its continuous projection to Z_l. Put

    ρ(g)=[[1,c(g)],[0,1]],    σ(g)=I₂.

Both are continuous. Every element and every Frobenius power has characteristic polynomial (X−1)², hence Euler factor (1−T)². Yet their invariant spaces have dimensions1 and2, so the representations are not isomorphic. The first is a nonsplit extension of trivial characters; both have the same semisimplification. This is a local procyclic example; no purity or geometric-origin assertion is attached to it.

### Rational isomorphism versus integral lattice

For G=C₂, the integral matrices

    A=diag(1,−1),    B=[[1,1],[0,−1]]

both square to I and have the same characteristic polynomials on every group element. Over Q₂ they are conjugate: the columns (1,0) and(1,−2) form an eigenbasis for B, of determinant−2. Over Z₂ they cannot be conjugate, because A becomes the identity modulo2 while B becomes a nonidentity unipotent matrix. Thus even semisimple rational representation equality does not recover the integral lattice.

### Isogeny versus rational-point structure

The curves E:y²=x³−x and E':Y²=X³+4X have an explicit degree-two isogeny

    X=x−1/x,    Y=y(1+1/x²),

extending over the smooth projective curves, with kernel {O,(0,0)}. Substitution proves the equation; a generic X has two x-preimages. Isogenous elliptic curves have equal rational l-adic representations and good Euler factors, by the elementary Tate-module effect of a finite-kernel isogeny. But E(Q)[2] has four elements and E'(Q)[2] has two: their rational-point groups are not identified by the trace data. The checker confirms equal finite-field counts for every odd prime below100. This is an explicit lost-coordinate example, not a proposed TT7 identification.

## 5. Newton reconstruction is conditional on rank and duality

For an invertible linear operator F of **known dimension r**, traces s_k=tr(F^k), k=1,...,r, determine det(1−FT) by Newton's identities

    c₀=1,    k c_k = −Σ_(i=1)^k c_(k−i) s_i.

Without a rank bound, no finite trace prefix determines the characteristic polynomial: adjoining all m-th roots of unity changes rank by m and contributes zero to each power trace of orders1,...,m−1. This example even preserves weight-zero purity. A rank-two spectrum {1,−1} and the rank-six spectrum obtained by adjoining {1,i,−1,−i} agree through the third trace but differ at the fourth.

With a proved rank-four symplectic similitude and multiplier μ, the eigenvalues occur in pairs α,μ/α. Only then do s₁,s₂ give

    det(1−FT)=1−s₁T+(s₁²−s₂)T²/2−μs₁T³+μ²T⁴.

The checker verifies this algebraic identity on exact paired spectra. Integrality or the parity of s₁²−s₂ is a necessary check, not a proof that a raw sequence is the trace of such an operator.

For TT7, the new extension packet `../motives/EXTENSION_TWIST.md` preserves the finite-field sign ε=χ_q(−1) and shows why a fixed prime-field minus sign changes on quadratic extensions. Even correctly extended raw sums still require exact boundary subtraction and a pure subquotient before they can supply these s_k. The finite computations alone do not certify rank4, multiplier q⁵, or a conductor.

## 6. Typed gates for the four proposed TT7 directions

1. **Definedness gate:** specify finite field, characteristic, indexing domain and exact evaluator; out-of-domain is a tagged rejection, not nontermination or numerical zero.
2. **Raw-identity gate:** retain the graph/cone normalization, field-size sign and boundary terms. Certify any proposed equality on this carrier.
3. **Representation gate:** construct the actual l-adic object or exact subquotient; prove its coefficient field, rank, purity/weight and ramification support. A fit is not this construction.
4. **Local-factor gate:** extract genuine power traces and invoke Newton with a proved rank or reciprocal-pairing hypothesis.
5. **Comparison gate:** establish the applicable residual representation and determinant conditions, construct its certified finite test set, then compare exactly. A guessed conductor cannot discharge the preceding gates.
6. **Conclusion gate:** record semisimplification first. Upgrade to representation, isogeny, integral lattice or geometric information only with a separate theorem.

These gates distinguish unfinished arithmetic construction from undecidability. The thirteen-row certificate in `ZERO_AND_FINITE_CERTIFICATES.md` already discharges a useful narrower predicate—four independent raw directions—without pretending to have discharged the representation gate.
