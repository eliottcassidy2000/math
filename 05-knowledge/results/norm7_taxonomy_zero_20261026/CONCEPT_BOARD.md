## Loose six/seven probes, pursued to a precise boundary

The inherited [six/seven geometry](../six_seven_geometry_20261025/README.md)
already supplies an actual map: Fano sign subgroups act on the eight central
branch conics, and regularity of that action matches freeness on the seven-root
hourglass. Canonical characters and branch incidence explain those integers.
This session keeps that map as input; it does not identify a general K3 with
an arbitrary object bearing the number six.

The quaternion (−1,δ) and the cone sign χ(−1) genuinely share the squareclass
−1. Their operations differ: the former tests whether δ is a norm from K(i),
and the latter evaluates the quadratic character of −1 in a finite field.
For example δ=2 makes the quaternion split over Q, while χ_q(−1) still varies
with q. Thus neither datum determines the other; δ and the ground field are
necessary coordinates. This is an exact obstruction to collapsing these two
arithmetic sign mechanisms into one invariant.

Paley7 and the order-seven carousel are both cyclic prime tournaments, but
their automorphism groups have orders21 and7. The numerical seven does not
identify their classification actions. Likewise the fourteen-element
noncubic bank in F₂⁴ belongs to a degree-four character expansion: its first
missed indicator is x1 x2 x3 x4. It is not a fourteen-speed LRC certificate.

The simplex/sphere/cube heuristic becomes useful only after a representation
and operation are named. Here the concrete Boolean carrier is a character
group, and the concrete cyclic carrier is a rotation action on binary words.
No map from a sphere or cuboid is needed for the proofs, and no such geometric
identification is inferred from the repeated integers.
