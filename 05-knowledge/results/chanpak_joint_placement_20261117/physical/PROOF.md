# Two independently growing physical scales in the three-contact repair

This is a new variable-center version of the three-contact move. The construction changes a strong flank height m **and** the dyadic contact scale G. The near-balance mismatch z can be fixed in advance, even at 1, rather than used to satisfy the shift congruence. It realizes large exact shifts while retaining the reflected +2. The source is not selectable at every prescribed absolute count; the full bank is still sparse.

The inspiration from [Zehavi's paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.18218v1), especially the introduction and §4, is limited: measure the surviving correction on its own graded piece instead of treating the full interaction size as its bound. No Iwasawa or curve theorem is used here. In this setting the controlling low-level symbol is the residue of bit reversal modulo 3; it survives even when the full integer reversal cannot be discarded.

## Physical core and independent scale

Use the reflected center D=(54,32,2,119,6,20,55), marked outgoing mask 26, with profile H=5, P₂=256, L=R=142, signed L⁻=−2,R⁻=0, and apex count 91. See the Farey flank proof for signed cover notation and independent literal controls. Introduce a strong flank R_m: a transitive order on 0,...,m+1 except last→0. Write q=2^m, a=q+1, p=4q+2(3^m−1), and l=2q−1. These are respectively its parameters q, its H, optional two-cover count, and its weighted starts when the apex beats only vertex 0. The tagged vertex owns exactly one full path end.

The left flank X consists of a transitive binary prefix u followed by R_m, with mark 0 on R_m; the right flank is the marked converse of a transitive block Y with even positive address w. Y has H=1 and its tags own no full path end. Swapping the marked flank roles gives exactly +2 by the signed determinant

    2[H(Y) e_X−H(X)e_Y] = 2.

This holds for each u,w, even after repairing their coarse contact bits. Set

    h=5a, P=256p, A=147p−10a, C=142a+256l.

For odd u and even w the lower count is

    H_m(u,w)=K_m+h(F(u)+F(w))+Au+Cw+Puw

for a fixed integer K_m. For an explicit form, set L_X=pu+l and d_X=2q−2+a(F(u)−1)+(p−2a)u+q[u odd]. Then

    H_m(u,w)=91a+5[d_X+a(F(w)−1)]+142(L_X+aw)+256L_Xw.

All assembled tournaments are strong. The extreme-back-edge tournament is nontransitive, so changing m is not a disguised bit padding on the two outer spines.

## Three-contact repair and the symbolic layer

Fix even m≥2, G=2^t≥4, odd r in [1,G−1], and s=G−1−r. For x=y+z positive and even, y>0, choose

    u=G(16x+5)+r,        w=G(16y+4)+s,
    u'=u+4G,            w'=w−4G.

This reverses exactly three old apex arcs: 0101→1001 and 0100→0000 in the four-bit blocks just above the suffix. The addresses do not change parity, length, or their sum. In particular both source and destination retain +2 companions.

Let J=PG², rev be t-bit reversal, and Delt the shift H_m(u',w')−H_m(u,w). The inherited low/high divide and the bit patterns give

    Delt = 3h y − (64J−h) z − B_r,
    B_r = 16J+4PG+8PG r+6h−4hG+8h rev(r)−4G(A−C).       (1)

For details, the product changes by 4G(w−u)−16G². The interior F changes are F(16x+9)−F(16x+5)=x+2 for even x and F(16y)−F(16y+4)=2y−4. The two suffix linear changes are 4(C_r−C_s), where C_r=G−1−rev(r), C_s=rev(r), and the prefix-parity sidecars cancel. Substituting x=y+z gives (1).

At the integer level rev(r) is highly nonlinear. But at the modulus required by y's denominator,

    rev(r) ≡ (−1)^(t−1) r (mod 3),
    B_r ≡ B_0 + 8(PG+h(−1)^(t−1)) r (mod 3h).

Let g=gcd(P,h). For even m, h≡1, P≡2 (mod3), and G and (−1)^(t−1) are opposite mod3. The r coefficient therefore has gcd exactly g with 3h. Moreover 5 does not divide p (its residues as m varies mod4 are 4,2,2,4), so g=gcd(p,a), and l≡−3 mod g. Consequently A−C≡768 mod g. Solvability is exactly

    Delt ≡ 3072 G (mod g).                                (2)

A full arithmetic progression of odd r's realizes the needed residue: if G≥6h/g, odd r covers every class modulo 3h/g, and all permitted residues r lie in one progression of step 6h/g. Fix **any** positive z (in particular z=1). For every even Delt of magnitude at most J satisfying (2), solve this congruence for r, then put y=(Delt+(64J−h)z+B_r)/(3h). Parity of (1) forces x=y+z even, so the assumed three-bit pattern is valid. Since B_r is between fixed positive multiples of J (its O(PG+hG) corrections are smaller than its main 16J+8PGr for G≥4), y is positive and Θ(J(z+1)/h), with uniform constants. This supplies exact shifts with a separately chosen flank height m and dyadic scale G, plus a prescribable near-balance index z.

If g>1, no nonzero signed dyadic power shift can pass (2) for any dyadic G. Indeed g divides 2^m+1 and 3^(m−1)−1, so the orders of 2 and 3 modulo g divide coprime integers 2m and m−1. A signed dyadic shift in (2) puts 3 in the subgroup generated by 2 (also −1=2^m), forcing 3≡1 mod g, impossible for odd g>1. No assertion that g=1 at every even m is made.

## Geometric limits and graded sparsity

On both sides of the repair,

    H_m(u,w) ≍ P G² y² ≍ P³G⁶(z+1)²/h².

The linear and F corrections are uniformly dominated by the product (F(x)≤x+1, A,C=O(P), |K_m|=O(P), and the actual marked expression gives the positive lower bound). For bounded z the admissible displacement scale reaches J=PG²=Θ(h^(2/3) H^(1/3)). If G is comparable to h and m tends to infinity through the allowed even heights, the shift scale is comparable to the power (log₂3+2)/(3log₂3+4)≈0.4095 of the source, still with the local phase gate. Taking G≥6h suffices.

The r-progression gives multiple coarse centers for fixed m,G,z,Delt. For adjacent legal r's, separated by d=6h/g, the y change is Θ(PGd/h): the linear 8PGd in B_r dominates the bit-reversal fluctuation at most 8hG. Both addresses rise by Θ(PG²d/h), and at bounded z their count rises by Θ(P³G⁵d/h²), since the F variation is at most O(h(u+w)) and smaller than the leading product change. Thus the r-scale creates a separation of order H h/(Gg), not an exact absolute selector.

Even allowing all legal |Delt|≤J, all positive z, all even m and every dyadic G≥4, the bank has at most **O(sqrt(X) log X)** endpoint values ≤X (both before/after shifts and either +2 companion). For fixed m,G,r,z, (1) allows O(J/h) choices of y. There are O(G) choices of r, and the size bound gives O(sqrt(X)h/(P^(3/2)G³)) eligible z. Multiply to obtain O(sqrt(X)/sqrt(P)) for each m,G. There are O(log X) possible dyadic G and Σ_m P^(-1/2) converges. If z is restricted to a fixed finite set, the bound improves to O(sqrt(X)): for given m, the sum of G³ is dominated by its largest feasible term O(sqrt(X)h/P^(3/2)), and multiplying by P/h and summing m gives the bound. Thus the richer physical scale family improves exact shift control but does not close prescribed-source placement.
