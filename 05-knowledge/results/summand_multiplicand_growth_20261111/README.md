## What this continuation settles

Two gaps explicitly left by
[S259](../session259_arithmetic_graph_growth_20261111/SYNTHESIS.md) now have
general solutions. Strict addition means using two distinct available
positive values; generation is simultaneous, with old values retained.

1. **Arbitrary finite seed sets have an exact closure.** If H(S) is the
   ordinary nonnegative additive monoid generated by S, then
   `Cl(S)=S union union_(a<b in S)(a+b+H(S))`. This also holds for strict
   products, and more generally for a commutative monoid with a positive
   additive weight. A retained seed m gives an exact finite description
   by 2m residue minima plus exceptional seeds. Adjoining any new seed
   has a finite update, even though it can unlock infinitely many targets.
2. **Every finite seed set with at least two values eventually reaches
   the same upper-frontier regime.** If d=gcd(S), its three largest
   values eventually become M,M-d,M-2d. Thereafter
   `M_(t+1)=2M_t-d` exactly. A three-term consecutive block in the
   normalized closure catches the moving maximum: its distance decreases
   by the gap between the two largest values at each step. Keeping only
   a shrinking window of deficits executes this chase exactly. The proof
   gives a computable finite bound, not the earliest transition time.

Both proofs, the one-seed update, logarithmic generation-depth bounds,
and the shrinking-window algorithm are in
[summand/CLOSURE.md](summand/CLOSURE.md). These settle the finite-seed
closure and eventual-frontier existence questions, not all transient
generation times or the distribution of lower holes.

## Three different meanings of one new integer

The same objects support different growth questions. Keeping their clocks
separate prevents a vertex-arrival count from being mistaken for generation.

| Growth clock | What changes at a step | Exact finite record |
|---|---|---|
| Numerical prefix, all vertices through n | The new target n receives all its legal parent pairs | Sum pairs floor((n-1)/2); nonunit product pairs (tau(n)-2-[n square])/2 |
| Adjoining one seed to a construction | A new generator may unlock an infinite closure | Two residue-minimum vectors and the exceptional seed set |
| One simultaneous generation | Every legal pair of currently available values produces its output | A full state initially; a shrinking upper window after a three-block is known |

The first row is inherited from THM-2422,
[`operation-fibres-summand-closure-and-twin-center-ancestry`](../../../01-canon/theorems/THM-2422-operation-fibres-summand-closure-and-twin-center-ancestry.md),
and S259. The other rows are developed here. A fourth clock, extending
the parent alphabet while allowing future outputs, is treated in
[papers/PROOF.md](papers/PROOF.md): a new parent n adds exactly the pairs
`{a,n}`, and their joint coordinates lie on `product=n*sum-n^2`.

## Doubles and squares share an operator, not just a resemblance

The map `a -> p^a` identifies strict summand events with strict product
events on one prime-power ray. It carries the forbidden double to the
forbidden square and preserves synchronous generations. Numeric cutoffs
change from M to p^M. Across all primes, multiplication becomes addition
of finitely supported exponent vectors; the weight
`sum_p v_p(n) log p` preserves integer arrival time.

For the labelled linear observer that includes each vertex itself and
its strict incoming parents, the reconstruction laws are

    g_add(n)=sum_(a<=n) f(a)-[2|n]f(n/2),
    g_mult(n)=sum_(d|n,d>=2) f(d)-[n square]f(sqrt(n)).

These have exact inverses with entries respectively in `{-1,0,1}` and
`{-2,-1,0,1,2}`. The same binary sign kernel governs both, but the
multiplicative inverse synchronizes one scale across all prime exponents
before summing. At `6 -> 36`, two scales reinforce to give 2; separately
inverting the two prime coordinates would instead give 0. In general
the entry `d -> d^2` is `1+mu(d)`.

See [VALUATION_CHART.md](VALUATION_CHART.md),
[summand/INVERSE.md](summand/INVERSE.md), and
[multiplicand/PROOF.md](multiplicand/PROOF.md). These are incidence
reconstruction theorems; graph reachability does not assert that both
parents of an event are live. The hostile example is `2 -> 8`: the arc
exists, but prime seeds alone cannot supply its other parent 4.

## How the attached papers changed the construction

The papers suggest retaining operators, filtrations, and transition maps
when passing to finite approximations. We realize an elementary version
on actual finite permutation graphs, with independently proved
Frobenius, Verschiebung, and twisted-rotation norm operations. The
prime norm has a positive mixed-term carry law. For the square norm,

    N_2(X+Y)=N_2(X)+N_2(Y)+V_2(XY).

Adding one fixed-point parent to an n-point alphabet therefore adds one
fixed diagonal and n swapped pairs, for a cardinality increase 2n+1.
This gives a literal fixed/free-orbit account of diagonal squares versus
off-diagonal pairs. Composite arity introduces intermediate orbit lengths;
it cannot be handled by keeping only fixed and free orbits.

The full constructions and positive/hostile examples are in
[papers/PROOF.md](papers/PROOF.md); precise locations and hypotheses in
the three attached papers are in [papers/SOURCES.md](papers/SOURCES.md).
We do not import geometric cohomology theorems into arithmetic graphs,
identify finite permutation graphs with the full Raynaud structure, or
infer Hamiltonian-path constructions from norm cardinalities.

## Inheritance and concept board

Anchor: exact one-integer growth and arbitrary seed closure. Niche:
labelled incidence inversion with square deletion. Wildcard: finite
permutation norms and compatible truncations. These lanes meet at six
concepts: **paired parents; equal-parent diagonal; seed-support ideal;
moving frontier; shared binary scale; retained transition maps**.

* Closest proved mechanism: S259's translated two-seed semigroup and
  deficit recurrence.
* Canonical hostile examples: a singleton cannot strictly double itself;
  prime-only seeds cannot supply 4; independent prime-axis inverses lose
  the shared scale at 6 -> 36.
* Corrected near miss: Fibonacci growth guesses for strict closure are
  replaced by the proved dyadic frontier and potentially long parity
  transient. A finite truncation closing is not proof that an expanding
  frontier has stabilized.
* Least-used relevant sidecars: original seed-support types, paired
  rather than individual parent arcs, and correction scale before
  projection to prime coordinates.
