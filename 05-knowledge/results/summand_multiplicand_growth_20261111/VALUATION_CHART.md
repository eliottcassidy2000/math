# Prime exponents make the two growth laws literally the same

Date: 2026-11-11. **PROVED IN NOTE:** the filtration-preserving valuation
dictionary, its strict-closure consequences, and coherent reconstruction
from finite prefixes. The arithmetic facts behind the dictionary are
elementary; no literature-priority claim is made. The stronger inverse
coefficient bounds are proved in the linked operator notes.

## 1. Keep both parents and distinguish two clocks

The strict summand event is the unordered pair `{a,b}->a+b`, with
positive a<b. The strict multiplicand event is `{a,b}->ab`, with
2<=a<b. Its two ordinary arcs are a shadow of this event. A live arc
from one parent does not assert that the other parent is available.

These conventions and the fixed/free swap decomposition are inherited
from **THM-2422**,
[`operation-fibres-summand-closure-and-twin-center-ancestry`](../../../01-canon/theorems/THM-2422-operation-fibres-summand-closure-and-twin-center-ancestry.md).
The latest growth clocks and persistence results are in
[Session259](../session259_arithmetic_graph_growth_20261111/SYNTHESIS.md).
The present note uses the full parent event when discussing closure,
and only the linear arc observer when discussing reconstruction.

Fix a prime p. The map

    a -> p^a,   a>=1,                                      (1)

is an isomorphism from the entire strict summand event structure to
the multiplicand event structure restricted to positive powers of p:

    a+b=c, a!=b   iff   p^a p^b=p^c, p^a!=p^b.             (2)

It also identifies weak events and their removed diagonals. The additive
double a+a=2a is precisely the multiplicative square p^a p^a=p^(2a).
Nothing about parent identity or recursive generation is lost in (1).

What changes is numerical arrival. The additive prefix through M becomes
the prime-power subgraph through p^M. Conversely, the prime-power part of
the integer prefix [2,N] is exactly the additive prefix through
floor(log_p N). Synchronous closure generations are identical under (1),
but integer birth times are exponentially reparametrized.

Thus the warning that logarithms need not give an evenly spaced integer
lattice has an exact useful exception: one prime-power ray does. This
does not identify the full multiplicand graph with a single additive chain.

## 2. The full multiplicand graph is a vector summand graph

Let P be the set of primes. Unique factorization gives the bijection

    n -> v(n)=(v_p(n))_(p in P)

from integers n>=2 to the nonzero, finitely supported vectors in
N_0^(P). It transports multiplication to vector addition, proper
divisibility to coordinatewise order, and unequal factors to unequal
vectors. Consequently the whole strict multiplicand event structure is
the strict summand structure on this positive vector monoid.

The integer cutoff is retained exactly by the weight

    w(alpha)=sum_p alpha_p log p:
    n<=N  iff  w(v(n))<=log N.                            (3)

Only finitely many primes occur in each prefix. There is no scalar
integer-grid replacement for all these independent coordinates.

This makes several previously adjacent dichotomies distinct operations:

| Integer operation or property | Prime-exponent operation or property |
|---|---|
| Multiply n by 2 | Translate v(n) by the unit vector e_2 |
| Square n | Dilate every coordinate: v(n)->2v(n) |
| Prime n | A coordinate unit vector e_p |
| Prime square n | Twice a coordinate unit vector 2e_p |
| Squarefree n | Every coordinate is 0 or 1 |
| Square n | Every coordinate is even |
| One prime-power ray | A single coordinate axis |

For example 12 has vector (2,1) at primes 2,3. It is neither square nor
squarefree. The square 36 has vector (2,2), and its two-prime interaction
cannot be seen on either single coordinate axis alone. The number 16
lies on an axis but is not a primitive strict source.

## 3. Exact generation consequences

The joint seed theorem in [summand/CLOSURE.md](summand/CLOSURE.md)
identifies strict closure in a positive commutative monoid as the seed
set together with the ideal generated by pairs of distinct seeds.
Applied through (1), every finite additive seed theorem immediately
becomes a theorem about strict products of prime powers, with its full
generation schedule preserved.

For example, for coprime 2<=a<b, the inherited two-seed theorem says
that ab is the last missing exponent. Therefore the strict product
closure of `{p^a,p^b}` contains every p^m with m>ab but misses p^(ab).
This is a statement inside one prime-power ray, not an interval of all
integers. Starting with 4 and 8, the power 64 remains absent, while
every power of 2 from 128 onward is present.

There is an exact universal seed distinction as well:

* The unique minimal seed set generating all positive integers under
  strict addition is `{1,2}`. Both have no strict positive parent pair;
  once present, 3=1+2 and n+1=1+n generate everything else.
* The unique minimal seed set generating all integers >=2 under strict
  nonunit multiplication is `P union {p^2:p in P}`. These are exactly
  the strict atoms from THM-2422. Every other integer has a strict
  proper factor pair, so induction on numerical value proves sufficiency.

At integer cutoff N>=2 the latter set has exactly
`pi(N)+pi(floor(sqrt(N)))` members. This is a minimal-basis identity,
not a new estimate for the prime-counting function.

Starting only with all primes generates exactly the primes and the
integers having at least two distinct prime factors. No higher prime
power can be generated: producing a p-power requires all leaves of
its construction tree to be p, whereas a strict binary tree must begin
with two distinct live values. Every mixed-prime product is generated
by first multiplying two different prime factors and then adjoining
the remaining prime factors. The running product is larger than any
remaining prime after the two largest distinct factors are chosen first.

The first shadow-versus-event hostile is 8. Its strict arc 2->8 exists
because 2*4=8, but the companion 4 is unavailable from prime seeds.
Ordinary directed reachability would therefore give the wrong closure.

## 4. The exact observer conjugacy

For additive vertex weights f(a), a>=1, record the weight at a new node
plus the weights on its strict incoming arcs:

    g(n)=sum_(a<=n) f(a) - [2|n] f(n/2).                  (4)

For multiplicative weights f(d), d>=2, the corresponding observer is

    g(n)=sum_(d|n,d>=2) f(d) - [n square] f(sqrt(n)).     (5)

The identity term f(n) is included in both formulas. Formula (1)
conjugates (5), restricted to n=p^m, exactly to (4) at m. Thus the
operator identities

    G(z)=F(z)/(1-z)-F(z^2),
    G(s)=zeta(s)F(s)-F(2s)

are not just notational parallels. The second identity is interpreted
in formal Dirichlet series; analytic convergence is unnecessary.
The first lives in ordinary formal power series with zero constant term.
The unit n=1 is excluded from the second. Extending this unmodified
formula to n=1 gives a zero first diagonal, so compatible prefix
inversion fails. A unit-present loopless observer instead restores
g(1)=f(1) and has the corrected series identity
`G(s)=zeta(s)F(s)-F(2s)+f(1)`; that is a different convention.

The [additive inverse](summand/INVERSE.md) and
[multiplicative inverse](multiplicand/PROOF.md) use the same finite
binary sign product, evaluated respectively on an integer shift and
on the independent prime exponents. This explains both their shared
Thue-Morse signs and their different coefficient bounds:

    additive inverse entries:       -1,0,1;
    multiplicative inverse entries: -2,-1,0,1,2.

Two multiplicative correction scales can reinforce only for a
squarefree source with an even number of prime factors. On one prime
axis they cancel instead. In particular the multiplicative inverse
entry from d to d^2 is

    1+mu(d).                                              (6)

It is 0 at d prime, 2 at d=pq for distinct primes, and 1 when d is
not squarefree. This is a literal place where squarefree/non-squarefree
and prime/composite affect information reconstruction differently from
the simple square/non-square birth indicator.

More precisely, put

    kappa_j(a,e)=(-1)^popcount(e-2^j a)

when `0<=e-2^j a<2^(j+1)`, and put it equal to zero otherwise. Then

    K_add(a,e)=sum_j kappa_j(a,e),
    K_mult(d,n)=sum_j product_(p|dn) kappa_j(v_p(d),v_p(n)).

The multiplicative sum is finite for d>=2. The common scale j must be
retained until after the product across primes. For d=6 and n=36, each
prime exponent goes from 1 to 2. The two scales have signs -1 and +1,
so each separately summed additive inverse is zero, but the synchronized
product is `(-1)(-1)+(+1)(+1)=2`. This is the precise information lost by
replacing a coupled exponent vector with independent scalar reconstructions.

## 5. Finite prefixes already determine the infinite reconstruction

For either observer, let A_N be its matrix on the available prefix.
Order vertices numerically. A_N is lower triangular with diagonal 1;
the equal-parent subtraction always concerns an earlier vertex.
Adding N+1 adds one row and one diagonal entry, leaving the old block
untouched. If r is the new incoming row, then

    A_(N+1) = [[A_N,0],[r,1]],
    A_(N+1)^(-1) = [[A_N^(-1),0],[-r A_N^(-1),1]].        (7)

This proves an exact incremental reconstruction algorithm. It also
proves compatibility with every prefix projection. A compatible
sequence of observed prefixes has exactly one compatible sequence of
reconstructed prefixes, simply by the finite inverses. Thus both
observers are automorphisms of the product module of all vertex
weights with its prefix topology. No limiting analytic estimate, or
cohomological vanishing assertion, is needed for this conclusion.

The supplied papers motivate retaining transition maps and operator
relations rather than only limiting totals; their separate roles and
hypotheses are recorded in [papers/PROOF.md](papers/PROOF.md).
Here compatibility is proved by (7). Equality of a numerical limit
alone would not have supplied this reconstruction theorem.
