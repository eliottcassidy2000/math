# Eight bit deletion through a two twos child

**PROVED:** the guarded eight-bit common-future deletion, exact exponent
phase, ordinary-source progression, and same-parent inverse-child payment
below. **FINITE-EXACT:** the declared literal and modular controls.
**CONDITIONAL:** ROOT completion requires the final child's actual ROOT
certificate. **OPEN:** universal guard coverage and grounding every emitted
child. No computation here expands the astronomical symbolic Mersenne source.

This closes a specific missing-rule obligation: the previous deletion-budget
calculation requested seven bits for `G5^36` and `G17^64`, but did not supply
an adequate deletion receipt. A new two-twos head composes with an existing
parent head to provide eight bits on an explicit joint native guard.
The supplied parent and inverse child are retained throughout.

## Inheritance and the useful change of scale

The inherited [225-head decoder](collatz_twoanchor_head_decoder_20261007b.md)
and [phase compiler](collatz_general_head_phases_20261007b.md) provide a
four-bit receipt at head `(1,2,9)` with three twos. Its child has exactly two
twos. The corrected short-run relation in
[THM-4601, two-anchor reduction](../../01-canon/theorems/THM-4601-two-anchor-reduction-of-the-residual-first-reset-two-branch.md)
is `X=81Y+10`, not the long-run relation `X=27Y-26`.
The [fixed-parent budget](collatz_completion_paper24_23_20261007b.md) already
separates a sufficient numerical deletion depth from an authenticated rule.

The closest mechanism is composition of two actual common-future receipts.
The hostile is a valid child head on the wrong exponent phase: even the
two-twos head `(1,1,14)`, whose exponent is 13 modulo 16, misses every emitted
parent phase in the declared bank. The corrected near miss is to mistake
the seven-bit size inequality for a seven-bit Collatz certificate.
The least-used sidecar is the actual middle-source word and its clock.

The live concepts are the changed affine anchor, exact binary phase,
ordered middle word, source-growth cutoff, ternary inverse guard, and
terminal proof. The map sends two guarded receipts to one receipt at the
same source; it preserves actual paths and strict payment. Compressing to
the total eight-bit drop alone would lose legality and the child obligation.
Those remain explicit in the words and native guards.

## The new child head and its parent intersection

For a valuation word w put `F_w(x)=(3^len(w)x+B_w)/2^sum(w)`.
The new two-twos pair is

```text
u = (1,1,1,1,1,1,1,1,22)
v = (4,1,3,3,1,3,3,1,1,1,2,2,3).
```

It satisfies

\[
 F_v(Y)=4F_u(X)+1\quad\text{when }X=81Y+10.
\]

The source and partner costs are 30 and 28, with lengths 9 and 13.
Its exact Mersenne child phase is

\[
 E\equiv924745901\pmod{2^{32}}.
\]

Putting K=E+4 meets the inherited head `(1,2,9)` at J=3:
`K=32945 mod65536`. This is the only intersecting head of the declared
225-head bank. The source's first four-bit receipt has terminal valuation
16; the middle source's corresponding valuation is 22. Their actual words
align, with one further edge needed at the middle before the second join.
This alignment, rather than equality of aggregate costs, licenses composition.

## Direct eight bit identity and native guard

Define fixed heads

```text
a = (2,2,2,1,2,9,16)
b = (2,2,4,1,3,3,1,3,3,1,1,1,2,2,3).
```

They have lengths 7 and 15, and costs 34 and 32. Direct rational carrier
calculation gives

\[
 \boxed{F_b(y)=4F_a(x)+1\quad\text{if }x+1=3^8(y+1).}       \tag{1}
\]

The slope equality and `F_b(-1)=4F_a(-1)+1` independently verify the full
identity, including the carry. Write `F_a(x)=(Px+B)/Q`, Q=2^34, P=3^7.
Its native odd endpoint cylinder is

\[
 x\equiv r=(Q-B)P^{-1}\pmod{2Q}.                         \tag{2}
\]

This residue is 1 modulo 4. For `n=2^(e+1)t-1`, t positive odd, the first
e valuations are ones and the resulting state is `x=2*3^e*t-1`.
After deleting eight bits, the corresponding state y satisfies (1).
The exact fixed-run source family is therefore

\[
 t=t_0+2^{34}s,\quad
 t_0\equiv\frac{r+1}{2}3^{-e}\pmod{2^{34}},\quad s\ge0,    \tag{3}
\]

where the canonical t0 is positive odd. Require e>=68. Every prefix through
a has coefficient at least `3^e/2^(e+34)>1` relative to n, since already
`3^68/2^102=(9/8)^34>1`. Initial ones also grow, and all carries are
nonnegative. Thus every nonempty source prefix through a is above n.

Put `Z=F_a(x)` and read `c=v2(3Z+1)` at this actual source. Equation (1)
makes the child endpoint `4Z+1` positive odd. Final odd integrality forces
every earlier child letter to be exact: a nonintegral dyadic state cannot
regain integrality under an odd numerator divided by a power of two, and
an even intermediate would make the next state nonintegral. The child
cannot have met ROOT earlier, since its preterminal endpoint exceeds 1
and positive exact steps cannot leave ROOT.

The actual receipt is

\[
\begin{aligned}
 n&\xrightarrow{\ 1^e,a,c\ }z,\\
 h=(n+1)/256-1&\xrightarrow{\ 1^{e-8},b,c+2\ }z.
\end{aligned}                                            \tag{4}
\]

Here `1^e` denotes e repeated valuation letters. The two words have equal
length and cost difference eight. The child is positive odd and strictly
smaller than n. A final z=1 is allowed as the first ROOT hit; otherwise (4)
leaves the explicit obligation to ground h.

For the Mersenne subfamily t=1, (2) becomes a power-of-three congruence
modulo 2^34. Exact discrete logarithm in its cyclic subgroup gives

\[
 \boxed{K\equiv924745905\pmod{4294967296},\qquad K>0.}     \tag{5}
\]

The period is minimal because 3 has order 2^32 modulo 2^34. Its least
positive representative already exceeds the cutoff e=K-1>=68, so every
positive member of (5) is valid. Formula (4) then pays `M_K` through `M_(K-8)`.

## Payment for the original inverse child

Let W be a nonempty native word in the inherited inverse generators
`G1,G5,G17`, let m be the supplied parent, and h=G_W(m). The exact depth
request in the inherited budget package is

\[
 D_{\min}=\operatorname{bitlength}\left(\left\lfloor
          \frac{m+1}{h+1}\right\rfloor\right),\qquad
 D_{\max}=v_2(m+1)-1.
\]

If the uniform budget of W is at most eight and m satisfies (3), prepend
the authenticated inverse-return word `h -> m` to (4). Its new final child
`(m+1)/256-1` is strictly below h. The receipt is therefore paid against
the original inverse child, not merely against the larger parent.

Each generator obeys `G_i(m)+1 >= (2/3)(m+1)` on its positive native guard.
Consequently every word of length at most 13 fits eight bits, since
`3^13<2^21` gives `(3/2)^13<256`. Some much longer words fit too:
`G5^36` and `G17^64` each have exact uniform depth seven. This statement
does not assert that every word has a Mersenne-native phase.

When W has Mersenne phase `K=r_W mod 2*3^(B_W-1)`, intersect it with (5).
The generalized CRT retains both guards if their parities agree. The period
is `2^32*3^(B_W-1)`; no source integer needs to be materialized. The selected
words above have compatible phases. Their least joint exponent/period bit
lengths are respectively `(137,145)` and `(740,741)`. Ordinary fixed-run
parents on the same joint binary/ternary guards are modest enough for exact
literal transport tests. An empty inverse guard or excessive budget is
rejected rather than repaired by changing the supplied source.
