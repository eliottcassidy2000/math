### What relative deletion proves, exactly

Let $B$ be a fixed source subset in a finite graph. Remove an unmarked vertex
whenever its current degree is at most five, and retain $B$. Every proper
six-colouring of the remaining graph extends across this deletion: reinsert
vertices in reverse order and avoid the at most five colours already used
by their neighbours. Restriction gives the converse. Therefore the complete
boundary relation on $B$ is **identical** before and after deletion.

The final fixed-port core is independent of deletion order. Any set whose
unmarked vertices all have at least six neighbours inside that set cannot
lose its first vertex in a legal deletion. Hence every terminal core contains
every such set; this characterizes the unique largest one. It need not be a
minimal witness under arbitrary vertex or edge deletion.

The receipts include deletion orders of 252 vertices for $G_0$ and 242 for
$G_1$, plus the full induced surviving graphs on respectively 64 and 472
vertices. Every step is verified against the current graph. A second,
reverse-priority peeling procedure reaches the same final vertex set.

in both configurations before the new mixed-core edges are considered.
The computed obstruction counts above describe the consumer's selected
