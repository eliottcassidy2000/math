## 1. Exact deterministic orbit orientations

### 1.1 The carrier and its inherited covariance

Let N>=3 be odd and H an odd-order subgroup of `(Z/NZ)^*`, of order r, such
that every h other than1 has `gcd(h-1,N)=1`. Pair the nonzero H-orbits O
with -O. There are b=(N-1)/(2r) pairs. For each pair let B_i be the real
skew-circulant matrix with symbol +1 on O, -1 on -O, zero elsewhere.
All B_i commute. A sign choice gives the genuine tournament matrix

    K = sum_i epsilon_i B_i,       epsilon_i in {-1,1}.

The inherited exact identity is

    sum_i B_i^2 = J-NI.                                      (1)

In particular K annihilates constants. On each nonconstant Fourier mode
its eigenvalue is i times a real Rademacher sum with coefficient-square
sum exactly N. This includes imprimitive modes when N is composite.

### 1.2 Constructive logarithmic bound

**Theorem 1.** For every integer m>=1 there is a deterministic algorithm,
using only integer arithmetic, which chooses the orbit signs and gives

    ||K||^(2m) <= (N-1)(2m-1)!! N^m.                        (2)

The operation count and integer bit lengths are polynomial in N and m.
Taking m=ceil(log_2(N-1)) gives

    ||K|| <= 2 sqrt(N ceil(log_2(N-1))).                    (3)

Thus these orbit-constrained families have deterministic quasirandom
sequences as N grows, with the O(sqrt(N log N)) scale of the inherited
random existence result. This does not prove an O(sqrt N) bound.

**Proof.** Put `P_m(K)=tr((-K^2)^m)`. It is nonnegative and dominates
`||K||^(2m)`. A scalar Rademacher sum of variance N has even moment at
most the corresponding Gaussian moment `(2m-1)!! N^m`: in the multinomial
expansion only even multiplicities survive, every coefficient product is
nonnegative, and Gaussian even moments of each individual variable are
at least1. Apply this to each of the N-1 nonconstant Fourier modes.

Reveal one sign at a time, choosing the sign with the smaller exact
conditional expectation of P_m. The mean of the two possibilities is the
previous conditional expectation, so the final value cannot exceed the
initial one. No floating Fourier comparisons are used by the algorithm.

Here is an explicit polynomial implementation. Store circulant matrices
as N integer coefficients of a cyclic convolution. For the remaining
random sum Y retain `M_j=E(Y^j)`, 0<=j<=2m. Adding an unused packet B uses

    M'_j = sum_{ell even, ell<=j} binom(j,ell) B^ell M_(j-ell).

To remove B, solve this triangular equation for the remaining moments,
starting at j=0. For each prospective assigned sum X+B or X-B, compute

    (-1)^m sum_{j=0}^{2m} binom(2m,j) tr(X_candidate^(2m-j) M_j).

All products commute and all coefficients are integers. Dense cyclic
convolution, truncated powers and these triangular recurrences require
polynomially many operations. Entries of the actual moments are bounded
by powers of N; intermediate binomial expansions likewise have polynomial
bit length in N,m. Finally `(N-1)^(1/m)<=2` and `(2m-1)!!<=(2m)^m`
give (3). QED.

**Ports and pins.** Prescribe any subset of the orbit signs first and
reorder the packets so those are assigned first. The same algorithm gives
`P_m(K)<=E(P_m(K)|pins)`. It does NOT replace this conditional budget by
the unconditional bound (2). At N=19, pinning the first two signs both+
raises the fourth-moment budget from9234 to10146. Both completions have
the latter value. This is an exact hostile to free conditioning.

### 1.3 A faster fourth-moment implementation

For an assigned matrix X, let R=sum_unused B_i^2. Commutativity gives

    Phi(X)=tr(X^4)+6tr(X^2 R)+3tr(R^2)-2sum_unused tr(B_i^4). (4)

When B is the next packet, with R still including B^2,

    Phi(X+B)-Phi(X-B)=8tr(X^3B+3XBR-2XB^3).                 (5)

Initially `Phi=3N^2(N-1)-2sum_i tr(B_i^4)`. The script maintains X,
X^2 and R, using sparse packet convolution to decide the sign in (5).
For fixed r its arithmetic operation count is O(N^2 r), plus the final
independent O(N^2) autocorrelation check. It produces the simpler bound
`||K|| <= [3N^2(N-1)]^(1/4)`.

Exact unpinned trace-fourth results at N=7,13,19,43,133,1807 are
294,2652,6498,77658,3860724,10188736974. At1807 the initial budget is
17593389294, with301 orbit decisions. These are certificates for this
algorithm, not optimal norms. In particular the earlier prime-quotient
construction can be better at an individual N; no pointwise dominance
claim is made. The higher-moment implementation is checked through
N=43,m=6; the efficient quartic implementation is checked through1807.

**Remaining target.** Exploit arithmetic structure of the Fourier
coefficients to remove the logarithm while allowing a quantified pin
budget. Gaussian comparison alone retains a factor growing with m.
For arbitrary, noncommuting matrices (4) is not the correct expansion;
this carrier restriction is essential to the stated implementation.

### 1.4 An exact parity floor and a sharper norm certificate

For any regular tournament matrix K of odd size N, put C=KK^t and

    Delta=tr(K^4)-N^2(N-1).

Then `Delta=||C-(NI-J)||_F^2`. The diagonal terms vanish. For distinct
vertices x,y, regularity gives `C_xy=4a_xy-(N-2)`, where a_xy counts their
common out-neighbours. Hence

    Delta>=0 if N=3 mod4;
    Delta>=4N(N-1) if N=1 mod4.                            (5a)

In the second case each off-diagonal difference C_xy+1 is2 mod4 and
has square at least4. The constructed unpinned matrices attain these
universal regular-tournament floors at N=7,9,11,13,19,43. Thus their
fourth moments are globally optimal among regular tournaments of those
sizes, not just among the chosen orbit orientations. Equality at
N=3 mod4 gives `KK^t=NI-J` and exact norm sqrt(N).

The known second moment also improves every individual norm receipt:

    ||K||^2 <= N + sqrt(Delta (N-3)/(2(N-1))).              (5b)

For N>=5, the nonconstant eigenvalues of C form (N-1)/2 pairs. If one
paired value is N+t, their deviations sum to zero, and Cauchy–Schwarz
on the remaining pairs gives `Delta>=2t^2(N-1)/(N-3)`. This proves (5b).
At N=3 regularity forces Delta=0 and the same conclusion holds. We do
not infer a flat spectrum merely from its random-family expectation.

## 2. The exact local Poisson obstruction for cotangent observables

Let F in C[x] be nonlinear and

    A_F = C[x,p] intersect C(F(x),p/F'(x)).

These are precisely source-polynomial observables recovered by rational
target reencoding of the separated cotangent lift. The inherited result
says every element has zero differential at a critical zero-momentum
source. We now measure the entire bracket obstruction there.

**Theorem 2.** If c is critical and `e=ord_c(F-F(c))>=2`, then for every
A,B in A_F, their source Jacobian `{A,B}=A_x B_p-A_p B_x` belongs to

    ((x-c)^e, p^e, ((x-c)p)^(e-1)) in C[x,p].               (6)

It consequently vanishes to total order at least e at (c,0). For every
F and every such c, some A,B in A_F attain total order exactly e.

**Proof of the ideal bound.** Put t=x-c. In formal local coordinates
choose z=t times a unit with `F-F(c)=z^e`, and put `P=p/(dz/dx)`.
Then `dx wedge dp=dz wedge dP` and

    F-F(c)=z^e,       p/F'(x)=P/(e z^(e-1)).

The formal cyclic action `(z,P)->(zeta z,zeta^(-1)P)`, zeta^e=1, fixes
both target functions. Hence every regular germ coming from A_F is in
the invariant formal ring. Its monomials have i-j divisible by e, so
the ring is generated topologically by

    U=z^e, V=zP, W=P^e,           UW=V^e.

The canonical brackets are

    {U,V}=eU,       {V,W}=eW,       {U,W}=e^2 V^(e-1).      (7)

The Leibniz rule, followed by formal completion, places the bracket of
any two invariant germs in `(U,W,V^(e-1))`. Transforming back multiplies
each of the three generators in (6) by a unit depending only on t.
Membership in this monomial ideal for a polynomial is the same in its
formal completion and its polynomial ring (inspect its Taylor monomials).
This proves (6) for the actual source-polynomial Jacobian. QED.

**Sharpness.** Let D(u) be the squarefree polynomial whose roots are the
distinct critical values of F. Then F' divides D(F): at a critical
point of index e_i, their respective orders are e_i-1 and e_i. Therefore

    A=F(x),       B=D(F(x))p/F'(x)

are source polynomials in A_F, and `{A,B}=D(F(x))`. Since D has a simple
zero at F(c), this bracket has order exactly e at (c,0).

For the monomial F=x^e the complete source ring is
`C[x^e,xp,p^e]`; hence (6) is exactly its generated Poisson-bracket ideal.
For general F we claim a local embedding into the cyclic invariant ring,
NOT equality with every invariant germ: other sheets, including different
ramification indices above the same value, can impose more conditions.

**Composition refinement.** For F=G composed with H, local ramification
indices multiply: `e_F(c)=e_H(c)e_G(H(c))`. The bound therefore multiplies
at an actually shared ramified passage. It need not multiply with the
number of iterations: the quadratic maps with negative 2-adic parameter
in the inherited note have many critical points but index only2 at each.
This distinction retains the integer depth and the particular source.

**Consequences and boundary.** This strengthens the already proved
exclusion inside the cotangent carrier; it does not extend that exclusion
to arbitrary planar maps. An affine F admits a polynomial symplectic pair.
Allowing the unrecovered source coordinates x,p also gives bracket1.
Neither is a counterexample to (6); each changes a stated hypothesis.

### 2.1 Shared critical values: the ambient global ideal is computable exactly

Pursuing that last missing coordinate gives a stronger result. Let b run
through distinct critical values. Put

    E_b=max_{F(c)=b} ord_c(F-b),
    c_j(u)=product_b (u-b)^ceil(j(E_b-1)/E_b).

The same pole argument used in the inherited full-field theorem, now
with the maximum index over each value, gives the exact description

    A_F = { sum_{j>=0} a_j(F)(p/F')^j : c_j divides a_j }. (14)

Indeed a rational target function whose pullback is polynomial must first
be polynomial in the momentum variable over C(u): coprimeness of its
numerator and denominator survives the field extension C(u)->C(x).
A pole in any resulting coefficient at u=b remains a pole at a root
of F-b, and division by F' cannot cancel it. Thus its coefficients are
polynomials. At a sheet of index e, regularity is exactly
`e ord_b(a_j)>=j(e-1)`. Maximizing over that fibre gives (14).

Set L=lcm_b E_b. Since `c_(j+L)=c_j c_L`, (14) also supplies a finite
global presentation by the generators F and
`H_j=c_j(F)(p/F')^j`, 1<=j<=L. This is an exact compiler, not a search
over a degree cap chosen without proof.

Let J_F be the ideal of C[x,p] generated by **all** brackets {A,B},
A,B in A_F. This is the ambient ideal, not an assertion about its
minimal intrinsic generators as an ideal inside A_F.

**Theorem 2b.** For every root c of D(F), write e=ord_c(F-F(c)) and
E=E_(F(c)). Include unramified roots, where e=1. Define

    I_c=((x-c)^e, ((x-c)p)^(E-1), (x-c)^(E-e)p^E).

Then exactly

    J_F = intersection_{D(F(c))=0} I_c.                   (15)

For e=E this is the ideal (6). For e<E it simplifies to

    I_c=((x-c)^e, (x-c)^(E-e)p^E).                        (16)

In particular every recovered Jacobian is divisible by
`(x-c)^min(e,E-e)` when e<E. If E>=2e the entire local ideal is just
`((x-c)^e)`. A larger ramification index on a different sheet can force
vanishing on the whole vertical fibre, not only at p=0.

**Proof.** Localize (14) away from critical values other than b=F(c).
Writing U=u-b, the resulting ring is generated by

    U, V=Uv, W=U^(E-1)v^E,       V^E=UW,

with coefficients rational in u whose denominators do not vanish at b.
For j=aE+d, 0<=d<E, the minimum allowed monomial is W^a when d=0 and
W^a V^d when d>0; its U exponent is exactly ceil(j(E-1)/E).
Their canonical target brackets are U, W and E V^(E-1), respectively
for (U,V), (V,W), (U,W). Thus the localized bracket ideal is precisely
`(U,W,V^(E-1))`. Localizing brackets does not introduce a different
ideal: the quotient rule expresses brackets with denominators using
brackets of the original numerators and denominators.

On the actual source sheet, U, V, W are respectively units times
`(x-c)^e`, `(x-c)p`, `(x-c)^(E-e)p^E`. Their localized ambient ideal is
therefore I_c. Away from D(F)=0 it is the unit ideal, because
`{F,D(F)p/F'}=D(F)`. Moreover D(F) belongs to J_F. The factors
`(x-c)^e` of D(F) are pairwise comaximal, so the Chinese remainder
decomposition of C[x,p]/(D(F)) combines these localized equalities into
(15). The simplification (16) uses E-1>=e. QED.

The regular companion-line case e=1,E=2 was already proved geometrically
in [THM-3567, §3](../../../01-canon/theorems/THM-3567-separated-rational-keller-maximal-observable-nodal-completion-no-go.md).
The added result here is the exact ambient ideal, arbitrary local indices,
and shared-value propagation between differently ramified sheets. The
older contraction is an inherited special case, not a new discovery.

**Sharp shared-sheet example.** For `F=x^6(x-1)^2`, the value0 has index6
at x=0 and index2 at x=1. At x=1, every recovered Jacobian is divisible
by `(x-1)^2`, for *all* momenta. At x=0 the local ideal is
`(x^6,(xp)^5,p^6)`, with no common x factor. Counting the critical
value only once, or using only the index of the observed sheet, misses
this difference. The pair in the sharpness proof attains order2 at x=1.

**Remaining boundary.** This computes the complete ambient bracket
obstruction of this already excluded cotangent carrier. A planar-Jacobian
construction seeking a different outcome must change the recovered
source algebra, not merely add another rational target expression to it.

### 2.2 The completed map's exact etale locus

There is a further geometric consequence, closing the maximal-locus
question explicitly left open by the scope of THM-3567 §3. Let
`Phi:A^2->Spec(A_F)` be the polynomial map defined by all recovered
observables. Over a neighbourhood of the critical value b excluding the
other critical values, its target is precisely

    V^E=UW,        U=u-b,        E=E_b.

It has a single singular point U=V=W=0; the gradient criterion proves
this directly. Away from the critical values the target is the original
smooth (u,v) chart.

**Corollary.** The non-etale locus of Phi is exactly the following union:

* every whole vertical line x=c with F(c) critical and e_c<E_(F(c));
* the points (c,0) with e_c=E_(F(c)).

On a lower-index sheet the formulas in the proof of (15) send the whole
line to the singular point. On a maximal-index sheet, p=0 maps to that
point, while p!=0 has W!=0. There V,W are smooth target coordinates
(`U=V^E/W`), and their source Jacobian is W, which is nonzero. Outside
all critical-value fibres the original cotangent Jacobian is1. These
facts prove both directions. At the excluded points an etale morphism
from the regular source would force the target local ring to be regular,
contrary to the displayed hypersurface singularity.

Thus a completed observable map can be etale at a nonzero-momentum point
where the original rational cotangent formula had a pole. This says
something precise about the *completed map to its different target*;
it does not remove the original pole or turn that target into the plane.

## 3. Uniform prime-pattern laws with a growing named bank

Inherit the Session107 prime tournament: vertices are primes q<=X with
q=3 mod4, and `p->q` iff the Legendre symbol (p/q)=+1. Its signed matrix
has `B_X=||K|| <<_epsilon X^(1/2+epsilon)` by the cited quadratic large
sieve. Also inherit its multi-fibre bound: for uniformly chosen vertices
from nonempty fibres of sizes n_i, the absolute edge-pattern error is at
most `(B_X/2) sum_(i<j)(n_i n_j)^(-1/2)`, with the explicit collision
correction recorded there.

Allow the nonzero integer bank a_1,...,a_s to depend on X, including its
length. Let

    Q=8 product_{ell odd prime dividing some a_i} ell,

and let r be the rank over F_2 of its prime-exponent parity matrix with
the sign bit separated. The feasible character colours form the inherited
affine code. Fix a constant A>0 and suppose `Q <= (log X)^A`.

**Theorem 3.** Uniformly over all these banks and all their feasible
colours c,

    n_c = Li(X)/2^(r+1) * (1+O_{A,L}((log X)^(-L)))        (8)

for every fixed L>0. In particular every feasible colour is populated for
all sufficiently large X. Conditionally on any k fixed feasible colours
and on distinctness, each labelled k-vertex tournament has probability

    2^(-binom(k,2)) + O_{A,k,epsilon}(X^(-1/2+epsilon)
                                      (log X)^(A+1)).     (9)

The constants and the onset inherited from Siegel–Walfisz are not asserted
effective. This allows an unbounded number of ports; it does not cover
arbitrarily fast conductor growth or thin recursively chosen fibres.

**Analytic input (CITED).** Siegel–Walfisz gives, for fixed A,B>0,

    pi(X;Q,a)=Li(X)/phi(Q)+O_{A,B}(X/(log X)^B),

uniformly for Q<=(log X)^A and (a,Q)=1. A primary published source is
Thorner–Zaman, *Refinements to the prime number theorem for arithmetic
progressions*, Math. Z.306 (2024), paper54,
[§1.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2108.10878v2#S1.SS2), following Theorem1.1.
Their log-weighted statement gives this form by partial summation; a
short initial range is absorbed by increasing the logarithmic exponent.
We do not use GRH or a claimed effective Siegel constant.

**Proof.** Exact CRT counting gives `phi(Q)/2^(r+1)` reduced classes for
each feasible colour, among those3 mod4. Sum the displayed prime-counting
estimate over exactly those classes. The relative error is at most
`O(phi(Q) log X/(log X)^B)`, independent of r. Choosing B>A+L+1 proves
(8). Every prime divisor of a bank entry divides Q and is already excluded
by reducedness. Square factors, repeated labels and negative labels obey
the same affine-code relations; there is no extra independence assumption.

There are at most phi(Q)/2 colours, so (8) gives
`n_min >> X/(log X)^(A+1)`. Substitute this bound into the inherited
multi-fibre estimate. The collision probability is at most
`binom(k,2)/n_min`; division by one minus that bound is harmless for fixed
k and sufficiently large X. This proves (9). QED.

For growing k the same explicit error formula remains valid while its
right side and the collision bound tend to zero. That is *absolute*
error: it does not prove relative asymptotics or existence of every
pattern when `2^(-binom(k,2))` is smaller than the error.

**What changed.** The previous theorem held for each fixed bank separately.
This version quantifies simultaneously over a growing bank and all its
feasible colours. Taking successively longer prime banks subject to the
displayed conductor cap is a concrete unbounded example. The cost is
controlled by the arithmetic conductor and rank, not by the raw number
of redundant named labels. Root-root edges stay fixed. Only roots3 mod4
are themselves vertices of this tournament; other integers remain probes.

**Remaining target.** Quantify a larger conductor window, retaining any
exceptional real-character term instead of erasing it. The growing-bank
law does not make the sparse Sylvester subsequence quasirandom.

## 4. Signed Abel residuals with monotone native refinement

Fix the genuine killed deterministic operator Q from Session106, with
active set Y, terminal set H, and original summable positive source law w.
For 0<z<1 put `a_z(x)=1-z^tau(x)`, with `z^infinity=0`, and a_z=0 on H.
It satisfies, on Y,

    a_z(x)=(1-z)+z a_z(Tx).                                (10)

The source-owned Abel quantity is `D_z=<w,a_z>`. It is defined with the
actual native integer clock, not a refreshed residue representative.

**Theorem 4 (signed residual certificate).** Let v be any explicitly
given summable signed function on Y and set

    e=w+zQv-v.

Suppose proved bounds 0<=l<=a_z<=u<=1 are available (take l=u=0 on H).
Then

    D_z=(1-z)sum_y v(y)+<e,a_z>,                           (11)
    D_z <= B_z(v):=(1-z)sum_y v(y)
                    +sum_y e_+(y)u(y)-sum_y e_-(y)l(y).  (12)

All series converge absolutely. The reversed bound substitutes l for u
on positive residual and u for l on negative residual. In particular
`D_z <= (1-z)sum v + ||e_+||_1` needs no unknown hitting times. A verified
upper bound B_z(v)<w(x) certifies termination of the *original* source x.
The sharper finite-time atom test from Session106 remains valid.

**Proof.** Pair (10) with v. Since Q is killed on H,
`<a_z,(I-zQ)v>=(1-z)sum v`. Substitute `w=(I-zQ)v+e`, then apply the
two-sided pointwise bounds separately to e_+ and e_-. This proves all
claims including preservation of negative residual credit. QED.

**Monotone paid refinement.** Suppose also

    u(x)>=(1-z)+z u(Tx) for every active x.                (13)

At a source with e(x)>0, choose 0<=b<=e(x) and set v'=v+b delta_x.
This consumes b units of that residual and sends zb units along the
actual next edge, except when that edge is terminal. Then

    B_z(v') <= B_z(v).

Indeed the function `f_y(t)=t_+u(y)-t_-l(y)` is nondecreasing, with slopes
l(y),u(y). Removing b from positive e(x) changes it by exactly -bu(x);
adding zb at Tx changes it by at most zbu(Tx). The change in the v term
is (1-z)b, so (13) proves the claim. This also covers a self-loop by
performing the subtraction then the addition at the same coordinate.
No sign of the residual is silently reset during the operation.

**Usable authenticated bounds.** A proved native survival prefix of
length k gives `l(x)=1-z^k`. A proved terminal arrival by L gives
`u(x)=1-z^L`. Combining such bounds globally requires (13) to be checked
for the monotonicity assertion; arbitrary individually correct upper
bounds do not automatically satisfy that extra inequality. The coarse
choice u=1 on Y always does. Known exact lifetimes satisfy equality.

**Why this is not yet universal entry.** For a fixed point b, its genuine
value a_z(b)=1. With w=delta_b, every residual certificate is at least1;
finite refinements with the coarse bounds never cross that atom. The
same obstruction survives if a finite quotient incorrectly averages b
with a terminating state. One still needs an independent arithmetic
family v_z and certified residual/tail estimates making (12) small.

The next target is now more permissive than an everywhere-positive
supersolution: allow a small positive defect, and retain negative defect
where a lower lifetime bound earns it. The required tail estimate remains
OPEN. No new previously uncertified Collatz source is claimed in this
packet, and simulated finite prefixes are not used as a universal bound.
