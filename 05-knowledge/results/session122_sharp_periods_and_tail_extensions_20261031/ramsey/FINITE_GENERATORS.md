# A finite-generator theorem and explicit threshold algorithm

2026-10-31. **PROVED, scoped.** This replaces the general Presburger step in
session121's effectiveness proof by an explicit recursive finite construction.
It concerns only the singleton/join/union/full-nonempty-C5 class D.

Use F,w,L as in `DYADIC_PERIOD.md`. The base row is F(1,B)=B, with N_1=1.
For each smaller clique budget a<A, suppose its exact finite prefix and
L(a)-periodic affine tail are known, valid for all B>=N_a.
All arithmetic below is exact rational/integer arithmetic.

## Theorem: every row is a finite unbounded-knapsack closure

An explicitly computable integer M_A exists such that, for every B>=1,

    F(A,B) = max { sum_j F(A,b_j) :
                  1<=b_j<=M_A, sum_j b_j=B }.              (1)

An empty sum is allowed only for B=0. Thus the entire infinite capacity row
is generated by a finite prefix under actual disjoint union.

Here is an explicit sufficient M_A. Set s=w(A), L=L(A), and

    D=(s-A)(L-1).

For each x=1,...,A-1 include the join cutoff

    floor(D/[s-w(x)-w(A-x)]).

List every coordinatewise maximal positive C5 clique-budget pattern as in
`ROWS_5_TO_8.md`. In constraint-cycle order put c_i=w(a_i), and define the
full polytope vertex maximum

    m=max(0, max_i c_i, max_i(c_i+c_(i+2)), (sum_i c_i)/2).

If (sum c_i)/2<s, include floor(D/(s-m)). The denominator is strictly
positive by strict superadditivity and positivity of every child.
If (sum c_i)/2=s, let y_i+y_(i-1)=c_i be the unique cycle dual. Every y_i
is strictly positive, as proved below. Set

    S=floor(D/min_i y_i),   N=max_i N_(a_i),

and include the sharp-root cutoff

    2N+S+2L-1.

Take M_A to be the maximum of all these cutoffs, L, and 1. For A=1 one can
take M_1=1 directly. This is a terminating recursion in A, not a hidden
call to an unknown threshold at the same clique budget.

### Proof

Sharp L-attainers and r disjoint copies of K_A give, for B=tL+r,

    F(A,B)>=stL+Ar,   0<=r<L.

Consequently 0<=sB-F(A,B)<=D uniformly. A maximizing graph has actual
independence number B: otherwise adding a singleton increases both order
and independence by one, stays within the budget, and contradicts maximality.
Flatten its top-level disjoint unions into connected constituents. Joins
and full nonempty C5 substitutions are connected, so these constituents
have roots of those types or are singletons. If one has actual independence
b and order n, its deficit sb-n is nonnegative. The deficits sum to the
whole graph's deficit, hence each is <=D.

A join has n<=(w(x)+w(A-x))b for some x in1,...,A-1, enlarging the actual
child clique budgets if necessary. Thus b is at most its listed cutoff.
At a C5 root, enlarge actual child clique budgets to a coordinatewise
maximal feasible pattern. Keep the actual child independence numbers b_i.
A child can be replaced by an F(a_i,b_i)-attainer, and that attainer has
actual independence b_i by the singleton argument above. Such replacements
preserve the root's actual independence b and its clique upper bound A.
They cannot increase order in a globally maximizing graph. Therefore the
root order is exactly sum_i F(a_i,b_i).

A nonsharp pattern satisfies n<=mb, so b is bounded by its listed cutoff.
For a sharp pattern the all-half polytope vertex is the unique maximizer:
all other vertices are strictly suboptimal by `DYADIC_PERIOD.md`. The
five active edge inequalities at that vertex are independent. The unique
dual coefficients are therefore strictly positive. Explicitly, if a dual
y_i were negative or zero, relaxing only that edge equation by a small
positive slack (holding the other four tight) yields a feasible nearby
point, because the original coordinates are all positive. The objective
would increase or stay equal, contradicting maximality or uniqueness.

Write sigma_i=b-b_i-b_(i+1)>=0. The dual identity and child slope bound give

    sum_i y_i sigma_i <= sb-n <= D,
    sum_i sigma_i <= S.

The invertible odd-cycle equations give 2b_i=b-t_i, where t_i is an
alternating sum of the five slacks, and |t_i|<=S. If b exceeds
2N+S+2L-1, choose the smallest b' congruent to b modulo2L with
b'>=2N+S. Then

    2N+S <= b' <= 2N+S+2L-1 < b,
    b'_i=(b'-t_i)/2 >= N.

The b'_i are positive integers with exactly the same slacks. The actual
root independence is b': at least one original edge slack was zero,
because b was the actual maximum of the five adjacent port sums.
Each child period L(a_i) divides L, since a_i<A and all L-values are powers
of two. Hence lowering every port by (b-b')/2, a multiple of L, within its
certified tail decreases the root order by exactly

    ((b-b')/2) sum_i w(a_i) = s(b-b').

Build this smaller C5 root using the child attainers, and append
(b-b')/L sharp L-blocks by disjoint union. Order and independence are
preserved exactly, with clique number at most A. Both the reduced component
and the appended blocks have independence at most M_A.

Apply this reduction to every large connected constituent of the maximizing
graph. Each resulting graph piece has independence <=M_A. Its order is at
most the corresponding F(A,b_j); replacing pieces by those attainers cannot
exceed the original maximum and proves the upper direction in (1).
The reverse direction is actual disjoint union. This proves the theorem.

## Explicit residue shortest paths and a quantitative threshold

Compute the exact finite prefix f_b=F(A,b), 1<=b<=M_A, by the finite
construction recurrence: joins and C5 roots call only smaller clique rows;
unions call the current row only at smaller independence budgets. Therefore
this prefix is effectively available by induction.

Write s=p/q in lowest terms. On residues modulo L, create, for each
b=1,...,M_A, an edge r -> r+b modL with nonnegative integer cost

    e_b=pb-qf_b,

and secondary positive weight b. Let (E_r,t_r) be the lexicographically
least (total cost,total weight) of a path from residue0 to r; the empty path
serves residue0. All residues are reachable because b=1 is available.
A lexicographically shortest path never repeats a vertex: removing a cycle
with positive cost reduces primary cost; removing a zero-cost cycle reduces
secondary weight. Thus

    t_r <= (L-1)M_A.

The sharp coin b=L has cost0. Equation(1) therefore gives, for every
B>=t_(B modL),

    F(A,B)=(pB-E_(B modL))/q.                               (2)

Indeed the shortest path realizes its residue at weight t_r, and zero-cost
L-coins fill the remaining weight; no coin combination has smaller cost.
Taking N_A=max(1,max_r t_r) proves the explicit threshold bound

    N_A <= max(1,(L(A)-1)M_A).

A finite scan of B<N_A against the known tail computes the least threshold
for period L. The least period itself is exactly L by `DYADIC_PERIOD.md`.
This gives a complete recursive algorithm for every row: finite prefix,
finite residue graph, shortest paths, and finite exceptions. No Presburger
quantifier elimination or unbounded search for an unseen stabilization point
is required. It is an effectiveness and quantitative-bound result, not an
assertion of polynomial time or small practical complexity.

## Implementation scope

`check_generators.py` instantiates these analytic bounds and residue shortest
paths for rows2..8 using their independently certified exact row oracles.
It checks prefix coin closure, residue penalties, path weights, and least
threshold1 in each of these rows. It does not implement an optimized arbitrary-A
prefix compiler, and finite controls do not replace the all-A proof above.

Reproduce alongside `check_rows.py`:

    python3 -B check_generators.py > generators.normal.json
    python3 -B -O check_generators.py > generators.optimized.json
    cmp generators.normal.json generators.optimized.json

The computed sufficient generator cutoffs for A=2,...,8 are respectively
6,15,14,55,74,49,35. The shortest-path sufficient thresholds are
1,3,3,7,7,7,7; a finite failure scan gives the least threshold1 in all seven
rows. These are sufficient generator cutoffs, not asserted minimal ones.

The hostile coin systems (length,order)={(1,1),(2,4),(M,2M)} for odd
M=3,11,101 have slope2, a sharp length2 coin, and deficit at most1, but their
odd residue first reaches zero deficit only at length M. Omitting that late
coin or applying its eventual residue cost below its first realizable length
gives a false answer. The checker reconstructs the actual shortest-path
length M and detects both losses. Thus bounded deficit alone does not imply
a short generator prefix; the connected-root/slack proof is essential.
