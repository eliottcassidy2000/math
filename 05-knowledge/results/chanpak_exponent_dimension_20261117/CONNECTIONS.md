# What is an exponent counting?

The common operation is **choose coordinates, impose compatibility, then
read**. There is no universal identification of exponent and dimension.

* A binary address of length k is a k-dimensional choice space.
  Fixing a tournament reflection identifies arc signs in orbits;
  adding a second reflection changes the orbit partition and therefore
  the dimension. In the associated count a codimension d really does
  cost a factor 2^d. In contrast, replacing a Hamiltonian path by
  a closed Hamiltonian frame fixes only one additional bit, but the
  count response is controlled by *which* even runs meet it, not by
  subtracting one from the count exponent.
* A path component of even size in edges contributes two traversals to
  the Fourier averaging proof. The exponent of 2 counts components,
  not all forced arcs. This is the same caution as the October
  [Boolean Fourier repair](../eulerian-boolean-fourier-repair-overnight-hexagon-sep05.md):
  a weighted null mode is not a literal Boolean kernel element without
  the crossblock compatibility. Here the exact forest support is what
  makes conditioning on an extra arc computable.
* The [Collatz source-atom study](../algorithmic_collatz_measure_20261005.md)
  distinguishes the uniform mass 2^-L of a binary cylinder from the
  4^-L tail of a certain atomic measure plus a possible exceptional
  representative; they are different views of the same address.
  The [three-DAG study](../collatz_posets_dags_zeta5_20260927.md) makes
  a similar separation between parity-word length, denominator
  exponent and the integer reachability order. For Chan–Pak, modular
  address coverage is the cylinder view, not a physical witness at
  the integer itself.
* The [square-sum Berggren cone](../ap_square_pythagorean_20261019.md)
  distinguishes thread address length from ordered size: its entry
  criterion is on the transformed parameters, and a uniform depth
  prior gives a different distribution from counting by height.
  The prior November 17 bridge imports this distinction into
  tournament migration: disjoint inverse chambers give a unique
  address in a skinny image, not coverage of the whole target axis.
  A variable-address apex has a different read, more like a
  bilinear coordinate chart than a three-branch recursion.
* The [ternary Smith double-pair law](../overnight2_20260906_smith_double_pair.md)
  has constant module rank eight but *varying* valuations of minors,
  including min-of-two walls. The independent count and the precision
  at which one can access that count are different invariants. In the
  metagraph, the analogous honest datum is the orbit rank **and** the
  consistency sign of any prescribed frame. Counting tile orbits
  alone is not enough to decide whether the frame occurs.
* The planar Jacobian [4+4 boundary analysis](../planar_jc48_sep08_four_four.md)
  works in a coefficient space with fixed pole multiplicities, but
  distinguishes its actual source chart and weighted differential
  from the degree-only record on the compactified fibre. This is
  an analogy to preserving the literal incoming/outgoing contacts,
  not an implication between the conjectures. The [lonely runner
  parity audit](../overnight3_20260906_lrc_consumers.md) gives another
  warning: a global Haar floor becomes useful only with the right
  tail domain and parity; an average floor does not imply the
  appropriate absolute witness.

The constructive side has a direct arithmetic trade: two bit addresses
produce a quadratic product from a linear number of vertices, and
a coordinated exchange can change that product's read by a small
prescribed signed amount. The same operation does not make the
baseline freely selectable. The next proof target is a **second
independent physical coordinate**: vary height while preserving a
small signed increment, with a certified inverse on every target in
the resulting interval. Having arbitrarily many exact gaps, a
positive mean drift, or exponentially many realizations will not
substitute for this.
