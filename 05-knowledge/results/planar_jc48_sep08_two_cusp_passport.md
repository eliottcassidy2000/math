## 1. Statement, inheritance, and retained data

Suppose a nonautomorphic complex planar polynomial Keller map has whole
nonproperness set one irreducible curve with normalization `A1`, exactly
two ordinary `(2,3)` cusps, and `N>=2` ordinary nodes, with no other
singularities. Write `d>1` for its mapping degree, `a` for the actual
generic affine fibre count along the smooth curve, `n_1,n_2` for its
actual cusp fibre counts, and `omega_p` for its deleted-complement
overlap at each node. Then

    d in {2a-1,2a},                 every omega_p=0.       (1)

More precisely, the possibilities left by this argument are

| d | a | Unordered cusp counts | Actual fibre at every node |
|---|---|---|---|
| `4k`, `k>=1` | `2k` | `{k,k+1}` | 0 |
| `4k+1`, `k>=1` | `2k+1` | `{k+1,k+1}` | 1 |
| `4k+2`, `k>=0` | `2k+1` | `{k+1,k+1}` | 0 |

The formal degree-two row is removed by the inherited degree-two theorem
or the deleted-length-one purity argument. In the first two rows every
nontrivial positive-meridian cycle is even and there are no deleted
inertia-fixed letters. We do not assert that every remaining row occurs.

If the actual affine complement is generated by at most three positive
curve meridians, then the inherited degree-two/three/four exclusions and
the present local argument give `d>=6`. At `d=6`, necessarily

    a=3, n_1=n_2=2, omega_p=0,
    meridian cycle type (3,1,1,1), global image A6.          (2)

There is an explicit common finite-group passport satisfying all these
local, Euler, node and three-generator conditions. Thus these conditions
alone do not exclude the six-sheet row. It is not a representation of
the actual curve complement unless its global access relations are paid.

## 2. An ordinary cusp gives an injection in every degree

Use left permutation actions and rightmost-first composition. At either
cusp, the actual re-access theorem supplies

    sigma tau sigma=tau sigma tau,     g=sigma tau,
    A subset Fix(sigma), B=gA subset Fix(tau),
    |A|=|B|=a,              n=|A intersect B|.              (3)

The last equality is geometric: a common label is fixed by the two
generators of the whole local group. Its degree-one normal extension is
the local target; the generic page is retained, and pure deleted
boundary prevents deleting just the cusp origin. Conversely an actual
cusp point has an etale inverse and gives such a common label. This
argument works for any n and does not require local transitivity.

Put `I=A intersect B`, `U=A\B`, `V=B\A`, and
`O=Omega\(A union B)`. Let `k=a-n`, `h=d-2a+n`.
The permutation tau fixes I and V, hence permutes U union O. If
`x in U` and `tau x in U`, both x and tau x are fixed by sigma.
The braid relation at x says `tau x=tau^2 x`, and therefore `tau x=x`.
Then g fixes x, so `x in A` implies `x in gA=B`, a contradiction.
Consequently

    tau U subset O,        k<=h,        2n>=3a-d.          (4)

This is a degree-uniform local restriction. No assumption `h<=1` or
`n<=1` is used.

There is a useful equality consequence. If k=h, then tau exchanges U
and O. At x in U, with `y=tau x in O`, the braid relation gives
`sigma y=tau sigma y`; thus sigma y is fixed by tau. It lies in V union O,
and tau has no fixed point in O. Therefore sigma O=V and sigma V=O.
Hence

    k=h => Fix(sigma)=A,
           every nontrivial sigma-cycle has even length. (5)

This includes k=h=0. It does not force transpositions: the six-label
control with O={0,1}, U={2,3}, V={4,5} and

    sigma=(0,4,1,5),       tau=(0,2,1,3)

satisfies every local equation with k=h=2. Its nontrivial cycles have
length four. Only the k=h=1 case forces a single transposition.

## 3. Node parity is an additional actual-sheet consequence

All positive meridians of the irreducible curve are conjugate globally.
If equality (5) holds at even one cusp, their fixed-letter count is
exactly a. At a node, each actual retained set is contained in its
meridian fixed set and has size a; it is therefore the entire fixed set.
The node's deleted sets are exactly the two permutation supports.

The two local node meridians commute. Their support intersection is
invariant under either permutation, and hence is a disjoint union of
nontrivial cycles of the first meridian. By (5) each such cycle has even
length. Thus

    equality at one cusp => every node omega_p is even.   (6)

Without the equality sidecar, deleted fixed letters can occur and this
parity conclusion is unjustified.

## 4. Euler, the N=2 scalar boundary, and the exact table

Let `W=sum_p omega_p` and `q=d-2a`. The actual fibre at a node is
`2a-d+omega_p`, where

    omega_p>=max(0,q).

The smooth curve stratum has Euler characteristic `-1-2N`, and the
ambient complement has characteristic N. Thus actual fibre integration
gives

    1=dN+a(-1-2N)+sum_p(2a-d+omega_p)+n_1+n_2
     =-a+n_1+n_2+W.                                    (7)

Adding (4) for the two cusps gives `n_1+n_2>=3a-d=a-q`.
Consequently

    N max(q,0)<=W<=q+1,   q>=-1.                         (8)

For N>=3, this already gives q in {-1,0}. At N=2 the scalar
inequalities still allow q=1. It must not be deleted without another
argument. In that case (8) forces W=2 and both overlaps equal one.
Both cusp inequalities are equalities, so (6) makes each overlap even,
a contradiction. This pays the extension from N>=3 to N>=2.
The explicit scalar hostile d=3,a=1,n_1=n_2=0 and overlaps(1,1)
passes (4), (7) and (8); it fails precisely this commuting-node test.

If q=-1, then W=0 and each cusp has `2n=a+1`. Thus a is odd,
giving the `d=4k+1` row and equality in (5) at both cusps.
If q=0 and a is odd, the integer lower bounds force W=0 and
`n_1=n_2=(a+1)/2`, giving `d=4k+2`.
If q=0 and a is even, there are initially two numerical possibilities:
W=1 with `n_1=n_2=a/2`, or W=0 with cusp counts `a/2,a/2+1`.
The W=1 case attains (5) at both cusps, so (6) excludes it.
The W=0 case attains (5) at one cusp and gives `d=4k`.
This proves (1) and the full displayed table. At every node the actual
count is consequently zero or one exactly as stated.

## 5. Three meridians: the minimum and exact abstract survivors

For a transitive action on d>1 labels generated by r permutations,
replace each nontrivial cycle by a tree on its support. The resulting
union graph is connected, so the total number of edges is at least d-1.
In particular three transpositions cannot act transitively on five or
more labels. This is the actual moved-label bound, not a claim that
absence of a common fixed label implies transitivity.

The named abstract degree-four control is

    d=4, a=2, cusp counts(1,2), every omega=0;
    global generators (12),(23),(34), generating S4;
    cusp1 pair (12),(23); cusp2 pair (12),(12);
    at every node use (12),(34).

Take each retained set to be the relevant full fixed set and its
re-accessed image. The first cusp intersection is {4}, the second is
{3,4}; at every node the supports are disjoint and the actual fibre is
empty. All local equations, Euler and three-generator transitivity hold.
This is why the cited degree-four exclusion remains load-bearing.

After the inherited exclusions in mapping degrees two through four,
the next numerical possibility is d=5, a=3, n_1=n_2=2. Equality (5)
has k=h=1, so every positive meridian is a transposition. Three such
generators cannot be transitive on five labels. Therefore d>=6.

At d=6 the table gives (2) except for the cycle type. A nontrivial
meridian fixes at least three labels, so it is either a transposition
or a single three-cycle. Three transpositions cannot be transitive.
Thus it is a three-cycle, its three fixed labels are all actual, and
the transitive group generated by these individual three-cycles is A6,
by the connected-support lemma already proved in the
[alternating passport](planar_jc48_sep06_alternating.md).

An exact six-label control is

    g1=(123), g2=(134), g3=(456), generating A6;
    cusp1 pair (123),(134), with intersection {5,6};
    cusp2 pair (456),(463), with intersection {1,2};
    every node pair (123),(456), with omega=0.

All displayed local meridians are conjugate within that same A6.
The actual retained sets are their full fixed sets. For example the
first cusp has A={4,5,6} and (sigma tau)A={2,5,6}. The complete Euler
check is `-3+2+2=1`, independently of the number of nodes. This passport
is compatible with any N>=2 and uses three positive-type generators.
It deliberately omits the actual curve's simultaneous access paths and
their global relations; it is not a homomorphism from an unspecified
affine complement group.
