supplies the missing transition between two different observers:

1. For a single new selectively attached vertex z, retain the induced-
   subset endpoint counts. Its odd-cycle decomposition computes the
   complete polynomial of the enlarged core C+z exactly.
2. Absorb z into that core. For every later **uniform** test modulo27,
   retain just `D_k=(k−1)! C_k` for k=1,...,5. All k>=6 vanish modulo27,
   and alternating separators prove these five coordinates are complete.

This is a valid finite observation procedure after the physical move.
The old-core one-selective-port cutoff is k<=6, whereas the enlarged-core
uniform cutoff is k<=5: their runs are counted relative to different
vertex sets. There is no incompatible cutoff.

The five-coordinate quotient alone cannot perform the selective move.

but the same named selective vertex gives counts9 and5. Thus the
endpoint/subset sidecar must be used before compression. Repeating
arbitrary selective additions requires retaining that richer state again.
Neither the quotient nor its finite size supplies an unbounded exact
positive target constructor.

This separation is suggested by the older Rule30 signalizer and
lonely-runner exit-observability work cited in the incoming bridge: a
small observation suite can be complete for one move language and fail
after enlarging the language. Here the cheap decisive test is an actual
pair of graphs with equal profiles and unequal selective responses.
