## What the repeated three-way split preserves

The odd-square bracket asks for a prime \(p\) and a larger multiple in the same interval between consecutive odd squares. It suffices to test \(2p\), and the complete prime list is \(2,3,11\). The divisor identity counts nontrivial proper divisors, prime ones, and squarefree ones as \(F,U,S\). Its solutions \(p,p^3,p^2qr\) provide a different ternary partition. The numbers 7 and 21 already distinguish them. Both appear as \(2\cdot3+1\) and \(2\cdot11-1\) from the square-bracket primes, while only 7 satisfies the divisor identity. [Bracket theorem](../procgen_numerology_20260923_odd_square_brackets.md) · [Divisor classification](../trijection_reset_20261020/arithmetic.md)

**PROVED reduction.** There is an exact role for divisor balance in Chan–Pak. The conjecture is equivalent to requiring, for each odd prime \(p\ne3,7\), a tournament of height \(21p^2\) with exactly three nonsingleton strongly connected components. Four prime factors must be divided among three component heights. The forbidden single 7 and the forbidden pair 21 force the multiset \(\{3,p,7p\}\). The three choices of squared prime on support \(\{3,7,p\}\) in turn detect a \(7p\) core, a \(p\) core, or both. Such three-component witnesses have no one-arc neighbor of coprime height. This explains why ordinal sums suffice for the equivalence while the literal unit forks above need a different construction. Existence for every eligible prime remains open. [Proof, class polynomials and edit costs](DIVISOR-TYPES.md)

**PROVED with finite-exact seed input.** For odd \(h\), the three children \(3h-2,3h,3h+2\) append the digits 0, 1, 2 to \((h-1)/2\) in base three. Use the same word for the Berggren tree of primitive triangles. The leading ternary digit can be either 1 or 2 at the root and is then lost, giving precisely two labels per triangle. On legs \((a,b)\) the three signed child gaps are \(a+b,-(a-b),-(a+b)\). The two outer children of \((3,4,5)\) create gap 7, and the middle branch triples the label while preserving the absolute gap. The entire gap-seven sector is \(3^j\{7,11,13,17\}\), \(j\ge0\). The missing counts 7 and 21 share triangles with attained counts 13 and 39, respectively. Thus \(2\cdot3+1=7\) occurs as the first new gap, while \(2\cdot11-1=21\) occurs as its tripled label. A primitive leg difference is odd with all prime factors 1 or 7 modulo eight, so none of the three bracket primes is itself such a difference. Keeping the leading digit is essential to predicting realizability. All allowed labels on the 594 tripling rays with leg difference at most 1,200 now have witnesses. ## What a Collatz edge contributes to a tournament

Give every positive integer \(u\) the fixed label \(b(u)=v_2(3\operatorname{oddpart}(u)-1)\). An odd-origin Collatz step changes the observed label to that of the next odd integer, and intervening halvings keep the label fixed. Any prescribed tournament on \(n\) distinct labels can be represented by choosing \(\binom n2\) such actual directed edges, even among starts that eventually reach 1. The adjacency test still matters. For labels summing to \(A\), exactly \(H(T)\) odd seed classes modulo \(2^{A+1}\) see a Hamilton path of \(T\) in their first \(n\) odd steps. The proof also applies to the plus sign. Conditioning on the labels forming a permutation gives probability \(H(T)/n!\). [Bucket realization, minimum number of trails and positive-cycle bound](BUCKETS.md)

For the negative sign, written on positive magnitudes, 7 belongs to the cycle through 5, while 21 runs through \(31,23,17\) in accelerated odd time. An odd iterate can land on 7 and can never land on 21, since every odd output is prime to three. Those properties distinguish many integers and do not characterize the two missing tournament heights. There is a literal local connection: reversing one arc changes a four-vertex height from 3 to 5 and switches the valuation orders \((1,7,3,2)\) and \((7,1,3,2)\). The seeds 541 and 555 meet at 19 and continue through 7. Their difference \(14=21-7=(2^7-2)/9\) is forced by exchanging the two valuations. [Common-terminal transposition theorem](BUCKET-INTERCHANGE.md)

The modular distinction is explicit. For a fixed sequence of \(L\) arithmetic child choices, half-indices transform as \(k\mapsto3^L k+D\) for a fixed \(0\le D<3^L\). This permutes residues modulo any integer prime to three, including every power of two. Modulo \(3^j\) it forgets the initial residue once \(L\ge j\). For an actual odd Collatz step of valuation \(b\), division by \(2^b\) instead uses the inverse of two at every odd modulus. To know its output modulo \(2^j\) requires the input modulo \(2^{b+j}\) when \(b\) is fixed. These two different losses of information explain why the powers of two in the seed formula coexist with powers of three in the common-terminal test.
