# Every prime power can pass while two supersingular primes together fail

**PROVED.** There are two explicit Farey constructions, indexed by \(d=15,33\), with the following property. For every positive modulus \(M\) and **every prescribed odd integer** \(N\), some actual receiver and exterior words realize the ordered Hamilton path counts \((N,N+2)\) modulo \(M\) if and only if \(d\nmid M\). In each construction the same six-vertex middle tournament, tags and edited arc are used throughout. The prescribed \(N\) can be \(E_{61}-1\). Each construction passes at every single prime to every precision. In the first one a simultaneous test at 3 and 5 suffices to reject an exact flip. Replacing its right receiver with one particular six-vertex tournament restores a literal gap of two and simultaneous local realizability at *every* modulus.

## Two physical left modules

Use the middle \(D\) with outgoing masks \((16,5,9,19,38,15)\), tag mask 35 and edit \((1,4)\). Put the left tournament \(A\), then \(D\), then the right tournament \(V\) in ordinal order with a common apex. The two exterior words have arbitrary addresses \(x,y\). For the right module take \(V_n=C_3[\mathrm{TT}_n,1,1]\), \(n\ge1\), with unrestricted tags. It has \(v=2^n+1\) paths and admits every number \(r\) of paths starting at a nontag from 0 through \(v\). Write \(d=H(A)\), \(u=d-e_A\) with \(e_A\) the number of full paths ending at a tag. The signed change on flipping \(D\) is **exactly** \(2(dr-uv)\), independent of \(x,y\). In the theorem the flipped orientation gives the first prescribed count. [Farey identity](E15-FAREY.md) · [Binary choice of the right tags](BERGGREN-RECEIVERS.md)

The second left tournament is itself \(C_3[\mathrm{TT}_5,1,1]\). Its tag set gives \(e_A=7\); in the first row \(e_A=1\). For \(d=15\), a signed change of \(-2\bmod15\) requires \(u(2^n+1)=1\bmod15\). Modulo three this forces \(n\) even. Modulo five it forces \(2^n=3\bmod5\), or \(n=3\bmod4\). For \(d=33\) the condition at three again forces \(n\) even, while at eleven it gives \(2^n=2\bmod11\), or \(n=1\bmod10\). Both constructions also exclude the opposite sign, since at three \(u(2^n+1)\) only takes the values 0 and 1. This proves impossibility whenever \(d\mid M\), for either assignment of the two target heights.

## Sufficiency at every other modulus

### Why pairs of prime powers settle the exact Farey determinant

**PROVED.** In the same right receiver family, fix *any* physical left flank with \(d=H(A)>1\) and \(0<u=d-e_A<d\) coprime to \(d\). Fix the desired sign \(\epsilon\in\{-1,1\}\) in \(dr-u(2^n+1)=\epsilon\). An exact unit of this sign exists if and only if it is feasible modulo each full prime power dividing \(d\) and modulo the product of every two such powers. Exterior addresses are arbitrary. This statement decides the size and direction of the actual flip.

For \(P=\ell^{v_\ell(d)}\), the condition is \(2^n=-\epsilon u^{-1}-1\bmod P\). The group generated by 2 in the units modulo \(P\) is cyclic since \(d\) is odd. If the right side is absent from that group, \(P\) alone excludes the sign. Otherwise there is one congruence \(n=a_P\bmod \operatorname{ord}_P(2)\). A joint test at \(P,Q\) passes exactly when \(a_P-a_Q\) is divisible by the gcd of the two orders. The generalized Chinese remainder theorem combines every such pairwise compatible list. Taking an arbitrarily large representative of the resulting width progression gives an integer \(r=(u(2^n+1)+\epsilon)/d\) in the full representable interval \([0,2^n+1]\). Its binary expansion constructs the right tags. Conversely an exact solution supplies all of its reductions. Thus a failed direction has an obstruction on at most **two** prime powers. If either direction is allowed, the union of two such obstruction sets has size at most four. The examples above attain the lower bound of two for a fixed sign. Placement at the absolute target \(N\) still imposes the two-flank height equation.

### Prescribing the absolute phase in the two example families

Let \(d=3s\), so \(s\) is 5 or 11, and suppose \(d\nmid M\). If \(s\nmid M\), take \(n\) a large positive multiple of all the multiplicative orders of 2 at odd primes dividing \(M\), also a multiple of the order of 3 at 29 when \(29\mid M\). If \(s\mid M\), then \(3\nmid M\); take \(n\) congruent to 3 for \(s=5\), or to 1 for \(s=11\), modulo the same common period, now including the orders of 3 at both \(s\) and 29 whenever those primes divide \(M\). An empty list of orders has common period one. In the first case \(v=2\) at all such odd primes. In the second \(v=9\) or 3 respectively. Thus \(v\) is coprime to \(M\), and \(\gcd(d,M)\mid uv-1\). Solve
\[
                            dr=uv-1\pmod M .
\]
Once \(n\) is large enough that \(2^n\ge M\), one residue representative of \(r\) is an available starting mass. The middle flip now has the required signed change modulo \(M\).

It remains to prescribe the absolute residue. Denote the seven-coordinate lower chart by \((h,q,c,t,e,b,k)\). Its count is
\[
 L=k+qxy+cy+tx+e[y\ {\rm odd}]+b[x\ {\rm odd}]+h(f(x)+f(y)).
\]
Here \(h=29dv\). Each odd prime dividing \(\gcd(h,M)\) is among \(3,s,29\) and has valuation exactly one in \(h\). The join identities and the two literal chart computations give the necessary surjectivity at these primes. In \(A\to D\), the unflipped profiles in the two rows respectively are
\[
 (435,69120,8510,33026,240,406,4679),\qquad
 (957,195840,132386,90925,528,145,63371).
\]
Flipping \(D\) preserves the first four entries. In joining \(V\), \(q=q_{AD}q_V\), \(t=q_{AD}t_V+v t_{AD}\), where \(q_V=2\cdot3^n+4\cdot2^n-2\). To see the last identity, count ordered assignments of all vertices to two path slots, allowing empty slots. When the two special vertices of \(V\) are separated, both induced counts are one. When they are together with a spine subset of size \(j>0\), the contribution is \(2^j+1\), and for \(j=0\) it is one.

At 3, \(q_{AD}=0\), and \(t_{AD}\) and \(v\) are units. This same argument applies at 5 in the first family. At 11 in the second family and at 29 in either family, \(q_{AD}\) is a unit. At 11 our choice gives \(q_V=12\bmod11\). At 29 it gives \(q_V=4,84,\) or \(12\bmod29\), all nonzero. Thus the bilinear expression \(k+qxy+cy+tx\) attains every desired residue at each of the odd primes in \(\gcd(h,M)\): if \(q=0\) use the unit slope, otherwise choose \(x\) with \(c+qx\ne0\) and solve for \(y\). Use CRT to choose the required residues with \(x,y\) both even. At 2 the initial lower count and \(N\) are both odd.

With those address residues fixed, the finite word controller can vary \(f(y)\) over every even residue of \(M\) while preserving \(y\bmod 2M\). Equivalently, the state \((y\bmod 2M,f(y)/2\bmod M)\) is reachable at every pair of coordinates. This compensates the entire remaining difference since it is divisible by \(\gcd(2h,M)\). This proves the asserted simultaneous sufficiency, including repeated prime factors. The resulting words can be very long. ## Keeping the same left module and restoring simultaneous coverage

For \(d=15\), use right rows \((4,9,18,21,35,15)\), tag mask 1. Here \((v,r)=(29,27)\), so \(15\cdot27-14\cdot29=-1\). For \(d=33\), use \((4,5,96,7,15,27,59)\), tag mask 18. Here \((v,r)=(47,37)\), so \(33\cdot37-26\cdot47=-1\). The two lower charts are
\[
\begin{aligned}
 &(12615,22118400,2736685,15127354,4785,11774,1895419),\\
 &(44979,169989120,115134029,114139715,14355,6815,77431121).
\end{aligned}
\]
Each satisfies \(\gcd(h,q,c,t)=1\). At each odd prime power dividing \(\gcd(h,M)\), one of \(q,c,t\) is therefore a unit. The same bilinear inversion works at full prime-power precision: when \(q\) is a unit choose \(x\) so \(c+qx\) is a unit; otherwise one of \(c,t\) is a unit and the other address may be zero. The reset word again finishes the congruence. Thus each replacement, fixed independently of \(N,M\), has an actual gap of two at every address and can steer its lower height to **every odd target modulo every modulus**.

For a compact simultaneous certificate at \(E_{61}\) take \(M=1,618,964,990,108,856,390\), the squarefree product of the 15 primes for which all supersingular \(j\)-values are in the prime field. These are exactly the Monster prime divisors. Put \(m=M/2\) and \(t=\operatorname{ord}_m(2)=18,489,240\). Appending the word \(1^t0^t\) keeps an even address fixed modulo \(M\) and adds one to \(f\) modulo \(m\). In the \(d=15\) replacement start at \(x=4,y=34\) and append 988,504,144,558,763 copies to \(y\). In the \(d=33\) replacement start at \(x=0,y=36,650\) and append 7,286,470,133,064 copies. The resulting tournament counts match the prescribed two endpoints modulo the full product. The actual heights at these huge words exceed \(E_{61}\). At either replacement, exhaustively allowing a single run of ones with any trailing zeros on each exterior address gives no exact \(E_{61}\) hit at any order.
