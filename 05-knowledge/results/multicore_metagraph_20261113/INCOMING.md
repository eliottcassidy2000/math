## A finite dyadic bank cannot sustain the exact ternary iteration

[Session 296, finite-atlas theorem](../session296_multicore_metagraph_20261113/cores/FINITE_ATLAS.md)
strengthens the limitation on our 159 uniform +2 transitions. An infinite
handoff from A·2^k+B to A'·2^l+B' under H↦3H+2 forces

    A'·2^s=3A,       B'=3B+2,

for an integer s. The 3-adic valuation of the leading coefficient increases
by one. A finite bank has only finitely many accidental handoffs and cannot
support an indefinitely increasing orbit, even with arbitrary parameter
reindexing. This elementary conclusion does not require the S-unit theorem
used there for the separate, stronger sparse-revisit claim.

Consequently our finite-depth chains genuinely prove their next entries,
but enlarging a finite bank of dyadic charts cannot alone prove indefinite
exact 3H+2 iteration. A proposed escape must specify unbounded core types,
an additional scale, or different arithmetic transitions and their physical
endpoint lifts.

## Internal cards and endpoint markers retain different information

[Session 296, metagraph §5](../session296_multicore_metagraph_20261113/metagraph/PROOF.md)
reconstructs an upper complement line from two actual oriented cards at
adjacent internal deletions for n≥4. Three selected proper cards, when only
their lower complement lines are retained, suffice for n≥5; no two fixed
proper masks of this latter kind suffice.

These cards retain internal path order and relative complement phase.
They are not a bounded list of distinguished endpoint vertices. Thus their
reconstruction theorem is compatible with our strong-tournament examples
where any fixed endpoint window fails to determine blue versus black.
Together they identify a useful division: end-rooted classes suffice for
forward counting, while exact complement incidence requires internal
alignment information. Neither statement supplies a smaller complete
metagraph state or a new A000568 term by itself.

## A contrasting escape changes the marked response at the same count

The later synchronization also brought
[Session 298's exact-target preparation](../session298_exact_target_geometry_20261113/README.md)
and its [two-width chart](../session298_exact_target_geometry_20261113/TWO_WIDTH.md).
These move a named root within a transitive block while preserving the
unrooted tournament, hence its whole Hamilton count. The changed marked
response opens infinitely many permitted entries into a balanced controller
at the displayed targets 2^m+1 and, in the two-width example, 47.

This suggests a different role for the metagraph: several marked states
over one ordinary node can have different outgoing permissions despite
identical full counts. Such neutral preparation can change the available
arithmetic operation before a growth step. It is not a counterexample to
the finite-bank obstruction, which fixes the exact 3H+2 iteration, and it
does not establish a ready source at every target. The next proof obligation
is an all-target source or a target-decreasing physical repair with its
specified marked coordinates, rather than another list of scalar matches.
