## 2 A derivative must leave the attachment algebra

Let V and J be the rational source module and radical classified in
[ALGEBRA95](ALGEBRA95.md). Choose M in J with M^3 nonzero and M^4=0,
as in the supplied clock certificate. Pick v with vM^3 nonzero. The four
vectors v,vM,vM^2,vM^3 are independent: applying a suitable power of M
to the first nonzero term proves this. Also v is not in VJ, since
v in VJ would imply vM^3 in VJ^4=0.

Every element of A preserves VJ. But a degree-lowering derivative on
this chain would send vM to v. Therefore **no element of A can implement
that derivative**, even allowing rational linear combinations of words.
This is an exact obstruction, stronger than the singular-positive-word
test for formal unipotent clocks. A representation can retain a chain
without its allowed operations being able to lower that chain.

On an abstract four-dimensional truncated polynomial module the usual
derivative and multiplication satisfy the finite Weyl boundary relation
`[d/dt,t]=I-4 E_top` (operators compose in the usual left-action order).
The boundary term is unavoidable by trace. The concrete attachment
algebra supplies the multiplication chain but excludes this derivative.
This connects the prior finite-Weyl calculation and the actual source
interface, without assuming a Jacobian-conjecture conclusion.

The prior planar Jacobian response formula is
`Q=(D(BQ)-B DQ)/kappa`, where `D(B)=kappa!=0` and D is a derivation.
It is the product rule with its mixed response retained. A single DQ
does not supply D(BQ). In the current algebra, attempting to infer that
missing operation from the existence of a nilpotent chain fails at VJ.
See [JP1--3](../joint_port_geometry_20261103/JACOBIAN_PROBES.md) for the inherited
response calculation and its scope. Neither generic injectivity nor a
complete classification of Jacobian counterexamples follows here.

## 3 Reflection has a frame, an interface and a clock

Native tournament complements reverse the edges outside a supplied path;
the path and actual old vertex names are essential parts of the operation.
The two-complement arc repair succeeds by changing those retained path
edges in exactly one old pair. The return's chart is not phase-neutral:
its retained-root phase changes from (1,1,0) to (3,0,1).

Hilbert reflections instead act on signed coordinate frames, while
Cayley--Dickson reduction modulo 2 deliberately forgets the multiplication
signs. Neither quotient recovers the lost frame automatically. The prior
[reflection-voltage calculation](../session166_planar_metagraph_20261103/reflection_voltages/REFLECTION_VOLTAGES.md)
and [honeycomb covers](../session167_recurring_structures_20261103/honeycomb/NOTE.md) supply
geometric examples where the cover and deck return are indispensable.
The common theorem is elementary but useful: an operation descends
through a representation only if every identification remains an
identification after that operation. Its source guards must descend too.

There is a useful extension. Multiplication by u on the quadratic ring
has matrix B=[[0,1],[1,5]], of exact order 12 over F13. The new radical
operator U=I+M has order 13 there. On the formal tensor module take
T=B tensor U. Then

`T^12=I tensor U^12`, `T^13=B tensor I`, `order(T)=156`.

The samples at 12 and 13 isolate the nilpotent and quadratic factors,
respectively, and together reconstruct T via `T=T^13*(T^12)^(-1)`.
The order follows because the two factors have coprime orders; a scalar
cancellation in the tensor product is impossible since U is unipotent.
This is a joint clock calculation on a specified 54-dimensional module,
not an attachment word. It explains a precise way to combine the older
12/13 trace loss with the new radical, rather than matching their numerals.
