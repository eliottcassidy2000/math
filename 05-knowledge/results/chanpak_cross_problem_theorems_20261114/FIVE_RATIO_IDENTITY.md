# Five physical source types remove the sign obstruction at a prescribed modulus

Let \(D_m=C_3[b,a,TT_m]\), \(m\ge1\), from the
[unbounded-rank construction](FERMAT_RANK.md). In a source made of
ordinal copies of \(D_m\) and singletons, then placed in the outer cyclic
wrap, put \(r(m)=Q_m/h_m\). Division by the full source count \(h\)
gives \(N/h=1+2^{s+1}\prod_m r(m)^{a_m}\) over rings where the
denominators are units.

**All-order construction.** For every odd modulus \(M>1\) coprime
to \(3\cdot5\cdot7\cdot11\cdot17\), one can choose at most five physical
source types \(D_m\), with \(m\) depending on \(M\), and nonnegative
multiplicities so that the strong outer wrap has \(M\mid N\) and
\(\gcd(M,hQ)=1\). The latter condition rules out merely putting a
common factor into both terms. Every individual odd prime power also
has such a physical unit construction, including powers of the five
exceptional primes.

## The identity

Temporarily evaluate the rational profile formula at negative integers;
no negative-width tournaments are implied:
\[
 r(j)=\frac{4\cdot2^j+2(3^j-1)}{1+2^j}\qquad (j\in\mathbf Z).
\]
The five values are
\[
 r(2)=32/5,\quad r(4)=224/17,\quad r(5)=204/11,\quad
 r(-2)=-28/45,\quad r(-3)=-308/243.
\]
They satisfy the multiplicative identity
\[
 r(-3)r(5)r(4)\left(r(2)/r(-2)\right)^2=-2^{15}.
\]
For example, stripping powers of 2 leaves the product
\((-7\cdot11/3^5)(3\cdot17/11)(7/17)(-9/7)^2=-1\).

Work modulo \(M\). Let \(L\) be the least common multiple of
the multiplicative orders of 2 and 3, and replace \(-2,-3\) by
positive representatives modulo \(L\). The chosen physical profiles
reduce to the displayed values and have unit numerators and
denominators; this follows directly from the same substitutions in
\(h_j=1+2^j\) and \(Q_j=4\cdot2^j+2(3^j-1)\).
The ratio identity now puts \(-1\) in the subgroup generated by the
five physical ratios and 2. Replace the exponent \(-2\) of
the \(r(-2)\) lift by its nonnegative remainder modulo its order.
Choose the number \(s\) of singletons so that
\(s+1\equiv-15\bmod\operatorname{ord}_M(2)\), with \(s+1>0\).
The other exponents in the identity are already positive. This is a
physical ordinal source, and substitution yields \(M\mid N\).
Each repeated coefficient and each width is \(O(\varphi(M)+1)\),
so one can even bound the source order by \(O(\varphi(M)^2+1)\).

For powers of primes 3, 5, 11 and 17, use transitive sources:
2 has even order at each such prime and hence at every power, so
some sufficiently large \(2^k\) is \(-1\) modulo that prime power.
For powers of 7, use copies of \(D_2\); \(r(2)\equiv5\bmod7\)
has order 6 and remains of even order at higher powers. Choose
the singleton exponent to make the power of 2 equal to 1,
and choose a positive ratio exponent giving \(-1\).
All involved profiles are units at these primes.

## Exact boundary of the result

The positive-width lift must change with the target modulus. This is
how five *roles* bypass the impossibility theorem for five fixed
profiles (indeed for any finite bank). The identity solves simultaneous
sign reachability for every good modulus, not only a density-one set.
It does not generally keep the resulting *positive integer* below the
next multiple of the modulus. Even when \(M\) is the prescribed prime
target, the proof gives a multiple, not equality. In fact the following three-prime obstruction shows that simultaneous
unit coverage fails for some moduli in this very bank.

## A genuine three-prime compatibility obstruction: 255

The same bank of all positive-width \(D_m\) cannot attain unit
divisibility by \(255=3\cdot5\cdot17\), even though each of its
two-prime subproducts has a unit construction in the bank.
This is not a failure of the negative-index proof: it is an
obstruction for **every** physical width and multiplicity.

If \(hQ\) is a unit modulo 255, every component profile
must be a unit. For a \(D_m\) factor, the requirement at 3
forces \(m\) even (otherwise \(1+2^m=0\bmod3\)).
The requirement at 5 then forces \(m=0\bmod4\).
At 17, \(2\) has order 8, so \(h_m\ne0\bmod17\)
forces \(m=0\bmod8\) among those multiples of 4.
For such \(m\), \(2^m=1\bmod17\) and \(3^8=-1\bmod17\);
if \(m=8\bmod16\) then \(Q_m=0\bmod17\).
Thus all selected widths must be multiples of 16. Each such
ratio \(Q_m/h_m\) is exactly 2 modulo both 5 and 17.
The singleton factor also multiplies the ratio by 2, so
the product in \(N/h\) is \(2^K\) for the same K at both primes.
The equations for \(-1\) require \(K=2\bmod4\) at 5
and \(K=4\bmod8\) at 17. These are incompatible.

For comparison, the proper two-prime subproducts are all feasible:
- \(15\): source \(D_4\), no singletons, giving \(N=465\).
- \(51\): three copies of \(D_{16}\), no singletons; the ratio is
  1 at 3 and 2 at 17, giving \(N/h=1+2\) at 3 and
  \(N/h=1+2^4\) at 17.
- \(85\): two copies of \(D_3\), one singleton, giving \(N=28{,}305\).

All three examples have \(hQ\) a unit at their indicated moduli.
This is a three-way incompatibility of multiplicative phases despite
pairwise physical witnesses. It does not rule out copies with a
common factor in the two terms, other source types, or the exact
Hamiltonian count 255 in the full spectrum.
