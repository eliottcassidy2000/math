# Full congruence coverage and sparse absolute coverage at a fixed cycle count

The cycle count below counts directed Hamiltonian cycles up to cyclic
rotation, not up to reversal. The local theorem is exact at every
modulus, on a physically specified family of strong tournaments.

## Construction and statement

Take any nonempty tournament R. Place it in the ordinal spine
l → R → r, where l,r are singletons. Paint l with 1 and r with 0;
paint R arbitrarily. Add one apex v, with v beating precisely the
vertices painted 1. Append any finite transitive word on the right
of the spine, either empty or ending in 0, referring to the same
apex. The initial 1 and terminal 0 give a bijection from Hamiltonian
paths of R to directed Hamiltonian cycles of each extended tournament:
remove the apex from the cycle and follow the ordinal spine. In
particular every tournament is strong and its cycle count is h=H(R).

In the initial painted spine B, let S be the sum over all nonempty
paths starting at a 1-painted vertex of H(B minus that path), with
H(empty)=1. Put N0=H(B+v). Then:

**Local image.** For each modulus M, the exact residue set of attainable
Hamiltonian path counts consists of the odd-compatible classes
with N ≡ N0 modulo gcd(h,S,M). Odd-compatible means no restriction if
M is odd, and an odd class if M is even.

**Absolute image.** For real X≥N0, at most
1+2(X−N0)/S different counts in this family are ≤X; here S>0.
Consequently even a family with *no* congruence obstruction can omit
a positive proportion of all odd integers. At the same fixed cycle
count one may make the displayed upper density arbitrarily small by
initially appending more zeros.

## Dynamics

Use the [ordinal cut-state transfer](
../chanpak_selective_target_20261114/CUT_STATE.md).
At a terminal 0 the signed bias is zero. Write N for the full count
and use h for the invariant spine count. Appending 0 and appending
1^j0 (j≥1) realize, respectively,

    A:      (S,N) ↦ (2S,N+S)
    B_j:    (S,N) ↦ (2^(j+1)(S+h)−2h, N+2^j(S+h)).

Indeed a 1 doubles S, adds h, sets the signed bias to h, and leaves
N unchanged. A 0 adds S plus the previous bias to N, doubles S
and resets the bias. This also shows the terminal-zero word language
is generated by A and the B_j. The seed S is even, and h,N are odd.
Hence B_j toggles precisely bit j of N among bits 0 through j.
For M with 2-part 2^a, optionally apply B_j successively for
j=1,...,a−1 to set those bits. All further B_j with
j≥max(1,a) preserve that binary output. No zero reset is needed.

Let m be the odd part of M and d=gcd(h,S,m), unchanged by every
physical append, since multiplication by 2 is invertible mod m.
Every increment of N is divisible by d. Subtract the constant
residue r0=N0 mod d from N and divide S,N−r0,h by d. Work with
these divided variables modulo m/d. At every prime dividing this
new modulus, at least one of h,S is a unit modulo the prime;
otherwise the original gcd was not maximal. We reuse the symbols
h,S,N for these divided variables in the following argument only.

## Exact odd-part compiler

Take X=S+2h and J=N−(S+h)/2. For a high block with b=2^j
the action is

    X ↦ 2b(X−h),       J ↦ J+X/2.

Arbitrarily high exponents give both b=1/2 and b=1 modulo the
odd modulus (powers of 2 are periodic). Call these maps U,V.
All such maps are permutations; inverses denote nonnegative
powers and physically preserve the already chosen binary output.
Put L=V U^(-1). It doubles X and leaves J; U subtracts h from X.
Composition is right to left. Then

    W = L U L^(-1) U^(-2):    (X,J) ↦ (X,J−3X/4−h).

Split m/d into coprime good and bad prime powers according as
the divided h is a unit, or divisible by the prime. On the good
factor use a power of U to put X=0, then W translates J by −h,
a unit including at 3. Set N on the entire good factor at once.
These moves may affect the bad factor, which is handled second.

On the bad factor X is a unit modulo every prime, since it starts
that way and U and V preserve the condition. From that state the
increment of N under U^k is

    k X/2 − h k(k+1)/4.

For each bad prime power, the difference between this expression
at k and l is (k−l) times the unit X/2−h(k+l+1)/4. Thus it is a
bijection modulo that prime power. Choose k by CRT, with k=0
on the good factor (U^k then acts identically there, by its
iterate formula). This attains the required scalar residue.
Combining with the binary part proves the first claim. For h=1,
this is also a shorter scalar proof of the prior
[one-apex congruence theorem](
../chanpak_least_missing_jacobian_20261114/MODULAR_ESCAPE.md):
park at X=0 rather than use the old 3-adic reset. We make no
claim here of joint surjectivity for S and N at bad primes.

## Why the count still has holes

For a suffix w of width L≥1 ending in 0, let t be the total
weight of its nonempty paths ending at a 0-painted vertex.
In a transitive spine every subset ending at position i gives
one such path, so t is the sum of 2^(i−1) over the zero
positions. In particular t≥2^(L−1). The cut transfer gives

    N(w) = N0 + S t + h (H(apex over w)−1) ≥ N0+S·2^(L−1).

The last term is nonnegative by Rédei (or odd-cycle packing).
Sum 2^(L−1) possible words at each width L up to the permitted
maximum. Count the empty suffix separately. This proves the
absolute bound, without estimating any equality of counts.
Appending initial zeros doubles S at each step and preserves
gcd(h,S) since h is odd.

**Same cycles, different congruence behavior.** Take the strong
four-vertex R with outgoing masks (8,1,3,6), h=5, Q=P_R(2)=32,
and starting weights (5,6,9,7). The initial S is
2(Q+sum of weights of the 1-painted vertices in R), by the cut
transfer. Paint none: (h,S,N0)=(5,64,69). This family reaches
every odd-compatible residue modulo every M while realizing
at most 1+2(X−69)/64 distinct values up to X, a limsup ≤1/16
relative to the odd numbers. Paint vertices 1 and 3 (zero-based):
(h,S,N0)=(5,90,77), so only N≡2 mod 5 is possible. The cycle count
is the same in both cases. In the first case 71 is locally
reachable at every modulus, but any nonempty suffix has N≥133,
so 71 is not in this one seed family.

## Transfer to arithmetic, and its limit

This is a concrete local-global distinction of the sort appearing
in [Zaremba's problem](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/2014/180-1/p03)
and in [Apollonian packings](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.02749).
The analogy is a projected count of a physical semigroup orbit.
Finite-state mixing can give full projected congruence coverage
while the forward orbit has far too few small representatives.
Apollonian reciprocity is a different mechanism; no reciprocity
obstruction to the full tournament spectrum is asserted here.
The new diagnostic is to evaluate **both** gcd(h,S) and the
cost S of reading a new digit, even when the cycle count has
already been fixed. A reusable Chan–Pak construction has to
change seed at the target scale, not just make its seed mix
well modulo more moduli.
