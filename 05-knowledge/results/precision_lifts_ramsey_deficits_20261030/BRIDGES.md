## A kernel flag, not separate favorable choices

DILATIONS.md D4 has a group-theoretic form. Let G be a finite abelian group,
let chi_i:G->R/Z be characters, and let alpha_i be fixed phases. Set
K_0=G and K_i=intersection_(j<=i) ker(chi_j). If the image chi_i(K_(i-1))
has order H_i>=2 at every stage, then there is g in G such that

    ||alpha_i+chi_i(g)|| >= min_j (H_j-1)/(2H_j)

for every i. Start at g=0. At stage i add an element of K_(i-1) whose
chi_i image brings the phase nearest1/2. Earlier phases stay exactly fixed.
The order-H_i grid has a point of norm at least(H_i-1)/(2H_i). Induction
proves the assertion. This is an elementary character/kernel lemma; it
does not assert novelty over general finite-group lifting.

For G=Z/d and chi_i(j)=v_i*j/d, the kernel is the multiples of the running
lcm L_i. D4 gives an exact rational decoder with one modular inverse per
stage. The object retained is the residual set of legal changes to the
same chosen label. Replacing it by separate maxima loses the conclusion.
The orders2,3,6 show how a last character can become fixed by previous
choices even though each character was initially nontrivial.

For the Collatz return boundary G=F5^2, arbitrary tangent increments are
not free legal operations: they require positive valuation words and their
actual clock fees. A kernel flag on G alone therefore cannot pay a return.
C6 retains the native words and leaves the two exact fee obligations.
This identifies the missing hypothesis in transferring the runner argument:
one needs a cost-controlled native realization of each desired correction.

## Squares preserve sampling overlaps

For M labelled values x_i, ordinary algebra gives

    (mean_i x_i)^2
      = (1/M) mean_i x_i^2
        + ((M-1)/M) mean_(i!=j) x_i*x_j.

The diagonal is essential: with M=2 and values1,-1, the off-diagonal
average alone is-1. For a function f on unordered pairs from an M-set,
the corresponding square splits into equal-pair, one-shared-label, and
disjoint-pair terms, with weights

    1/C(M,2), 2(M-2)/C(M,2), C(M-2,2)/C(M,2).

Count the second pair relative to a fixed first pair to prove the identity.
No independence assumption is involved. Dauth's rooted Ramsey certificates
also multiply conditional squares by genuinely nonnegative neighborhood
slacks. Their overlap terms live on the same labelled coloring, explaining
why separate port inventories cannot replace them.

Applying this pattern to Collatz would require an actual source-supported
joint law or a pointwise sum-of-squares identity on legal transitions.
Treating repeated times as independent residue samples supplies neither.
The existing Bellman checks are pointwise inequalities, and their explicit
artificial extremizers identify the extra paths admitted by the abstraction.
