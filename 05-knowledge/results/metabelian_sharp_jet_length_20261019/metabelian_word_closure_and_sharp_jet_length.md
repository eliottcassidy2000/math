# A two-flow metabelian group: exact words, closure, and sharp jet length

19 October 2026. **Self-contained proof, characteristic zero.** This continuation distinguishes exact finite generation, Lie generation, and point-adic closure, then measures the exact cost of approximating a shear absent from the finite group. The principal-part normal form and closure mechanism extend the earlier source calculations; no historical-novelty claim is made.

## 1. Results and conventions

Let K be any characteristic-zero field, R=K[[x,y]], and m=(x,y). We use the Poisson bracket {A,B}=A_x B_y−A_y B_x and Hamiltonian derivation D_H={−,H}. Let Γ be the group generated by all scalar-time point maps of the two fixed polynomial Hamiltonians

    G=x²y,              H=x³.

They have order three, so their formal flows converge m-adically. Write their generators as

    M_t(x,y)=(x/(1−tx), y(1−tx)²),      t∈K,
    T_c(x,y)=(x,y+cx²),                 c∈K.

Here M_t is the G-flow at time t; T_c is the H-flow at time −c/3. All generators fix the origin, are tangent to the identity, and preserve the Poisson bracket. They are rational functions regular as germs at the origin. A *word length* counts each nonidentity M_t or T_c as one factor. All scalar times are allowed; this is not the group generated just by two time-one maps.

The results are:

1. The polynomial maps in Γ are exactly the quadratic vertical shears T_c.
2. Its m-adic closure consists exactly of

       (x/(1−tx), y(1−tx)²+h(x)),   t∈K, h∈x²K[[x]].       (1)

   The polynomial intersection of this closure is every vertical polynomial shear (x,y+h(x)), h∈x²K[x].
3. The Lie algebra generated by G,H contains every x^n, n≥3. Nevertheless, the flow (x,y+x³) is not an exact finite Γ word.
4. To agree with (x,y+x³) modulo m^(N+1), a finite Γ word needs at least N−2 distinct nonzero partial-fraction sites when N≥3. This pole bound is sharp. Its minimum word length is

       0 for N≤2;       4 for N=3;       2N−3 for N≥4.      (2)

   For N≥4 a rational construction over Q attains the last bound and has its first nonzero error exactly in degree N+1.
5. Any such approximation for N≥3 has reduced x-denominator degree at least 4(N−2) in its vertical correction. Thus increasing jet agreement requires unbounded rational pole complexity, even with freely chosen scalar times and pole locations.

The topology uses discrete coefficients: agreement modulo each m-power must eventually be exact. No analytic limit of scalar parameters is used.

## 2. Exact normal form

Use the birational canonical coordinates

    z=1/x,    w=−yx²,    {z,w}=1.

Then G=−w and H=z^(−3). The generators become

    M_t:(z,w)↦(z−t,w),
    T_c:(z,w)↦(z,w−c/z⁴).

Every finite word has the unique form

    (z,w)↦(z−a,w−F(z)),
    F(z)=Σ_j c_j/(z−t_j)⁴,                             (3)

where the sites t_j are distinct elements of K, the coefficients c_j are nonzero, and the sum is finite, possibly empty. Existence follows by composing translations and vertical additions. Conversely, conjugation realizes every summand:

    M_(−t) ∘ T_c ∘ M_t
       =(x, y+c r_t(x)),
    r_t(x)=x²/(1−tx)⁴.                                 (4)

Unique partial fractions give uniqueness of F, and the first coordinate gives a uniquely. This describes a semidirect product of the additive group K with the direct sum of one-dimensional K-spaces indexed by the sites t∈K. Translating z shifts the sites; it does not introduce new pole orders.

If a word is polynomial in x,y, its first coordinate x/(1−ax) forces a=0. It is then

    (x,y+f(x)),     f(x)=x^(−2)F(1/x)=Σ_j c_j r_(t_j)(x). (5)

Every t_j≠0 produces a fourth-order pole at x=1/t_j. Other sites cannot cancel that pole. Thus polynomiality forces every nonzero site to be absent. The remaining site t=0 gives f=c_0x². This proves result 1.

For a word returning the total M-time to zero, there is a useful chronological description. Apply

    M_(t_1), T_(c_1), M_(t_2−t_1), T_(c_2), ...,
    M_(t_p−t_(p−1)), T_(c_p), M_(−t_p).                (6)

Its final map is (5) with sites t_1,...,t_p. Each T inserts a coefficient at the accumulated M-time when it is applied. A separate T_(c_0) at accumulated time zero supplies the site-zero term.

## 3. Point-adic closure and Lie generation

Expand the translated kernels:

    r_t(x)=Σ_(k≥0) binom(k+3,3) t^k x^(k+2).           (7)

For any N≥2, choose N−1 distinct sites, for example 0,1,...,N−2. The matrix sending their coefficients to the coefficients of x²,...,x^N is a Vandermonde matrix multiplied by nonzero diagonal factors binom(k+3,3). It is invertible over K. Therefore a finite K-linear combination of the r_t matches any prescribed h∈x²K[[x]] through degree N. Each such finite combination is an exact Γ shear by (4). This proves density of those shears without taking any scalar coefficient to a limit.

Every finite Γ word belongs to the family (1): this follows from (3), or by composing a vertical shear with M_t. Conversely, any h in (1) can be approximated by the preceding vertical corrections and composition with the fixed M_t. To see that (1) is closed, the x² coefficient of its first coordinate is t. Point-adic convergence forces t eventually to be exactly constant. The coefficient of y in the second coordinate is then fixed, and the remaining one-variable series can converge only to another h∈x²K[[x]]. The m-adic topology has a countable neighborhood basis, so this also identifies the topological closure, rather than only its convergent sequences.

A member of (1) is polynomial precisely when t=0 and h is polynomial. This proves result 2.

On the other hand,

    {x^n,G}=n x^(n+1),       {x^m,x^n}=0.

Consequently the Lie algebra generated by G and x³ is exactly

    KG ⊕ span_K{x^n:n≥3}.

It is infinite-dimensional and metabelian; its lower central series does not terminate. In particular x⁴ belongs to this Lie algebra, and its scalar flow gives cubic vertical shears. By result 1 those nonzero cubic shears are absent from the exact finite group. They do belong to its point-adic closure. Lie brackets, exact finite products, and closure are therefore genuinely different operations here.

This is a controlled example beyond the finite-dimensional/nilpotent collapse in [the companion Hamiltonian Lie note](../planar_autonomous_lie_jets_20261019/finite_dimensional_hamiltonian_lie_obstruction.md). It does not say that exponentiating every element of an infinite-dimensional generated Lie algebra yields an exact finite word.

## 4. A lower bound including the pole-free site

Fix N≥3. Suppose a Γ word agrees with S=(x,y+x³) modulo m^(N+1). Its first coordinate is x/(1−ax). The x² coefficient is a, so a=0. No nonzero net M-time can hide in a higher-order remainder.

Combine repeated sites and remove zero coefficients. Write its vertical correction as

    f(x)=c_0x²+Σ_(i=1)^p c_i x²/(1−t_i x)⁴,           (8)

where the t_i are distinct and nonzero, and every c_i≠0. The coefficient c_0 is arbitrary and may vanish. Matching the target jet is equivalent to the moment conditions

    c_0+Σ_i c_i=0,
    Σ_i c_i t_i=1/4,
    Σ_i c_i t_i^k=0       for 2≤k≤N−2.              (9)

Define L(P)=c_0P(0)+Σ_i c_iP(t_i). These conditions say

    L(P)=P′(0)/4       whenever deg P≤N−2.           (10)

Let Q(T)=∏_(i=1)^p(T−t_i). If p≤N−3, the polynomial TQ(T) has degree at most N−2 and vanishes at every supported site, including zero. But (10) would give

    0=L(TQ)=Q(0)/4≠0,

a contradiction. Hence p≥N−2. This proof explicitly allows the pole-free polynomial term c_0x²; ignoring it is unnecessary.

Every nonzero site is an actual fourth-order pole in x. Thus the reduced denominator of (8) is a nonzero scalar multiple of

    ∏_(i=1)^p (1−t_i x)⁴,

and has degree 4p≥4(N−2). Addition of the site-zero term cannot cancel these poles. This proves the lower pole-complexity bound.

## 5. All pole-minimal approximants and balanced sites

Put p=N−2 and choose any p distinct nonzero sites t_1,...,t_p. Let Q be their monic root polynomial as above, and set

    c_i=−Q(0)/(4 t_i² Q′(t_i)),       1≤i≤p,
    c_0=Q′(0)/(4Q(0))=−(1/4)Σ_i 1/t_i.             (11)

These are the unique coefficients satisfying (9). Indeed, on the p+1 distinct nodes 0,t_1,...,t_p, the functional P↦P′(0)/4 on polynomials of degree at most p has unique Lagrange-interpolation weights. Differentiating the cardinal basis at zero gives exactly (11). All nonzero-site weights c_i are nonzero.

The next moment follows by evaluating Q(T)T against the actual supported-node functional. Write m_k=Σ_i c_i t_i^k for k≥1. Since every supported node is a zero of TQ(T), and m_1=1/4 while m_2,...,m_p vanish,

    m_(p+1)=−Q(0)/4≠0.

Thus every pole-minimal approximant (11) has the exact expansion

    f(x)=x³−[binom(N+2,3)Q(0)/4]x^(N+1)
                +O(x^(N+2)).                        (12)

In particular its agreement is through degree N, and no further.

For N≥4 we have p≥2. Choose the rational sites

    t_i=i           for 1≤i≤p−1,
    t_p=−1/H_(p−1),    H_(p−1)=Σ_(i=1)^(p−1)1/i.   (13)

They are distinct and nonzero in every characteristic-zero field, and Σ_i1/t_i=0. Consequently c_0=0. Formula (11) then gives an approximant supported on precisely p nonzero sites and no site-zero shear. These rational nodes require no field extension or algebraic closure.

For example, N=4 uses t_1=1,t_2=−1 and weights 1/8,−1/8:

    f(x)=(x²/8)[(1−x)^(−4)−(1+x)^(−4)]
         =x³+5x⁵+O(x⁷).

For N=3, take t_1=1, c_1=1/4, c_0=−1/4. Then f=x³+(5/2)x⁴+O(x⁵).

## 6. Sharp scalar-flow word length

A word whose total M-time is zero traces a walk of accumulated times starting at zero and ending at zero. Every nonzero partial-fraction site present in (8) must be visited at an application of a T factor. With p distinct nonzero sites:

- at least p nonidentity T factors are required;
- at least p+1 nonidentity M factors are required to leave zero, visit p distinct nonzero values, and return to zero.

Repeated visits, extra intermediate times, and cancellations cannot reduce either lower bound. Thus every approximant with p≥1 has length at least 2p+1, and for N≥4 the pole bound gives length at least 2N−3.

For the balanced sites (13), formula (6) realizes the correction with exactly p T factors and p+1 M factors. All the times in that expression are nonzero, and all the coefficients in (11) are nonzero. The resulting word therefore has length 2p+1=2N−3. This proves sharpness over every characteristic-zero field.

It also describes the necessary site condition for a shortest word: it must use p=N−2 nonzero sites and no additional site-zero coefficient. By (11), its sites must satisfy Σ_i1/t_i=0, and its nonzero-site weights are the unique ones in (11). Different visit orders give the same final vertical shear.

When N=3, the lower bound is only three factors. A three-factor returning word with a nonzero site would have to be M_t,T_c,M_(−t), with c≠0. Its x² correction c cannot match the zero x² coefficient of x³. Thus three factors are impossible. The construction at the end of section 5 uses T_(−1/4),M_1,T_(1/4),M_(−1), exactly four factors. For N≤2 the target jet is the identity, represented by the empty word. This completes (2).

## 7. What survives locally, and what fails globally

The cubic shear has polynomial coordinates and a polynomial inverse. Its absence from the exact finite group is not a Jacobian obstruction; it is an obstruction in this particular two-flow generation problem. The explicit approximants converge point-adically to it, but their finite poles proliferate at the sharp rate p≥N−2. A fixed upper bound on the number of nonzero poles would bound the attainable contact order by N≤p+2.

These poles lie on affine divisors x=1/t_i in the original plane. Calling them merely poles at infinity would be incorrect. The approximants here are rational maps, not polynomial automorphisms; their growing denominator degree does not directly instantiate [the earlier criterion about a uniform boundary divisor](../planar_autonomous_lie_jets_20261019/finite_symplectic_jets_and_boundary_control.md) for inverses of polynomial automorphism approximants. What the present example proves is a separate, exact rational pole-complexity barrier. Recovering a global theorem for polynomial Keller maps still requires an additional invariant compatible with the relevant global class.
