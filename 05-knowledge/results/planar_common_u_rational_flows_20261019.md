# Explicit rational Hamiltonian flows beyond the joint-boundary cone

2026-10-19. **PROVED:** over any characteristic-zero field, the source-completed Hamiltonian flows of f(x²t), with f′(0)=0, are explicit birational determinant-one maps. Their finite common-invariant words are polynomial exactly in the identity-cancellation case. This supplies rational nonpolynomial outputs outside the Newton cone, including flows fixing both distinguished curves pointwise. It is not a polynomial Keller map counterexample or a resolution of planar JC.

## 1. Source completion and exact formula

Work in the original polynomial coordinates x,t over a characteristic-zero field K, with {A,B}=A_xB_t−A_tB_x. Put u=x²t and q=1+u. Let f∈K[Z] satisfy f′(0)=0. For λ∈K define δ_f={−,f(u)}. Direct computation gives

    δ_f(u)=0,   δ_f(x)=x²f′(u),   δ_f(t)=−2xtf′(u).

Write f′(u)=u b(u). Then δ_f(x) is divisible by t and δ_f(t) by t². Consequently δ_f maps t^n K[x][[t]] into t^(n+1) K[x][[t]] for every n≥0. Its scalar-time exponential is well-defined and continuous in the source t-adic completion. The same estimate justifies the inverse exponential and finite compositions.

Set a=f′(u) and D=1−λxa. Since a is divisible by t, D is a unit of K[x][[t]], with polynomial coefficient in x at each t-degree. The flow is exactly

    X=exp(λδ_f)(x)=x/D,
    T=exp(λδ_f)(t)=tD².                              (1)

Indeed δ_f^n(x)=n! x^(n+1)a^n, giving the geometric series. Also δ_f(t)=−2xta, δ_f²(t)=2x²ta², and δ_f³(t)=0, giving the finite expression for T. The formula is therefore the specified scalar-time source exponential, not merely a formal-time rational identity. In particular X≡x mod t and T≡t mod t², so the entire line t=0 is fixed pointwise.

The hypothesis f′(0)=0 matters for this source completion: if f′(0)≠0 and λ≠0, the geometric series for X is not t-adically convergent. Formula (1) still defines a rational formal-time flow, but that broader interpretation is not used here.

## 2. Birationality, Jacobian and domains

Equation (1) gives X²T=x²t=u, so u and q are preserved exactly as rational functions. Replacing λ by −λ gives the rational inverse: its denominator is 1+λXa=1/D. Thus (1) is a birational map of the plane, defined on D≠0, whose rational inverse has the same form with reversed time.

Its Jacobian determinant is one. One direct verification uses the rational coordinate pair (x,u): since du=2xt dx+x²dt,

    dx∧du=x² dx∧dt.

At fixed u, ∂X/∂x=1/D², while X²=x²/D². Therefore dX∧du=(1/D²)dx∧du=X²dx∧dt. Since u=X²T, also dX∧du=X²dX∧dT. Cancellation in the rational function field gives dX∧dT=dx∧dt. This calculation is a rational identity; it does not require treating (x,u) as global polynomial affine coordinates.

For λ≠0 and nonconstant f, D is nonconstant and gcd(D,x)=1 in K[x,t], since D≡1 mod x. Thus x/D is not polynomial. In fact D is nonconstant because xa≠0 and is divisible by x. There is no cancellation in its first coordinate and hence no polynomial realization equal to this completed flow. The map is not a global polynomial Keller map; Jacobian one on a rational domain does not supply properness or global regularity.

## 3. Exact commuting-word classification

All δ_f commute because they are f′(u) times {−,u}, which kills their coefficients. For f_i′(0)=0 and λ_i∈K, put

    F(Z)=Σ_i λ_i f_i(Z).

Finite composition equals exp(δ_F), with

    X=x/(1−xF′(u)),   T=t(1−xF′(u))².               (2)

This follows either from convergent commuting exponentials or by directly composing the rational formulas using invariant u. Every such word is birational and rational. It is polynomial if and only if F′=0, equivalently F is constant in characteristic zero, exactly when the word is identity. Nontrivial cancellations are allowed; nonconstant individual Hamiltonians do not prevent the total word from cancelling.

## 4. The q-divisible Hamiltonian subfamily

Within f′(0)=0, the condition f(−1)=f(0) is equivalent to

    f(Z)=c+Z²(Z+1)g(Z),   c=f(0),   g∈K[Z].

Indeed f−c is divisible by Z², and the extra root −1 supplies the coprime factor Z+1. In source coordinates this says

    H=f(u)=c+t²q R(x,t),   R=x⁴g(x²t).              (3)

For every nonzero g, each supported monomial of R has the form x^(4+2k)t^k, of boundary weight 2k−(4+2k)=−4. Thus (3) is strictly outside the [Newton cone](planar_joint_boundary_newton_cone_20261019.md), which requires weight at least −2. No conflict with its polynomial-rigidity theorem arises.

A particularly simple example is

    f(Z)=Z²(Z+1),   a=u(3u+2),
    X=x/[1−λxu(3u+2)],
    T=t[1−λxu(3u+2)]².                              (4)

For λ≠0, this is a nonidentity rational nonpolynomial source flow with R=x⁴. In contrast, [packet 122](planar_riccati_invariant_transcendence_20261019.md) proves coordinatewise transcendence for nonzero scalar-time flows generated by nonconstant polynomials of E=x²t²q, whose basic member has R=x². These are different invariant families.

## 5. What happens on the second boundary

Parametrize q=0 rationally by

    x=−1/s,   t=−s²,   s=xt.

The curve itself has s≠0. Since XT=xtD=s−λu f′(u), the induced rational transformation on this boundary is

    s ↦ s+λ f′(−1).                                (5)

For (3), f′(−1)=g(−1). When g=1, this is translation by λ. Its infinitesimal boundary derivation is ∂_s, which does not raise the order at s=0. This explains why the previous joint-boundary convergence argument cannot be transferred to this example. Equation (5) is an identity of boundary rational function fields; for a nonzero translation it is not an everywhere-defined self-map of the punctured parameter line. In particular the point s=−λ f′(−1) lies on the denominator locus when that value is nonzero.

There are also nonpolynomial rational flows fixing both boundaries pointwise. Choose

    g(Z)=Z+1,   f(Z)=Z²(Z+1)²,
    f′(Z)=2Z(Z+1)(2Z+1).

Then f′(0)=f′(−1)=0. The denominator restricts to 1 on both t=0 and q=0, so the rational map is defined and identity along each entire affine curve. It remains nonpolynomial for every nonzero λ. Thus pointwise fixing both curves, without the earlier completion and polynomial hypotheses, cannot justify an inference of polynomiality or properness.
