# Two pendant leaf-pairs: parity rigidity beyond hereditary symmetry and automorphisms

19 October 2026. **PROVED, scoped; independently [reviewed](review/CHERRY_EXTENSION_REVIEW.md).** This is a separate extension of [TWIN_SIGNATURE_PARITY.md](TWIN_SIGNATURE_PARITY.md), not an assertion that its recursive automorphism filter classifies every good tree. No historical-priority claim is made.

## 1. The universal local reduction

Let S be an oriented graph. For an arc e, deletion–reversal gives, pointwise on every tournament host T,

    emb(S,T) + emb(reverse_e S,T) = emb(S-e,T).

This equality counts injective embeddings; the host may have more vertices than S. Its proof is that any two distinct host vertices support exactly one of the two possible arc directions. It is false for arbitrary directed hosts that may omit both arcs.

Suppose x,y are pendant leaves with the same neighbor v. If their two arcs point the same way, swapping x,y is an oriented automorphism and every embedding count is even. If they point opposite ways, reversing the edge incident to x produces equal directions. The reversed term therefore has even embedding count, so

    emb(S,T) = emb(S-vx,T) modulo 2.

The deleted-edge graph has x as an isolated vertex. This is a deletion on the SAME vertex set; discarding the isolated vertex would change the count and invalidate the next step.

**Theorem 1 (two-cherry vanishing).** Suppose an undirected graph has two disjoint pairs of pendant leaves, attached at distinct centers. Every orientation has even injective embedding count into every tournament host.

**Proof.** If either pair has equal directions, its transposition proves the claim. Otherwise apply the preceding deletion–reversal reduction to one edge from each pair. The second pair is unaffected by the first deletion. The resulting graph has two isolated vertices, whose swap acts freely on embeddings. Its count, and hence the original count, is even. ∎

This applies to arbitrary remaining graph structure. It is not merely an orientation-automorphism test. In particular the original oriented graph may have trivial automorphism group.

## 2. A minimal nine-vertex tree outside both old sufficient classes

Let U have vertices 0,...,8 and edges

    01, 02, 03, 04, 15, 16, 27, 78.

The pairs {3,4} at 0 and {5,6} at 1 are pendant leaf-pairs, so Theorem 1 proves every orientation has identically even tournament embedding count.

Deleting vertices 4 and 6 leaves the asymmetric spider S(1,2,3): its center is 0 and its three branches are 3; 1-5; 2-7-8. Thus U is not hereditarily symmetric.

The un-oriented automorphism group is exactly C2 x C2, generated by the two leaf-pair swaps. Orient all listed edges in their increasing direction except 04 and 16. Both pairs then have opposite directions, so neither swap is allowed. No other un-oriented automorphism exists: the center and the distinct remaining rooted branches fix the rest of the tree. This orientation therefore has trivial automorphism group. The recursive formula in the core packet gives A(U)=16 asymmetric orientation classes, comprising 64 of the 256 labelled orientation masks.

Consequently U is good but is in neither of the two classes

    hereditary symmetry; every orientation has an even automorphism group.

The proof is structural, for every tournament host. The core packet's exact n<=8 nonconstancy certificates show that order nine is minimal for any good tree outside hereditary symmetry, hence also for this stronger counterexample. The conjectural replacement “good iff hereditary or A(U)=0” is therefore refuted, even if it was proposed only as a possible repair.

For the displayed asymmetric orientation, the transitive nine-tournament has 260 embeddings, while the graph's own directed non-tournament host has exactly one. This retains the all-host versus tournament-host distinction.

## 3. Constructive closure and its stopping point

The leaf-pair reduction can be applied locally before attempting larger graph-algebra calculations:

1. An equal-direction pendant pair proves zero immediately.
2. An opposite-direction pendant pair can be replaced by one retained leaf and one isolated vertex, modulo two.
3. A second isolated vertex proves zero by a free swap.

The retained isolated vertex is the essential new coordinate. An algorithm that immediately removes isolated vertices would erase the mechanism. This explains why the earlier recursion, which detects automorphisms of the original orientation, could not see this family.

Through eleven vertices, the number of trees with two distinct pendant-pair centers, and the number outside both hereditary symmetry and A=0, are respectively:

    n=7:  2 and 0
    n=8:  6 and 0
    n=9: 14 and 3
    n=10:38 and 12
    n=11:94 and 33.

These are finite filter counts, not a classification of all good trees. The general classification remains open. The global Rédei and unavoidable-shaving extremal questions also remain untouched: every orientation in this two-cherry class has even count.

## 4. Reproduction

Run `python3 -B verify_two_cherry.py` and `python3 -B -O verify_two_cherry.py` beside the frozen core `verify_twin_signature.py`. The receipts agree. The checker verifies all 256 orientation masks by source-side deletion/reversal certificates: 192 have a direct leaf-swap automorphism, and 64 follow both deletion steps to two isolated vertices. It checks the 16 asymmetric orientation classes and the census above, and independently verifies the concrete 260 transitive embeddings and one self-host embedding. These are controls for the universal proof, not an enumeration of all tournament hosts.
