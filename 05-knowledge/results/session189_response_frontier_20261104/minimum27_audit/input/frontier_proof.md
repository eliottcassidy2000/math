# Original-module collisions require highest prime quotients of order at least nine

## 1. Exact scope and independently reviewed inputs

Fix a named tournament T. Select any family of modules of this **original** T and toggle each edge according to the parity of selected modules containing both endpoints. Repetitions cancel; the family may overlap, nest or cross. Let U be the simultaneous XOR target on exactly the same physical names.

The full response F_T(a,c) is the Hamilton count after adding actual ports x->y, with a_v=[v->x] and c_v=[v->y]. Equality means equality at every physical query, not equality of a few coefficients, aggregate endpoint statistics, or independently relabeled objects.

## 2. Restoring all other highest changed nodes at the same query

Assume T differs from U but F_T=F_U. The actual query a=c=0 has x,y before every old name and therefore F_T(0,0)=H(T). Hence H(T)=H(U). The reviewed hereditary equality theorem supplies

    q_h(T[S])=q_h(U[S])

for every fixed old-name subset S and every h, where q_h counts ordered spanning covers by h nonempty directed paths.

The inherited reduction gives a nonempty antichain of highest changed original strong nodes. They are pairwise disjoint, and every changed old edge lies inside one of them. All their exterior incidences are unchanged. By input1, every highest changed quotient is prime of order at least eight.

Suppose one highest changed node M has exactly eight original children A_0,...,A_7. These fixed physical child sets remain modules on both sides. A changed original prime quotient can only become its converse. Every corresponding child has the same complete q profile by hereditary equality, even if many descendant operations occurred.

Let V agree with U inside M and with T everywhere else. The exterior is a fixed ambient for the same module M. Input2 supplies an actual child-uniform query z with

    F_T(z) != F_V(z).                                    (1)

Both ports have M's original incidence to every exterior name. The proof of input2 permits arbitrary profile-preserving target child interiors; it does not assume that the children literally remain unchanged or that their changes are single reversals.

For any other highest changed node N, the complete profiles of T[N] and U[N] agree. M and N are disjoint modules, so their cross arcs are uniform. Both ports imitate M outside M and thus see N uniformly. Accordingly N is a module in the **same actual augmented graph for z**. Exact occupied-run substitution allows replacing T[N] by U[N] without changing the queried Hamilton count.

Restore every such N successively. Pairwise disjointness and unchanged boundaries keep every substitution valid. All changed old edges lie within the highest-node antichain, so the final tournament is exactly U. Consequently F_V(z)=F_U(z), contradicting(1).

No detectability theorem is required for another highest node N, even if its quotient has unresolved larger order. Its equal complete profile and the actual ports' uniform incidences suffice. The argument is exact in the integers and is not a presumed noncancellation of arbitrary scalar gaps.

## 3. Necessary frontier and what it does not assert

**Theorem.** In a nontrivial full named-response collision obtained by simultaneous XOR of modules of the original source, **every highest changed original strong quotient must be prime of order at least nine**.

In particular, if every prime quotient in T's original strong-module tree has order at most eight, every original-module XOR target different from T is detected by the full named response. The inherited exclusions cover linear nodes of arbitrary order and all smaller possible prime-quotient orders; there is no omitted order-four exception or nonlinear two-child case.

This does not assert a collision at order nine, exclude all larger prime quotients, or establish unrestricted named-response injectivity. It is source-relative, not a claim of pairwise injectivity among arbitrary family outputs. The original source's modules need not remain modules of an intermediate target, so that stronger conclusion does not follow by composing XOR families. Arbitrary inversion sequences using new/current modules are outside scope.

## 4. Separate prime-eight root-family battery

Fix a prime-eight source core, its actual child sets and any fixed rooted ambient. Restrict selected original modules to sets contained inside this core, and select the whole core oddly. Do not select any operation involving an exterior name.

The prime-eight baseline theorem supplies a fixed battery B of one or three child-uniform queries. Pair each with J(a,c)=(1-c,1-a) on **core bits only**, leaving both ports' exterior incidences unchanged. The battery B union J(B) has at most six actual queries and is independent of the selected family target.

Every proper original module lies in one prime child. The target quotient is therefore the converse, while every target child's complete profile is coordinatewise at least its source profile by hereditary original-module monotonicity. The whole-core converse does not change any child's complete profile.

At least one baseline query is nonzero. Complete augmented-profile duality gives opposite exact baseline ambient contrasts for it and its core converse-complement. Choose the member with negative source-minus-baseline contrast. Its child-uniform run substitution has nonnegative coefficients, so increasing target child profiles cannot cancel that strict negative contrast. Thus this battery detects every such root-containing family relative to its fixed source. For the specified ambient, one baseline-negative member can be fixed for all those targets; choosing that sign may depend on the ambient.

This restricted source-relative result is distinct from Section2. It neither absorbs exterior operations nor replaces the equal-profile restoration argument for other highest changed nodes.
