## 1. Uniform rules: the end of the dilation family and the p mod 4 dichotomy

THM-4724 had killed the derivatives `D_c`. The general dilation rule `f_phi(S) = {x : phi(1_S(c_1 x), ..., 1_S(c_k x)) = 1}` dies by a collapse: at primes with `v_2(p-1) = 3` where every `c_i` is an eighth-power residue, the `K_max(p)`-invariant tournaments (the antisymmetric functions on `Z_8`, two multiplier types) all give the pattern `(b, ..., b)`, so `f_phi` is constant on them. Chebotarev supplies infinitely many such primes once the compatibility obstruction `zeta_(2^(e+1)) in Q(zeta_(2^e), c_i^(1/2^e))` is excluded, which a norm to `Q(zeta_(2^(e-1)))` does for `e >= 3` (`-zeta_(2^(e-1))` is not a square there); for `e = 2` it can fail (`i = 2((1+i)/2)^2`), which is exactly why `D_4` failed at `p = 5, 13, 17` in the earlier searches. First collapse primes: `c = 2`: 73, 89, 233; `{2,3}`: 601; `{2,3,5}`: 17881.

The signed-set Burnside principle puts the circulant problem in the same frame as Royle et al.: multipliers (or multipliers and power maps, `Gamma`) act on the class set with the free involution `x -> -x`; the orbits of tournaments equal the orbits of `Gamma`-even graphs. With the reference section `R = QR_p` (available iff `p = 3 mod 4`) the sign of `x -> a x^j` on a symmetric `S` is `(-1)^{(|S|/2)[a non-residue]}`, so even = even number of classes, every even circulant is `Gamma`-even, and the Paley rule — orient each pair against `QR_p`, take the backward classes or their complement, whichever has an even number of classes — is a canonical `Gamma`-equivariant type bijection (two-to-one on labelled sets exactly along `S ~ -S`). At `p = 1 (mod 4)` the flip `x -> x^{(p+1)/2} = x chi(x)` is in `Gamma`, fixes every symmetric set, and flips exactly its non-residue classes; a set with one residue class and one non-residue class is even but `Gamma`-odd, so `Gamma`-orbits of even circulants strictly exceed those of tournaments and no `Gamma`-equivariant (in particular no parameter-free multiplicative) rule exists. Census: `power_equivariance_fast.out`.

Reading: the prime-circulant bijection is natural exactly when a canonical reference tournament exists (`QR_p`), i.e. exactly when the odd part of `Z_p^*` has index two. HYP-9342 lists what is left (additive rules).

## 3. The Jacobian web

**State of the art in the repository** (inventory by subagent, 2026-10-20): JC is false for `n >= 3` (THM-1300, the Alpöge map `F = (u^3 z + y^2 u(4+3xy), y + 3xu^2 z + 3xy^2(4+3xy), 2x - 3x^2 y - x^3 z)`, `u = 1 + xy`, `det JF = -2`, triple collision), Dixmier is false for `A_n`, `n >= 3`; the Keller monoid (THM-1330), every generic degree `>= 3` (THM-3438), the Gao–Gallagher tangent-sweep family (THM-4625), the torus quotient of the fixed map with Jacobian `2(2-3v-t)^2` and its compression/correction no-gos (THM-3543/3549), the dual-field criterion `JC_2 <=> (dimension three: surjective dual field => automorphism)` (THM-4626), equivariant `JC_2` for every torus (THM-1345, THM-1370), GMC(2) proved with a complete Lean proof (THM-2022), planar strata through degree 22 and the row programme to row 15; absent from canon before this session: the Markus–Yamabe counterexample, the Nagata automorphism, Hilbert 14 / LND kernels, buildings / Nagao / property (T).

**What this session adds (THM-4727).** The ramification-exponent law (R): for a map equivariant for one positive and several negative weights, `det JF = x_1^{W-W'} l^{1-W'} det J Fbar`, where `l = X_1/x_1` is the orbit scaling and `Fbar` the quotient map; a Keller map therefore has `W = W'` and a quotient map that is Keller for the degenerate form `l^{1-W} dv`: the twisted planar Jacobian equation `{lA, l^2 B} = c l^2` for weights `(1,-1,-2)`. The entire known dimension-three corpus is the `deg l = 1` stratum of this equation (Proposition E), so "classify the counterexamples in dimension three (in the equivariant class)" is the planar problem (T) with integrality `A in (v,t)`, `B in (v^2,t)`; the Gao–Gallagher side conditions are its integrality. In dimension two the exponent is `0` for `(1,-1)` and the line quotient kills every fold for `(1,-w)` (Proposition D): the mechanism needs a mixed action and a quotient of dimension two, which first coexist in dimension three.

**The structure from other areas.** Three dimension-three phenomena with the profile "true/tame in dimension two, false/wild from three" are mixed-weight torus phenomena over a two-dimensional quotient plane: the Alpöge Keller collision (weights `(1,-1,-2)`, a generically 3:1 quotient map etale off `l = 0`; the fold surface over `l = 0` and the fixed plane cover the fixed hyperplane `2 + 1`, the two fold sheets meeting on one orbit as `x, -x` exactly at the triple collision; `fold_degree_check.out`), the Markus–Yamabe counterexample (weights `(1,2,-1)`, escape along an orbit over an equilibrium `(18,-12)` of the quotient field; its Keller companion is an automorphism), and the Nagata wild automorphism (weights `(3,1,-1)`, quotient `(yz, xz^3)`). All three were verified symbolically here. The atlas in HYP-9343 adds Dehn's invariant (a tensor of two incommensurable quantities), Banach–Tarski (free subgroups of `SO(3)`), Poincaré–Bendixson, cancellation and Hilbert 14, and the rank-one/rank-two reading of `Aut(A^2)` (tree) versus `Tame(A^3)` (2-complex); these are analogies, typed as such.

**The planar counterexample.** It would be the `l = 1` case: a fold that has gone to infinity. Constraints (all cited): torus-rigid (THM-1345/THM-4727 D), degree `>= 101`, non-prime degree and pencil height `>= 8` (THM-3550), target pencil of total degree `>= 6` (THM-3544), equivalently a dimension-three Keller map with a surjective dual field (THM-4626) or a counterexample to HC(4) (Meng–Yang). The creative argument the owner asked for, honestly weighed: the Alpöge-type maps show that a Keller map can be generically 3:1 while its quotient map is etale off a single line, the orbits over that line being collapsed and folded; the law `det J Fbar = c l^{W-1}` shows that this costs a factor `l^{W-1}` in the quotient Jacobian, and in the plane there is no quotient to pay with: a planar Keller map is etale everywhere and its missing preimages can only escape along the Jelonek set at infinity. A planar counterexample would therefore have to be "all escape at infinity": the sub-125 degree classification and the pencil floors are measurements of how far out that fold must sit. Nothing here decides `JC_2`.
