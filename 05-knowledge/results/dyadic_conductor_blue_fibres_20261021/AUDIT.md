# Internal mathematical audit of the conductor-tower packet

Date: 2026-10-21. This is the producing worker's audit, not an independent referee report.

## Objects and maps checked

- Classes mean proper invertible ideal classes. The conductor ideal itself is not treated as invertible.
- The residue quotient includes both odd scalar residues and all Eisenstein units. Omitting the unit factor would incorrectly multiply every class number by three.
- The conductor parameter f and idoneal parameter d are related by d=3(f/2)². They are not the same variable.
- Residue reduction is identified with actual ideal extension using prime-to-conductor contraction; the extension tables are not selected abstract homomorphisms.
- The reduced-form computation uses a second independent transport: choose odd leading coefficient, then halve the square-root root modulo it.
- A fibre of G8→G4 is a kernel torsor; the two-element class set G4 is a G8 quotient orbit. These are not asserted to have the same whole-group action.

## Main positive predicate check

For a finite blue parent, ε and η are the added membership bits in the balanced worker's left and reflected-right subsets. The zero-bit section gives the marked origin. The exact map is θ(ε,η)=B^ε C^η. Its genus signs are χ8=(-1)^ε, χ−8=(-1)^η, χ−4=(-1)^(ε+η). Thus actual conductor extension retains exactly the equality/XOR predicate on the newly inserted bits.

The balanced worker independently confirmed that these ε,η conventions agree with the explicit Hamilton-path parametrization and shore swap. This confirmation is about the interface, not an independent audit of the arithmetic proof.

## Hostile controls

1. A finite black parent has four black lifts, including parity-zero lifts. Therefore the arithmetic kernel is not a global blue criterion without the parent-coincidence sidecar.
2. Left-inactive and right-inactive two-element fibres have different stabilizers. In the blue-preserving marking these are <B> and <C>, while the conductor kernel is <A>.
3. At f=16, w has order four and w² is the nontrivial kernel of extension to f=8. Its reduced forms are (13,8,16) and (4,4,49), respectively; its inverse (13,-8,16) is distinct.
4. Every lift of A to G16 has order four, so the extension is nonsplit.
5. Shore reflection swaps B,C at f=8, but the image of G16[2] is {E,B}; hence no commuting group-automorphism lift exists. The finite checker exhausts all eight automorphisms of G16 as a second control.
6. Geometric negation induces the identity on ideals, and conjugation induces inversion. Both act trivially on G8, so neither can silently become a nontrivial class translation.
7. A principal ideal generated by a norm-ball point has identity class; nonintegral residue representatives cannot be silently replaced with the original ball carrier.
8. At non-dyadic f=40, the norm-modulo-8 kernel has six elements while the square subgroup has three. The explicit class α=1+8ω has norm57 and a negative norm-modulo-5 Legendre character, so it is not square. The genus-coordinate theorem is restricted to dyadic f.
9. No electrical edge/weight/response map or Hamilton-reversal-metagraph adjacency is supplied by the arithmetic quotient. The balanced packet's bypass-edge correction remains necessary.

## All-order versus finite evidence

The exact recurrence v2(B_r)=r+2, proved by induction for w^(2^r)=A_r+B_rω, proves the generator order at every dyadic conductor. Finite tests through f=128 do not establish this quantifier; they test the implementation and labels.
