# Three trees that should not be conflated

**PROVED:** forest/seam gauge factorization, frame addresses in the Berggren and Price trees, Price-gate tomography of ordered eligibility lists, and exact rank on the Berggren slope. The Pythagorean tree theorems are classical; this note uses them as measurement charts, not as a proof of count coverage.

## The physical tree and its three sections

An ordered Hamiltonian path in a two-hub tournament is $R_0\to x\to R_1\to y\to R_2$ in its hub order. Each $R_i$ is an increasing path on a disjoint subset of spine vertices and can be empty. Removing the hubs leaves a spanning linear forest on the spine with **at most** three actual components. For counting, the forest must have its runs labelled in order, retain the hub order, and retain the empty-run sentinels; forgetting these can identify different paths. A singleton run is a genuine component with coincident first/last positions.

Lock the last vertex of $R_0$, the first and last vertices of $R_1$, the first vertex of $R_2$, and all applicable empty sentinels. Also lock the hub order and check the required arcs, including $x\to y$ for empty middle. The potential intervals for **unanchored** sites in the three nonempty runs are $(-\infty,\max R_0)$, $(\min R_1,\max R_1)$, and $(\min R_2,\infty)$; an empty run has empty eligibility. Each frame is either impossible (a site has no available run) or its admissible labelled forests form a rectangle $\prod_i E_i$, where $E_i$ lists eligible runs at site $i$. Assign each site, sort within runs, and join at the certified hub arcs; this yields exactly one path and is invertible. Hence the disjoint sum over frames is $\sum_F 2^{\alpha(F)}3^{\beta(F)}$. The overlaps belong to the intervals of potential membership, not to the three disjoint vertex sets of any realized forest.

By contrast the switching chart uses **binary** information. Modulo simultaneous flip of all vertices, each edge constraint asks for one prescribed XOR between its endpoints' switch bits. A constraint graph with $m$ edges and $c$ components including isolates has rank $n-c$, with $m-n+c$ independent cycle parities to satisfy. Its compatible fraction of switching classes is $2^{-(m-n+c)}$, and each compatible class has $2^{c-1}$ representatives satisfying the constraints. In particular $m=n-1$ gives a transversal precisely for a spanning tree. The forest on $s$ spine vertices with $c$ nonempty components has $s-c$ constrained edges; including the two hubs as initially isolated vertices leaves $c+1$ switch bits. The $c+1$ seam edges in the complete Hamiltonian path spend all of them. The $3^\beta$ forest multiplicity is **not** the size of a switch fibre: it counts different choices of the tree, whereas the XOR chart works after its edges are fixed.

There is an exact pairwise-interference formula. For prescribed ordered paths $P,Q$, read each class in its unique $P$-compatible representative. If a common undirected edge gets opposite path directions, no such representative contains $Q$. Otherwise the fraction containing $Q$ is $2^{-|E(Q)\setminus E(P)|}$: the $P$-compatible representatives have all non-tree edge bits independently free. For example, exchange two singleton outer runs while retaining both hubs and the middle run; the shared middle junctions agree, and the two new outer junctions give fraction $1/4$. This measures how distinct forest readings coexist; it does not count them for a prescribed switching class.

## Pythagorean triples as lossless addresses

Enumerate the sites in a feasible frame with more than one eligible run, in spine order, omitting all forced sites. Assign the three run labels to the three letters of either the Berggren or Price tree, once for the frame. A legal word specifies a unique primitive Pythagorean triple by starting at $(3,4,5)$ and applying the letters chronologically. By unique tree descent that triple recovers the whole word and, from the frame, the original path. Both trees give injections; they do not generally give the same triples for the same words. Collision between frames is not excluded unless the frame tag is also retained. For one fixed tournament a global injective encoding is to prepend a letter for the hub order and then write the run label of **every** spine vertex, including anchors. Its paths sit at level $s+1$, generally as a proper subset.

* **Berggren:** in Euclid coordinates $0<m<n$ with coprime, opposite-parity entries, the children are $(n,2n-m),(n,2n+m),(m,2m+n)$, in disjoint slope $n/m$ intervals $(1,2),(2,3),(3,\infty)$. See the convention in [THM-3357](../../../01-canon/theorems/THM-3357-berggren-three-branch-walsh-level-collapse-and-parent-circuit.md). The slope maps for a parent's slope $t$ are $2-1/t,\ 2+1/t,\ 2+t$. The first and third are increasing, the middle decreasing. Thus to read a frame's triples in increasing slope, use the **last** site's allowed digits in branch order first and divide into equal groups (product of the remaining eligibility sizes). Then peel sites right-to-left, reversing the comparison direction exactly when stripping a middle letter. This gives exact rank/unrank of all paths of a marked frame without enumerating them. The first differing digit *from the right*, not conventional lexical order, decides the comparison, with possible reversal from the shared suffix.
* **Price:** in reduced $q/p\in(0,1)$ with opposite-parity parameters, the children have fractions $2q/(p+q),\ (p-q)/(2p),\ (p+q)/(2p)$. They remain reduced because $p\pm q$ are odd and coprime to $p$ and to $q$. The first child has even numerator; the latter two have odd numerators and lie on opposite sides of $1/2$. Its inverse halves the even entry, replaces the other entry by its absolute difference from the halved value, then sorts. The difference becomes zero only at $(q,p)=(1,2)$, the primitive root. See [Price, The Pythagorean Tree: A New Species, §§4–5](https://arxiv.org/abs/0809.4324).

Both trees retain enough information to invert at each step. The product $2^\alpha3^\beta$ supplies no such decoder for the original choices of a frame.

## An exact binary subtree inside the Price ternary chart

The Pythagoras family $P(n)=(2n+1,2n(n+1),2n(n+1)+1)$, $n\ge1$, has Euclid pair $(n,n+1)$. Price's first and third branches send it to $P(2n)$ and $P(2n+1)$; its middle sends it to the Plato family $((2n+2)^2-1,4n+4,(2n+2)^2+1)$. Thus starting from $P(1)$, staying in the Pythagoras family means exactly “never choose the middle branch.” It is a complete binary subtree: every $P(n)$ has the binary address given by the bits of $n$ after the leading 1, so uniqueness of Price descent also forbids re-entry after a middle move.

Choose a marked run label $r$ for Price's middle digit. The number of a frame's triples **on** the Pythagoras family is $\prod_i |E_i\setminus\{r\}|$. The number whose address first exits at position $i$ is
$$
 [r\in E_i]\prod_{j<i}|E_j\setminus\{r\}|\prod_{j>i}|E_j|.
$$
These formulas retain names of runs and their chronology. In the same tournament with word **10200** (letter $2[x\to v]+[y\to v]$, $x\to y$), label the spine vertices $0,\ldots,4$. The anchor tuples $(3,2,4,0)$ and $(1,2,4,0)$, in the order “last, first, last, first” defined above, have respective free sets $E=\{0,2\}$ and $E=\{1,2\}$. Both have weight 2. Singling out run 1, the first has both addresses in the Pythagoras family, whereas the second has one there and one at the immediate Plato gate. Singling out run 2, the frames have identical gate counts, illustrating what one observer can miss.

### Two-observer reconstruction

Let $S_{r,i}$ count paths whose first $i$ free-site choices avoid $r$; equivalently their first $i$ Price ancestor steps stay in the Pythagoras family under that assignment of run labels. Then
$$
 S_{r,i}/S_{r,i-1}=1-[r\in E_i]/|E_i|.
$$
The denominator never vanishes because we omit forced sites. The values $1,1/2,2/3$ state respectively absent, present in a binary choice, and present in a ternary choice. **Two** distinct singled-out runs suffice to reconstruct all the ordered lists: at least one measured run is present at every free site, and any nontrivial ratio gives $|E_i|$, after which the third membership follows by subtraction. One observer does not suffice, as shown by run 2 on the two frames above. This uses the binary/ternary Pythagorean geometry to reconstruct exactly which overlaps were dropped by the smooth monomial count.

The older [ternary Berggren bridge](https://github.com/eliottcassidy2000/math/blob/1b91742ce/05-knowledge/results/ternary_berggren_20260925.md) already separated residue-completed adding-machine behavior from native integer descent. The use here is different: a branch-address readout is attached to a literal Hamiltonian frame, and the two Price probes reconstruct which slots are actually available at each digit.

## Proof boundary

The inverse operates on a **marked frame plus a triple**, or on sufficiently enriched gate profiles. A target integer count provides neither. Nor is there a theorem saying a Price gate preserves an absolute Hamiltonian count while moving to a smaller source. The classical trees have a branch selector plus a strictly decreasing inverse on their actual parameters; the missing analogue for the global count is a selector retaining enough endpoint data to complete descent. The pairwise switching formula likewise concerns fractions of labelled classes, not a guaranteed phase of the physical fixed-spine wheel.
