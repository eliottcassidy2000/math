# Target passports: what can be selected after the sevenfold seams

This session treats the absolute count as the input and separates three tasks: changing an exact signed correction independently, choosing its residue, and landing at the requested integer. The first two are now constructive in new families. The third has a complete decision rule within one infinite family and remains open in general.

- [Prefix inverse and all-modulus phase compiler](PREFIX_INVERSE.md): arbitrary even binary prefix on a genuine full-boundary +14 switch. A prescribed quotient can be decoded at all permitted heights in O(√p+log p) low-bit cells, and at large heights in one cell per height. Separately, at **any one fixed height**, literal prefix words hit every odd-compatible residue modulo any modulus. The 2-adic program and odd program can be made independent; phase control does not bound the output against the absolute target.
- [Independent ordinal digits](ORDINAL_DIGITS.md): block variants share the entire interface read by neighbors, yet change the absolute offset independently. A seven-block bank gives 20 sevenfold counts at a fixed height; three copies jointly realize all odd quotient residues mod 144. CRT decodes an actual absolute candidate, and the equality is checked separately. The [prime certificate replay](replay_prime_digits.py), using the [certificate data](prime_cert.json), verifies three exact prime-quotient examples (two at the same height), not an infinitude or exhaustive range claim.
- [Contact ledger and deletion growth barrier](CONTACT_PASSPORT.md): the linear slope and quadratic offset live on the same contact set. Trimming away untagged ordinal tails proves that a bounded deletion count gives only finitely many dyadic coefficient rays; it also yields polynomial growth of the deletion path count for certain prescribed prime classes. The first deletion counts for an integral sevenfold slope and exact offsets −7 and +7 are 13 and 15, with explicit all-order constructions.
- [Signed variable offsets](SIGNED_OFFSETS.md): four literal two-cover tiles give exact signed targets with variable smooth offsets. For a fixed noncancelling offset, using the wrong reader sign forces polynomial growth in the source count. This is an arithmetic constraint on physical profiles, not a bound inferred from a search.
- [Homogeneous-gap obstruction](GAP_WORDS.md): regardless of the growth rates, no one-apex word made of finitely many growing gaps of the same symbol gives the plus dyadic ray. With two gaps, either symbol is allowed and neither sign works. This explains why the separate changed-core signed-ray constructions are needed; it says nothing about unrestricted apex contact words.

**Remaining proof obligation:** pick a core or a boundary from the prescribed p so the exact interval lands there, not only its congruence class. Neither independent digits nor full phase reach determines that center. The full Chan–Pak conjecture remains open.
