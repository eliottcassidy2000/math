# A geometric obstruction that survives projecting from the full tournament

The [OpenAI no-five-coloring preprint and Lean scope](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/main/lean/docs/158.md) say that the Euclidean unit-distance graph does not admit five colors. By graph-coloring compactness this entails some finite six-chromatic unit-distance subgraph (restrict to a minimal finite counterexample and delete a vertex to see its chromatic number is exactly six). This is not the same as producing a small geometric specimen. In the other direction, [Haugland's 2131-vertex construction](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04542) gives explicit five-chromatic unit-distance geometry built from a structured sevenfold template, avoiding the Moser spindle. [Koshelev–Kupavskii–Voronov](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.18907v1) use character-law rigidity and compatible finite colorings with prescribed colors to remove exceptional pairs in a three-dimensional measurable-coloring argument. The concrete question here, inspired also by the [repo's port-relation work](../six_colour_interface_profile_20261015f.md), is whether retaining selected incidences also retains numerical weights when the other degrees of freedom are summed out.

Let Ω(T) be the graph of directed odd cycles in T, with edges for intersecting vertex sets. The [repo's induced conflict embedding theorem](../chanpak_cross_problem_theorems_20261114/CONFLICT_GRAPHS.md) embeds every finite graph as a selected induced Ω-subgraph. In a single SCC of T with more than one vertex, an odd cycle of length n or n−1 meets every other one and yields a universal vertex of the conflict component. Different SCCs of T have disjoint cycles.

**Projection obstruction.** Let G be any finite connected unit-distance graph in the plane with chromatic number at least four. In every induced selected-cycle representation of G, the full unweighted odd-cycle packing measure differs from the desired hard-core law of G, even on its selected marginal. The discrepancy can be bounded exactly in terms of the unselected part.

Proof. A planar unit-distance graph with a universal vertex is at most three-colorable. Indeed all remaining vertices lie on the unit circle centered at that vertex. A unit-distance chord there joins angles separated by ±π/3. Thus the remaining graph is a subgraph of disjoint 6-cycles, hence bipartite. The universal vertex can use a third color. In an induced selected representation of connected G, all selected cycles lie in one conflict component, so its universal vertex z must be unselected. Write E for the graph of the unselected cycles in that component, and I(E;2) for its independence polynomial at activity 2. Summing the full hard-core weight over unselected cycles, the odds of a selected independent set S against the empty selected state are
```
weight(S)/weight(empty) = 2^|S| I(E−N(S);2) / I(E;2).
```
Other conflict components cancel from this ratio. If S is nonempty, z is in N(S). As z is universal even among E, I(E;2)=I(E−z;2)+2. Consequently the odds are at most 2^|S| · (1−2/I(E;2)), strictly below their desired unconstrained value 2^|S|. This holds for every nonempty selected independent set, including a singleton. ∎

This shows the discrepancy without any assumption about the coordinates of a finite six-chromatic witness or about all unselected conflict incidences. It also explains exactly why a satisfactory finite selected incidence object does not lift to a tournament with the same partition function. One can condition the touching unselected cycles not to be used, or assign them activity zero; unrestricted Hamiltonian counts do neither. The inequality is an odds distortion, not a uniform distortion bounded away from zero as |E| grows, nor a proof that scalar partition-function coincidences are impossible.
