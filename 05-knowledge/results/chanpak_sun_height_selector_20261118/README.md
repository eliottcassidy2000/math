# Height control for the clone family, by a single high bit

This continues the [half-grid selector](../chanpak_half_grid_selector_20261118/README.md).
We replace exponential vertex cost for unrestricted dyadic
selection by an explicit quadratic bound, using a fixed marked
core. We also give a complete absolute inverse for the entire
clone family. The inverse rejects many actual tournament counts;
this is not yet a universal Chan–Pak selector.

## 1. The analogy to Sun: a local match and a height bound are different obligations

In [Sun, arXiv:2611.14605v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.14605v2),
§6 arranges separate local conditions on a shared elliptic
comparison point, while keeping the real-side estimates independent
of the CRT progression. In §7 the height inequalities are used
to get exactness rather than just compatible local approximations.
The Lucas construction in Lemma 6.4 is also a useful warning:
a valid divisibility condition can force exponentially large
auxiliary values. The paper's 26 is its number of existential
rational variables: 25 continuously free coordinates plus one
compiler coordinate (§8), not a bound on their sizes. We borrow the
separation of two obligations; its Diophantine result does not give
a tournament construction.

The earlier [24/25/26 lattice lens](../lattices_modular_repunits_20261021.md)
distinguishes a 24-dimensional positive lattice from its
Lorentzian extension of signature (25,1), rank 26. Here the
operational analogue is to keep the independent value and height
coordinates separate rather than to identify a rank with a size.
Concretely, on the half-grid, 49 and 51 sit at 24.5 and 25.5,
straddling 25; the neighboring whole numbers 24 and 26 are not
in that proper-half layer. The interval product has the exact
companion 25²−1=24·26. In the actual path-count coordinates the
one-arc pair 49/51 yields 2499=49·51, and the other side of the
squared center is 2501=41·61, using
4·5⁴+1=(2·5²−2·5+1)(2·5²+2·5+1).
Both are physical counts by ordinal products; this is a concrete
small height-control example, not a uniform interval theorem.

## 2. A triangular-height lift at fixed core size

The exact read for the previous core, with zero clones and an
odd left tag u, is

    H(u)=25u+11F(u)+17,   F(0)=1,
    F(2q+1)=F(q),   F(2q)=F(q)+q+[q odd] for 2q>0.

Let r be the ordinary binary width of u and B its r-bit reversal.
For b≥1, form v = u + 2^(r+b): prepend one high bit, separated
from the old word by exactly b zeroes. Both u and B are odd.
With s(u)=u−F(u), the previous concatenation calculation
specializes to

    s(v)−s(u)=2^b B,
    H(v)−H(u)=2^b(36·2^r−11B).

The coefficient in parentheses is odd. For a direct derivation,
the high prefix before appending the r old bits is q=2^b;
F(q)=q+1. When appending the old bits, the cross term in F is
q·RC_r(u), with RC_r(u)=2^r−1−B; q even removes the boundary
bonus. Subtract F(u) to get the displayed read. Thus the change
flips just the desired bit modulo 2^(b+1), preserving every
lower bit. It simultaneously has induced physical meaning:
the entire old tagged path stays in order, and we add the
high bit and its zeros ahead of it, leaving all old arcs.

Start at u=1; both H and the target N are odd. Work upward
b=1,...,L−1. Prepend the bit exactly when H differs from N in
bit b. This deterministic rule gives H≡N mod 2^L. The left
width is at most

    1 + Σ_(b=1..L−1)(b+1) = L(L+1)/2.

The whole strong tournament has at most L(L+1)/2+6 vertices.
Because F(u)≤u+1, the witness count is less than
36·2^(L(L+1)/2)+28. In terms of the modulus M=2^L this is
M^(O(log M)), not exponential in M. The general all-moduli
selector from the prior packet remains valid; this new bound
is specifically dyadic.

The bound is sharp for this particular algorithm: take the
target residue generated by choosing the prepend operation
at every stage. A first skipped bit would give a different
residue and later moves cannot change that bit. No claim is
made that every construction needs quadratic width.

## 3. The absolute decoder for every clone depth

Let k≥0 vary, h=12·2^k−1, R=36·3^k−h. The exact count for
an odd address u is

    H(k,u)=R u + hF(u)+2h−5 .

Here R≥24·3^k and gcd(R,h)=1. In particular a target N can
use only k with 24·3^k≤N. For a chosen k, choose the smallest
r≥0 with R·2^r≥√N; enumerate possible low r-bit suffixes s
for u, odd if r>0. Write u=2^r q+s. At that fixed suffix,
a putative exact witness must solve

    D=Aq+hF(q)+hg[q odd],
    A=R·2^r+h·RC_r(s),
    D=N−(2h−5)−Rs−h(F(s)−1).

Here g is 1 for a nonempty suffix with leading 0, else 0.
For r=0 use s=0 and RC=0. This is the binary boundary
identity from the [earlier inverse strategy](../chanpak_half_grid_selector_20261118/README.md),
now applied to the whole variable-depth core. It gives two
simultaneous restrictions:

    q = D·(R·2^r)^(-1) mod h,
    max(0,ceil((D−2h)/(A+h))) ≤ q ≤ floor((D−h)/A).

The interval is forced by 1≤F(q)≤q+1. Dividing its length by
the step h gives O(1+N/A²) possibilities; since A≥√N this is
O(1) per suffix. Evaluate exact equality only for those
possibilities, keeping positive odd u. No large modular
witness has to be constructed to decide this absolute query.

The sum of suffix budgets is O(√N+log N), using the
geometrically increasing lower bound for R. These are
integer candidate evaluations of polylogarithmic bit cost,
not polynomial time in the bit length of N. For every
accepted N the graph itself has at most
floor(log₂(N/24))+7 vertices: k+bitlength(u)+6 is bounded by
the read H≥24u·3^k. The inverse is complete for this grammar,
not for arbitrary tournaments. For instance it rejects
239 and 343, both realized by other constructions.

## 4. Why improving height alone in this grammar cannot finish

For k fixed, H≥24u·3^k, so at most X/(48·3^k)+1 positive
odd u can give H≤X. Summing over k gives at most X/32+O(log X)
distinct counts below X, even allowing **every** binary
address, not just the congruence compiler's words. At most
a sixteenth of odd absolute targets can be covered
asymptotically. The modular puzzle can be solved in this
grammar at every modulus; the absolute puzzle cannot.
In particular a uniform witness bound H<M cannot hold for
its representatives of all odd residues modulo large even M.
For the fixed zero-clone core the coefficient **25** gives an
even sharper bound: H≥25u+28 allows at most X/50+O(1) outputs.
If all odd residues mod an even M have representatives with
H≤CM in that fixed core, then C≥25−o(1). The central 25 thus
also occurs as an exact height obstruction, independently of
the nearby-square example.

The next step is therefore structural. The exact inverse
shows which numerical height interval and congruence a
candidate must enter. A richer choice of core is needed to
change those intervals, and a useful new family would let
the target choose the core without making the two
coordinates independent by assumption.

Run `python3 verify.py` for literal fixed-core hosts, residue
lifts through ten bits, the signed square example, and
absolute inverse controls against exhaustive small queries.
