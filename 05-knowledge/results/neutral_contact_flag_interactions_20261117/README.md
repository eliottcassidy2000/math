# Growing a neutral cube: the exact four-contact interaction

**Result.** There are arbitrarily many independently selectable *internal* contacts preserving the exact ±1 correction. Their absolute centers have a finite exact reader, but the choices do not add like independent digits: the fourth mixed difference is strictly positive for any four sites, even for four ordered balanced two-site swaps. This architecture is sparse. The result explains both what grows and why that growth cannot yet place every center.

## 1. A neutral cube with no bound on dimension

Use the [signed ordinal tiles](../signed_ordinal_unit_centers_20261117/README.md) A and B, of path counts 3 and 161 and signed half-charges −1 and +54. Put a transitive separator V of m vertices *between* them: A→V→B. Give each separator vertex its own ordinary shared-port bit g_i, 1 if it beats both switch ports and 0 if both ports beat it. Arbitrary changes in these common contacts do not change either tile. Each separator singleton contributes factor 1 and zero charge in the signed product. The half-gap stays −161+3·54=1 for every bit word, both tile orders, and every continuation by additional shared exterior vertices. The separator vertices are genuinely internal, not disposable global sources or sinks.

There is no third contact letter within this local-balance compiler on a transitive block: the Hamiltonian start/end and two-cover endpoint tests distinguish different arrival masks there. In fact the start weight allowing an empty complement at vertex j of the ordered n-vertex transitive block is 2^(n−1−j), since any subset of later vertices may accompany it; its subset-sum label is injective. Matching all four menus necessarily matches that total weight. A third letter needs a different balance mechanism or a different core.

## 2. What the center actually reads

For arbitrary fixed nonport graph and variable common contacts, let C(g) be the midpoint of the two switch counts. It is a multilinear Boolean polynomial of degree at most 4. For a fixed ordered vertex permutation, every compatible path is represented by a product of literals g_i or (1−g_i) times a fixed 0/1 factor. Each of two ports has at most two incident path arcs; if a common-contact vertex is adjacent to both ports it requires both literals and contributes zero. Thus at most four distinct variables matter per surviving trace. Averaging the two branches gives the center polynomial; on this unit cube it is the minus count plus one, so its coefficients are integers. Möbius inversion recovers the whole polynomial from the 1-, 2-, 3-, and 4-site probes (and the zero probe), rather than testing every contact word. This reconstructs the reader, not an inverse.

**Exact quartic kernel.** For a<b<c<d in the transitive separator, write p=b−a−1, q=c−b−1, r=d−c−1. Let h_A,h_B be the Hamiltonian counts of the two bounding tiles (in either order). Then the coefficient of g_a g_b g_c g_d is exactly 2h_Ah_B times

    K(p,q,r) = 2^(p+r)(4·3^q+3) + 2[p=0]·2^r + 2[r=0]·2^p + [p=r=0].

It is independent of the earlier/later separator lengths or any other contacts. This formula holds for any fixed bounding tiles, whether or not the switch has unit gap.

**Proof.** To use all four variable contacts the path must have both ports as internal vertices. It consists of three pieces split by the ports. Write i for the last separator vertex before the first port, j and k for the first and last in the middle piece, and l for the first after the second port. The four sites occur in some permutation with j<k. The middle piece stays entirely inside the separator. Then all of A is in the first piece, all of B in the last piece. The four literals are g_i(1−g_j)g_k(1−g_l), with positive top coefficient one. For each remaining separator vertex t the choices of piece are exactly t<i, j<t<k, or t>l. These choices are independent because each piece follows the transitive order. Vertices outside the four sites' span have only one choice. Over the twelve eligible permutations, the triple of available alphabet sizes in the three gaps has the following multiplicities:

| Alphabet sizes | Multiplicity |
|---|---:|
| (2,3,2) | 4 |
| (2,1,2) | 3 |
| (0,1,2) | 2 |
| (2,1,0) | 2 |
| (0,1,0) | 1 |

With 0^0=1 this gives K. There are h_A h_B choices for the two full tile paths and two orders of the ports, yielding the prefactor. None of those paths uses the switched edge, so this part is identical in both branch polynomials.

Thus fourth differences are always strictly positive. A third difference is an affine function of the unresolved bits with strictly positive slopes, giving exact extremal bounds on that interaction from the two extreme bit settings. Lower-order shifts can still change sign. In particular one cannot make four single-site contacts additive by fixing the other sites, or by changing the arithmetic of the two bounding tiles.

## 3. Balanced swaps do not clear the top interaction

Take four disjoint ordered pairs of sites, so the two sites of each pair precede both of the next pair. In each pair let a bit swap move the 1 left (01→10), preserving its weight. The fourth mixed difference is still strictly positive. For adjacent pairs starting at a_1<a_2<a_3<a_4, the value is exactly the unbundled coefficient with sites a_1,a_2−1,a_3−2,a_4−3: the three interior gaps each contract by one.

To prove this, substitute the endpoints of each pair into K with alternating signs. If the widths are d_1,...,d_4>0, then the term 2^(p+r)3^q factors across choices with right-end multipliers 2^(−d_1), (2/3)^d_2, (3/2)^d_3, 2^d_4. Its alternating sum is strictly positive: exactly two factors (1−multiplier) are negative. The term 2^(p+r) has multipliers 2^(−d_1),2^d_2,2^(−d_3),2^d_4, again strictly positive. The p=0 correction can occur only for the right endpoint of pair 1 and the left endpoint of pair 2. The resulting single negative sign is canceled by the negative alternating product on pairs 3 and 4; otherwise the correction vanishes. The r=0 correction is symmetric, and the joint term has either positive sign or is absent. Setting all d_i=1 gives the stated contracted K by direct substitution. Between two equal-weight settings of one contiguous block with one-way transport of the ones, their difference is a nonnegative sum of ordered two-site left moves. Hence four such disjoint ordered block transports also have strictly positive top mixed effect by multilinearity. No interleaved all-order assertion is made.

For four consecutive adjacent swaps, the unit tile example has exact fourth difference 12·2·3·161=**11592**, while every one of the 16 settings has the same ±1 seam. Direct literal checks on smaller graphs, and the separately verified large transfer, reproduce the kernel. As a basic diversity consequence, a cube of k free ordered swap pairs has at least floor(k/4)+1 distinct centers *at constant Hamming weight*. If there were only d≤floor(k/4) values, the product of C−value over those d values would vanish as a Boolean polynomial. Its multilinear coefficient on any 4d sites is strictly positive: only disjoint products of d quartic coefficients can contribute at that degree. This contradicts vanishing. This is a lower bound, not saturation.

## 4. The field-with-one-element connection, with the specializations separated

The older [ground-state reflection](../../../07-reflections/the-fundamental-truths-in-every-language.md) and [Cayley–Dickson/F1 discussion](../../../agents/broadcast/archive/MSG-463-from-kind-pasteur-2026-03-22-kind-pasteur-2026-03-22-s20bl.md) used F1 as the minimal transitive or one-point skeleton. The precise transfer here is the finite-set flag count, in the spirit of [Szczesny's pointed-set model](https://arxiv.org/abs/1006.0912), not a claim that an ordinary field of cardinality one exists or that F1 forces a tournament to be transitive:

- In a gap of length ell with t available runs, choosing run sizes n_1,...,n_t gives an ordered decomposition of a pointed ell-element set. At q→1 the Gaussian multinomial for flags with these dimension increments becomes the ordinary multinomial. Summing all sizes gives t^ell, exactly the local factors in the kernel. Equivalently, use inversion weight q^{inv(word)} on run allocations; within fixed sizes the word polynomial is the Gaussian multinomial. The q=1 count forgets the inversion weights, **not** the four endpoint marks which determined where the run choices were available.
- The [archived independence-polynomial view](../../../01-canon/theorems/THM-326-staircase-hp-equals-ip.md) and [Grinberg–Stanley's odd-cycle expansion](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.05569) give a second specialization. If Z_T(s)=Σ s^{number of cycles} over vertex-disjoint odd directed cycle packs, then Z_T(0)=1 is the empty-pack ground term, Z_T(1) counts uncolored packs, and H(T)=Z_T(2). These are three distinct readings. The [old contact cube](../two_address_gap_selector_20261117/NEUTRAL_CUBE.md) has, in all four settings, signed cycle polynomial Z_plus−Z_minus=5s−2s². Its value is 3 at s=1 and 2 at s=2. So the Hamiltonian unit is real signed cancellation, not one uncolored object and not the formal q=1 flag limit. The direct cycle replay prints all eight layer vectors.
- The [nullcone boundary caution](../../../01-canon/theorems/THM-1825-transitivity-is-the-deepest-nullcone-point-not-the-whole-unstable-cone.md) offers a parallel: transitivity is the most degenerate point, not the whole unstable set. Here likewise the one-point skeleton is a useful origin, not a classification of the extra structure. The earlier [curried continuations](../curried_births_tooth_diagram_20261117/README.md) tell us what to retain: equality under future queries. A flag without ports is too coarse; endpoint states are what make the count composable.

## 5. Readable is not selectable

In this actual fixed two-tile family, any one of the 2^m internal words supports at least 3^m paths. Use a template A_0 V_0 B_0 x A_1 V_1 B_1 a A_2 V_2 B_2. Choose distinct departures from x and a into A, distinct arrivals from B to x and a, and paths through the remainder of each tile. The V_j can be empty. Every separator vertex independently chooses any of the three runs without ever testing its contacts. Consequently there are at most O(X^log_3(2)) distinct centers up to X. [Allowing both exterior tapes](TRANSFER.md) only adds at most two logarithmic factors; the internal growth obstruction is not a matter of missing a better local swap.

The [exact finite reader](TRANSFER.md) tracks empty or (first,last) per run, hence 125 states before symmetry, 35 after sorting the three run states. The fixed tiles give a terminal readout from their endpoint covers. Independent literal subset DP checks all 60 counts through separator length 3 (both orders and both port orientations), and the transfer checks parity, gap and the 3^m floor through length 10. Exact rational reduction gives center Hankel rank 10 for either tile order, 18 jointly. The surrounding-word response to 01 versus 10 has rank 6, not a scalar carry. For example, in A→B the terminal swap center difference C(01)−C(10)=310480 is not the single-bit difference C(1)−C(0)=−111862.

**Next specific test:** vary the physical core so that the neutral alphabet grows faster than the unconditional three-run allocations, or make interleaved moves with cancellation of the quartic interaction without losing too many address bits. Tuning the two fixed tiles only multiplies the quartic kernel. A finite control emphasizes that the issue is not address collisions here: at each length 3 through 10 all centers within either order are distinct and the two orders are disjoint at that length. This is finite, and lexicographic monotonicity already fails: in A→B, 0→1 decreases the count, whereas 000→001 increases it. Neither growing the cube nor reconstructing its readout solves universal absolute placement.
