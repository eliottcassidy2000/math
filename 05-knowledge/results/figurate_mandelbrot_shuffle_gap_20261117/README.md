# Period 3p: the shuffle gap and two failed ways to stagger a square

**PROVED:** product-gap theorem for arbitrary tournament cuts, preservation obstruction even with extra vertices, affine one-apex obstruction, two-long-tail obstruction, and a generating function for alternating figurate staggers. **FINITE-EXACT:** physical witnesses at periods 15, 21, 33, 39, 51. **OPEN:** a single construction for every prime p>3.

Let \(X=2^p,\ F=X+1,\ Q=F^2+2\). The [previous packet](../crossover_rounding_mandelbrot_20261116/README.md) shows that the Mandelbrot component count at period \(3p\) is \((2^{p-1}-1)Q\) for prime \(p>3\). The first factor has its physical Fibonacci fan. This note addresses the second.

## 1. A gap at the product for every tournament cut

Partition \(T\) into two nonempty induced tournaments \(A,B\), and put \(h_A=H(A),h_B=H(B)\). Call an \(A\)-path marked if it is Hamiltonian in \(A\) and one of its consecutive arcs lies in a directed triangle with some vertex of \(B\). Let \(d_A\) be the number of marked paths; define \(d_B\) dually. Then
\[
H(T)\ \ge\ h_A h_B+d_A h_B+d_B h_A-d_A d_B.
\]
Moreover \(H(T)=h_Ah_B\) if and only if no strong component of \(T\) crosses the partition. In particular any larger value exceeds the product by at least \(\min(h_A,h_B)+1\) (all Hamiltonian counts are odd).

**Proof.** Fix paths \(a_1,\ldots,a_s\) and \(b_1,\ldots,b_t\). Their order-preserving shuffles are ideals in the rectangle of cells \((i,j)\), the cell recording \(b_j\) before \(a_i\); inclusion forces all cells with \(i'\ge i,\ j'\le j\). Each neighboring shuffle swaps two adjacent unlike letters. Orient the edge toward the arrangement preferred by the cross arc in \(T\). Every swap removes one disagreement with the preferred set \(S=\{(i,j): b_j\to a_i\}\), so there is a sink; precisely the sinks are physical Hamiltonian shuffles. This extends the elementary [path-merging argument](https://www.imada.sdu.dk/u/jbj/DM201/turnering.pdf).

If \(S\) is an ideal, it is the unique sink: any other ideal can add a minimal missing cell of \(S\) or delete a maximal extra cell. Otherwise find comparable cells \(u<v\), \(u\notin S,v\in S\). Starting from the ideal generated by \(v\) cannot lose \(v\); starting from the complement of the filter generated by \(u\) cannot acquire \(u\). These runs end at different sinks. A nonideal preference has an adjacent bad comparable pair, by walking along a rectangle chain from \(u\) to \(v\). This is exactly a mixed triangle involving consecutive vertices of one of the two paths. A marked \(A\)-path therefore has at least two shuffles with *every* \(B\)-path, and conversely. Different pairs give different Hamiltonian paths, because restriction recovers the two paths. Inclusion-exclusion on marked pairs proves the inequality.

If \(d_A=d_B=0\), every outside vertex sees a given strong component of \(A\) uniformly: otherwise on a Hamiltonian cycle of that component there is an arc creating a mixed triangle, and that arc occurs in some full Hamiltonian path of \(A\) by cutting the cycle elsewhere and concatenating the other components. Singletons are immediate. Every orientation across two ordered components of \(A\), read relative to any fixed vertex of \(B\), is monotone along the component order (otherwise a full \(A\)-path has an adjacent transition making a marked triangle). The same holds with \(A,B\) switched. Each component is thus a module in \(T\); the quotient on all components has no directed triangle and is transitive. This proves the equality assertion. If no component crosses the partition, ordinary ordinal multiplication proves the converse.

**Consequence for the square.** If both complementary parts have \(H=F\), arbitrary coordinated changes to the cross arcs leave \(F^2\) or jump to at least \(F^2+F+1\). Keeping the arithmetic midpoint \(F\) while changing counts to \(F-2u,F+2u\) also requires \(4u^2+2u\ge X\) to reach \(Q\). Constant-sized edits to the two factor counts do not suffice; a genuine asymmetric split needs \(u\) of order at least \(\sqrt X\).

Even **arbitrarily many extra vertices cannot save two disjoint induced strong \(F\)-path blocks**. The induced union must already have \(F^2\), hence its cut is uniform. If an extra vertex is mixed on one block it makes a larger strong subtournament there, with count at least \(F+2\) by the theorem; multiplying by the untouched other block through the theorem overshoots \(Q\). So both blocks are modules in the final tournament. If a quotient triangle touches a block, lift it: the three cyclic orderings give at least \(3F^2\) paths if it touches both blocks. If it touches one block and two extra vertices, first obtain \(3F\) paths there and apply the cut bound with the other block. Thus neither module meets a quotient triangle and both survive as whole strong components. The final count is then a multiple of \(F^2\), not \(F^2+2\).

## 2. Changing a literal square, not just keeping it as an induced core

The [one-apex odd-cycle formula](../../../01-canon/theorems/THM-4748-two-reversed-arcs-exact-path-counts-the-seven-mod-eight-lines-and-the-mersenne-side-three-arcs-from-transitivity.md) for loss positions \(L\subseteq\{2,3,\ldots\}\) above a transitive spine is
\[
H=1+\sum_{t\in L}2^{t-1}-\sum_{s<t\in L}2^{\max(t-s-1,1)}.
\]
The one-apex word \(1\,0^p\,1^p\,0\) (1 means the apex beats the vertex) is another exact square \(F^2\), obtained by classifying 1-to-0 endpoint pairs according to their two runs.

No fixed finite list of affine loss positions \(a_i p+b_i\), at positions at least 2, realizes \(Q\) on an unbounded set of integer \(p\), even on a fixed arithmetic progression. Indeed, the largest slope must be 2: its coefficient in the exponential polynomial is the sum of the corresponding \(2^{b_i-1}\), times \(1-\sum_{a_i=0}2^{-b_i}\), strictly positive. Its required value is 1. Both factors are dyadic rationals, so each must be a power of two. The second belongs to \((1/2,1]\), hence equals 1, leaving no slope-zero loss. The first equals 1, so there is exactly one leading loss. The constant term now comes only from the vacuum 1 minus pair penalties within equal-slope groups, hence is at most 1, not the required 3. For rational affine positions, restrict to a residue progression on which positions are integral and reindex; the leading target coefficient becomes a power of two, with the same argument. This rules out affine-position fixed-cardinality repairs, not all one-apex sources.

A separate obstruction uses induced inclusion rather than fixed positions. The odd-cycle pack identity implies any one-vertex increase of exactly 2 has a unique odd cycle through the new vertex \(x\), and it is a triangle \(x a b x\): more generally a unique new pack must be a single odd cycle, and any cycle through \(x\) requires some cut arc from the outneighbors of \(x\) to the inneighbors, which creates a triangle. Thus \(a\to b\) is the only such cut arc and deleting \(a,b\) leaves a transitive tournament. In the outneighbors, \(a\) has at most one predecessor \(u\): two predecessors give a two-edge path ending at \(a\), which makes a 5-cycle through \(x\). If \(u\) exists it beats everyone else in that side. Dually \(b\) has at most one successor \(v\) in the inneighbors, losing to everyone else on that side. Both exceptions cannot coexist, as \(x,u,a,b,v,x\) would be a 5-cycle. The old tournament is therefore a transitive order (inneighbors then outneighbors) with just \(b\to a\) reversed over a span of at most two; its count is 1 or 3. By induced monotonicity and oddness, **no** larger \(H\ge5\) core can grow by 2 while remaining induced, even via multiple vertices. So even a square center that is not made of separate Fermat blocks has to be changed, not only extended. This extends the small cases in [the archived increment analysis](../increment_gap_analysis.out).

## 3. A dyadic stagger that looks right but cannot close

At a normalized root use \((H,g,E,O)\) with \(O=E+g\), \(A=2E>0\) and \(I=H-A\), and the [sink/near-sink recurrence](../prime_packing_inverse_20261116/README.md). Suppose the full variable part is \(N^{p+a} W N^{p+b}\), with fixed offsets and a fixed word \(W\) ending in \(N\) (if it ends in \(S\), absorb the first following \(N\) into \(W\)). Write \(w=|W|\), \(z\) for the low-to-high address of \(W\), \(d=2^w-1-z\), and \(r=\operatorname{rev}_{w-1}(d)\). Then \(E\) after \(W\) is \(2^w E+gr\): the sum \(E+O\) doubles at every bit and subtracts \(g\) exactly at each \(N\). Using the exact count formula for \(W\) and the two pure \(N\) tails gives
\[
H(p)=A2^{w+a+b}4^p+(2gr2^b-Ad2^a)2^p+
      I+g(f(z)-1)-2gr .
\]
If \(d=0\) the middle coefficient vanishes. Otherwise forcing the leading coefficient to 1 makes the middle coefficient \(2gr2^b-d2^{-w-b}\). Let \(D\) be \(d\) with its trailing zeros removed, \(L\) the bit length of \(D\), and \(R=\operatorname{rev}_L(D)\). The valuations of the two terms sum to \(-L\), since \(g\) is odd. To subtract to the integer 2 they must agree and hence both equal \(-L/2\). Thus \(L\) is even and \(gR-D=2^{1+L/2}\). At \(L=2\) we have \(D=R=3\), impossible. For \(L\ge4\), \(D,R\) are \(L\)-bit odd numbers, so \(1\le g<3\) and oddness forces \(g=1\). But the valuation of \(R-D\), if nonzero, equals the index of the first differing bit counted from the outside pair; this is at most \(L/2-1\). It cannot equal \(1+L/2\). Hence **no fixed middle motif separating two growing pure near-sink tails** produces \(Q\). The obstruction comes from the phase and its bit reversal, not from inability to build the quadratic leading term.

## 4. Figurate rows, toothpicks, and what the interface remembers

The archived [Vandermonde tail](../../../03-artifacts/drafts/vandermonde-tail-polygonal-polyhedral-2026-07-15-S313.md) expresses a simplex number as \(\sum_i\binom{k-1}{i}\binom{j}{i+1}\); the polygonal slice keeps exactly \(i=0,1\). Triangular numbers are on the common boundary. The discarded modes are explicit and nonnegative. The archived [stagger atlas](../../../01-canon/theorems/THM-1360-figurate-stagger-atlas-pisot-ladder-proth-mersenne-relational.md) shows that constant column shifts \(s=0,1,2\) in Pascal produce doubling, Fibonacci, and the supergolden recurrence.

A stagger can alternate rather than hold a fixed slope. Let \(d_0,\dots,d_{b-1}\) be periodic nonnegative column increments, \(s_k=\sum_{i<k}d_i\) extended periodically, and \(D=s_b\). Then
\[
\sum_{n\ge0}\sum_{k\ge0}\binom{n-s_k}{k}t^n
=\frac{1}{1-t}\cdot
\frac{\sum_{j=0}^{b-1} t^{s_j}(t/(1-t))^j}
     {1-t^D(t/(1-t))^b},
\]
where out-of-range entries are zero. Sum each column, then group by its index modulo \(b\). The two alternations \(1,2,1,2,\ldots\) and \(2,1,2,1,\ldots\) have different initial numerators \(1-t+t^2\) and \(1-t+t^3\) over the same denominator \((1-t)^2-t^5\). Both have the exact recurrence \(a_n=2a_{n-1}-a_{n-2}+a_{n-5}\) after the initial coefficients, and the same growth rate but different counts. The positive dominating pole is determined by the mean increment, via \(1-t=t^{1+D/b}\); the numerator records the phase. As an elementary absolute difference, the two stagger series differ by \(t^2(1-t)/((1-t)^2-t^5)\).

The same distinction is present in [the toothpick recurrence](https://arxiv.org/abs/1004.3036): at dyadic stages \(T(2^k)=(2\cdot4^k+1)/3\), so \(T(2^{k+1})=4T(2^k)-1\). Its repeated growth still carries a fixed boundary correction. The [archived chirality note](../../../07-reflections/chirality-toothpicks-and-why-tournament-counts-are-even-one-lefschetz-parity-boxeph-S213.md) compares the fixed toothpick axis with the converse involution on tournaments: fixed elements plus mirrored pairs give an exact parity ledger, not a witness for the missing pair. In the shuffle rectangle, the two distinct sinks come from a marked forbidden comparability, **not** from assuming the whole tournament is self-converse. For \(Q\), the analogous row bookkeeping must preserve the growth modes \(4^p,2^p\) but change the constant mode without freezing the center or merely flipping a mirror. The previous obstructions say which local modes do not have that freedom.
