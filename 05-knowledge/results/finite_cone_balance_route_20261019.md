# Finite boundary cones: a precise sufficient route and two obstructions

**2026-10-19. Status: PROVED transfer criteria and obstructions; CONDITIONAL all-7-thin route.**

This note follows `poset_four_collar_boundary_20261019.md` and the independent lemma packet `ideal_transfer_positivity_20261019.md`. It does not prove the 1/3–2/3 conjecture for all 7-thin posets. Its purpose is to replace a vague finite-state hope by explicit, finitely checkable proof obligations.

## 1. The exact marked-kernel certificate

Let a finite poset extension process be cut at two ideal ranks. Let T be the nonnegative integer matrix counting all paths between its left and right ideal states. For a comparison E whose outcome is decided within that interval, let T_E count those same paths on which E holds. Thus `0≤T_E≤T` entrywise.

Let l be the true row vector of prefix counts and r the true column vector of suffix counts. The true comparison probability is

`p(E)=l T_E r/(l T r)`.

The denominator is positive for a realizable nonempty extension family. This formula retains both boundary measures, rather than substituting uniform distributions on the induced interval.

### Theorem 1. Bilinear cone certificate

Suppose the possible l belong to the nonnegative cone generated by finitely many rows `u_1,…,u_a`, and the possible r belong to the nonnegative cone generated by finitely many columns `v_1,…,v_b`. A sufficient condition for E to be β-balanced for every such context is

`u_i (T_E−βT) v_j ≥ 0`,
`u_i ((1−β)T−T_E) v_j ≥ 0`

for every generator pair i,j.

These conditions are also necessary if all generator pairs themselves are allowed contexts and have positive denominator.

**Proof.** Write `l=Σ_i α_i u_i` and `r=Σ_j γ_j v_j`, with nonnegative coefficients. Each deficit is the sum of the displayed generator deficits with coefficients `α_iγ_j`. Necessity follows by selecting a generator pair. ∎

For β=1/3, one can clear denominators and check the integer expressions `u_i(3T_E−T)v_j` and `u_i(2T−3T_E)v_j`. This is the direct many-state extension of the four-collar argument. A fixed Gold decision tree also works if its appropriate marked-path deficits pass the same generator tests. Separate existential witnesses for different generator pairs do not suffice.

### Exact one-sided variant

At a rank k after both members of a comparison have appeared in every reachable ideal, let F(I) count all prefixes and C(I) those satisfying E. Then

`p(E)=Σ_I C(I)r(I)/Σ_I F(I)r(I)`.

Thus `βF(I)≤C(I)≤(1−β)F(I)` for every reachable ideal I is a sufficient fixed-witness certificate against *every* nonnegative suffix weighting. It is necessary if arbitrary weights concentrated at one state are allowed. Actual suffix laws form a smaller cone; testing all nonnegative weights can be unnecessarily strong.

## 2. A finite certificate that would settle a bounded-degree class

Fix D≥1. The displacement-window construction gives finitely many local ideal-state types, with at most `binom(2D,D)` states per layer, and finitely many 0–1 one-step transfer matrices after canonical local relabeling. This is a finite alphabet with arbitrary word length, not a finite set of posets.

The underlying legal-poset language can itself be presented finitely. Set `R=2D−1`; when a new reference-order label is introduced, choose its incomparable earlier labels among only the last R labels, make every older label smaller, test that its predecessor set is an ideal, and update the incomparability counts of recent labels. Store the last R induced order and their past-incomparability counts, rejecting a count above D. Transitivity violations involving the new point are exactly failures of the predecessor-ideal test. All earlier points outside the window are forced predecessors, so the test is local. End-of-word padding supplies the true boundaries. A finite look-ahead buffer then emits the corresponding rank-window transition matrices. Every degree-D poset in any reference extension is covered by the known bandwidth bound.

Here is one precise sufficient certificate. Provide the following finite data and proofs.

1. A finite directed presentation covering every legal reference-extension encoding of the target class. Its start and finish data must include the actual finite-poset boundaries. An overapproximation is allowed, provided the following tests hold for every accepted word in it.
2. At each presentation state, finitely generated nonnegative left and right cones containing the appropriate initial or terminal boundary vectors.
3. For every transition matrix T from state q to state q', exact inclusions `C_q T⊆C_q'` and `T D_q'⊆D_q`. It is enough to verify these on cone generators, with exact nonnegative coefficients.
4. Finitely many certified marked blocks. Each chooses one actual incomparable pair and satisfies Theorem 1 against the cones at the block's two boundaries.
5. A finite-language check showing every accepted encoding of a nonchain poset contains at least one certified block. This can be checked after deleting/forbidding all certified block occurrences and testing whether any accepted nonchain word remains. No unproved pumping bound on weighted comparisons is allowed here.

### Theorem 2. Sufficiency of the finite certificate

If data satisfying 1–5 exist for every poset of incomparability degree at most D, then every nonchain poset in that class has a β-balanced pair.

**Proof.** Induction on transitions puts every actual prefix and suffix vector in its assigned cone. Condition 5 selects a certified occurrence on each complete accepted encoding. Apply Theorem 1 at that occurrence. ∎

The theorem is a verification reduction, not a completeness theorem for this certificate format. No certificate satisfying 1–5 for D=7 is presently supplied. In particular, finite state alone does not prove that suitable finitely generated invariant cones and a finite covering collection of witnesses exist.

## 3. Automatic mixing makes strict certificates local

The independent positivity note proves that every D-step ideal kernel is strictly positive. More sharply, after positive diagonal row/column scaling its entries lie between 1 and `C_D=binom(D,floor(D/2))`. The standard Hilbert-projective contraction coefficient is therefore at most

`κ_D=(C_D−1)/(C_D+1)`.

For D=7, `C_D=35` and `κ_D=17/18`. This constant is sharp over all posets of degree D. Thus failure of positivity or uniform projective mixing is not the structural obstruction at D=7.

For positive vectors x,y put

`d_H(x,y)=log(max_i x_i/y_i)−log(min_i x_i/y_i)`.

After t consecutive D-step kernels, arbitrary nonzero nonnegative boundary vectors have output distance at most

`2 log(C_D) κ_D^(t−1)`.

The first kernel supplies the projective-diameter bound; every subsequent one contracts it. The same statement holds for row vectors by transposition. The case D=1 has projective diameter zero and can be handled directly.

### Corollary 3. Buffered strict-witness transfer

Take a marked central interval, preceded and followed by t such D-step blocks. Keep all these local transfer matrices fixed while allowing arbitrary nonzero nonnegative boundary vectors beyond both buffers. If one choice of boundaries gives the central comparison probability in

`[β+ε,1−β−ε]`

and

`tanh(log(C_D) κ_D^(t−1)) ≤ ε`,

then every choice of exterior boundary vectors gives a β-balanced central pair.

**Proof.** Relative to the reference boundaries, the logarithmic oscillation of the prefix-weight ratio is at most `2 log(C_D)κ_D^(t−1)`, and the same holds for suffix weights. A complete central path is reweighted by the product of those two ratios. Its log-likelihood oscillation is therefore at most `H=4 log(C_D)κ_D^(t−1)`.

For any probability law tilted by a positive weight whose maximum/minimum ratio is at most `exp(H)`, the maximum possible change of an event probability is `tanh(H/4)`. To see this, extremal weights take only their minimum and maximum on the event and its complement; maximizing `Rp/(1−p+Rp)−p` over p gives `(sqrt(R)−1)/(sqrt(R)+1)` for `R=exp(H)`. Apply this bound to the event E. ∎

This gives a rigorous finite-buffer mechanism whenever a positive slack is available. It does not handle an exact equality certificate by approximation, and it does not ensure that the required strictly balanced central comparison exists.

For a concrete D=7 bound, ε=1/100 is protected by t=106 blocks on each side, hence 742 ranks on each side: `log(35)<4` and the exact rational inequality `(17/18)^105<1/400` make the displayed error smaller than 1/100. This is a universal conservative buffer bound, not a claim that every local poset supplies a comparison with that slack.

## 4. Fibonacci contexts have an irrational minimal exact cone

Let Q_n be the inherited thin poset on 1,…,n with `i<j` exactly when `j≥i+2`. Its incomparability graph is the path, so its maximum degree is 2.

At an interior rank k the two ideals are

`I_k^0={1,…,k}`,
`I_k^1={1,…,k−1,k+1}`.

In this order the one-step ideal matrix is

`A=[[1,1],[1,0]]`,

and the two-step matrix is strictly positive:

`T=A²=[[2,1],[1,1]]`.

The final one-step terminal column is `(1,1)^T`. Restricting to an even number 2t of interior transitions, the exact suffix-count vector is

`h_t=T^t(1,1)^T=(F_(2t+2),F_(2t+1))^T`,

where `F_1=F_2=1`. These are genuine finite-poset suffix laws. Their slopes decrease strictly from 1 to `1/φ`, where `φ=(1+sqrt(5))/2`.

Consequently the smallest *closed* cone containing all h_t is bounded by the rays `(1,1)` and `(φ,1)`. One boundary ray is irrational. No finite collection of rational vectors generates this exact closed cone.

This is not an obstruction to rational outer cones: for example, larger rational invariant cones exist, and positivity gives arbitrarily accurate outer approximations. It is an obstruction to the claim that a finite positive transfer alphabet automatically has a minimal exact context cone with finitely many rational generating rays. Parity is deliberately fixed here; mixing together all terminal parities can enlarge the cone and hide this obstruction.

## 5. Boundary witnesses cannot be replaced by stationary bulk witnesses

The same Q_n gives a second exact warning. Its linear extensions correspond to independent sets of adjacent transpositions. For the incomparable pair i,i+1,

`Pr(i+1 precedes i)=F_i F_(n−i)/F_(n+1)`.

With both distances to the boundaries tending to infinity, this approaches

`1/(φ sqrt(5))=(5−sqrt(5))/10 < 1/3`.

At the first pair the probability instead approaches `φ^(−2)>1/3`. The finite poset has its balancing boundary even though its translation-invariant bulk supplies no balanced adjacent comparison. All nonadjacent pairs are comparable.

Therefore automatic positive mixing does not remove the boundary from the proof. A finite cone strategy must either certify a boundary witness or prove witness migration to a later certified block. A method requiring a balanced witness at every bulk type fails already at D=2.

## 6. Literature and status
