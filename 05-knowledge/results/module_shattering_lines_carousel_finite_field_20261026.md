## 2. Theorem 1: every complement line shatters every module

**Lemma (no immediate re-entry).** Let `M` be a module of `T` and `P = (p_0, ..., p_(n-1))` a Hamiltonian path. If `p_j in M` and `p_(j+1) notin M`, then `p_(j+2) notin M` (when it exists); symmetrically, if `p_i notin M` and `p_(i+1) in M`, then `p_(i-1) notin M`. *Proof.* `p_j -> p_(j+1)` and `M` is a module, so every vertex of `M` beats `p_(j+1)`; if `p_(j+2)` were in `M` it would beat `p_(j+1)`, contradicting the path arc `p_(j+1) -> p_(j+2)`. The entering case is the same with the arcs reversed. QED. So the positions of `M` along `P` form intervals separated by at least two outside vertices.

**Theorem 1 (PROVED).** Let `T` be a tournament on `n >= 3` vertices, `P` a Hamiltonian path, `K = K_P(T)`, and `M` a module of `T` with `2 <= |M| <= n - 1`. Then `M` is not a module of `K`.

*Proof.* Since `M` is proper and nonempty, `P` has a path arc between `M` and its complement: a leaving arc `p_j -> p_(j+1)` with `p_j in M`, `p_(j+1) notin M`, or an entering arc. Take a leaving arc and put `x = p_(j+1)`. In `T`, `p_j -> x`, so (module) `y -> x` for every `y in M`; in `T^op`, `x -> y` for every `y in M`. The partner `K` has `T^op`'s orientation on every pair except the path pairs, which carry `T`'s orientation. The pair `{p_j, x}` is a path pair, so `p_j -> x` in `K`. Pick `y in M`, `y != p_j`. The path neighbours of `x` are `p_j` and `p_(j+2)`, and `p_(j+2) notin M` by the lemma, so `{y, x}` is not a path pair and carries `T^op`'s orientation `x -> y` in `K`. Hence `x` sees `p_j` and `y` differently in `K`, and `M` is not a module of `K`. The entering case is symmetric (`x = p_(i-1)`, `p_(i-2) notin M`). QED.

**Corollaries.** (a) (Involution.) `K_P(K_P(T)) = T`, so applying Theorem 1 to `K` shows that the module lattices of the two ends of a line meet only in the singletons and `V`: every proper non-singleton module of either end is "new" to the other. (b) (Decorated trees.) The strong-module trees `D(T)` and `D(K)` share no internal node other than the root, as vertex subsets; the decorated tree of a line partner is built afresh from `(D(T), P)` and is never a function of `D(T)` alone (two presentations of one class give, in general, partners with different trees). (c) (LCA.) For `u, v` in a proper non-singleton module `M` of `T`, `LCA_T(u, v)` lies in the subtree of `M`; in `K` the pair's LCA is a node of a tree none of whose internal nodes is `M` or any other old node. The arc `u -> v` itself is reversed by the line unless `{u, v}` is a path pair, but the *reason* for its orientation, the quotient at the LCA, is not transported: lines reconnect the LCA structure completely. (d) (Non-closure of composition families.) Any family of tournaments all having a module of the same vertex set, or all having the same tree shape with at least one proper non-singleton module (in particular every substitution fibre `Q[T_1, ..., T_k]` with some `|T_i| >= 2`, every stack with a non-singleton block, every cyclic-triple fibre of a non-prime tournament), is closed under no complement line: the partner of a non-prime tournament along any Hamiltonian path has none of its modules. This is the owner's "caution" in its strongest form, and it explains why the carousel fibre's Theorems D-E concern a different (block-cut) operation.

*Information reading.* A module is a set that the rest of the tournament cannot distinguish, i.e. a block of indistinguishable information for every outside observer; a line keeps only the information carried along the path and reverses everything else, and the lemma shows that the path always re-exposes a module from inside (the exit vertex) and from outside (the next vertex) at once, which is exactly what destroys the block.

## 3. Theorem 2: the normal-basis dictionary of the carousel fibre

Fix odd `m >= 3` and a normal element `alpha` of `F_(2^m)` over `F_2` (the normal basis theorem: `alpha, alpha^2, alpha^4, ..., alpha^(2^(m-1))` is an `F_2`-basis). Write `x_w = sum_i w_i alpha^(2^i)` for `w in {0,1}^m`.

**Theorem 2 (PROVED, CITED ingredients).** (i) The Frobenius `x -> x^2` acts on normal-basis coordinates as the cyclic rotation `w -> (w_(m-1), w_0, ..., w_(m-2))`; hence the carousel classes (necklaces, Theorem C) are exactly the Frobenius orbits on `F_(2^m)`, with `T_w ~= T_(w')` iff `x_(w')` is a Galois conjugate of `x_w`. (ii) For `d | m`, `w` is `d`-periodic iff `x_w in F_(2^d)`; so the least period of `w` is the degree of `x_w` over `F_2`, and the surviving cyclic symmetry of `T_w` is `Aut(T_w)/C_3^m ~= C_(m/d) = Gal(F_(2^m)/F_2(x_w))`, the Galois group of the big field over the field generated by the decoration. (iii) The number of carousel classes of least period `d` is the number of monic irreducible polynomials of degree `d` over `F_2`, `P_2(d) = (1/d) sum_(e|d) mu(e) 2^(d/e)` (Gauss), and `N_2(m) = sum_(d|m) P_2(d)` is the number of Frobenius orbits; the table of Theorem C (`2, 6, 18, 56, 186, 630` for `d = 3, 5, 7, 9, 11, 13`, and `2` for `d = 1`) is that classical table. (iv) Presentation counts are proportional to the degree: with `H_m` the common Hamiltonian-path count of the fibre (F4), `tc(T_w) = H_m / (3^m m/d) = (H_m/(m 3^m)) d`, every presentation is black, and the mass identity (4) is the orbit-size sum `sum_(orbits) d = 2^m`. At `m = 3`, `H_3 = 462915` and `tc = 5715, 17145` for `d = 1, 3`. (v) (`q`-ary and `F_1`.) With `q` child types and `q` a prime power, the same dictionary holds for `F_(q^m)` over `F_q` (necklaces over a `q`-letter alphabet, `N_q(m) = (1/m) sum phi(e) q^(m/e)`, period = degree over `F_q`); with one child type (`q = 1`) there is a single class `Q_m[A, ..., A]` with `Aut/Aut(A)^m = C_m`, the full rotation group, which is the Galois group `Gal(F_(1^m)/F_1) = C_m` of Kapranov-Smirnov's field extension of the field with one element (`F_(1^m) = {0} u mu_m`), and `tc = H/(|Aut(A)|^m m)`: the "degree 1" case. (vi) (LCA in the subfield lattice.) For two decorations of least periods `d, d'`, the common rotation symmetry is `C_(m/lcm(d,d'))`, the field generated by both is `F_(2^lcm(d,d'))` and the common subfield is `F_(2^gcd(d,d'))`; in the subfield tree of `F_(2^(p^k))` (a chain) the lowest common ancestor of two decorations is the smaller field.

*Proof.* (i) `(sum w_i alpha^(2^i))^2 = sum w_i alpha^(2^(i+1))` in characteristic 2, which is the rotated coordinate vector. (ii) `Frob^d` fixes `x` iff rotation by `d` fixes `w`; the fixed field of `Frob^d` is `F_(2^d)`, and the least `d` with `x in F_(2^d)` is the degree. (iii) The orbit of `x` under `Frob` has size `deg x`, and its elements are the roots of the minimal polynomial; so orbits of size `d` are in bijection with irreducible polynomials of degree `d`, counted by Gauss's formula, which is also Theorem C's `P_2(d)` (Möbius inversion of the primitive-word count). (iv) is (2) with F4's constancy of `H`; every `T_w` is non-self-converse (odd reflection), so `bm = 0`. (v) and (vi) are the same statements over `F_q`, resp. the Galois correspondence `d | m <-> F_(2^d)`. QED.

The dictionary makes the owner's phrase literal, and (iv) is the metagraph consequence: within one cyclic-triple fibre the node fibre sizes are proportional to the field degrees of the decorations, `tc : d` constant on the fibre.

## 5. The LCA and `F_1` reading, briefly

Session56's interface says that a tournament *is* its LCA tree plus the quotients at the nodes; Theorem 1 says a complement line keeps none of that tree, only the path. So a line-compatible classification must be indexed by presentations `(T, P)`, i.e. by the decorated tree together with the path's run structure through it (THM-3121's ordered path-cover profiles are the exact data at each node), which is the owner's "retain path data" made precise; the carousel fibre shows the extreme case, where `tc(T_w)` presentations lead to `tc(T_w)` different trees. The field with one element enters through the decorations: a prime quotient with `Aut(Q) = C_m` and `q` child types is an `F_(q^m)`-structure when `q` is a prime power (Theorem 2), an abstract necklace structure for other `q`, and the one-type case is the `F_(1^m)` structure whose Galois group is the whole rotation group; the LCA in the lattice of subfields is the gcd of the periods. These are readings, not new theorems beyond Theorem 2.

## 6. Terminology

- **Module shattering**: Theorem 1; a line **shatters** every proper non-singleton module of either end.
- **Fresh tree**: the strong-module tree of a line partner, sharing no internal node with the original.
- **Decoration field**: `F_2(x_w)`, of degree the least period; **surviving symmetry** `Gal(F_(2^m)/F_2(x_w))`.
- **Degree law**: `tc(T_w) = (H_m/(m 3^m)) d`.
- **Injective fibre**: a class whose distinct tilings lead to distinct partner classes (all four carousel classes at `n = 12`).
