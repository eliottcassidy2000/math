# Inversion reciprocity for regular-tournament circulation series

Session23b. PROVED in the stated scopes, with independent audit in the sibling session23b-reciprocity-review directory. This is a structural transfer of classical lattice-cone reciprocity, not a claim to invent that theorem or to identify variable inversion with Euclidean sphere inversion.

## Definitions
Let T be a regular tournament on n=2r+1 vertices, m=nr arcs, and let B be its signed vertex-arc incidence matrix. Put V=ker B over R, d=m-n+1=r(2r-1), and K=V intersect R^m_{>=0}. For n=1 use d=0 and the zero cone. A flow is an integral point of K and its weight is the sum of its arc coordinates. This is neither an Euler-tour count nor a Hamilton-path count.

For a tournament automorphism or anti-automorphism a, let A_a permute the arc coordinates. An anti sends arc (u,v) to (a(v),a(u)), so it still permutes actual arcs of T. Write

R_a(q)=sum_{f in K intersect Z^m, A_a f=f} q^{|f|}.

Let R=R_identity. Let f_T be the ordinary Aut(T)-orbit flow series. If T is self-converse, let k_T count ordinary flow orbits fixed by converse and let b_T=(f_T+k_T)/2 count the extended-group orbits. If T is not self-converse, k_T is zero and no extended group on this one labelled T is asserted. All these series are rational by the fixed-cone/Burnside proof in the companion flow packet.

## Theorem
For every a above,

R_a(q^-1)=(-1)^d q^m R_a(q).

Consequently R, f_T, k_T and, where defined, b_T satisfy the same functional equation. It is an identity of rational functions; q^-1 is not a substitution inside a convergent nonnegative power series at q=0.

For n=1 every series under consideration is1 (or the stipulated zero k), and the identity is immediate. The proof below assumes n>=3.

### Step1: all-ones is an interior lattice translation
Regularity implies B1=0. Every arc permutation fixes 1. In the fixed linear space V_a={v in V:A_a v=v}, the cone K_a contains the strictly positive vector1. Therefore it is full-dimensional in V_a, and its relative interior is exactly the vectors with every coordinate strictly positive. Its interior lattice points are precisely

relint(K_a) intersect Z^m = 1+(K_a intersect Z^m).

Both directions follow by subtracting or adding one in every coordinate, preserving integrality, the balance equations, and the fixed equations. Thus its interior series is q^m R_a(q).

Classical reciprocity for a pointed rational lattice cone of dimension d_a gives R_a(q^-1)=(-1)^(d_a) times its interior series. The lattice here is V_a intersect Z^m. All nonzero points have positive total weight, so the one-variable specialization is legitimate. This is the usual cone reciprocity obtained from simplicial fundamental parallelepipeds and compatible half-open triangulations.

It remains to prove d_a≡d mod2. That parity is essential: averaging unrelated reciprocity signs would not be valid.

### Step2: ordinary automorphisms
Every tournament automorphism group has odd order. Indeed, an involution would transpose some pair and both preserve and reverse its arc, which is impossible; Cauchy's theorem excludes even group order. Hence any ordinary a has odd order.

For a real finite-order operator of odd order, all nontrivial eigenspaces occur in complex-conjugate pairs; the eigenvalue−1 does not occur. Its fixed-space codimension is therefore even. Applying this to A_a on V gives d_a≡d mod2.

### Step3: anti-involutions
Suppose j is an anti-automorphism and j²=1. It cannot fix two vertices, since their arc would have to reverse while its ends stay fixed. Since n is odd, j has exactly one fixed vertex and r transposed pairs.

On the m-dimensional arc space E, A_j fixes exactly the r arcs whose endpoints are the transposed pairs. Therefore tr(A_j|E)=r. The incidence image is W={x in R^n:sum x_i=0}, and

B A_j = -P_j B.

The trace of P_j on the full vertex space is1. The constant vector contributes1, so tr(P_j|W)=0; the induced operator on W is -P_j and also has trace0. The exact sequence0->V->E->W->0 consequently gives tr(A_j|V)=r. Since A_j is an involution,

dim Fix(A_j|V)=(d+r)/2=r².

Both r² and d=r(2r-1) have parity r.

### Step4: all anti-automorphisms
Every nontrivial vertex cycle of an anti-automorphism has length2 modulo4, with at most one fixed point. An odd cycle of length>1 would give an odd power fixing at least two vertices, impossible for an anti-map. A cycle of length divisible by4 would have an even power interchanging a pair, impossible for an automorphism. Thus a has order2s for odd s.

Set j=a^s and h=a². Then j is an anti-involution, h has odd order, they commute, and they generate the cyclic group generated by a. Hence Fix(a|V)=Fix(h|Fix(j|V)). The odd-order parity argument on Fix(j|V) gives

dim Fix(a|V) ≡ dim Fix(j|V) ≡ d (mod2).

This completes the fixed-cone equation. Ordinary and anti-coset Burnside averages now have a common sign and common q^m shift, proving all stated orbit identities.

## Consequences for explicit numerators
Suppose a nonzero one of these functions is written

F(q)=N(q)/product_(i=1)^s (1-q^(ell_i)), with N a polynomial and L=sum ell_i.

Then rational substitution gives

N(q^-1)=(-1)^(d-s) q^(m-L) N(q).

If s≡d mod2, this is a palindromic numerator identity q^(L-m)N(q^-1)=N(q). Because every orbit series has constant term1, its numerator constant term is1. When the displayed representation has matching parity and is polynomial, its degree is exactly L-m and its leading coefficient is1. Cancellation and different denominator presentations must be handled before calling a recurrence minimal.

For the R5 candidate raw formula, the denominator (1-q³)^3(1-q⁴)^2(1-q⁵) has s=6 and L=22. Since m=10, the numerator must be palindromic of degree12. The anti-fixed denominator (1-q³)(1-q⁵)(1-q⁶)(1-q⁸) has s=4 and the same L=22, again forcing degree12. These constraints independently explain the symmetry in both computed numerators. They are checks, not substitutes for the numerator derivations.

For C3, the equation is (1-q^-3)^-1= -q³(1-q³)^-1. For Paley7, every fixed-cone and orbit series has the common sign− and shift q²¹, although different fixed cones can have dimensions15,9,3,... . No equality of their denominators or pole orders is implied.

## Scope and obstruction
Regularity is used exactly to make the all-ones arc vector a circulation. A strongly connected nonregular tournament can have positive integral flows, but subtracting one in every coordinate need not preserve balance. Therefore a universal q^m interior shift is not asserted there. The strong order4 example has raw flow GF1/((1-q³)^2(1-q⁴)); its inversion shift is q¹⁰ with sign−, not q⁶ with sign−, because its primitive interior lattice point has weight10 rather than its six-arc all-ones vector.

This supplies a falsifiable all-order coefficient constraint for regular-tournament flow enumeration. It neither classifies original Hamilton-path metagraph wiring nor turns an electrical flow into a path count. The parallel with geometric inversion is a clearly typed analogy: this map sends a formal variable q to1/q and translates interior lattice points; the tetrahedral construction sends Euclidean points x to4x/|x|². No map between those carriers is given.
