# Compressed blue observer tournaments

Let $G$ be a Moore graph of degree $d$, with $m=d^2+1$ sites. Partition its labels as observed $O$ and hidden $U$; the adjacency blocks are $D=A_{O,O}$, $B=A_{O,U}$, $C=A_{U,U}$. We will retain one source for every label but only one observer for each label of $O$. It suffices that $G[U]$ have maximum degree $t$ with $t^2+t\le d-1$.

## Exact decoder

The cross observation records $D+I$ and $B$, but omits the block $C$. The Moore matrix identity implies
$$BC=J-DB-B,\qquad C\mathbf1=d\mathbf1-B^\top\mathbf1,$$
and $B^\top B=(d-1)I+J-C^2-C$. Recover $C$ as the unique real symmetric entrywise nonnegative matrix with zero diagonal satisfying the first two linear equations. The actual $C$ is feasible. For the strict bound $t^2+t<d-1$, the Gram equation is positive definite. For equality, $C^2+C$ has largest eigenvalue at most $t^2+t$, reached only on the $+t$ eigenspace of $C$ (the eigenvalue $-t$ gives strictly less). Vectors in $\ker B$ have zero sum and belong to this eigenspace, by taking the quadratic form of the Gram equation. On each component, the $t$ eigenspace is zero unless the component is $t$-regular, in which case it consists of constants by the Perron argument. Thus the difference of two symmetric feasible matrices is supported on pairs of $t$-regular components and constant on each such block. Its diagonal condition makes each same-component block zero, and off-component changes are nonnegative since the true $C$ had no such edges. The zero row sums force all the changes to vanish. Hence the decoder is exact even at the singular threshold.

## Literal tournament, spine, and optional markers

Order each of $O,U$ linearly with adjacent last labels across the cut. Pair each observed source with its observer and pair the hidden sources by reversing their order (one fixed middle vertex if needed). On the observed sources, hidden sources, and observers separately use transitive orientations, in their order, their order, and reverse order, respectively. The observed source to observer arc bits are $D+I$. The hidden source to observer bits are $B^\top$. Set each observed-source/hidden-source arc to be the reverse reflection of its paired hidden-source/observer arc. This gives every arc, and the pairing reverses the tournament because $D+I$ is symmetric. The concatenation observed sources, hidden sources, observers is a Hamilton spine by the chosen adjacent last labels across the cut. The pairing reverses that spine, so the presentation is blue.

For an unmarked decoding add two marker pairs $a\leftrightarrow b$ and $\alpha\leftrightarrow\beta$. For a core site $v$, set $(a\to v,v\to b)$ to $(1,0)$ on observed sources, $(1,1)$ on hidden sources, and $(0,1)$ on observers. Also $\alpha$ beats every core site and $\beta$ loses to every core site. The six marker arcs are
$$a\to b,\ a\to\alpha,\ a\to\beta,\ \alpha\to b,\ \beta\to b,\ \beta\to\alpha.$$
Now $\alpha$ is identified as the unique indegree-two site and $\beta$ the unique outdegree-two site, provided all core sites and $a,b$ have both degrees greater than two. The second in-neighbor of $\alpha$ besides $\beta$ is $a$, and the second out-neighbor of $\beta$ besides $\alpha$ is $b$. Their patterns separate all three transitive chains, giving their orders and the observer label alignment intrinsically. Inserting $a,\alpha$ at the start and $\beta,b$ at the end of the old spine preserves the blue property. In our degree-seven and degree-57 constructions $|O|$ is much larger than $d$, so all degree claims hold: core observed sources have outdegree at least $d+1$, indegree at least $|O|-d-1$, and observers are their opposites; hidden sites have both degrees at least $|O|-d$. These tournaments are strong as well: the complement of $G[O]$ is connected when $|O|\ge 2d+2$; nonedges link observed sources by two-step paths through their diagonal observers. Every hidden site reaches and is reached by this core through noncontacts. The four markers enter the same strong component by the prescribed arcs.

## Concrete compression

A maximum-degree-$d$ graph admits a partition into $p=\lceil(d+1)/(t+1)\rceil$ sets each of induced max degree at most $t$: minimize total within-set edges, then a vertex with $t+1$ same-set neighbors would have a strictly better destination because otherwise it has at least $p(t+1)>d$ neighbors. Take any $\lceil m/p\rceil$ sites in the largest class. For degree 57, $t=7$, $p=8$, hence $|U|\ge407$. This gives at most 6093 marked or 6097 unmarked blue vertices, conditional on existence of $G$, rather than 6500.

For Hoffman–Singleton take all 25 sites in the pentagon half, inducing degree two. This gives 75 marked or 79 unmarked blue vertices rather than 100. The replay uses the exact matrix identities to certify decoding on a scrambled unmarked tournament; its numerical linear solver is used merely to locate a candidate, which is then checked by exact integer equalities and the proved uniqueness.

For the pentagon and Petersen one can instead hide a closed neighborhood: its induced star is recovered directly from degrees, with its unique hidden-degree-$d$ center and all other hidden degrees one. This gives fixed-spine blue presentations on seven and sixteen vertices respectively. No marker-count improvement is asserted for those two small cases.
