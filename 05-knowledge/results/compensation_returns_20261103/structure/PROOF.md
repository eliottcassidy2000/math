## 2. Unbounded deletion count with fixed endpoint mass

Write C2(T,a) for two births: x beats only a and loses to every other old
vertex; y beats x and loses to every vertex of T. No old arc changes. The
inherited exact count is H'=H+2S_a(2). The first newborn x has start defect0,
and repeated C2 at each transported first newborn multiplies H by3.

Define D0={a0}. Apply C2 at a0, then at x1, then at x2, and so on, where
xj is the first newborn at step j and yj the second. For every j≥0,

    H(Dj)=3^j,                 end_a0(Dj)=1.                 (1)

The first equality is the inherited zero channel (the singleton also has
start defect0). For the second, when j≥1 the only in-neighbor of a0 is x1.
Moreover

    Dj−a0 = y1 -> D_(j−1),

where the smaller copy is rooted at x1 with later newborns. Every Hamilton
path ending a0 therefore ends its preceding portion at x1, and the forced
component order removes y1. Induction gives endpoint mass1. This also
provides the exact graph recursion behind the equality.

Now start with a strong prepared reader wrapper T0, inner marked vertex w,
and last reader vertex a0 from a final0. Deleting w leaves

    U -> {a0},             H(U)=q0.

All U vertices dominate every vertex of Dj, and only a0 in Dj beats w.
Consequently, after j zero-channel cap steps, including the first positive
C2 step at a0,

    Tj−w = U -> Dj,
    H(Tj−w)=3^j q0,       end_w(Tj)=q0.                    (2)

The second equality counts paths ending w by their preceding endpoint:
it must be a0 in the terminal component, so it contributes
H(U)end_a0(Dj)=q0. Thus growing q and bounded endpoint mass are compatible.
For j≥1 the full count is 3^(j−1)H(T1), where
H(T1)=3H(T0)+2sigma_a0. Formula(2) does not assert an unchanged full count.

If a new single0-reader vertex z is added to any actual T, beating only w,
the inherited one-birth law gives

    H'=H+S_w(2)−end_w(T),
    sigma_z=end_w(T),
    end_w(T')=H(T−w),       H(T'−w)=H(T−w).                (3)

Hence q0=1 in (2) supplies a start-ready1 port after a lone0 at every cap
depth. The cost S_w(2)−1 remains and must be paid; this is not a neutral

## 3. A terminal deleted triangle cannot prepare either digit

Let D=T−w=TT_m→C3 with m≥3. Allow **every** star at w for which T is strong.
Strongness forces w to beat the first transitive vertex and at least one
triangle vertex to beat w. No outer-root-zero condition is needed below.

**Theorem.** No vertex v of T has S_v(2)−H(T) in{1,2}.

Every transitive vertex has at least three out-neighbors in the triangle;
the converse rooted-defect bound excludes it. At w, outdegree at least3
is likewise excluded. Outdegree1 means its sole out-neighbor is the first
transitive vertex d1; then sigma_w=end_d1(D)=0. At outdegree2, the other
outport is either transitive or in the cap. A transitive second outport
leaves C3 in the residual, excluding the exceptional sigma2 case. A cap
outport leaves m−1≥2 transitive vertices. In the converse READY word every
one has the same non11 letter (they beat the cap port and lose to d1), so
the accepted five-word language again fails. Outdegree2 already excludes
sigma1. This handles the apex without a false singleton-cover injection.

For a triangle vertex v, write its cyclic successor u and predecessor z,
so v→u→z→v. If v beats w, it has outdegree2. Start defect2 would require
the converse exceptional language of READY§2. If the residual is not
transitive it fails immediately. If it is transitive, the first converse
letter is z. Every subsequent transitive vertex has one coordinate0,
since it beats u. A00 letter is forbidden; avoiding00 forces all m letters
to be the same10 or01. Two identical non11 letters are already forbidden
by the five-word language. Thus sigma2 is impossible, and outdegree2
excludes sigma1.

If w beats v, then v's sole out-neighbor is u, so

    sigma_v=end_u(T−v).

Let b_i=[d_i→w] along TT_m and set
C=sum_(i=1)^m (1−b_i)2^(m−i). Directly partition a path ending u at w:
the remaining triangle vertex z must lie before w, and every selected
post-w transitive vertex precedes the final u. This gives

    sigma_v = [z→w] (C+[w→u]).                            (4)

Since w beats d1, C≥2^(m−1)≥4. Thus (4) is either0 or at least4.
The bound m≥3 matters: the seven guarded m=2 stars include three graphs
with a small start defect, as the finite controls record.

## 5. Complete q=3 order and entry bounds

If H(T−w)=3, multiplicativity over the ordered strong components implies

    T−w = TT_a -> C3 -> TT_b.                             (9)

Indeed every other component has count1 and is a singleton; a strong
component of count3 is C3. Assume T is strong and n=a+b+4≥5.
If a,b>0, the same forced-opposite-extremes argument as (6) gives

    H(T) >= 12·2^(a+b−2)=3·2^(n−4).                      (10)

If exactly one tail is empty, choose an actual outgoing or incoming cap
port of w required by strongness. Its rooted two-slot C3 count is3, while
the remaining a+b−1 transitive vertices can be assigned freely. Therefore

    H(T) >= 3·2^(a+b−1)=3·2^(n−5).                        (11)

The a=b=0 boundary is order4 and is separate; no negative-power claim is
needed. These elementary bounds are special to the q=3 deletion carrier.

**Initial-cap classification.** If D=C3→TT_b, b≥2, and T is strong, no
vertex other than the apex w has start defect1 or2. The apex has start
defect2 exactly when it beats precisely two cap vertices and loses to
every tail vertex and the third cap vertex. There is no start defect1.

Triangle vertices have outdegree at least b+1≥3.

For the tail vertices, the final z beats only w and has
sigma_z=3[the preceding tail vertex beats w]. A penultimate
vertex v beating w has outdegree2 and a residual containing C3, so its
defect is at least6. If w beats v, its sole out-neighbor is z. Write
U=C3→TT_(b−2). Appending z to a Hamilton path of U+w is legal except when
that path ends w, giving

    sigma_v=H(U+w)−end_w(U+w) >= H(U)=3.                  (12)

For the inequality, w beats some cap vertex by strongness. In each of the
three Hamilton paths of U, insert w before the first vertex it beats;
this is a legal, nonterminal insertion and deletion recovers the original
path. The third-last tail vertex either has outdegree≥3, or outdegree2
with residual C3; earlier vertices have outdegree≥3. This treats every
non-apex vertex.

For the apex, strongness requires an out-neighbor in the cap. Outdegree1
therefore gives sigma_w=end_c(D)=0. Outdegree at least3 is excluded by the
degree bound. With exactly two outports, two cap outports give sigma_w=2:
the two bad covers follow the directed edge between those outports, placing
the third cap vertex in either slot; all tail vertices lie after w.
This is also the READY word11^b10. If the outports are one cap vertex c
and the tail vertex t_i, indexed0≤i<b, the independent audit's direct join
count gives

    sigma_w=3·2^i + [i=0] + 3[i=b−1] >=4.                (13)

Indeed J_T(t_i,w)=J_D(t_i,c)+end_t_i(D)=3·2^i+3[i=b−1],
while J_T(c,w)=[i=0]. These two bad-join types exhaust sigma_w. This proves
the complete classification, including the previously missing apex.

**Explicit hostile and repaired claim.** An earlier draft incorrectly
excluded every initial-cap vertex. The family with exactly two cap outports
is a counterexample at every b≥1. Its exact count is

    H=6·2^b+1,               sigma_w=2.                  (14)

There are2 paths starting w,3 ending w,2 joins from the third cap vertex
to a cap outport, and6·2^i joins from tail vertex t_i to the two cap outports.
Their sum is2+3+2+6(2^b−1), proving (14). At b9 this is3073, not either
desired count. The failed implication was extending the valid non-apex
argument to w. The strongest survivor is the precise exception
classification above; the independent audit prompted this repair before
the packet was marked stable.

**Entry-height theorem.** For any strong T of order n≥7 with H(T−w)=3,
if some vertex has start defect1 or2, then

    H(T) >= 3·2^(n−4).                                   (15)

Interior caps satisfy (10). Terminal caps have no such entry by Section3,
since their transitive prefix has length n−4≥3. Initial caps have only
the exception (14), which is larger than the bound in (15). Thus the
theorem includes the apex rather than incorrectly requiring an interior cap.

At H1409 or1427, (11) excludes n≥14. Retaining13 original names forces
n=13, and (15) then requires H≥1536 for either positive entry, a contradiction.
This rules out a strong q=3 preparation retaining all13 names at those
same counts. It does not require the outer-root end defect to remain zero.
