## 2. Why the first two obstructions are global in their stated classes

**Unit deletion.** H(T-w)=1 forces T-w transitive. The inherited one-apex
formula then counts every possible T from its ordered binary cut. Strong
one-apex graphs satisfy H>=2+2^(n-3), so both targets require n<=13.
All2047 strong words of orders3 through13 are checked by the formula,
literal before/after partitions, and independent Hamilton subset DP;
neither target occurs. SCC multiplication removes arbitrary weak padding.
The older feedback-word formula and its sharper extrema are credited,
not claimed anew. See [proof and certificate](recovery/ONE_APEX.md).

**Deletion count3.** Its deleted graph is exactly `TT_a -> C3 -> TT_b`.
The [structural proof](structure/PROOF.md) gives, for strong T of order n>=7,

    H(T-w)=3 and some S_v(2)-H(T) in {1,2}
        implies H(T) >= 3*2^(n-4).

At n13 this is1536, already above both targets; preserving more names only
increases the bound. The terminal-cap case has no such entry. Interior
caps have the bound by a forced ordered two-path join. An initial-cap
exception does have entry2 at w, but its exact count is6*2^b+1 and satisfies
the stronger bound. This exception was caught during independent review;
it is retained as a hostile control, not hidden by the final inequality.
The same theorem holds for end defects by applying it to the converse.

Thus any strong positive-entry preparation at these counts retaining the
thirteen names must have deletion count at least5 at the retained mark0.
This is a necessary condition, not an existence theorem. The first
[fixed q5 carrier](star/q5/PROOF.md) also fails: its one strong1409 graph
has root defect5, no end-zero vertex anywhere, and no positive entry;
