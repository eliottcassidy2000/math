# The Alpöge–Claude RH proof and absolute geometry: where the routes meet

## Status and how to read it

- **Formally verified RH and Dirichlet GRH.** [Alpöge's November 20 announcement](https://alpo.ge/remark.pdf) describes the result and releases the Lean development. The [Lean FRO and Mathlib joint statement](https://leanprover-community.github.io/blog/posts/riemann-hypothesis/) certifies that the RH proposition matches the vetted statement and that the exported proof passes independent checkers. [Andrew Sutherland's independent verification record](https://math.mit.edu/~drew/grh/) describes the clean rebuild, the axiom checks, and separate challenges for RH and all Dirichlet characters. This is not a theorem for arbitrary Hecke or automorphic \(L\)-functions, nor a statement about an institutional prize decision. The previously linked [arXiv:2611.24748](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24748) is a different paper, on Hilbert's tenth problem for number fields.
- **Human-readable guide, not a replacement proof.** Start with §§1–2 of [Slijepčević's 94-page outline](https://hexagonmath.org/pdf/2611.00812v1) for the endpoint and window quantifiers, then §§3–4 for the auxiliary fields and the slope comparison. Read §§1, 3, 4.1 and 5 of [the Arakelov perspective](https://hexagonmath.org/pdf/2611.00833v1) alongside it. The latter explicitly marks some inputs as hypotheses of its exposition; that is a statement about what the human-readable note itself reconstructs, not a finding of missing axioms in the verified theorem.

## Five different views of the argument

1. **Oscillation becomes a one-sided test.** The logarithmic derivative puts primes and prime powers on one side, and zeros as poles on the other. Smoothing by \((y/n-1)_+\) keeps the oscillation from an off-line zero but makes the prime observable suitable for estimates. Using positive weights on a fixed family of twists prevents opposite-sign cancellation of zero multiplicities at a common pole, in the zero region away from the removed main terms. The windowed Landau step (outline Prop. 2.2) is stronger than invoking classical eventual Landau blindly: it suffices to find arbitrarily late complete windows \([Y,Y^\lambda]\), with \(\lambda>1\) fixed, on which the weighted observable is bounded above by \(y^\theta\). The outline uses \(\lambda=3/2\). A bound at the left endpoint, or on average over the window, does not give that premise.
2. **Choose a host that can see the signal.** Auxiliary quadratic twists are chosen so that their smoothed sums are small on the needed scales. Abelian CM hosts, and character orthogonality on their Galois groups, turn counts of specified local Frobenius types into a weighted signal containing the character under test. Swamping the original character by averaging over unrestricted twists would lose the problem. The positivity of character weights at zero poles is different from saying that each twisted prime coefficient is nonnegative.
3. **Stepanov's multiplicity is replaced, not copied.** Over a finite field, an auxiliary function and a high order of vanishing make each counted point consume more of a fixed degree budget. In the arithmetic construction the chain of theta lifts on definite unitary groups, and jets of theta sections on CM abelian varieties, provide the multiplicity mechanism. At good inert primes the height-two formal group supplies a local divisibility gain, with a loss depending on the jet at the conjugate place. The proof is about \(p\)-adic divisibility across actual primes, not polynomial differentiation over a one-element field.
4. **Arakelov degrees keep the global ledger.** Extra divisibility at finite primes decreases an operator's finite-place height. A Bost-type slope inequality compares source and target Hermitian lattices, including archimedean norms; it prevents the prime gain from growing without limit. Injectivity or its averaged replacement is essential: an evaluation map that annihilates a whole source direction gives no contradiction from high divisibility. At the simplest linear-algebra level an injective square integer matrix with each column \(i\) divisible by \(d_i\) has \(\log|\det M|\ge\sum_i\log d_i\), whereas Hadamard gives \(\log|\det M|\le\sum_i\log\|M_i\|\). The arithmetic argument is not this toy determinant: it needs the actual comparison on growing Hermitian lattices, local matching, nonvanishing, and slope and norm bounds.
5. **Balance, then eliminate.** In the geometric exposition, averaging over a chain of length \(m\) amortizes some errors while others increase with \(m\); the scale of the resulting balance is \(m\) around \(\sqrt{x}\), up to slack powers (the outline's displayed master parameter is about \(x^{1/2-\varepsilon/16}\)). A schematic post-normalization error \(A/m+Bm\) makes the reason for such a square root transparent if \(A\) is order \(x\) and \(B\) order one; this is an illustration, not the exact error formula. For a fixed hypothetical zero with real part \(\beta>1/2\), first pick slack and \(\theta\) so the upper-bound exponent is strictly between \(1/2\) and \(\theta<\beta\). Only then use the large-window estimate and the analytic no-zero criterion. This is the dependency needed in our [earlier tournament windows note](../chanpak_window_endgame_20261123/README.md), not the transfer of the GRH conclusion to Chan–Pak.

## An exact interface to the F₁ counting distribution

Work in log-scale \(t=\log y\). For a Dirichlet character \(\chi\), set
\[
 M_\chi=\sum_{n\ge2}\Lambda(n)\chi(n)\,\delta_{\log n},\qquad
 F_\chi(t)=\sum_{n\le e^t}\Lambda(n)\chi(n)(e^t/n-1).
\]
Then \(F_\chi=k*M_\chi\), where \(k(u)=(e^u-1)\mathbf1_{u\ge0}\). In distributions \(k' = e^u\mathbf1_{u\ge0}\), so \((\partial_u^2-\partial_u)k=\delta_0\). In particular
\[
 (\partial_t^2-\partial_t)F_\chi=M_\chi,\qquad
 \int_0^\infty F_\chi(t)e^{-st}\,dt
 =-\frac{L'(s,\chi)}{s(s-1)L(s,\chi)}\quad(\Re s>1).
\]
The transform follows by computing \(\int_0^\infty(e^u-1)e^{-su}\,du\), with absolute convergence. A nonprincipal renormalizing term \(e^t L'(1,\chi)/L(1,\chi)\) changes neither the interior distributional inverse nor off-main-term poles. In the principal case the additional linear-in-\(t\) main term supplies the corresponding smooth correction; it must not be silently dropped. The identity is between the **finite-prime** channels; completion adds an archimedean gamma distribution.

Here is the exact local link to the proof's geometry. For a quadratic character \(\eta\) and an unramified prime \(p\), the inert part is obtained by the projector weight
\[
 (1-\eta(p)^j)\log p =
 \begin{cases}2\log p,&p\text{ inert and }j\text{ odd},\\0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}
\]
If \(a/w=y\), then \((a/p^j-w)_+=w(y/p^j-1)_+\). Thus the basic good-inert gain summand is a Riesz-smoothed prime-power distribution \(M_{\boldsymbol 1}-M_\eta\) on unramified primes, times \(w\). Ramified and archimedean terms, other characters produced by the host, and the local error terms are separate. This identifies the common input, not the slope bound.

In [Connes–Consani's scaling-site covers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.06560), primes correspond to periodic orbits of length \(\log p\), and Frobenius in an abelian extension is the orbit's monodromy. The same filter is the trace of the **virtual** character \(\boldsymbol1-\eta\) on the \(j\)-fold monodromy; its coefficient \(1-\eta(p)^j\) is never negative, but the virtual character is not an ordinary representation (dimension zero, nonzero trace at inert elements). This cleanly separates three things: a geometric encoding of prime data, a positive local gain, and the global quantitative inequality. The first two do not by themselves give the third.

## What “F₁” can and cannot mean here

- **Polynomial point counts / absolute zeta.** In [Soulé's framework](https://www.mathnet.ru/php/archive.phtml?jrnid=mmj&option_lang=eng&paperid=149&wshow=paper), if \(N(q)=\sum a_iq^i\), the normalized \(q\to1\) limit gives \(\zeta_{N}(s)=\prod_i(s-i)^{-a_i}\). With \(\mathbb P^1\) the count is \(q+1\), and this gives \(1/[s(s-1)]\), not the completed Riemann zeta. The polynomial model has only finitely many integral Tate factors; it could not retain the infinite nontrivial zero spectrum by itself.
- **Compatible Frobenius lifts.** [Borger's \(\Lambda\)-geometry](https://arxiv.org/abs/0906.3146) encodes descent through compatible endomorphisms lifting Frobenius at the primes, rather than demanding \(q=1\) as an actual finite field. [Manin's original proposal](https://www.numdam.org/book-part/AST_1995__228__121_0/) asks for something stronger than a point-count fit: an absolute base and a cohomological or intersection-theoretic account of the completed zeta.
- **Arithmetic and scaling sites.** [Connes–Consani's zeta construction](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/6804644F3837A06EA306376E893CE22A/S0010437X09004692a.pdf/schemes_over_1_and_zeta_functions.pdf) uses a distributional count whose Mellin transform is \(-\partial_s\log(\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\zeta(s))\); its discrete part is exactly the untwisted \(M_\chi\) above in log coordinates. It is already positive away from the singular boundary \(y=1\), independently of RH. That positivity is not the Weil positivity that would force a spectral line. The [Arithmetic Site](https://arxiv.org/abs/1502.05580) and [2026 absolute-curve refinement](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.06604) give geometric structure to this program; the checked theta-lift argument need not supply an absolute cohomology, a positive pairing on the arithmetic square, or a self-adjoint Hilbert–Pólya operator.
- **The finite-field comparison is precise.** For a smooth projective curve over \(\mathbb F_q\), the trace formula expresses the point count as \(q^r+1-\sum_i\alpha_i^r\). [The Weil bound](https://www.numdam.org/articles/10.1007/BF02684373/) is the additional weight assertion \(|\alpha_i|=\sqrt q\); a formal trace formula is not that assertion. Alpöge's argument proves the ordinary and Dirichlet zero locations through bounds on a smoothed arithmetic observable. The absolute-geometric question now asks for structure beyond the zero locations: can one recognize those arithmetic estimates as geometric positivity or obtain the operator, pairing, and boundary data independently?

## What transfers to the tournament project

The transferable method is to keep an exact observable and prove a separate inequality with the same quantifiers, not to import the resulting zero bound. The [strip F₁ deformation](STRIP-F1.md) exhibits a real point-count model but also proves that the \(q=1\) shadow can forget the integer height and depends on the chosen lift. The [Farey spectral comparison](FAREY-SPECTRUM.md) tests square-root cancellation on the actual recent transfer graph; a determinant zeta function by itself cannot impose it. Neither project asserts a new proof of RH, an absolute cohomology, or an exact Chan–Pak inverse.
