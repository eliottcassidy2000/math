## 1. Three precise conclusions and their different hypotheses

Throughout, work over `C` with

    W=(P1_x x P1_z)\{z=x^2}, t=1/(z-x^2),
    U0=A2_(x,t), Uinf=A2_(r,b_D),
    x=1/r, t=-r^2-r^4 b_D,
    D={r=0}, omega=dx wedge dt=r^2 dr wedge db_D.

Let `L_j={P in O(W): deg_t(P|U0)<=j}` be the
[proved global filtration](planar_jc48_sep08_dg_quadratic.md).
Use `J(F,G)=F_xG_t-F_tG_x` on `U0`.

**Theorem A — the full global square-prefix class.**
For every `H in L2`, `L in L1` with `deg_t H=2`, put `F=H^2+L`.
There is no **polynomial** `G in C[x,t]` of any degree with
`J(F,G)` a nonzero constant. In this statement the mate is not required
to extend to `W`.

**Theorem B — an arbitrary global quartic first coordinate.**
There are no `F,G in O(W)` with `J(F,G)=lambda!=0` and `deg_t F=4`.
Here **both coordinates must be global**. The global approximate-root
supplier in Section 5 is exactly what gives the stronger entry.

**Corollary C — every nonzero output direction avoids L4.**
If `F,G in O(W)` have `J(F,G)=lambda!=0`, then for every
`(alpha,beta)!=(0,0)`,

    alpha F+beta G not in L4.                       (1)

The mate's degree is unrestricted in all three statements. Theorem A
is not a rational-mate exclusion: Section 6 gives an actual global
quartic with nonconstant `L` and an exact rational mate. Theorem B
does not replace its global second coordinate by a merely rational or
arbitrary polynomial second coordinate before applying root gluing.

The closest proved mechanisms are
[leading coefficient exactness](planar_jc48_sep08_leading_exactness.md),
[the complete degree-eight differential classification](planar_jc48_sep08_boundary_exactness.md),
the seventeen marked case suppliers in Section 4, and
[the base-only Jacobian root-gluing theorem](planar_jc48_sep08_quartic_boundary.md).
The live concepts are the full global coefficient space, labelled finite

## 2. From an arbitrary leading coefficient to the exhaustive finite table

For Theorem A write

    H=N(x)t^2+P(x)t+Q(x), L=M(x)t+R(x).

The complete original global rules are

    N!=0, deg N<=8,
    P=2N8 x^6+2N7 x^5+sum_(i=0)^4 p_i x^i,
    Q=N8 x^4+N7 x^3+(p4-N6)x^2+(p3-N5)x+q0,
    M=sum_(i=0)^4 m_i x^i,
    R=m4 x^2+m3 x+r0.                               (2)

The nine coefficients of `N`, five free coefficients of `P`, `q0`,
five coefficients of `M`, and `r0` are the complete section space.
No finite active jet, constant boundary value, or position condition is
assumed in (2). In particular `N` is allowed to be a nonzero constant.

The leading coefficient of `F` as a quartic in `t` is `N^2`.
If a polynomial mate existed, it would be rational; applying the proved
leading-field theorem with a fourth root of `N^2` gives

    dx/sqrt(N) exact in K=C(x)(sqrt(N)).             (3)

Here `K` is the field generated by one square root. For square `N`,
it is `C(x)`; the two components of a split radical equation are not
mistaken for a connected curve. Nonzero scalar multiplication of `N`
multiplies (3) by a nonzero complex constant, so it changes neither
exactness nor the finite root multiplicities.

The original infinity multiplicity is determined **without a projective

    m_infinity=8-deg N.                             (4)

Indeed the local leading section is `r^8N(1/r)`, whose first nonzero

At `deg N=0`, all eight zeros are concentrated at original infinity.
Equation (4), rather than a permutation of an unmarked binary divisor,
is the routing rule in the next table.

## 3. The four exact labelled position conditions

All points named in the same row are pairwise distinct and finite.

**C411.** For finite partition `4+1+1`, let `p` be the fourfold
root and `q,r` the simple roots. Require

    2p=q+r.                                        (5)

**C611.** For finite partition `6+1+1`, let `p` be the sixfold
root and `q,r` the simple roots. Put `d1=p-q`, `d2=p-r`. Require

    3(d1+d2)^2=4d1*d2.                             (6)

**C431.** For finite partition `4+3+1`, let `p,q,r` have
multiplicities four, three, and one respectively. Require

    3/(p-q)+1/(p-r)=0,
    equivalently (p-q)+3(p-r)=0.                   (7)

The triple and simple root labels in (7) are not interchangeable.

**C5111.** For finite partition `5+1+1+1`, write `u=x-p`, with
the fivefold root at `p`, and

    N=u^5(a u^3+b u^2+c u+d),
    a*d!=0, residual cubic squarefree.

Require `b=c=0`. The full scalar of `N` is retained in `a,d`.
With this condition the cubic discriminant is `-27a^2d^2!=0`,
so the three other roots are indeed distinct and nonzero in `u`.

These loci are nonempty. Examples are `(p,q,r)=(0,1,-1)` for
C411, `(0,1,k)` with `3k^2+2k+3=0` for C611, `(0,3,-1)`
for C431, and `N=x^5(x^3+1)` for C5111. The C611 quadratic
is coprime to `k(k-1)`, so its example loses no distinctness condition.
None of these examples is asserted to have a polynomial Jacobian mate.

The first three conditions transform covariantly under affine source
scaling and translation, with their root labels preserved. The compilation
does not use an arbitrary projective change of `x`, which changes the
differential's weight and the distinguished infinity placement. Each
case supplier separately pays its actual source/surface normalization
and every remaining finite-position parameter.

## 5. From any global quartic coordinate to the square-prefix theorem

Assume now that **both** `F,G` are global on `W`,
`J(F,G)=lambda!=0`, and `deg_t F=4`. The proved
[quartic boundary theorem, Sections 1–4](planar_jc48_sep08_quartic_boundary.md),
with its [full independent audit](planar_jc48_sep08_quartic_boundary_audit.md),
has exactly these inputs. Its conclusion is a global canonical quadratic
approximate root

    F=H^2+L, H in L2, L in L1, deg_t H=2.           (8)

The required gluing argument is worth specifying, because it is not
globality of the first coordinate alone. On `U0`, the proved finite-pole
mechanism for a base-only nonzero Jacobian produces a polynomial
canonical root with leading coefficient `N` and remainder of `t` degree
at most one. The mate reduction uses only constant polynomial shears in
`F`, so there is no degree restriction on `G`.

On the other full chart, globality gives two polynomials `Fbar,Gbar`
with

    J_(r,b_D)(Fbar,Gbar)=lambda r^2.                 (9)

The extended supplier explicitly allows a nonzero base polynomial
Jacobian with zeros, including (9); it is not a constant-unit theorem
applied at a point where its premise fails. The affine fibre change
preserves the quartic degree over `C(r)`. If `N` is the chosen leading
coefficient of the first canonical root, then

    Nbar=r^8N(1/r)

is polynomial, since its square is the polynomial leading coefficient
of `Fbar`. The base-only theorem supplies a polynomial canonical root
of `Fbar` with leading coefficient `Nbar`.

But `H(1/r,-r^2-r^4b_D)` has the same leading coefficient and square
remainder of fibre degree at most one. Matching coefficients of degrees
four, three, and two makes the canonical root unique. Therefore this
rationally transformed root is exactly the polynomial root on the
second chart. It is regular on both **full** charts, as is `L=F-H^2`.
This proves (8), including its globality and exact filtration degrees.
A sign change of the chosen root causes no problem if the same sign is
used in both charts. The bounded independent helper reread by
`orthogonal_returns` confirms these precise globality hypotheses.

Now `G|U0` is an ordinary polynomial mate of `H^2+L`. Theorem A contradicts it, proving Theorem B. This argument
does not assert that `H` or `L` is a linear output combination of
`F,G`, or that either auxiliary function has a constant-Jacobian mate.
Those assertions would be an invalid shortcut through the older
low-degree theorem.

Finally, if a nonzero constant output combination
`P=alpha F+beta G` belonged to `L4`, complete its coefficient row to
an invertible constant `2 by 2` matrix. Its other row gives a global
`Q`, and `J(P,Q)` is that matrix's nonzero determinant times `lambda`.
The combination cannot be constant. If `deg_t P=4`, Theorem B applies.
If `deg_t P<=3`, the proved
[DG exclusion through cubic degree](planar_jc48_sep08_dg_quadratic.md),
Section 4, applies. That inherited corollary explicitly uses
[THM-2063 / one-fiber-linear-planar-keller-pairs](../../01-canon/theorems/THM-2063-one-fiber-linear-planar-keller-pairs.md),
[THM-2071 / quadratic-fiber-square-parity-gate](../../01-canon/theorems/THM-2071-quadratic-fiber-square-parity-gate.md),

with their full unrestricted-mate scopes. Thus all cases contradict
`P in L4`, proving corollary C.

## 6. Sharp controls for the information discarded by each map

The binary partition alone does not specify a route. The polynomial
`N=x^4` has one finite fourfold root and fourfold infinity, and
`dx/sqrt(N)=dx/x^2=d(-1/x)` is exact. In contrast,
`N=x^4(x-1)^4` has two finite fourfold roots and no infinity root.
Its two residues are `2` and `-2`, both nonzero. Their zero sum does
not make the differential exact. The latter is among the fifty uniformly
rejected finite partitions; it is not row 4 under another name.

Globality of `F` alone does not glue its canonical root. The inherited
hostile

    F=t^4+2x^4t^2, H=t^2+x^4

has global `F`, but the canonical `H` has a genuine `r^-4` pole on
the second chart. Both original partial derivatives of `F` vanish at
`t=0`, so it has no polynomial constant-Jacobian mate and does not
contradict the gluing theorem. The point of this control is to preserve
the missing **global mate** premise, not to propose a Keller example.

There are actual rational mates even with all global square-prefix
hypotheses. At `p=0`, put

    q=1+x^2t, v=xq,
    H=x^2q^2+q, L=v, F=H^2+L,
    G=1/(2v)-H.                                     (10)

The full two charts show `H in L2`, `L in L1` and `F in L4` with
`deg_t F=4`. Direct differentiation gives `J(F,G)=1`. Nevertheless
`G` has the actual affine principal part `1/(2x)` at `x=0`, with
`t` fixed. Hence it is neither a polynomial mate nor a function in
`O(W)`. This is the proved shared-six rational submersion. It blocks
replacing the polynomial conclusion in A, or the global second coordinate
in B and C, by an arbitrary rational mate. No Keller realization is
inferred from this rational control.
