## 2. Theorem A: presentation counts, the single-presentation classes, and the pendant nodes

*Prior art.* (A1) is LEM-003 with THM-849 section 1; (A2) is THM-2450 section 2; (A3) is THM-2477's all-order theorem together with THM-2453's palindromic count; they were drafted here as new and corrected after a grep of the canon (MISTAKE-647). The proof of (A3) below is retained as a *second route* (circulants and the uniform prefix-rerouting theorem instead of the closing property and Benhocine-Wojda; the successor-automorphism step is common to both). (A4) is a corollary of THM-4729 L2 and (A5) corrects HYP-9347 (4).

**Theorem A (A1-A3 CITED; A4, A5 PROVED).** Let `T` be a tournament on `n` vertices with strong components `B_1 > ... > B_m`.

(A1) Two Hamiltonian paths of `T` have the same tiling iff they lie in one `Aut(T)`-orbit; hence `tc(T) = H(T)/|Aut(T)|` is the number of distinct tilings (presentations, "views") of the class.

(A2) `H(T) = prod H(B_i)`, `|Aut(T)| = prod |Aut(B_i)|`, hence `tc(T) = prod tc(B_i)`.

(A3) `tc(T) = 1` iff every `B_i` is `K1` or `C3`. The number of such classes of order `n` is the number of compositions of `n` into parts 1 and 3, `a(n) = a(n-1) + a(n-3)` (A000930: `1, 1, 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 9, 13, 19, 28, ...`), which the census reproduces at `n = 5..9` (`4, 6, 9, 13, 19`); their `H = |Aut| = 3^j` with `j` the number of `C3` blocks.

(A4) (Rotation inequality.) If `T` is strong and `C = (v_0, ..., v_{n-1})` is a Hamiltonian cycle, let `Z_C <= Z_n` be the group of cyclic shifts of `C` that are automorphisms of `T`. The `n` rotations of `C` are Hamiltonian paths realising exactly `n/|Z_C|` distinct tilings, all closed; hence `tc(T) >= n/|Z_C|`, `tc(T) < n` forces a nontrivial cyclic automorphism along every Hamiltonian cycle, and a strong tournament with trivial automorphism group has `tc = H >= n`. (Equivalently, from THM-4729 L2: the closed presentations number `n HC/|Aut| <= tc`, and the `Aut`-orbit of `C` contains `|Aut|/|Z_C|` Hamiltonian cycles.)

(A5) (Pendant nodes.) In the converse-merged fundamental black graph `B` of THM-4731 the pendant (degree-1) nodes are exactly (i) the converse pairs of non-palindromic stacks over `{K1, C3}`, `(a(n) - p(n))/2` of them with `p(n) = pal13(n)`, and (ii) the self-converse classes with `tc - bm = 2`. At `n = 5..9` the classes of kind (ii) are exactly `(1, S4, 1)` at `n = 6` and `(1, 1, S4, 1, 1)` at `n = 8`, both with `(H, |Aut|, tc, bm) = (5, 1, 5, 3)`, so `B` has `1, 3, 3, 6, 7` pendant nodes at `n = 5..9`. The degree-0 nodes of `B` are exactly the pure-blue classes (`tc = bm`).

*Proof.* (A1) Two paths `P, P'` have the same tiling iff the positional map `v_i(P) -> v_i(P')` preserves every arc, i.e. is an automorphism carrying `P` to `P'`; `Aut` acts freely (LEM-003), so orbits have size `|Aut|` and their number is `H/|Aut|`.

(A2) A Hamiltonian path of a non-strong tournament must exhaust each strong component before entering the next one (all arcs between components point downward), so the Hamiltonian paths are exactly the concatenations of Hamiltonian paths of `B_1, ..., B_m`, and every automorphism preserves the condensation order, so `Aut(T) = prod Aut(B_i)`.

(A3) (<=) `K1` and `C3` have a single tiling (the three rotations of `C3` all read "apex backward"), and (A2). (=>) By (A2) every block has `tc = 1`, so it suffices to show that a strong tournament with a single tiling is `K1` or `C3`. Let `T` be strong on `n >= 3` vertices with one tiling, and `C = (v_0, ..., v_{n-1})` a Hamiltonian cycle (Camion). The rotation starting at `v_i` reads, at tile `(a, b)`, the orientation of `{v_{i+a}, v_{i+b}}`; since all rotations read the same tiling, `v_{i+a} -> v_{i+b}` iff `v_a -> v_b`, and comparing the rotation at `0` with the rotation at `-a` gives `v_a -> v_b` iff `b - a in S := {d : v_0 -> v_d}`: `T` is the circulant tournament on `Z_n` with symbol `S` (this is the successor-automorphism step of `merged_metagraph_finite_pure_atoms_20261020` section 3). A circulant is regular, so `n` is odd. For `n = 3` it is `C3`; for `n = 5` both symbols give the regular tournament `T5`, which has `tc = 15/5 = 3`. For odd `n >= 7` the rotation tiling `t_C` (tile `(a, b)` forward iff `b - a in S`) is `sigma`-fixed, i.e. blue, whereas the uniform prefix-rerouting theorem exhibits a non-palindromic (black) Hamiltonian path, whose tiling is not `t_C`; so `tc >= 2`, contradiction. The count is the number of compositions into parts 1 and 3 because distinct block sequences give non-isomorphic stacks and `|Aut(C3)| = H(C3) = 3`.

(A4) Rotations `i` and `j` have the same tiling iff the positional map `v_{i+a} -> v_{j+a}` is an automorphism, i.e. the shift by `j - i` lies in `Z_C`; so the rotation tilings are in bijection with `Z_n / Z_C`. Each rotation is closed because its apex pair `{v_i, v_{i-1}}` carries the cycle arc `v_{i-1} -> v_i`. If `Aut(T)` is trivial the rotations are `n` distinct tilings and `tc = H >= n`.

(A5) By THM-4731 (3) the degree of a merged node in `B` is `tc` at a non-self-converse node (one node for the converse pair of classes) and `(tc - bm)/2` at a self-converse class. Degree 1 therefore means either a non-self-converse class with `tc = 1`, i.e. by (A3) a non-palindromic `{K1, C3}`-stack (its converse is the reversed stack, a different class, and the pair is one node), or a self-converse class with `tc - bm = 2`; degree 0 means `tc = bm` (every presentation blue, i.e. pure-blue; the palindromic `{K1, C3}`-stacks have `tc = bm = 1` by THM-2444's rigidity lemma). The census identifies the `tc - bm = 2` self-converse classes at `n <= 9` as the two stacks above (section 3's class files, `(H, |Aut|, tc, bm) = (5, 1, 5, 3)`; `S4` has five tilings of which three are blue, and the symmetric wings of singletons change nothing). QED.

*Information reading.* `tc` counts the distinct ways the class can be read along a Hamiltonian path, the number of distinct "views" of THM-849's observers. Single-view classes are the stacks of the two atoms `K1` (no intransitivity) and `C3` (the minimal intransitive atom, whose three views coincide by rotation). The leaves of the black network are the converse pairs of those stacks and the `S4`-centred palindromic stacks; `T5`, the next atom, already has three views, and every strong tournament has at least `n / |Z_C|` views by (A4).

**Lemma C (PROVED).** If `T` is strong with `tc(T) = 3`, then for every Hamiltonian cycle `C` the index `n/|Z_C|` is 1, 2 or 3: `T` is a circulant with respect to `C`, or `|Z_C| = n/3`, or `n = 2 mod 4` and `|Z_C| = n/2`. *Proof.* By (A4), `n/|Z_C| <= 3` and divides `n`, and `|Z_C|` is odd (tournaments have no automorphism of even order), so `n/|Z_C| in {1, 2, 3}`, and `2` requires `n/2` odd. QED. (The audit exhibits the index-2 case at `n = 6`: the two strong self-converse classes with `(H, |Aut|, tc) = (45, 3, 15)` have Hamiltonian cycles with `Z_C` of order 3 and exactly two rotation tilings; so the case is not vacuous for larger `tc`.) The data give `tc = 3` for strong classes only at `n = 5` (`T5`), and the stack product then gives:

**Corollary (FINITE-EXACT for `n <= 9`; conjectured for all `n`, HYP-9352 (1)).** The classes with `tc = 3` are exactly the stacks over `{K1, C3, T5}` with exactly one `T5` block; their number is `sum_j C(n - 5 - 2j, j) (n - 4 - 2j)` (`1, 2, 3, 6, 11` at `n = 5..9`, matching the census: at `n = 8, 9` the `(H, |Aut|) = (45, 15)` members are the stacks containing both a `T5` and a `C3`).

The smallest strong `tc` grows with `n` (`1, 5, 3, 9, 9, 17, 25` at `n = 3..9`), so the strong spectrum leaves every small value behind; proving `tc >= 5` for strong `T notin {K1, C3, T5}` (the content of the corollary for all `n`) would follow from a lower bound `H(T) > 3 |Aut(T)|` for strong `T` of order `>= 6`, which the data support by a wide margin (the minimum strong `H` is `15, 25, 45, 75` at `n = 6, 7, 8, 9` against `|Aut| <= 3, 21, 9, 81` there; the strong class count `178,133` at `n = 9` agrees with THM-4729's independent value) but which is not proved here.

## 4. The forbidden fibre sizes 7 and 21 (THM-1370, CITED)

The presentation counts occurring at `n = 9` are the odd numbers `1, 3, 5, 9, 11, 13, 15, 17, 19, 23, ...`: every odd value below 2883 other than `7` and `21` occurs, and above 2883 the spectrum is sparse up to its maximum 3299 (at `n = 7, 8` the same two gaps are the only ones below 63 and 611 respectively). The two values `7` and `21` are likewise missing from the set of Hamiltonian-path counts `H` of all classes at every `n = 5..9` (`63` is also missing at `n = 7` but occurs at `n = 8`). That `7` and `21` are not Hamiltonian-path counts of any tournament is canon: THM-1370 (boxeph, 2026-07-20) proves it for all `n` by exactly the argument below (multiplicativity over strong components, the one-line insertion lemma, the Busch floor and the exhaustive small spectra); outside the repository, MathOverflow question 232751 (answers by bof and Gordon Royle; see D. Grinberg's Math 530 lecture notes, Lecture 12, Remark 1.1.11, version 2026-04-27) excludes 7 and 21 by a strong-order bound with a small enumeration and shows every other odd number in `[1, 80555]` is attained; whether every odd number above 80555 is attained is open there and in THM-1370 (which lists completeness as a conjecture), and nothing here bears on it. The proof was re-derived here before THM-1370 was found (MISTAKE-647) and is kept only because the census supplies the strong minima through `n = 9` and the metagraph consequence.

**Theorem B (= THM-1370, CITED; the metagraph consequence is this session's).** No tournament has exactly 7 or exactly 21 Hamiltonian paths. Consequently a node of the line metagraph with trivial automorphism group (a rigid class) never has fibre size `tc = 7` or `21`; at `n <= 9` no node of any kind has these fibre sizes.

*Proof.* Let `h(n)` be the minimum number of Hamiltonian paths of a strong tournament on `n` vertices. (i) Insertion monotonicity: for any tournament `T` and vertex `v`, every Hamiltonian path of `T - v` extends to a Hamiltonian path of `T` (insert `v` at the front if `v` beats the first vertex, at the end if the last vertex beats `v`, and otherwise between two consecutive vertices `a -> v -> b` where the sign changes), distinct paths extending to distinct paths, so `H(T) >= H(T - v)`. (ii) Moon's theorem: a strong tournament on `n >= 4` vertices has a strong subtournament on `n - 1` vertices, so `h(n) >= h(n-1)`. (iii) The census (strong iff `closed > 0`) gives `h(3), ..., h(9) = 3, 5, 9, 15, 25, 45, 75`, which is `5^((n-1)/3)`, `3 . 5^((n-3)/3)`, `9 . 5^((n-5)/3)` for `n = 1, 0, 2 (mod 3)`, exactly Busch's values (Electron. J. Combin. 13 (2006), N3, Theorem 1 and Corollary 1: `h(n) = 3 beta^(n-3), beta^(n-1), 9 beta^(n-5)` for `n = 0, 1, 2 (mod 3)`, `beta = 5^(1/3)`); the strong Hamiltonian-path counts at `n = 5, 6, 7` are `{9, 11, 13, 15}`, `{15, 17, 19, 23, 25, 27, 29, ...}` and `{25, 27, 29, ...}`; by (ii) `h(n) >= 25` for `n >= 8`. (iv) By (A2), `H(T) = prod H(B_i)` over the strong blocks. If `H(T) = 7` then some block has `H = 7` (7 is prime), impossible: a strong block with `H = 7` would need `n <= 4` by (iii), where the counts are 1, 3, 5. If `H(T) = 21` then the blocks have counts multiplying to 21, so either one block has `H = 21` (impossible: strong counts at `n <= 7` avoid 21 and `h(n) >= 25` beyond) or two blocks have counts 3 and 7 (impossible by the first case). For a rigid class `tc = H`, giving the fibre statement; the `n <= 9` statement for all classes is the census. QED.

Whether `7` or `21` can occur for a non-rigid class (`H = 7 |Aut|` with `|Aut| >= 5`, e.g. `H = 35, 49, 63`) is HYP-9352 (2).

## 5. Descent potentials: `c3` descends (prior art), `H` does not

`black_descent_20261020c/cycles.md` proves an exact line law for the number of cyclic triangles (`c3(T^P) - c3(T) = s(a) - s(b) - 1` along the line of the word `P = (a, ..., b)`) and that every strong black-active node has a black neighbour with fewer cyclic triangles, so `c3` is a descent potential on the black graph whose sinks are the nodes without such a neighbour; the packets `bridge_hubs_and_double_descent_20261020` and `scc_basins_20261020d` build on it, and black connectivity remains open there. The natural second candidate, `H`, is not a potential of this kind: the census column `minHblack` (the minimum `H` over the black neighbours of a class) shows `4, 19, 99, 867, 13258` non-pure-blue classes at `n = 5, 6, 7, 8, 9` with no black neighbour of strictly smaller `H` (at `n = 9`, 16 of them have a neighbour of equal `H` and the other 13,242 only larger ones), with `H` values spread through the range (at `n = 8`: `3 (x6), 5 (x5), 9 (x12), 11 (x4), 13 (x4), 15 (x20), ...`). Every pendant stack is such a sink (its single black neighbour is larger), but so are hundreds of interior classes, so `H` carries no gradient structure on the black graph.

## 7. Terminology

- **Presentation count / view count** `tc(C)`: the number of distinct tilings of a class; **single-presentation (single-view) class**: `tc = 1` (THM-2477's "rigid" class).
- **Strong spectrum** `S(n)`: the set of presentation counts of strong tournaments of order `n`; every class's count is a product of strong-spectrum values by (A2).
- **Rotation group** `Z_C`: the cyclic shifts of a Hamiltonian cycle that are automorphisms; the **rotation inequality** `tc >= n/|Z_C|`.
- **Pendant nodes of `B`**: converse pairs of non-palindromic `{K1, C3}`-stacks and self-converse classes with `tc - bm = 2`.
- **Forbidden fibre sizes**: 7 and 21 (THM-1370).
- **H-descent sink**: a non-pure-blue class with no black neighbour of smaller `H`.
