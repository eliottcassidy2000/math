# The level-22 oldform operator and the boundary of positivity transfer

**CITED modular identification and oldform decomposition; PROVED operator,
finite-field and cone consequences; FINITE-EXACT controls. Collatz coverage OPEN.**

Inheritance: [eta coordinates](eta11_lossless_coordinates_20261005.md),
[the marked level-11 dessin](level11_dessin_golden_torsion_20261005.md), and
[the incoming adaptive mixture flow](collatz_adaptive_mixture_flow_20261005.md).
The closest mechanism is the Hecke/Frobenius characteristic polynomial. The
hostiles are a negative fourth power, noncommuting ordered affine carries, and
equal endpoints with opposite payment. The necessary sidecars are the lattice,
operator, source and guard. This note adds an explicit interface to a classical
oldform example; it does not claim a new modularity theorem or Collatz basin.

## 1. What changes at level 22

Write

\[
f(q)=\eta(\tau)^2\eta(11\tau)^2
=q\prod_{n\ge1}(1-q^n)^2(1-q^{11n})^2,
\qquad f_2(q)=f(q^2).
\]

**CITED:** $f$ is the weight-two normalized newform of conductor 11 associated
with $E:y^2+y=x^3-x^2-10x-20=X_0(11)$.
[Elkies's conductor-11 entry](https://people.math.harvard.edu/~elkies/nature.html)
also identifies this curve as $X_0(22)/w_{22}$. The curve $X_0(22)$ itself
has genus two, so it is a different object from its elliptic quotient.

The exact decomposition

\[
S_2(\Gamma_0(22))=\mathbf C f\oplus\mathbf C f_2,
\qquad S_2(\Gamma_0(22))^{\rm new}=0
\]

is explicitly **CITED** in [Stein, Example 9.6, printed page
162](https://wstein.org/books/modform/stein-modform.pdf#page=176).
His section 9.2 defines the degeneracy map $f(q)\mapsto f(q^t)$.
Thus 22 is the ambient level of these two oldforms; the underlying newform and
elliptic curve still have conductor 11. Merely replacing 11 by 22 in the eta
product is not this construction.

For an independent dimension check, the standard genus formula is

\[
g=1+\frac{\mu}{12}-\frac{e_2}{4}-\frac{e_3}{3}-\frac{c}{2}.
\]

See [Milne, Theorem 2.22](https://www.jmilne.org/math/CourseNotes/MF.pdf#page=37).
At level 22 the index is 36, the cusp count is four, and both elliptic counts
are zero, giving genus two. The weight-two cusp-form dimension equals the
genus. The leading terms $f=q+\cdots$, $f_2=q^2+\cdots$ prove independence.
At level 11 the corresponding data are $12,2,0,0$, giving genus one.

## 2. The explicit operator, not just its trace

Use the unscaled basis $(f,f_2)$ and column coordinates. On series,

\[
U_2\sum a_nq^n=\sum a_{2n}q^n.
\]

At level 11, $T_2=U_2+2V_2$, where $V_2f=f_2$, and $T_2f=a_2f=-2f$.
Consequently, on the level-22 oldspace,

\[
U_2f=-2f-2f_2,\qquad U_2f_2=f,\qquad
A=[U_2]_{(f,f_2)}=\begin{pmatrix}-2&1\\-2&0\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{1}
\]

Stein's cited example gives its characteristic polynomial $X^2+2X+2$;
(1) specifies the basis and the complete integral operator. It follows that

\[
A^2+2A+2I=0,\qquad A^4=-4I,\qquad A^8=16I.
\tag{2}
\]

For the curve $E$, the good-prime-2 local factor is $1+2T+2T^2$.
Thus (1) realizes the same local polynomial through an oldform operator.
This is a polynomial/operator comparison at a specified place, not a claim
that the entire Galois action is generated by this one matrix.

On the chosen lattice modulo 3,

\[
\overline A=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
J\overline A J=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&1\end{pmatrix},\quad
J=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{3}
\]

The polynomial becomes $X^2-X-1$, irreducible over $\mathbf F_3$.
Its root generates $\mathbf F_9$, has order eight, and acts transitively
on the eight nonzero vectors. This agrees, up to basis, with the inherited
Frobenius-at-2 action on $E[3]$. The latter identification is a **CITED**
torsion/Frobenius theorem, not something inferred from the finite census.
The golden match does not lift to the displayed matrices modulo 9: traces
are $7$ and $1$, and determinants are $2$ and $8$.

## 3. An exact positivity obstruction

A real cone here is closed under addition and multiplication by nonnegative
scalars; it is pointed when $C\cap(-C)=\{0\}$. No topological closure
assumption is needed.

**PROVED:** there is no nonzero pointed cone $C\subset\mathbf R^2$ with

\[
A(C)\subset C.
\]

Indeed, $v\in C$ implies $-4v=A^4v\in C$, hence $-v\in C$, and
pointedness forces $v=0$. This proof is invariant under a real change of
basis. In particular, this full two-dimensional operator cannot become a
nonnegative transfer matrix after a real similarity. The eighth power is a
positive scalar, but retaining only that power discards the intermediate
operator phase. This does not prohibit a larger signed representation, a
nonfaithful positive quotient, or other source-labelled Collatz operators.

The coefficient sequence already warns against literal coefficient weights:

\[
f=q-2q^2-q^3+2q^4+q^5+2q^6-2q^7+0q^8+\cdots.
\]

Negative coefficients prevent nonnegative weights; squared magnitudes still
vanish at index 8. Adding a positive correction could restore full support,
but no Collatz flow inequality follows from doing so.

### A positive eight-state lift exists

The obstruction is to pointed positivity in the same two-dimensional
representation. There is an explicit positive representation with a signed
quotient. On eight basis vectors indexed cyclically by $j=0,\ldots,7$, set

\[
P e_j=w_j e_{j+1},\qquad
w_j=\begin{cases}1&j\text{ even},\\2&j\text{ odd}.\end{cases}
\]

Then $P$ preserves the positive orthant and $P^8=16I$. Let

\[
J=\begin{pmatrix}
0&1&-1&1&0&-1&1&-1\\
1&0&-1&2&-1&0&1&-2
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Direct multiplication gives $AJ=JP$. More structurally, its column $j$
is $A^j(0,1)^\mathsf{T}/2^{\lfloor j/2\rfloor}$, and (2) closes the cycle.
The first two columns are an integral basis, so $J:\mathbf Z^8\to\mathbf Z^2$
is surjective, and $\ker J$ is $P$-invariant. Thus the signed oldform
operator is an actual integral quotient of this positive weighted graph.

Columns $j+4$ and $j$ are negatives. Their positive combinations map the
positive orthant onto the entire real plane. The quotient therefore destroys
pointed positivity, exactly as the two-dimensional obstruction requires.
The eight graph vertices retain a cyclic phase with eight possibilities;
amplitudes and linear combinations still matter. No minimal-dimension claim
is made, and no map from this cycle to Collatz transitions or ROOT is supplied.

## 4. What Langlands actually preserves here

The arithmetic side has the rank-two Tate module

\[
T_\ell E=\varprojlim_r E[\ell^r],\qquad
\rho_{E,\ell}:\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbf Q}/\mathbf Q)
\longrightarrow\operatorname{GL}_2(\mathbf Z_\ell).
\]

For $p\ne11,\ell$, arithmetic Frobenius has characteristic polynomial

\[
X^2-a_pX+p,\qquad a_p=p+1-\#E(\mathbf F_p).
\]

Modularity matches these local data with the weight-two newform. This is a
concrete instance of the arithmetic/automorphic correspondence discussed in
[Emerton, sections 5.6 and 7](https://math.uchicago.edu/~emerton/pdffiles/reciprocity.pdf).
The two-dimensional Galois module and the associated automorphic
representation are different types of object; the latter is not simply this
two-dimensional complex matrix space. Characteristic polynomials retain
trace and determinant, not an ordered Collatz route or its source guard.

The commuting good-prime Hecke algebra also cannot faithfully preserve a
noncommuting affine alphabet through a multiplicative map. For actual odd
valuation letters $F_a(n)=(3n+1)/2^a$,

\[
F_2F_1(n)=\frac{9n+5}{8},\qquad
F_1F_2(n)=\frac{9n+7}{8}.
\]

These are distinct rational maps, each with an infinite positive legal source
cylinder: $n=11\pmod {16}$ for word $(1,2)$, and $n=9\pmod {16}$ for
word $(2,1)$. There is no claim that both words are legal on one source.
Any multiplicative map of this formal affine semigroup into a commutative
algebra identifies these two orders. Recovering source-aware paths therefore
requires the ordered operator and guard outside this commutative quotient.

Even an exact endpoint is insufficient for payment: $3\xrightarrow{1}5$
grows, while $13\xrightarrow{3}5$ decreases. Both are legal paths. The
original source is essential when testing the inequality.

## 5. The nearby 22 in inverse Collatz has a different origin

The incoming [adaptive mixture note](collatz_adaptive_mixture_flow_20261005.md)
chooses, for each odd $3$-unit $n>1$, an exponent $1\le a\le6$ such
that $z=(2^an-1)/3$ is odd and divisible by 3. Then

\[
U(z)=n,\qquad z<\frac{64}{3}n<22n.
\]

The last 22 is an integer upper bound on an elementary size ratio. No map
identifies that bound with a modular level or conductor. In the mixture
construction, positive formal counter weights likewise do not show that
every integer has a grounded ROOT route.
