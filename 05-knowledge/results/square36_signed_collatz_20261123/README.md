# Signed Collatz steps, the first 36 square-sum vertices and tournament path counts

There are two ways to carry the split/inert distinction into a graph. Multiplying odd integers preserves a two-valued character. A square sum constrains two endpoints at once. Keeping these two observables separate gives a complete obstruction for consecutive signed Collatz steps and a positive construction of 72 tournament counts with square adjacencies.

**One-arc tournament result.** Of the 118 square-sum edges on the 72 heights \(100v\pm1\), exactly 101 occur as the two counts of tournaments differing in one arc. Those edges have exactly one Hamilton cycle, the lift of a path from 2 to 8 through the first 36 square-sum vertices. Thirteen is the minimum common tournament order for its pairwise witnesses. At height 199, continuing along the cycle would require switching the strong core from order 8 to order 10 while retaining the count, which is impossible by neutral flips. A target number can therefore specify two individually feasible moves that cannot be composed. [All-order edge classification](PHYSICAL.md) · [Sheet symmetry and the surviving path](LIFT.md)

## Which sign is being used?

For every odd integer put \(\chi_4(x)=+1\) at \(x\equiv1\pmod4\) (split, S) and \(-1\) at \(x\equiv3\pmod4\) (inert, I). Multiplication gives SS = II = S and SI = I. In additive coordinates \(b(x)=(x-1)/2\pmod2\), this is \(b(xy)=b(x)+b(y)\). Ordinary parity uses the ring \(\mathbf F_2\): even/odd addition is XOR and multiplication is AND. On odd primes, split and inert here agree with splitting in the Gaussian integers. For composite odd moduli the character records the parity of the number of inert prime factors counted with multiplicity. For example, both factors of 21 are inert.

On odd integers, negation interchanges the two residue classes. For the signed Collatz maps on nonzero odds one has \(U_+(-x)=-U_-(x)\). Hence the three classical positive cycles through 1, 5 and 17 for the fixed minus sign give the three corresponding known negative cycles of the usual plus map. This conjugacy applies to entire orbits. Fixed sign, alternation in time, and a sign chosen from the current input give different dynamics. The unrestricted fixed-sign cycle question remains open. [Bell and Lagarias, introduction](https://www.impan.pl/shop/en/publication/transaction/download/product/82190) · [Cycle lists and comparison](COLLATZ.md)

For an odd modulus \(m>1\), a branch with prescribed valuation \(j\) acts by \(x\mapsto 2^{-j}(3x+e)\pmod m\). It has an inverse modulo \(m\) exactly when \(3\nmid m\). Its two sign choices have the same slope and differ by \(2^{1-j}\), a unit for every odd \(m\). Thus both choices occur as legitimate modular maps in both mod-four classes of modulus. The choice \(e=\chi_4(x)\) has a separate intrinsic justification on integer inputs: exactly this sign permits at least two halvings. At an even modulus division by two loses a bit of precision, so to compute a quotient modulo \(2^b\) one retains the numerator modulo \(2^{b+j}\). These distinctions specify what information the multiplication table retains.

**Local versus exact inversion.** Every center of a signed Collatz square-sum star has finite degree, by reducing the exponent modulo three to get three elliptic curves. Yet at center 79, every finite set of modular square tests accepts arbitrarily large exponents. Two fixed guards can recur together at infinitely many centers by a Pell equation. Any three fixed distinct guards occur together at only finitely many centers. [Proof and exact ternary density](LOCAL-GLOBAL.md)

**A finite modular stopping rule for count selection.** From exactly 199, ten binary digits determine either target 201 or 701 among all one-arc outcomes. Requiring a square sum lowers the latter threshold to eight bits. Across the exponent-61 perfect number, a single physical arc from an exact lower host succeeds if its gain is 2 modulo \(2^{276}\). A strong lower host needs only 274 bits. These are sufficient tests, and an arc passing the exponent-61 test remains open. [Bounds, sharp small example and the coherent walk 43–57–199](PRECISION.md)

**Exponent 61.** The two neighboring odd labels of every odd-exponent Euclid midpoint lie opposite each other on an induced square-sum six-cycle. At exponent 61 an explicit such cycle has actual tournament witnesses at five of its six labels. The upper endpoint is the remaining absolute height. At a general even midpoint there is always an odd-label square-sum detour of at most five steps in a window of width \(O(M^{3/4})\) about the midpoint \(M\). That exponent is optimal for bounded-length detours. [Five realized labels and factored tournaments](DETOUR.md) · [Sharp local window](LOCAL-WINDOW.md)
