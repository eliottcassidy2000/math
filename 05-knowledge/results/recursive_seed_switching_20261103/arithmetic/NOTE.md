## ARITH-1. The exact rank-four integral 2-kernel

Let r be the nonnegative reversed zero mask. For r>0 the reader word is the
low-to-high binary expansion of r, complemented, so its last bit is 0. Put
A(0)=0 and A(r)=H_one-apex(word(r))-1. The inherited inverse gives

    A(2u+1)=A(u),
    A(2u)=A(u)+u+(u mod 2).                              (1)

Both hold for u=0. Induction proves A(r) is even and 0<=A(r)<=r. In the even
case, when u is odd use evenness to strengthen A(u)<=u to A(u)<=u-1.

With epsilon(r)=r mod 2, the state (A,r,epsilon,1) has the exact MSB-first
bit updates

    0: (A,r,epsilon,1) -> (A+r+epsilon,2r,0,1),
    1: (A,r,epsilon,1) -> (A,2r+1,1,1).                (2)

More precisely, the integral module generated by all sequences
A(2^j n+a), 0<=a<2^j, has the Z-basis

    f1=A(n),   f2=n+epsilon(n),   f3=2n,   f4=2.

It is closed under both sections because

| sequence | value at 2n | value at 2n+1 |
|---|---|---|
| f1 | f1+f2 | f1 |
| f2 | f3 | f3+f4 |
| f3 | 2f3 | 2f3+f4 |
| f4 | f4 | f4 |

Conversely f2=A(2n)-A(n), f3=A(4n)-A(2n), and
f4=A(4n+2)-A(n)-f3, so every displayed generator belongs to the kernel module.
Their evaluation determinant at n=0,1,2,3 has absolute value 16. They are
independent, and the kernel therefore has rank exactly four. This is a weighted
integer representation, not a four-state Boolean automaton.

For a fixed actual seed, write its eight fields as (h,e,t,F,p,q,P2,S3), and
fix a prefix clock M=2^s, s>=1. Define

    K=t-q+Mp>0,   d=q-e,   c=h+MF.

Then q>0, 0<=d<=q, and the exact target is

    N(r)=Kr+qA(r)+d epsilon(r)+c.                       (3)

For a residual D=N-c the binary sections are exactly

    even: (K,q,d,D) -> (2K+q,q,q,D),
    odd:  (K,q,d,D) -> (2K,q,0,D-K-d).                (4)

This proves the section equations used by [selector/inverse.py](../selector/inverse.py).
Repeated even sections have K_j=2^j K+(2^j-1)q, and d_j=q for j>=1. The ratio
K_j/q is strictly increasing without bound. Consequently a literal finite
bank of these coefficient tuples cannot close under all sections, even after
normalizing by a common scalar. This says nothing against a finite controller
with integer registers, or another representation of the accepted branches.

The sibling selector lane also identifies why global binary search is invalid.
For m>=2, A(2^m-2)=2^(m-1) and A(2^m-1)=0, so the adjacent target difference
is K+d-q2^(m-1), eventually negative for every fixed q>0. By contrast a
section with U=floor(D/K) is strictly increasing on 0,...,U whenever
K>q(U-1)+d: its adjacent difference is at least K-qu-d at index u.
Thus exact bisection is sound after that guard, as used by the selector, even
though it is never sound globally for this family.

Reducing (2) modulo any m, in particular 49 or 343, gives a finite automaton
for the scalar N(r) mod m at fixed seed and clock. It does not give exact
acceptance, nor the full eight-field state, ten-coordinate natural-pair phase,
or an independently named spare's joint attachment tensor. Those remain
separately typed sidecars; the full named graph always suffices to recover them.

## ARITH-2. The full exact address/value relation is not regular

Use ordinary binary notation read most significant digit first, synchronize
the two tracks by adding leading zeroes to the shorter track, and optionally
add common leading zeroes. The graph {(r,A(r)):r>=0} is not a regular language
of these paired binary digits.

For m,k>=2, equation (1) gives

    r=(2^m-1)2^k,       A(r)=r-2^m+2.                  (5)

Indeed the first m ones contribute zero, the first subsequent zero contributes
2^m, and the remaining zeroes contribute (2^m-1)(2+4+...+2^(k-1)).
Intersect a hypothetical regular graph with the regular paired-digit language

    (11)^(m-1) (10) (00)^(k-2) (01) (00),   m,k>=2.  (6)

Here each parenthesized pair is one alphabet symbol. Its input is 1^m0^k,
and its output represents r-2^k+2. Thus it lies in the graph exactly when m=k.
Erase the symbols (10),(01), replace (11) by a and (00) by b. The resulting
language is exactly {a^j b^j:j>=1}, which is not regular: pumping an initial
a-block changes its length without changing the b-block. Regular languages
are closed under intersection and such letter-erasing maps, a contradiction.
The words (6) already have nonzero initial digits on both tracks, so optional
common padding cannot affect the argument. Omitting r=0 changes nothing.

The same conclusion holds for the full exact graph of (3), for every fixed
actual seed and prefix clock. To prove this, fixed-coefficient binary arithmetic

    y=Kr+qz+d epsilon(r)+c                              (7)

is a regular synchronized three-track relation. A direct finite automaton
reads from the least significant end, retaining the initial parity bit and
the addition carry. The coefficients are fixed, so the carry is bounded;
reversing all words gives an MSB-first automaton. If the graph of N were
regular, intersect it with (7) and project away y. Since q>0, the projected
relation forces z=A(r). If y requires more digits than r,z, projection leaves
extra common leading zeroes. Removing or adding these is a regular operation
(left quotient by (00)*, followed if desired by prefixing (00)*), and recovers
the chosen padded graph convention for A. This contradicts the preceding proof.

This obstruction is deliberately narrow. It does **not** exclude a regular
set of attained targets, a regular selected subgraph, a recursive seed
selector, a nonlinear encoding, or a finite controller with unbounded integer
registers. It also does not assert that the union after forgetting addresses,
seeds, and clocks is nonregular. A recognizer retaining the full seed/clock
index and every witness would inherit the obstruction by fixing those indices.

## ARITH-3. A graph-level grading for enlarging seeds

This mechanism was proposed by the parent lane and checked here. Let Y and B
be nonempty tournaments, retain w in B, and use the ordinal sum Y->B. Every
D/O newborn loses to all of Y. Hence, as literal marked graphs,

    Reader(Y->B,w,word) = Y->Reader(B,w,word).           (8)

Ordered path-cover polynomials multiply on ordinal sums: each path crosses
from Y to B at most once, and each ordered bin splits uniquely at that cut.
Consequently prepending Y scales the two field grades by

    (h,e,t,q)       -> H(Y)   (h,e,t,q),
    (F,p,P2,S3)     -> P_Y(2) (F,p,P2,S3).              (9)

For t the bin beginning at w cannot contain Y, leaving Y in one other bin;
for S3 it leaves two other bins, explaining the different factors. These
grades are preserved by the inherited D/O updates, so (9) holds after every
word, not merely for the initial seed.

For Y=C3, H(Y)=3 and P_Y(2)=12. Reducing the outer prefix clock from s to s-2
when s>=3 therefore sends N to exactly 3N and raises the full vertex count by
one. This operation has a finite clock budget and does not provide +2 or +4.
For the strong four-vertex tournament with H=5, P_Y(2)=32; this primitive pair
is a plausible alternative seed enlargement, with no coverage claim.

Strongness also has a useful boundary. For any nonempty marked seed B, a
nonempty word ending 0, and a nonempty outer transitive prefix, the completed
zero-ready wrapper is strong. Its last newborn x receives an arc from every
other B-block vertex except w and has x->w, so every block vertex reaches w.
In the wrapper, w->root and root beats every vertex except w. The prefix
supplies root->prefix->w, proving strong connectivity. The block itself need
not be strong. Empty words require the separate condition that every block
vertex reach w; a weak seed can fail it. The canonical prefix may cease to be
transitive after an ordinal seed enlargement, so this is an enlargement of
the marked-block grammar, not preservation of that narrower canonical scope.
