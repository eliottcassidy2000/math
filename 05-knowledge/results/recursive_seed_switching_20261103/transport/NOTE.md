## What acceptance would buy constructively

Suppose an inverse supplies an actual strong target reader wrapper of order
n for a requested N, with at least as many vertices as the current named
source, and enough slots for the required distinct port roles. Assign all
old physical names injectively to those slots and birth any additional
names with their final incidences. Comparing the two resulting tournaments
leaves at most n(n-1)/2 old-arc discrepancies. The inherited one-helper
theorem repairs each with two native complements and deletes only the
helper. Thus acceptance with this capacity condition supplies a named
native program of at most n(n-1) complements and peak order n+1.

For a nonempty zero-ending reader, the inherited bound is
`n <= floor(log2 N)+3`. Hence such accepted targets admit `O(log^2 N)`
native complements and `O(log N)` vertices. This is a bound on program
length and graph size, not a running-time bound for finding the target or
finding all guarded Hamilton words. The empty-reader case is separate.
The full named relation, rather than a quotient field key, carries every
joint attachment and permits its phase to be recomputed.

The proof obligation is therefore target acceptance plus capacity and
declared port roles. The local seed promotion discharges it at one failed
bank target; the next two targets test switching back into the old bank.
It does not prove that this menu, a bounded birth budget, or any specified
seed-switching policy terminates at every future target.
