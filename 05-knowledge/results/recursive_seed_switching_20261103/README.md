## The inverse is now recursive and avoids the linear integer scan

Let A(r) be the one-apex reader count minus one, with A(0)=0. Its exact
binary sections are

    A(2u)=A(u)+u+(u mod 2),   A(2u+1)=A(u).

For each actual seed and fixed clock, target inversion becomes
`Kr+qA(r)+d(r mod 2)=D`, with K,q>0 and 0<=d<=q. Splitting by parity gives

    even: (K,q,d,D) -> (2K+q,q,q,D),
    odd:  (K,q,d,D) -> (2K,q,0,D-K-d).

Negative residual and gcd tests prune safely. Once a section satisfies
`K > q(floor(D/K)-1)+d`, it is strictly increasing on its entire possible
range and exact bisection applies. The complete algorithm uses
`O(1+sqrt(qD)/K)` section nodes per clock, with additional logarithmic
evaluation costs. Global bisection would be wrong: this reader has downward
jumps at arbitrarily large binary boundaries.

For the bank miss, the new decoder checks 56,499 section states and 305,060
scalar evaluations. The previous independent interval inverse also finds
zero hits after checking 1,473,275 candidate integers. Both have complete
word/clock bounds. The remaining difficulty is acceptance, not deciding
membership in a declared seed family.

The [arithmetic proof](arithmetic/NOTE.md) sharpens the distinction between
a finite number of registers and a finite number of states. The reader's
integral 2-kernel has rank exactly four, but its full synchronized binary
address/exact-value graph is not regular. This does **not** rule out a
recursive selector, a regular selected subgraph, or a finite controller
with unbounded integer registers. Modulo 49 or 343, the scalar registers
become finite; that does not recover full named-port phase or attachments.

## The Hilbert connection supplies a physical recursive interface

Recovering the earlier Hilbert source atlas, marked Gray carries, and
mirror-boundary work suggests keeping one permanent named zero seal.
Free reader vertices are inserted immediately before that seal. This
retains the old induced graph and its boundary names at each birth.

Label the free words by reflected Gray codes on the finite Hilbert grid.
Deleting their last two vertices gives the named parent cell; consecutive
scan cells differ in exactly one mark–tail arc and therefore admit a
two-native-move realization with one temporary helper. The orientation/
carry controller has eight reachable states, while the exact scalar
calculation retains four integer registers plus seed and name data.

There is also an eight-native-move reset to any other vertex of the current
unsealed graph: change the two old/new mark incidences at the seal and
outer root. The old reader is **absorbed into the new seed**. This is not
permission to reinterpret the same small seed and word at another mark.
It supplies physical reentry for a proposed seed, while leaving its target
equation unpaid. Neighboring Hilbert cells can have arbitrarily large
Hamilton-count jumps, so no discrete intermediate-value argument follows.
See the [exact atlas, reset and hostile controls](hilbert/NOTE.md).

## What is now missing

The next proof target is a recursive acceptance rule with a well-founded
measure: when a bank fails, specify a seed promotion/absorption, mark and
clock that accepts both required targets and permits the next return.
The present two-birth menu and eight-move reset are mechanisms for such a
rule, not a proof that every failure is repaired. A larger fixed bank also
has not been ruled out; the counterexample concerns precisely 219 keys.

Once an actual accepted target has enough vertices for the old names and
required port roles, the inherited arc compiler realizes it using at most
n(n-1) native complements and peak order n+1. Nonempty reader witnesses
have n<=floor(log2 N)+3. The new direct adjacent-pair guard implementation
removes an unnecessary exponential search inside that compiler; it does
not solve target selection. Full prime/7p coverage additionally needs a
terminating target reduction and entry argument beyond this one source tree.
