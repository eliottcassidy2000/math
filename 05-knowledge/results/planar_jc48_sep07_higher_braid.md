## 5. Two meridians in an arbitrary group

No permutation type or degree enters this calculation. Write the
original four marked positive meridians as \((a,b,c,d)\). A word of
the form \(P\sigma_iP^{-1}\) fixes precisely when the affected
entries after \(H_P\) agree. We use this equivalence in both directions;
the source separately verifies the free-group prefixes and the converse
substitutions.

For case 79 the upper prefix \((3,2,3,2)\) exposes
\[
 a,\quad bcdcd^{-1}c^{-1}b^{-1},
\]
and the lower prefix \((-3,-3)\) exposes \(b,d^{-1}cd\). Thus the
two fixed-word conditions are exactly
\[
 b=d^{-1}cd,\qquad a=bcdcd^{-1}c^{-1}b^{-1}.
\tag{20}
\]
All four entries lie in the subgroup generated by the original positive
meridians \(c,d\).

For case 77 the upper prefix \((2,-1,-2,1)\) exposes
\(bcb^{-1},c^{-1}b^{-1}abc\), while the lower prefix
\((-1,-2,-3,-1,-1)\) exposes \(c^{-1}b^{-1}cbc,d\).
Their equalities are equivalent to
\[
 a=bcbcb^{-1}c^{-1}b^{-1},\qquad d=c^{-1}b^{-1}cbc.
\tag{21}
\]
The original positive meridians \(b,c\) generate all entries.

For case 97 both words in (17) have outer conjugator \(R=(2,3)\).
Make the free meridian basis change
\[
 (A,B,C,D)=H_R(a,b,c,d)=(a,bcb^{-1},bdb^{-1},b).
\tag{22}
\]
This is a geometric braid change of basis in the punctured fibre. It
is **not asserted to be a braid coming from any global u-loop**. It
preserves the generated subgroup, and all four entries are still
positive meridians. Removing the common conjugator leaves the words
\[
 (2,2,-1,2,1,-2,-2),\qquad
 (-1,-2,-1,-3,-1,2,1,3,1,2,1).
\tag{23}
\]
For the first, put \(u=BCBC^{-1}B^{-1}\). Its prefix
\((2,2,-1)\) exposes \(u^{-1}Au,BCB^{-1}\). Equality is equivalent
to \(A=BCBCB^{-1}C^{-1}B^{-1}\). The second prefix
\((-1,-2,-1,-3,-1)\) exposes \(C^{-1}B^{-1}CBC,D\). Thus
\[
 A=BCBCB^{-1}C^{-1}B^{-1},\qquad D=C^{-1}B^{-1}CBC.
\tag{24}
\]
These are exactly (21) in the changed meridian basis. The two positive
meridians \(B,C\) generate the entire original image subgroup.

## 6. All-degree whole-support exclusion and controls

We recover the elementary all-degree bound of the
[preceding two-meridian result](planar_jc48_sep07_next_braid.md).
If a transitive permutation group on \(d>1\) labels is generated by
\(r\) permutations each moving at most \(\delta\ge1\) labels, then
\[
 r(\delta-1)\ge d-1.
\tag{25}
\]
Replace each nontrivial cycle of each generator by a tree on that cycle's
support. The union graph has precisely the permutation-group orbits as
its connected components. A nonidentity generator contributes at most
its moved-label count minus one edges, and an identity contributes zero.
Transitivity requires at least \(d-1\) edges, proving (25). If
\(\delta=0\), transitivity on more than one label is already impossible.

For an **irreducible whole nonproperness curve** of a hypothetical Keller
map, let \(d\) be the generic degree and \(a\) the actual fibre count
at a generic smooth point of the curve. These \(a\) retained unramified
sheets are fixed by every positive curve meridian. Consequently the
meridian permutations move at most \(\delta=d-a\) labels; deleted
fixed sheets only improve this estimate. Irreducibility ensures the
same count at the two generating positive meridians. The source cover
over the complement is connected, so its degree-d monodromy is
transitive. For \(r=2\) and \(a\ge d/2\), (25) is impossible:
\[
 2(\delta-1)\le d-2<d-1.
\tag{26}
\]

The inherited actual-sheet Euler ledger for a whole irreducible
affine-line-normalized curve with one unibranch cusp and \(N\) ordinary
nodes is
\[
 n+\sum_{p\text{ node}}\omega_p=1,\qquad
 \omega_p\ge\max(0,d-2a).
\tag{27}
\]
Here \(n\) is the actual cusp fibre count. If \(A_p,B_p\) are the
two retained smooth-branch sheet sets at a node, then
\(\omega_p=|(\Omega\setminus A_p)\cap(\Omega\setminus B_p)|\)
is the overlap of their **deleted complements**. The actual node fibre
count is \(|A_p\cap B_p|=2a-d+\omega_p\); (27) follows by the same
Euler integration and actual local page intersection used in the
[ordinary-cusp passport](planar_jc48_sep06_cusp_passport.md) and
[odd-cusp extension](planar_jc48_sep06_odd_cusp.md).
Its derivation does not depend on the cusp's odd exponent. Since
\(N\ge2\) here, \(d>2a\) would make the right-hand sum at least
two, a contradiction. Thus (26) excludes all three whole-support
realizations. No degree-eight or degree-ten census is used, and the
curve certificate alone does not assert a general Jacobian theorem.

Two controls locate the combinatorial boundary. Two disjoint cycles
can have no common fixed label and still generate an intransitive
group; common fixed labels are not the criterion. Conversely, two
\(\delta\)-cycles sharing one label generate a transitive group on
\(2\delta-1\) labels, so (25) is sharp as an abstract support bound.
The coprojected cusp/node in (14) is the geometric hostile to imposing
distinct singular projection values. The common conjugator in (22)
is the type control against claiming an unobserved actual base loop.
