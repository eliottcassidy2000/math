# Tiling-fibre mass is a stationary measure of the regular return walk

**Status: PROVED, in the fixed-order scopes below.** This is a separate corollary of the audited binary-incidence theorem. It changes none of the frozen endpoint or Gram statements. The reversibility and group-orbit lumpability mechanisms are standard; the structural identification is that their exact quotient masses are the existing fixed-path tiling-fibre counts.

## 1. The actual walk and its reversible measure

Fix a positive order n and a nonempty labelled score fibre F_d. Use the binary incidence matrix B of `gram_return_addendum.md`: its columns are precisely the realized intermediate tournaments, and `B[T,S]=1` when `S=K_P(T)` for a Hamiltonian path P of T. Define

    H_T = sum_S B[T,S] = H(T),
    c_S = sum_T B[T,S],
    D_H = diag(H_T), D_c = diag(c_S).

Every H_T is positive, since a tournament has a directed Hamiltonian path. Every c_S is positive by the definition of the realized column set. Thus

    P = D_H^(-1) B D_c^(-1) B^T                         (1)

is defined. This is the following literal bipartite walk:

1. From T, choose an incident intermediate tournament S uniformly.
2. From S, choose a neighbouring source-fibre tournament U uniformly.

The first choice is equivalently a uniform Hamiltonian path of T, because its intermediate witness is unique. The second is equivalently a uniform directed Hamiltonian path of S with the required endpoint pair, because those and only those paths return to F_d, again with unique targets.

**Theorem.** P is stochastic and reversible with respect to the positive measure `mu(T)=H(T)`.

**Proof.** Its row sums are

    sum_U P[T,U]
      = (1/H_T) sum_S B[T,S] (1/c_S) sum_U B[U,S]
      = (1/H_T) sum_S B[T,S] = 1.

Moreover

    H_T P[T,U] = sum_S B[T,S] B[U,S]/c_S
               = H_U P[U,T].                           (2)

This is detailed balance. Summing over T proves that `H_T/sum_V H_V` is a stationary probability distribution. No irreducibility or uniqueness assumption was used. QED.

The return-presentation matrix W is `BB^T`, not P. The different walk `D_M^(-1)W`, where `M(T)=sum_U W[T,U]`, has reversible measure M. Formula (1) is the uniform-first-path, then uniform-legal-continuation walk; it is generally neither the uniform-path-pair walk nor the square of the unrestricted tournament-metagraph walk.

## 2. Strong lumpability under relabelling

Now let n be positive and odd, and let F_d be the regular fibre, `d=((n-1)/2,...,(n-1)/2)`. The full symmetric group S_n acts on its labelled tournaments and on its realized intermediate tournaments.

For a permutation g, applying g to every vertex of a path gives

    g K_P(T) = K_(gP)(gT).

Consequently `B[gT,gS]=B[T,S]`, `H(gT)=H(T)`, and `c(gS)=c(S)`. Equation (1) therefore gives

    P[gT,gU]=P[T,U].                                   (3)

Let C and D be ordinary isomorphism classes of regular tournaments at this fixed order. Define

    Pbar[C,D] = sum_(U in D) P[T,U],  for any T in C.   (4)

If another representative is `gT`, then (3) and the invariance of D show that the sum is unchanged. Thus (4) is well-defined: the partition into ordinary isomorphism classes is **strongly lumpable**, for every initial distribution on labelled states.

Summing (2) over the two classes proves detailed balance for the quotient with class measure

    m(C) = sum_(T in C) H(T).

Orbit-stabilizer gives `|C|=n!/|Aut(T)|`, so

    m(C) = n! H(T)/|Aut(T)| = n! h(T),                 (5)
    h(T) = H(T)/|Aut(T)|.

The common factor n! cancels on normalization. Therefore

    pibar(C) = h(T) / sum_(ordinary regular classes D) h(D).  (6)

## 3. Converse merging

Let `T^op` reverse all arcs and let `rev(P)` reverse the Hamiltonian vertex word. Directly from the nonpath-complement definition,

    K_(rev(P))(T^op) = (K_P(T))^op.                     (7)

Converse preserves regularity, commutes with relabelling, and permutes the realized intermediate columns. Hence H and c are converse-invariant and

    P[T^op,U^op] = P[T,U].                             (8)

The orbits of the action generated by relabelling and converse are exactly the converse-merged ordinary classes. The argument of Section 2 proves strong lumpability to these merged classes as well.

Let `kappa(T)=1` when T is isomorphic to its converse and `kappa(T)=2` otherwise. The merged class consists of kappa ordinary classes. Converse preserves both H and automorphism-group order, so its measure is

    m_merged([T]) = kappa(T) n! H(T)/|Aut(T)|.

Thus the normalized stationary merged-class distribution is

    pibar_merged([T])
      = kappa(T) h(T) / sum_(merged regular classes [U]) kappa(U) h(U).  (9)

There is no factor two for a self-converse class.

## 4. Why this is exactly the existing tiling-fibre weight

The automorphism group acts freely on the ordered Hamiltonian paths of T: fixing an entire ordered vertex word fixes every vertex. Normalizing each such word to the fixed observer path produces one fixed-path tournament presentation. Two words yield the same presentation exactly when they differ by an automorphism of T. Hence the number of fixed-path tilings belonging to an ordinary class is exactly

    h(T)=H(T)/|Aut(T)|.

A converse-merged class has one ordinary class when self-converse and two otherwise, so its fibre size is exactly `kappa(T)h(T)`. These are the established fibre formulas in `THM-781-hamiltonian-path-inverse-metagraph-fibre.md`, Section 2, and `THM-796-three-sorted-recursive-tiling-line-node-incidence.md`.

Equations (6) and (9) identify these existing combinatorial fibre sizes with the stationary masses of an actual, explicitly defined reversible regular-return walk. They do not merely compare unrelated scalar counts.

The correspondence retains the full incidence B to determine transitions. Fibre size alone does not determine a transition row. Nor does strong lumpability here contradict the non-lumpability of deterministic all-tile complement under the original tiling-to-node map: the present chain averages over incident paths and then over the admissible regular returns on a relabelling-invariant labelled state space. It is a different, explicitly randomized construction.

## 6. Boundaries

- The regular quotient assertions concern each fixed positive odd order separately. An empty even-order regular universe is not assigned a Markov chain.
- The realized intermediate set is used; no claim is made that every tournament in a score shell occurs.
- The labelled parity components remain. A stationary measure need not be unique; neither labelled nor quotient irreducibility is asserted in general.
- This does not prove deterministic complement descends to nodes, that black moves connect them, or that scalar fibre weights determine legal transitions.
- It implies no ordinary H-spectrum completeness theorem or all-order component classification.
