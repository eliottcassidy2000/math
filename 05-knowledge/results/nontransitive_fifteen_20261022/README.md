## 1. Inheritance and the exact source type

and $h=H(T)/|A|$ its number of automorphism orbits of directed Hamilton words.
A word is blue when its positional reversal is an anti-automorphism of the
entire source. Write $b$ for the number of blue orbits and

\[
\Delta=h-b.
\]

Thus $\Delta$ counts black word orbits. For self-converse sources the
converse/reversal involution pairs the black orbits, so $\Delta$ is even.

## 2. The two-family theorem

**Prime-shore theorem.** Suppose a strong self-converse tournament has a
marked Hamilton cycle with an odd prime number $q\ge5$ of shores, each of
size $m\ge3$. If a shore is a full $A$-vertex orbit, then

\[
\boxed{\Delta\ge2(q-1).} \tag{2}
\]

The hypothesis concerns the full source automorphism group, not merely the
cyclic subgroup generated by $R^q$.

**The label action.** Every full $A$-vertex orbit is a union of shores.
Label the cyclically ordered shores by their containing full orbits, obtaining
a length-$q$ necklace $w$. A singleton shore-orbit supplies a label appearing
exactly once, so $w$ is nonconstant. Since $q$ is prime, its $q$ rotations
are distinct.

Every anti-automorphism normalizes $A$. All anti-automorphisms differ by an
element of $A$, and hence induce the **same** permutation $\tau$ on the
set of full vertex orbits. A blue word must therefore have label string equal
to $\tau$ applied to its reversed string. If two rotations $s,t$ of the
necklace satisfy this condition, composing their two reflection equations gives

\[
\operatorname{rot}_{2(s-t)}w=w.
\]

Because $q$ is odd prime and $w$ is nonconstant, $s=t\pmod q$.
Thus at most one rotation in either family below can be blue. This argument
does not presume that $\tau$ fixes individual labels.

**Family C: cycle cuts.** Take the $q$ cuts beginning at the first $q$
successive vertices of the marked Hamilton cycle. Their label strings are

\[
(\operatorname{rot}_s w)^m,\qquad 0\le s<q.
\]

They are actual Hamilton words, occupy $q$ distinct $A$-orbits, and at
least $q-1$ of these orbits are black.

**Family B: whole-shore blocks.** Choose a directed Hamilton cycle inside
each shore. Such a cycle exists because the induced tournament is strong.
Start at any vertex of shore $s$, cut that shore's cycle to get a Hamilton
path starting there, and let $v$ be its last vertex. The marked edge
$v\to R(v)$ leads to the next shore. Cut its internal cycle at $R(v)$,
and continue until all $q$ shores have been visited once. There is no imposed
last-to-first edge, so this constructs a Hamilton **path** without a closure
assumption. Its label string is

\[
\operatorname{expand}_m(\operatorname{rot}_s w),
\]

where expansion repeats each letter $m$ times. Expansion commutes with
reversal and block-boundary rotation. The same reflection argument gives
$q$ distinct orbits with at least $q-1$ black.

**Separation.** A singleton-shore full orbit occurs in one consecutive run
of $m\ge3$ vertices in every block word. In every cycle-cut word its
vertices are separated by $q-1\ge4$ other vertices. An automorphism fixes
each full orbit setwise, so these two families cannot share an $A$-orbit.
Adding their black orbits proves (2). No converse mates need to be counted
again. ∎

The actual singleton-label argument also permits composite $q$, with
the bound $2(q-\gcd(2,q))$; that extension is independently used in the
concurrent structural packet. The prime formulation above is sufficient here.
Without primeness or a singleton label, a shorter-period necklace can collapse
the family: $(0,1,2)^3$ has only three distinct rotations, not nine.

## 3. Why this closes the $q=5,m=3$ branch

The automorphism group of a tournament has odd order: an involution would
swap the endpoints of some edge and reverse that edge. Thus every full
vertex orbit has odd size. Here each full orbit is a union of three-vertex
shores, so its number of shores is odd.

If the source is not vertex-transitive, the only partitions of five shores
into odd full-orbit multiplicities are

\[
3+1+1,\qquad1+1+1+1+1.
\]

Both contain a singleton shore. Theorem (2) gives $\Delta\ge8$, excluding
six. Vertex-transitive order-15 sources were already excluded by the separate
complete cyclic-Cayley receipt cited in §1. This closes the whole large branch
without inspecting its $2^{30}$ binary frames.

required; it is not asserted to be the full automorphism group. Isomorphic

For a word $P$, the key is

\[
K_T(P)_{ij}=\mathbf1[P_i\to P_j],\qquad i<j.
\]

Equality of two complete keys is equivalent to the unique word-position map
being a source automorphism. Blue means the positional reversal is anti on

orbits; when self-converse, parity increases this to at least eight. Every
