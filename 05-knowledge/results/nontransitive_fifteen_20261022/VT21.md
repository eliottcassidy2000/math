## 1. Why two groups suffice

Let $G=\operatorname{Aut}(T)$ for a vertex-transitive tournament on 21
vertices. Its order is odd, since a nontrivial involution would swap some edge
endpoints. We use the **CITED** Feit–Thompson theorem that every finite group
of odd order is solvable, with the same status and source as
[the inherited order-35 proof](../center_transitivity_20261021c/ORDER35.md).
The primary theorem is printed on page 775 of
[Feit–Thompson, Pacific Journal of Mathematics 13 (1963)](https://msp.org/pjm/1963/13-3/pjm-v13-n3-p01-p.pdf).
We do not re-prove that input. The new argument below concerns the regular
subgroup and the exact remaining conjugation guard.

Choose a minimal nontrivial normal subgroup $N\lhd G$. Solvability makes
$N$ elementary abelian of some prime characteristic $p$. Its orbits all
have the same size, a nontrivial prime power dividing 21; hence each has size
$p\in\{3,7\}$. Write $q=21/p$ for the number of blocks. On each
$p$-block an abelian transitive action has regular cyclic image $C_p$.
Choose coordinates on those blocks. The action of $N$ is described by
nonzero linear functionals

\[
\ell_i:N\longrightarrow\mathbb F_p,\qquad 1\le i\le q,
\]

whose common kernel is zero, by faithfulness of the permutation action.

Two block functionals are either proportional or linearly independent.
Proportionality is a $G$-invariant equivalence relation on the blocks:
conjugation changes the variable in $N$, and a change of regular cyclic
coordinate multiplies a functional by a nonzero scalar. Since the induced
action on the prime number $q$ of blocks is transitive, all equivalence
classes have equal size. Thus there is either one class or $q$ singleton
classes.

### Coupled functionals

If all functionals are proportional, their common kernel being zero implies
$\dim N=1$. Thus $N=C_p$, acting regularly on each block. Choose a
$q$-element $g\in G$ whose image permutes the blocks as a $q$-cycle;
one obtains it from a Sylow $q$-subgroup mapping onto the block action.

Conjugation by $g$ on $N$ has order dividing the $q$-part of
$|\operatorname{Aut}(C_p)|=p-1$. For the two possible ordered prime pairs,

\[
(p,q)=(7,3):\ (p-1)_q=3,
\qquad
(p,q)=(3,7):\ (p-1)_q=1.
\]

Therefore $g^q$ centralizes $N$ and fixes every block. The **blockwise
centralizer** of the regular $C_p$ action is $C_p^q$, a $p$-group.
But $g^q$ is a $q$-element. Consequently $g^q=1$.

The group $\langle N,g\rangle=C_p\rtimes C_q$ has order $pq=21$
and is transitive: $g$ moves between blocks and $N$ moves freely inside a
block. It is therefore regular.

This step retains the new noncentral possibility. At order 35 the two primes
do not divide the other's predecessor; at 21, the action of $C_3$ on
$C_7$ may be nontrivial. Replacing the blockwise **centralizer** by the
normalizer would not justify the order argument. Nor does the proof cover an
arbitrary prime pair for which $q^2\mid p-1$ without further work.

### Independent functionals

If each pair of functionals is independent, the map
$(\ell_i,\ell_j):N\to\mathbb F_p^2$ is onto. Thus $N$ is transitive
on the ordered vertex pairs between those two blocks. All edges between
them have one common direction. The source is a lexicographic product of a
vertex-transitive $q$-vertex quotient and a common vertex-transitive
$p$-vertex fibre tournament. The fibres are isomorphic because $G$ is
transitive on blocks.

Each prime-degree vertex-transitive tournament has a regular cyclic subgroup,
by the transitive Sylow argument. After fixing fibre isomorphisms, the **full
automorphism group of the tournament** contains their product action and
hence a regular $C_p\times C_q\cong C_{21}$. This subgroup need not be
contained in the originally selected transitive subgroup; retaining the full
source automorphism group is essential.

We have proved that every vertex-transitive order-21 tournament has a regular
subgroup of order 21. There are exactly two isomorphism types of groups of
that order. Indeed a Sylow7 subgroup is normal, and a Sylow3 complement acts
on it either trivially or by an order3 automorphism. The two nontrivial
actions are isomorphic after inverting the generator of $C_3$. The two
groups are

\[
C_{21},\qquad C_7\rtimes C_3.
\]
