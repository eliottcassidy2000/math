## 2. Exact kernel obstruction to gluing a quotient move

Let local full frame spaces be finite abelian groups `G_i`, with prescribed
local lift cosets `r_i+K_i`. These may be endpoint lift sets of an already
authenticated local move. Set `G=product_i G_i`, `K=product_i K_i`, `r=(r_i)`.
Let `D:G -> B` be the homomorphism recording **all actual shared-port
mismatches**, and let the required mismatch be `delta in B`. Then

    compatible full lifts exist iff delta-D(r) belongs to D(K).       (1)

Equivalently the obstruction is the class of `delta-D(r)` in `B/D(K)`.
It is independent of the chosen local representatives. When it vanishes,
choose `k0` with `D(k0)=delta-D(r)`; all compatible full lifts are exactly

    r+k0+ker(D restricted to K),                                    (2)

so their number is `|ker(D restricted to K)|`, not `product_i |K_i|`.

**Proof.** Write every candidate uniquely as `r+k`. The shared-port equation
becomes `D(k)=delta-D(r)`. This has a solution precisely under (1); subtract
one solution to obtain the kernel torsor (2). Changing `r` by an element of
`K` changes the defect by an element of `D(K)`. QED.

This is constructive by finite enumeration or by the inherited integer
congruence/SNF compiler. Integer quotient variables must be retained:
`Az=b mod d` means `Az-diag(d)t=b`, not a rational or representative-blind
identity. If an electrical guard is not constant on a local coset, split
its actual accepted states first or use the exact finite encoding below.
A modulus-eight output is not automatically a group character.

    sum_(s: owner_v(s)=a) z_(i,s) = y_(v,a)                         (3)

at **every** occurrence of v. For distinct-port selection, impose
`sum_(i,s: literal_port(s)=p) z_(i,s) <= 1`, with bounded integer slack.
Target-incidence margins are exact integer rows if required. These equations
are in bijection with the declared compatible injective selected packets:

## 6. No-recycling matching and a genuinely necessary three-slot move

After all shared owners are fixed to literal values, let a bipartite graph
have slots on one side and actual eligible literal ports on the other.
Eligibility includes every per-slot guard and pin. Injective assignments
are matchings saturating the slot side.

**Standard alternating-exchange theorem, applied here.** Any two such
assignments are connected by alternating-cycle exchanges and alternating-
path shifts ending at an unused port. The symmetric difference has degree
at most two and decomposes into disjoint even cycles and paths with port
endpoints. Flip a whole cycle simultaneously. On a path, propagate the
vacancy from the endpoint unused by the current matching, one slot at a
time. Every intermediate assignment remains eligible and injective. This
is constructive and retains every fixed actual flag and per-slot guard.
If all ports are occupied, only the alternating cycles remain. There is
no general budget-two theorem: a cycle may require more slots to change
simultaneously when no spare eligible port exists.
