## 2. Complete affine-multiplier genus classification

Let `(alpha,beta,gamma)!=0` and define

`I=p^2(p^3-y^2)(alpha+beta p+gamma y)`.

**Theorem A.** The generic invariant function field `K(p,y)/K(I)` is geometrically integral. Its smooth proper model has geometric genus

| Coefficient condition | Genus |
|---|---:|
| `beta=gamma=0`, `alpha!=0` | 2 |
| `gamma=0`, `beta!=0` | 3 |
| `gamma!=0` | 6 |

Thus the entire nonzero affine-multiplier family has no genus-zero or genus-one generic invariant. Coefficients may lie in any characteristic-zero field; no genericity condition on their specialization is assumed.

### All univariate multipliers, including the quadratic-cover cases

The following parent-proposed extension strengthens the gamma=0 part to every degree and includes repeated factors.

**Theorem A'.** For any nonzero polynomial R(p) of degree m, write `R=A_0(p)^2 B_0(p)` with B_0 squarefree and put `b=deg B_0`. Then the invariant `I=p^2D R(p)` has geometrically integral generic fibre of genus

`g=(m+b+4)/2>=2`.

This decomposition exists over K itself: take A_0 monic and leave the nonzero scalar factor of R in B_0. No square root of a coefficient is adjoined. The degree relation is `m=2 deg A_0+b`, so m and b have the same parity.

Let c=I be transcendental over K. On the generic fibre,

`y^2=p^3-c/(p^2 R(p))`.

The birational coordinate `v=pA_0(p)B_0(p)y` gives

`v^2=B_0(p)(p^5R(p)-c)`.                                (1)

The two factors on the right are coprime, since a root of B_0 is a root of R and the second factor takes the nonzero value -c there. The second factor is squarefree: a repeated root would also be a root of the nonzero polynomial `(p^5R)'` over K, forcing p and then c to be algebraic over K. Thus the right side is squarefree of odd degree `m+b+5`. It is not a square even over an algebraic closure of K(c), which proves geometric integrality. The double cover, including ramification at infinity, has genus `(m+b+4)/2`. This proves A'. A nonzero constant R gives m=b=0 and genus two; a nonconstant affine R is squarefree with m=b=1 and genus three, proving the first two rows of Theorem A.

### The cubic-cover case: all coefficients retained

For nonzero gamma divide I by gamma, absorb that scalar in the generic constant, and rename `alpha/gamma,beta/gamma` as alpha,beta. Write `A(p)=alpha+beta p`. The generic equation is the monic cubic in y

`F=y^3+A y^2-p^3y-Ap^3+c/p^2=0`.                        (2)

All subsequent calculations are over an algebraic closure of K(c), with c transcendental over K. At p=0, every root of (2) has valuation `-2/3`: a nonnegative valuation leaves the unique pole `c/p^2`, while for negative valuation the lowest terms must be `y^3` and `c/p^2`. Thus (2) has no rational root in the p-function field. Being cubic, it is irreducible over that field even after extending constants. This proves geometric integrality and the degree-three covering.

The exact discriminant numerator is

`N=p^4 disc_y(F)`

`=4p^7(p^3-A^2)^2+4Ac p^2(9p^3-A^2)-27c^2`.             (3)

It has degree thirteen in p with leading coefficient 4 for **every** alpha,beta, and `N(0)=-27c^2!=0`. All thirteen roots are simple. Here is a uniform proof of that assertion, not a finite parameter test.

Set `J=p^2(p^3-y^2)(y+A)` and

`f_1=J_y/p^2`, `f_2=J_p/p`.

Direct polynomial elimination gives

`Res_y(f_1,f_2)`

`=p^3[169p^3-120beta^2p^2-148alpha beta p-40alpha^2]`

`    *[p^3-(alpha+beta p)^2]`.                           (4)

Its leading coefficient is 169, independent of alpha,beta. Consequently, a simultaneous critical point with p nonzero has p algebraic over K, and f_1 then makes y algebraic over K. Its value J(p,y) cannot equal the transcendental c.

A triple root of (2) is also impossible at generic c: the equations `F_y=F_yy=0` force `y=-A/3` and `A^2+3p^3=0`, again giving p,y algebraic over K. At a double but not triple root, a repeated root of the discriminant in p would force `F_p=0`. To see the precise local identity, translate that double root to y=0 and write the cubic at that parameter as `y^2(y+q)`, q nonzero. Its first-order perturbation by `epsilon V(y)` changes the discriminant at first order by `-4q^3 V(0) epsilon`. Thus vanishing of the discriminant derivative means `F_p=0`. On F=0, `F_p=-J_p/p^2` and `F_y=-J_y/p^2`, contradicting (4). This proves the claimed simplicity.

Therefore there are thirteen simple ramification points over distinct nonzero finite p-values. Each has ramification index two. There are no other finite nonzero ramification points because (2) is monic and has nonzero discriminant elsewhere.

### Both boundary contributions

At zero, make the cubic base change `p=r^3`, `y=r^-2 Y`. Multiplication by r^6 gives an equation with initial polynomial `Y^3+c`. Its three simple roots are permuted transitively by the cubic deck transformation, since the original y has valuation `-2/3`. Hence the original cover has one point over p=0, of ramification index three. It contributes two to the ramification divisor.

At infinity set `p=r^-2`, `y=r^-3 Y`. Multiplying (2) by r^9 gives exactly

`(Y^2-1)(Y+beta r+alpha r^3)+c r^13=0`.                 (5)

At r=0 the three simple roots are `Y=1,-1,0`. Under the quadratic deck involution, `(r,Y)` changes to `(-r,-Y)`. The two nonzero branches are exchanged and descend to one index-two point. The zero branch is fixed and unramified. Equivalently its native expansion is `y=-A+O(p^-5)`, an ordinary series in `p^-1`. The contribution at infinity is one.

The total ramification is therefore `13+2+1=16`. Riemann--Hurwitz for this degree-three map gives `2g-2=-6+16=10`, so g=6. This completes Theorem A.

## 3. Same-invariant outer compositions have no rational nonzero time

**Theorem B.** For I in Theorem A or A', every nonconstant polynomial f over K and every scalar `lambda!=0`, the completed Hamiltonian flow generated by `H=f(I)` cannot send both p and y to rational functions in K(p,y). Consequently it cannot be a polynomial specialization on the actual source at that time.

The carrier entry is exact: `f(I)-f(0)=I g(I)` is divisible by `p^2D`. Thus all convergence, actual depth and affine-source preservation hypotheses of the inherited completed-carrier theorem hold. The argument uses the invariant I, which the flow fixes, rather than presuming geometric integrality over K(f(I)).

For the nonzero displacement, write `f(z)=f(0)+f_k z^k+...`, with `k>=1` and `f_k!=0`. In the logarithmic chart,

`I=tau W(s)+O(tau^2)`,

`W(s)=s^8(alpha+beta s^2+gamma s^3)!=0`.

For the univariate case of A', the same expression is `W(s)=s^8R(s^2)!=0`. The following proof applies to either W without change.

The derivation of f(I) raises tau valuation by at least k. Since `p=s^2+tau`, its first response is

`delta(p)=2k f_k s W(s)^k tau^k+O(tau^(k+1))`.

All higher iterates of p start at order at least 2k. Hence

`exp(lambda delta)(p)-p`

`=2lambda k f_k s W(s)^k tau^k+O(tau^(k+1))`.            (6)

The same nonzero coefficient with lambda replaced by n lambda occurs after every positive iterate n. No numerical specialization of s is made.

If both images were rational, the injective flow map would restrict to a field embedding over K(I), hence to a nonconstant selfmap of the smooth proper generic curve. In characteristic zero and genus greater than one, Riemann--Hurwitz forces degree one. The automorphism group is finite, contradicting (6). This is the inherited high-genus mechanism, now supplied with the complete new genus classification.

The literal source comparison retains the topology sidecar. On the fixed inputs p,y the logarithmic images lie in `K[s][[tau]]`. The map `s->xt,tau->t` from `K[[s,tau]]` to `K[x][[t]]` is injective because its monomials have distinct exponent pairs. It intertwines the polynomial derivations and their convergent series. A proposed rational literal source image can be cleared of its denominator and pulled back by `x=s/tau,t=tau`; injectivity then gives a rational logarithmic image, already excluded. This uses the same fixed-input comparison as the incoming nonrational theorem. It neither identifies the full D-adic completion with the Laurent field nor asserts an arbitrary completed element has finite pole depth.

Constant f or lambda=0 gives the identity and is excluded from the theorem. The two source maps of distinct flows may have different invariant fields; the theorem makes no claim about their composition.

## 4. Complete monomial-invariant composition escape

The cheap new escape candidate is `H=(pD)^2`. Its primitive invariant `pD` is outside the incoming `p^aD`, `a>=2`, carrier family, while H is inside the universal carrier. The invariant nevertheless has genus two. The following classification handles every positive monomial invariant at once.

Let a,b be coprime positive integers and set `J=p^aD^b`. Let f be nonconstant and let k be the vanishing order of `f(z)-f(0)` at z=0.

**Theorem C.** The generator `f(J)` belongs to `K+p^2DA` if and only if `ak>=2`. The generic invariant field `K(p,y)/K(J)` is geometrically integral of genus

`g=[a+3b+(a mod 2)+(b mod 2)-2]/2 >=2`.                 (7)

Whenever the carrier entry holds, every nonzero scalar time of its completed Hamiltonian flow fails to send both p,y to rational functions. This includes a=1 provided k>=2. Arbitrary positive exponents not coprime reduce to this statement by taking their gcd into the outer polynomial.

**Entry.** Write `f(J)-f(0)=J^k g(J)`, with `g(0)!=0`. Since p and D are coprime irreducibles in K[p,y], their exact valuations are ak and bk. The universal-carrier criterion is therefore `ak>=2,bk>=1`; the second is automatic here. If either original monomial exponent is zero, a nonconstant outer polynomial cannot supply the missing irreducible factor, so it does not enter this carrier.

**Primitive constant-field parametrization.** Put c=J, transcendental over K, and choose integers u,v with `au+bv=1`. The torus curve `p^aD^b=c` has the birational parameter

`p=c^u z^b`, `D=c^v z^-a`, `z=p^v D^-u`.                (8)

These formulas hold over K(c) itself; no unexplained constant root is adjoined. With `w=z^ceil(a/2)y`, the complete curve becomes

`w^2=c^(3u) z^(a+3b)-c^v`,                             if a is even;

`w^2=z[c^(3u) z^(a+3b)-c^v]`,                          if a is odd.       (9)

Every nonzero root is simple, and in the odd case zero is also simple. Thus the model is geometrically integral. Its finite branch roots number `a+3b+(a mod 2)`; infinity branches precisely when b is odd. Formula (7) follows from the double-cover Riemann--Hurwitz formula. With a,b coprime and positive, the smallest genera are two, attained at `(a,b)=(1,1),(2,1)`.

**Iteration.** In logarithmic coordinates, `J=tau^b p^(a+2b)` and

`{p,J}=2b s tau^b p^(a+2b)`.

For the outer leading term above, delta raises tau valuation by at least bk and

`delta(p)=2bk f_k s^(2(a+2b)k+1) tau^(bk)+O(tau^(bk+1))`.

Every positive time iterate has a nonzero leading displacement. Retaining K(J) and applying the same finite-automorphism argument proves the rationality claim. The actual carrier entry and fixed-input source comparison are as in Section 3.
