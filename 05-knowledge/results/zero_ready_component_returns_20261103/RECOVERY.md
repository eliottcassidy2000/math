# Inheritance, concept board, and next constructive tests

The assigned anchor is the prime/7p realization problem, with 7 and 21
excluded. The niche is an exact inverse for changing the zero-ready strong
component. The wildcard is the link between grammar memory, named path
covers, and the older square-sum/ordered-program interfaces.

The closest proved mechanism is the
[polarized path-bin calculus, C1](../polarized_path_calculus_20261103/CALCULUS.md),
combined with the
[one-helper native arc compiler, section 5](../adaptive_metagraph_reformulation_20261103/METAGRAPH.md).
The canonical hostile is the actual 81/27 source pair: equal uniform
observers do not determine a named cut. The corrected near miss is treating
the N^3 unit dial as repeatable without checking the old-root start count.
The least-used relevant sidecar is the joint endpoint cover J(v,w), now an
exact entry guard rather than an optional annotation.

The reader was compared against literal small graphs, an independent binary
recurrence, the exact inverse, and actual large endpoint gap counts. Its
positive signal was pursued through three complete rounds rather than a
large unstructured tournament census. The old grammar's negative signals
were pursued until they yielded all-length inverses or exact attachment
guards, with their restricted scopes preserved.

The next useful experiments are structural:

1. Classify the failed integer intervals by seed fields and low binary
   digits; seek a recursive rule that changes the seed when one branch has
   no solution. A successful rule must decrease a target measure and retain
   an actual marked seed, not just solve its field congruences.
2. Add J(v,w) to the unit-dial entry condition and search for a source rewrite
   preserving the required target equation while restoring J=1. The actual
   source's minimum 53 and the order-four J<q hostile are mandatory tests.
3. Use the full polynomial interface when mixing retained/new-root births
   with the opposite-singleton/sink reader. The separate finite carriers
   do not yet give a finite closure for the mixed language.
4. Test a proposed acceptance grammar at 1537279 and at the incoming
   all-clock prototype's first failed reentry before extending finite depth.
   Neither a larger numerical prefix nor another isolated balanced output
   resolves that source-reentry issue.

The older
[adaptive two-port/square-sum packet](../adaptive_state_induction_20261103/README.md)
uses all 25 primes at most 100 (ending at 97), with ascending square-sum
boundary 8. Its map to the present problem is the need to retain boundary
names across an operation: a partition into five groups or an aggregate
edge count forgets which old elements meet the next insertion. The
preserved predicate is extendibility with the declared boundary; the
destroyed information is the named intersection/attachment pattern.
The needed sidecar here is the joint path-cover interface. No equivalence
between a square-sum graph and these tournaments is asserted; orientations,
ties, and Hamilton-count predicates are different. No new five-group
classification was computed in this session.

Similarly, the ordered-program bridge in
[coverage machines](../coverage_machine_induction_20261103/README.md)
maps a grammar word and retained control ports to an actual sequence of
graph operations. The order and guards matter as in a priority program;
a scalar count or field phase loses execution feasibility. The present
inverse supplies a terminating decoder for one grammar, not a universal
machine-to-spectrum surjection. These are useful interface analogies with
explicit losses, not substitutes for the target-acceptance proof.
