AN EFFECTIVE CUBIC ORDER BOUND FROM BLACK HAMILTON-PATH DEFECT
2026-10-20

THEOREM
Let T be a strong tournament of order n>=3 and A=Aut(T). Let H be its number
of directed Hamilton words, h=H/|A| its number of path orbits, and b the number
of blue path orbits. A word P is blue if its positional reversal rho_P is an
anti-automorphism. In particular b=0 when T is not self-converse. Put
Delta=h-b. For an integer d>=0 satisfying Delta<=d, set s=d+2 and let r be
the smallest power of two at least 4s(d+1). Then

    n <= 2sr < 16(d+2)^2(d+1).

In particular every such tournament satisfies

    Delta > (n/16)^(1/3)-2.

The action of A on Hamilton words is free, since a permutation fixing an
ordered list of all vertices is the identity. Blue status is invariant
under A, because rho_(aP)=a rho_P a^(-1). Thus Delta is exactly the
nonnegative integer number of black path orbits.

PROOF

1. Odd-order obstruction and the cycle-cut quotient.

No tournament automorphism is a nonidentity involution: a transposed pair
would have its edge reversed. Thus no automorphism has even order, since
an element of even order has a nonidentity involutory power. Pairing group
elements with their inverses also shows that |A| is odd.

Choose a Hamilton cycle, whose existence follows from the classical cycle
theorem cited below. Label its vertices modulo n, with successor R:x->x+1.
Let A intersect <R> have order m and index q=n/m; it is generated by R^q,
and m is odd. Its cycle cuts are P_i=(i,i+1,...,i+n-1). The unique vertex
permutation taking P_i to P_j is R^(j-i), so the cuts meet exactly q path
orbits, indexed by i modulo q.

The positional reversal of P_i is rho_i(x)=2i-1-x. If no cut is blue, all
q cut orbits are black. Otherwise shift the labels so P_0 is blue. Since
rho_i rho_0=R^(2i), P_i is blue exactly when q divides 2i. Hence exactly
gcd(2,q)<=2 cut orbits are blue. Consequently

    q <= Delta+2 <= d+2=s.                                  (1)

This argument does not assume that T is self-converse. In the
non-self-converse case the stronger q<=Delta holds.

2. Rotation orbits give degree and connectivity bounds.

The q vertex orbits O_0,...,O_(q-1) of <R^q> have odd size m. Each induces
a regular tournament, since its cyclic automorphism action is transitive.
Thus every vertex of T has outdegree at least

    (m-1)/2 = n/(2q)-1/2 >= n/(2s)-1/2.                     (2)

We need two connectivity estimates:

    kappa(T) >= ceil(n/3) if q=1;
    kappa(T) >= ceil(m/2) if q>=2.                          (3)

Here kappa is the minimum number of deleted vertices leaving a nonstrong
tournament; the one-vertex tournament is considered strong.

First, in a regular tournament of order m, suppose deleting a vertices
leaves a nonempty split X->Y, of sizes x,y. Comparing the fixed outdegree
(m-1)/2 with the average outdegrees on X and Y gives

    (m-1)/2 >= (x-1)/2+y,
    (m-1)/2 <= (y-1)/2+a.

Since m=x+y+a, these inequalities give y<=a and x<=a, so m<=3a.
A nonstrong tournament always admits such a split: take Y to be the set
reachable from a vertex that cannot reach all vertices, and X its
complement. This proves the q=1 bound in (3).

For q>=2 the Hamilton cycle gives a directed perfect matching from O_i
to O_(i+1), indices cyclic. Suppose deleting a<m/2 vertices leaves a
nonempty split X->Y. Write a_i for the number deleted in O_i. No orbit is
empty. Call an orbit split if it meets both X and Y. Applied within a
split orbit, the preceding regularity inequalities bound each of its
remaining parts by a_i, so a_i>=m/3. There is at most one split orbit,
since two would require a>=2m/3>m/2.

If no orbit is split, each is labelled X or Y, and both labels occur.
The cyclic sequence of labels has a Y-to-X transition. Its perfect
matching cannot be destroyed by fewer than m deletions, a contradiction.

If O_0 is the only split orbit, write x_0=|X intersect O_0| and
y_0=|Y intersect O_0|. The unsplit labels O_1,...,O_(q-1) cannot have a
Y-to-X transition, so they consist of X labels followed by Y labels,
with either block possibly empty.

- If all are X, the matching O_0->O_1 forces y_0<=a_1, while regularity
  gives x_0<=a_0. Hence m=x_0+y_0+a_0<=2a_0+a_1<=2a, impossible.
- If all are Y, the matching O_(q-1)->O_0 forces x_0<=a_(q-1), while
  regularity gives y_0<=a_0. Again m<=2a, impossible.
- If both labels occur, q>=3 and the two neighboring orbits are distinct.
  The two boundary matchings force y_0<=a_1 and x_0<=a_(q-1). Hence
  m<=a_0+a_1+a_(q-1)<=a, impossible.

For q=2 the sole unsplit orbit falls in one of the first two cases; no
neighbor is counted twice. For m=1 the zero-deletion assertion is simply
strongness of T. Therefore every integer a<m/2 preserves strongness,
which proves the second bound in (3).

3. A large order supplies a removable power-of-two cycle.

Suppose, for a contradiction, that n>2sr. If q>=2, equations (1),(3) give

    kappa(T) >= m/2 = n/(2q) >= n/(2s) > r.

If q=1, instead they give

    kappa(T) >= n/3 > 2sr/3 >= 4r/3 > r,

because s>=2. Also r>=8 and n>2r. By the cited cycle theorem, T has a
directed r-cycle. Let S be its vertex set. Deleting S leaves a strong
tournament, of order n-r>r>=8.

4. Outdegree averaging forces many prefix attachment points.

Suppose every vertex outside S is beaten by at most d+1 vertices of S.
Summing the outdegrees of the vertices of S, using (2), gives

    r(n/(2s)-1/2) <= r(r-1)/2 + (d+1)(n-r),

because S has exactly r(r-1)/2 internal edges. Equivalently,

    n(r/(2s)-d-1) <= r^2/2-r(d+1).

By r>=4s(d+1), the left coefficient is at least r/(4s)>0. Therefore

    n <= 2sr-4s(d+1) < 2sr,

a contradiction. Thus some v outside S is beaten by at least t=d+2
vertices of S.

5. A prefix clique contradicts the number of black orbits.

Take a Hamilton cycle in the strong remainder T-S and cut it to obtain
a Hamilton word W beginning at v. For t distinct vertices of S beating
v, cut the fixed r-cycle immediately after each such vertex and append
the same W. Every concatenation is a directed Hamilton word.

For two different resulting words P,Q, the unique permutation u with
Q=uP fixes the complement of S and acts on S as a nonidentity power of
its r-cycle. Since r is a power of two, u has even order. It cannot be
an automorphism, so the t words occupy t distinct path orbits.

At most one is blue. Indeed, if both P and Q were blue, rho_P and
rho_Q=u rho_P u^(-1) would be anti-automorphisms, making

    rho_Q rho_P = u (rho_P u^(-1) rho_P)

an automorphism. The two factors have disjoint supports: the first is
supported in the first r positions of P, the second in its last r
positions, and n>=2r. Both factors have the same even order. A product
of disjoint-support permutations of the same order has that order,
contradicting the odd-order obstruction.

Hence at least t-1=d+1 distinct path orbits are black, contrary to
Delta<=d. In a non-self-converse tournament all t are black. This proves
n<=2sr in all cases.

6. Constants and inversion.

Minimality of the power of two gives r<8s(d+1), including when its lower
threshold is itself a power of two. Consequently

    n <= 2sr < 16s^2(d+1)=16(d+2)^2(d+1).

Taking d=Delta yields

    n < 16(Delta+2)^2(Delta+1) <= 16(Delta+2)^3,

which implies the stated strict inverse bound. QED.

DEPENDENCIES AND SCOPE

The only external theorem used is strong-tournament pancyclicity (including
Hamiltonicity): J. W. Moon, "On subtournaments of a tournament," Canadian
Mathematical Bulletin 9 (1966), Theorem 1, p. 297. Every vertex of a strong
tournament lies on a directed cycle of each length from 3 to its order.
The theorem statement was independently checked in the primary PDF.

DOI: https://doi.org/10.4153/CMB-1966-038-7
Primary PDF:
https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/5D7CCBE66AB07BB3F90F771D1CD3CD08/S0008439500052875a.pdf/on-subtournaments-of-a-tournament.pdf

The group, degree, connectivity, and prefix arguments are proved above.
No Dixon automorphism-size bound, Thomassen Hamilton-cycle lower bound,
or finite exhaustive classification enters the proof. Thus the argument
removes those counting dependencies for general bounded-defect finiteness.
The deliberately coarse bound does not replace the sharper Delta=2
classification and makes no claim of sharpness or further classification.
