An elementary explicit cubic order bound from black defect
Status: NEW PROOF CANDIDATE; independent audit pending.
2026-10-20

Let T be a strong tournament of order n>=3. Write h=H(T)/|Aut(T)|, let b be its number of blue Hamilton-path orbits (b=0 if T is not self-converse), and let Delta=h-b. For an integer d>=0 suppose Delta<=d. Put s=d+2. Let r be the smallest power of two at least4s(d+1).

CLAIM: n<=2sr<16(d+2)^2(d+1). Thus every strong tournament satisfies Delta>(n/16)^(1/3)-2. This is an effective elementary strengthening of bounded-defect finiteness. The previous sharper classification for Delta2 remains unchanged; this general bound is deliberately coarse.

1. Cycle-cut structure and degrees
Choose a Hamilton cycle with successor R. Let its rotational automorphism subgroup have odd order m and index q=n/m, so it is generated by R^q. Its cuts meet exactly q Hamilton-path orbits. If no cut is blue, all q are black. If one is blue, write its reflection r0; the other cut reversals differ by R^(2i). Thus the blue cut-orbit indices satisfy q|2i and there are gcd(2,q)<=2. Hence q<=d+2=s.

The q rotation orbits each have odd size m and induce regular tournaments. Therefore every vertex has outdegree at least(m-1)/2>=n/(2s)-1/2.

2. Connectivity supplied by rotation orbits
A regular tournament on m vertices has vertex connectivity at least ceil(m/3): after deleting a vertices, any nontrivial split X->Y obeys |X|,|Y|<=a by averaging internal degrees, hence m<=3a. Consecutive rotation orbits along the Hamilton cycle are joined by a directed perfect matching. These observations imply kappa(T)>=ceil(m/3).

When q>=2, the stronger bound kappa(T)>=ceil(m/2) holds. Suppose deleting a<m/2 vertices leaves X->Y. No orbit is empty. A split orbit requires at least m/3 deletions, so there is at most one split orbit. With none, the cyclic X/Y orbit labels have a forbidden Y-to-X perfect matching. With one split orbit O0, the unsplit labels in cyclic order are X...X,Y...Y. If all are X, its Y part has size<=a1 by the matching O0->O1, while its X part has size<=a0 by regularity; hence m<=2a0+a1<=2a. The all-Y case is dual. If both labels occur, both split-side sizes are bounded by deletions in the two neighboring orbits, hence m<=a. Every case contradicts a<m/2. This is the previously audited strengthened rotation-connectivity argument, reproduced here.

3. Assume the claimed order bound fails
Suppose n>2sr. If q>=2, kappa(T)>=m/2=n/(2q)>=n/(2s)>r. If q=1, kappa(T)>=n/3>2sr/3>=4r/3>r. Also n>2r.

By the cited Moon pancyclicity theorem, T contains a directed cycle of length r. Let S be its vertex set. Suppose every outside vertex is beaten by at most d+1 vertices of S. Summing the outdegrees of vertices in S, using the lower bound from1, gives

  r(n/(2s)-1/2) <= r(r-1)/2 + (d+1)(n-r),

so

  n(r/(2s)-d-1) <= r^2/2-r(d+1).

Since r>=4s(d+1), the left coefficient is at least r/(4s)>0. Consequently

  n <= 2sr-4s(d+1) < 2sr,

contradiction. Hence some v outside S is beaten by at least t=d+2 distinct vertices of S.

4. The prefix clique contradicts bounded defect
Since kappa(T)>r, T-S remains strong. It has a Hamilton path starting at v, obtained by cutting a Hamilton cycle there. Cut the r-cycle on S immediately after each of t vertices that beats v, and append the same Hamilton path on T-S. This gives t Hamilton words.

Relative permutations between different words are nontrivial powers of the same r-cycle supported in the first r positions. Since r is a power of two, all these permutations have even order. Therefore these words occupy distinct Aut(T) orbits, because a tournament automorphism group has odd order.

At most one word can be blue. If P,Q were both blue and Q=uP, their positional reversals yield the automorphism u(r_P u^{-1}r_P). The two factors have disjoint supports (first r and last r positions, since n>=2r) and the same even order. Their product has even order, impossible. Thus at least t-1=d+1 distinct path orbits are black, contradicting Delta<=d. In the non-self-converse case all t are black, so the contradiction is even stronger.

This proves n<=2sr. By minimality of the power of two, r<8s(d+1), yielding n<16s^2(d+1). The inverse bound follows from n<16(Delta+2)^2(Delta+1)<=16(Delta+2)^3.

Dependencies and scope
- Tournament automorphisms have odd order: elementary involution obstruction.
- Hamiltonicity/pancyclicity: Moon, On subtournaments of a tournament (1966), cited in the audited center-classification packet.
- The cycle-cut quotient and rotation-connectivity arguments are reproduced above.
- No Dixon automorphism-size bound, Thomassen Hamilton-cycle lower bound, or finite exhaustive classification is used.
- This proof is not yet independently approved. It does not classify individual defects or determine a sharp extremal function.
