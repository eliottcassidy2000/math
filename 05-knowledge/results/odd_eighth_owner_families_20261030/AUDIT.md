### Why the maximum computation is exhaustive

`scout.maximum` tests t=k/(a+b) for every pair of speeds with replacement.
A positive local maximum of the minimum-distance envelope occurs where
opposite slopes meet, or at the half-integer cusp of one speed (a=b).
These are precisely such clocks. Reflection covers the other half-circle.
No coprimality restriction is imposed on k: reducing k/(a+b) can produce
a denominator that is not itself any pair sum.

Independently, `safe` intersects literal closed intervals
[(k+r)/v,(k+1-r)/v]. To certify the claimed maximum r, it checks that
Safe(r) is exactly the reported points and that Safe(r+delta) is empty,
where delta=1/(4*vmax^2+1). Every possible maximum value, including r,
has reduced denominator at most2*vmax by the slope argument above. Two
distinct such values differ by at least1/(4*vmax^2), so emptiness at this
smaller delta excludes a missed larger maximum. This is a rational
separation certificate, not a numerical tolerance.

The terminal derivative checks use a much smaller rational displacement
1/[100(1+vmax)^4]. They compare actual interval measure with the proved
local germ. Those finite evaluations support the symbolic active-wall
proof; they are not by themselves a proof for every sufficiently small
epsilon or every q.
