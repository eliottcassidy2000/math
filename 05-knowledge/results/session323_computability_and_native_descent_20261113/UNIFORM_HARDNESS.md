# Fixed Chan–Pak versus uniform coverage of compositional tournament languages

## The fixed problem, and the missing quantifier

The fixed Chan–Pak assertion is Π1, not just in the naive Π2 form “for each target, some tournament realizes it.” This is inherited from the repo's Camion–Busch note. Here is the precise compression step. The SCC sequence gives H as a product of the strong component counts. A nontrivial strong block has H≥its order by cutting a Hamilton cycle at every edge. For H=h>1 discard singleton blocks: the sum of remaining sizes is at most the sum of component counts, which is at most their product h (for two factors a,b≥3, a+b≤ab; then induct). For h=1 a singleton suffices. Thus the real target predicate is decidable by a primitive-recursively bounded exhaustive search, and the fixed conjecture is the universal closure of that predicate.

Busch's sharper floor, H≥5^((n−1)/3) for a strong order-n block, gives H≥3^(n/3) for n≥4 (check (5/3)^4>5), and at n=3 equality holds. Hence every realized h>1 has an SCC-compressed witness of order at most floor(3 log_3 h), with equality attainable on the pure C3 sequence as a bound for arbitrary compressed witnesses. A prime target has a single strong block and the sharper bound 1+floor(3 log_5 h). For binary input length L, literal exhaustive adjacency enumeration followed by subset DP gives a deterministic 2^O(L²) decision algorithm; alternatively enumeration of vertex permutations uses polynomial space in L. Neither bound says that a polynomial-length adjacency certificate alone is polynomial-time checkable: its exact Hamilton count remains to be computed. Guessing the O(L²)-bit witness and using a PP oracle to compare its #P Hamilton count to h gives the generic NP^PP upper bound; no matching lower bound is asserted.

If the fixed conjecture is false, some actual h fails the bounded check, and sufficiently strong arithmetic can prove that particular finite check false. Therefore independence of the fixed conjecture from such a theory would imply its truth. Neither Π1 form nor the uniform results below establish its independence. In particular a single fixed sentence is not a uniform decision problem to which undecidability can be attributed by comparing it with a parameterized problem.

## One fixed physically realized target ray

Let R_i, i≥1, be the transitive tournament on i+2 vertices with just the arc joining first and last reversed. It is strong and self-converse. Every odd directed cycle uses the reversed arc and an odd-size subset of the i intervening vertices; all these cycles intersect. The odd-cycle formula gives H(R_i)=1+2·2^(i−1)=2^i+1. Every induced subtournament of R_i is transitive unless it retains both endpoints and at least one intermediate vertex, in which case it is some R_j with j≤i. The universe below consists of ordinal sums of R_i and singletons, with primitive recursive recognition via SCCs (try all finite orderings inside a proposed R_i block). Define a fixed target ray F_k=2^(2^k)+1 for k≥0, independent of the language.

If an odd prime p divides F_k and also 2^i+1, its order for base 2 is exactly 2^(k+1), so 2^k divides i. Thus no product of block counts with i<2^k can equal F_k. In the subuniverse where only R_(2^j) may occur, the Fermat counts are pairwise coprime, and F_k occurs precisely when R_(2^k) is allowed at some order, provided arbitrary padding is allowed after the permitting threshold. No Fermat primality assertion is used.

## Exact uniform classifications

These classifications are for effectively presented classes or explicit compiler templates. The cited arithmetic-hierarchy labels use standard index problems: nonhalting, totality, finiteness (FIN), and cofiniteness (COF). The lower bounds below are direct reductions. They do not imply anything about the fixed ambient spectrum beyond its distinction from these index problems.

**All targets, two-way padding and hereditary/ordinal closure: Π1-complete on the displayed universe.** Two-way padding means that adding or deleting singleton SCCs never affects admission of a nontrivial core. For a fixed target, each nonsingleton block count is at least its order and divides the product, so the singleton-free core has order at most the target. Thus even with a membership validator there is a primitive-recursive bounded check, giving the Π1 upper bound for uniform primitive-recursive validators. For hardness, compile an arbitrary machine e into the class of ordinal sums in which a block R_i is admitted iff e has not halted within i steps. This validator is primitive recursive and isomorphism/converse invariant. The allowed block sizes form a prefix, so the class is hereditary under induced subtournaments. It is closed under ordinal sum and invariant under adding/deleting singleton SCCs. If e never halts, all ray targets occur. Otherwise all allowed i are bounded, and a sufficiently large Fermat target is absent by the coprimality argument. Coverage is equivalent to nonhalting.

**All targets, one-way padding: Π2-complete even with primitive-recursive validation and ordinal closure.** One-way padding only demands closure under adding singleton SCCs. At order n, allow an ordinal sum iff, for every R_i component, a machine e halts on each input j<i within n steps. Validation is primitive recursive and converse invariant, while increasing order by a padding or ordinal join can only help. If e is total, each R_(2^k) is admitted with sufficiently many singleton SCCs. If e diverges on input j, then admitted i never exceed j, so large Fermat targets cannot occur. General membership gives the Π2 upper bound ∀k∃witness. The time budget hidden in padding is exactly why an ambient order compression does not apply to this validator. The class need not be hereditary: deleting singleton padding can discard the computational time evidence.

**All targets, c.e. rather than decidable validation: Π2-complete even with hereditary/two-way padding and ordinal closure.** For a core R_i require that the machine halt on all j<i, without any fixed budget. The admitted family is uniformly c.e., isomorphic/converse invariant, and the condition is independent of padding and hereditary by the prefix property. The same totality reduction works. Structural compression alone does not remove the existential time needed for admission.

**Eventual targets: Σ2 versus Σ3.** For primitive-recursive validators invariant under two-way padding, the bounded core test gives ∃K∀k≥K R(k), hence Σ2. For hardness restrict letters to R_(2^k), allow the kth letter iff a fixed computably enumerable set W_e has no new element at stage k, and take all ordinal sums and singleton padding. This is primitive recursive (bounded stage simulation); the Fermat ray has finitely many failures iff W_e is finite. This family is not claimed hereditary. With one-way padding the upper bound is ∃K∀k≥K∃witness, hence Σ3. For hardness allow letter R_(2^k) in a tournament of order n iff k has entered W_e by stage n, and apply this criterion to all letters. This is primitive recursive, converse invariant, padding-addition closed and ordinal-sum closed. Existence of an admitted witness for F_k is equivalent to k∈W_e, so eventual coverage is equivalent to cofiniteness. Fixed ray, unbounded order, and variable validator are essential here.

## Proof-theoretic consequence and scope

For a consistent recursively axiomatized arithmetic theory T satisfying the standard hypotheses of Gödel's second incompleteness theorem, let e be a machine searching for a T-proof of contradiction. The first nonhalting compiler gives a primitive-recursive hereditary, ordinal-closed, padding-invariant language. The statement that its fixed Fermat ray is covered is equivalent to Con(T), with the equivalence provable in PA (and in much weaker arithmetic supporting the elementary construction). For T extending PA, T cannot prove its true coverage sentence if consistent. This is a concrete nonuniform proof obligation inside an exceptionally tame tournament language, not a claim that any of the ordinary tournament constructions use such a hidden validator, or that the fixed Chan–Pak assertion is unprovable. The extra expressiveness is in the variable language membership condition, not in the Hamilton-count map.

As a practical checkpoint for any proposed constructive proof, specify whether its membership/repair condition is decidable, whether singleton padding can be removed without losing the witness, and whether the construction is a single fixed grammar rather than an arbitrary indexed validator. An unbounded target statement can move up the hierarchy when one of those properties is removed, without any change to its underlying tournament objects.

A further fixed-spectrum simplification uses only ordinal multiplication and the known witnesses at 49, 63 and 343 (the repo's fixed-H inventory records these; no extra census is needed). Chan–Pak is equivalent to the simultaneous assertions that every odd prime p≠7 occurs and every 7p with p an odd prime other than 3,7 occurs. Indeed with no factor 7, multiply prime witnesses; with exactly one factor 7, use one 7p if the remaining factor has any prime p≠3,7, or use 63 if it is a power of 3 of exponent at least two. The excluded leftovers are exactly 7 and 21. With exponent at least two on 7, use 49 and 343 since every integer at least two is a nonnegative combination of 2 and 3. Both infinite target types require a strong witness: any nontrivial factorization of a prime is impossible, and 7p would need the absent factor 7. Thus the genuinely open quantifier may be placed over those strong subfamilies without introducing a time budget from padding.

For the Π1 upper bound on validators, closure under deleting singleton SCCs alone suffices for nontrivial targets. In particular any induced-hereditary class has the bound if its membership is primitive recursive, whether or not it admits arbitrary singleton additions. The two-way restriction in the Π1-completeness theorem is stronger than necessary for the upper bound; it makes the contrast with one-way-addition hardness especially explicit.
