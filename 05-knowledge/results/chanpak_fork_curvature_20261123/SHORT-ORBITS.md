# Short repeatable words for the modular continuation controller

Let \(p\) be odd and \(d=(p-1)/2\); work with the continuation
column vector \(v=(Q_1,\ldots,Q_d)\) over \(\mathbf F_p\).
Use \(P=D^2\), where \(D=\operatorname{diag}(1,\ldots,d)\) is a
singleton source, and let \(W_+\), \(W_-\) be a cyclic sandwich
at even and odd order respectively. In either parity,
\[
 (W_\sigma v)_s=s^2v_s+s(v_{s+1}-v_{s-1}),
 \qquad v_0=0,\quad v_{d+1}=\sigma v_d.
\]
Here \(W\) in an even-order word means \(W_+\). The positive
physical move \(V=D W_- D\) is a singleton, an odd-order
sandwich and another singleton; it returns to even order.

The following finite certificates show complete nonzero orbits
without a full vector-space search. Read each word chronologically.
The index is that of the cyclic word's multiplicative subgroup in
\(\mathbf F_{p^d}^\times\); it is not the index of the entire move
group.

Let \(S\) be a word matrix and \(o=(p^d-1)/\text{index}\).
The replay checks \(S^o=I\), and \(S^{o/\ell}\ne I\) for every prime
\(\ell\mid o\). A listed prime divisor \(\ell\) of \(o\) has
multiplicative order \(d\) for the base \(p\). Since \(o\) is coprime
to \(p\), the matrix is semisimple; some eigenvalue has multiplicative
order divisible by \(\ell\), so its degree over \(\mathbf F_p\) is
at least \(d\). Thus \(S\) is irreducible, and
\(\mathbf F_p[S]\) is a field of size \(p^d\) acting regularly on
nonzero vectors. Identify the vector \(e_1\) with the field unit via
the Krylov basis \(e_1,Se_1,\ldots,S^{d-1}e_1\).

If \(o=p^d-1\) the one word is a Singer cycle and suffices by itself.
Otherwise two nonzero vectors lie in one \(S\)-orbit exactly when
their \(o\)-th powers in this field agree: the kernel of this power
map is precisely the subgroup generated by \(S\). Starting at
\(e_1\), the replay searches at most 100 successive \(S\) powers
of each discovered representative, testing the two permitted
generators for new cosets. Every checked move is positive, and the
exact number of distinct keys found equals the index in every case.
Every generator used in a row is invertible, so its inverse is itself
a positive power. Hence all nonzero states communicate, starting
from any nonzero filler, including the even transitive filler on two
vertices. The bound of 100 is an exploration cap, not an assumption
about unseen keys; certificate success requires the exact total.

**Why parity really matters at 23.** The even \(W\) is singular,
with a nonzero kernel certified by the replay. A word using only
\(P,W\) either fixes \(e_1\) (no \(W\)) or gives a vector in
\[
 \bigcup_{j=0}^{d-1} P^j\operatorname{im}(W).
\]
Indeed the last \(W\) is followed only by powers of \(P\) and
\(P^d=I\). That union has at most \(d p^{d-1}\) elements, so
the original two moves cannot have a complete nonzero orbit.
The parity detour \(V\) repairs this: its listed cyclic word has
index two, and one application of \(P\) after that word to \(e_1\)
moves to the other coset.

Zero is realized by the cyclic tournament on \(p\) vertices, whose
rotation acts freely on every ordered path-cover set, followed by
one singleton for even parity. Adding a singleton maps all states
bijectively to odd parity. Sandwich outputs still distinguish all
states: for first differing coordinate \(j\), \(j-1\) wraps read
its difference in coordinate 1 with coefficient \((j-1)!\).
Thus the exact fixed-parity memory is \(p^d\) at the certified
primes, including the new ones. This is modular reachability, not
absolute-height reachability.
