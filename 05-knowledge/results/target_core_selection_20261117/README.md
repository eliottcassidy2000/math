# Target-first core selection on the two prime axes

**Proved:** a deterministic exact inverse for the two-terminal-apex seam family, a no-prime-passport theorem for all two-core seams at 7p, and a transfer law for moving a cut without changing its tournament. A Hadamard overlay supplies a literal localized switch into 7·67, but a fixed nonzero patch cannot be extended indefinitely by common padding. These results do not prove the full spectrum conjecture.

## 1. Two axes and small structure

The first odd square 9 bounds exactly the initial prime set 2,3,5,7. The order-four microcosm has four tournament classes, but only the path counts 1,3,5: the triangle plus a source and the triangle plus a sink have the same count and are converse, not separate counts. This small census has real global force for 7, because [Busch's bound](https://www.combinatorics.org/ojs/index.php/eljc/article/view/v13i1n3) rules out any strong witness of count 7 on five or more vertices. The count 21 additionally needs the strong order-five and order-six exclusions; it does not follow just from 7 being forbidden, since ordinal multiplication by 3 has no inverse on the spectrum. The count 63 is the explicit counterexample to that inverse, and fills the rest of its ×3 chain. The binary inverse below uses actual two-way subset choices. The identity 2+2=2·2=2² is an arithmetic coincidence, not a reason the tournament count 7 is excluded. The arithmetic triple {2,3,11} points to the first prime on the other side of this small-prime block. It is also a clean test: both 11 and 7·11 escape the binary-apex selector, although both are genuine counts.

By ordinal multiplication, the infinite targets reduce to odd primes p other than 3 and 7 on the first axis, the corresponding 7p on the second, and the known sporadic seeds 49,63,343 (plus 1 and 3). The 7p axis cannot be generated by multiplying the p-axis witness by a 7-path tournament.

## 2. The absolute target specifies two binary addresses

For a fixed terminal vertex v define its profile (a,b,c) as in the [two-core law](../metagraph_two_core_20261117/README.md): full paths, ordered covers by one or two paths with the first ending v, and full paths starting v, respectively. The right profile (d,e,f) is written on the converse before gluing. The seam count is ad+be−cf.

Let the deletion of v be a transitive spine ordered from low to high label. For each spine vertex i, put bit x_i=1 iff i beats v. For each terminal predecessor i, all subsets of the earlier spine can precede i, and the complement is the optional second run. This makes b=1+Σ x_i·2^i. Take x=b−1 in its usual binary length. Its top bit is 1 and certifies an actual frame ending at v (the natural spine then v). Starting at v forces the natural spine after it, and so c=1−x_0=b mod 2. The full path count is a=F(x) with

    F(0)=1,
    F(2z+1)=F(z),
    F(2z)=F(z)+z+(z mod 2).

Indeed every nontrivial odd cycle contains v, so by the [odd-pack identity](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.05569) it contributes twice with no other cycle present. For spine ends i<j of such a cycle one needs x_i=0,x_j=1, and the even number of intermediate vertices gives the contribution 2^(j−i−1)+[j=i+1]. Removing the low bit gives the recurrence by summing the contributions with i=0. In particular F(x)≤x+1 by induction. With two signs selected independently the target equation is exactly

    N = b e + F(b−1)F(e−1) − [b,e odd],    b,e≥2.             (1)

This is an all-order physical parameterization, not a fit to sample counts. Either side can be chosen from the target. It contains only one-apex *cores*, not necessarily one-apex final tournaments.

### Carry the low address into the high address

By symmetry b≤e, and be≤N. For each b≤√N, let a=F(b−1), choose L=2^k to be the first power of 2 at least √(N/b), and write e−1=Lq+r, 0≤r<L. Repeatedly removing the low bits gives

    F(Lq+r)=F(q)+A(r)q+δ(r)(q mod 2)+F(r)−1,

where A is the bit-reversed complement of the k-bit string r and δ=1 if its highest bit is 0. To see the extra term, the contribution at a zero digit i is the quotient above it plus the next bit. The quotient contains q with coefficient 2^(k−i−1); the next bit can depend on q only for i=k−1. Define B=bL+aA and M=N−b(r+1)−a(F(r)−1)+[b odd][r even]. The remaining equation reads

    M=Bq+aF(q)+aδ(q mod 2).

Use 1≤F(q)≤q+1: the only candidate q satisfy

    ceil((M−2a)/(B+a)) ≤ q ≤ floor((M−a)/B),    q≥0.

The interval has bounded size because a≤b, B≥bL, M≤N and N/(bL²)≤1. Substitute each q in (1). This costs O(N^(3/4) log N) arithmetic operations on O(log N)-bit integers: sum O(L) over b≤√N, with O(log N) work per low address. This is a complete decision/inverse for this grammar at any target. It is not a claim of polynomial complexity in log N, nor that the answer is always nonempty.

Examples shown as p: (left address,right address) for p; then for 7p:

    17:          (2,8)           (3,21)
    30011:       (3,6559)        (13,13150)
    30013:       (7,3534)        (2,97564)
    1000000007:  (3,191311747)   (5,748796549)
    1000000009:  (7,86246185)    (29,139006537)

There are exact negative controls in both directions: 109 escapes (1) while 7·109 hits, and 877 hits while 7·877 escapes. Both 173 and 7·173 escape (1). These are exclusions from the grammar, not from the tournament spectrum. The older extreme-arc two-size family is more rigid still: its formula is equivalent to 4N−3=(2^(j+1)+3)(4·2^k+3), so a prime shifted target 4N−3 blocks that family instantly.

## 3. A divisibility constraint from three small physical readers

Let content mean gcd(a,b,c) for a marked profile. The gcd of all actual strong two-core readings with this left profile (left size at least two) is exactly its content. Every reading is a multiple of the content. Conversely, use right terminal profiles of TT₂, TT₃ and C₃, whose three readings are a+2b, a+4b and 3a+3b−c. Their gcd is odd. Taking differences first recovers divisibility of b, then a and c. The three readers also separate any two profiles (the determinant of their reading matrix has magnitude 2).

For any eligible port on a core of order at least two, b≥a>c≥0: splitting a path at the port gives the first bound, and at least one path ends there and cannot also start there. Also b−c is even by the singleton-tail parity identity. If p divides each a,b,c for a seam of count 7p, divide that profile by the odd prime p, writing A,B,C. Then B−C≥2 and

    7 = Ad+(B−C)e+C(e−f) ≥ d+2e ≥ 3d.

Thus d=1. A nontransitive tournament has a directed triangle and its odd-pack count would be at least three, so the right core is transitive. Its terminal is its sink, hence f=0 and e=2^(m−1)≥2. As B≥A and B≥2, 7=A+eB forces e=2, A=1, B=3, which requires odd C by parity but C<A. Contradiction. Hence neither side can carry p in its content. If p≠7, both contents are 1 or 7 and at most one can be 7. For 49 both are primitive by the same argument. For prime count N=p both contents are primitive, even more directly since ad+be−cf>a,d. A single content 7 is possible: the interval circulant of order seven has H=175 and two-slot count 1988; append an ordinal sink as its terminal port, then read it against TT₂ to obtain 4151=7·593 with left content 7.

## 4. Moving the marked cut: a useful equivalence, not extra coverage

Write Q=diag(1,1,−1). A singleton can pass between neighboring excess-one cuts in exactly two ways. If the outgoing arc stays the same and skips it, the singleton moves as an ordinal source on either terminal core, S(a,b,c)=(a,2b,0). If the outgoing arc changes, the singleton is the exit of the first seam and the entrance of the second. The root-changing near-source update is

    J(a,b,c)=(a+b−c,a+b,a−c).

Add new terminal y with v→y, y beating all other old vertices. First coordinate is the seam count. Last coordinate counts old paths not starting v. For the middle coordinate, a first run ending y is either singleton y (a choices for the optional remainder) or an old run ending v followed by y (b choices). J is unimodular; both J and S are self-adjoint for Q. More importantly, the two parenthesizations give literally the same labelled tournament after moving the singleton. This explains why reselecting the cut in a fixed witness is not by itself selecting a different witness for a new target.

There is a full serial transfer for a fixed middle block X with a frame from entry u to exit w. If A points only by v→u into X, with all other cross arcs X→A, the output port is w. Its h-read row q=(d,e,−f) is the profile of (X^op,u) with the last sign negated. Its start-read row is r=(D,E,−F), where D is the number of X paths starting w, F starts w and ends u, and E counts ordered nonempty two-covers of X whose first run starts w and second starts u. This follows by taking paths that start w and separating paths that use the exceptional arc or not. To get the cover row, append the near-source J at w *within* X, keeping u as entry, and form its h-read q₊. The full matrix T_X has rows q, q₊−q+r, r, by the J formula for the new h-read. In a three-block serial seam with no first-to-last upward arc, shifting the middle across the cut gives

    T_(X^op with reversed ports) = Q T_X^t Q.

Both reads count the same original tournament. The physical test profiles of TT₂, TT₃ and C₃ are a spanning set, so equality of their bilinear evaluations forces the displayed matrix identity. This is a retained three-coordinate transport, not a fresh scalar deposit. A bridge skipping the entire middle block transports the diagonal matrix (H(X),P₂(X),0) instead, since X is now an ordinal prefix in either orientation.

## 5. The binary/Hadamard overlay permits a finite local switch, not arbitrary padding

Continue the [two-tiling sign chart](../third_cover_binary_tilings_20261117/README.md): diagonal 1, upper signs of one tournament, below the diagonal opposite signs of the other. The shared directed frame is imposed off the diagonal. For the normalized matrix M=I+L+K, L symmetric zero-diagonal is supported on disagreements and K skew on agreements. Hadamard orthogonality implies [L,K]=0.

Suppose all disagreements lie in a proper vertex subset S. The cross block B=K_(S,Sᶜ) has sign entries, and L_SB=0. Every disagreement degree is therefore even. Internal row orthogonality gives M_SS M_SSᵗ=nI−BBᵗ. On the image of L_S this Gram operator is exactly nI; its trace gives the necessary inequality n·rank(L_S)≤|S|². Consequently no **fixed nonzero disagreement patch** can remain a Hadamard switch under unbounded common padding. This is independent of the rank of the Hamiltonian count reader.

If the patch is a single disagreement cycle of length s, its signed adjacency has kernel only for even s, in which case the sign product must equal (−1)^(s/2) and the nullity is two. This follows by solving L_Sx=0 around the consecutive vertices: the signed recurrence alternates every two indices and must close. If s≡0 mod 4 the signed cycle is balanced and can be switched to all positive edges. There are then simple +2 and −2 eigenvalues of L_S. The skew K_SS commutes with L_S and must vanish on each of those one-dimensional spaces. But (I+L_S+K_SS)(I+L_S+K_SS)ᵗ has eigenvalues 9 and 1 there, whereas both must equal n. Hence s≡2 mod 4. Combining n>s, n divisible by 4, and n(s−2)≤s² gives n=s+2 (s≥6). Thus a cycle switch forces essentially all of the order to change with it.

A literal s=6, n=8 example has framed upper/lower tournaments with outgoing bit rows

    T = (102,252,88,49,225,68,136,45)
    U = (34,180,121,19,225,72,139,45).

They differ on one 6-cycle and have counts 345 and 469=7·67. Both decoded halves carry the natural Hamiltonian frame and the overlay has orthogonal rows. This is a real switch into one of the prime targets but not a mechanism for picking its absolute count at all heights.

### An arithmetic sieve for a localized cycle

The cycle's half-length m=s/2 is odd. Switch signs inside S so that its adjacency is L=P+P⁻¹, where P is the signed cyclic shift with P^(2m)=−I. Its real eigenspaces for L are two-dimensional rotation planes for P (the conjugate P eigenvalues pair, with distinct real parts pairwise). Since internal K is skew and commutes with L, it commutes with P on every such plane. P has a cyclic coordinate vector, so internal K is a real polynomial in P; its coefficients in the basis 1,P,…,P^(2m−1) are integers, since applying the polynomial to the cyclic coordinate vector reads those coefficients up to sign. Write K=F(t)+P G(t) with t=P², t^m=−I. The Gram target nI−BBᵗ differs from nI only on ker L. The orthogonal projector onto this kernel is an even polynomial in P, since the kernel is exactly the t=−1 eigenspace; BBᵗ is a scalar on it because the two exterior sign columns are orthogonal with squared norm s (their dot product vanishes by orthogonality of the two full exterior rows). The odd part of (I+L)²−K² therefore gives F(t)G(t)=1+t⁻¹. The two skew summands of K are themselves skew, as transposition preserves the even/odd split.

At t=−x, x a nontrivial m-th root of unity, put z=x+x⁻¹. Evaluating the even Gram part gives |F(−x)|²+|G(−x)|²=2m−1+z, and the product equation gives |F(−x)|²|G(−x)|²=2−z. Thus D(m,z)=(2m−1+z)²−4(2−z) must be a square in the real cyclotomic field. If 3 divides m, take z=−1. Both squared magnitudes are then integers of product 3, so their sum 2m−2 must be 4: m=3. If 11 divides m, the norm of D from the primitive eleventh-root field cannot be square. The exact resultant is Q²−R, with

    Q=32m⁵−96m⁴−96m³+172m²+164m+23,
    R=704m⁴+5280m³+10384m²+7920m+2112.

For m≥19 it lies strictly between (Q−1)² and Q²: R is positive, and after shifting m by 19 the difference from (Q−1)² has all positive coefficients. For m=11 the norm is 13271131595041, strictly between the squares of 3642956 and 3642957. That exhausts the positive odd multiples below 19. This gives a literal role for {2,3,11}: the factor 2 in the disagreement length is mandatory, 3 in its half-length survives only at the smallest example, and 11 in its half-length cannot survive. This is a necessary condition on the Hadamard chart, not on general tournaments.

More generally, for each fixed odd prime ℓ dividing m, the norm polynomial of D over the real ℓ-th cyclotomic field is nonsquare. For each conjugate z put r=√(2−z) in (0,2); one root of D as a polynomial in m is ((r+1)²−2)/2>−1/2 and the other is <−1/2. The former roots are strictly ordered as z varies, so they are simple in the norm polynomial. This polynomial has even degree and square leading coefficient. Its square root at positive infinity differs from its polynomial part with rational coefficients by O(1/m). The rational polynomial at integers lies on a fixed denominator grid, and the discrepancy cannot vanish for large m (its squared difference is a nonzero polynomial). Hence integer squares can occur only for bounded m for each fixed ℓ. Any unbounded family of localized single cycles would need half-lengths whose least prime factor escapes to infinity.

A second literal switch, s=10, n=12, is recorded in the replay. It gives 194243=7·27749 and 181161. The sparse switches are genuine but sit alongside a proof that increasing the matrix size must change the patch.
