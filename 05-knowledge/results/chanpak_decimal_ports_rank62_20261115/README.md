# Decimal rungs, an exact signed counter, and the ambient span-three certificate

These results distinguish three operations that can look identical in a list of counts: finding another literal witness, adding one in an exposed signed port, and dilating a register by the base 1000. They do not give a single positive count with all three operations available.

- **[The requested decimal rungs](THOUSAND.md).** The same one-mark directed-triangle carrier realizes 1,001, 1,000,001 and 1,000,000,001 at orders 12, 21 and 31. The old Session 82 square-sum triple used 1,001,000 as its third number, not the initial 1. The exact divisor clocks explain why earlier strong components cannot simply be retained when going to the next rung. The new billion-scale aliquot trace connects back to 1, independently of tournament realization.
- **[A physical unipotent counter](TWO_PORT_SIGNED.md).** One strong two-apex tournament on 16+10k vertices has deletion-port readings whose difference is exactly 2k. Inserting ten new spine sites at a fixed cut, with all old arcs retained, raises that difference by two. Each ordinary count separately grows exponentially. The counter is an exact signed observable on the same physical host, not an absolute Hamilton count.
- **[Sharp append-only obstruction](UNIPOTENT_OBSTRUCTION.md).** Any nested induced chain with k strict increases has final H at least the strong floor at order k+2, and this is sharp. A finite-bandwidth periodic word has eventually constant or exponential ordinary H, never nonconstant polynomial. In the stationary ordinal repeated-block model with permanent apices, a raw decimal ladder needs at least nine such apices; this is a necessary condition, not an existence result.
- **[Decimal scaling, and the cost of removing the signs](DECIMAL_PORTS.md).** A single unmarked linearly growing transitive module costs a factorial at the ten-slot mode. Splitting the physical probes into a binary and a five-slot packet gives a fixed deletion-readout bank whose signed sum is exactly 3·1000^k. Three is the smallest positive integer coefficient possible for signed fixed acyclic growing-module layouts of the stated type, even allowing negative length shifts. A raw coefficient one is impossible in any fixed unmarked linear-growth substitution layout: acyclic layouts have the factor-three obstruction, and cyclic layouts grow superexponentially.
- **[Rank 62 to the 13-unit syllable cone](SYLLABLE_BRIDGE.md).** A literal letter with no distance-three backward arc suffices to cap any syllable run. The cap kills the unobserved full-transfer coordinates and allows the 1.7699 certificate in arbitrary span-3 contexts. The estimate charges interruptions explicitly; rank at least two is automatic throughout the seven-vertex-trimmed bulk of a strong prime-count word. Twenty-three short phases have the rank bridge, but only the original 13 have the growth cone. The same bridge plus an exact finite sieve excludes one fixed 13-unit motif repeated between fixed ends as an explanation of the decimal ladder.

The useful next target is a selective contact that turns the physical signed counter into a positive ordinary readout without the factor-three penalty, or a cover of arbitrary span-3 words by certified syllables plus a cone for the remaining recurrent pieces. **Chan–Pak and the unrestricted span-3 growth bound are open.**
