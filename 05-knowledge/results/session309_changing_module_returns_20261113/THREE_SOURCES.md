# Inverting the next construction: three structural sources

This short design note distinguishes moves before composing them.

1. **Target-directed Collatz.** Session 305 gives a bounded mixed-sign descent to seeds 1 and 5 avoiding both forbidden counts even in the dyadic ladders. That certificate does not confine the intermediate odd targets to primes and 7p; the [prime-tree obstruction](PRIME_COLLISION.md) shows why no bounded finite affine menu can do this globally. Thus the state space must include certified composite carriers; the prime predicate is a terminal selection, not an invariant.
2. **Same-sector Lonely Runner refill.** The [hypergraph and historical notes](HYPERGRAPH_SEAMS.md) track a timing/sector sidecar rather than merely an extra unit of aggregate capacity. In the tournament proof the same target count is matched, the moved vertices must be genuine ordinal tail sites, and the anchor must be recalculated. Even the intrinsic dyadic valuation is not the number of available sites. Finite sectors are useful for rejecting incompatible handoffs; they do not create a physical witness.
3. **Uniform hypergraphs and historical adjacency.** A transversal of all directed triples is a certificate of a transitive spine, not automatically a fixed finite palette of strong components. The former can grow, and that growth is essential: [the S-unit barrier](FIXED_BANKS.md) rules out indefinitely reusing a finite set of strong-component types in these two charts, even with unbounded ordinal composition.

The remaining exact inverse condition is geometric as well as arithmetic: find a changing actual module, show that the physical return exposes another eligible trailing block, and show that the right target-directed class of states has such a return without assuming the full conjecture. The one-time replenishment and unbounded ordinal-migration circuit answer different pieces. They cannot simply be concatenated without their different entry coordinates being verified.
