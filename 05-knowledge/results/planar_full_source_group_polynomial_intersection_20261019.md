# Polynomial intersection of the full source Hamiltonian-flow group

2026-10-19. **PROVED FROM CITED INPUT.**
The polynomial intersection is exactly the source polynomial shear group.
Every nonidentity shear has an explicit three-factor realization using
x-dependent polynomial Hamiltonians, with every chosen factor rational
and individually nonpolynomial. No minimal-factor or historical-novelty
claim is made.

## 1. Statement and conventions

Let K be a characteristic-zero field and let

    𝒜=K[x][[t]],   {a,b}=a_x b_t−a_t b_x,   δ_H={−,H}.

For a polynomial source Hamiltonian

    H=c+t²R(x,t),   c∈K,   R∈K[x,t],

its scalar-time source flow is the continuous function automorphism
`E_(H,λ)=exp(λδ_H)` of 𝒜. Its point-map notation is
`φ_(H,λ)=(E_(H,λ)x,E_(H,λ)t)`. A point map is called polynomial when
both of these coordinates belong to `K[x,t]` in the original source
coordinates.

Let Γ_x be the group of finite words generated by all these flows with
`H_x≠0` and `λ∈K`. No degree or Newton-support bound is imposed on H.
Then

    Γ_x ∩ {polynomial source maps}
       = {S_g=(x+g(t),t) : g∈tK[t]}.                 (1)

For every nonzero `g∈tK[t]`, choose the unique polynomial `f∈t²K[t]`
with `f′=g`, and define

    J=x³t²,   D=1−x²t,   H_g=f(tD³).               (2)

There is a three-factor realization of S_g, in chronological point-map
order:

    J at time −1, then H_g at time +1,
    then J at time +1.                              (3)

Both J and H_g are x-dependent polynomial source Hamiltonians. Each of
the three point maps in (3) is rational, regular in the source t-adic
completion, and nonpolynomial. The case `g=0` is the identity, already
represented by the empty word; no assertion of three nontrivial factors
is needed in that case.

Function-automorphism products act on the rightmost function first. If
point maps `φ_1,…,φ_r` are applied chronologically in that order, their
pullback is the function product `E_1⋯E_r`, and their final point map is
`φ_r∘⋯∘φ_1`. This convention is used throughout.

## 2. Source convergence and the universal polynomial upper bound

For any allowed H,

    H_t∈tK[x,t],   H_x∈t²K[x,t],
    δ_H(t^m 𝒜)⊂t^(m+1)𝒜   for every m≥0.           (4)

The exponential series is therefore coefficientwise finite modulo every
t-power, for every scalar λ. It defines a continuous automorphism of 𝒜
with inverse `E_(H,−λ)`. The Hamiltonian derivation is a derivation of
the Poisson bracket, so its exponential preserves that bracket. Every
required coefficient involves only finitely many terms and can be checked
after a sufficiently high finite t-adic truncation; the identities then
hold in the inverse limit.

Consequently each generator, and every finite word in them, has
coordinates `(X,T)` satisfying

    X≡x mod t,   T≡t mod t²,   {X,T}=1.            (5)

The congruences are preserved by composition and inversion. They also
ensure that substitution of arbitrary source series is well-defined:
the coefficient of any fixed t-power receives only finitely many terms,
and every such coefficient is a polynomial in x.

Suppose now that X and T are polynomials. Over an algebraic closure K̄,
(5) is a polynomial Keller map fixing the line `t=0` pointwise. By
Lemma 2.3 of Janusz Gwoździewicz,
[*Injectivity on one line*](https://arxiv.org/pdf/alg-geom/9305008),
it is a polynomial automorphism. That lemma is stated over arbitrary
algebraically closed characteristic-zero fields, so it applies after
this scalar extension.

The image T of the irreducible coordinate t under that polynomial-ring
automorphism is irreducible in `K̄[x,t]`. Since t divides T by (5),
`T=αt` with `α∈K̄*`. Its first source jet forces `α=1`. Therefore

    T=t,   1={X,t}=X_x,

and characteristic zero gives `X=x+g(t)` for `g∈K[t]`; the restriction
at `t=0` gives `g(0)=0`. This proves the inclusion from left to right
in (1). It uses only the source jets and Jacobian condition, so it
applies to arbitrary finite words, including words whose individual
factors have nonrational formal coordinates. No descent assertion for
the inverse is required: the displayed coordinates themselves give the
shear and its inverse over K.

## 3. An exact rational source flow

For `J=x³t²`, put `u=x²t`. Direct calculation gives

    δ_J x=2xu,   δ_J t=−3tu,   δ_J u=u².

Its scalar-time flow has the exact rational formula

    a_s(x,t)=(x/(1−su)², t(1−su)³).                 (6)

Indeed its initial value is `(x,t)`, and differentiating in s verifies
the three equations. Its Taylor series at `t=0` is the unique source
exponential: each coefficient is polynomial in s, and the derivation
in (4) raises t-order. In particular, setting an arbitrary `s∈K` in
(6) agrees with `exp(sδ_J)`, without analytic convergence or a branch
choice.

The denominator `1−su` has constant t-coefficient 1. The formula
also gives `u↦u/(1−su)` and the group law
`a_r∘a_s=a_(r+s)`, so `a_s^−1=a_(−s)`.
Write `A=exp(δ_J)` for the time-one function automorphism and `a=a_1`
for its point map. Thus

    A(x)=x/D²,   A(t)=tD³,
    A^−1=exp(−δ_J).                                (7)

For `s≠0`, the rational function `x/(1−su)²` is not polynomial:
its numerator is coprime to its nonconstant denominator. This already
checks that both outside factors in (3) are individually nonpolynomial.

## 4. Polynomial conjugate Hamiltonian and exact cancellation

Fix `g∈tK[t]`, and let `f∈t²K[t]` satisfy `f′=g`. Characteristic zero
permits the coefficientwise integration. The function flow of f is

    S=exp(δ_f),   S(x)=x+g(t),   S(t)=t.

Although A is not a polynomial automorphism, its t-coordinate in (7)
is polynomial. Therefore

    A(f(t))=f(tD³)=H_g∈t²K[x,t].                   (8)

Furthermore,

    (H_g)_x=−6xt²D² g(tD³).                         (9)

For `g≠0`, the last factor is nonzero: since `tD³=t+O(t²)`, substitution
preserves the first nonzero t-term of g. Thus H_g is genuinely
x-dependent, not one of the polynomial-shear generators that were
excluded from the definition of Γ_x.

Poisson preservation by A gives, for every `h∈𝒜`,

    A δ_f A^−1(h)
       =A{A^−1h,f}={h,A(f)}=δ_(H_g)(h).

Conjugation commutes with the convergent source exponential. Hence,
writing `B=exp(δ_(H_g))`,

    B=A S A^−1,   S=A^−1 B A.                       (10)

The second identity is exactly the pullback product for the chronological
point maps `a^−1`, then the H_g time-one flow, then `a`. Thus it proves
(3), with the displayed signs and order. In point-map notation the
middle flow is `b=a^−1∘S_g∘a`, and the final composition is

    a∘b∘a^−1=S_g.                                  (11)

All three factors are source flows of polynomials in the allowed class.
This proves the opposite inclusion in (1).

## 5. Explicit rational middle factor and its nonpolynomiality

The middle factor in (10) is rational because it is a rational conjugate
of a polynomial shear. More explicitly, for any scalar s put

    v=tD³,
    P_s=x+sD²g(v),
    Q_s=D+tP_s²=1+t(P_s²−x²).

The entire scalar-time flow of H_g is

    b_s=φ_(H_g,s)=(P_s/Q_s², tQ_s³).                (12)

To derive it, apply a first, obtaining `(x/D²,v)`; the shear at time s
then gives `(P_s/D²,v)`. In the last application of `a^−1`, its
factor `1+X²T` is `Q_s/D`. This gives (12). Equivalently,
`exp(sδ_(H_g))=A exp(sδ_f) A^−1`. The identity at `s=0` has
`P_0=x,Q_0=1`, as required.

Since `g(v)∈tK[x,t]`, we have `P_s≡x mod t` and `Q_s≡1 mod t²`.
Thus the rational denominator in (12) is a source unit, and the formula
belongs to the original t-adic completion.

Assume `s≠0` and `g≠0`. In the UFD `K[x,t]`,

    gcd(P_s,Q_s)=gcd(P_s,D)=gcd(x,D)=1.              (13)

Here the first equality uses `Q_s=D+tP_s²`; the second uses
`P_s=x+sD²g(v)`; and `D+tx²=1` proves the last coprimality. Moreover
Q_s is nonconstant. If it were constant, its value at `t=0` would make
it 1, whence `(P_s−x)(P_s+x)=0`. The congruence `P_s≡x mod t` and
characteristic zero rule out `P_s=−x`. The alternative `P_s=x` would
force `sD²g(v)=0`, also impossible. Thus (13) proves that `P_s/Q_s²`
is nonpolynomial. In particular the middle factor at `s=1` is
individually nonpolynomial.

This direct argument complements the general single-flow theorem of
[packet 129](planar_single_hamiltonian_polynomiality_20261019.md), which
also applies by (8) and (9). Notice the coordinate-level distinction:
all three factors chosen in (3) have polynomial second coordinate, but
nonpolynomial first coordinate. The theorem asserts that the pair is
nonpolynomial; it does not require both coordinates to be nonpolynomial.

For the explicit example `g=t`,

    f=t²/2,   H_g=t²(1−x²t)^6/2,
    P_1=x+tD^5,   Q_1=1+2xt²D^5+t³D^10.

The chronological word with Hamiltonians `J,H_g,J` at times `−1,+1,+1`
is exactly `(x+t,t)`.

## 6. Relation to the narrower groups and scope

The family of
[packet 130](planar_affine_x_rational_word_rigidity_20261019.md) contains
only Hamiltonians

    H=axt²+f(t),   a≠0,   f∈t²K[t].

Its finite-word polynomial intersection is only the identity. The
source closure studied in
[packet 131](planar_source_closure_formal_shears_20261019.md) nevertheless
contains every shear in (1). The present result enlarges the allowed
Hamiltonian class: the cubic-x polynomial J and its conjugate H_g lie
outside the packet-130 family. Every source polynomial shear is then
attained by a finite word, with no passage to a closure. Thus the
packet-130 principal-part obstruction is specific to its generating
family and cannot be imposed on the full group Γ_x.

There is also a useful exact consequence. Let Γ_all be the finite-word
group obtained by allowing every polynomial `c+t²R(x,t)`, including
x-independent Hamiltonians. Each x-independent generator is a source
polynomial shear and hence already belongs to Γ_x by (3), or is the
identity. Therefore

    Γ_x=Γ_all.                                      (14)

In words, excluding all individually polynomial generators does not
change the full generated group.

The theorem classifies the polynomial intersection of this specified
source-flow group, not the group itself. It neither identifies a
joint-boundary completion nor resolves the planar Jacobian conjecture:
the fixed-line condition is essential in the cited upper-bound input.
There is no claim that three is the minimal number of factors, and no
historical-novelty claim.
