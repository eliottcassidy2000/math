# Artin–Schreier routing and exact critical periods in characteristic two

2026-10-20. Elementary finite-field deductions from the bounded-routing theorem. The finite-field and quadratic-critical-period facts below are classical mechanisms; the contribution here is an explicit basis-free routing formulation and its link to the session's circulant construction. This does not bound rational cycles in characteristic zero.

## 1. The same derivative without choosing a normal basis

Let K=F_(2^m), let sigma(a)=a², and let D(a)=a²+a. Quotient K by the cyclic Galois group generated by sigma. These orbits are the monic irreducible polynomials over F_2 whose degrees divide m, including X for the zero orbit. D commutes with sigma and its kernel is {0,1}.

For b of exact degree d over F_2, put t=Tr_(F_(2^d)/F_2)(b). The equation a²+a=b has two roots in F_(2^d) if t=0, and otherwise two conjugate roots in F_(2^(2d)) of exact degree 2d. This follows directly from cumulative Frobenius addition: any root satisfies a^(2^d)+a=t. If t=0 it lies in F_(2^d); its degree cannot be smaller than d because b=a²+a. If t=1 it is fixed by sigma^(2d) and not sigma^d; its degree is a multiple of d dividing 2d, hence 2d.

Thus the orbit graph has:

- indegree zero exactly when the ambient trace Tr_(K/F_2)(b)=1;
- indegree two exactly when the minimal-field trace t=0;
- indegree one when t=1 and m/d is even.

In the indegree-two case the roots cannot be Galois conjugate: a conjugacy between them would stabilize b, so its exponent would be divisible by d, which already fixes each root. In the indegree-one case sigma^d interchanges the roots. These statements include b=0 and b=1 with their ordinary degree-one minimal polynomials.

## 2. An intrinsic stable-space test

Write m=e u, where e=2^{v_2(m)} and u is odd. Let L=F_(2^e), the unique subfield of K of that size. Then

    W = ker Tr_(K/L) = im(sigma^e + I) = im D^e.

Indeed Tr_(K/L) telescopes to zero on the image of sigma^e+I. The latter has kernel L and hence image dimension m-e over F_2. The relative trace is surjective because it is the identity on L (the extension degree u is odd), so its kernel has the same dimension. This proves equality. D preserves W and is invertible on W because its only nonzero kernel element 1 has relative trace 1. Thus W is exactly the stable Fitting subspace and every orbit reaches it within e steps.

At a branch in W, cut the unique incoming orbit outside W. At any other branch, cut the predecessor whose minimal polynomial has the larger binary coefficient mask. Follow a source until its next edge is cut and return the edge's target. The general routing theorem proves this is a bijection

    irreducible-polynomial orbits in K with ambient trace one
       ↔ irreducible-polynomial orbits in K with minimal-field trace zero.

The inverse starts with the designated cut predecessor and follows unique retained predecessors back to a source. Both traverse at most e edges. Every derivative step either preserves the exact field degree or halves it, so the odd part of the degree is preserved.

Unlike the necklace implementation, this cut rule compares minimal polynomials rather than rotations in a chosen normal basis. It is therefore intrinsic once the simple ordering of binary polynomials is specified. One may implement it with any finite-field presentation: field isomorphisms preserve the relative trace, derivative, and minimal polynomials. It need not coincide with the lexicographic necklace matching under every chosen normal basis.

The resulting irreducible count identity is

    sum_(d|m, m/d odd) I_1(d) = sum_(d|m) I_0(d),

where I_t(d) counts monic irreducibles of degree d and trace t, including I_0(1)=I_1(1)=1. Since odd degree is preserved, the same construction restricts for every odd r and a>=0 to a bijection from trace-one irreducibles of degree 2^a r onto the disjoint union of trace-zero irreducibles of degrees r,2r,...,2^a r (take m=2^a r). Thus I_1(2^a r)=sum_(j=0)^a I_0(2^j r). Here trace means the coefficient of X^(d-1), equivalently the sum of conjugate roots. The bijection proves the identity with a preserved odd-degree class, not merely a numerical equality.

## 3. Quadratic critical periods are encoded by minimal degree and trace

In characteristic two iterate f_c(x)=x²+c from x=0. Its nth value is

    P_n(c)=c+c²+...+c^(2^(n-1)).

This follows by induction, and

    P_n(c)²+P_n(c)=c^(2^n)+c.

If P_n(c)=0 and c has exact field degree d, then c^(2^n)=c and hence d divides n. Conversely for n=kd,

    P_n(c)=(k mod 2) Tr_(F_(2^d)/F_2)(c).

Therefore the least positive return time of the critical point 0 is exactly

    d, if the minimal-field trace is zero;
    2d, if the minimal-field trace is one.

This proof covers c=0 (period one) and c=1 (period two). It shows why a trace-zero primitive degree-n factor and a trace-one primitive degree-n/2 factor occur together in the characteristic-two critical-period dictionary. It does not assert that all trace-zero parameters of degree dividing m have critical period exactly m.

The polynomial P_n has formal derivative one, so all its roots are simple. Exact critical-period factors can therefore be separated by the ordinary divisor-period decomposition. The spectral/necklace routing above gives a concrete way to move between trace sectors while controlling the dyadic part of the degree.

## 4. Source boundary and relation to real dynamics

Baker–Chen–Li–Qian, [Necklaces, permutations, and periodic critical orbits for quadratic polynomials](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.12924v2), Theorem A and §2.2, relates primitive necklaces, trace-refined irreducible polynomials and real critical parameters using normal bases and kneading theory. The trace-preserving normal-basis correspondence is attributed there to Reutenauer. The present elementary derivation is consistent with that source; no literature-priority claim is made.

The companion prime-circulant packet composes its source necklaces with their real-parameter bijection. The current note instead stays in characteristic two and gives the explicit relative-trace/Fitting cut rule. A characteristic-two period calculation cannot be used to rule out rational cycles of z²+c over Q. In particular, it says nothing that settles the general rational-period conjecture, Collatz, or the planar Jacobian conjecture.
