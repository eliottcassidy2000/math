## 2. The defect identity and the object-relative pronormal lemma (THM-4728, statements 1-2)

For odd `H <= S_n`, `N = N_(S_n)(H)`, and a type `x`, let `m_x(H)` be the number of `N`-orbits on the `H`-fixed labelled objects of type `x` (= `Aut(x)`-classes of `S_n`-conjugates of `H` in `Aut x`), and `m^ev_X` the number of those orbits on which `sgn_X` (the edge-reversal character) is trivial on `N cap Aut X`. A *witness* is a sign-odd graph type with `m^ev_X >= 1`. THM-4721 Theorem R (`Gamma = N`) says `#N`-orbits on `Fix_T(H) = #N`-orbits on the `N`-even `H`-fixed graphs; grouping by type,

`tau_T(H) - tau_E(H) = W(H) + E_even(H) + E_wit(H) - E_T(H)`,

with `W` the number of witnesses and `E_T, E_even, E_wit` the sums of `m - 1` over tournament types, sign-even graph types and witnesses. So the defect is the witness count corrected by multiplicity excesses, and vanishes for multiplicity-free `H` iff there is no witness. This is the formula HYP-9341 (b) asks for; Proposition Q of THM-4721 is its weighted form; the Codex census packet derived it the same day as its equation (2) from the orbits of `Aut X` and of its sign kernel on the embedded copies of `H` (`a_X = m_X`, `a_X^+ - a_X = m^ev_X`, `Z = W + E_wit`, `F_T = E_T`, `F_E = E_even`).

**Pronormal Frattini lemma.** If `H <= Aut X` is pronormal in `Aut X` and `X` is sign-odd, then some odd-sign automorphism normalises `H`: for odd-sign `a`, `H` and `H^a` lie in the sign kernel `B`, pronormality gives `c in <H, H^a> <= B` with `c H^a c^(-1) = H`, and `ca in N(H)` has sign `-1`. Hence every witness has `H` non-pronormal in `Aut X`. (The session-10 pronormal-quotient theorem uses pronormality in `S_n`; the Frattini step only needs it in `Aut X`; the Codex audit note states the exact object-relative condition, transitivity of the sign kernel on the copies of `H`, of which pronormality in `Aut X` is a sufficient case. For `K_(5,5)` and `C15`, `H = C5(A) x C3(B)` and its conjugate under the part swap, `C3(A) x C5(B)`, are not conjugate inside `S_5 x S_5`, so `H` is not pronormal in `Aut K_(5,5) = S_5 wr S_2`.) Sylow subgroups of `Aut X` are pronormal, which is why `p`-groups behave, through the exact-Sylow-sector matching of the packet; a `p`-subgroup itself need not be pronormal in `Aut X` (`C3` in `S_6`), so the lemma does not by itself reprove the `p`-group case.

## 4. The affine-rule dichotomy: an independent reproduction of the Codex ideal obstruction (THM-4724 withdrawn)

This section was written as a theorem (THM-4724) before the origin re-check; the Codex packet `affine_circulant_ideal_obstruction_20261020`, pushed concurrently, proves the same dichotomy by the same ideal argument and goes further (complete normal form `h(R) Delta`, admissibility by nilpotent height, sharp coverage law, exhaustive verification for `m <= 12`). The draft is kept here as an independent reproduction with its own finite controls, and the ID was released (mac-mini reserved THM-4724 for other content the same day).

Write `m = (p-1)/2` and read symmetric connection sets as words in `R_m = F_2[x]/(x^m - 1)` (rotation = multiplication by `x`; multiplier types = rotation classes = isomorphism types, by Turner and the Sylow argument of THM-4721). **Valuation criterion (= the packet's Lemma 5).** A word `h` of primitive period `d` is sign-even iff `v_(x+1)(h) >= 2^(v_2 m) - 2^(v_2 d) + 1` (the root word `r` has `h = r (x^m-1)/(x^d-1)`, `v_(x+1)(x^k - 1) = 2^(v_2 k)`, and sign-even is `wt(r)` even, i.e. `(x+1) | r`). Checked exhaustively for `m <= 14` and on random words to `m = 40` (`circulant_affine_rule_dichotomy_20261019.out`).

**Dichotomy (= the packet's Theorems 3-4).** An `F_2`-affine multiplier-equivariant map from skew sets to symmetric sets, with image inside the sign-even sets and inducing a bijection of types, exists iff `p = 3 mod 4` or `p = 5`. *Proof.* The image of such a map is a rotation-invariant affine subspace of `R_m` containing `0` (the empty graph's type must be hit), hence an ideal `I`; by type-surjectivity and rotation-invariance `I` contains every sign-even word, in particular `1 + x` (primitive, weight `2`, `m >= 3`), hence the ideal `(1 + x)` of all even-weight words, hence for `m` even the all-ones word, which has even weight `m` and is sign-odd (root weight `1`, repetition `m` even): contradiction. For `m` odd, `(1 + x)` is exactly the sign-even set (root parity equals total parity when `m/d` is odd), realised by `Phi_A(f) = f * (delta_1 + 1_H)` (Theorem A of the generator-free packet, read as a convolution) and by `Phi_a(f) = f + f o a`, `a` any generator of the squares (both are instances of the packet's normal form with `gcd(h, x^m - 1) = x + 1`); `m = 2` is the zero rule. ∎ Exhaustive search over all cosets of all ideals of `R_m` for `p <= 31` (beyond the packet's `m <= 12`): admissible ideals exactly `(1 + x)` for `p = 7, 11, 19, 23, 31`, the zero ideal for `p = 5`, none for `p = 13, 17, 29`; `Phi_A` and `Phi_a` are type bijections for `p = 7..31`.
