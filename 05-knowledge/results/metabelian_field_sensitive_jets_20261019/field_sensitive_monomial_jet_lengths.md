# The scalar field changes the shortest Hamiltonian word

19 October 2026. **Self-contained sharp formulas, characteristic zero.** This extends the cubic-jet calculation for the two Hamiltonians G=x²y and H=x³ to every higher monomial vertical shear. The upper bounds over real fields are rational constructions; over algebraically closed fields they use roots of unity. No historical-novelty claim is made.

## 1. The generating pair and the target

For a characteristic-zero field K, let Γ_K be the group generated by all scalar-time maps

    M_t(x,y)=(x/(1−tx),y(1−tx)²),
    T_c(x,y)=(x,y+cx²),                       t,c∈K.

These are the flows of x²y and x³, with a scalar rescaling for the second. Each nonidentity M_t or T_c counts as one factor. Coefficients are discrete, and jet agreement means exact equality modulo (x,y)^(N+1).

Fix an integer r≥4 and the target shear

    S_r(x,y)=(x,y+x^r).

For N<r its N-jet is the identity, so the minimum word length is zero. Henceforth N≥r, and write

    k=r−2≥2,        D=N−2≥k,        b_j=binom(j+3,3).

For a word matching this jet, let p be the number of distinct nonzero sites t appearing with nonzero coefficient in its unique vertical partial-fraction normal form. These are actual fourth-order poles at x=1/t. Let p_min(K;k,D) be the minimum possible p, and let ℓ_min(K;k,D) be the minimum word length.

## 2. Sharp answers

### Real scalar fields

For every subfield K of R, including Q and R,

    p_min(K;k,D)=max(k,D−1),

    ℓ_min(K;k,D)=2k+2       if D=k,
                 2D        if D≥k+1.                  (1)

Equivalently, the minimum length is 2r−2 for N=r and 2N−4 for N≥r+1. All upper bounds can be attained with rational scalar times. The second regime includes N=r+1, whose length is again 2r−2.

### Algebraically closed scalar fields

For every algebraically closed characteristic-zero K,

    p_min(K;k,D)=max(k,D−k+1),

    ℓ_min(K;k,D)=2k+2                 if k≤D≤2k−1,
                 2(D−k+1)+1          if D≥2k.          (2)

In terms of r,N, the length is 2r−2 for r≤N≤2r−3, and 2N−2r+3 for N≥2r−2. The constructions only need the relevant p-th roots of unity, so they also work over any characteristic-zero field containing those roots, without requiring algebraic closure of the whole field.

For all characteristic-zero fields, the lower bound

    p_min(K;k,D)≥max(k,D−k+1)                          (3)

holds. A coefficient-gap criterion below specifies the extra field dependence when neither displayed class applies.

The minimum reduced x-denominator degree is exactly 4p_min in every case covered by (1) and (2). The two scalar-field classes can therefore differ both in shortest word length and in the minimum number of rational poles needed for the same formal jet.

## 3. Normal form, moments, and exact word cost

The [companion two-flow calculation](../metabelian_sharp_jet_length_20261019/metabelian_word_closure_and_sharp_jet_length.md) gives the canonical coordinates z=1/x,w=−yx². There M_t translates z by −t, while T_c adds −c/z⁴ to w. Thus every finite word has first coordinate x/(1−ax), where a is the total M-time. For N≥2, matching the first coordinate x even through degree two forces a=0.

Consequently every word considered here is a vertical shear with unique correction

    f(x)=c_0x²+Σ_(i=1)^p c_i x²/(1−t_i x)⁴,           (4)

where the t_i are distinct nonzero elements of K and c_i≠0. The coefficient c_0 may vanish. Conversely, every finite correction (4) is an exact Γ_K word: visit the accumulated M-times t_1,...,t_p, insert T_(c_i) at the corresponding visits, and return to zero. Insert T_(c_0) at zero if needed.

For p≥1 the exact word length of the specific shear (4) is

    2p+1+1_(c_0≠0).                                  (5)

Indeed, its p nonzero sites require at least p T factors and at least p+1 M factors to start at zero, visit them, and return. A nonzero site-zero coefficient requires a further T factor at zero. The chronological visit construction attains those bounds. Partial-fraction uniqueness rules out generating a missing site-zero coefficient by cancellations among other sites.

Expanding the kernels gives

    x²/(1−tx)⁴=Σ_(j≥0) b_j t^j x^(j+2).

Jet matching in (4) is therefore equivalent to

    L(P)=[T^k]P(T)/b_k       for every deg P≤D,        (6)

where

    L(P)=c_0P(0)+Σ_i c_iP(t_i).

This is the kth derivative functional at zero, divided by k!b_k. The distinction between a pole-free coefficient c_0 and the nonzero pole sites is essential for the factor count.

## 4. Universal lower bounds and the coefficient-gap criterion

Let

    Q(T)=∏_(i=1)^p(T−t_i)=Σ_(i=0)^p q_i T^i,
    q_p=1,     q_0≠0.

If p<k, then T^(k−p)Q(T) has degree k≤D and vanishes at every supported site, including zero. Its coefficient of T^k is 1, contradicting (6). Hence p≥k.

If p≤D−k, then T^kQ(T) also has degree at most D and vanishes at every supported site. Its coefficient of T^k is q_0≠0, another contradiction. Thus p≥D−k+1. This proves (3).

More generally, for every j≥1 with p+j≤D, applying (6) to T^jQ yields

    q_(k−j)=0,                                      (7)

with coefficients outside 0,...,p interpreted as zero. Applying (6) to Q itself, when p≤D, gives

    c_0=q_k/(b_k q_0).                              (8)

For k≤p≤D these are also sufficient for a representation supported on the chosen sites 0,t_1,...,t_p, allowing some weights to be zero. To prove this, first interpolate the functional on polynomials of degree at most p using those p+1 distinct nodes. Divide any polynomial of degree at most D by TQ(T). Its remainder has degree at most p; conditions (7) make the target functional vanish on every allowed multiple of TQ. This extends the interpolated identity to all degree-D polynomials.

The weights in this criterion are explicitly

    c_0=q_k/(b_k q_0),
    c_i=(1/b_k)[T^k] { TQ(T)/(t_i(T−t_i)Q′(t_i)) }.
                                                               (9)

Thus, for a general characteristic-zero K, the optimization reduces to finding a squarefree polynomial split over K, with nonzero roots and the specified coefficient gap. Requiring c_0=0 adds the condition q_k=0. In the minimal constructions below no nonzero-site weight vanishes, as can be seen explicitly or from the already proved pole lower bound.

## 5. The real-root obstruction

Assume all sites lie in R. Then Q has distinct real roots, and every derivative of Q that has positive degree also has distinct real roots: Rolle's theorem provides a root in each successive open gap, accounting for the full degree and giving strict interlacing.

If D−p≥2, equations (7) force

    q_(k−1)=q_(k−2)=0.

Since p≥k and k≥2, these equalities say Q^(k−2)(0)=0 and Q^(k−1)(0)=0. The derivative Q^(k−2) would have a repeated root at zero, contradicting strict interlacing. Therefore p≥D−1. Together with p≥k, this proves the real pole lower bound in (1).

There is a second, separate use of the same obstruction. If p=D−1 and D≥k+1, equation (7) forces q_(k−1)=0. If c_0 vanished, equation (8) would force q_k=0 as well. Then Q^(k−1) would have a repeated root at zero. Hence every pole-minimal real approximant in this regime has c_0≠0.

When D=k and p=k, the leading coefficient q_k is 1, so (8) again forces c_0≠0. Formula (5) now gives the claimed lower word lengths:

- D=k: at least 2k+2;
- D≥k+1: at least 2(D−1)+2=2D.

Using an additional nonzero site cannot improve these bounds: even with c_0=0 it costs at least two extra site/visit factors while saving at most one site-zero factor.

## 6. Rational real constructions

Put p=max(k,D−1). Start with the p−1 positive rational roots 1,...,p−1 and write

    A(T)=∏_(i=1)^(p−1)(T−i)=Σ_j a_j T^j.

Both a_(k−2) and a_(k−1) are nonzero and have opposite signs. Define

    t_p=a_(k−2)/a_(k−1)<0,
    Q(T)=(T−t_p)A(T).                               (10)

All p roots are distinct, nonzero, and rational. By construction q_(k−1)=0. The weights (9) match the desired jet: when D=k, degree-p interpolation suffices; when D≥k+1, we have p=D−1 and the single extra condition (7) is exactly q_(k−1)=0.

Strict interlacing also gives q_k≠0 and q_(k−2)≠0. Therefore c_0≠0. No other weight can vanish, because that would yield a representation on fewer nonzero sites than the real lower bound. The visit construction has precisely 2p+2 factors, attaining (1) over Q and hence over every real subfield.

For D≥k+1 the agreement has exact order N: applying the supported-node functional to T²Q shows that the next moment is −q_(k−2)/b_k. Thus

    f(x)=x^r−[b_(D+1)q_(k−2)/b_k]x^(N+1)
                  +O(x^(N+2)),                     (11)

and the displayed error coefficient is nonzero. When D=k, construction (10) actually matches one further degree, consistently with the equal minimum lengths at N=r and N=r+1.

## 7. Cyclotomic constructions and the complex optimum

Let p=max(k,D−k+1) and suppose K contains all p-th roots of unity. Let the nonzero sites t_i run over those roots, and set

    c_i=t_i^(−k)/(p b_k),
    c_0=−1/b_k     if p=k,
    c_0=0          if p>k.                           (12)

The elementary root-sum identity

    Σ_(t^p=1) t^m = p if p divides m, and 0 otherwise

shows that the moment Σ_i c_i t_i^j is 1/b_k exactly when j≡k modulo p, and is zero otherwise.

If p=k, the only such indices from 0 through D≤2k−1 are 0 and k. The site-zero coefficient in (12) cancels index zero. If p>k, then p=D−k+1 and the only index from 0 through D is k itself. This proves the desired jet matching and attains the universal pole lower bound.

For p=k, c_0 is necessarily nonzero for every pole-minimal representation, because q_k=1. Thus its minimum length is 2k+2; a representation with more sites cannot be shorter. For p>k the construction has c_0=0, attaining the universal walk lower bound 2p+1. This proves (2).

These constructions also have explicit first errors. For p=k the first error occurs at degree 2k+2=2r−2, with coefficient b_(2k)/b_k. For p>k it occurs at degree p+k+2=N+1, with coefficient b_(p+k)/b_k. No analytic use of complex numbers occurs: the proof is algebraic over the field generated by the relevant roots of unity.

## 8. Smallest length separation

Take r=4 and N=6, so k=2,D=4. Both field classes require three nonzero sites, but:

    real scalar times:       minimum length 8;
    algebraically closed:    minimum length 7.

The rational real construction has sites 1,2,−2/3 and

    c_0=−7/40,
    (c_1,c_2,c_3)=(2/25,−1/160,81/800).

It has four T factors, including c_0, and four M factors. Its first error is −(112/15)x^7.

Over Q(ζ_3), use the three cube roots of unity a and weights c_a=a/30, with no site-zero term:

    f(x)=(x²/30) Σ_(a³=1) a/(1−ax)⁴
        =x⁴+(28/5)x⁷+O(x^10).

This has three T factors and four M factors. The root sum is exactly

    f(x)=x⁴(10+16x³+x⁶)/(10(1−x³)⁴),

so its final coordinates have rational coefficients. Nevertheless, no word of length seven using only real scalar times can realize its degree-six target jet. The field dependence concerns allowed intermediate factors, not merely the field of the final jet.

There is also an exact failure of descent for group membership: this rational shear belongs to Γ_(Q(ζ_3)) but does not belong to Γ_R at any finite word length, and therefore does not belong to Γ_Q. Its poles at the primitive cube roots of unity are actual fourth-order poles: the numerator factor 10+16x³+x⁶ takes the nonzero value 27 when x³=1. Every exact Γ_R vertical shear, by (4), has only real poles. A rational map defined over the base field can thus require a scalar extension to be generated by this fixed pair.

A pole-count separation already occurs for r=5,N=7: real scalars need four nonzero sites and ten factors, whereas cube-root sites need three nonzero sites and eight factors.

## 9. Scope, relation, and finite controls

The finite Γ_K group still has only quadratic vertical shears as polynomial members. These higher monomial shears are reached only in its point-adic closure; all finite approximants in the theorem have affine rational poles. The theorem quantifies a generation/approximation problem for this fixed Hamiltonian pair. It gives no representation theorem for arbitrary Keller maps, no new general Jacobian criterion, and no Hadwiger–Nelson chromatic bound.

The [cubic-jet companion](../metabelian_sharp_jet_length_20261019/metabelian_word_closure_and_sharp_jet_length.md) supplies the earlier k=1 boundary case: p_min=D and, except at the first nontrivial jet, length 2D+1 over every characteristic-zero field. The real-root obstruction used here specifically requires k≥2. The coefficient-gap, root-sum, and strict-interlacing proofs are given in full above; no external classification theorem is needed.
