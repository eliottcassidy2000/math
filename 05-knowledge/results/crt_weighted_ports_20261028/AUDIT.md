## Proof checks

- C1 tests two legal words on every nonzero proper-order quotient. It proves
  necessity for descended reflection, and a separate Frobenius calculation
  proves the F4 converse. It does not exclude all primitive-order factors.
- C2 uses actual comaximal ideals, not integer-cardinality CRT alone. The
  adaptive lower bound counts leaves of a p-valued query tree, with the base
  coordinate already known. It does not establish three-probe sufficiency.
- Finite behavioral minimality uses translations **and** multiplication by
  phi to turn a compatible subgroup into an ideal. The smaller {+1,R} menu
  is credited to Session94 where established.
- Marking loops retain full permutations, use actual odd roots, and count
  three edges for each triangle. The root stays fixed under the chosen
  conjugator. Products in the scripts are written right to left; to execute
  the returned factor list, traverse its triangles in reverse list order.
  Constructing large endpoint marks is not given unit bit cost.
- W1-W2 average group elements uniformly. W4-W5 instead sum original source
  weights over distinct joint-orbit tuples. The two operations are not conflated.
- E1 assumes deterministic source weights independent of the sampled group.
  Adaptive source selection from that same randomness needs a new conditional
  argument. Signed extensions satisfy the variance identity but not the
  positive mass lower bound unless its hypotheses are met.
- E1a checks the common-prefix convention including the branching node.
  A terminal sibling difference is an actual null vector; refinement can
  activate it, so a present-depth kernel quotient is not future-closed.
- E2 uses the inherited exact correlated division clock. Its optimality is
  only within the span of a constant and that terminal clock, not all nonlinear
  observations. E9 and the explicit equal-clock hostile exhibit both sides.
- E8 is a uniform legal-word calculation. The golden reader's source-specific
  numerator, rational denominator, odd charge and integer clock remain required
  for an arithmetic consequence.
