# The factorial lock: what changing a reader cannot recover at unit scale

**All orders.** Take two tournaments B,B' of the same order n. Their
ordered path-cover polynomials have difference f=P_B−P_B'. Suppose
that f(1)=...=f(r)=0. In a context built by any finite succession of

1. ordinal sum with any fixed tournament on either side;
2. cyclic substitution C3[1,A,X] with a fixed nonempty A and current X,

the resulting two path counts differ by a multiple of
[
 M_{n,r} = 2 r! (2r+1)!\times
 \begin{cases} 1& n\text{ odd},\\ r+1& n\text{ even}.\end{cases}
]
Here “fixed” means common to the two arms at that stage, not common
across stages. In (2) A can change at every stage. Interchanging A
and X gives the same polynomial. The result also includes a terminal
linear read of the first two evaluations with integer coefficients,
such as any of the six published 67/69 readouts applied to its
standard cyclic source, because for r>=1 the march from arity 2 to
arity r+1 still uses the factors 2,...,r. The claim says nothing
about readers that are not generated by these operations, and does
not constrain the difference of two unrelated contexts.

## Actual operations and two independent factorials

Let P_X(t) count ordered decompositions of X into t directed paths,
including empty slots. By the odd-cycle packing expansion, each
packing has coefficient 2 to the power of its number of cycles and
one factor t for each uncovered vertex or cycle. Hence P_X is monic,
has no constant term for nonempty X, and has only exponents of the
same parity as its order. Its off-leading coefficients are even.
In an ordinal sum each colored path crosses the seam at most once,
so the two polynomials multiply.

For F_A(X)=C3[1,A,X] orient the blocks cyclically. An odd cycle
through the singleton takes one nonempty run through each of A,X;
off that cycle the two remaining blocks form an ordinal sum. Mark
the two chosen runs and enforce even total run size by the sign
sum 1+(-1)^(sum of their sizes). In the unsigned sum each odd
packing block chooses one of t ordinary slots or the run's color;
in the signed sum that last choice has weight −1. Remove the empty
run from each choice. This proves the physical operator
[
 L_A g(t)=t\{P_A(t)g(t)
       +[P_A(t+1)-P_A(t)][g(t+1)-g(t)]
       +[P_A(t-1)-P_A(t)][g(t-1)-g(t)]\}.
]
One arity step is therefore the most a wrap can reveal, regardless
of its order or choice of module A. In expanding a composition
beginning at arity 1, each walk that reaches arity j>=r+1 has
an up-step across every level i→i+1 for 1<=i<=r. At that step
its weight is i times an integer. Thus every contributing walk
to these j is divisible by r!, including those that later go down
and up again. All arities j≤r give zero against f. This is the
transport factorial.

The source factorial has a different origin. Because the leading
term cancels and every remaining coefficient is even, f/2 is an
integer polynomial. Its parity, its zero at 0, and the vanishing at
±1,...,±r imply by monic division
[
 f(t)\in
 \begin{cases}
 2\prod_{j=-r}^{r}(t-j)\,\mathbb Z[t]&n\text{ odd},\\
 2t\prod_{j=-r}^{r}(t-j)\,\mathbb Z[t]&n\text{ even}.
 \end{cases}
]
The product of consecutive integers is divisible by (2r+1)! at
each integer t. In the even case use the integer-valued identity
[
 t\binom{t+r}{2r+1}
 =(r+1)\left(2\binom{t+r}{2r+2}+\binom{t+r}{2r+1}\right).
]
This gives the extra factor r+1 for *every* integer evaluation, not
just t=r+1. The two factorials multiply. This is a signed path
expansion, so no positivity assumption or no-cancellation claim
is needed for the individual coefficients.

For exactly r unit wraps, only the all-up route can distinguish
the inputs; its difference is exactly r! f(r+1). More generally,
if the r wrappers are L_(A_i), indexed from outermost to innermost,
the difference equals
[
 r! f(r+1)\prod_{i=1}^{r}[P_{A_i}(i+1)-P_{A_i}(i)].
]
Thus choosing large or complex modules on the way to that first
observable difference can only multiply it. Their polynomial
coefficients are nonnegative and monic, so the factors are positive
integers. Ordinal pads also multiply the sole contributing term.
A depth-r terminal read starting at arity 2 has the corresponding
first-observation formula one wrap earlier.

## Sharp physical controls in both parities

At order five the two H=15 sources with row masks
(22,24,10,1,12) and (6,12,24,17,3) have Q=108 and 120, and their
one-wrap counts differ by 12. This is sharp for r=1, and one
ordinal singleton makes the even r=1 bound sharp as well.

For r=2 these pairs are new explicit examples. Rows use zero-based
bits and list outgoing neighborhoods.

|---|---|---|---|---|
|9|436,225,18,279,450,348,141,300,70|388,21,328,211,421,143,179,262,106|2269,37124|311991,311751|

Both common profiles are indistinguishable to any standard 67/69
reader based only on (H,Q). One unit wrap still gives equal scalar
counts. After two unit wraps they give respectively
807333 versus 806853 (gap 480), and 4265735 versus 4264295
(gap 1440). These attain the proved divisors. No minimal-order claim
is made for r=2. The general formula is sharp on the larger formal
integer-polynomial lattice by the displayed primitive product and
unit wraps; sharpness on actual tournaments beyond r=2 remains open.

## What would bypass the lock?

There are two layers to the old “same local state” intuition.
Sources can be indistinguishable until a reader accesses a genuinely
new observation. Even at the first observation, the absolute
difference can be constrained to a large sublattice. For example,
after matching H,Q, the entire common-context menu above cannot
yield a difference of 2, or even of 6, although it is sufficient
to detect every different full cover polynomial. Thus moving
from a two-bin collision to a selective third bin cannot by itself
repair a missing odd target; either vary a lower observation as
part of the repair, or leave the singleton-cycle / ordinal grammar
and use a reader with genuinely different joint contacts.
