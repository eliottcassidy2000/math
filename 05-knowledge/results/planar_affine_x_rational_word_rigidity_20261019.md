# Polynomial-only-identity theorem for affine-x Hamiltonian words

2026-10-19. **PROVED, characteristic zero.** A finite word in the source
flows below is polynomial exactly when it is the identity. The proof is
elementary: explicit rational integration and uniqueness of principal
parts. No historical novelty claim is made.

## Statement and order convention

Let K be a characteristic-zero field. For `i=1,…,r`, choose

    H_i=a_i x t²+f_i(t),
    a_i∈K*,   f_i∈t²K[t],   λ_i∈K,

and let `φ_i=exp(λ_i{−,H_i})` be the original source t-adic flow. Apply
these point maps in chronological order `φ_1`, then `φ_2`, through `φ_r`.
Equivalently, their pullback automorphisms form the product
`exp(λ_1δ_1)⋯exp(λ_rδ_r)` acting on functions.

Then every finite word is a rational source map regular as a formal
t-adic map, and

    both output coordinates are in K[x,t]
        ⇔ the word is the identity.

The Hamiltonians need not be functions of a common invariant. Negative
times and zero times are allowed, so the assertion applies to the entire
group generated by this specified family of flows.

## 1. Exact rational normal form

Make the birational canonical change of variables

    z=1/t,   w=xt²,   {z,w}=1,
    x=wz²,   t=1/z.

Put `F_i(z)=f_i(1/z)` and `ℓ_i=λ_i a_i`. The Hamiltonian becomes
`a_i w+F_i(z)`, so its differential equations are

    dz/ds=a_i,   dw/ds=−F_i′(z).

At time λ_i, its map is therefore exactly

    (z,w) ↦ (z+ℓ_i, w+[F_i(z)−F_i(z+ℓ_i)]/a_i).      (1)

This formula is the original source exponential, not a different branch.
Indeed `δ_i(1/t)=a_i`, and Taylor expansion of `F_i(z+ℓ_i)` at `z=∞`
reproduces the exponential term by term in t. The derivation raises
t-adic order on Laurent series as well as on ordinary source series.

Define cumulative shifts

    β_i=Σ_(j<i) ℓ_j,   L=Σ_i ℓ_i.

Induction using (1), in the stated chronological order, gives the full
word in normal form

    (z,w) ↦ (z+L,w+G(z)),

    G(z)=Σ_i [F_i(z+β_i)−F_i(z+β_i+ℓ_i)]/a_i.        (2)

All poles of G lie at points of K. Each `F_i` is a linear combination of
`z^(−m)` with `m≥2`. Its translated difference is `O(z^(−3))` at infinity.
Thus G is proper and `G(z)=O(z^(−3))`. Returning to the source coordinates
gives the exact rational map

    T=t/(1+Lt),
    X=x(1+Lt)²+(1/t+L)²G(1/t).                       (3)

The decay of G ensures `X≡x mod t` and `T≡t mod t²`, as required for
the source flow.

## 2. Conservation of every principal-part coefficient sum

Write

    F_i(z)=Σ_(m≥2) c_(i,m) z^(−m),

with only finitely many nonzero coefficients. By (2), the unique
partial-fraction expansion of G has the form

    G(z)=Σ_p Σ_(m≥2) C_(p,m)/(z−p)^m,

where p ranges over the finite set of actual or possible poles. Explicitly,

    C_(p,m)=Σ_i (c_(i,m)/a_i)
                 [1_(p=−β_i)−1_(p=−β_i−ℓ_i)].        (4)

Coincident poles are combined in this formula; no distinct-pole
assumption is needed. A term of order m contributes the same coefficient
with opposite signs at its two endpoint poles. Hence, separately for
every order m,

    Σ_p C_(p,m)=0.                                    (5)

These are coefficient sums, not a claim about a residue theorem for
higher-order poles. Translation preserves the individual principal-part
coefficients exactly. The uniqueness used here is ordinary rational
partial-fraction uniqueness: at each pole, multiply by its highest pole
power and evaluate, then descend through lower orders. There is no
polynomial part because G tends to zero at infinity.

## 3. Polynomiality leaves one pole, then conservation kills it

Suppose (3) is a polynomial map. If `L≠0`, its second coordinate
`t/(1+Lt)` is not polynomial, since its numerator and nonconstant
denominator are coprime. Therefore `L=0`. Equation (3) reduces to

    T=t,   X=x+t^(−2)G(1/t).

Polynomiality of X now says `t^(−2)G(1/t)=g(t)` for some `g∈K[t]`, or
equivalently

    G(z)=z^(−2)g(1/z).                                (6)

Thus G has no finite pole except possibly at 0. In the unique expansion
above, all `C_(p,m)` with `p≠0` vanish. Equation (5) then forces
`C_(0,m)=0` for every m as well. Since there is no polynomial part, G is
zero. Together with `L=0`, this makes (3) the identity.

Conversely, the identity is polynomial. This proves the theorem. ∎

## Finite identity criterion

The proof also provides an exact, finite cancellation test:

    the word is the identity
      ⇔ L=0 and C_(p,m)=0 for every pole p and order m,

with the coefficients given by (4). These conditions permit cancellations
between different Hamiltonians. Reversing the word generally changes
the cumulative shifts β_i and the individual coefficients; it does not
change the validity of the theorem or the coefficient-sum conservation.

## Scope and verification

This is a different-invariant composition result for the specific family
`H_i=a_ixt²+f_i(t)`, with nonzero a_i and `f_i∈t²K[t]`. It does not
classify arbitrary Hamiltonians, arbitrary different-invariant words,
or infinite products. It says that the pair is polynomial only in the
identity case, not that both individual coordinates must be nonpolynomial.
It gives no resolution of the planar Jacobian conjecture.

The signs in the canonical equations, source inverse transformation,
chronological composition order, coincident-pole case, properness at
infinity, and principal-part uniqueness were checked directly. No
numerical census, fixed-line theorem, or external classification is used.
