# Gold-budget dynamics, the eleven-cell Collatz carrier, and a signed Golay lift

**2026-10-19. Status: PROVED finite conjugacies and decoder limitation; proposed proof architecture remains OPEN. No general Gold Partition or Collatz proof is claimed.**

## 1. The exact carrier we start from

The inherited [heavy-child Gold reduction](gold_heavy_child_20261014j.md) says that a first comparison with minority probability x leaves its heavy child an obligation

    g(x) = (1-2x)/(1-x).

This is the required minority threshold for a second comparison, not the actual conditional probability of a specified second comparison. We study its projective extension. Its matrix and square are

    M = [[-2,1],[-1,1]], det M = -1;
    g²(x) = 3-1/x, represented by [[3,-1],[1,0]].

In particular the determinant sign distinguishes one budget step from two. Any application to a poset must separately construct the comparisons that meet the obligations. Iterating the formula alone does not construct them.

Put α=(3-√5)/2 and β=(3+√5)/2. These are the fixed points of g; αβ=1. The coordinate

    z=(x-α)/(x-β)

conjugates g to multiplication by -β and g² to multiplication by β²=φ⁴. This follows by direct substitution, or by fixing 0 and infinity and evaluating at x=infinity. Thus the golden unit already occurs in the exact two-comparison inequality, before any modular reduction.

## 2. Eleven states: an actual finite dynamical conjugacy

Work on the projective line over F_11. Choose √5=4, so α=5, β=9. Then

    z=(x-5)/(x-9),       x=(9z-5)/(z-1),
    z(gx)=2z,           z(g²x)=4z.

Delete the fixed point x=9. The remaining eleven projective budget states are exactly the affine z-line F_11. On them g has cycle partition 1+10, whereas g² has partition 1+5+5.

The inherited [THM-4592](../../01-canon/theorems/THM-4592-the-eleven-squares-fibonacci-torus-is-the-period-five-collatz-cycle-set.md) identifies the eleven points in Q ∩ Z₂ fixed by the fifth iterate of the **standard** Collatz map C (odd branch 3x+1, even branch x/2) with F_11, and C with multiplication by 4. Its carrier consists of 0, the five points of the 1/13 cycle, and the five points of the -5 cycle. Let κ be that exact inherited labeling. Then

    Ξ(x)=κ⁻¹((x-5)/(x-9))
    Ξ(g²x)=C(Ξ(x)).

This is a genuine conjugacy between specified finite systems. The map g itself is a square root of C on this finite carrier: it interchanges the two nonzero five-cycles and g⁵ reflects the labels z→-z. That reflection is not numerical negation of the rational Collatz points.

Transporting z→z+1 back to budget coordinates gives

    a(x)=(2x+7)/(x+6) mod 11.

Hence g² a g⁻²=a⁴. Equivalently g⁻² a g²=a³. The group generated by a and g² is the same affine group of order 55 as the golden-cell group and the mod-11 BS(1,3) quotient. Including g gives the full affine group of order 110, because 2 has order 10 modulo 11. The extra budget step thus asks precisely for the signed extension that was absent from the single Golay syndrome representation.

## 3. Why one budget step swaps the reciprocal Golay modules

Use the explicit construction in [golden signed relation geometry](golden_signed_relation_geometry_20261019.md). Let K=F_243, let α_11∈K have order eleven, and for v∈F_3^11 set

    S_+(v)=Σ v_i α_11^i,     S_-(v)=Σ v_i α_11^(-i).

Coordinate relabeling i→3i induces Frobenius F:z→z³ on each syndrome. Coordinate reflection i→-i interchanges the two syndromes. Since 2=-3² modulo eleven, relabeling by the one-step budget multiplier 2 acts on the paired modules by

    G(z_+,z_-)=(z_-^9,z_+^9).

Consequently G²=(F⁴,F⁴), which is the forward golden/Collatz multiplier 4, and G⁵ exchanges the two modules. The scalar coordinate Σv_i is fixed. This gives a linear representation of the full signed budget action on F_3 ⊕ K ⊕ K.

There is also a nonlinear realization on a single K. Let E₂ be the vectors of Hamming weight at most two. The ternary Golay syndrome map S_+:E₂→K is a bijection; write d for its inverse. Let J reflect coordinates and set

    J_dec(z)=S_+(Jd(z)),       G_dec(z)=J_dec(z^9).

The decoder intertwines every coordinate permutation preserving E₂ and the relevant syndrome action. In particular J_dec commutes with F. Therefore

    G_dec²(z)=z^81,      G_dec⁵=J_dec,      G_dec^10=id.

Thus two budget steps are linear Frobenius, while one budget step needs a reciprocal-module exchange or a nonlinear decoder. This is an exact distinction of representations, not evidence that conditional poset counts obey ternary field addition.

### A sharp new limit on the linear patches

The published signed-geometry packet proves that ker S_+ ∩ ker S_- is the repetition code, whose nonzero words have weight eleven. For any z,w∈K the decoder carry

    c=d(z+w)-d(z)-d(w)

lies in ker S_+ and has weight at most six. Therefore

    J_dec(z+w)=J_dec(z)+J_dec(w)  iff  c=0.             (1)

Indeed the difference of the two sides is S_-(c); if zero, c lies in the intersection code and its weight forces c=0.

**Theorem.** The largest F_3-linear subspace U⊆K on which J_dec restricts to a linear map has dimension exactly two.

**Proof.** On such U, (1) makes d additive. Also d(λz)=λd(z) for λ∈F_3, by uniqueness of weight-at-most-two representatives. Hence d(U) is a linear code all of whose words have weight at most two. Any r-dimensional linear code contains a word of weight at least r: use a systematic generator and choose all r information coordinates nonzero. Thus dim U≤2. Conversely, vectors supported on any fixed two coordinate positions form a two-dimensional linear subspace inside E₂. Its injective S_+ image has decoder equal to the inverse linear map, and J_dec is linear there. ∎

The bound also applies to G_dec because Frobenius is a linear automorphism. A single syndrome retains the whole signed action as permutations, but cannot retain it linearly on any subspace larger than two dimensions. This quantifies the information obstruction rather than merely observing nonadditivity in one example.

## 4. The larger prime family

For an odd prime p≠5, let α,β be the roots of X²-3X+1 in F_(p²), with αβ=1. The same cross-ratio coordinate conjugates g² to multiplication by β².

- If 5 is a square modulo p, the two fixed points lie in F_p. Put h=ord(β²)>1. On P¹(F_p), g² has two fixed points and (p-1)/h cycles of length h. Deleting one fixed point gives an affine carrier. At p=11,19,31 one can choose β=9,6,20 respectively, giving h=5,9,15.
- If 5 is a nonsquare modulo p, β^p=α=β⁻¹. The cross-ratio images of P¹(F_p) form the norm-one group of size p+1, and β² has order h dividing (p+1)/2. There are no rational fixed points; all (p+1)/h cycles have length h.
- At p=5 the two fixed points coalesce. The nonidentity parabolic map has one fixed point and one cycle of length five.
- At p=2, g² has a single three-cycle on P¹(F_2).

These statements follow from diagonalizing the projective matrix in the split or quadratic extension field, with the repeated-root case handled directly. No assertion that maximal orders occur for infinitely many primes is needed.

This gives a split/inert/ramified trichotomy attached to the discriminant 5. Pythagorean middle-branch recurrences such as x→2+1/x instead have discriminant 8 and unit 1+√2. The shared structure is quadratic-unit projective dynamics; the arithmetic fields and admissible objects remain different.

## 5. A precise obstacle to transferring the finite conjugacy into a proof

Every rational j/N strictly between zero and one occurs as an actual poset comparison probability. Take a chain y₁<⋯<y_(N-1) and an isolated x. Its N extensions insert x uniformly in the N gaps, and Pr(x<y_j)=j/N.

Fix any projective residue r∈P¹(F_11) and either open interval I=(0,1/3) or I=(1/3,1/2). There is a reduced fraction j/N in I having projective residue r. For finite r choose N≡1 mod11 and j≡r mod11, with N sufficiently large; choose j/N in I and remove any common divisor. Because N is not divisible by11, this preserves the residue. For r=infinity choose N divisible by11 and j≡1 mod11, again sufficiently large; common-factor reduction leaves exactly a denominator divisible by11 and numerator prime to11.

Thus **every modular budget state is realized both by an unbalanced and by a strictly balanced comparison in a genuine finite poset**. In particular the eleven-state identification cannot decide the real 1/3 inequality. These simple posets satisfy the Gold conjecture; this is an obstruction to an inference, not a counterexample to Gold.

A useful proof architecture would keep two layers:

1. the finite/projective carrier, organizing symmetries and possible comparison strategies;
2. exact integer extension counts and real inequalities, transporting the actual budget.

The new endpoint-flag Gold certificates provide a concrete realization of the second layer: each path slack is a linear form in nonnegative context weights. The finite golden dictionary may guide selection of strategies, but a selector covering every realizable count cone is still missing. The key next question is whether a finite collection of such strategies covers a structurally defined family of exact boundary cones while allowing witnesses to move between neighboring blocks.
