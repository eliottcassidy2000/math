## 2. Generic dual testers retain exactly the mixed maximal minors

Let `R` be any commutative ring with identity, let `A` be `n` by `r`,
`1<=r<n`, and let `B` be `n` by `s`. Introduce an `r` by `n` matrix `U`
of algebraically independent variables. Define

```text
S_U(A,B)=det(UA) B-A adj(UA) U B.                       (1)
```

**Theorem A.** The ideal in `R` generated by all coefficients in `U` of
all entries of (1) equals the ideal of all `(r+1)`-row minors containing
the `r` columns of `A` and exactly one column of `B`.

For an increasing `r`-subset `I` of the row indices, write `A_I,B_I` for
the corresponding restrictions. The stronger entrywise identity is

```text
S_U(A,B)=sum_(|I|=r) det(U[:,I]) R_I,
R_I=det(A_I) B-A adj(A_I) B_I.                         (2)
```

To prove (2), use Cauchy--Binet and its cofactor identity

```text
adj(UA)U=sum_I det(U[:,I]) adj(A_I) E_I,
```

where `E_I` selects the rows in `I`. Both are integer polynomial
identities: for example the latter follows by equating coefficients of a
first-order perturbation of `A` in Cauchy--Binet. Therefore they hold over
arbitrary `R`, including rings with zero divisors and positive
characteristic. If `j` belongs to `I`, row `j` of `R_I` vanishes. Otherwise
its entry in bank column `alpha` is the bordered determinant with rows
`I` followed by `j`, columns `A` followed by `B_alpha`. This is the claimed
mixed minor with the corresponding row sign. Distinct sets `I` use
disjoint column sets of `U`, hence disjoint monomial supports in
`det(U[:,I])`. Each determinant monomial has coefficient `+1` or `-1`.
Consequently each coefficient of (1) is one of these signed minors, and
every such minor occurs. This proves the ideal equality without division
or a factorial multiplier.

Over a field, if `rank A=r`, all residuals vanish identically exactly when
all columns of `B` lie in `im A`. Over `R[det(A_I)^(-1)]`, the same
equations are equivalent to solvability, with the unique solution
`X=A_I^(-1)B_I`. These assertions do **not** extend to arbitrary global
module membership: `A=(2,0)^T`, `B=(1,0)^T` has every mixed minor zero but
has no integral solution. At rank drops even field membership can fail:
take `A=0`, `B!=0`. Coupled unknowns between several original columns
must first be represented as one complete, possibly vectorized, linear
system. Applying (1) to independent columns does not enforce an omitted
shared-parameter condition.

The cheap complex Gram hostile is

```text
A=(1,i,0)^T, B=(0,0,1)^T over C.
rank A=1, A^T A=0, A^T B=0, rank[A|B]=2.
```

Thus the bilinear normal equations report no obstruction although the
raw equation is inconsistent. Hermitian conjugation repairs positivity
but introduces conjugate variables, and the planar coefficient problem
supplies no analogue of the real S-matrix defect budget. Theorem A uses
neither positivity nor a preferred inner product.

## 3. Complete integral classification of the actual Student response

The tangent change in THM-4308 is `v_(m-1) -> v_(m-1)+theta v_0'`,
`deg theta<=m-1`. Its next-row response is exactly

```text
delta G_m=-x theta+(x²+6)/(2m) theta'.                 (3)
```

For every integer `m>=2`, choose source lattice `Z[x]_(<=m-1)` and target
lattice `(1/(2m)) Z[x]_(<=m)`. Multiplying the target coordinates by `2m`
identifies (3) with the integer operator

```text
L_m=(x²+6) d/dx-2m x,
A_m[k,j]=6j if k=j-1; j-2m if k=j+1; 0 otherwise,
0<=k<=m, 0<=j<=m-1.                                  (4)
```

This is a declared arithmetic model of (3), not a claim that all Keller
coefficients are integral. It includes every allowed theta in (3), before
the later depth restrictions. In particular its row-14 matrix is 15 by 14,
not the restricted 15-by-9 matrix in THM-4415.

Put `r=ceil(m/2)`, `s=floor(m/2)`. Define the following two lists of
positive edge weights, in the stated order:

```text
E_m=(2m,12,2m-2,24,2m-4,...,12(r-1),2m-2(r-1)),
O_m=(6,2m-1,18,2m-3,...,6(2s-1),2m-(2s-1)).          (5)
```

The first has `2r-1` entries and the second `2s` entries. Empty ranges in
these descriptions contribute no entries. For a finite list `w`, define
`F_w(k)` to be the gcd of all products of `k` nonadjacent entries of the
list; set `F_w(0)=1` and `F_w(k)=0` if no such selection exists. It is
computed without enumerating selections by

```text
F_(w_1,...,w_i)(k)
 =gcd(F_(w_1,...,w_(i-1))(k),
      w_i F_(w_1,...,w_(i-2))(k-1)),                 (6)
```

with no-edge initial arrays `(1,0,0,...)` at indices `-1` and `0`.

**Theorem B.** Every determinantal divisor of (4), and hence its entire
Smith form over `Z`, is given by

```text
Delta_0=1,
Delta_k=gcd_(a+b=k) F_(E_m)(a) F_(O_m)(b), 1<=k<=m,
d_k=Delta_k/Delta_(k-1),
coker L_m = Z direct-sum (direct-sum_(k=1)^m Z/d_k Z). (7)
```

These are positive integers with `d_k | d_(k+1)`. Formulae (5)--(7)
give a factorization-free classification for all `m` using `O(m²)` gcd
and integer multiplication operations. This is an arithmetic-operation
bound, not a bit-complexity bound.

**Proof.** Separate even and odd theta powers, and correspondingly odd
and even target powers. The even-source block is square of size `r`;
its bipartite nonzero-entry graph is a path with successive absolute
weights `E_m`. The odd-source block has `s` columns and `s+1` rows;
its graph is a path with weights `O_m`. Thus the entire support graph is
a disjoint union of two paths. A square submatrix cannot have two
different perfect matchings: their symmetric difference would contain
an alternating cycle in this forest. Its nonzero determinant is therefore
a single signed matching product. Conversely any matching gives a
square submatrix with that unique perfect matching and a nonzero
determinant. Every `k`-minor is consequently zero or one of the products
in (7), and every matching product occurs as a minor. Matchings of a path
are nonadjacent edge selections. Splitting them according to whether the
last edge is present proves (6), and splitting between the two paths
proves (7). Both blocks have a full-column-rank minor, so `Delta_m>0`.
The usual integer Smith theorem gives the claimed invariant factors and
cokernel. This proof uses the actual coefficients of (3), not an unrelated
matrix with the same rational rank. QED.

## 4. Saturation and a sharp joint denominator

The primitive rational left-nullspace covector `lambda_m` has odd entries
zero and positive even entries obtained by clearing denominators and
then dividing their common gcd in

```text
lambda_0=1,
lambda_(2k)/lambda_(2k-2)=6(2k-1)/(2m-2k+1).           (8)
```

Equation `lambda A_m=0` follows directly from (4). Since (4) has rank
`m`, this is its full rational cokernel. Formula (8) reproduces the
primitive rows printed for `m=5,6,7,8` in THM-4308.

**Corollary C.** Write
`K_m={b in Z^(m+1):lambda_m b=0}`. Then

```text
K_m / L_m(Z^m) = direct-sum_(k=1)^m Z/d_k Z.           (9)
```

For any integral compatible bank `B`, the unique rational solution bank
`X` of `A_m X=B` has common denominator equal to the exponent of the
subgroup generated by the classes of its columns in (9). It divides
`d_m`, and `d_m` is the least positive integer that clears every such
bank. These are iff conditions: an integral lift exists precisely when
all the column classes vanish.

Indeed the primitive covector surjects from `Z^(m+1)` to `Z`. Its kernel
is the saturation of `im A_m`; the torsion subgroup of the Smith cokernel
is exactly (9). The order of a target class is the least `q>0` for which
`q b` has an integral lift, equivalently `q X` is integral. Taking several
columns takes the least common multiple of these orders. A finite abelian
group with invariant factors as in (7) has exponent `d_m`, and this
exponent is attained.

An actual two-column sharp hostile at `m=2` is

```text
A_2=((0,6),(-4,0),(0,-3)), lambda_2=(1,0,2),
Smith(A_2)=(1,12),
L_2(-1/4)=x, L_2(x/3)=2-x².
```

Both target columns satisfy the exact rational Student equation. Neither
has an integral lift; together they require denominator `lcm(4,3)=12`.
No sum of real energies is involved.

At the actual row index `m=14`, the invariant factors are

```text
(1,1,1,1,1,1,1,2,2,6,12,12,840,720720),
|K_14/im L_14|=2092278988800.
```

The seven target columns `x,x³,x⁵,x⁷,x⁹,x¹¹,x¹³` all satisfy the Student
equation. Their unique rational lifts have respective least denominators

```text
(28,182,2184,4004,20020,18018,16016).
```

Their least common multiple is `720720`, so this explicit bank attains
the full arithmetic exponent. Even after localizing at `2,3,7`, the
torsion exponent remains `715=5*11*13`. Thus clearing the visible factor
`2m` in (3) does not by itself remove all arithmetic information.

For an arbitrary bank, the following independent inverse retains the
last raw compatibility equation and computes the precise denominator:

```text
theta_m=theta_(m+1)=0;
for k=m,m-1,...,1:
    theta_(k-1)=(b_k-6(k+1)theta_(k+1))/(k-1-2m);
accept if and only if b_0=6 theta_1.
```

The denominators never divide by zero. A further useful boundary is that
every torsion prime is at most `max(m,3)`: an explicit maximal minor is
the product of `2(m-k)` for `0<=k<r` and `6(2k+1)` for `0<=k<s`.
The determinantal divisor divides this minor. This is a prime-support
upper bound, not a claim that every smaller prime occurs.

Over a characteristic-zero field the torsion in (9) disappears, leaving
only the inherited Student equation. In particular this note does not
strengthen THM-4415's rational obstruction, does not invalidate the memory
repair of THM-4426, and does not provide a terminal-row compatibility
condition for free. To combine (1) with a collision or Hasse bank one must
first assemble the actual shared operator and retain every raw equation.
The possible extension is then to compute that operator's saturation
sidecar, rather than substitute a Gram rank or a single scalar energy.
