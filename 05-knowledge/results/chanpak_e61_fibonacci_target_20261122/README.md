# Which Fibonacci receiver addresses can cross a perfect number?

**PROVED.** Let \(p\ge2\), \(A=2^{p-1}\), and suppose \(M=2^p-1\) is prime. Let \(D\) be **any** singly tagged tournament having at least two full paths ending at its tag. Graft any receiver with a unique tagged-start Hamilton path onto \(D\), absorb its transitive tail into the right address, and add the common apex. If the resulting tournament has \(E_p-1=AM-1\) Hamilton paths, its positive normalized right address cannot end in \(p-1\) equal bits. Neither tournament order is bounded. In particular, this excludes those addresses for every equal-deletion-count, equal-start-mass, single-tag unit flip with signed-start increase two, including the six-vertex core of the first exponent-31 construction.

## Why a large homogeneous suffix forces a factor

Let \(a=H(D)\), \(b\) be its unsigned tagged-start mass, and \(d\ge2\) the number of full paths ending at its tag. For a normalized single-tag receiver \(V\), let \(g\) be its full path count, \(Q\) its number of ordered two-slot path covers allowing empty slots, and \(s,m\) the numbers of covers whose first slot starts or ends at its tag, respectively. Write \(e\) for its number of full paths ending at the tag, and put \(h=ag,\ c=bQ+as\). The graph to be counted has an ordinal deletion \(D\to V\), a common apex and a transitive right flank. Its count is \(K_j+cy+h f(y)\), \(j=y\bmod 2\), where \(f(0)=0,\ f(2u+1)=f(u),\ f(2u)=f(u)+u+[u\text{ odd}]\). The right address encodes the reverse complement of the contacts following the receiver. Every admissible receiver decomposes as one strong rooted component with a single tag followed by arbitrarily tagged singleton components. Concatenate the latter contacts with the exterior contacts to obtain the normalized right word, removing trailing forced sinks if necessary. The forbidden pattern concerns this combined address. [Graft law and Fibonacci classification](../chanpak_e31_physical_20261122/README.md)

Splitting each full path at its tag gives \(b\ge a\ge d\), and similarly \(s,m\ge g\ge e\). Swapping the two slots gives \(2m\le Q\), since the two slots cannot both end at the tag. Cutting each full path at every one of its \(n+1\) positions proves \(Q\ge(n+1)g\). The signed tag-start mass is \(-e\) by the dual signed-end identity. For a single tag the receiver apex count is \(g+s-e\). A path using a nonempty slot after the apex starts that slot at the tag, while a path ending at the apex must finish its previous slot elsewhere. The first case never uses the tag in the other slot. The same formula gives \(a+b-d\) for \(D\). Substitution in the graft law gives
\[
 K_1-c=ag-bm-d,\qquad K_0=K_1-ae .
\]
Consequently \(h/c<1/2\), \(0<K_0+1\le K_1+1<c\), and \(0<c-K_1-1<c/2\). For the final bound use \(bm\le bQ/2\) and \(d\le a\le ag\). Positivity of \(c-K_1-1\) uses \(b\ge a,\ m\ge g,\ d\ge2\). When a flip in \(D\) increases its signed read by two while preserving \(a,b\), its old value is \(-d\le-2\); the receiver transmits the unit half-gap for every address.

For final ones write \(y=A(z+1)-1\). Since \(f(y)=f(z)\), exactness gives
\[
 AM=cA(z+1)+R,\qquad R=K_1+1-c+h f(z).
\]
The inequalities above give \(AM>c(A-1/2)\), hence \(c<2A\) since \(M=2A-1\). Also \(z<M/c\); thus \(-A<R<A\) because \(f(z)\le z\). As \(A\mid R\), we have \(c(z+1)=M\). The primality of \(M\) and \(c>1\) force \(z=0, c=M\). But then \(R=K_1+1-c<0\).

For final zeros write \(y=Az, z\ge1\). Repeated doubling gives
\[
 AM=(c+h)Az+R,\qquad R=K_0+1+h\big(f(z)-z+[z\text{ odd}]\big).
\]
Here \(z<M/c\) and \(f(z)\le z-[z\text{ odd}]\), since \(f\) is even and bounded by its argument. If \(z\ge2\), then \(c<A\) and \(-A<R<A\), forcing \((c+h)z=M\), impossible. If \(z=1\), the residual is \(K_0+1\in(0,c)\subset(0,2A)\). Its only possible multiple of \(A\) is \(A\). This would give \(2K_0=c+h\). However,
\[
 2K_0-c-h=b(Q-2m)+a(s+g-2e)-2d .
\]
For the singleton receiver the right side is \(-2d<0\). For a larger receiver the full path starting at the tag ends elsewhere. Put that path in either slot with the companion slot empty; these two distinct covers give \(Q-2m\ge2\). The same path gives \(g-e\ge1\), hence \(s+g-2e\ge2\). The displayed difference is then at least \(2b+2a-2d>0\). This excludes the last case, with no further assumptions on the core.

## A carry inverse and internal runs

For the numerical results below use deletion rows \(D=(4,5,24,19,35,15)\), vertex 0 tagged, and flip \((1,3)\). Here \((a,b,d)=(25,28,10)\); the flip decreases \(d\) by two without changing \(a,b\). For this core the last obstruction also reads \(28(Q-2m)+25(s+g-2e)=20\), impossible in the numerical semigroup generated by 25 and 28.

For a shorter prescribed terminal run of width \(1\le \ell\le p-1\), put \(B=2^\ell\) and \(P=AM/B\). For ones the residual is \(R=K_1+1-c+h f(z)\) and \(z=(P-R/B)/c-1\). For zeros it is \(R=K_0+1+h(f(z)-z+[z\text{ odd}])\) and \(z=(P-R/B)/(c+h)\). Bound \(z\) above by \(Z=\lfloor((AM-1-K_j)/c-j(B-1))/B\rfloor\) using \(f(y)\ge0\). In the first case enumerate integer carries in \([(K_1+1-c)/B,(K_1+1-c+hZ)/B]\) congruent to \(P\pmod c\); in the second use \([(K_0+1-hZ)/B,(K_0+1)/B]\) modulo \(c+h\). Substitution is necessary and sufficient. This tests \(O(1+h AM/(c^2B^2))\) prefix candidates for a chart with nonnegative intercepts. With 48 low bits fixed, the 610 receivers needed only 319,694 tests in total.

**Shorter-suffix rule across exponents.** Fix a chart and an integer \(s_0\ge0\) with \(2^{2s_0+1}h<c\). For all sufficiently large Mersenne-prime exponents \(p\), neither terminal run of length \(p-1-s_0\) can occur in a solution. Indeed \(B=A/2^{s_0}\) and \(Z<2^{s_0+1}A/c\). The two residuals divided by \(B\) have absolute value at most \(2^{2s_0+1}h/c+O(1/A)<1\), with constant depending only on the fixed chart and \(s_0\). A zero carry makes the corresponding slope \(c\) or \(c+h\) divide \(2^{s_0}M\). Once \(M>c+h\), that slope must divide \(2^{s_0}\), yet \(c>2^{s_0}\). For a normalized receiver of order \(n\), \(c/h\ge n+2\) by cutting full paths in the two factors. Thus any \(s_0\) with \(2^{2s_0+1}<n+2\) qualifies independently of the receiver's internal shape. The same inequality with the actual \(c/h\) can be stronger.

Independently, every positive address of at most three runs is all ones or \(1^a0^b1^d\) for \(a,b\ge1,d\ge0\). Writing \(X=2^a-1,Y=2^b,Z=2^d\), one has \(y=XYZ+Z-1, f(y)=X(Y-1)+1\). Given \(b,d\), the target specifies \(X\) by one exact division, so there are \(O(p^2)\) tests per receiver. No such right address hits at exponent 61 through receiver order 9.

There is also a uniform bound for an **unnormalized** receiver, allowing arbitrary tag sets with unique total tagged-start count. Set
\[
 U_n=n!\sum_{i=0}^n2^{-\lfloor i/2\rfloor-\lfloor(n-i)/2\rfloor}.
\]
Fix the cut between ordered slots. In each slot, independently swapping each disjoint consecutive pair of entries leaves just one orientation of every such pair compatible with a path. This gives \(Q\le U_n\). Deleting an apex maps its paths injectively to two-slot covers, and each individual start, end or absolute signed mass is at most \(Q\). The graft formula and \(u=1-g\) give
\[
 0<K_j\le (78+28/(n+1))U_n,\quad c\le53U_n,\quad
 h\le25U_n/(n+1),\quad h/c\le25/(28(n+1)).
\]
Positivity uses the signed-start identity, so \(K_1\ge K_0>0\). If \(c+h<M\) and \(\max(c,K_0+1,K_1+1)+2Ah/c<A\), then either run of \(p-1\) bits forces a zero carry by the same two equations. Both slopes lie strictly between 1 and the prime \(M\). At exponent 61 these elementary bounds cover every receiver on at most 19 vertices, namely \(F_{38}=39,088,169\) tagged isomorphism types in total.

To test a homogeneous block followed by \(t>0\) arbitrary low bits, write \(y=2^t x+r\) and let \(r^*\) reverse its \(t\) padded bits. The binary splitting law replaces the slope by \(c'=2^tc+h(2^t-1-r^*)\) and the adjusted intercept by \(K'_i=K_{r\bmod2}+cr+h f(r)+hi[r<2^{t-1}]\), \(i=x\bmod2\). The same residual test now applies to the \(p-1\) low bits of \(x\). For each order below the bounds alone exclude a run of 60 zeros or ones followed by any number of bits from zero through the indicated maximum, for every tagged receiver of that order.
