# Sparse digits cannot carry Mills floors, even at bounded gaps

2026-11-28. This extends the [finite affine rail obstruction](../chanpak_buenos_aires_mills_20261128) to moving exponents, arbitrary integer bases, and observations that may skip steps. The application is to bounded-run one-apex tournaments, not unrestricted tournaments.

## 1. Rigidity theorem

Let \(b\ge2\) be an integer. Write \(b=B^g\), where \(g=\gcd_{p\mid b}v_p(b)\), so \(B\) is not a proper power. Let \(c_j\) be integer exponents with \(2\le c_j\le C\), and define \(E_0=1,\ E_{j+1}=c_jE_j\). Take \(A>1\). Suppose that along infinitely many times \(j_0<j_1<\cdots\) with \(j_{s+1}-j_s\le D\), there are positive integers \(h_{j_s}\) such that
\[
h_{j_s}\le A^{E_{j_s}}\le h_{j_s}+1,\qquad
h_{j_s}=\sum_{i=1}^{r_s}\epsilon_i b^{e_i},\quad r_s\le R,\ \epsilon_i\in\{-1,1\},\ e_i\ge0 .
\]
Then \(A^{E_J}=B^k\) for some time \(J\) and integer \(k\ge1\), and every later power is exactly \(B^{k E_j/E_J}\). In particular:

- In **any** integer base, actual floors at bounded gaps cannot be primes of bounded signed weight: the eventual powers of \(B\) are composite.
- In base two, they cannot even be odd integers of bounded signed weight. This remains true if a bounded number of arbitrary steps is allowed between the sparse counts.
- The theorem needs no limits on the relative spacing of the nonzero digit positions.

### Compactness of normalized signed sums

Cancel opposite terms at a position and carry \(b\) equal terms to position one higher. Each operation decreases the number of terms. The resulting signed coefficients have absolute value at most \(b-1\); there are at most \(R\) occupied positions. If the largest position is \(e\), the leading sign has to be positive since *all* possible lower coefficients of size \(b-1\) sum to \(b^e-1\). Even maximizing the opposing contribution from at most \(R-1\) lower positions leaves \(h\ge b^{e-R+1}\). Hence in \(h/b^{\lfloor\log_b h\rfloor}\), every term exponent is bounded above by \(R-1\). In any limit we can pass to a subsequence on which the number and signs of terms are fixed and each exponent is constant or tends to \(-\infty\). Each limit mantissa in \([1,b]\) is therefore a nonzero rational sum of at most \(R\) signed powers of \(b\), allowing negative exponents. The two endpoints represent the same log phase.

### Elimination of irrational log phases by a unit equation

Let \(t_j=E_j\log_b A\bmod 1\). The bounded error at the observed times ensures that the observed \(t_{j_s}\) have the same cluster set as the phases of \(h_{j_s}\). Take any cluster point \(t\), represented by a mantissa \(y\in[1,b]\) as above. The transition from one observation to the next multiplies the phase by some integer \(d_s=E_{j_{s+1}}/E_{j_s}\in[2,C^D]\). From a subsequence tending to \(t\), successively stabilize its next multiplier, then its next two, and so on. For the resulting one-sided itinerary \(d_0^*,d_1^*,\ldots\), all the phases \(m_i t\) are cluster points, where \(m_0=1,\ m_i=\prod_{\ell<i}d_\ell^*\). This uses constants on nested infinite subsequences; for each finite horizon take the limit along its own subsequence.

Consequently \(y^{m_i}\), after scaling by a power of \(b\), is a sum of at most \(R\) signed powers of \(b\). Absorb the scaling into the exponents. Delete all zero subsums; the number of terms and the ordered sign pattern range through a finite set. After dividing by \(y^{m_i}\) the surviving variables \(x_\ell=b^{a_\ell}/y^{m_i}\) belong to the fixed multiplicative group generated by \(b,y\), and satisfy \(\sum\epsilon_\ell x_\ell=1\) nondegenerately. The [Evertse–Schlickewei–Schmidt theorem](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/2002/155-3/p04) gives finitely many tuples for each term count and sign pattern, applying it to the corresponding direct power of that group. (For one term the conclusion is immediate.) Two different \(m_i\) therefore share a normalized coordinate, so \(y^{m_i-m_{i'}}=b^{a-a'}\). Thus \(\log_b y\) is rational. If its reduced denominator is \(q\), the prime valuations in \(y^q=b^{q\log_b y}\) show \(q\mid g\). Every cluster phase lies in \(F=(1/g)\mathbb Z/\mathbb Z\). For a prime base, this is just zero; for a nonprimitive base it is necessary to keep the whole finite subgroup, not just zero.

### Expansion makes the conclusion exact

The distances from \(t_{j_s}\) to \(F\) now tend to zero. Choose a radius less than \(1/(2g C^D)\). Once the distances stay below this radius, write \(t_{j_s}=f+\eta\bmod1\) with \(f\in F\) nearest and \(\eta\) the signed error. Multiplying by \(d_s\) multiplies the distance by \(d_s\) exactly, because \(d_s f\in F\) and \(|d_s\eta|<1/(2g)\). A nonzero distance would grow at least by a factor of two per observation, contradicting convergence to zero. Thus \(t_{j_s}\in F\) eventually, which gives the asserted exact power at an observation time \(J\) and at all later times. The proof applies to approximate closed floors; it has not used strictness at the upper endpoint.

## 2. Weak local shell chains: the missing endpoint is the whole obstruction

Suppose \(h_0\ge2\) and an infinite sequence of odd integers satisfies the integer shell tests
\[
h_j^{c_j}\le h_{j+1}<(h_j+1)^{c_j}.
\]
The **closed** intervals \([h_j^{1/E_j},(h_j+1)^{1/E_j}]\) are nested because their endpoints before taking roots are integers. Their lengths tend to zero (for example by the derivative of \(x^{1/E_j}\) on \(x\ge1\)); the limit \(A\) is \(>1\). If signed binary weight stays bounded even just at bounded gaps, apply the theorem in base 2. From time \(J\) on oddness forces \(h_j=2^{kE_j/E_J}-1\), one below the power, at **every** step, not just observations. The common endpoint \(A\) is excluded by the half-open floor intervals. For example, \(h_j=2^{c^j}-1\) passes every local shell test and has signed weight two, but does not yield the stated floors from any real seed. This example is arithmetic; it does not assert that each of its \(h_j\) is a tournament count.

## 3. Tournament consequences and sharpness of bounded gaps

In the [one-apex interval law](../chanpak_contact_run_complexity_20261128), a tournament with \(r\) active contact runs has count \(r+1\) plus one signed power of two per nonempty run interval. Its signed weight is at most \(r(r+1)/2+\lfloor\log_2(r+1)\rfloor+1\). A join of finitely many such hosts multiplies counts, and multiplying expansions multiplies their term bounds. Thus in a genuine infinite Mills-style odd floor construction, for each fixed pair of caps on number of hosts and number of runs per host, there must be arbitrarily long time intervals with **no** witness under those caps. The statement is stronger than excluding any single finitely parameterized sequence of exponents.

One cannot replace bounded gaps by just infinitely many observations. In fact, for fixed \(c\ge2\), a dense \(G_\delta\) set of positive parameters \(t=\log_2 A\) has infinitely many visits to actual two-run tournament counts \(1+2^k\) (\(k\ge2\)). These counts are realized by runs of lengths \(1,k\): the formula gives \(3-2-2^k+2^{k+1}=1+2^k\). For each \(j\) the intervals
\[
\left(\frac{\log_2(2^k+1)}{c^j},\frac{\log_2(2^k+2)}{c^j}\right)
\]
enforce those floors. For any cutoff \(J\), the union over \(j\ge J,\ k\ge2\) is open dense in \(t>0\): choose \(j\) large and \(k/c^j\) in the interior of a prescribed interval, then use \(\log_2(2^k+2)-k\to0\). Intersect the unions and use the Baire theorem. All these visits have signed weight at most two. The theorem implies their gaps are unbounded. This separates genuine recurrence at bounded gaps from a construction that hits infinitely many scattered counts.

There is a contrasting measure statement: for fixed \(R\), almost every \(t>0\) has only finitely many bounded-weight floor hits. On a compact interval \(0<u\le t\le v\), the digit normalization bounds all exponents of possible floors at time \(j\) by \(vc^j+R\). The number of candidate sums is \(O_{v,R}((c^j)^R)\). Each has a floor interval of length at most \(O(c^{-j}2^{-uc^j})\) in \(t\), since its integer height is at least \(2^{uc^j}-1\). This is summable after multiplying by the candidate count. Borel–Cantelli proves the claim, and countable unions handle every \(R\) and all \(t>0\). Thus scattered visits can be topologically generic despite having probability zero for a random seed.

The obstruction applies to sparse **signed** digits, so it is not evaded by a long borrow string. It says nothing against an unrestricted Chan–Pak construction, or a schedule with genuinely growing contact complexity.
