# Exact span-sensitive avoidance in a split orthogonal space

Session 27, 2026-10-22. This is a self-contained structural correction and finite verification, not a claim of a new finite-geometry theorem or a proof of extremality in dimension 96.

## 1. Setup and counting lemma

Let q be an odd prime power, r≥1, and V a split nondegenerate quadratic space of dimension 2r over F_q. Write Q(x,y)=x·y in hyperbolic coordinates and β(u,v)=Q(u+v)−Q(u)−Q(v). “Totally isotropic” means Q vanishes identically on the subspace, equivalently β vanishes there in odd characteristic. A projective line below means a one-dimensional vector subspace, not a two-dimensional vector subspace/projective-geometric line.

Let M_r be the set of all r-dimensional totally isotropic subspaces, including both orthogonal families, and choose L uniformly from M_r. Define N_0=1. Then

N_r = |M_r| = 2 ∏_{i=1}^{r−1}(q^i+1).

For every totally isotropic d-dimensional W, 0≤d≤r,

Pr(W⊆L) = N_{r−d}/N_r = 1 / ∏_{j=r−d}^{r−1}(q^j+1).

The empty product for d=0 is 1. At d=r, the factor j=0 is 2; omitting it gives an incorrect answer. A nonisotropic W has containment probability zero.

### Proof

The standard Witt decomposition gives W⊥/W a split nondegenerate quadratic form of dimension 2(r−d). For completeness, the local splitting step chooses w≠0 singular and u with β(w,u)≠0, and replaces u by u−Q(u)w/β(w,u). This produces a hyperbolic plane containing w; repeating the Witt decomposition extends a totally singular basis to a hyperbolic basis. Consequently maximal isotropic subspaces containing W correspond bijectively, by L↦L/W, to maximal isotropic subspaces in W⊥/W. There are N_{r−d} of them.

To compute N_r without presupposing a polar-space formula, count nonzero solutions of x·y=0. If x=0 there are q^r choices of y; for each x≠0 there are q^{r−1} choices. After excluding the zero vector and dividing by q−1, the number of isotropic one-spaces is

s_r = (q^r−1)(q^{r−1}+1)/(q−1).

Every L contains (q^r−1)/(q−1) one-spaces. Every isotropic one-space lies in N_{r−1} members of M_r by the quotient correspondence. Double-counting incidences gives

N_r (q^r−1)/(q−1) = s_r N_{r−1},

so N_r=(q^{r−1}+1)N_{r−1}. Starting at N_0=1 gives the displayed product, including N_1=2. Taking the quotient N_{r−d}/N_r proves the containment formula, with the d=r case handled by N_0=1. ∎

Restriction to a single family changes the model and is not covered by the theorem as stated.

## 2. Exact forbidden-set certificate

Let B be a finite set of distinct isotropic one-spaces. Define its span poset

P(B) = {span(S): S⊆B},

ordered by subspace inclusion, with 0=span(∅). It includes nonisotropic spans. Let μ_B(0,W) be its Möbius function, defined by μ_B(0,0)=1 and, for W>0,

μ_B(0,W) = −∑_{U∈P(B), U<W} μ_B(0,U).

Then the exact avoidance probability is

A(B) = Pr(∀b∈B, b⊈L)
     = ∑_{W∈P(B), W totally isotropic} μ_B(0,W) / ∏_{j=r−dim W}^{r−1}(q^j+1).

In particular, an avoiding maximal isotropic space exists if and only if A(B)>0. Equivalently,

C(B) = ∑_{W∈P(B), W totally isotropic} μ_B(0,W) N_{r−dim W}

is a positive integer. This integer is exactly the number of avoiding maximal isotropic spaces, not an estimate.

### Proof of the Möbius grouping

Ordinary inclusion-exclusion for events E_b={b⊆L} gives

A(B)=∑_{S⊆B} (−1)^|S| Pr(span(S)⊆L).

Set c(W)=∑_{S⊆B: span(S)=W}(−1)^|S|. For any W in P(B),

∑_{U≤W}c(U)=∑_{S⊆B∩W}(−1)^|S|.

Here B∩W means the forbidden one-spaces contained in W. The right side is 1 for W=0 and 0 otherwise: every nonzero W in the span poset contains at least one generating member of B. This is precisely the defining triangular recurrence for μ_B(0,W). Therefore c(W)=μ_B(0,W). Substitution of the containment formula proves the identity. ∎

The Möbius function is that of the span poset generated by B, not of the entire subspace lattice unless those posets actually agree. A matroid flat can equivalently be represented by all forbidden lines contained in its vector span.

### Union bound

Since Pr(b⊆L)=1/(q^{r−1}+1),

A(B) ≥ 1−|B|/(q^{r−1}+1).

Thus |B|<q^{r−1}+1 is sufficient for existence. Equality in this numerical bound alone does not decide existence. The exact formula may certify avoidance when the union bound is inconclusive.

## 3. Hostile dependence checks

Take three distinct one-spaces b1,b2,b3 in a fixed totally isotropic two-space H. Each pair spans H and all three still span H. Hence

A({b1,b2,b3}) = 1−3p1+3p2−p2 = 1−3p1+2p2,

where p_d=Pr(W⊆L) for an isotropic d-space. The span poset consists of 0, the three atoms, and H, with μ(0,H)=2. Treating the triple as a rank-three constraint is wrong. Pairwise orthogonality implies that the span is isotropic, but does not determine its dimension.

For a direct demonstration that an orthogonality graph alone is insufficient, use r=3 and q=3, with e1,e2,e3 in a hyperbolic basis. The sets

B_dep={〈e1〉,〈e2〉,〈e1+e2〉},
B_ind={〈e1〉,〈e2〉,〈e3〉}

both have the complete orthogonality graph K3. The first has triple rank 2, the second rank 3. Here p1=1/10, p2=1/40, p3=1/80, giving

A(B_dep)=3/4=60/80,
A(B_ind)=1−3p1+3p2−p3=61/80.

The minimal extra information needed for this grouping is the actual linear-dependence/span data of the forbidden configuration, together with which spans are isotropic. The orthogonality graph does identify isotropic subsets (cliques), but not their ranks.

## 4. Exact algorithm and certificate format

Input: an exact field model, a matrix for Q or β, and a deduplicated list of forbidden one-spaces represented by canonical nonzero vectors. For general prime powers use exact extension-field arithmetic, not integer reduction modulo q.

1. Verify the ambient form is nondegenerate and split, and each input line is isotropic. If a list contains nonisotropic lines, discard them explicitly: they can never occur in an isotropic L.
2. Canonicalize spans by exact reduced row echelon bases. Starting with {0}, repeatedly adjoin span(W,b) as each b is processed. This constructs P(B).
3. Order spans by dimension and compute μ_B(0,W) using exact subspace-containment tests and the displayed recurrence.
4. Test Q on a basis and β on every basis pair to decide whether W is totally isotropic.
5. Compute C(B) with integers. Check 0≤C(B)≤N_r; positivity is the exact existence certificate.
6. Preserve the field/form definition, forbidden input and its provenance, canonical span bases, containment data, Möbius values, isotropy flags, and the final integer sum. A proposed explicit avoiding L can instead be verified directly against every forbidden line.

Equivalent coefficient-update algorithm: initialize c(0)=1. For every forbidden line b, replace the dictionary c by c' satisfying c'(W)+=c(W) and c'(span(W,b))−=c(W) for every old entry W. At the end c is μ_B grouped by spans. Nonisotropic spans can safely be pruned because all their superspaces remain nonisotropic; no discarded term can ever return to the isotropic part. Using the full span poset is the simplest certificate specification.

These are exact finite algorithms, not polynomial-time guarantees. There may be exponentially many distinct spans and enormous input sets. Even a perfect avoidance computation does not certify that a lattice-derived forbidden set was complete. Completeness of that input is a separate mathematical/computational obligation.

## 5. Independent finite reproduction

`verify.py` uses only the Python standard library and constructs Q(x)=x1*x3+x2*x4 over F3. It enumerates all 40 projective one-spaces and all 130 two-dimensional subspaces directly from pairs of independent projective vectors, deduplicating via RREF. It tests the quadratic form to obtain the maximal isotropic spaces; the predicted counting formula is not used to generate or select them.

The script independently compares actual containment against the formula on every span of every subset of the 16 isotropic lines. It then checks avoidance for all 2^16=65,536 forbidden subsets: one side directly checks intersections with the enumerated maximal isotropic planes, the other evaluates rank-aware inclusion-exclusion by a subset zeta transform. Separately, it constructs actual span posets and verifies the Möbius recurrence/grouping for the empty set, the complete set, and 100 seeded pseudorandom subsets. It verifies the hostile rank-two triple explicitly. All assertions passed: 8 maximal isotropic planes, every isotropic line in exactly 2 planes, all 65,536 forbidden subsets verified, and all 102 Möbius-poset checks verified. The hostile triple is avoided by 4 of the 8 planes, so its probability is 1/2. The complete forbidden set is avoided by none. The avoidance-count histogram (count of avoiding planes: number of forbidden subsets) is {0:41503, 1:17288, 2:5188, 3:1208, 4:284, 5:48, 6:16, 8:1}. Full machine output is in `results.json`.

A second independent script, `verify_graph_counterexample.py`, enumerates all 33,880 three-dimensional subspaces of F3^6 directly in canonical RREF form, finds 80 maximal isotropic spaces, and directly counts 60 avoiders for B_dep and 61 for B_ind. Thus the identical-graph/different-probability counterexample is independently enumerated, not merely evaluated using the formula. Output is in `graph_counterexample_results.json`.

Run: python verify.py; python verify_graph_counterexample.py

## 6. Relevance and limits for the supplied lattice manuscripts

The review in `session27-papers-review/REVIEW.md` identifies the relevant discriminant pairing as symmetric over F7, not alternating. Conditional on its being split of dimension 48, the correct model has r=24:

N_24=2∏_{i=1}^{23}(7^i+1),
Pr(b⊆L)=1/(7^23+1),
7^23+1=27,368,747,340,080,916,344.

A rigorously complete forbidden set with fewer than this many isotropic lines would imply an avoiding gluing by the union bound. In particular, a genuinely proved bound near 10^12 would still suffice after correcting the orthogonal/symplectic error. But the supplied review reports that no reproducible derivation of that forbidden-set bound is given. This note does not produce the actual short-vector forbidden set or prove its completeness, does not establish the split hypothesis for an unspecified construction, and does not certify that a specific rank-96 lattice has minimum 10. It supplies the correct conditional counting and a dependence-sensitive exact criterion, not the missing lattice certificate.
