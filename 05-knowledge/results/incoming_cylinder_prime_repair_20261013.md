## Actual stopping time versus coefficient stopping time

For `T(n)=n/2` or `(3n+1)/2`, actual first descent `sigma` is not exactly
periodic at fixed depth. Already `1` and `5` are congruent modulo four,
but `sigma(1)=infinity`, `sigma(5)=2`, whereas the coefficient stopping
time `kappa` is two for both. This refutes the unqualified periodicity
step in THM-4627 part 1, not its asymptotic limit.

Here is a repair that retains the source's height. Each depth-j parity
word has `T^j(n)=(3^a n+c)/2^j`, with `0<=c<=3^j-2^j`. Let `H_k` be the
maximum of `ceil(c/(2^j-3^a))` over all words at all depths `j<=k` with
`3^a<2^j`, taking zero for an empty maximum. It is finite and `H_k<3^k`.
No actual descent can precede coefficient descent. If `n>H_k` and
`kappa(n)<=k`, actual descent occurs at that same step. Thus the two
truncations agree outside an explicit finite exceptional interval.

For a bounded finite-word functional `F`, define the genuinely periodic
model `f_k^*(n)=F(word through kappa(n))` when `kappa(n)<=k`, and zero
otherwise. Do the same for `g`. For affine forms `a_i n+b_i`, let
`P_i(k)` be the Haar probability of `kappa(a_i y+b_i)>k`, and let
`R_k` be the full-period mean of the product of these models. An explicit
safe bound is

```
| N^-1 sum f(a1*n+b1)g(a2*n+b2) - R_k |
 <= ||f|| ||g|| [P_1(k)+P_2(k)+(4*2^k+4*H_k)/N].
```

The product's period remainder costs at most `2*2^k/N`; the two tail
indicator remainders together cost at most `2*2^k/N`; at most `H_k`
exceptional sources per affine form each cost twice the relevant norm.
These estimates give the displayed bound. Haar tails tend to zero by
Terras, including in each fixed affine coset. First send `N` to infinity
at fixed `k`, then send `k` to infinity. This proves the same limit and
retains the elementary limiting lag identities. It does **not** justify
the earlier error estimate with no exceptional-height term, or a free
