# Arithmetic resolution and the symmetry hidden inside path counts

**Research synthesis; independent review PASS. Chan–Pak remains OPEN.**

This session gives three precise ways that prime-adic structure emerges from growing tournament families. They are different mechanisms, not three names for one universal principle: a digit address, an automorphism stabilizer, and an exponential-mode response.

## 1. One construction, two correlated prime geometries

The inherited Session286 reset has a binary address at each end of a chain. The [reset proof](reset/PROOF.md) promotes its fixed-length decoder to an explicit inverse-limit object. A bilinear expression in two convergent digit series describes the limit. At13 it is a four-branch Cantor set of dimension log(4)/log(13); a different normalized two-count response gives a2-adic Cantor set of dimension2/3. Finite tournament witnesses realize every finite address with a proved precision budget.

The same bit address parametrizes both geometries. Their paired residues therefore have4^r possibilities, not16^r. This is a positive compatibility theorem and a warning against independently filling the two primes by CRT. An explicit Hölder map connects the images; it does not identify the two p-adic fields.

A further positive result removes the local digit shortage under **formal addition**: two copies of the2-adic limit set sum to all Z_2, and exactly three copies of the13-adic set sum to all Z_13. Explicit stable-derivative certificates lift every next digit, so this is not inferred from a finite residue census. Finite same-size tournament observables realize the normalized sums to any requested precision. However, no operation turning these formal sums into one tournament with that exact path count is supplied. The missing physical additive operation and absolute height remain separate obligations.

## 2. Destroying visible symmetry does not destroy its path-count divisor

For the balanced ternary tournament core of order n=3^e, the [symmetry proof](symmetry/PROOF.md) derives an exact labelled distribution from the inherited cyclic-wreath orbit grammar. Independent random attachments color the old vertices; recursively equal child types contribute local symmetry tolls. These tolls are dependent, and their covariance is retained.

At a fixed number of attachment colors, the visible3-primary stabilizer has a rigorously computed linear mean, positive linear variance, law of large numbers and central limit theorem. If k named newborns are attached by independent fair bits, visible pointwise symmetry disappears around k=(log_2 n)/2, with a Poisson law in the critical window.

Yet an inherited endpoint-action theorem still forces a power of3 into every Hamiltonian count. The path uses only2k old contacts with newborns, so ancestral actions on endpoint-conditioned path classes survive even when they are no longer automorphisms of the full child. To obtain a3-adic unit count from this core requires at least ceil(n/6) newborns by that floor. This is a necessary condition, not a sufficient construction.

The new comparison also retains the difference between pointwise and full automorphism groups: the orbit of the newborn tuple contributes an explicit bounded factor. For logarithmically many suitable random attachments, even H/|Aut| retains valuation at least n/2−O((log n)^2) with probability tending to1. That quotient bound is not an exact formula for H.

## 3. A fixed symmetry core can acquire new arithmetic modes as it grows

The [inflation proof](wildcard/PROOF.md) studies replacing one vertex of a fixed tournament by a transitive module of size m. Hamiltonian paths split into runs through the module. The complete run inventory gives a finite Stirling/exponential expansion and a sharp bound on the number of possible runs. This is a special, explicit use of the inherited path-cover substitution theory, not a new universal substitution principle.

At good primes the response has exact analytic phase functions. After removing coefficient content, a nonzero Fermat-quotient tangent gives a constructive Hensel inverse onto a full p-adic ball. A Vandermonde test distinguishes phase cancellation from genuine tangent freezing. Physical module sizes and integer heights are retained throughout; a lifted residue is not an exact prescribed integer count.

The exact interior criterion is particularly short: for the nonconstant mode support S={j≥2:b_j≠0}, the p-adic closure has nonempty interior exactly when p does not divide gcd(S). A vanishing first tangent may only require a deeper local inverse, rather than remove the interior. This classifies all primes for the fixed core from its finite mode support.

The five-vertex cyclic prime core supplies a striking all-size example: its inflation count is −5+7·2^m+2·3^m. Its exact dyadic closure is {15,41,105} together with the two balls1+16Z_2 and13+16Z_2. Since its nonconstant modes are2 and3, this particular family has an open local image at every prime, despite logarithmically sparse integer support. Smaller cores in this one-module scheme have only the modes1 and2 and hence only a singleton unbounded-size dyadic limit set. Thus order5 is a genuine first birth of an open dyadic component in this specified construction, while its symmetry quotient can remain fixed.

## 4. Toward Chan–Pak: what the arithmetic does and does not buy

The three exact controls are complementary: recover a construction address, quantify the hidden symmetry debt, and lift desired local residues. They do not automatically compose. The reset changes the continuing interface; the symmetry result imposes a floor; and an inflation lift can have an enormous literal count despite agreeing with a small target modulo a high prime power.

A useful next theorem must give both physically realizable digit supply and a height/anchoring bound, while keeping an interface that permits the next step. The session does not prove such a theorem, an eventual odd-count threshold, or a full classification of all prime tournament cores.
