## Three different p-adic consumers

1. **Address recovery.** The reset construction turns a binary two-ended address into a 13-adic observable. A second physical response places the same address in a 2-adic geometry. The map preserves the address, not independent choice of residues at the two primes. Finite physical witnesses carry an explicit approximation depth.
2. **Symmetry cost.** Attachment colors act on a recursive tournament's automorphisms. The group-stabilizer exponent measures visible symmetry; a different inherited endpoint-action argument forces divisibility of Hamiltonian counts even when that visible symmetry is gone. The lost datum in identifying them is the action on path endpoints rather than on the whole extension.
3. **Interpolation.** Transitive-module inflation exports literal counts to finite exponential modes. P-adic derivatives permit exact residue lifting. The actual module size and the exponentially growing integer count are indispensable: an analytic ball is not an interval of integer counts.

These maps answer different questions. No map between all three target spaces, and no unrestricted integer selector, is assumed.

## A falsifiable next step

A useful future construction should simultaneously retain: a physically renewable root interface, complete local digits with a known inverse, and an Archimedean bound placing the actual count in the prescribed target window. The current lanes prove parts of this list separately and exhibit their losses. Searching for an operation that preserves all three is sharper than asking whether p-adic density by itself settles Chan–Pak.
