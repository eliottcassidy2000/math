## 1. Mersenne normal form

For generators M_n=2^n−1 with n>=2, replace every M_6 by M_2²M_3. After normalization every remaining index has a primitive prime divisor. Cancel equal generator factors from two equal products; a largest remaining index has a prime dividing no smaller-index factor, contradiction. This proves uniqueness, using the cited Bang–Zsigmondy theorem. Thus all relations are generated by M_6=M_2²M_3. The count formula 1+min(floor(c2/2),c3), exact r factorizations of63^(r−1), and prescribed-factor-count uniqueness follow. Excluding index3 removes the sole relation, even with index6 retained.

**Essential boundary checked and incorporated by producer:** M_1=1 is excluded. If admitted, three-factor uniqueness fails: M_6 M_1²=M_2²M_3. Infinite unit insertions also make unrestricted factorization fibers infinite. This boundary does not affect the two-run grammar with a,b>=1, whose factors have indices at least2.

The two-run factorization theorem determines an unordered exponent pair, but off-diagonal pairs must also yield distinct numerical profiles to claim profile multiplicity2. Directly the D-coordinate difference between O^aZ^b and O^bZ^a is 2^b−2^a, so it is nonzero when a!=b. No claim about multiplicities in the full grammar follows.
