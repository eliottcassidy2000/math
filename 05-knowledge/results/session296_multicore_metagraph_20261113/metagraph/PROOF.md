# The ordered-mask lift: recursive frames, phase, and class mass

Let X_n be the fixed Hamilton frame on 1→2→…→n, with one bit per unordered nonconsecutive pair E_n, bit 1 meaning the forward arc. Write a for free-bit complement and r for reversal of the index order followed by converse; on E_n, r just reflects the coordinates. A line is an a-orbit (two endpoints for n≥3), blue if each endpoint is fixed by r, black otherwise. A node is an ordinary tournament class merged with its converse. These three sorts are distinct; the inherited span is THM-796, including the two-end pullback and its phase torsor. THM-830 already has the repaired card and single seam bit for internal deletion. The statements below use *unrepaired* (genuine inherited Hamilton order) cards and arbitrary masks.

## 1. Exact gluing from any marked subset

Fix S⊆[n], |S|=k≥1. The k vertices carry the induced increasing order. Let P(S)={e∈E_n:e⊆S}, and D(S) consist of successive pairs in S that are not adjacent in [n]. Call D(S) the **shortcut mask**. The induced order on S is Hamiltonian precisely when every bit in D(S) is 1. Given u∈X_k, prescribing the genuine ordered card on S fixes exactly the bits P(S): the shortcut bits to 1 and the other old pairs from u. There are
`2^(|E_n|−|P(S)|)`
lifts, where |P(S)|=binom(k,2)−#{j:j,j+1∈S}=|E_k|+|D(S)|.

The blue count is zero if two prescribed values in one r-orbit disagree. Otherwise it is
`2^(|E_n/r|−#{r-orbits meeting P(S)})`.
Indeed a blue lift makes one independent choice per unprescribed reflection orbit. If S is reversal-invariant, this compatibility is exactly u blue; shortcuts reflect to shortcuts, so impose no further condition. In particular, inserting a single vertex away from its reflection position yields zero, one or two blue lifts: compatibility is exactly the same orbit test, and only the chord from the inserted vertex to its reflection could be a free unprescribed orbit. It is absent if those positions are adjacent. For a central insertion at an odd upper order n≥3, the number is zero unless u is blue, then 2^((n−3)/2); among the n−3 free coordinates incident to the central vertex none is reflection-fixed, so there are (n−3)/2 pairs. This includes n=3.

The rule extends to any list of prescribed cards on S_1,…,S_t: take the union of their prescribed coordinates and test agreement, then test reflection agreement for blue lifts. This is a direct arbitrary-mask analogue of the existing three-face overlap; the retained information is the *actual incidences*, not just cardinalities of overlaps. There are no further orientation constraints in the full frame cube. For one card, black lifts are total minus blue.

## 2. Lines choose a shortcut phase

Suppose D(S)≠∅ and n≥3. A line has a genuine S-card iff its shortcut bits are constant on either endpoint, in which case exactly one endpoint is good. This defines a partial function from line instances to X_k, not to an ambiguous lower line. The good endpoint is the **shortcut phase** selected by the mask. For a prescribed lower frame, the number of such upper lines is exactly the lift count in §1, with the same blue count: no two of the good endpoints are complements. For a single internal deletion, |D(S)|=1, so *every* upper line has exactly one good endpoint and the resulting map onto X_(n−1) has constant fibre 2^(n−2). This is a different quotient from repaired deletion, which deletes at both endpoints and retains a phase bit. At n=3 the sole upper line maps to the sole lower frame.

For several masks with at least one shortcut each there is a simultaneous same-endpoint lift iff the prescribed cards agree on overlaps. Then again divide by no factor of two, and use the blue orbit test. If the requirement is that each mask have a genuine card *at either endpoint*, their shortcut masks must each be monochromatic, but different masks may choose different phases. Build the equivalence relation on the union of shortcut coordinates generated by co-membership in a mask. With c equivalence classes on d distinct coordinates, the number of lines is `2^(|E_n|−d+c−1)`: choose each class bit and unconstrained bits independently, then quotient by free complement. Blue lines satisfy the same relation after additionally identifying e with r(e) for all e∈E_n; precisely `2^(C−1)` where C is the number of classes in this enlarged relation on E_n. The two tests are different: requiring a common phase identifies all d coordinates instead of just co-members. If D(S)=∅, both endpoints yield genuine cards, and one must keep two incidences rather than apply a uniqueness formula.

## 3. Push to nodes with the correct mass

For a merged node u, choose representative T, let s=1 if self-converse and 0 otherwise, and set q(u)=2−s, a(u)=|Aut(T)|, h(u)=H(T). The inherited tiling multiplicity is M(u)=q(u)h(u)/a(u): each ordered Hamilton path has trivial stabilizer, and ordinary classes are either one or two converse partners. Thus each named tiling of u contributes weight a(u)/h(u) to A000568; equivalently u contributes q(u), not M(u). The readout is exact but requires the class weights and is not a runtime improvement by itself. The blue degree plus black degree at u is M(u), with loops counted twice. Converse is already quotiented at nodes, not an extra relabelling of tilings when doing this sum. This keeps apart **frame mass** M, **class mass** q, **line instances**, and **shortcut-phase incidence**.

By projecting each genuine S-card to its node and each upper frame to its node, §§1–2 generate a span with integer multiplicities at every n. An unmarked node is not a complete recursive state: even endpoint deletion fails to descend (THM-796/metagraph_growth). The new point is the exact conditional law when shortcut-compatible intermediate frames, and possibly several masks, are retained. This neither claims a smaller complete state space nor implies that arbitrary assignments of node totals alone are realizable.

## 4. Hand controls

At n=5, S={1,3,5} has two shortcut coordinates 13,35 exchanged by reflection, and the independent fixed coordinate 15. For either lower frame, P has size 3 and |E_5|=6, giving eight lifts. There are four reflection orbits at order 5, two hit by P, so four blue lifts. At n=4, S={1,3,4} has shortcut 13. Reflection pairs 13 with 24, and fixes 14. Thus each lower frame has two lifts and exactly one blue lift, with the incident middle reflection pair already frozen. At n=5, choose S={1,3,4,5} and T={1,2,3,5}; the independent singleton shortcut masks 13 and 35 each choose their own endpoint. Every line then has both cards as separate shortcut-phase incidences, but just half its lines admit both on a common endpoint. Every blue line admits a common endpoint because reflection ties 13 to 35.

## 5. Two adjacent deletion cards reconstruct the upper line

For n≥4 take adjacent internal positions j,j+1. Use the genuine card map from §2 separately at these two positions. The pair of *actual lower frames* reconstructs the upper line. Each card encodes its entire word on P([n]\\{j}), including the forced-1 shortcut bit, up to the phase it chose. Both P-sets contain the chord 1n. Complement each prescribed word if its bit at 1n is 1, so the corner reads zero in both. Their union contains all E_n: the only pair potentially missed is {j,j+1}, which is frozen and hence not in E_n. Consequently the two normalized words agree on their overlap iff the cards can coexist, and their unique gluing gives the upper line with apex-zero representative. The number of overlap coordinates is |E_(n−2)|+1: of the consecutive surviving pairs exactly one skips the adjacent two-vertex deletion. The overlap rule also handles n=4.

At even order choose j=n/2. Reflection interchanges the two central deletions and reflects each lower card. Hence a compatible pair gives a blue line exactly when the cards are reflections of each other. For the converse implication, the pair forces reflection to fix the reconstructed line; reflection fixes the chord 1n, whereas complement flips it, so reflection must fix its two endpoints separately. This gives an exact reconstruction scheme using internal genuine cards rather than endpoint faces plus an apex bit. The old two-end phase torsor does not contradict it: that scheme has no card covering the corner.

A weaker phase-only variant uses three proper lower *line instances*, not actual cards, for n≥5. Use the prefix, suffix and S={1,2,n−1,n}. The first two are contiguous and their free coordinate sets cover E_n except the corner 1n. They can align their phases along any common free chord (available n≥5). The last mask has just one shortcut and always supplies a genuine frame at one endpoint; then forget its phase and record only its lower line. It still carries the relative bits between 1n and 1,n−1 (both free in that four-vertex frame), restoring the corner. Three is optimal for fixed masks if each card reports only its lower complement line (or inadmissibility). Let J,K be the nonempty omitted sets of any two proper masks. If they intersect, toggle a free chord incident to a common omitted vertex; every vertex of [n] has such a chord. If they are disjoint and some j in J, k in K are nonadjacent, toggle that unseen chord jk. Otherwise J×K consists entirely of path edges; since the path has no K₂,₂, at least one side is singleton, say J={j}. If j is interior, toggle its shortcut {j−1,j+1}. Its single-deletion card always exists and changing the shortcut just changes which phase is selected, invisible after passing to the lower line. The other mask omits a neighbour of j, so does not see that chord. If j is an endpoint, K must be its lone neighbouring singleton; the same argument uses that neighbouring internal vertex's shortcut. In every case toggling one free chord changes the upper line (it is not global complement). Thus no two fixed proper phase-only masks suffice.

Two oriented cards are likewise optimal for fixed proper masks at n≥4: any omitted vertex has a free incident chord, invisible to that card and its shortcut tests. At n=4 no collection of proper phase-only masks can reconstruct a line: every proper card has at most three vertices, at most one shortcut (so is admissible), and only one lower complement-line instance. Thus the phase-only and oriented-card reconstruction thresholds differ already at this first nontrivial order.
