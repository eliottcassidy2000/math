## Why the merged tiling metagraph has these weights

A **presentation** names the Hamilton frame; a **line instance**
pairs its two free-bit complements; a **merged node** folds ordinary
isomorphism and converse. A **shortcut-phase card** records which
endpoint of a line actually gives a surviving directed path when
an internal vertex is deleted. These are distinct objects.

- [Exact mask and gluing laws](metagraph/PROOF.md). Two adjacent
  internal cards, each retaining its actual oriented lower frame,
  determine the line at every order n≥4. For n≥5, three proper
  cards are enough if only the lower unoriented line is retained,
  and no two fixed proper masks are enough. Arbitrary joint
  deletion conditions and blue lifts are finite binary equality
  constraints with exact counts.
- [Node, color and A000568 readout](metagraph/READOUT.md). An ordinary
  class contributes H/|Aut| presentations. The colored merged graph
  counts each blue-incident node once and every other node twice
  for A000568. Two end-parent merged nodes need not even determine
  blue vs black; actual frame cards, not summed incidences, glue.
  None of this claims to compute the isomorphism mergers faster.

The common point is operational: what looks like a complete label
at one size may not determine a compatible lift. The replacement
certificates preserve the next operation's named root guard; the
tiling cards preserve the actual overlap and shortcut guard.
The [separate checks](audit/REVIEW.md) distinguish proofs for all
orders from literal finite controls.
