# Even pairs, odd midpoints, and difficult first births without a census

**What the even-only idea can and cannot do.** For a target h, take its first even level E. Its actual first order is E−1 if some order-E witness has a singleton strong component, otherwise it is E. One representative or a list of scalar even totals does not carry that bit. The [parity laws](parity/PROOF.md) include a counterexample at H=15 and an exact parity classification of Moon's 3/5-smooth interval chains. The [marked pair identity](root/PROOF.md) then says what an odd midpoint can read: the two even levels plus a genuinely nonnegative interaction Γ of the deleted pair. Two six-vertex witnesses with equal multisets at every even induced size have different odd deletion multisets.

**Depth before breadth.** I recovered [97,309 at exact order twelve](root/FRONTIER_97309.md) from earlier certificates and followed one literal pair deletion: 97,309 at order 12, 23,395 and 24,041 at order 11, and 5,803 at order 10, all at their certified first orders. The marked correction is 55,676; it is too large to be neglected as an interpolation error. A separate [targeted last-contact search](root/DEEP_TARGETS.md) certifies **100,003, 210,077, 210,091 and 458,759 first at order 12**, without a census of order-12 tournaments. It also supplies order-13 witnesses for 681,163 and 700,021, whose first orders remain 12 or 13 from these certificates. The [cut and residue solver](depth/PROOF.md) bounds whole cells of addresses, with separate limits for a template minimum and the true global minimum.

**Two vertices as a physical operator.** Adding a cyclic wrapping pair gives a [closed recurrence on slot polynomials](root/PROOF.md), and its shell counts decode higher slots triangularly. But the odd-order version immediately makes the Hamilton count divisible by 3, so it cannot handle odd-order primes. Different contacts give [three all-order induced ×7 constructions](construct/PROOF.md) and a four-count example p,q,7p,7q with p=882961 and q=129607 prime. The determinants give exact inverse profiles, but do not realize an arbitrary pair of requested targets.

**The 3,4,5 and p²qr analogy.** Order, Hamilton count, and slot scaling are different readings. A triangle has order/count (3,3), a strong four-block has (4,5), and ordinal stacking gives count 15 at order 7. A cyclic pair wrapped around the triangle compresses that count to its actual first order 5. The neutral pair contributes q² only to the slot polynomial, so the factor 2² belongs to the P₂ channel; 5·3·2² is a mixed reading, not an even Hamiltonian count.

No simple closed formula for every first order or proof of Chan–Pak follows. The sharpened next task is to choose the pair interaction or physical core from the target, not to fill the next row of an exhaustive census.
