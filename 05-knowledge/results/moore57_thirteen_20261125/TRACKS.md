# A forced spanning subgraph with exactly the cycle lengths 5 through 1737

**PROVED, conditional on the host existing.** Every degree-57 diameter-two Moore graph, about every prescribed root, contains the same explicitly defined spanning subgraph with precisely these simple cycle lengths:
\[
                            5,6,\ldots,1737 .
\]
Every listed length occurs through the root. This representation uses 6,385 edges on all 3,250 vertices and is independent of the actual coordinate permutations. It imposes no symmetry assumption on the host.

Order the 57 neighbors of the root as columns numbered 0 through 56. Keep their 57 root edges and the 56 edges from each column vertex to its own slots at distance two. Keep only the perfect matchings between successive slot columns, which give 56 disjoint horizontal paths, each meeting all 57 columns. This last fact follows by transporting a starting slot consecutively along bijections; no compatibility around a closed loop is required. Renumber the slots by their starting labels. This gives the asserted universal spanning graph. Compare the [arbitrary branch matching construction](BRANCH-ROUTES.md), which retains many more matchings to route every requested internal and crossing adjacency. Keeping the horizontal paths of any spanning *tree* on the branch labels likewise trivializes its matching coordinates; all missing matchings remain genuine completion data.

## A length inverse and an exact multiplicity formula

In degree \(d\), choose \(j\ge2\) distinct column vertices in the sequence \(x_1,\ldots,x_j\), with \(x_i\in\{0,\ldots,d-1\}\). Start and end at the root. Between successive column vertices travel along one horizontal track. Each such segment has length \(2+|x_{i+1}-x_i|\). Assigning different tracks to the \(j-1\le d-1\) segments always makes the cycle simple. Conversely every simple cycle through the root yields such a sequence, because a simple path within one track must be monotone. Its length is exactly
\[
                      2j+\sum_{i=1}^{j-1}|x_{i+1}-x_i|.
\]

Track assignments even give the **number of labelled simple cycles through the root of each length**. For the sequence form the closed intervals between its successive labels. Two segments may use the same track exactly when their intervals are disjoint, including endpoints. Order the intervals by increasing left endpoint, breaking ties arbitrarily. If \(b_i\) earlier intervals still contain that endpoint, they all need different tracks. The number of choices is therefore \(\prod_i(d-1-b_i)\). Multiply the corresponding power of \(z\) by this product and sum over all sequences of distinct labels of sizes 2 through \(d\); divide by two to forget direction. This is an exact generating polynomial. The observation retained by the plain length formula is the total travel; the additional data needed for multiplicities are the interval overlaps.

For the degree-57 existence assertions it suffices to put the integers 0 through 27 on the low side, and 28 through 56 on the high side. Alternate chosen labels from the two sides. An interior label contributes twice its value to the high sum or to the low sum, an endpoint contributes once. With \(n_L+n_H=j\), the number of low endpoints is \(1+n_L-n_H\); the other endpoints are high. Thus any independent choices on the two sides with these multiplicities realize length \(2j+S_H-S_L\). The [finite integer recurrence](track_lengths.py) enumerates the three possibilities at each label (unused, endpoint, internal), retaining the number chosen, the number of endpoints, and a bitset of achievable weighted sums. It gives **every integer from 5 through 1737**, with no gaps. This is a finite proof for the explicit spanning graph, with no hypothetical Moore adjacency matrix as input.

## Why the upper endpoint is exact

The total travel counts crossings of the cuts between consecutive integers on a line. For a path or a cycle through distinct labels in \(d=57\) columns, the cut after the first \(h\) labels can be crossed at most \(2\min(h,57-h)\) times, by degree two. The sum of these bounds is 1624. For a path on all 57 labels the bound cannot be reached: saturation of each of the two middle cuts requires all 56 transitions to cross that cut, leaving no transition incident to the middle label 28. Hence every root cycle has length at most \(114+1623=1737\); using fewer columns gives at most \(112+1624\).

For a cycle avoiding the root the formula instead uses a cyclic itinerary with \(j\) segments (allow \(j=2\), using two distinct tracks). Such cycles all have even length by parity of the total line travel. To exceed 1737 would require \(j=57\), travel 1624. Saturating the two middle cuts forces each of the 28 strictly low and 28 strictly high labels to spend both its incident edges crossing its respective middle cut. Every one of the 57 closed travel intervals must then touch column 28, using a different physical slot there. Only 56 slots exist. Cycles using no column vertex cannot occur within disjoint horizontal paths. This proves sharpness for the spanning graph; larger cycles in a full Moore completion are allowed.

## Seven, twenty-one, and thirteen observed on actual graphs

The same track construction in Petersen (degree 3) has exactly the lengths \(5,6,8,9\). For two columns it contributes 5 and 6. For three columns travel is 2 or 3, giving 8 and 9. The root-free subgraph is bipartite and the same middle-cut argument bounds its length by eight. Petersen itself has no 7-cycle: such a cycle would be induced, since every chord of a heptagon makes a triangle or quadrangle, and the exact induced-cycle calculation below has none. On the other hand the degree-7 track construction in Hoffman–Singleton contains **every length from 5 through 37**, including 21. Here 21 also occurs as the multiplicity of its adjacency eigenvalue \(-3\). [Hoffman–Singleton spectrum, Rowlinson and Sciriha](https://www.academia.edu/115511820/Some_properties_of_the_Hoffman_Singleton_graph)

The [induced-cycle census](induced_cycles.cpp) counts chordless cycles by their least label and first direction. Petersen's induced lengths are 5 and 6. Hoffman–Singleton's are 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10, 12, 13, 16, 18: it has a chordless 13-cycle and every 21-cycle has a chord. These predicates give different answers even on a single known graph. Compile with `c++ -std=c++17 -O3 induced_cycles.cpp -o /tmp/moore13-induced` and run the binary. The Chan–Pak holes 7 and 21 refer to the number of Hamilton paths of an entire tournament. Ordinary cycle length, induced cycle length and eigenvalue multiplicity all have independent exact readings here.

The concurrent small-graph census also identifies the symmetry of the 126,000 induced 13-cycles in Hoffman–Singleton. They form one orbit, with a reflection as stabilizer. Keeping a cyclic orientation gives a free transitive action of the automorphism group. This makes the exterior contact inventory of a single 13-cycle valid for every one of them. ## At least 84 edges must break any pairing of all 56 tracks

**PROVED conditional implication.** Pair the 56 sites in each of the 57 branches arbitrarily, allowing an independent pairing in every branch. Let the involution swap all paired sites and fix the root and its 57 neighbors. In any degree-57 Moore completion **at least 84 of its original edges map to nonedges**. These failures involve at least two branch pairs since a single pair of branches supports just 56 edges. In the sparse spanning construction one can pair the tracks the same way in all columns, preserving every one of its 6,385 edges. Thus a completion must break this apparent symmetry on edges outside that spanning construction. This quantifies the obstruction to retaining sheet symmetries in the [earlier transport model](../moore57_switching_flags_20261124/TRANSPORT.md).

Write \(P\) for the permutation matrix, \(A\) for the full adjacency matrix and \(E=(7I-A+J/65)/15\) for its orthogonal projector onto the \(-8\) eigenspace. The trace of \(P\) is 58 and that of \(AP\) is zero because all swapped vertices belong to the same branch. Thus \(E\) has rank 1520 and \(\mathrm{tr}(EP)=(7\cdot58+50)/15=152/5\). For the orthogonal projector \(D=(I-P)/2\), the positive contraction \(EDE\) has trace \(3724/5\). Its eigenvalues \(x_i\) satisfy \(\sum x_i(1-x_i)\ge4/25\). For a fixed sum, moving two fractional coordinates towards the endpoints minimizes this concave sum, leaving at most one fractional coordinate, here \(4/5\). Expanding the commutator of the projections gives
\[
 \|A-PAP\|_F^2=225\|EP-PE\|_F^2
   =1800\sum_i x_i(1-x_i)\ \ge\ 288 .
\]
Each lost undirected edge contributes four to the squared norm after counting both orientations and the equally many gained edges. We can sharpen 72 to 84 using the [101-vertex support bound for a nonzero Moore trade](../moore57_metagraph_seams_20261124/moore-switch-trades.md). Write \(m\) for the lost edges. Pair the touched sites by \(P\); their number of pairs is \(n\ge51\). Between two such pairs the old branch matching supplies zero, one or two edges. Exactly the single-edge case produces a defect. Form a simple graph \(F\) on these pairs with one edge for every such defect. At each touched pair, the two sites have the same degree in the original graph. The unaffected contacts supply equal degrees to both, so the lost contacts also divide equally. Hence every degree of \(F\) is positive and even. Moreover \(m\) itself is even: on any two branches identify the slots through their old matching. The two slot-pairings form a disjoint union of even alternating circuits. A common pair displaces nothing and a nontrivial circuit displaces its entire even number of slots.

In the normalized sums and differences of the paired sites, \(A-PAP\) is block off-diagonal, since it anticommutes with \(P\). In its off-diagonal block, an edge of \(F\) contributes a signed unit to each of the two transpose positions, and all other entries are zero. Thus \(\|A-PAP\|_2\le\rho(F)\), where \(\rho\) is the adjacency spectral radius. For a vertex \(i\) of degree \(d_i\), the sum of its neighbors' degrees is at most \(2m-2n+2+d_i\), by subtracting \(d_i\) and the degrees of its \(n-1-d_i\) nonneighbors, each at least two. Taking the inner product with a nonnegative Perron eigenvector gives \(\rho^2-\rho\le2m-2n+2\le2m-100\).

Suppose \(m\le82\). Then \(\rho\le(1+\sqrt{257})/2<80/9\), so \(\rho^2\le64+\rho<656/9\). Write \(u_i=\min(x_i,1-x_i)\). Each \(900u_i(1-u_i)\le\rho^2\): in the splitting by \(E\), the off-diagonal block of \(D\) times its transpose equals \(EDE-(EDE)^2\), and \(\|A-PAP\|_2=30\|ED-DE\|_2\). Since \(900(4/45)(41/45)=656/9\), all \(u_i<4/45\). Rounding each \(x_i\) to its nearest integer shows that \(\sum u_i\ge\mathrm{dist}(3724/5,\mathbb Z)=1/5\). The Frobenius identity above gives \(m=450\sum u_i(1-u_i)>450(41/45)(1/5)=82\), a contradiction. Evenness gives the claimed 84.
