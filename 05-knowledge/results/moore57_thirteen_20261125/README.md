# What the first 29 square-sum labels determine in a Moore graph

**OPEN.** Existence of the degree-57 diameter-two Moore graph and the Chan–Pak conjecture both remain open. The implications below use the Moore axioms on 3,250 vertices. The small graphs, the square-sum computations and the tournament constructions can be studied unconditionally.

## Exactly how much of the square-sum graph fits

Let \(Q_n\) join distinct numbers in \(\{1,\ldots,n\}\) whose sum is a square. The largest induced square-sum prefix in the pentagon, Petersen and Hoffman–Singleton graphs has respectively **3, 5 and 14** vertices. In every possible degree-57 Moore graph the cutoff is **29**. Any specified vertex can play label 29. The very next label makes a triangle on \(6,19,30\), with edge sums \(25,36,49\). This distinguishes two questions at the Hamilton-cycle threshold 32. The 32-cycle occurs, but its entire square-sum graph contains an obstruction. [Induced-prefix construction and sharp cutoffs](MOORE-CHART.md) · [Square-sum Hamilton thresholds, Gerbicz](https://oeis.org/A090461)

The same nine independent labels \(1,2,4,10,11,13,16,17,18\) generate every \(Q_n\) from 18 through 29 by taking unique common neighbors *within the prefix*. Nine is optimal there. Applying the same rule in an ambient Moore graph generates all 3,250 vertices from these same labelled seeds. The missing construction datum is the common-neighbor operation on pairs whose midpoint lies outside the prefix. A local refinement gives at least **2,720** successful fourth choices for every independent triple that closes to a Petersen graph. A single missing edge in a ten-vertex Petersen pattern detects whether the original triple generates everything. ## What thirteen carries across the historical sessions

The [historical concordance and proofs](THIRTEEN.md) follows Singer difference sets, the Conway puzzle, Paley difference classes, the Ramsey graph on 13 vertices, square-sum arrivals, Pythagorean reflections, the lonely runner, metagraph enumeration, Mersenne lifting and sporadic-group arithmetic. Four transfers make exact predictions here.

* **A Gaussian integer gives two constructions.** Squaring \(3+2i\) gives \(5+12i\), hence the triple \((5,12,13)\). Taking the quotient by \(3+2i\) gives \(i=5\) in the field of 13 elements. Under an order-13 automorphism, adjacent cyclic orbits with this step ratio force a cubic graph \(G(13,5)\). Contracting the spokes recovers the thirteen-vertex Ramsey graph. The cubic graph has 26 Hamilton cycles. More generally the Gaussian family has exact odd girth \(a+b+2\); the Moore eigenvalue bound leaves only 23 and 25 among its possible odd girths above 21 for an induced embedding. The occurrence of either embedding remains open. The class-number-two occurrence of 13 in the two-grid session belongs to a different quadratic ring. [Gaussian proof and Hamilton census](THIRTEEN.md) · [Two-grid analysis](../two_grids_ring_or_ideal_class_13_37_20261027.md)
* **Projective incidence reconstructs the exterior contacts.** With an order-13 symmetry there must be five cyclic orbits of each of the six possible steps and 220 independent orbits. Four matchings between two independent orbits develop the lines of \(PG(2,3)\). The simultaneous Singer calculation then determines exactly nine exterior orbits covering the 117 nonincident point–line pairs. [Orbit proof and Paley colors](THIRTEEN.md) · [Singer exterior reconstruction](../moore_fixed_order_spectra_20261124/SINGER-ORDER13-MODULES.md) · [Ishida, §7.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.29183v2#S7.SS3)
* **Twenty-four retains a phase ambiguity.** The old wheel uses the fact that 2 has order 12 modulo 13. Each such phase leaves two choices modulo 24. The same divisor 12 occurs in the rank-24 Niemeier system \(A_{12}^{\,2}\) with Coxeter number 13. For the Moore crossing problem, even all four-consecutive-phase margins combined leave a 1,980-dimensional space of real five-phase inventories before graph realization is imposed. * **The directed cycle placements determine a tournament height.** Two five-vertex tournaments of heights 11 and 13 both contain four cyclic triples. They have respectively one and two directed five-cycles, so the odd-cycle collection formula gives their exact difference of two. The Petersen graph lacks an ordinary 7-cycle. Hoffman–Singleton contains ordinary 21-cycles, but every one has a chord, and its longest induced odd cycle has length 13. The number 1,729 appears as an eigenspace dimension of a possible Moore graph and also as an actual tournament height admitting a one-arc step to 1,731. These observations give no further forbidden height. Transporting a tournament height while preserving induced adjacency is a stricter requirement: no height-13 tournament embeds in one of height 27. ## Exact Chan–Pak crossings obtained from the five-vertex pair

**PROVED.** For each \(w\ge1\), reversing a single arc in a strong tournament on \(w+4\) vertices realizes
\[
                  6\cdot2^w-1\ \longrightarrow\ 6\cdot2^w+1 .
\]
The first examples are \(11\to13,\ 23\to25,\ 47\to49,\ 95\to97\). For either minimal marked \(11\to13\) pair, replace all three vertices away from the marked arc independently by arbitrary nonempty tournaments. The gap remains exactly two if and only if two particular vertices stay singletons and the third replacement is transitive. More generally the designated vertex can carry any tournament \(C\). The resulting strong pair straddles \(6Q_2(C)\) with half-difference exactly \(H(C)\), where \(Q_2\) counts ordered covers by two directed paths with empty slots allowed. This gives the exact scope of a proposed recursive step and a mod-four rejection test for other substitutions. The exponent-61 perfect number is 1 modulo 3, excluding it as a midpoint in this construction. [Classification, coefficient table and replay](CHANPAK-PORT.md)

For the conjecture, the unresolved conversion is from a desired odd integer to **placements of pairwise vertex-disjoint directed odd cycles in a tournament** having that total weight at 2. The Moore graph can force a long interval of ordinary cycle lengths while leaving exactly that information undetermined. [Chan and Pak](https://www.math.ucla.edu/~pak/papers/DominoPermanent16.pdf)
