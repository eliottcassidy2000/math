# What an order-13 symmetry forces in the graph

A degree-57 Moore graph would have 3,250 vertices. Adjacent vertices have no common neighbor and distinct nonadjacent ones have exactly one. This note considers the extra hypothesis that it has an automorphism of order 13. We obtain restrictions on its undirected edges and identify three previously studied objects in their physical placement: a projective plane, the Paley graph, and a Ramsey graph. The degree-57 graph and the existence of this symmetry remain open. The Gaussian odd-girth theorem below is unconditional.

## Spectrum first: thirty cyclic orbits

**CITED.** Such an automorphism acts freely. There are 250 orbits with 13 vertices each. Write their coordinates in the field of 13 elements so a common generator adds one everywhere. Each orbit induces either an independent set or a 13-cycle of some step \(s\), up to sign. Two distinct unoriented internal steps would form a parallelogram of length four. [Mačaj and Širáň](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.laa.2009.07.018)

**CITED PREPRINT and elementary specialization.** Ishida's Theorem 1.2 and §7.3 give exactly 65 vertices adjacent to their image for every nonidentity power of the automorphism. It follows that **five orbits have each of the six step classes** and the remaining 220 are independent. This sharpens the earlier trace-only candidates 30, 120 and 210 for the total number of cyclic orbits. In the earlier equitable-half rank calculation the applicable upper bound for crossing rank is therefore 117, with nullity at least eight. [Ishida, v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.29183v2#S7.SS3) · [Earlier half calculation](../moore_fixed_order_spectra_20261124/FIFTH-POWER-HALVES.md)

Here the specialization requires only freeness. Over the 13-adic integers the vertex permutation module is free over the cyclic group ring, with 250 basis orbits. The spectral projector \(E_7=(400I+50A-J)/750\) is integral at 13. Its image is a projective module over this local group ring, hence free. The ring is local because reduction modulo 13 gives \(\mathbb F_{13}[u]/(u^{13})\), with \(u=g-1\), and the group ring is finite over the complete local base. Thus the trace of any nonidentity group element on this image vanishes. Direct evaluation of \(\mathrm{tr}(gE_7)\) gives \((50a_1-3250)/750=0\), with \(a_1\) the number of vertices adjacent to their image. Every orbit of the queried step contributes thirteen such vertices.

## Four contacts are a projective plane; three contacts have Paley colors

**PROVED, conditional on the graph and action.** On ordered orbit labels \(U,V\) record the offset set \(S_{UV}\), with \(U_x\sim V_{x+d}\) for \(d\in S_{UV}\). The reverse set is its negative. Write \([d]=\{d,-d\}\) and represent the six classes by 1 through 6. Varying the zero of an orbit shifts its contact sets; its unsigned internal differences are invariant.

At an independent orbit the unsigned differences of unordered pairs from all exterior contact sets partition the six classes **exactly once**. They count common neighbors between two specified vertices of the orbit, through a third orbit. At a cyclic orbit of step \(s\), remove \([s],[2s]\) first. They already have their unique direct or two-step route. Thus each set of size \(m\) consumes \(\binom m2\) distinct places in the remaining four or six. A cyclic orbit either has four double contacts or one triple and one double. In the latter case the triple must use the classes \(s\{[3],[4],[6]\}\). Its remaining double has difference \([5s]\).

A contact of size four consumes all six differences at *both* ends. It can therefore join only independent orbits, and these links form a matching. The four-element offset set is an affine image of \(\{0,1,3,9\}\), a cyclic \((13,4,1)\) Singer difference set. To check the only finite classification here, the 52 affine images are exactly the 52 four-subsets with six different unsigned pair differences. Its translates are the 13 lines of \(PG(2,3)\). Thus the two orbits induce the point–line incidence graph on 26 vertices. Each of the 13 points and lines has 53 exterior neighbors on distinct orbit labels. Exactly nine orbit labels touch both panels, each once on each side, because the nine nonincidence offsets must have one two-step route each. There are 44 labels on each side touching that panel alone, and 151 touching neither. A shared matching pair carries one of the nine missing offsets, so retaining just the numbers of external neighbors loses the offset assignment.

Write a link of size three as an edge in a graph on the 250 orbit labels. Its components are isolated vertices, paths, or **even cycles**. Along any component with at least two edges, all orbits are independent, and the edges alternate between triples whose pair differences are squares and triples whose pair differences are nonsquares in \(\mathbb F_{13}\). Here are all eight possible difference triples without repetition, represented unsigned.
\[
 123,\quad134,\quad145,\quad156,\quad235,\quad246,\quad256,\quad346.
\]
They follow by taking three positions \(0,x,y\) and forming \([x],[y],[x-y]\). The only complementary pair is \(134\) with \(256\), precisely the quadratic residues with their complement. Each degree-two site of the triple-link graph uses both. A cyclic site necessarily has a mixed signature, a dilate of \(346\), so its triple is an isolated component. Both endpoints cannot be cyclic, by the next test. Its independent partner then needs exactly three additional doubles. If there are \(h\) projective-plane pairs and \(t\) cyclic orbits using a triple, necessarily \(t+2h\le220\). A common nonresidue relabeling swaps the Paley colors throughout a component without changing the alternation. [Exact finite configurations](thirteen.py)

## The quadratic root picks a unique crossing and a Ramsey quotient

Normalize one cyclic step to 1 and write the other as \(t\in\{1,\ldots,6\}\). Equal-step orbits cannot be joined: one matching between them would make a square with their parallel internal edges. Otherwise single matching links are locally possible, and the following gives **all** possible nonzero differences \([d]\) between two crossing matching offsets.

| Second step \(t\) | Allowed double differences \([d]\) |
|---:|---|
| 2 | 5, 6 |
| 3 | 5 |
| 4 | 6 |
| 5 | none |
| 6 | 3, 4 |

Indeed exclude the unsigned classes of \(1,2,t,2t\) for triangles and two-step internal routes, and also \(1+t,1-t\) for the squares with one internal edge on each side. A square using four cross edges would require a repeated difference, impossible for just two voltages in odd characteristic. These exhaust the ways a triangle or square can occur inside the pair. The table allows at most two unsigned differences, while a triple needs three. It follows that no two cyclic orbits have a triple contact.

In particular the ratio with \(t^2=-1\), namely \([5]\), is the only distinct step ratio permitting just one matching. A linked pair at this ratio induces the generalized Petersen graph \(G(13,5)\): two cyclic layers of steps 1 and 5 and a matching between corresponding indices. Contract the matching. The result is \(\operatorname{Cay}(\mathbb Z/13,\{\pm1,\pm5\})\), the repo's unique triangle-free Ramsey graph on thirteen vertices with independence number four. Hence every five spoke labels contain two with an internal-neighbor edge, and four pairwise avoiding labels are possible. For comparison the matching in Petersen \(G(5,2)\) contracts to \(K_5\). An actual link between the indicated two Moore orbits is an additional hypothesis. [Ramsey graph and uniqueness provenance](../ramsey_saturated_ports_20261030/PROOFS.md) · [McKay's Ramsey census](https://users.cecs.anu.edu.au/~bdm/data/ramsey.html)

The local quarter-turn has a concrete cost if one tries to orient the odd cycles canonically. On the Ramsey quotient the map \(x\mapsto5x\) exchanges the two step classes and exchanges the Paley colors because 5 is a nonsquare. Its square is reversal \(x\mapsto-x\). A translation-invariant orientation chooses one direction for each of the two circuits, so the square takes every such orientation to its converse. The four possible orientations form a single free orbit of the quarter-turn. The same square reverses every translation-invariant tournament completion on the 13 labels. Consequently preserving the quarter-turn as an undirected symmetry and reading it as a directed tournament automorphism requires incompatible orientation choices. This is a local symmetry statement; extension of the quarter-turn to the hypothetical global graph is a separate requirement.

**FINITE-EXACT.** \(G(13,5)\) has a simple cycle of every length from 7 through 26 inclusive. There are 26 heptagons and 26 Hamilton cycles, counted up to cyclic rotation and traversal reversal. The Hamilton cycles form exactly two free orbits under translation: an invariant Hamilton cycle would use zero or all thirteen spokes, while a spanning cycle uses a positive even number. Petersen has respectively 12, 10, 15, 20 cycles of lengths 5, 6, 8, 9 and no others. The [cycle replay](thirteen.py) fixes the least vertex of each cycle and divides the two traversal directions. Thus 7 and 21 are actual cycle lengths in the Gaussian 13 patch. The forbidden Hamiltonian-path counts 7 and 21 concern a different observable on tournaments.

### Exactly which third-orbit contacts complete the core routes

In this linked pair the unresolved differences inside the two layers are \(\{3,4,5,6\}\) and \(\{1,2,4,6\}\), respectively, represented unsigned. Between layers the four internal routes through one spoke and the direct spoke already cover \(0,\pm1,\pm5\), leaving eight **signed** differences. Thus sixteen slots remain. For one exterior orbit, choose one of its vertices, and let \(P,Q\subseteq\mathbb F_{13}\) be its neighbor indices on the layers. Its contribution is the unsigned pair differences in \(P\), those in \(Q\), and the signed differences \(Q-P\). These entries must all be unresolved and be distinct within each field. Cyclic translates of the representative then cover exactly the indicated classes.

**FINITE-EXACT.** There are 86 distinct nonempty slot masks. Disjoint covers of all sixteen slots give 76 different multisets of contact sizes \((|P|,|Q|)\), omitting orbits with a single overall contact or none. By number of exterior orbits meeting both layers the profile counts are

|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Size profiles | 3 | 11 | 18 | 20 | 12 | 8 | 4 |

At the minimum of two such orbits, up to swapping panels, the full profile is either \((2,3),(2,1),(2,0),(2,0),(0,2)\) or \((2,2),(2,2),(2,0),(2,0),(0,2),(0,2)\). The two common orbits split the eight cross classes as \(6+2\) or \(4+4\).

The masks come from all subsets of size at most three on each layer, imposing the displayed distinctness tests. At any uncovered pivot slot branch over every fitting mask containing the pivot; record the sorted size multiset at exhaustion. Conversely each exact cover gives a local extension: realize each mask on its own independent exterior orbit, leave all exterior-to-exterior edges absent, and add single-contact orbits until each core vertex has degree 57. Each charge to a core vertex through a mask is bounded by twice the number of slots that mask uses, so even the crude bound of 32 initial exterior neighbors per core vertex is below the allowance of 54. At most 16 mask orbits and 108 single-contact orbits are needed among the available 248. The slot partition supplies exactly the missing routes and forbids all triangles and squares in this partial graph. Degrees, adjacencies and missing routes of the exterior vertices remain to be completed globally. [Exact-cover replay](thirteen.py)

## Prescribing the shortest odd cycle and testing its size in a Moore graph

**PROVED.** For coprime positive integers \(a,b\) of opposite parity, put \(N=a^2+b^2\) and \(t=a/b\pmod N\). Then \(t^2=-1\pmod N\) and the odd girth of \(G(N,t)\) is exactly \(a+b+2\). Every prescribed odd \(g\ge5\) occurs, with the smallest order in this Gaussian family equal to \((g-2)^2+1\).

To see the lower bound, project a closed walk to the matching quotient, counting signed steps of size 1 and \(t\) in an integer pair \((x,y)\). Closing means \(x+t y=0\bmod N\). Its kernel lattice has the orthogonal basis \((a,-b),(b,a)\): both vectors solve the congruence, and their determinant is \(N\), the full index. Each basis vector has taxicab length \(a+b\). Any other nonzero vector, apart from their negatives, has Euclidean length at least \(\sqrt{2N}\ge a+b\). A closed walk traverses an even number of spokes. On an odd cycle this makes \(x+y\) odd, ruling out the zero displacement. A mixed cycle therefore has at least \(a+b\) internal steps and at least two spokes. The cycle along one layer has length \(N\ge a+b+2\). Equality in the mixed bound is achieved by the two straight runs of lengths \(a,b\) with signed displacement \((a,-b)\) and two spokes. The runs have distinct interior vertices. For fixed sum \(a+b=g-2\), consecutive \(a=(g-3)/2,b=(g-1)/2\) minimize the sum of squares and meet the coprimality and parity conditions. They give \(N=((g-2)^2+1)/2\) and \(t=g-2\bmod N\).

For \(g=5,7\) this gives Petersen and the 26-vertex Ramsey patch above. The Gaussian integer \(3+2i\) has norm 13, and its square \(5+12i\) gives the Pythagorean triple \((5,12,13)\) used in a previous square-sum construction. In the cyclic quotient \(i\) may be read as 5 since \(3+2\cdot5=13\). This explains exactly why 5 is the exceptional crossing ratio and why two signed runs of lengths 3 and 2 give a heptagon. The [square-sum reflection theorem, THM-4510](../../../01-canon/theorems/THM-4510-sum-graphs-as-reflection-orbits-pythagorean-zigzags.md) uses the same parameters on the Hamilton path through \(1,\ldots,143\), with a different adjacency rule.

**PROVED exclusion for a hypothetical degree-57 Moore graph.** Any induced cubic subgraph on \(v\) vertices satisfies \(v\le550\). Subtract the mean from its indicator vector and apply the least-eigenvalue bound \(-8\) to the resulting quadratic form. This gives \(3v-57v^2/3250\ge-8(v-v^2/3250)\), as claimed. Therefore every Gaussian chart above with odd girth at least 27 is too large to occur induced. Above 21 the only odd girths surviving this necessary test are 23 and 25. For 23 the possible periods are 221 and 233; for 25 they are 265 and 269. Enumerate \(a+b=g-2\), \(2(a^2+b^2)\le550\), \(\gcd(a,b)=1\); the [replay](thirteen.py) gives exactly these choices. None can have its translation by one extend to an ambient graph automorphism: its order would be divisible, respectively, by 17, 233, 53 or 269. All four fall outside the possible prime divisors \(2,3,5,7,11,13,19\) in [Mačaj and Širáň's classification](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.laa.2009.07.018). Existence of an asymmetric embedding at these sizes is open. This bound concerns induced Gaussian charts, not all cubic graphs or the odd girth of the ambient Moore graph.

Daneshgar and Madani give an integer-programming formula for the odd girth of general \(G(n,k)\). The calculation above solves the Gaussian subfamily by orthogonality and supplies the induced-size obstruction using the ambient Moore spectrum. [Daneshgar and Madani](https://arxiv.org/abs/1501.06551)
