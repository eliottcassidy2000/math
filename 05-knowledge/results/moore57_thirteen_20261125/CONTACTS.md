# A mandatory 13-cycle and the exact pair-cover problem around it

**CONDITIONAL on existence, proved from the Moore axioms.** Every induced path of ten edges in a degree-57 Moore graph extends, with the original path fixed, to at least **40 induced thirteen-cycles**. The resulting cycles have disjoint added vertex pairs. Around any such cycle there are 52 pairs of sites whose unique short connection leaves the cycle. Their contact patterns have only **58 possible multiplicity profiles** at the level of the boundary pair equations. Candidate graphs must also satisfy the internal-neighbor constraints below. The global existence problem remains open.

## Closing a prescribed path

In a degree-\(d\), diameter-two Moore graph the endpoints \(a,b\) of an induced path of length \(k\ge3\) have a unique common neighbor \(s\). The \(d-1\) neighbors of \(a\) other than \(s\) are perfectly matched to the \(d-1\) analogous neighbors of \(b\): for each such neighbor of \(a\), take its unique common neighbor with \(b\). Triangle and quadrangle exclusion give the inverse and exclude \(s\). Each matching edge closes the path with three edges unless an added vertex equals or touches an interior vertex. On the \(a\)-side each of the \(k-1\) interior vertices forbids at most one site (itself if adjacent to \(a\), otherwise the unique common neighbor). The first and second interior vertices forbid the same site. The same argument at \(b\) excludes at most \(k-2\) sites there. Thus at least
\[
                        d+3-2k
\]
distinct induced \((k+3)\)-cycles extend this particular path. The endpoints have no further adjacency to the opposite added site; that would create a second common neighbor. Induced paths of length ten exist from every chosen vertex at degree 57: each earlier vertex rules out at most one possible next neighbor at a step. This strengthens the earlier [seven-cycle closure from a five-edge path](../moore57_compact_blue_routes_20261124/moore-gram-overlap.md) to a uniform quantitative statement about prescribed paths. A separate [rooted chart](MOORE-CHART.md) gives a longer interval of forced cycle lengths.

## Pair routes and boundary crossings

Number a fixed induced cycle by \(\mathbb Z/13\). A vertex off the cycle sees a set \(S\) of boundary sites. Distinct sites in \(S\) are at cyclic distance at least three, since a smaller distance gives a triangle or quadrangle. Conversely, every pair at cyclic distance 3, 4, 5 or 6 has exactly one common neighbor, necessarily exterior. Its owner is the block \(S\) containing that pair. Thus the 52 edges of the complement of the squared cycle must decompose into cliques of sizes 2, 3 and 4. Repeated singleton and empty contact sets are allowed.

Let \(x_j\) be the number of exterior sites with \(j\) boundary contacts; write \(t=x_3\), \(q=x_4\). Counting sites, single incidences and pairs gives
\[
 (x_0,x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4)=(2574-t-3q,\ 611+3t+8q,\ 52-3t-6q,\ t,\ q).
\]
Each four-site set has cyclic gaps 3,3,3,4 and is a translate of \(\{0,3,6,9\}\). The translates indexing chosen blocks cannot be separated by \(\pm3,\pm6\), since two blocks would share a pair. In the cyclic order generated by step 3 they must therefore have spacing at least three: \(q\le4\).

**FINITE-EXACT boundary classification.** For each \(q\), precisely the integers \(0\le t\le t_{\max}\) occur in the boundary pair problem:

| \(q\) | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| \(t_{\max}\) | 15 | 13 | 11 | 9 | 5 |
| Minimum sites with exactly two contacts | 7 | 7 | 7 | 7 | 13 |

The exact finite program chooses among the 65 admissible triples and 13 quadruples, allowing a given pair in at most one chosen block. Give each uncovered pair its own site. Add the singleton sites locally needed to bring every boundary degree to 57; even using each pair separately consumes at most eight of the 55 available incidences at each site. Reduce the number of triples by splitting a triangle into three two-contact blocks. This proves the full interval from each certified maximum, giving 58 profiles in all. Exhaustion here means the pair problem, before specifying edges among outside sites. Run `python3 cycle_contacts.py` with OR-Tools. [Exact model](cycle_contacts.py)

Draw the 52 pairs as chords in their boundary order. Of the \(\binom{13}{4}=715\) potential alternating pairs, removing the thirteen cyclic-distance-two chords loses \(13\cdot10\) crossings, with thirteen counted twice. Boundary edges contribute none. Thus 598 pairs alternate. A pair of alternating chords belongs to the same physical owner exactly once per four-contact site (the two diagonals of its four labels). Consequently **exactly \(598-q\)** alternating pairs of two-edge routes have four different endpoints and different internal vertices. In a drawing with all these routes on the same side of the cycle, each such pair must cross. Each crossing of two physical edges can serve at most nine route pairs, since an edge to a contact of size four participates in at most three routes. This gives at least \(\lceil(598-q)/9\rceil\) physical crossings in a general-position one-disk drawing of the boundary contacts. The distinction between a shared vertex, a chord crossing and a crossing edge of a graph partition is essential here.

## An exact test for the remaining edges

For every exterior site with contact set \(S\), the contact sets of its **exterior** neighbors partition the complement of \(S+\{-1,0,1\}\), ignoring empty sets. Each excluded label is already reached directly or along the cycle; each other label needs exactly one two-edge route. In particular a four-contact site has exactly one singleton-contact neighbor at the uncovered thirteenth label and **52 zero-contact neighbors**. This is a placement rule on individual sites beyond the size table.

This also gives an exact reconstruction test. On 3237 exterior sites choose contact blocks realizing the pair equations, assign the singleton incidences as above, and give a site of type \(S\) exterior degree \(57-|S|\). Enforce the partition rule on each site. With cycle and contact edges restored, all 3250 vertices now have degree 57, and all pairs involving a cycle vertex have their Moore incidence. To finish, forbid triangles and quadrangles in the exterior graph and forbid an exterior two-edge path joining two distinct exterior sites with a shared boundary contact. Indeed a mixed triangle with one boundary vertex or a mixed square using a cycle edge is already excluded by the partition rule; the exterior two-edge test rules out a square visiting just one cycle vertex, and the pair cover rules out a square alternating twice with the cycle. Then the entire graph has girth at least five; the \(1+57+57\cdot56=3250\) distinct sites in every radius-two ball give diameter two. Conversely a Moore graph meets every listed constraint. The cycle chart fixes a necessary subconfiguration in all candidates, with no hypothesis on an equitable split or an automorphism.
