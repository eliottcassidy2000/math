# Cartier rank and period dichotomy

Status: PROVED CONSEQUENCE, conditional on the explicitly inherited all-prime two-step congruence and inert non-fixed-point necessity theorem. No new independent certification of their modular inputs or novelty claim is made. This supplement does not modify the frozen Frobenius audit packet.

## Exact statement

Let p be prime, R=Z/p²Z, G=sum c(n)t^n in R[[t]], G(0)=1, and C_p(sum a(n)t^n)=sum a(pn)t^n. Put K=C_pG and M=RG+RK, the submodule of the full formal-series module, with coefficientwise equality at every n>=0.

Inputs:
1. C_p²G=G modulo p² for every prime.
2. For p=7 or p modulo7 in {1,2,4}, C_pG=G modulo p². For every other prime, C_pG differs from G already modulo p. This stronger mod-p necessity is essential: mere inequality modulo p² would not suffice for freeness.

Then:
- If p is split or p=7, M is free rank1, C_p acts as identity, and G has exact Cartier period1.
- If p is inert (p modulo7 in {3,5,6}), M is free rank2 with basis (G,K). C_p acts by the swap matrix [[0,1],[1,0]], has characteristic and monic minimal polynomial X²-1, and G has exact Cartier period2, both modulo p and modulo p². These primes are odd. The eigenspaces in M are precisely R(G+K)/2 and R(G-K)/2, and M is their direct sum.

The characteristic polynomial here is that of C_p restricted to this finite free module M, not of the infinite series module R[[t]]. The period assertion concerns G, not every vector of M: the plus eigenline is fixed.

## Proof and ring audit

Since C_p²G=G, C_pK=G, so M is stable and the swap rule is exact. For inert p, the non-fixed-point input provides some n>=1 such that the coefficient of G-K at n is nonzero modulo p, hence is a unit of R. The constant coefficients of both G and K are1. Thus aG+bK=0 implies a+b=0 by the constant coefficient, followed by a(G-K)=0. Its unit coefficient forces a=0, hence b=0. This proves freeness over the ring R, not just independence over its residue field. No uniform location or bound on that witness is claimed.

For a useful finite-coordinate view, the two rows at indices0 and n form [[1,1],[c(n),c(pn)]], whose determinant c(pn)-c(n) is a unit. This is a left inverse certificate for the embedding of R² into the full formal-series module; finite-coordinate freeness is sufficient because the defining module relation is coefficientwise at every index.

The swap characteristic polynomial is X²-1. It annihilates the operator; no monic degree1 polynomial does, since C_pG=K is independent of G. For any polynomial, division by the monic polynomial X²-1 leaves a degree<2 remainder; independence also shows this remainder cannot annihilate C_p unless zero. Thus the full annihilator ideal in R[X] is generated by X²-1.

Every inert p is odd, so2 is a unit. In basis (G,K), the vectors e+=(G+K)/2 and e-=(G-K)/2 form a basis because their change-of-basis determinant is -1/2, a unit. Their eigenvalues are +1 and -1. Conversely solving C_p(aG+bK)=+/-(aG+bK) gives exactly the stated eigenlines. G=e++e- has both nonzero components. Input2 rules out period1, while input1 gives period2. The same reasoning modulo p proves exact period2 there.

In the split/ramified branch K=G, so M=RG. This is free rank1 because the constant coefficient of G is1. The operator is identity and has characteristic/minimal polynomial X-1. The prime2 belongs here (2 modulo7 is a square residue), and no division by2 is used in its proof. At7 the conclusion uses the separate ramified input, not an unramified character calculation.

A scalar-eigenvector interpretation of G at inert primes is false, even for a scalar other than -1: K=lambda G would force lambda=1 from constants, contradicting the unit difference coefficient. No identification with a Paley adjacency operator, tournament, Frobenius action on an unrelated module, or Collatz dynamics is asserted. This is a coefficient-module Cartier dichotomy with the same split/inert prime partition.
