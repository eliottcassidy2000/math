# Finite affine solvability, correlated CRT, and legal lifts of every group element

2026-10-04. **PROVED:** the corrected finite group, its derived series,
the modulo95 obstruction, and the guarded all-element lifting theorem.
**FINITE-EXACT:** the explicitly bounded checks below. **OPEN:** completion
of every supplied positive source. Constructing an ancestor of a supplied
hub is a different quantifier. No historical-priority claim is made.

The positive result is that every element of the correct finite affine
group can be realized by a legal inverse Collatz path from a supplied hub
coprime to3. It can be realized arbitrarily high above that hub, preserving
a supplied first-hit root certificate. Its potentially enormous exponent
has a short exact representation. Solvability alone supplies neither the
integer guards nor a descending path from an arbitrary supplied source.

## 1. Inheritance and the live board

The [affine blueprint audit](collatz_blueprint_20260921_affine.md), §§2–3,
already proves that unrestricted inverse branches generate
`Z[1/6] semidirect <2,3>`, a metabelian group. It also proves freeness of
the positive inverse-word semigroup, its exact ternary guard, and its
ternary-source/dyadic-endpoint progression correspondence. These facts
are inherited. The [marked-axis note](geometry_collatz_drift_carriers_20261004.md)
retains the ordered carry, while the incoming
[branch-toll note](collatz_branch_toll_rank_20261004.md), §3, demonstrates
that a nonzero commutator can prevent two operation orders from both being
legal. The [bounded-depth obstruction](collatz_partition_cover_20261004.md)
excludes universal smaller-child coverage by a uniformly bounded pair of
word depths; it does not exclude the constructed ancestors below.

Closest mechanism: exact guarded inverse words. Canonical hostile:
`E(1)=1/3`, despite E being a permutation modulo every modulus coprime6.
Corrected near miss: local CRT generator choices need not combine to one
global word. Least-used sidecar: one global pair of slope exponents.

The concept board is **derived series / shared slope exponents / ordered
carry / ternary precision / supplied hub / first-hit height**. Anchor:
retain legality in a group-inspired coverage construction. Niche: identify
the exact composite-modulus group. Wildcard: turn a finite Cayley walk
into a compressed legal certificate. Burnside's theorem motivates the
question; the proofs below use elementary affine algebra instead.

**CITED, checked:** Burnside's theorem says a finite group of order
`p^a q^b` is solvable. The original [1904 publisher record](https://londmathsoc.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1112/plms/s2-1.1.388)
and the theorem in [Etingof et al.'s author-hosted lectures](https://math.mit.edu/~etingof/cltrunc.pdf)

about the prime factors occurring in individual affine slopes.

## 2. The exact finite group and the modulo95 CRT obstruction

Fix `m>1`, `gcd(m,6)=1`. Put

    D(x)=2x, E(x)=(2x-1)/3,
    H_m=<2,3> inside (Z/mZ)^*,
    G_m=<D,E> inside AGL(1,Z/mZ).

Division here is modular inversion. The group also equals the one
generated by the unguarded shortcut branches `T0=x/2,T1=(3x+1)/2`, since
these are the inverses of D,E. The exact statement is

    G_m={(a,b): a in H_m, b in Z/mZ},
    G_m = (Z/mZ,+) semidirect H_m,  |G_m|=m |H_m|.       (1)

**Proof.** Every generated multiplier is in H_m. Conversely the rational
identity

    [D,E]=D E D^-1 E^-1 : x -> x-1/3

reduces modulo m to a translation generating the entire additive group.
Consequently every translation belongs to G_m. Removing E's intercept
gives scaling by2/3; together with D this gives precisely H_m. This proves
(1). The quotient by translations is abelian, so `G_m'` is contained in
translations. The displayed commutator generates all translations, hence

    G_m'=(Z/mZ,+),  G_m''={identity}.                    (2)

Thus its derived length is exactly2. This holds even when |G_m| has more
than two prime divisors: |G_19|=342 and |G_95|=3420. Burnside's order
hypothesis is unnecessary for this particular group.

**Correction to a printed source formula.** [Monks–Monks–Monks–Monks,
arXiv1204.3904, Theorem3.1 and its CRT paragraph](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1204.3904)
state a product of all local multiplier groups. The full product is not
generally generated by the same two global units. At m=95=5*19,

    ord_5(2)=4, ord_19(2)=18, ord_95(2)=36,
    3=2^31 mod95.

Therefore H_95 is cyclic of order36. The two local groups have orders4
and18, whose product is72. The actual affine-group order is3420, while
the printed product would give6840. This is an exact counterexample to
that formula, not a claim about the paper's other theorems.

The missing coordinate is visible through quadratic characters. Both2
and3 are nonsquares modulo5 and modulo19, so every global multiplier has

    chi_5(a)=chi_19(a).                                (3)

The right side defines an index-two subgroup of the72 units; its36
elements are exactly H_95. For instance slope7 is allowed in each local
unit group but violates (3). The independent finite scan finds95 the
smallest failure among all smaller admissible moduli.

For general `m=product q_i`, CRT still embeds H_m in `product H_(q_i)`.
It is the **image of the single map**

    Z^2 -> product H_(q_i), (u,v) -> (2^u 3^v mod q_i)_i. (4)

It projects onto each factor but can be a proper subgroup of the product.
One common exponent pair, or equivalent relations between its local
characters, is the required sidecar. This correction does not remove
translations, change either generator's modular order, or invalidate the
translation-based proof of arithmetic-progression sufficiency. No such
dependency is inferred merely from the printed formula's failure.

## 3. Why a group identity is not yet a legal path

On positive integers D is always a legal inverse shortcut edge. E is
legal exactly at `x=2 mod3`, and then its output is odd. The classical
formal translation word, read chronologically, is

    T0, E, T0, T1, D, D : x -> x+1.                    (5)

It has **empty legal integer domain**: after a legal E the next T0 tries
to halve an odd integer. If either earlier step is illegal, the path has
already failed. Consequently even a useful unit translation in the group
cannot simply be applied as that particular integer proof move.

For a positive inverse word w in D,E, let L be its length, e its E-count,
and B its carry. Starting from B=e=0, update

    D: B<-2B;       E: B<-2B+3^e, e<-e+1.

Then

    W(x)=(2^L x-B)/3^e.                               (6)

The inherited guard theorem says every intermediate edge is legal iff
`2^L x=B mod3^e`. For e>0 the corresponding input residue is a unit
modulo3. Briefly, the final congruence reduces to every prefix congruence;
at a legal E step a positive input is at least2 and its output is positive.
This supplies exactly the coordinate that (5) forgot.

## 4. A constructive legal lift of every finite affine element

**PROVED.** Let m be as above, let u be a supplied positive odd integer
coprime to3, and let g belong to G_m. There is a positive odd integer n>u
and an actual accelerated Collatz route from n to u such that:

1. every odd state before u is strictly greater than u;
2. n is also coprime to3;
3. the inverse route's formal affine map, reduced modulo m, equals g;
4. infinitely many such n are obtained with one fixed inverse word and
   an arithmetic progression of initial doubling exponents.

A supplied first-hit route from u to1 therefore splices to a first-hit
route from n to1. No route home for u is assumed or supplied by the group.

**Construction.** Let N=|G_m|. In the positive Cayley digraph, draw the two
edges `h->D h` and `h->E h` from each h. This finite graph is strongly
connected, since inverses of each generator are positive powers of that
generator. Every vertex has in-degree and out-degree2. An Euler circuit
starting at the identity uses its2N edges once. Each vertex is the target
of exactly one E-edge, so the N prefixes ending at E-edges visit all group
elements exactly once. These are the marked prefixes.

Append one extra E, obtaining a master word W of length `L=2N+1` and
E-count `e=N+1`. Let c be its unit input guard modulo `3^e`.
Because2 generates the units modulo every power of3, solve

    2^k u=c mod3^e,  0<=k<2*3^(e-1).                 (7)

For completeness, `ord_(3^e)(2)=2*3^(e-1)` follows from
`v3(4^(3^j)-1)=j+1`; cubing `1+3^d t`, with3 not dividing t, increases
this valuation exactly once. The implementation solves (7) one ternary
digit at a time, testing exactly three lifts at each stage.

Set `J=lcm(2*3^N,ord_m(2))`. Increase k by multiples of J until `k>=2L`.
This preserves both (7) and the initial scaling modulo m. Every marked
prefix is then legal after D^k. Its modular action is `h D^k`; right
multiplication by D^k permutes G_m. Select the marked prefix for which
`h D^k=g`. Its last E makes the endpoint odd. The extra terminal E in the
master word ensures that every marked endpoint is a3-unit: once an
integer is divisible by3, doublings keep it divisible by3 and no later E
can be legal.

**Strict-height proof.** For any positive legal input x,
`D(x)>=x/3` and `E(x)>=x/3`. After any j<=L letters of W, the state is
therefore at least `2^k u/3^j`. Since k>=2L and `4^L>3^L`, this is strictly
greater than u. The initial doublings also exceed u. Reversing the chosen
prefix gives a first-hit route to u, with no root visit before it. This
proves all the path and splicing assertions.

Increasing k again by any positive multiple of J keeps the same selected
prefix, modular map, guards and height bound. Its endpoint increases
strictly and without bound. This proves the infinite-family assertion.

The construction uses at most N accelerated inverse edges. Its unaccelerated
shortcut cost is `k+j` for a marked prefix of length j<=2N. The initial
exponent satisfies `k<2L+J`; this is a finite bound, generally enormous.
The theorem is a complete modular-action lift, not an efficient search for
a smaller child. In fact its height guard deliberately constructs larger
ancestors. The familiar residue-representative result follows by choosing
g(x)=x+(a-u), but equality with a prescribed integer source does not follow.

## 5. Compressed certificate and precision budget

The script stores u, m, g, k and the selected D/E word, without expanding
2^k. If its carry data are (j,r,B), its exact endpoint is the expression

    n=(2^(k+j)u-B)/3^r.                               (8)

The certificate reader checks the ternary guard, modular affine target,
last E, output ternary unit, and the sufficient bound k>=2j. To recover
n modulo any positive q, compute the numerator modulo `q*3^r` and only
then divide by3^r. This remains correct when q has a factor3. A second
reader evaluates each D/E step modulo a decreasing precision budget,
consuming one factor3 at each E. The two readers are independent.

Grouping doublings between E-letters produces the exact accelerated
inverse exponents. There are r odd steps and total halving cost k+j.
For the ordinary map with separate `3n+1` and halving operations the cost
is k+j+r. These can be huge integers with short binary representations.
No claim equates storage size with the time required to print n or all of
its literal halving steps.

One reproducible root example for m=5 and g(x)=x+1 has

    k=5524538283,
    w=DDDDEDDDEDEEDDEDEEDEDEEDEEDE,
    inverse odd exponents=(5524538288,4,2,1,3,2,1,2,2,1,2,1,2).

It describes a strict first-hit root certificate with13 odd steps, while
the source itself has billions of bits. Its residues modulo5,9,19,4096
are respectively2,2,2,2553. This example illustrates constructive coverage
of modular targets and the cost of restoring the missing ternary guard.

## 6. Repeated-word fuel and a guarded refuelling compiler

The same source-dependent discrete logarithm gives a focused repeat
construction. Let a fixed inverse block w have length L, r>0 E-letters,
carry B, and put q=2^L,d=3^r. Its repeated affine map is

    W^t(x)=[q^t x-B(q^t-d^t)/(q-d)]/d^t.

Since q-d is a3-unit, for a **positive integer** x the exact guard is

    w^t is legal iff (q-d)x-B=0 mod3^(rt).             (9)

This follows by reducing the displayed numerator modulo d^t and applying
the inherited whole-word guard theorem. It is an integrality criterion
with a positive-source premise; it does not call a negative or nonintegral
trajectory a positive Collatz path. Each repetition consumes r ternary
digits unless the input is precisely the rational fixed point. A finite
integer valuation cannot fund indefinitely many copies.

For a supplied positive odd3-unit u, choose a by

    2^a u=B/(q-d) mod3^(rt+1).                        (10)

This is again one base2 discrete logarithm. Increase a in the joint
period `lcm(2*3^(rt),ord_m(2))` until `a>=2Lt`. If w ends E, the certificate
`D^a w^t` constructs a positive odd3-unit n, with every new state above u
and a strict first-hit route to u. Positivity and height follow from the
same prefix bound as §4; they are not inferred from (10) in isolation.
The target affine map is computed from this certificate, rather than
being independently prescribed as in the Cayley-walk theorem.

The extra digit in (10) ensures `W^t(2^a u)=B/(q-d) mod3`, hence the
endpoint remains a3-unit. It is sufficient, not necessary for that unit
property. For w=E, the exact single-digit fuel at x=5 gives E(5)=3, a leaf;
the same amount at x=11 gives E(11)=7, which is a3-unit. The script's
`repeat_refuel` retains the extra digit and the strict-height guard.

This pays for any requested finite repeat count. It constructs a new
ancestor; it does not prove that an arbitrary fixed supplied integer has
unlimited fuel, and does not produce a descending obligation merely by
raising a. A supplied root certificate is still the only home premise.

Connection contract: source = a supplied hub and a finite affine target;
target = a guarded inverse path and its constructed ancestor; map = Cayley
walk, source-dependent ternary lift, and marked-prefix selection;
preserved = the entire modular affine action and actual common future;
forgotten by the group alone = legality, ordered carry, magnitude, exact
integer source, and a root premise. The restoration sidecar is precisely
the word, exponent k, ternary guard and strict-height certificate.

The lifting theorem pays this information explicitly. It does not prove
that a supplied unresolved integer equals any constructed ancestor, that
the ancestor is smaller, or that an unproved hub reaches1. Universal
coverage of supplied sources therefore remains open.
