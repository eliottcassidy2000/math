## 1. Half-supported prefix clique

Suppose n>=2r and r is a power of two. Let S support a directed r-cycle, let T-S be strong, and let a vertex v of T-S be beaten by t vertices of S. Starting a Hamilton word W of T-S at v is possible: cut a Hamilton cycle immediately before v (the singleton case is immediate). Each of the t corresponding cuts of the S-cycle, followed by W, is a Hamilton word.

Any two such words differ by a nonidentity power u of the r-cycle supported in their first r positions. Its order is even. They cannot be automorphic, since Aut(T) has odd order: an involution would reverse the edge joining a transposed pair. If both are blue, multiplying their positional reversals yields u(rho u^{-1}rho), where rho reverses one word. The supports of u and rho u^{-1}rho are disjoint since n>=2r, so this product has the same even order as u, impossible for an automorphism. Nor can they be converse/reversal mates: that would make u rho an anti-automorphism (up to replacing u by its inverse), whose square u(rho u rho) is an even-order automorphism by the same disjoint-support argument.

Thus these t distinct path orbits contain at most one blue orbit; their black orbits are pairwise not mates. For self-converse T, reversal/converse is an involution on path orbits with blue fixed points. Consequently Delta>=2(t-1). An entry configuration, where v beats t vertices of S, has the same consequence: apply any ambient anti-automorphism to the complete configuration, reversing the cycle direction. Strongness of the complement and support sizes are preserved.

## 2. Rotation shores and strong complements

Label a marked Hamilton cycle 0->1->...->n-1->0. Its automorphic rotations are generated by R^q. The q shores A_i={i+qj:0<=j<m} are cyclically permuted internally by R^q. Each induced shore is vertex-transitive, hence regular of odd order m, hence strong for m>=3. For consecutive shores the marked-cycle arcs give a perfect matching A_i->A_{i+1}, indices modulo q; the wrap matching may shift the j coordinate and is still a perfect matching.

If q>=2 and S is a proper subset of A_0, then T-S is strong. Indeed A_1,...,A_{q-1} are intact strong shores, linked forward by the perfect matchings. Every remaining vertex x of A_0 has an incoming matching arc from A_{q-1} and an outgoing matching arc to A_1. One such x closes the chain, making the entire complement strong. For q=2 this says that the sole intact shore is strong and every remaining A_0 vertex has arcs both into and out of it. This proof is about literal matchings, not an assumed automorphism-invariant score partition.

## 3. Odd-shore balance obstruction: q>=2 implies m<=7

Assume q>=2 and m>=9. By strong pancyclicity choose a directed eight-cycle S inside A_0. It is proper since m is odd. Its complement is strong by section2, and n=qm>=18 ensures the half-support condition.

Choose any other shore B. Rotation transitivity shows that every vertex of A_0 beats the same number k of vertices of B. Thus the total number of arcs S->B is 8k. If every vertex of B were beaten by exactly four vertices of S, this total would instead be 4m. Equality would imply 2k=m, impossible because m is odd. Therefore some vertex of B is beaten by at least five vertices of S or beats at least five vertices of S. Section1, including its entry dual, forces Delta>=8.

Consequently every strong self-converse Delta<=6 tournament with q>=2 has m<=7. This is precisely where merely using an arbitrary external vertex would fail: four exits are not enough. The additional coordinate is the exact odd-shore total 8k, not an averaged heuristic.

## 4. The single-shore case: q=1 implies n<=47

A regular tournament of order n has vertex connectivity at least ceil(n/3). To see this directly, suppose deletion of s vertices leaves a nonstrong tournament. Split its surviving SCC order into nonempty X->Y, of sizes x,y. Comparing the average full outdegree on X with regular degree (n-1)/2 gives y<=s. Comparing the average full outdegree on Y gives x<=s. Hence n=x+y+s<=3s.

If q=1, T is regular and n is odd. If n>=49, deleting any16 vertices leaves a strong nonempty tournament, since ceil(n/3)>=17. Choose a directed16-cycle S by strong pancyclicity. Any remaining vertex has at least8 incident arcs toward S in one direction. Section1, with entry dual if needed and n>=32, forces Delta>=14. This contradicts Delta<=6. Hence n<=47.

## 6. General quadratic self-converse defect bound

Let d>=0 be an integer, and T strong self-converse with Delta<=d. Put r_s equal to the least power of two at least4 and strictly greater than d. Put r_1 equal to the least power of two strictly greater than d+2. Then

    n <= max((d+2)(r_s-1), 3r_1-1).

For q>=2, if odd m>=r_s+1, use an r_s-cycle inside a shore. Exact cross-edge counting against another odd shore gives at least r_s/2+1 exits or entries, hence Delta>=r_s>d. Thus m<=r_s-1. For q=1, n>=3r_1+1 gives connectivity>r_1, a removable r_1-cycle, and at least r_1/2 exits or entries. Thus Delta>=r_1-2>d. Since n is odd, n<=3r_1-1. Combining q<=d+2 proves the bound. This is O(d^2), improving the inherited cubic bound for this self-converse scope. It is not a claimed general non-self-converse improvement.

## 8. Score-run obstruction and the sharpened degree47 bound

Suppose T is strong and self-converse with Delta<=6, q>=5 and m>=5. Every full-score class must contain at least five rotation shores.

First consider a score class consisting of just one shore A, of full score s. Choose a directed four-cycle in A. The odd-shore count against any other shore supplies at least three exits or entries. The proper-shore deletion lemma supplies a strong complement, and n>=25 ensures the half-support condition. Thus the prefix lemma gives at least four black orbits: at least two chosen prefix orbits and their mates. Every chosen prefix has four consecutive vertices of score s, whereas cycle cuts have isolated occurrences of score s. Their mates have a four-vertex run of score n-1-s; self-converseness ensures this complementary score also comprises exactly one shore. Thus these four black orbits are disjoint from all marked-cycle cut orbits.

Now suppose a full-score class consists of c shores with 2<=c<=4. Choose a shore A in this class, a directed four-cycle S inside A, and ANOTHER shore B of the SAME full score s. Odd-shore counting against B supplies a terminal v in B giving at least three exits or entries. In the exit case every constructed prefix word starts with four vertices of S FOLLOWED BY v: it has a run of at least FIVE vertices of score s. A cycle cut cannot have five consecutive vertices of this score, since the full cyclic shore word has only c<=4 shores of that score, and q>=5. The mates similarly have a run of five vertices of score n-1-s; that complementary class has the same shore multiplicity c, so cannot occur in a cut word either. In the entry case work in the opposite tournament, which has the same path counts and whose score classes are complementary; the identical five-vertex run argument applies.

In either case the cut family already supplies at least q-gcd(2,q) black orbits, even if no cut is blue (then all q are black). Adding the four disjoint black orbits gives

    Delta >= q-gcd(2,q)+4 >=8,

a contradiction. This proves that every shore-score multiplicity is at least5.

Consequently, for the remaining q=5,6,7,8 and m=5 or7:
- q=5 or7 implies T is regular, since two score classes would require at least10 shores.
- q=6 or8 is impossible: at even n there is no self-complementary degree, so self-converseness requires at least two complementary degree classes, again at least10 shores.

The regular connectivity and sixteen-cycle argument in section4 applies to EVERY regular tournament, not just the q=1 subcase. It excludes regular centers of order at least49. Hence q=7,m=7 is also impossible. Combining the cases:
- q=1 gives n<=47;
- 2<=q<=4 gives n<=28;
- q=5 gives n<=35;
- q=6 gives m<=3 and n<=18;
- q=7 gives m<=5 and n<=35;
- q=8 gives m<=3 and n<=24.

Therefore **deg R6<=47**, superseding the intermediate56 bound within this packet. The safe inherited order18 recurrence onset is consequently n>=60, since the numerator degree is at most47+12=59. This is still an analytic necessary reduction, not a full center classification or an earliest recurrence-onset claim.

The mechanism extends the incoming intrinsic score-word distinction: the decisive extra coordinate is the terminal's score, which upgrades the prefix run from four to five. It uses no fresh all-class census. Earlier versions of section8 only excluded singleton classes; this strengthening is a new proof refinement, not a correction of those weaker necessary conditions.
