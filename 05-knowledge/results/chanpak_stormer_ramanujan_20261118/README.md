# Størmer's signatures, the four tournaments, and Ramanujan twins

This continues the [Machin and minus packet](../chanpak_repeatable_minus_machin_20261118/README.md).
New exact pieces are a shared two-arc construction for odd centers 9 and
15, literal tournament readings for every argument in Størmer's four-term
formula, an exact Gaussian explanation of the coefficients, and a
classification of the order-four skew-Hadamard tournament signatures.

## 1. The four formulas and the four classes

Write [x]=arctan(1/x). For two distinct integral denominators x>1 and
nonzero integral coefficients, the classification of π/4 as a two-term
combination has precisely the following formulas (up to term order).
See [Størmer's original classification](https://books.google.com/books/about/Solution_compl%C3%A8te_en_nombres_entiers_m.html?id=hv01LYVBC9kC).

    π/4 = [2]+[3]
        = 2[2]−[7]
        = 2[3]+[7]
        = 4[5]−[239].

The two signs at 7 are important. The Gaussian vectors (2+i)^2=3+4i
and (3+i)^2/2=4+3i are reflected across the diagonal. Their angles
are π/4 ± [7], respectively. Their common correction is exactly a
unit in the diagonal coordinates. This makes the proposed analogy
with the converse pair scientifically useful: it respects an
explicit involution, not just a coincident cardinality.

On the tournament side the score multisets are 0123 (transitive,
H=1), 1113 and 0222 (source/sink plus triangle, H=3 each), and
1122 (the unique strong class, H=5). The last is self-converse,
but not circulant: a vertex-transitive tournament has odd order.
There is a further exact test. With A the skew sign dominance
matrix, I+A is Hadamard exactly when |pf(A)|=3. Indeed
det(A)=pf(A)^2 and its four rows have norm √3, so equality in
Hadamard's determinant bound is exactly orthogonality. In the
four score classes |pf| is respectively 1,3,3,1 (all 64 labeled
orientations are checked). Thus only the two converse classes
supply the order-four skew-Hadamard matrices. The analogy of the
remaining strong class with Machin can use the shared 5, its path
count and Machin's first denominator, but not circulancy or a
proved natural bijection.

The three-term expression is valid and is outside the
two-term restriction, not a counterexample to Størmer:

    (4+i)^3(20+i)=993+992i;
    (993+992i)(1985+i) lies on the positive diagonal.
    π/4=3[4]+[20]+[1985].

Neither 1/4 nor 1/20 is a ratio of two Hamilton tournament counts:
both counts must be odd. The argument denominator 1985=5·397
is a tournament count itself. Conversely 7 is a missing count
but 1/7 **is** a tournament ratio, e.g. 17/119 below.
Allowing a ratio is genuinely different from asking for a count.

## 2. Exact coefficient selection in Størmer's longer identity

The four-term identity is

    π/4 = 12[49]+32[57]−5[239]+12[110443].

In the factorization of x+i over Gaussian integers, use signed
valuations (prime's multiplicity minus its conjugate's). Take
prime representatives 1+2i, 2+3i, 24+25i, of norms 5,13,1201.
The exact signed columns are:

| x | norm 5 | norm 13 | norm 1201 |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 49 | 0 | 0 | 1 |
| 57 | −3 | 1 | 0 |
| 239 | 0 | 4 | 0 |
| 110443 | 8 | −1 | −1 |

Only the ramified factor 1+i is left out. The three vanishing
conditions on a diagonal product have one-dimensional integer
kernel, generated by (12,32,−5,12): the norm-1201 column ties the
first and last coefficients, the norm-5 column gives the factor
3 versus 8, then norm-13 forces the extra factor of 4. The Gaussian
product after division has equal positive coordinates, and
alternating rational arctan bounds put the angle sum between
1/2 and 1 radians, ruling out extra turns. This is a linear
integer constraint on a fixed Gaussian support, not a classification
of arbitrary four-term formulas.

The 49 entry makes the 24/25 connection exact:
49+i=(1+i)(25−24i), because 25−24=1 and 25+24=49.
The odd prime 1201=24²+25² canceled by 110443 is the norm
of those adjacent coordinates. This is a different role for
25 from 50=2·25=(7²+1), the second negative-Pell center.
At that center, both 49 and 51 have order-seven witnesses
differing by one arc; the replay gives the two masks. None of
these identities alone makes every halfway number a repair center.

Each of 49,57,239 is a literal Hamiltonian count, and so is
110443=179·617, via ordinal gluing of verified smaller witnesses.
Thus all four arguments of Størmer's long identity can be read
as arctan(1/H), unlike the three-term identity above.
The coefficients themselves need not be tournament counts.

## 3. The two arcs, and why 119 and 239 sit on one rung sequence

Use the earlier [marked-apex scalar-read formula](../chanpak_power_three_return_gates_20261118/README.md)
with left prefix address a and t≥1 appended zero tags. On the core
side use either of these three-vertex models:

| sign at a−F(a)=2 | outgoing rows | apex outmask | (h,W,κ) |
|---|---|---:|---|
| plus | (6,4,0) | 2 | (1,6,3) |
| minus | (2,4,1) | 6 | (3,6,5) |

The exact counts are 6a·2^t+κ−h(a−F(a)). The two models
differ in exactly two arcs, core 0–2 and apex–2, and the entire
spine is shared. In this fixed pair, the condition for a central
unit bracket of this form is a−F(a)=2. Its only positive odd
solutions are 3 and 5. Indeed F(n)≤n+1 by induction, with
F(2b+1)=F(b)≤b+1 (and ≤2b+1); applying this to odd a≥7
gives a−F(a)≥3. The small cases are explicit.

Let m=t+1. This proves two *physical* families with the same
core switch: 9·2^m±1 at order m+5 and 15·2^m±1 at order m+6,
for every m≥2. Both are strong, as the apex reaches the core
via the first spine vertex, and every core vertex reaches an
in-contact. Insert a final losing spine tag for the next induced
extension within either sign. The second family gives 119/121
and then 239/241 at consecutive levels. Here 119=7·17 is
strongly realized despite the missing factor 7, and this proves
the physical ratio 17/119 used in Section 1. The switches are
ordinary arcs, not asserted to be native metagraph edges.

## 4. Ramanujan thresholds and the Hadamard interpretation of the square

The requested list is the initial [Ramanujan primes](https://arxiv.org/abs/0907.5232),
defined by the persistent lower bound on π(x)−π(x/2), where π(x)
in this paragraph is the prime-counting function. The exact
thresholds R3=17, R9=71, R17=179, R22=239, R23=241 follow from
the replay's sieve, tail-bounded by Sondow's explicit
R_n<4n log(4n). In particular 239 and 241 are consecutive
Ramanujan primes and literal twin-prime counts in one two-arc
family. The other factor 617 of 110443 is prime but not
Ramanujan: R49=607 and R50=641; the interval count at 617 is 50,
but it drops to 49 at 626. This distinction is the genuinely
useful analogy to target windows: a threshold that persists
is different from a single good numerical landing.

Finally 57122=239²+1 is 2 mod 4, hence cannot be a real Hadamard
order (normalizing and comparing three rows forces 4 to divide
every order greater than two). Its double 114244 does have an
ordinary Hadamard matrix by [Paley type II](https://doi.org/10.1002/sapm1933121311)
over F_(239²). To see the distinction directly, the quadratic
character matrix with symmetric all-ones border and zero
diagonal is a symmetric conference matrix C of order q+1,
C²=qI for q=239². For distinct field entries, the character
correlation is −1: substitute the ratio of their translated
coordinates and sum over the field excluding one value. The
border cancels that −1. With A=C+I and B=C−I, the block matrix
[[A,B],[B,−A]] has ±1 entries and square 2(q+1)I. Over F_239
instead, the character is skew and the Paley triangle-orientation
rule generalizes to the Paley tournament of order 239, giving
a skew Hadamard border of order 240. None of these matrices of
order >4 can be circulant by the [formal classification](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/main/lean/docs/179.md).
The field-square and doubling split is an exact realization
distinction, not a prediction about Chan–Pak.
