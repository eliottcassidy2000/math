# A literal covering wheel on marked lengths, with an exact modular obstruction

Use the exact language in [the 24:13 classification](ray/RAY.md). The
letter at each spine position still records the two apex wins.
Let d=(H(A→B)−H(B→A))/2 be signed half-gap. Write the primitive
words as short P,Q (length 12, half-gaps ±1) and long U{V,W}*Z
(length 38+12j, half-gap #W−#V). All are on the same marked line
and concatenate with exact addition of their gaps. Every tape so
obtained is strong in both orientations: in each primitive word
B beats the first site, the last beats B, and A has both arc
directions against the spine; composition shares both apices.

## Exact lengths, not just residues

If a tape has b long primitive returns and a short units (one for
every P,Q, or V/W loop inside a long return), its length is
12a+38b and d is a sum of a signs. Conversely all a,b with
|d|≤a and a≡d mod 2 are realized by a short atoms with the right
signs and b copies of UZ. Thus the exact set of lengths is

    n = 12|d| + 24k + 38b,   k,b ≥ 0.

The empty tape at d=n=0 may be discarded if strongness is required.
In particular +2 cannot occur at spine length 24 or 120 **on this
fixed marked ratio**. This is not an obstruction at those lengths
for arbitrary two-apex tapes. At fixed d all sufficiently large even
lengths occur: dividing the translated length by two, the semigroup
is generated by 12 and 19. Choosing the number b of 19s modulo 12
shows the largest hole is 12·19−12−19=197 (b=11), so every even
n≥12|d|+396 occurs and n=12|d|+394 does not. For +2 the exact
all-even cutoff in this ray is therefore 408, and 406 is excluded.

There is a literal finite covering wheel mod 120 for these lengths.
For each even residue of n−12|d|, choose 0≤b<12 with
38b≡n−12|d| mod24, then choose 0≤k_0<5 with
24k_0+38b≡n−12|d| mod120. Five neutral PQ pairs add exactly
120 to the spine and leave d unchanged. In sufficiently large
lengths each residue therefore has an explicit producing word, not
just a modular prediction. For a fixed d and large even n, choose
the bounded b first; choose the signs of all a short atoms with
their net displacement d. Unique primitive factorization supplies
binom(a,(a+d)/2) distinct tapes at the same length and gap, which
is Ω_d(2^(n/12)/sqrt(n)). These are literal words, not a claim
about distinct absolute Hamiltonian counts.

## The same wheel cannot prescribe the marked amplitudes

Let L_a=13ℓ and R_a=13r be the two marked addresses divided by 13.
For the short atoms P,Q their respective (ℓ,r) residues mod120
are (30,45),(45,30). For **every** long primitive atom U{V,W}*Z
the pair is (69,69) modulo120. This assertion is independent of
its loop word: at the raw binary address use modulus 1560=13·120.
The trailing block has 19 digits, so only its prefix mod195
matters; V,W and their reverse-complements have first-row raw
address zero mod195, and 2^12≡1 mod195. Thus the terminal
residues equal those of UZ and rc(UZ), both 897 mod1560.

For forward amplitude ℓ a new atom shifts the earlier amplitude
by its binary length and adds its own. For r the same formula
applies in reverse order. Modulo15 every short atom is the
identity and each long atom acts as t↦4t+9, interchanging 0 and
9. The width modulo four determines the parity of the long count.
Modulo8 the last forward/first reverse atom alone decides the
amplitude: each is 5 or 6. Therefore each of (ℓ,r) independently
must belong to the following two-element set:

    n ≡ 0 mod 4: {30,45} modulo 120
    n ≡ 2 mod 4: {54,69} modulo 120.

Conversely, at fixed d, every pair in the corresponding Cartesian
square is realized for all even n≥12|d|+444: choose P or Q freely
at the first and last position to set both mod-8 phases, then
fill the middle at the required half-gap. Its length n−24 clears
the exact conductor even after shifting the half-gap by at most
two. We can program **all** even length classes mod120 while at
the same time only four joint amplitude phases are possible at
each width. This separates three distinct problems: width,
interface and absolute H. The width wheel is constructive; it
has no hidden implication of prescribed count coverage.

## The exact entropy scale is much smaller than the count scale

In each primitive word the first two letters are 13 or 31 and the
last two are 20 or 02. Thus in every nonempty ray word we can use
the first letter 3 and last letter 0 at those ends for a cycle
through A, and the distinct letter 1 and letter 2 for a cycle
through B. For every assignment of the n−4 remaining interior
spine sites to the A cycle, the B cycle, or neither, retain the
assignment when the two chosen subsets are both even. The spine
is transitive, so these are literal disjoint directed odd cycles.
There are (3^(n−4)+3)/4 such assignments, by the two parity
filters (all these n are even). Each pack contributes 4 to the
Hamilton count, while the three-run count gives a universal
upper bound 2·3^n. Consequently H=Θ(3^n) on the entire nonempty
24:13 ray, regardless of signed gap and mutual arc.

At y=1, the grammar series is the even part of
1/(1−2x^12−x^19). Thus its even coefficients equal those of the
recurrence f_n=2f_(n−12)+f_(n−19), with f_0=1 and f_n=0 for n<0.
Let λ>1 satisfy 2λ^(−12)+λ^(−19)=1. Induction gives f_n≤λ^n.
Since gcd(12,19)=1, past its coin threshold f_n is positive;
inducting from any 19 consecutive positive coefficients gives
f_n≥cλ^n thereafter. Taking both orientations, the number of
labelled programs with both counts ≤X is Θ(X^γ), where

    γ = log_3 λ ≈ 0.0717704.

Distinct absolute counts are at most this numerous. This tightens
the [coarser sparsity bound](ray/RAY.md) without assuming counts
are injective. Even a perfect modular count wheel within this
ray has density zero at absolute scale.
