## M1 (factorial moments = overlap volumes) — PROVED

> **Theorem M1.** For every `0 ≤ j ≤ n`,
> ```
>   E_μ[ C(N, j) ] = S_j           (binomial-moment form)
>   E_μ[ N(N−1)⋯(N−j+1) ] = j! · S_j   (factorial-moment form).
> ```

**Proof.** Pointwise, `C(N(t),j)` counts the `j`-subsets `I⊆[n]` with `t∈⋂_{i∈I}D_i`:
```
  C(N(t),j) = Σ_{|I|=j} ∏_{i∈I} 1_{D_i}(t) = Σ_{|I|=j} 1_{⋂_{i∈I} D_i}(t).
```
Integrate against `μ` and use linearity: `E[C(N,j)] = Σ_{|I|=j} μ(⋂_{i∈I}D_i) = S_j`. The
factorial form is `j!·C(N,j)`. ∎

**Corollaries.**
- **(a) The first moment is config-free.** `E[N] = S_1 = Σ_i μ(D_i) = 2nδ`. *Every* speed set
  has the same mean depth `2nδ → 2`; the first moment carries no information (it is the S550
  measure bound).
- **(b) Loneliness is an alternating sum of overlap volumes (inclusion–exclusion).**
  Since `Σ_{j≥0}(−1)^j C(k,j) = (1−1)^k = 𝟙[k=0]`,
  ```
        p_0 = μ(N=0) = Σ_{j=0}^{n} (−1)^j S_j .
  ```
  The lonely measure is *exactly* the inclusion–exclusion alternating sum of the multi-arc
  overlap volumes.
- **(c) Covariance / Poisson reference.** A `Poisson(λ)` law has `E[C(N,j)] = λ^j/j!`, i.e.
  reference overlaps `S_j^{Pois} = (2nδ)^j / j!`. The config's departure from Poisson is the
  excess sequence `S_j − (2nδ)^j/j!`. At `j=2`: `Σ_{i<j} Cov(1_{D_i},1_{D_j}) = S_2 −
  \binom{n}{2}(2δ)^2`.

**Verified exactly** (`lrc_depth_rigor_moments_s599c.py`, n=4,5): `S_j` from `{p_k}` equals
`S_j` from direct arc-intersection for `j=1,2,3`; `Σ(−1)^j S_j = p_0` exactly (e.g. AP n=4:

## M2. Full finite moments recover the atom; proper truncations can lose it — PROVED

For the fixed finite number n of speeds, M1 already gives

\[
             p_0=\sum_{j=0}^{n}(-1)^j S_j.
\]

Thus a **finite** moment list determines p_0 exactly. The stronger general
inversion is proved in [THM-561, miss-zeta-factorial-atom-inversion](THM-561-miss-zeta-factorial-atom-inversion.md):

\[
 p_k=\sum_{j=k}^{n}(-1)^{j-k}\binom jk S_j.
\]

For a proper truncation, Bonferroni gives upper and lower bounds. A
strictly positive odd partial sum **does** certify p_0>0. For example,
S_1<1 gives p_0>=1-S_1>0. This condition can occur within the declared
arc model by taking delta<1/(2n), even though it does not settle the
critical LRC parameters.

There is a valid weaker obstruction on the universe of arbitrary bounded
integer-valued probability laws. For every k>=1 define two laws on
{0,...,k+1} by

\[
 p_j^{\rm even}=2^{-k}\binom{k+1}{j}\mathbf1_{j\ {
m even}},\qquad
 p_j^{\rm odd}=2^{-k}\binom{k+1}{j}\mathbf1_{j\ {
m odd}}.
\]

Both sum to1. Their binomial moments agree through order k, since the
(k+1)st alternating finite difference kills every polynomial of degree
at most k. Yet their zero atoms are2^{-k} and0. Therefore a fixed
truncation need not determine the zero atom when the support bound is
larger than the truncation. These laws are **not claimed to be realizable
covering-depth laws of integer speed sets**. Generated-word compatibility
is an additional constraint, as the later LRC work emphasizes.

**Correction lineage.** The old M2 said that no finite moment functional
can certify the sign of p_0, and inferred indistinguishability from three
examples with *different* S_2 values. Neither implication follows. The
finite inclusion-exclusion identity itself refutes the first assertion
at fixed n. The old table's sub-Poisson second moments also do not imply
p_0<exp(-2n delta); a second moment alone does not order the zero atom.
The numerical examples remain examples, not a universal no-go theorem.

## M3. The cyclic spectral representation — PROVED with its space retained

Let H=L²(X,mu) and M_N f=Nf. Then for Omega=1,

\[
 \langle\Omega,\mathbf1_B(M_N)\Omega\rangle
       =\mu(N\in B)=\sum_{k\in B}p_k.
\]

The cyclic subspace generated by Omega is

\[
 H_N=\overline{\{g(N):g\text{ a bounded Borel function}\}}
        =L²(\sigma(N),\mu),
\]

viewed as a subspace of H. It need not equal H. For a finite-valued N on
the circle, functions may vary inside a positive-measure level set while
g(N) is constant there. Thus Omega is **not in general cyclic on the
ambient L²(X,mu)**.

The map g -> g(N) is an isometry from L²({p_k}) onto H_N, because
its squared norm is sum_k p_k |g(k)|². It sends the constant1 to Omega
and conjugates multiplication by k to M_N restricted to H_N. Consequently:

- the masses p_k determine the **marked cyclic representation**
  (H_N,M_N|H_N,Omega), including the weights of its distinguished vector;
- they do not retain the multiplicities or internal geometry of the
  fibres in the full ambient representation;
- sigma(N) is the smallest sigma-algebra making N measurable;
- the extreme Rayleigh quotients on H_N are the minimum/maximum k with
  p_k>0, and Borel functional calculus acts by g(N).

This is the precise spectral statement. A measure class alone does not
specify a distinguished vector's weights. The earlier identification of
all five informal principles with a complete invariant of the ambient
space discarded these qualifications.

## Consequences and boundary scope

For the declared **open** danger arcs, their complement is the closed
lonely set {t: all ||v_i t||>=delta}. Its measure is p_0. Positive p_0
implies nonemptiness, but p_0=0 does not imply emptiness: boundary witnesses
may be finite. For speeds1,...,n and delta=1/(n+1), the surviving points
are a/(n+1) with gcd(a,n+1)=1, so this is a concrete nonempty zero-measure
case. The continuous function min_i ||v_i t|| retains those witnesses,
whereas the depth distribution alone can forget them.

At fixed n the full finite overlap list reconstructs p_0. For truncated
lists, exploit the generated overlap constraints before declaring an
abstract moment obstruction realizable. A Poisson reference is a useful
comparison law, not a theorem that the actual zero atom lies on a
specified side of it.
