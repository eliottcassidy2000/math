# What the session changed

## The successful change of viewpoint

The earlier repair fibers held a core in one position. Repeating nontrivial
cores multiplied their Hamilton counts and scaled a unit repair. This session
instead moved a single physical core through singleton words while retaining
both start- and end-sensitive path-cover coordinates. Equal full interfaces
became a finite carry graph with an integer gain on each directed edge.

Reachability alone was insufficient. A graph-theoretic carry state need not
be physical, and a gcd of cycle gains need not be a realizable gain. The first
positive construction needed a physical core in the relevant strongly connected
carry component. Two genuinely forward closed walks with gains +34 and -90
then gave a unit gain by eight copies of the first and three of the second.
No edge was reversed and no negative repetition was used. The later twelve-bit
loop gave the same phenomenon directly and much more efficiently.

The arithmetic filter changed the search direction. Closed gains are divisible
by 2h/gcd(h,q), and by twice the invariant odd content. Thus an unsaturated
h>1 core cannot give a unit closed gain. A saturated core can. Saturation was
harmful for one repeated-product strategy, but is useful here because one fixed
core moves rather than being repeated. The successful h=45 carrier does not
make the final scalar counts share a growing divisor.

## What the independent lanes contributed

- The two-sided physical inverse supplies strict numerical descent and an
  exact graph-valued membership question. It prevents substituting a scalar
  representative with the wrong endpoint/response state. It still lacks a
  proof that a successful branch exists for every target.
- The source-cost lane turns an endpoint-swapped pair into an infinite
  near-dyadic twin family with optimal route entry. It shows why existence
  of a deficit and affordability at a given exponent are different claims.
- The sparse/ribbon lane separates all-order sparse-budget bounds from finite
  repeat-block spectral tests. Neither rules out the successful dense-core
  moving construction, and neither is an all-constructor impossibility result.
- The complete small-core census and its independent canonical audit locate
  the first short unit-loop mechanism. Their bounded-order maximum width does
  not contradict the larger deletion graphs produced by repeated movements.

## Why the interval result is not the completed proof

Removing the protective flanks lowers the witness order to 12k+9, but exposes
a real endpoint-state jump. Count-neutral deletion is not state-neutral.
The common bare-apex graft has a terminal anomaly; even that anomaly has an
unordered-set exception at k=6. Tracking the full state prevents a false
blanket obstruction or a false unit-preservation theorem.

More decisively, X^j and Y^(k-j) are themselves binary contexts. Any larger
bare interval placement can be absorbed into a k=0 prefix/suffix placement
around the same core, with exactly the same marked graph. The new intervals
organize that language; they do not enlarge its image. Independent complete
recognition through 10^8 finds only 907,984 occupied odd targets in this
language. These finite omissions are not counterexamples to Chan–Pak.

The surviving questions are concrete:

1. Can a varying physical core family provide target placement with controlled
   size and retained endpoint data?
2. Can a proper two-sided predecessor always be selected from the finite,
   graph-valued inverse for every admissible target?
3. Can changing a protected core port, or interleaving dilated physical fibers,
   escape the exterior-suffix obstruction? The named-port failures proved here
   concern the k=1 source pair, not every k or every sequence.

The fixed-carrier diameter bound only limits one-core movement with fixed h,q.
It does not prohibit a successful varying-core selector. Likewise the context
census is complete below its cap but makes no asymptotic density claim.
