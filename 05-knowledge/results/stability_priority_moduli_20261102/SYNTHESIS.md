## A precise obstruction to copying a small counterexample upward

**PROVED.** Append r ordinal fair pairs to any fixed weighted tournament A
of order n. Write A_r for the result. Block triangularity and ordered path
concatenation give

    H(A_r)=H(A),
    u(A_r)=(3/4)^r u(A),
    |v(A_r)|=(1/4)^r |v(A)|,
    V(A_r)=V(A)+r/4.

Consequently

    Delta(A_r)-kappa_(n+2r) V(A_r) -> H(A)-1.             (S1)

Indeed the two determinant terms vanish and
kappa_(n+2r)(V+r/4)->1. When H(A)>1 the padded defect is eventually
strictly positive, independently of its initial sign. The H=1 case is
already classified by the inherited scalar theorem. Thus this natural
H-preserving extension cannot reduce every remaining small-order stability
question to the new n>=276 theorem. This is an obstruction to this operation,
not an exhibited counterexample to stability.

The same caution applies to copying A by ordinal powers: H multiplies,
whereas V adds. Reproducing H is not reproducing the normalized gap.
No A000568 enumeration extension follows from these statements.

## Square-sum paths are guarded permutations, not newly born tournaments

Let G_n have vertices 1,...,n and edge xy exactly when x+y is a square.
The published-contributor record
[A090461](https://oeis.org/A090461) attributes the all-n>=25 path construction
to Gerbicz; [A071983](https://oeis.org/A071983) records the small counts up
to reversal. The linked forum proof was inaccessible (HTTP 403) in this
session, so its all-size result is **CITED**, not independently reproved.
Existing repository constructive square-sum packets are retained as their
own stronger executable sources, not replaced by that access failure.

The old **PROVED** source
`THM-4505-small-graphs-encoding-arithmetic-square-sum-zigzag-fences-collatz-alphabet.md`
explains the first n=15 path via its two forced leaves 8 and 9 and the
Pell-compatible square targets 9,16,25. The onsets 15,16,17 and isolated 23
are graph-order phenomena. Tournament exclusions 7,21 are *values of a path
count*. There is no type-preserving map between those two lists here.

There is, however, a precise universally valid encoding. For every ordering
pi of 1,...,n, form the transitive tournament oriented forward in that order,
and retain the original square-sum edge guard. Its unique Hamilton path is
legal exactly when every adjacent pair of pi lies in G_n. Thus the number
of accepted ordered tournaments is twice the undirected square-sum path
count for n>=2. The guard is essential: every transitive tournament has a
Hamilton path regardless of n, including n=2 where G_n has no edge.
For n>=2 the ordered square-sum count is even by reversal, while every
ordinary tournament's Hamilton-path count is odd. Hence a count-preserving
identification of these unweighted ordered path sets is impossible.

Equivalently, the subset automaton has states (S,last) and permits an
extension only to an unused square-sum neighbor. The source set and final
full-set condition are realization guards. Its accepting paths are not
Hamilton paths through all automaton states. FRACTRAN likewise requires
priority and divisibility guards; a rule may recur many times in one run.
The common operation is guarded word composition, not an automatic
tournament structure at order 25.

Incoming `../stability_priority_spectrum_20261102/FRACTRAN_CLOCKS.md`
adds a useful contrast: the terminating (1/2,2/3) program has exact trace
class count 1+h+floor((h+1)^2/4), whereas the reciprocal returning family
here has h+2. These are actual counts of distinguishable finite responses,
not counts of primes or graph vertices. Priority, a decreasing rank versus
a two-step return, and the retained clock explain the different growth.
