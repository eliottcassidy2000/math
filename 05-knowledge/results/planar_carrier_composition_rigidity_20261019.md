# Finite compositions of universal carrier flows have no nonidentity polynomial realization

2026-10-19. **PROVED + INDEPENDENTLY REVIEWED, using a cited classical theorem:** every finite composition of the universal carrier flows, with arbitrary different invariants and scalar times, has a polynomial realization on the original source plane if and only if that realization is the identity. Cancellation to the identity is permitted. **OPEN:** nonidentity rational realizations, repairs outside this carrier class, source-chart entry, and planar JC. This is not a proof of the Jacobian conjecture or a priority claim.

## 1. The exact extension of packet 105

The [single-carrier result](planar_carrier_factor_rigidity_20261019.md) excluded polynomial scalar times by retaining one invariant and proving finite order. Its different-invariant composition gap is now closed for **finite compositions of the same universal source-preserving carrier class**. No common invariant of the composition is assumed.

Let K have characteristic zero. Use the original source polynomial ring K[x,t], ordinary bracket {f,g}=f_xg_t−f_tg_x, and

    q=1+x²t, p=tq, y=xt²q, D=p³−y²=t³q².

For arbitrary R_i∈K[p,y], c_i,λ_i∈K, put

    S_i=c_i+p²D R_i(p,y),  δ_i={−,S_i},  E_i=exp(λ_iδ_i).

The zero carrier and zero time are allowed. The [completed Hamiltonian theorem](planar_jc_long_20260906_hamiltonian.md) constructs these operations on the unlocalized D-adic completion of B₂=K[x,x²t,p,y]. We also construct their compatible original-source t-adic operations explicitly below. A polynomial realization means polynomials X,T∈K[x,t] equal to the images of x,t in the source t-adic completion. It does not merely mean polynomial expressions in the cusp coordinates p,y.

**Theorem.** For every finite word C=E_r⋯E_1, if C(x),C(t) are source polynomials, then C(x)=x and C(t)=t. Equivalently the group generated by these completed flows intersects the polynomial endomorphisms of the original source plane only in the identity.

## 2. The classical input, and why it is legitimate here

Janusz Gwoździewicz, *Injectivity on one line*, [primary author text](https://arxiv.org/html/alg-geom/9305008), Theorem 1.1, proves that a polynomial plane map over an algebraically closed characteristic-zero field with nonzero constant Jacobian is an automorphism if its restriction to one affine line is injective. Its Lemma 2.3 states the particular case needed here: a Keller map satisfying F(x,0)=(x,0) is an automorphism.

We apply this established special-case theorem after base change to an algebraic closure. The argument below proves identity there, which descends to K. We do not assume the general Jacobian conjecture. The author text's theorem and fixed-line lemma, not any unproved generalization, are the external dependency.

## 3. Source convergence and finite composition

For each i, direct substitution gives

    S_i−c_i=t⁵q⁴ R_i(tq,xt²q).                         (1)

Thus δ_i x∈t⁴K[x,t], δ_i t∈t⁵K[x,t], and

    δ_i(t^n K[x][[t]])⊂t^(n+4)K[x][[t]]  (n≥0).

Each exponential is a well-defined continuous algebra automorphism of K[x][[t]], with inverse obtained by negating its scalar time. The usual exponential identities follow quotient by quotient, where each derivation is nilpotent. Every E_i fixes the line t=0 pointwise. Every finite word C has the same properties and preserves the source Poisson bracket, hence has formal Jacobian one.

If its images X,T are polynomials, injectivity of K[x,t]→K[x][[t]] makes det D(X,T)=1 and X(x,0)=x,T(x,0)=0 polynomial identities. Gwoździewicz therefore makes F=(X,T) an automorphism.

The source-series construction matches the inherited one on B₂: this ring is unlocalized and lies in K[x,t], so D^N B₂⊂t^(3N)K[x,t]. The inclusion extends continuously to its D-adic completion and identifies the defining partial sums. The induced image of t=D/p² agrees with its source exponential by multiplicativity; E_i(p)=t+O(t²) is nonzero. This statement is about this exact unlocalized ring, not an arbitrary completion with inverted coefficients. The common-ring comparison below also works term by term and remains valid for finite compositions.

## 4. The second boundary cannot be read directly from t-adic congruences

The polynomial q is a unit in K[x][[t]]. Therefore q-divisibility of every finite partial sum alone would say nothing about the limit's restriction to q=0. We instead retain an explicit common ring where both boundary evaluations are faithful enough for the task.

Put s=xt and τ=t. Then

    x=s/τ,  p=s²+τ=:h,  y=sh,  D=τh²,
    S_i−c_i=τh⁴R_i(h,sh),
    {f,g}=τ(f_sg_τ−f_τg_s).

Work in

    B=K[s][[τ]][τ^(−1)],

whose elements have polynomial s coefficients and a uniformly bounded negative τ exponent. Each δ_i has

    δ_i s=τ(S_i)_τ∈τh³K[s,τ],
    δ_i τ=−τ(S_i)_s∈τ²h³K[s,τ].                       (2)

It consequently raises τ-order at least one on B. Its exponential and every finite composition are defined as continuous automorphisms of B. In particular E_i(x) belongs to τ^(−1)K[s][[τ]], while E_i(t) belongs to K[s][[τ]].

There are two useful homomorphisms:

    σ:B→K[x]((t)),   s↦xt, τ↦t;
    ε:B→K((s)),     s↦s,  τ↦−s².                     (3)

The first is injective. A monomial s^aτ^b goes to x^a t^(a+b), and distinct pairs remain distinct. Each target t coefficient is a finite polynomial because b is bounded below and a≥0. It is continuous for the indicated valuations and intertwines the derivations on source polynomials. The same holds for their exponentials and finite compositions, by convergence of the defining series. Thus a hypothetical polynomial F representing C in the source completion represents the same C on x=s/τ,t=τ in B.

The second homomorphism is also well-defined and continuous: a term with τ exponent j has s-order at least 2j after evaluation, so every coefficient receives only finitely many contributions. Inverting τ gives only finitely many negative s powers. By (2),

    ε∘δ_i=0,
    ε∘E_i=ε,
    ε∘C=ε.                                           (4)

These identities hold first on Laurent polynomials by the chain rule and the factor h³, then on B by continuity. Consequently a polynomial realization satisfies

    X(−1/s,−s²)=−1/s,  T(−1/s,−s²)=−s².              (5)

The parametrization x=−1/s,t=−s² identifies the function field of q=0 with K(s); its coordinate ring is K[x,x^(−1)]. Its embedding into K((s)) is injective. Hence (5) is actual pointwise fixedness of the entire affine curve q=0, not just a statement on an unrelated completion.

## 5. Finishing without a common invariant

We already know that F is a polynomial automorphism and fixes t=0 pointwise. Therefore its pullback preserves the prime ideal (t) and T=at for a nonzero constant a. Since det DF=1,

    a X_x=1,  X=a^(−1)x+f(t).

The identity X(x,0)=x forces a=1 and f(0)=0. Thus F=(x+f(t),t). Equation (5) now gives f(−s²)=0. Injectivity of K[t]→K[s], t↦−s², forces f=0. This proves the theorem.

The argument does not require an invariant H common to the different flows and does not claim every finite word is nonidentity. For example E_S(λ)E_S(−λ)=id, and every scalar time of a constant Hamiltonian is the identity. The theorem decides the possible polynomial output, not a normal form or word problem for the completed group.

## 6. A separate algebraic explanation of the missing shear

There is an independent way to finish once Gwoździewicz gives a shear. Let A=K[p,y] and Â its unlocalized D-adic completion. The inherited carrier formulas give

    δ_i(A)⊂DA,  δ_i(D)∈D²A,  δ_i(x)∈A.

Thus E_i preserves Â and E_i(x)−x∈Â; by induction C(x)−x∈Â for every finite word, even with different invariants.

The normal form y²=p³−D identifies every element of Â uniquely as

    ∑_{j≥0} D^j (a_j(p)+y b_j(p)),   a_j,b_j∈K[p].

Under p=s²+τ,y=s(s²+τ), a nonzero element of D-order n has τ-order n and leading coefficient

    s^(4n)(a_n(s²)+s³b_n(s²)).                        (6)

The parenthesis is nonzero: its even and odd powers cannot cancel. This proves injectivity of Â→K[s][[τ]], and also

    image(Â)∩K[[τ]]=K.                               (7)

Indeed subtract a constant if necessary. A first nonzero τ coefficient at order n≥1 would be a nonzero scalar on the right, but (6) is divisible by s^(4n). At order zero the even/odd decomposition shows a constant can come only from a_0 constant and b_0=0, so the subtraction is legitimate.

The same injective σ identifies a source shear difference f(t) with f(τ) in this common ring. Since C(x)−x∈Â, (7) forces f constant, and line fixing forces zero. This argument independently explains why no pure source translation survives the complete carrier algebra. It is not necessary to assume that the finite word preserves any one S_i.
