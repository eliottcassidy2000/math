## 1. The register decomposition (PROVED by inspection, FINITE-EXACT for the products)

| fraction | flag move | data change |

| 55/1 | none -> 11 | +5 |

So the owner's blocks are: **B1** = the control toggle between the two idle states (no data change; flags 11, 13); **B2** = the 7-and-5 block with the start and exit instructions (data 5 and 7; flags 11, 13, 17); **B3** = the 3-to-7 block (3 consumed in states 11 and 17, 7 produced on the returns 29 -> 11 and 19 -> 11; flags 11, 17, 19, 29); **B4** = the 2-and-5 exchange (5 -> 2 + 3 in state 17, 2 consumed in state 19, 5 produced in state 23, and the state-free 2 -> 3 + 5; flags 13, 17, 19, 23). The three blocks of four pairwise share two flags ({11, 17}, {13, 17}, {17, 19}) and all three contain 17, the only flag with three outgoing instructions (78/85, 19/51, 1/17: "a 5 left?", "a 3 left?", "neither": the decision state of Conway's division loop).

**Bipartiteness.** The flag digraph without the two state-free loops (15/2, 1/7) is bipartite with parts {11, 17, 23} and {13, 19, 29, none}: every instruction moves between the parts (13 -> 17, 17 -> 13, 17 -> 19, 19 -> 23, 23 -> 19, 11 -> 29, 29 -> 11, 19 -> 11, 17 -> none, none -> 11, 13 -> 11, 11 -> 13). Hence every closed walk of flag moves has even length: PRIMEGAME's control alternates test states and act states, and there is no odd cycle, in particular no three-move cycle, in its flag machine.

**The reciprocal pair and the macro laws.** 11/13 and 13/11 are the only two fractions of the program whose product is 1 (the only reciprocal pair). Over all ordered products of 2, 3 and 4 fractions, the products equal to 1 or to a fraction of the program are exactly those obtained by inserting the pair 11/13 x 13/11 (78 of length three, 6 of length four, 2 of length two); no product of one fraction from each of B2, B3, B4 is a fraction or 1. So the program has no macro law beyond the control toggle, and in particular the three data blocks do not compose to a control move.

## 2. Triangular and Fibonacci numbers in the fractions (PROVED lemma; NUMEROLOGY for the matches)

The 19 distinct numerators and denominators are 1, 2, 7, 11, 13, 15, 17, 19, 23, 29, 33, 38, 51, 55, 77, 78, 85, 91, 95. The triangular ones are 1 = T_1, 15 = T_5, 55 = T_10, 78 = T_12, 91 = T_13; the Fibonacci ones are 1, 2, 13, 55.

**Lemma (semiprimes).** For primes p < q, pq is triangular iff q = 2p + 1 or q = 2p - 1. Proof: 2pq = k(k + 1) with k, k + 1 coprime forces {k, k + 1} = {p, 2q} or {2p, q}; the first gives 2q = p + 1 (impossible for p < q) or p = 2q + 1 (impossible), the second gives q = 2p + 1 or q = 2p - 1. QED (checked for every semiprime below 10^5, no violation). So 15 = 3 x 5, 55 = 5 x 11 and 91 = 7 x 13 are triangular because the program pairs a prime with 2p - 1 or 2p + 1 (5 = 2 x 3 - 1, 11 = 2 x 5 + 1, 13 = 2 x 7 - 1), and 78 = 6 x 13 with 13 = 2 x 6 + 1 is the same pattern on the composite 6; the non-triangular composites 33, 38, 51, 77, 85, 95 pair primes that are not doubling partners. The Sophie Germain pair (5, 11) is the start instruction 55/1.

**Ming's theorem (CITED: L. Ming, Fibonacci Quart. 27 (1989) 98-108).** The only triangular Fibonacci numbers are 1, 3, 21, 55. ## 3. The merged metagraph is the quotient of the complement-move graph (PROVED identification)

A tiling of order n (THM-4729) is a labelled tournament on {0, ..., n-1} containing the base path 0 -> 1 -> ... -> n-1, i.e. a point of the marked carrier X_n of THM-4809 / THM-4810. The line partner kappa(t) reverses every free pair: kappa = tau_P with P the base path, the native complement move of THM-4810 along the marked path. The metagraph's nodes are the isomorphism classes of tilings with converse classes merged, and its lines are the kappa-pairs {t, kappa t}; a line is blue when sigma (converse followed by i -> n-1-i, which keeps the base path) fixes it. Hence the merged tiling line metagraph at order n is the quotient of THM-4810's labelled complement-move graph restricted to the base-path moves, and re-marking along another Hamiltonian path is exactly a black K move of THM-4732. Two consequences were in canon before THM-4810 and should have been cited there: THM-4732 Lemma 2 ("an even number L of black moves needs n <= 2L + 2 and C(n-1, 2) even; every black distance is odd for n = 0, 3 mod 4") is the detour form of THM-4810's parity invariant (one move toggles C(n-1, 2) pairs), and THM-4810's two-move criterion is its L = 2 case (two moves toggle at most 2(n-1) pairs, kappa toggles C(n-1, 2), so distance two is impossible for n >= 7). The three-move law of HYP-9347 (1) reads, in THM-4810's Lemma 1: for every closed blue line (t, kappa t) between non-pure-blue classes there are legal Hamiltonian paths Q_1 of t, Q_2 of tau_(Q_1) t, Q_3 of tau_(Q_2) tau_(Q_1) t with E(Q_1) xor E(Q_2) xor E(Q_3) = E(P), the base path as the symmetric difference of three successive legal paths (exhaustive at n = 6, 8, 9, 10 in THM-4732; the (n-4, 3) layer proved for n >= 11 in the endpoint-three packets). Score monotonicity (THM-4810, Theorem L) adds only that L is constant along a blue line and changes by at most one per black move, which does not constrain a three-move detour.

## 4. The owner's mappings, tested exactly

**B2, B3, B4 against the three nodes at n = 4.** The three blocks of four pairwise share two flags, so their shared-flag graph is a triangle; the order-4 merged metagraph is a star at the strong node with one loop (T_4 - S_4, pair - S_4 twice, S_4 - S_4). No labelling of the blocks by the nodes carries the one onto the other (REFUTED as a graph correspondence). What does transfer is a hub: every block of four passes through the decision flag 17, as every line of order 4 passes through the strong node S_4 (DICTIONARY). The natural labelling by invariants is B2 -> T_4 (the block holding the three pure fractions, against the one pure-blue node), B4 -> the converse pair (the block whose moves 19 -> 23 -> 19 reverse each other), B3 -> S_4 (the block producing the 7-tokens, against the strong hub); it is a reading, not a theorem.

**The prime factorizations against the ten nodes at n = 5.** The fractions contain exactly ten distinct composite numbers, 15, 33, 38, 51, 55, 77, 78, 85, 91, 95 (nine semiprimes and 78 = 2 x 3 x 13). Joining two composites when they share a prime factor gives a graph with 20 edges on these ten vertices; the order-5 merged metagraph has 18 distinct non-loop adjacencies (plus loops at the two nodes with H = 11 and 13). Over all 10! bijections at most 15 of the 20 edges land on adjacencies (40 bijections attain it); for 20 random graphs with 20 edges the same maximum is 14-17 with mean 15.25. So the composite graph does not fit the metagraph better than a random graph of its size: NUMEROLOGY, in agreement with the earlier tests of the ten primes (S70, mac-mini). The ten merged nodes' tiling counts 1, 1, 2, 3, 5, 9, 9, 10, 11, 13 contain the Fibonacci numbers 1, 1, 2, 3, 5 on the five nodes with a nontrivial automorphism or a merged pair, and 13 on the node with H = 13; the two nodes with H = 11 and 13 are joined by a doubled black line, as 11 and 13 are joined by the reciprocal pair. These are count matches (NUMEROLOGY).

**The n = 11 three-move law.** The law (HYP-9347 (1), THM-4732 (3), the (n-4, 3) layer at n >= 11) is a statement about odd black distances in the metagraph, where C(n-1, 2) is odd for n = 11 and distance two is impossible for n >= 7; PRIMEGAME's flag machine is bipartite and has no odd closed walk at all. The two objects are opposite on exactly this point: the complement grammar's shortest detours are odd (three), the prime sieve's control cycles are even (two and four). No PRIMEGAME counterpart of the three-move law exists (section 1), and nothing here bears on the open structural proof for n >= 8.
