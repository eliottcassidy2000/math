## 2. Triangular and Fibonacci numbers in the fractions (the identities CITED from session129; the lemma is OEIS A068443's comment, proved here and checked; NUMEROLOGY for the matches)

The 19 distinct numerators and denominators are 1, 2, 7, 11, 13, 15, 17, 19, 23, 29, 33, 38, 51, 55, 77, 78, 85, 91, 95. The triangular ones are 1 = T_1, 15 = T_5, 55 = T_10, 78 = T_12, 91 = T_13; the Fibonacci ones are 1, 2, 13, 55.

**Lemma (semiprimes; the comment of OEIS A068443, "these triangular numbers are equal to p(2p +- 1)", CITED).** For primes p < q, pq is triangular iff q = 2p + 1 or q = 2p - 1. Proof: 2pq = k(k + 1) with k, k + 1 coprime; k or k + 1 in {1, 2} gives k(k + 1) <= 6 < 2pq, so {k, k + 1} = {p, 2q} or {2p, q}; the first gives 2q = p + 1 (impossible for p < q) or p = 2q + 1 (impossible), the second gives q = 2p + 1 or q = 2p - 1. QED (checked for every semiprime below 10^5, no violation). So 15 = 3 x 5, 55 = 5 x 11 and 91 = 7 x 13 are triangular because the program pairs a prime with 2p - 1 or 2p + 1 (5 = 2 x 3 - 1, 11 = 2 x 5 + 1, 13 = 2 x 7 - 1), and 78 = 6 x 13 with 13 = 2 x 6 + 1 is the same pattern on the composite 6 (the identities 15 = T_5, 55 = T_10, 78 = T_12, 91 = T_13 are session129's); the non-triangular composites 33, 38, 51, 77, 85, 95 pair primes that are not doubling partners. The Sophie Germain pair (5, 11) is the start instruction 55/1.

**Ming's theorem (CITED: L. Ming, Fibonacci Quart. 27 (1989) 98-108).** The only triangular Fibonacci numbers are 1, 3, 21, 55. The program contains 1 (three pure fractions) and 55 (the start); the repository's Hamiltonian-path spectrum contains 1 = H(transitive), 3 = H(C_3), and the forbidden 21 (THM-1370). This is a four-element coincidence and nothing more (NUMEROLOGY). Likewise 15 = f(6), the strong floor of order 6, and 91 is the order-7 value with the most classes (15); none of these is a map (every value at least 49 is strong-only at order 7, so the factor 7 of 77 and 91 carries nothing).

## 3. The merged metagraph is the quotient of the complement-move graph (a definitional remark: THM-4809, THM-4729 Proposition K)

A tiling of order n (THM-4729) is a labelled tournament on {0, ..., n-1} containing the base path 0 -> 1 -> ... -> n-1, i.e. a point of the marked carrier X_n of THM-4809 / THM-4810. The line partner kappa(t) reverses every free pair: kappa = tau_P with P the base path, the native complement move of THM-4810 along the marked path. The metagraph's nodes are the isomorphism classes of tilings with converse classes merged, and its lines are the kappa-pairs {t, kappa t}; a line is blue when sigma (converse followed by i -> n-1-i, which keeps the base path) fixes it. Hence the merged tiling line metagraph at order n is the quotient of THM-4810's labelled complement-move graph restricted to the base-path moves: THM-4809 already defines tau on X_n and its reset cells ([x], [tau x]) are the unmerged lines, and THM-4729's Proposition K calls the merged metagraph the convention-free quotient, so this is a remark, not a theorem. A K move of THM-4732 is kappa applied after re-marking along another Hamiltonian path Q, black when the positional reversal of Q is not an anti-automorphism. The parity arithmetic is shared with THM-4732 Lemma 1 ("an even number L of black moves needs n <= 2L + 2 and C(n-1, 2) even; every black distance is odd for n = 0, 3 mod 4"): THM-4815 (C) already records that THM-4810's Theorem P and THM-4732 Lemma 1 are the even and odd cases of the same toggle-count parity, so the link is canon, not a correction made here (the pre-audit draft cited Lemma 2 and called it a missed self-citation). The L = 2 case of THM-4732 Lemma 1 (no two-move detour for n >= 7, since two moves toggle at most 2(n - 1) pairs while kappa toggles C(n-1, 2)) and THM-4810's Theorem C2 (two moves to the transitive class) are two instances of the composition law, not the same statement. The three-move law of HYP-9347 (1) reads, in THM-4810's Lemma 1: for every closed blue line (t, kappa t) between non-pure-blue classes there are black Hamiltonian paths Q_1 of t, Q_2 of tau_(Q_1) t, Q_3 of tau_(Q_2) tau_(Q_1) t with E(Q_1) xor E(Q_2) xor E(Q_3) = E(P), the base path as the symmetric difference of three successive black legal paths (exhaustive at n = 6, 8, 9, 10 in THM-4732; the (n-4, 3) layer proved for n >= 11 in the endpoint-three packets). Score monotonicity (THM-4810, Theorem L) says only that L changes by at most one along every line, blue or black; the audit's census shows that every blue line at orders 5 and 7 changes L by exactly one (at order 6, 20 of 32 do; black lines split 14 / 10 at order 5 and 7,812 / 8,316 at order 7 between changes 0 and 1), so L is not constant along blue lines and does not constrain a three-move detour.

**The small metagraphs recomputed** (independent code, brute-force canonical forms): order 3: 2 tilings, 2 classes, 2 merged nodes (T_3 with H = 1 and C_3 with H = 3, one tiling each), 1 line, blue, both nodes pure-blue. ## 4. The owner's mappings, tested exactly

**B1 against the single node at n = 3.** The merged metagraph at order 3 has two nodes and a single line, joining the transitive tiling to the cyclic one; the two tilings are each other's unique kappa-partners, as 11/13 and 13/11 are each other's unique reciprocals in the program. **B2, B3, B4 against the three nodes at n = 4.** The three blocks of four pairwise share two flags, so their shared-flag graph is a triangle; the order-4 merged metagraph is a star at the strong node with one loop (T_4 - S_4, pair - S_4 twice, S_4 - S_4). What does transfer is a hub: every block of four passes through the decision flag 17, as every line of order 4 passes through the strong node S_4 (DICTIONARY). The natural labelling by invariants is B2 -> T_4 (the block holding the three pure fractions, against the one pure-blue node), B4 -> the converse pair (the block whose moves 19 -> 23 -> 19 reverse each other), B3 -> S_4 (the block producing the 7-tokens, against the strong hub); it is a reading, not a theorem.

**The prime factorizations against the ten nodes at n = 5.** The fractions contain exactly ten distinct composite numbers, 15, 33, 38, 51, 55, 77, 78, 85, 91, 95 (nine semiprimes and 78 = 2 x 3 x 13). Joining two composites when they share a prime factor gives a graph with 20 edges on these ten vertices; the order-5 merged metagraph has 18 distinct non-loop adjacencies (plus loops at the two nodes with H = 11 and 13). Over all 10! bijections at most 15 of the 20 edges land on adjacencies (40 bijections attain it); for 20 random graphs with 20 edges the same maximum is 14-17 with mean 15.25. So the composite graph does not fit the metagraph better than a random graph of its size: NUMEROLOGY, in agreement with the earlier tests of the ten primes (S70, mac-mini). The ten merged nodes' tiling counts 1, 1, 2, 3, 5, 9, 9, 10, 11, 13 (S70's count coincidence) carry the Fibonacci numbers 1, 1, 2, 3, 5 on TT_5, 1 -> C_3 -> 1, the C_3-end pair, T_5 and Q_5 (the pre-audit draft misdescribed these five nodes), and 13 on the node with H = 13; the two nodes with H = 11 and 13 are joined by a doubled black line, as 11 and 13 are joined by the reciprocal pair. These are count matches (NUMEROLOGY).

**The n = 11 three-move law.** The law (HYP-9347 (1), THM-4732 (3), the (n-4, 3) layer at n >= 11) is a statement about odd black distances in the metagraph, where C(n-1, 2) is odd for n = 11 and distance two is impossible for n >= 7; PRIMEGAME's flag machine is bipartite and has no odd closed walk avoiding the two state-free loops. The two objects are opposite on exactly this point: the complement grammar's shortest detours are odd (three), the prime sieve's control cycles are even (two and four). No PRIMEGAME counterpart of the three-move law exists (section 1), and nothing here bears on the open structural proof for n >= 8.
