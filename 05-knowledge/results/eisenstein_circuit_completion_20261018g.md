## 2. Every minimal positive support has at most three types

A positive circuit is a relation $\sum_i c_i v_i=0$ with distinct types,
positive relatively prime integer coefficients, and no smaller positively
dependent support. In a real plane its support has two or three elements.
Indeed a minimal convex expression for zero uses at most three vectors:
an affine dependence among four permits reducing a coefficient to zero.
The two-type case here is an opposite pair, with coefficients $(1,1)$.

For three noncollinear vectors $(v,w,z)$ the kernel is generated by

$$
(\det(w,z),\det(z,v),\det(v,w)).
$$

The support is positive precisely when these entries have one strict sign.
Choose their positive sign and divide by their gcd. This gives a complete
finite classification, without a search cutoff on coefficients:

| Support size | Sorted coefficients | Number | Physical members |
|---:|---|---:|---:|
|2|$(1,1)$|6|2|
|3|$(1,1,1)$|10|3|
|3|$(1,1,2)$|12|4|
|3|$(1,1,3)$|6|5|
|3|$(1,2,3)$|12|6|

The checker examines all 66 pairs and all 220 three-type subsets with
integer determinants. Thus every primitive coefficient is at most three.
This is a classification of minimal **supports**, not an assertion that
every bounded integer zero sum decomposes into available primitive
circuits without exceeding its multiplicities.

For example

$$
(1,0)+2(0,1)+(-1,-2)=0
$$

has four members, but none of its pairs or three-member subsets sums to
zero. This is the first reason that the inherited extraction can improve.

## 3. A proved residual bound with a sharp homogeneous constant

Let $c_v$ be nonnegative integer multiplicities. Exhaust every available
primitive circuit, meaning that none of the 46 coefficient vectors fits
coordinatewise within the remaining counts. Put

$$
V=\sum_v c_vv,\qquad M=\sum_v c_vQ(v).
$$

**Circuit residual theorem.**

$$
\boxed{\max(M-80,0)^2\le 28Q(V).}                 \tag{1}
$$

Proof. Call a type heavy when $c_v\ge3$. A positive dependence among heavy
types would contain a circuit whose coefficients are all at most three;
that circuit would still be available. Hence the heavy types admit a
strict separating linear functional. They lie in an open half-plane,
and therefore within six consecutive directions of the twelve-type list.
The empty heavy set is permitted.

For the first six directions use $\ell(a,b)=a+4b$. Direct integer
comparison gives $Q(v)\le\ell(v)$ on all six. The twelve possible arcs
have the following corresponding functionals $(u,v)$, meaning $ua+vb$:

```
(1,4), (-1,5), (-4,5), (-5,4), (-5,1), (-4,-1),
(-1,-4), (1,-5), (4,-5), (5,-4), (5,-1), (4,1).
```

Each has squared dual norm

$$
\|\ell\|_{Q^*}^2=\frac43(u^2+uv+v^2)=28,
$$

and each satisfies the exact finite identity

$$
2\sum_{v\text{ in the alphabet}}\max(Q(v)-\ell(v),0)=80. \tag{2}
$$

Every type outside the chosen arc is light, with multiplicity at most
two. Types inside the arc have nonpositive majorant deficit. Consequently

$$
M-\ell(V)\le
\sum_v c_v\max(Q(v)-\ell(v),0)\le80.
$$

Cauchy--Schwarz in this explicit positive definite form gives
$\ell(V)^2\le28Q(V)$. If $M>80$, the preceding inequality also gives
$\ell(V)>0$, proving (1); otherwise (1) is immediate. The receipt retains
the actual, often smaller, positive light-defect bill at every terminal.

The coefficient 28 cannot be uniformly decreased while retaining any
fixed additive allowance. Five copies of $(1,0)$ and three of $(-1,1)$
give $M=14$, $V=(2,3)$ and $Q(V)=7$. Scaling their multiplicities retains
this circuit-free two-type support and gives $M^2=28Q(V)$ at every scale.
No sharpness claim is made for the additive allowance 80.

This is a compression **into the existing energy**. For instance, $r$
copies of $(1,0)$ have no positive circuit, mass $r$ and terminal energy
$r^2$. The theorem supplies no upper bound on that energy. Nor does
absence of an available primitive circuit certify that no more elaborate
bounded zero-sum submultiset exists.

An exact hostile is one copy of each of
$(-2,-1),(0,-1),(1,0),(1,2)$. Their sum is zero and their source norm is
eight, but none of the 46 primitive circuits fits those multiplicities.
Thus primitive-support saturation is not integer zero-submultiset
completion, and the additive allowance cannot simply be removed. The
theorem correctly retains $M=8\le80$ when $V=0$. A complete bounded
integer-kernel selector would be a further target, not a property of this
greedy consumer.

fixed order, preferring smaller member counts, then lexicographic data.
For each circuit it repeatedly consumes the least remaining source IDs
of each required type. Counts only decrease, so an earlier unavailable
circuit cannot become available later. One pass yields the saturation
needed for (1). This need not maximize the amount removed.

$$
\sum_{s<t}\bigl(2a_sa_t-a_sb_t-b_sa_t+2b_sb_t\bigr)

$$

ordinary unaccelerated Collatz step counts.
