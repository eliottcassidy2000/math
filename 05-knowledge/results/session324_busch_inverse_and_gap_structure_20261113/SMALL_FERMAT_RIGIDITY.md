# Rigidity at 35, 119 and 1799

Every realizer of 35, 119, and 1799 is rigid, not merely the displayed witnesses. An automorphism acts freely on ordered Hamiltonian paths, so its order divides H. The SCCs are linearly ordered and preserved individually; a prime-order action has some nontrivial orbit inside one SCC. If the order is 7, a 7-vertex orbit induces a circulant with H=175 or 189 and Q(2)=1988 or 2016. These are the eight choices of one step from each opposite pair, checked in the replay. If the order is 5, a 5-vertex orbit is cyclic regular with H=15 and Q(2)=120.

For H=35, order 7 is impossible by H≥175 on its orbit. There cannot be two 5-orbits, since the positive odd-cycle packing formula gives H≥15² for two disjoint induced blocks (take independent packings within them). With one 5-orbit, it is homogeneous relative to all fixed vertices. If it is a full SCC, 15 would divide H, impossible. Otherwise the strong SCC's quotient contains that orbit mark in a directed triangle. A two-run itinerary through it and the quotient Hamilton paths give at least 3H(orbit)+F₂(orbit)=H(orbit)+Q(2)(orbit)=135 paths, also impossible. This excludes all prime-order possibilities for H=35.

For H=119 and 1799, Busch excludes automorphism primes 17 and 257 respectively, because an SCC carrying a p-orbit has order at least p. The 7-orbit already exceeds 119. At H=1799 there cannot be two 7-orbits (175²>1799), and a single 7-orbit as a full SCC would again contribute a nondivisor. With other vertices in its SCC the same two-run bound is at least 2163, exceeding 1799. This proves rigidity at all three values.


The two-visit lower bound uses only [Busch's insertion/merge lemma](https://www.combinatorics.org/ojs/index.php/eljc/article/download/v13i1n3/pdf/): choose a triangle through the quotient mark v, take the itinerary v→a→b→v, and insert the remaining quotient vertices in it, keeping both v visits. Paths obtained from this itinerary are disjoint from the lifted one-visit quotient paths. The same merge lemma injects pairs of paths on two disjoint induced orbits into paths on the whole tournament, since restricting to the orbits recovers the pair. Hence no positivity assumption about uncounted cross-edges is needed. [The companion bound](RAIL_BOUNDARY.md#symmetry-of-seven-times-fermat-targets) limits larger Fermat targets to group orders 1 and 7, but does not eliminate order 7 there.
