## 1. The user's star/path intuition becomes an exact family

For the specified rank convention, transported tournament converse D obeys

\[
 D(K_{1,r+2})=P_3\sqcup rK_1 \qquad(r\ge0).                 \tag{1}
\]

The [converse packet](tournament_converse_context_20261011d.md) proves this
by identifying the first weighted Lyndon word a^r b with the first connected
automorphism-even graph, the star. A literal labelled inverse keeps two
leaves and detaches the others. Completing the path back into a star loses
which pair was retained, so reconstruction requires exactly binom(r+2,2)
marker states. The four-vertex case has three choices. Star automorphisms
prove there is no unmarked equivariant way to choose one of them.

There is also a general operation: adjacent Lyndon factors L<M fuse into LM.
Every fusion order terminates in the same normal form. On graph types this
gives an associative ordered-join product. Ordinary disjoint union agrees
with this product exactly when its two sorted factor lists already meet
in nonincreasing order. That comparison is the actual retained context.

Converse reverses the product order and reverses every strong letter. It
does not preserve chromatic number: the exact order-six hostile has a
bipartite input and a three-chromatic output. Thus the direct star operation
is useful structure, not a colouring-preserving general graph duality.

## 2. A smaller interface can legitimately forget more

Suppose future contexts touch only the retained P3 core. The extra star
leaves and the extra isolates have exactly the same possible boundary
colourings for every k>=2. Reconstructing the entire graph is unnecessary
for that decision problem. However, for each fixed proper core colouring,
the two numbers of extensions are (k-1)^r and k^r respectively. At palette
six their multiplicity ratio is (5/6)^r, whenever a ratio is defined.

The [weighted boundary packet](boundary_context_weights_20261011d.md)
formalizes the general distinction. If pi is a boundary equality partition
with j blocks, and w_F(pi) counts extensions of one fixed colour-named
representative, then

\[
 P_F(k)=\sum_\pi (k)_j w_F(\pi),\qquad
 w_{F\cup_B H}(\pi)=w_F(\pi)w_H(\pi).                     \tag{2}
\]

Forgetting a port adds its j existing-colour alternatives and k-j new-colour
alternatives. The multiplication in (2) is justified by disjoint interiors
after the boundary is fixed. Taking positive support keeps existence but
forgets multiplicity. This gives a precise additive/multiplicative mechanism,
not a general independence assumption.

Every boundary partition has a separating abstract context made from a
K_k palette anchor. Hence the full boundary relation is minimal against
all abstract graph contexts. At k=6 those anchors are unavailable as plane
unit-distance graphs. Restricting the admissible geometry changes what a
context can observe and can permit further compression; abstract minimality
alone does not rule that out.

## 3. What the attached union paper contributes

[union packet](graph_union_context_20261011d.md) reads all five pages and

| Construction | Colour bound or exact criterion | Required context |

| Glue two fragments in the same palette | intersection of boundary relations | named ports, disjoint interiors and palette alignment |

The paper's first-difference construction extends to mixed palette sizes.
For nondecreasing k_i>=2, source girth at least k_d+2k_(d-1)-2 excludes
mixed-layer triangles in the transitive closure. Thus palettes2 and3 need
only girth5. With a source of chromatic number6 this gives an abstract
union with clique number3 and chromatic number6, using the cited high-girth
existence theorem. Added closure edges retain original monotone paths;

## 5. A sharper target for six versus seven

A finite k-colourable unit-distance subgraph F with two omitted unit-separated
ports that are equal in every k-colouring is an exact equality tester.
Adding that available edge produces a (k+1)-chromatic graph. Conversely an
edge-minimal non-k-colourable unit graph yields such a tester by deleting
any edge. This is an equivalent finite target, not an existence proof.

For k=6, every colouring of a tester must connect the ports in each of five
two-colour Kempe channels. At most two channels can have a two-edge path,
because unit-separated points have at most two common unit neighbours.
At least three channels therefore have length at least four; a colour-class
count gives at least eleven vertices in F. These are necessary filters,
not a competitive global lower bound or sufficient certificate.

The new separator routine either produces a literal recolouring separating
the ports, or returns the paths blocking all single swaps in the supplied
colouring. A unit rhombus proves that the latter does not establish equality
in every colouring. Thus a productive next search can retain exact coordinates,
complete six-colour boundary relations, and the Kempe path receipts together.
The opposite universal target is to prove that every proposed unit edge can
be separated by some recolouring before insertion. Neither direction is
completed here.
