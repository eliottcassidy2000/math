# Session 43 extension: an exact affine compiler for all forward-polynomial congruences

Status: proved for every tournament order. Exact verification is supporting evidence, not the all-size proof. This extends the frozen `session43-cyclotomic` packet without modifying it.

## 1. The integer affine hull

Keep F_T(x), A_n(x), and the odd-cycle expansion from THM-114 / THM-4756 and the companion packet. For fixed n define

    C_s(x)=2(x-1)^(2s) A_(n-2s)(x),
    1<=s<=floor((n-1)/2).

Then the exact integer affine hull of all n-vertex forward-step polynomials is

    Aff_Z{F_T} = A_n + sum_s Z C_s.                         (1)

Here an integer affine combination has integer coefficients summing to one; negative coefficients are allowed. This is NOT the realizability set.

### First inclusion: all tournament polynomials lie in this affine lattice

A nonempty collection R of r vertex-disjoint directed odd cycles contributes

    2^r (x-1)^(2s) A_(n-2s)(x) = 2^(r-1) C_s(x),
    2s=|V(R)|-r.

The empty collection contributes A_n. Thus every F_T has the unique expansion

    F_T=A_n+sum_s alpha_s(T) C_s,
    alpha_s(T)=sum_(R: |V(R)|-|R|=2s) 2^(|R|-1).           (2)

Every alpha_s is a nonnegative integer. Uniqueness follows because C_s has a zero of exact order 2s at x=1: A_(n-2s)(1)=(n-2s)! is nonzero. Together with A_n, the polynomials form a linearly independent family of size ceil(n/2), the dimension of reciprocal polynomials of degree at most n-1.

### Reverse inclusion: explicit actual tournaments form a unitriangular family

Let B_s be the tournament whose first 2s+1 vertices are increasingly oriented except that its final-to-first arc is reversed, and whose remaining vertices are appended transitively with all cross-arcs forward. Set B_0 to be transitive.

Every directed cycle of B_s contains the single reversed arc. There are no two vertex-disjoint cycles, and its directed (2j+1)-cycles are counted by

    binomial(2s-1,2j-1),

because their intermediate vertices are chosen from 2s-1 available ordered vertices. Therefore

    F_(B_s)=A_n+sum_(j=1)^s binomial(2s-1,2j-1) C_j.        (3)

The coefficient matrix is integer unitriangular: its diagonal entries are one. Hence each C_s is an integer combination of the differences F_(B_j)-A_n. This proves (1).

Consequently the Z-linear span is

    Span_Z{F_T}=Z A_n + sum_s Z C_s.                       (4)

Both equalities have explicit integral inverses, obtained by triangular subtraction. No search over tournament isomorphism classes is involved.

## 2. Every universal linear congruence needs only O(n) actual tournaments

Let L be any additive map from integer polynomials to an abelian group, and fix a target value b. Then

    L(F_T)=b for every n-vertex tournament T

holds if and only if it holds for the ceil(n/2) actual tournaments B_s, including B_0. Indeed (1) says every F_T is an integer affine combination of that finite family. Examples include coefficient congruences, residue-class sums, root-of-unity reductions, and any joint linear collection of these.

This applies to LINEAR or AFFINE-LINEAR identities. It does not test positivity, nonlinear incidence constraints, port compatibility, spectral gaps, or whether a candidate affine-lattice point is a realizable tournament polynomial.

## 3. Exact universal cyclotomic valuations

For any prime p and pi=zeta_p-1, use v_pi(0)=infinity. The exact universal exponent is

    D_p(n) = min_T v_pi(F_T(zeta_p))
           = min( v_pi(A_n(zeta_p)),
                  min_s [v_pi(2)+2s+v_pi(A_(n-2s)(zeta_p))] ).  (5)

For p=2, v_pi(2)=1; for odd p it is zero. Formula (4) proves this because the ideal generated by all evaluations is generated by the displayed O(n) Eulerian evaluations. Equation (3) also shows that the minimum is attained by some B_s, not merely by a formal linear combination: the generator and B_s lists are related by a unimodular integer matrix.

For p=2 and even n, all entries are zero, so D_2(n)=infinity. For odd n, (5) reduces to the closed sharp formula already proved in the companion packet. For odd p, (5) is an exact all-size computation rule; a further elementary closed formula is not assumed.

At p=3 all values are units times integers with an even pi-valuation. Hence D_3(n)/2 is the exact universal rational-integer power of 3 in the scalar specialization. This directly generalizes the old mod9 theorem without enumerating tournaments.

### Supported closed-form conjecture for odd primes

The companion lower bound is

    D_p(n) >= 2 ceil((n-max_(1<=m<=n)s_p(m))/2),  p odd.

The exact compiler finds equality at all 404 checked pairs 1<=n<=101 and p in {3,5,7,11}. These are exact computations using proved actual tournament formulas, not random tournament samples. Equality for all odd primes and orders remains a conjecture in this packet. At p=2 the unrounded digit-sum bound is sometimes strictly weaker, as already proved.

## 4. A precise limit: Hamiltonian parity, not the missing-count spectrum

Taking L(F)=F(0)=H(T), one has A_n(0)=1 and C_s(0)=2. For every n>=3,

    Aff_Z{H(T): |T|=n}=1+2Z.                              (6)

Thus the only nontrivial universal integer congruence on H alone is parity (or its equivalent formulations). In particular no conjunction of universal affine-linear congruences on H can distinguish 7 or 21 from other odd numbers. Realizability, not the affine hull, must supply such exclusions.

A stronger concrete hostile occurs at n=5:

    F*(x)=A_5(x)+3C_1(x)
         =7+38x+30x^2+38x^3+7x^4
         =3F_(B_1)(x)-2A_5(x).                            (7)

It has nonnegative integer coefficients, palindromicity, total mass 5!=120, and nonnegative integer excess coordinates alpha_1=3, alpha_2=0. It passes EVERY universal affine-linear congruence valid for order5 because its affine certificate has coefficients summing to one. Yet it is absent from the exact complete labelled order5 census. This is an explicitly verified order5 nonrealizability statement, not a new proof of global absence of Hamiltonian counts 7 or 21.

### Structural nonrealizability, without the census

For n>=5, equation (2) gives alpha_1=c_3 and alpha_2=c_5+2t_(3,3), where t_(3,3) counts unordered disjoint directed triangle pairs. Thus alpha_2=0 implies no directed 5-cycle and no two disjoint directed triangles. Every strongly connected tournament of order at least5 has a 5-cycle, by Moon's classical pancyclicity theorem. Consequently all strong components have order at most4, and at most one can be cyclic (two cyclic components would supply disjoint triangles). A tournament on at most4 vertices has at most2 cyclic triangles. Therefore every realizable profile satisfies

    alpha_2=0 implies alpha_1<=2.                          (8)

This proves A_n+3C_1 is nonrealizable for every n>=5, independently of its other coefficients. At n5 its coefficients also pass all nonnegativity and mass guards, as shown above. The small triangle bound follows directly from c_3=binomial(4,3)-sum_v binomial(outdeg(v),2)<=2; the minimum latter sum at total outdegree6 is2.

Primary source: J. W. Moon, “On Subtournaments of a Tournament,” Canadian Mathematical Bulletin9(3),1966, Theorem1, https://doi.org/10.4153/CMB-1966-038-7 . The same paper's Theorem3 extremizer already uses the reversed-extreme-arc tournament underlying B_s; our contribution is its unitriangular congruence/compiler use, not invention of that classical tournament.

This explains why a complete metagraph wiring compiler cannot stop at scalar congruence guards: even their entire affine closure admits false candidates. Shared incidence and realizability constraints have genuine additional work to do.
