Let R_k=(Z/5^k)[δ]/(δ²−5), μ=(1+δ)/2, g=μ⁴, n=5^k. Multiplication by μ is the Fibonacci companion matrix Q in basis1,μ. Put A=Q².

## 100,50,25 are successive full clocks at modulus25

Since g−1=μ²δ, binomial lifting gives
v_δ(g^d−1)=1+2v_5(d)
before vanishing in R_k. Modulo δ, μ=3 has order4, so μ has order4n, A order2n, g order n. At n=25 these are100,50,25.

Conjugation satisfies bar(μ)=−μ^(−1)=μ^(2n−1). An element μ^d is scalar iff fixed by conjugation, since2 is a unit. Thus
4n divides (2n−2)d.
The gcd of4n and2n−2 is4, so the first scalar time is n. The scalar μ^n has order4. The first time μ^d=±1 is2n. For A and g, both scalar and signed clocks are n.

These are different observation quotients, not competing definitions of one period.

## Trace loses a different factor

The trace pairing in basis1,μ has matrix [[2,1],[1,3]], determinant5. Its radical modulo5^k is the size-five ideal
J=δ^(2k−1)R_k.
Indeed solving2x+y=x+3y=0 gives5x=0 and y=−2x.

Two adjacent traces determine the whole trace sequence by Cayley–Hamilton. A shift d is invisible precisely when μ^d−1∈J. Reduction moduloδ forces4|d, and the valuation formula gives the least such shift4·5^(k−1). Since A and g also generate R_k, the analogous trace clocks are2·5^(k−1) and5^(k−1).

At modulus25:
| observer | Q | A | g |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| full matrix |100|50|25|
| scalar quotient |25|25|25|
| global sign quotient |50|25|25|
| complete trace history |20|10|5|

Discarding scalar phase and discarding the trace radical are distinct losses.

## Doubling the modulus does not double the clock

The dyadic period is pi(2^a)=3·2^(a−1). One proof starts from Q³=I+2Q and Q⁶=I+4(I+2Q); successive squaring raises the exact2-adic valuation by one after the base case. CRT gives
pi(2^a5^k)=lcm(3·2^(a−1),4·5^k), a,k≥1.

The exact chart is:
| modulus | full Q | full A | full g | scalar Q | signed Q | trace Q |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
|25|100|50|25|25|50|20|
|50|300|150|75|75|150|60|
|100|300|150|75|150|300|60|
|200|300|150|75|150|300|60|

Full and arbitrary scalar return times combine by lcm. Global signed times require the SAME sign at every CRT component. This is an important hostile to a naive lcm rule for signed clocks:
Q^150=49I modulo100, and Q^150=149I modulo200.
Neither scalar is globally±1. At100 its local signs are +1 modulo4 and−1 modulo25. At200 the component modulo8 is5, not±1. The next scalar return300 is the full return.

An unpublished preliminary local chart incorrectly used150 for the signed100 clock. The published verifier and this note use300 and retain the explicit scalar witness. Preliminary scratch files are not evidence for the final chart.

## Same cardinality200, different groups

Let H=<g>. Any additive normalizer of H conjugates the algebra generated by g to itself. Since g=(7+3δ)/2 has a unit δ coefficient, that algebra is R_k. Therefore every normalizer element uniquely has the form x↦u σ(x), u a ring unit and σ a ring automorphism.

The unit group splits:
R_k^×=(Z/5^k)^× × H.
Their intersection is trivial: a scalar in H has norm1, hence is±1, and H has odd order. Their orders multiply to4·5^(2k−1), the full unit count.

For k≥2, a ring automorphism sends δ to bδ. Trace preservation rules out a scalar part, and the ring equation requires5(b²−1)=0 modulo5^k. Thus b=±1+5^(k−1)t, giving a cyclic group C10. These automorphisms preserve H, the norm-one units with residue1 moduloδ. Hence
N_k=R_k^× ⋊ C10,
N_k/H ≅ C_(4·5^(k−1)) × C10,
|N_k/H|=8·5^k.
At k=1 the automorphism group is C4 instead, and the effective quotient is C4×C4, of order16.

This quotient is faithful on the independent signed clock-orbit choices. To fix every bank state one must fix each oriented H-orbit. The orbit of1 forces u∈H. After removing it, the orbit ofδ requires bδ=g^jδ. If5^(k−1) does not divide j, the odd valuation of g^j−1 below2k−1 cannot equal the even valuation of the scalar b−1. Otherwise g^j is1 moduloδ^(2k−1), forcing b=1 modulo5^k. The kernel is exactly H.

At k=2 the effective group is therefore
E_200=C20×C10,
abelian of exponent20 with Sylow5 subgroup C5².

There is ANOTHER natural group of order200: G=<μ,conjugation>. Its relation is
c μ c=μ^49.
The exponent49 is1 modulo4 and−1 modulo25, so
G ≅ C4×D50,
where D50 denotes the dihedral group of order50. This group is nonabelian, has exponent100 and Sylow5 subgroup C25. Its derived subgroup is exactly H, and its bank image is only G/H=C4×C2, of order8.

Thus two natural objects both numbered200 are not isomorphic. The distinction between cyclic C25 and elementary C5² links directly to the old regular-action cancellation story: equal group orders do not determine cycle inventories.

## Compare the scale laws, not just one equality

The one-fibre information rank from PORT_INFORMATION is
D_0=4·5^(2k−2).
It equals pi(5^(2k−2)) for k≥2, but equals pi(5^k) only at k=2. This is an arithmetic equality of dimensions/orders, not a constructed conjugacy.

As k grows:
- the effective normalizer size8·5^k multiplies by5;
- the information rank4·5^(2k−2) multiplies by25;
- the conservative spectral theorem constant200 stays fixed.

Their coincidence at one small scale cannot replace the missing maps.
