## 1. An owned subset-overlap selector

Let C,D be named seven-cells in an equitable tournament partition. In the
present carrier their internal degree is three and their cross-block
degree q is three or four. For u in C normalize its actual row on D to
a three-subset R_u:

    R_u = N^+(u) intersect D,                 q=3;
    R_u = D minus N^+(u),                    q=4.

For each v in D retain both internal templates

    O_v = N_D^+(v),       I_v = N_D^-(v).

They also have size three. Consequently

    |R_u intersect O_v|=3 iff R_u=O_v,
    |R_u intersect I_v|=3 iff R_u=I_v.

If one named block/template orientation has exactly one matching pair
(u,v), both owners are intrinsically identified. This is an all-size
equal-cardinality subset principle, not a theorem that every block has
a unique match. Empty, repeated, and nonunique matches remain retained
as such. The finite program uses **both** template orientations: converse
leaves normalized cross-rows unchanged and exchanges O and I, preserving
the owner selection.
