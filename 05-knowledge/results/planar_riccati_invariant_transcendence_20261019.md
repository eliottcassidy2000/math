# Rational invariant fibres, transcendental scalar-time source coordinates

2026-10-19. **PROVED by formal algebra:** for the invariant E=x²t²(1+x²t), every nonzero scalar time generated by a nonconstant polynomial f(E) has each original source-coordinate image transcendental over K(x,t). A finite composition from this commuting family is the identity precisely when its effective polynomial Hamiltonian is constant; otherwise both coordinate images are transcendental. **OPEN:** arbitrary different-invariant rational compositions, other Hamiltonians, source-chart entry, and planar JC. No historical novelty claim is made.

## 1. The new rational-route target

The [Newton-cone theorem](planar_joint_boundary_newton_cone_20261019.md) excludes nonidentity polynomial outputs for a larger family but deliberately leaves rational outputs open. Its new boundary member R=x² has a rational generic invariant fibre, so the earlier high-genus obstruction does not apply. Here the obstruction is the dependence of a Riccati scalar-time transformation on the invariant parameter.

Work over any characteristic-zero field K in the original source coordinates x,t, with {F,G}=F_xG_t−F_tG_x. Put

    q=1+x²t,  s=xt,  E=x²t²q.

The exact field change is

    E=s²+s⁴/t,
    t=s⁴/(E−s²),  x=(E−s²)/s³.                      (1)

Thus K(x,t)=K(s,E), and s,E are algebraically independent. For fixed generic E, the invariant curve has rational function field K(E)(s). Direct differentiation gives

    {s,E}=s²−E,  {E,E}=0.                            (2)

Let f∈K[Z] be nonconstant and λ∈K be nonzero. Write

    δ_f={−,f(E)},  Φ=exp(λδ_f)(s),
    X=exp(λδ_f)(x),  T=exp(λδ_f)(t).

**Theorem.** Each of Φ, X, and T is transcendental over the original rational source field K(x,t)=K(s,E). In particular no nonzero scalar time of this family gives a rational source map.

All statements concern the specified completed scalar-time exponential, not arbitrary automorphisms of the rational generic fibre. No fixed-line or Jacobian-conjecture theorem is needed in this packet.

## 2. Faithful completion transport

Retain the Newton weight w(x)=-1,w(t)=2 and the joint-boundary ring J from packet 119: its nonnegative source-monomial series have finitely many supported monomials below each weight bound. It embeds injectively into K[x][[t]]. Here w(s)=1 and w(E)=2.

The formal power-series ring K[[s,e]], equipped with weights 1 and 2 on its abstract variables, maps continuously to J by s↦xt and e↦E. This map is **injective**. Decompose a nonzero series into its finite weighted-homogeneous polynomial pieces. Its first nonzero piece remains nonzero after substitution, by algebraic independence of s,E; different weights cannot cancel. Each weight-bounded truncation contains only finitely many source monomials, so the substituted series belongs to J. We henceforth write E also for the abstract second variable when no confusion arises.

If f(Z)=a_0+∑_{k≥1}a_kZ^k, then

    f(E)−f(0)=t²q R_f,
    R_f=∑_{k≥1}a_k x^(2k)t^(2k−2)q^(k−1).

Every expanded monomial of the kth summand has weight 2k−4≥−2. Thus these Hamiltonians lie in the Newton cone. Their derivations raise weight by at least one and source t-order by at least one, so their scalar exponentials exist in J and in the source t-adic completion and agree there.

On K[[s,E]], the same derivation is exactly

    δ_f=f'(E)(s²−E)∂_s,                             (3)

which raises weighted order at least one. The injective substitution intertwines the derivations and their convergent exponentials. In particular the source expression exp(λδ_f)(xt) is the image of the formal series Φ∈K[[s,E]].

Algebraicity and rationality are compared inside a common field: the source injection extends from J to its fraction field and then to K(x)((t)). For an algebraic relation concerning Φ, first rewrite its coefficients using K(x,t)=K(s,E), then clear denominators in s,E. The resulting relation has coefficients in K[s,E] and gives an identity in J. Injectivity of K[[s,E]]→J transports this identity back into K[[s,E]]. No specialization of a rational denominator is assumed legitimate before this step.

## 3. An exact formal Riccati solution

Set a(E)=λf'(E), and define formal series

    C(E)=∑_{n≥0} a(E)^(2n) E^n/(2n)!,
    S(E)=∑_{n≥0} a(E)^(2n+1) E^n/(2n+1)!.

These are well-defined in K[[E]], and

    C²−ES²=1,
    Φ(s,E)=(sC−ES)/(C−sS).                          (4)

The denominator has constant term one, so the quotient belongs to K[[s,E]]. To verify (4) without analytic functions, temporarily regard λ as formal. Termwise differentiation gives C_λ=f'(E)ES and S_λ=f'(E)C. The determinant identity is constant in λ and equals one at zero. The displayed quotient then satisfies

    ∂_λΦ=f'(E)(Φ²−E),   Φ|_(λ=0)=s.

Uniqueness of the formal differential equation gives the exponential series. Every weighted coefficient is a polynomial in λ, so the identity specializes to every scalar λ∈K. No analytic exponential or complex continuation is involved.

At s=0,

    Φ(0,E)=−ES(E)/C(E).                             (5)

For an independent formal variable z, put

    g(z)=−Φ(0,z²)/z=zS(z²)/C(z²),
    U(z)=λz f'(z²),  P(z)=U'(z).

Then g∈zK[[z]], g(0)=0, and termwise formal differentiation of the odd/even series gives

    g'=P(z)(1−g²).                                  (6)

Here P is a **nonzero polynomial**: f is nonconstant, λ≠0, U is a nonzero odd polynomial, and characteristic zero makes its derivative nonzero. The usual notation tanh(U) describes g, but no transcendence theorem about named analytic functions is used.

## 4. Specialization does not discard the algebraic relation

**Lemma 1.** If F∈K[[s,E]] is algebraic over K(s,E), then F(0,E) is algebraic over K(E).

**Proof.** Clear denominators in a nonzero polynomial relation to obtain Q(s,E,F)=0 with Q∈K[s,E,Y]. Remove the greatest power of s dividing every coefficient of Q. The resulting relation is still valid, since K[[s,E]] is a domain, and its specialization Q(0,E,Y) is a nonzero polynomial. Evaluating the formal identity at s=0 gives Q(0,E,F(0,E))=0. This proves the claim; a nonzero specialized constant polynomial would simply contradict the identity. ∎

There is also an explicit rational removable-pole version. If F=A/B with coprime A,B∈K[s,E], then B(0,E) cannot vanish identically. Otherwise s divides B, and BF=A in K[[s,E]] would force s to divide A as well. Consequently F(0,E)=A(0,E)/B(0,E)∈K(E). This addresses the potential forbidden denominator before evaluating at s=0; it does not assume it away.

## 5. An algebraic logarithmic-derivative obstruction

**Lemma 2.** If P∈K[z] is nonzero, the differential equation

    g'=P(1−g²),   g(0)=0

has no solution g∈K[[z]] algebraic over K(z).

**Proof.** If such g existed, let r=(1+g)/(1−g). Its numerator and denominator have constant term one, so r is nonzero and algebraic, and

    r'/r=2P.

Set L=K(z,r), a finite separable extension of K(z). The derivation d/dz extends uniquely to a separable algebraic extension: differentiating a minimal polynomial determines the derivative because its derivative in the algebraic variable is nonzero. Thus every embedding into a normal closure commutes with this derivation. Taking the product over the embeddings proves the logarithmic-derivative norm identity directly:

    n=Norm_(L/K(z))(r)∈K(z)*,
    n'/n=Tr_(L/K(z))(r'/r)=2[L:K(z)]P.              (7)

For a nonzero rational function n=A/B, its logarithmic derivative is A'/A−B'/B and has Laurent order at least one at infinity: it is O(z^(−1)). It cannot equal a nonzero polynomial. The right side of (7) is nonzero in characteristic zero, a contradiction. ∎

For the rational-only obstruction, even the norm step is unnecessary. A rational solution of (6) cannot have a pole at infinity, because differentiation lowers its growth exponent while P g² raises it. If it has finite limit c there, comparison first forces c=±1; its first nonconstant inverse-power term then has different orders on the two sides. The only rational solutions are therefore the constants ±1, incompatible with g(0)=0. The norm argument strengthens this to every finite algebraic extension.

## 6. Transcendence of the invariant coordinate and each source coordinate

If Φ were algebraic over K(s,E), Lemma 1 would make Φ(0,E) algebraic over K(E). Substituting E=z² and dividing by z would make g algebraic over K(z), contradicting Lemma 2 and (6). Therefore Φ is transcendental.

The flow fixes E. Apply it to the polynomial identities obtained from (1). In the common fraction field one obtains

    XΦ³+Φ²−E=0,
    Φ⁴+TΦ²−TE=0.                                   (8)

If X were algebraic over K(s,E), the first equation would make Φ algebraic over the finite extension K(s,E,X), hence algebraic over K(s,E), a contradiction. The polynomial in Φ is nonzero because E≠0. If T were algebraic, the second equation is monic in Φ and gives the same contradiction. Thus **each** of X and T is transcendental over the original rational source field. This proves the theorem.

The generic invariant fibre is nevertheless rational. Equation (4) is a Möbius transformation in s after adjoining its time coefficients. The obstruction is their transcendental dependence on the generic invariant parameter, not the genus of the fibre.

## 7. Exact commuting cancellation criterion

Let f_1,…,f_r∈K[Z] and λ_1,…,λ_r∈K. The Hamiltonian derivations for f_i(E) commute: they are f_i'(E) times the same derivation {−,E}, which kills their coefficients. Their convergent exponentials therefore satisfy

    exp(λ_rδ_(f_r))⋯exp(λ_1δ_(f_1))=exp(δ_F),
    F(Z)=∑_{i=1}^r λ_i f_i(Z).

If F is constant, the composition is the identity. If F is nonconstant, the theorem with scalar time one says each source-coordinate image is transcendental. Hence this commuting family has a rational source realization **if and only if F is constant**, exactly the identity-cancellation case.

This criterion is not asserted for arbitrary different-invariant words from packet 119. It is a complete result for this one rational-invariant family and its finite commuting compositions.
