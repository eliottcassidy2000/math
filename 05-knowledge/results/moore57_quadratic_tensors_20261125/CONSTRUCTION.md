# A missing matching forces symmetry in prime-degree covers

**PROVED.** Let \(p\ge5\) be prime. Take \(p\) branches of \(p\) sites each and put a perfect matching between every two branches. If this graph has girth at least five, its holonomy on a branch is **nonsolvable**. For \(p=7\) the holonomy must contain \(A_7\). Here holonomy means the permutation group generated by transport around closed walks in the complete graph on the branches. The result concerns these local transports, regardless of the automorphism group of a larger host.

**CONDITIONAL degree-57 consequence.** Suppose seven branch sets in a rooted Moore chart admit the maximum seven disconnected eight-branch extensions. The known equality conditions split a common fiber into eight atoms of size seven and require parallel affine-plane labels **between** the atoms. The theorem forces the transports **within every atom** to generate \(A_7\) or \(S_7\). In particular choosing Fano-plane linear maps on the seven positions of even one atom makes this candidate impossible. This also applies to *any* seven-sheet component on seven branches, without assuming the extremum. The earlier degree-56 primitivity result constrains the full chart. Its conclusion alone gives no such subgroup restriction on a seven-sheet component of seven chosen branches. ## The one missing matching

Between two distinct branches \(i,j\), the direct matching and all \(p-2\) two-step transports must be pointwise disjoint. A collision would give a triangle or square. Conversely these tests detect every triangle or square, since a matching is bijective. The \(p-1\) maps miss exactly one destination per source, and exactly one source per destination. Their complement is therefore a permutation, the missing matching \(F_{ij}\). Every component of the cover has the same number of sites per branch and contains at least \(p-1\) by these routes. Thus the cover is connected.

First suppose all matchings can be written as translations of \(\mathbb F_p\). Write \(a_{ij}\) for their increments, with \(a_{ji}=-a_{ij}\) and \(a_{ii}=0\), and \(s_i=\sum_k a_{ik}\). The missing increment is determined by summing the field elements, whose sum is zero:
\[
 f_{ij}=-a_{ij}-\sum_{k\ne i,j}(a_{ik}+a_{kj})
        =a_{ij}-s_i+s_j .                                      \tag{1}
\]
Thus the missing cover is the original one in a new choice of coordinates on the branches.

Take \(\zeta=\exp(2\pi i/p)\). In this Fourier mode the adjacency is the Hermitian \(p\)-square matrix \(M\) with zero diagonal and off-diagonal entries \(\zeta^{a_{ij}}\). Use \(D=\operatorname{diag}(\zeta^{-s_i})\). For each \(i\ne j\), the direct and two-step phases plus the missing phase sum to zero. On the diagonal \((M^2)_{ii}=p-1\). Hence
\[
              DMD^{-1}=g(M),\qquad g(X)=(p-1)I-X-X^2.           \tag{2}
\]
Here \(g(M)\) uses ordinary matrix multiplication. Since \(D^p=I\), the polynomial iterate \(g^{\circ p}\) fixes \(M\). Its real algebraic eigenvalues have period one or \(p\) under the scalar polynomial; no rationality of an individual eigenvalue is assumed. With \(h(x)=-x-1/2\), the same scalar dynamics in the translated coordinate are \(h(g(x))=h(x)^2+1/4-p\). For seven sheets the constant is \(-27/4\).

## The Fourier spectrum forces a square

All eigenvalues cannot be fixed. The fixed roots are \(-1\pm\sqrt p\), and no multiset of \(p\) such roots sums to zero. Since \(g(M)\) and \(M\) have the same spectrum, one nonfixed eigenvalue forces a complete orbit of size \(p\). This exhausts the dimension and all eigenspaces are one-dimensional. Equation (2) implies \(D\) cyclically permutes them. For a unit eigenvector \(v\), the vectors \(v,Dv,\ldots,D^{p-1}v\) therefore form an orthonormal basis. In this basis \(D\) is a cyclic shift because \(D^pv=v\). Its diagonal entries in the original branch basis must consequently run through every \(p\)th root of unity exactly once.

Index the branches by \(t\in\mathbb F_p\) so that \(D_{tt}=\zeta^t\). The matrix of eigenvectors now has entries \(v_t\zeta^{rt}\). Its row norms give \(|v_t|=p^{-1/2}\). Writing the eigenvalue in column \(r\) as \(\lambda_r\), spectral decomposition yields
\[
 M_{tu}=v_t\bar v_u\sum_{r=0}^{p-1}\lambda_r\zeta^{r(t-u)} .
\]
For pairwise distinct \(t,u,w\), the product \(M_{tu}M_{uw}M_{wt}\) depends only on their differences, since the three squared magnitudes \(|v_t|^2,|v_u|^2,|v_w|^2\) are constant. Therefore all triangle voltages are invariant under the branch shift \(t\mapsto t+1\). The antisymmetric increments \(c_{tu}=a_{t+1,u+1}-a_{tu}\) have zero sum on every triangle. With \(b_0=0,\ b_t=c_{0t}\), this gives \(c_{tu}=b_u-b_t\) for every pair.

The branch shift thus lifts to the graph automorphism \(\tau(t,x)=(t+1,x+b_t)\). It commutes with the deck shift \(\delta(t,x)=(t,x+1)\), and \(\tau^p=\delta^{\sum b_t}\). Together they act transitively, have order at most \(p^2\), and hence act regularly on the \(p^2\) sites. The graph is an undirected Cayley graph of an abelian group. Its \(p-1\ge4\) neighbors at the identity include two generators \(a,b\) with \(b\ne \pm a\). Then \(0,a,a+b,b\) form a square, contradicting girth. This proves the cyclic obstruction for every prime in the stated range. The boundary is genuine: the 9-cycle, projected to the triangle by taking indices modulo three, is a three-sheet translational example.

For a direct algebraic diagnostic at seven, put \(g(x)=6-x-x^2\). A period-seven orbit forming this spectrum would have sum zero. Its starting eigenvalue would be a common root of the two integer polynomials
\[
              g^{\circ7}(x)-x,\qquad \sum_{r=0}^6 g^{\circ r}(x).
\]
Their reductions modulo five have greatest common divisor one, as checked by ordinary polynomial division in the replay. Their leading coefficients are units modulo five, so there is no common algebraic root in characteristic zero. The general Cayley argument also explains geometrically why the seven-step orbit fails.

## Affine slopes and the seven-point linear group

For solvable transitive holonomy on a prime number of sites, a minimal nontrivial normal subgroup is elementary abelian and transitive by primitivity. An abelian transitive subgroup of a faithful permutation group is regular. This normal subgroup consequently has order \(p\), and its normalizer is the affine group of the line over \(\mathbb F_p\). By propagating coordinates from one branch along a spanning tree, every matching can now be written as an affine map in that group. Two affine maps with unequal slopes meet in one argument. The direct and every two-step transport have disjoint images pointwise, so their slopes agree. Thus the edge slopes satisfy the multiplicative triangle identity. Rescaling each branch from an anchor makes all slopes one. This reduces solvable holonomy to the excluded translations.

In degree seven, the only remaining transitive groups up to permutation conjugacy are \(\mathrm{GL}_3(2),A_7,S_7\). [Degree-seven transitive-group catalogue](https://galoisdb.math.upb.de/groups?deg=7) · [GAP group libraries](https://docs.gap-system.org/doc/ref/chap50_mj.html)

Suppose all matching maps are linear on the seven nonzero vectors of \(\mathbb F_2^3\). At an ordered branch pair normalize its six maps by returning to the first branch along the inverse direct matching. This gives \(I\) and five triangular holonomies, pairwise disjoint at each nonzero vector. The seventh missing map is linear: it is the pointwise sum of the six maps, because the sum of the seven nonzero vectors is zero. Its images permute the nonzero vectors by the missing-matching count.

**FINITE-EXACT.** Every sharply transitive seven-set in \(\mathrm{GL}_3(2)\) containing \(I\) is one of its eight cyclic Singer subgroups of order seven. The replay enumerates the 168 choices of an ordered basis and the 48 maps fixing no nonzero vector. It finds exactly eight six-element sets of pairwise disjoint maps among these derangements. In each case adjoining \(I\) gives precisely the powers of any one nonidentity member.

Fix branch \(i\). The five triangles at \(i\) through directed edge \(ij\) therefore lie in a Singer subgroup \(H_{ij}\) of its fiber. The same triangle on \(i,j,k\), read backwards, puts its inverse in \(H_{ik}\). This nonidentity element of order seven determines its cyclic subgroup, so \(H_{ij}=H_{ik}\). The triangles at \(i\) generate all its holonomy, by using the star at \(i\) as a spanning tree. The entire holonomy would be cyclic, already impossible. The only surviving options are \(A_7,S_7\).

## A seven-cycle of the branches cannot lift, even if the atoms can move

**PROVED using a finite-exact search.** For a seven-sheet \(K_7\) cover of girth at least five, the group of automorphisms permuting its fibers has order coprime to seven and is intransitive on the seven fibers. The full holonomy result makes its action on fibers faithful. Indeed an automorphism fixing every fiber commutes on one fiber with every triangle transport and hence with \(A_7\). Its action there is trivial because the point stabilizer in \(A_7\) fixes only that point. Transport then fixes every site.

If an automorphism induced a 7-cycle on the fibers, it would have order exactly seven. Label one fiber arbitrarily and propagate these labels around the cycle using the automorphism. In this gauge the automorphism is \((i,x)\mapsto(i+1,x)\) for \(i\in\mathbb F_7\). The matching from \(i\) to \(i+d\) is a permutation \(P_d\) independent of \(i\), with \(P_{-d}=P_d^{-1}\). Only \(P_1,P_2,P_3\) need be given. Their simultaneous conjugation has no effect on the route tests, so choose one representative per cycle type of \(P_1\).

The exhaustive replay assigns these three permutations in order. Whenever a direct or two-step route is known, it rejects coincident destinations with the same starting label and ending branch. Tests from source branch zero suffice by translation, and it suffices to test destination differences 1, 2 and 3 by inversion. It visits respectively 15, 75,600 and 13,376,160 tuples at the three depths, with **zero** complete solutions. The same code in degree five finds 11 passing tuples after fixing a first-permutation conjugacy representative, before dividing by the remaining simultaneous symmetries. In degree three it finds none, as expected: the branch rotation of the 9-cycle can lift in order nine, while this enumeration requires a lift of order three.

There is a consequence for **the entire seven-exception Moore candidate**, even allowing permutations of its eight atoms. If a chart automorphism cycled the seven common branches, take one atom whose orbit length \(r\) under that automorphism is coprime to seven. One exists since there are eight atoms. Raising the automorphism to the \(r\)th power now stabilizes that atom and still cycles the seven core branches. Restricting to the atom gives the forbidden lift. Thus the stabilizer of the common seven-branch set cannot act transitively on those branches, by Cauchy's theorem in the induced degree-seven permutation group. The statement concerns symmetry of the specified branch chart; actual graph automorphisms need not stabilize this branch set.

## Five-cycles fail too, forcing solvable branch symmetry

**PROVED using a finite-exact search.** The faithful action on the seven fibers of any such atom contains no element of order five. Normalize a putative five-cycle as \(\sigma=(1\,2\,3\,4\,5)\), fixing branches 0 and 6. Propagate the coordinates of branch 0 along its six matchings to make them identities. Any lift of \(\sigma\) must now act by the *same* sheet permutation \(h\) in every branch, since it preserves the anchor matchings. Its fifth power is the identity by faithfulness, so \(h\) is the identity or a five-cycle. If it is the identity the five maps from branch 6 to the five moving branches are equal in these coordinates. The two-step routes from 6 to 0 then coincide. Thus fix \(h\) to a five-cycle up to common coordinate conjugation.

Every remaining matching is now specified by just three seeds: one map from 6 to 1, one from 1 to 2, and one from 1 to 3. Their translates under \(\sigma\) are conjugated by \(h\), and reverse maps are inverses. Each map is a derangement because the route via the anchor is the identity. For the first seed quotient by the simultaneous conjugations centralizing \(h\); retain all coordinates for the next two seeds. Testing each single orbit together with the anchor leaves 40, 720 and 720 choices respectively. Of the 28,800 pairs in the first two domains, 114 pass the joint route tests. None of the resulting **82,080** complete tuples has all route destinations distinct. Run `python3 -B five_cycle.py` for the full enumeration. [Exact fixed-anchor replay](five_cycle.py)

Therefore the group of fiber-preserving automorphisms of each atom has order of the form \(2^a3^b\) and is solvable by Burnside's two-prime theorem. For the eight-atom equality case, the same conclusion holds for the **induced action on the common seven branches**. As before, a five-cycle or seven-cycle on those branches would preserve some atom after raising it to a power coprime to the cycle length, since the number of atoms is eight. Every prime divisor of a subgroup of \(S_7\) is among 2, 3, 5, 7. Thus the available relabelling symmetries of the base form a solvable group even though within-atom transports generate at least \(A_7\). The kernel of the induced action on the seven branches of the full ambient graph is outside this bound.

## A positive construction at five

The obstruction distinguishes the alphabet of local permutations. For \(p=5\) one can realize the girth condition with holonomy exactly \(A_5\). With coordinates \(0,1,2,3,4\), take all anchor matchings to be the identity and use the following image lists, with inverse lists in the reverse direction.

| Branch pair | Permutation images |
|---|---|
| 12 | 1, 2, 3, 4, 0 |
| 13 | 2, 0, 4, 1, 3 |
| 14 | 4, 3, 0, 2, 1 |
| 23 | 1, 4, 3, 0, 2 |
| 24 | 3, 2, 4, 1, 0 |
| 34 | 2, 4, 1, 0, 3 |

Every direct and two-step list for each pair is pointwise disjoint. The six maps are even and generate 60 group elements. This constructs a 25-vertex graph, and demonstrates that nonsolvable local mixing can attain the one-missing-matching conditions. A 49-site realization with seven branches or a full degree-57 Moore graph would require additional work.

## An inverse obstruction from puncturing a Moore graph

**PROVED, conditional on the ambient Moore graph.** Start with a diameter-two Moore graph of degree \(k=7\) or \(57\), delete a vertex \(v\), then delete edges to make the remaining \(k^2\) vertices regular of degree \(k-1\). Whenever such a deletion succeeds, the resulting graph has eigenvalue \(-1\) of multiplicity **exactly one**. Its unique perfect one-error-correcting code (a set whose closed one-neighborhoods partition the vertices) is the old neighborhood of \(v\). In particular puncturing Hoffman–Singleton this way can never supply a seven-sheet cover of \(K_7\), even with a completely free choice of new fibers.

Indeed let \(H=N(v)\), with the other \(k(k-1)\) surviving sites called leaves. The edges deleted after puncturing must form a perfect matching of the leaves. Each head already has degree \(k-1\) and every leaf has degree \(k\). Every leaf has precisely one head neighbor. The vector equal to \(-(k-1)\) on heads and 1 on leaves is a \(-1\) eigenvector of the punctured and matched graph. For a second independent eigenvector, one could take a nonzero combination \(w\) with \(\sum_{x\in H} w_x=0\). Extend it by zero at \(v\). Regularity of the punctured and matched graph gives \(w\perp\mathbf1\). For the original Moore adjacency \(A\), we would have \((A+I)w=Pw\), where \(P\) is the deleted matching, zero on all other sites. Its norm is at most \(\|w\|\). The two nonprincipal eigenvalues of \(A\) are \(2,-3\) at degree seven and \(7,-8\) at degree 57. Thus \(\|(A+I)w\|\ge2\|w\|\), a contradiction.

For any perfect code \(C\) of this regular graph, the vector \(1_C-\mathbf1/k\) has eigenvalue \(-1\). The unique eigendirection just computed makes membership constant separately on heads and leaves, and \(|C|=k\) forces \(C=H\). Each fiber of a cover of \(K_k\) is such a code. Thus no such cover can come from this construction. Conversely the punctured graph remembers its old heads intrinsically. Its leaves split into the original fibers by their unique head neighbors. Each leaf now lacks a matching partner in exactly one other fiber, which identifies the fiber in which its deleted edge must be restored. If at most one edge was removed per unordered pair of fibers, these data reconstruct all the deleted edges uniquely. The obstruction uses the full eigenvalue gap. At degree three deleting a vertex and a suitable matching from Petersen does give a 9-cycle.

Run `python3 -B construction.py` from this directory for the algebraic filter, the seven-point subgroup calculation and both positive controls.
