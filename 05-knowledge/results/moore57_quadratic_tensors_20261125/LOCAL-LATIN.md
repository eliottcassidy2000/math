# An abelian obstruction on one Latin square with every other branch free

**PROVED.** In any diameter-two Moore graph of degree \(q+1\), \(q\ge3\), choose any root, any anchor branch and any other destination branch. Let \(L\) be the resulting Latin square of order \(q\). Its universal abelian group with generators \(r_i,c_x,s_y\) and defining relations \(r_i+c_x=s_{L(i,x)}\) is exactly \(\mathbb Z^2\), generated by two arbitrary constant labels. In particular **no individual board can have a nonconstant additive quotient**. This is a local test with the transport permutations outside the two chosen stars completely unrestricted. The global group-of-derangements obstruction assumes a common regular group for the matching permutations. ## Two lists with the same two holes

Normalize all matchings to the anchor as identities. Fix the destination \(i\). Its table has an identity row, indexed by the anchor, and one row \(L_k\) for each of the other \(q-1\) branches \(k\ne i,\mathrm{anchor}\). Take any nonidentity row \(b\) and source slot \(x\) in its branch. Write \(y=L_b(x)\), so \(y\ne x\). For each \(k\) other than the anchor, \(i\), and \(b\), let \(t_k\) be the slot reached from \(x\) in \(k\).

The list of routes from \(x\) to the anchor consists of \(x,y,t_k\). The list to \(i\) consists of \(y,x,L_k(t_k)\). Each list contains all \(q\) slots once. Hence, among the \(q-2\) included rows, the selected columns \(t_k\) and symbols \(L_k(t_k)\) both enumerate precisely the complement of \(\{x,y\}\). As \(x\) varies the selections use every cell in each included row once, since each intermediate matching is a bijection. This is a resolvable system of punctured transversals, forced by the physical location of the intermediate vertex.

An additive labeling of the table is equivalent, after using the identity row, to
\[
                         f(L_k(t))-f(t)=a_k,\qquad a_{\rm id}=0
\]
in an arbitrary abelian group. Put \(A=\sum_k a_k\). Sum over a punctured transversal for \(b\). Its two slot lists coincide, so \(A-a_b=0\). This holds for every nonidentity row. Fixing a column \(x\), the other rows attain every \(y\ne x\); all \(f(y)\) at these locations equal \(f(x)+A\). For any two symbols a third exists, so all symbol values agree. All \(a_k=0\). Reading the original presentation now leaves independent constant row and column labels, giving the claimed group. In the Petersen case \(q=2\) the third symbol is absent and its two-slot Latin square really is a cyclic table.

It follows that the commutator subgroup of the permutation group generated by the rows acts transitively on the slots. Indeed the whole group acts transitively by the Latin property. Its action on the orbits of its commutator subgroup is transitive and abelian, hence regular after dividing out its kernel. The orbit labels would give exactly the forbidden additive quotient if more than one survived. An isotope of a group table of order 56 is also excluded: any such group is solvable by the \(p^a q^b\) theorem and has nontrivial abelianization. Permuting row, column or symbol names transports an additive labeling.

## Two small linear systems per board

Set \(f(0)=0\). Form integer rows from
\[
     f(L_k(t))-f(t)-f(L_k(0))+f(0)=0
       \qquad(k\ne\mathrm{id},\ t\ne0).
\]
There are only \(q-1\) unknowns. In the universal group for this reduced system summing a single row's translation rule over all \(t\) gives \(q a_k=0\). The first column includes every symbol, so this group is killed by \(q\). It vanishes exactly when the displayed matrix has rank \(q-1\) modulo every prime dividing \(q\). A degree-57 candidate therefore needs rank **55 modulo 2 and modulo 7** at each such square. The test is necessary, and makes no assumptions about the group's action on other branches.

## How far a repair must move

Fix a proposed nontrivial quotient labeling \(f\) with values in a finite abelian group and proposed row shifts \(a_k\), with the identity shift zero. A defective cell is one for which the displayed translation rule fails. Let \(e_k\) count defects in row \(k\) and \(D=\sum e_k\). Whenever a nonidentity row \(b\) has \(a_b\ne A\), each of its \(q\) punctured transversals contains at least one defect outside that row. These transversals partition the cells in the included rows, so
\[
                                D-e_b\ge q .
\]
For a bijective labeling by an abelian group of order \(q\) and distinct fitted shifts, at most one nonidentity row can have \(a_b=A\). A Latin board differing from this group table must change at least two rows, since the other \(q-1\) already prescribe the last. Each changed row has at least two altered cells. Some changed row \(b\) therefore has \(a_b\ne A\), giving **at least \(q+2=58\) altered cells** at order 56 in this normalized comparison. In a nonconstant quotient that is exact on some rows, at least three nonidentity rows must fail to have *any* constant shift on an extendible board: with one exception use a puncture at another row to force its defect function constant; with two use a puncture at one exception to force the other constant. The constant functions on the remaining rows justify those sums in any abelian group.

The obstruction also restricts permissible search moves. In a two-row Latin trade that exchanges the entries over one alternating cycle of length \(\ell\) in the column permutation, summing the successive label differences round that cycle gives \(\ell(a_i-a_j)=0\) for any existing quotient. For an \(\mathbb F_7\) quotient and \(7\nmid\ell\), the two rows agree in quotient value at every exchanged entry, so the quotient survives. In particular any sequence of intercalate switches starting from the cyclic order-56 table retains its reduction modulo seven and cannot reach a valid Moore board. This applies independently of the number or locations of those switches.
