# What the first seven integer parameters see

Take \(f_z(x)=x^2-z\). The Mandelbrot parameter in the usual convention is \(c=-z\). It is the orbit starting at the critical point \(0\) that determines membership. For real \(z\ge0\) that orbit is bounded exactly for \(0\le z\le2\). Indeed \(b=(1+\sqrt{1+4z})/2\) is fixed. If \(z\le2\), then \(z\le b\) and \([-b,b]\) maps into itself. If \(z>2\), the second critical iterate \(z^2-z>z>b\) and the subsequent iterates increase to infinity. Individual periodic orbits can still exist at parameters outside this interval.

| \(z\) | All integer cycles | Orbit beginning at zero |
|---:|---|---|
| 0 | \(0\) and \(1\) fixed | \(0\) fixed; also \(-1\to1\) |
| 1 | \(0\leftrightarrow-1\) | on that cycle; \(1\to0\) |
| 2 | \(2\) and \(-1\) fixed | \(0\to-2\to2\); also \(1\to-1\) |
| 3 | \(1\leftrightarrow-2\) | \(0\to-3\to6\to33\to\cdots\) |
| 4, 5 | none | escapes |
| 6 | \(3\) and \(-2\) fixed | escapes |

For every integer \(z\), rational periodic points are integers: in lowest terms, a nonunit denominator squares at each step. A fixed integer \(a\) requires \(z=a(a-1)\); its partner is \(1-a\). A genuine two-cycle is \(a\leftrightarrow-1-a\) with \(z=a(a+1)+1\). These give the observed missing intervals in parameter space. They exhaust the integer cycles: on a nonfixed orbit, taking consecutive differences and multiplying \(f_z(x)-f_z(y)=(x-y)(x+y)\) round the orbit gives \(\prod_i(x_i+x_{i+1})=1\). Every factor is an integer unit, so all successive differences have the same positive absolute value. A finite arithmetic lattice with steps of one fixed length is a path and cannot contain a simple cycle of length greater than two.

At the rational parameter \(29/16\) the three-cycle is \(-7/4\to5/4\to-1/4\to-7/4\). Its adjacent sums \(-1/2,1,-2\) still multiply to one, illustrating exactly where the integer proof loses its force. The global period-four and period-five exclusions need the arithmetic of their respective dynatomic curves. Poonen's conditional polynomial portrait bound is nine rational points including infinity. The count of twelve portrait types, the twelve-point result for a different rational-map family, and the repository's twelve points across two parameters have distinct meanings. ## The actual parameter of the Moore spectral calculation

For \(z=m-1/4\) with integral \(m\), translating \(x=t+1/2\) gives the integral polynomial \(t^2+t-m\) in \(t\). A rational periodic \(t\) must be integral, and the adjacent-difference proof above applies with factors \(t_i+t_{i+1}+1\). Hence every finite rational cycle has period one or two. Fixed points occur exactly when \(m\) is a square; a genuine two-cycle occurs exactly when \(m-1\) is a positive square, by the respective discriminants \(4m\) and \(4(m-1)\). In the seven-sheet obstruction the actual iterate has \(z=27/4\), so \(m=7\) and **there are no finite rational preperiodic points at all**. The Fourier eigenvalues of the proposed chart may be algebraic, so the [trace and transport proof](CONSTRUCTION.md) supplies the essential additional obstruction. The example \(29/16\) crosses a denominator boundary at which integer unit differences cease to control the period. ## A twelve-point theorem that applies to the three-cycle lift

The earlier projective lift for \(z=29/16\) sends \(v=(-7,1)\) to \(w=(-5,-1)\) and then to \(w-v=(2,-2)\). Its six successive vectors are \(v,w,w-v,-v,-w,v-w\). Sending a vector \((X,Y)\) to \(X/(4Y)\) carries these to two repetitions of the three-cycle. [THM-4146, rational three-cycle order-six lift](../../../01-canon/theorems/THM-4146-rational-three-cycle-order-six-lift-horizontal-divisor-fibre-firewall.md)

**PROVED application.** Take the orbit lattice \(\Lambda=\mathbb Zv+\mathbb Zw\). It has index \(|\det(v,w)|=12\) in the ambient integer lattice. Relative to the basis \(v,w\), the convex hull is the hexagon with vertices \(\pm(1,0),\pm(0,1),\pm(-1,1)\). Its six sides are primitive, its area in fundamental parallelograms is three, and Pick's formula gives a single interior lattice point. The dual polygon in \(\Lambda^*\) is given by \(|a|,|b|,|a-b|\le1\), also with six primitive sides. Thus this period-three model realizes the \(6+6=12\) case of the Poonen–Rodriguez-Villegas twelve-point theorem. Rotation by \(90^\circ\) in these basis coordinates intertwines the hexagon shift with its inverse-transpose action on the dual. The lattice matters: in the ambient \(\mathbb Z^2\) the same polygon has area 36, eight boundary lattice points and 33 interior lattice points. The twelve counts boundary points of a primal and dual lattice polygon. The rational polynomial has eight finite preperiodic points at this parameter. [Poonen–Rodriguez-Villegas, *Lattice Polygons and the Number 12*](https://math.mit.edu/~poonen/papers/lattice12.pdf)
