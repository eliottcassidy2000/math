# 55, 13, and 33: where the two missing moves actually bind

25 November 2026. This note follows [the rigid fourth-branch patch](NESTED-CORES-AND-RIGID-FOURTH.md), and carries historical results forward as distinct measurements, not as evidence from repeating a number. There are two new exact tests: a synchronized three-coordinate obstruction for the Moore fixture, and an all-order one-arc barrier at tournament height 55.

## 1. The degree-55 site has two missing rows, but the first obstruction is elsewhere

The central branch order is \(56,0,28\). At site \((1,1)\) the 51 new placements leave two exterior branches, 13 and 33, and these two unoccupied destinations in each central coordinate:
\[
             \{3,16\},\qquad \{43,49\},\qquad \{24,45\}.
\]
The 14 completed sites in branch 1 already occupy exactly the slots \(2\bmod4\) in *both* branches 13 and 33. In branch 13 the best available pairwise hits are at sheet 16 with triple \((16,36,24)\) and sheet 29 with \((29,43,45)\). No sheet in branch 33 hits even two of the three lists. The missing rows 13 and 33 are chart addresses, not automorphism invariants.

Here is the stronger obstruction. Look through **all 3,024 physical triples**, before assigning them to branches. Each of the three two-coordinate projections onto its \(2\times2\) box contains **all four pairs**, but **none of the eight full triples exists**. So all twelve pair tests are positive, while the synchronized test is empty. No amount of recoloring or exchange of branch labels that preserves the physical triple set can close the two holes with the 51 current contacts unchanged. For example, the pair \((3,43)\) exists, but its unique third slot is 1; the pair \((43,45)\) also exists, but its unique first slot is 29. The two projections are witnessing different objects.

Separate the cost of physical rearrangement from the cost of distinct exterior branches. Keep the original 14 completed neighborhoods and the marker \((1,1)-(2,7)\). Keep the same physical table and forbid reusing any occupied target. Now **drop only the requirement that the 53 new contacts belong to distinct exterior branches**. An exact conflict-driven search, indexed by first central coordinate, finds:

| Held out of the 51 reference contacts | Result in the relaxed problem |
|---:|---|
| At most six changed | no solution |
| Seven changed | ten solutions; the best one uses only 46 distinct branch labels |

The explicit best witness retains 44 reference triples, covers all three coordinate complements, and still has seven repeated branch uses. The complete case with distinct exterior branches previously failed through 13 changes; the two bounds distinguish the two bottlenecks, and cannot be added. The physical relaxation permits several contacts in one exterior branch and is not itself a degree-57 neighborhood.

Completeness of the replay: choose an open first-coordinate row, fit a candidate, and release every still-held reference row whose coordinates it occupies. Once chosen, the candidate is fixed deeper in that search branch. Any target solution prescribes a candidate for the selected open row and forces all its conflicting reference rows to change. The branches thus exhaust repairs within the given budget that are generated from the two holes. Any disconnected set of changes can be omitted, and at the minimum feasible budget such an extra set is impossible. The ten minimum-budget outputs are therefore counted once each, not an estimate from random search.

This is the same precise quantifier issue as the [November parameter-synchronization example](../quadratic_cycle_synchronization_20261125/QUADRATIC-CYCLE-SYNCHRONIZATION.md): one parameter (or one physical vertex) must supply every constraint, not a different witness for each projection. It also transfers the [order-11 port atlas](../moore_fixed_order_spectra_20261124/PENTAGON-PORT-ATLAS.md) correctly. There, a color contains \(55=5\cdot11\) ports, and a five-coordinate signature is uniquely determined by any two of its entries. Unique identification by a pair does not make independent pair choices consistent. That 55 is a conditional color size, whereas the present 55 is the current degree of a particular site. Likewise the one-square Latin test's 55 is the expected rank after deleting the constant direction on 56 sheets, not either of these graph measurements.

## 2. A 55-height barrier that really does hold at every order

**No tournament of height 55 can change to height 53 or 57 by reversing one arc.** This is not a failure to realize 55. The [November converse census](../residues_monoid_tower_tree_20261116/CONVERSE.md) gives four order-seven classes at 55, forming two different converse pairs. The [October order-eight spectrum](../../../04-computation/experiments/h_spectrum_sweep_20261027_n8.out) gives eight order-eight classes, all nonstrong. Our independent exhaust visits 220 fixed-spine masks of height 55 at order seven, all strong by the component factorization, and 440 at order eight, none strong. It checks every single arc at order seven and both singleton paddings, with neither neighbouring height attained. The counts of fixed-spine masks are not counts of isomorphism classes.

For why the finite search is enough, strong component heights multiply in their linear order, and only singleton strong components have height 1. By [Busch's lower bound](https://doi.org/10.37236/1141), no strong tournament of order at least nine can have height 55. Thus a height-55 tournament, at any order, has either one strong height-55 seven-site component or two strong components of heights 5 and 11, with all other components singleton. Those two smaller heights require at least four and five vertices, respectively. An edit internal to a component has unchanged multiplicative factors outside it. When the component carries all 55 it reduces to the checked seven-site case; when it does not, the other factor rules out a jump of 2.

For a cross-component edit, let \(u\to v\) go from component \(U\) to later \(V\). No old directed cycle uses that edge. Reversal adds a triangle for each out-neighbour of \(u\) in \(U\), each in-neighbour of \(v\) in \(V\), and each intervening vertex. The [odd-cycle packing identity](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.05569) makes the change nonnegative and gives at least two paths per new triangle. Thus a +2 flip needs at most one triangle. If both endpoint components are nontrivial there are at least two. With one nontrivial endpoint there can be no intervening vertex; a +2 change then reduces to the checked singleton pad of a height-55 component, since any other nontrivial component outside those two would multiply the change by 5 or 11. With two singleton endpoint components, zero intervening sites gives no change; one gives a promoted triangle with the full factor 55 outside, hence a jump of 110; more than one already gives at least two triangles. This exhausts the −2 and +2 possibilities.

**Two edits are enough, with one common initial move.** With the fixed spine \(0\to1\to\cdots\to6\), index nonconsecutive pairs lexicographically and mark a reversed pair with a bit 1. Mask 81 has height 55. Reverse \(\{2,4\}\) to reach mask 593 of height 65. From there reverse \(\{0,4\}\) for 53 (mask 597) or \(\{0,3\}\) for 57 (mask 595). They certify that each minimum distance over all height-55 fixtures to its respective value is exactly two. They share an exact setup move, but their intermediate height is not between 53 and 57. This is a precise warning about monotone descent. It proves nothing about the existence of a +2 move at the exponent-61 perfect number.

## 3. Carry-forward index across the repo

- **March, Chebyshev doubling and the golden unit.** The old [Lucas–Lehmer/Chebyshev packet](https://github.com/eliottcassidy2000/math/commit/edfcad56b62b57691d62e931eae8f612866be5cd) uses \(L_{2n}=L_n^2-2(-1)^n\). The companion Fibonacci product is \(F_{2n}=F_nL_n\), so \(55=F_{10}=5\cdot11\). That is the same integer factorization that governs the two-component tournament case, not a dynamical conjugacy of the graphs.
- **March through November, Paley, Ramanujan, and the kissing of two uses of 13.** The [odd-alphabet dossier](../moore_fixed_order_spectra_20261124/THIRTEEN-ODD-ALPHABET.md) carries the old Paley-11 exact count: \(95,095/55=1729=7\cdot13\cdot19\), where 55 is the automorphism-group order and 1729 is a path-orbit count. The October [metallic-unit audit](../../../01-canon/theorems/THM-4656-wall-sun-sun-golden-orbit-explosion.md) gives yet another typed 13, a Pell-unit orbit-lifting example, not a golden Wall–Sun–Sun prime.

The [converse census](../residues_monoid_tower_tree_20261116/CONVERSE.md) shows \(33\) has five first realizers at order six, \(1+2\cdot2\), and **13 order-seven classes**, \(1+2\cdot6\). - **October square-sum prefixes and the next vertex after 32.** The [square-reflection crossing note](../moore_fixed_order_spectra_20261124/SQUARE-REFLECTION-CROSSING.md) used the first 25 and 32 vertices. Vertex 33 gives a clean next control: 13 and 33 are nonadjacent, and their unique common neighbour in the full positive-integer square-sum graph is **3**. Indeed the needed squares differ by \(20=(v-u)(v+u)\); the unique positive integer pair is \(u=4,v=6\). This creates the wedge \(13-3-33\) at the first prefix containing 33. In contrast, the differences \(55-13=42\) and \(55-33=22\) are \(2\bmod4\), so neither of those label pairs can share a square-sum neighbour. This is a small test of route uniqueness, not the branch mechanism in the Moore fixture.
- **November, the simultaneous cycle reads.** The [13-cycle packing](../moore_fixed_order_spectra_20261124/APEX-EXCHANGE-AND-CYCLE-PORTS.md) gives 35 symmetry types of three disjoint induced 13-cycles in Hoffman–Singleton, whereas translating one cycle under the tested order-five action cannot pack three. The [Singer analysis](../moore_fixed_order_spectra_20261124/SINGER-ORDER13-MODULES.md) gives the order-13 phase test; this is a separate symmetry assumption from the order-11 atlas. The [quadratic synchronization note](../quadratic_cycle_synchronization_20261125/QUADRATIC-CYCLE-SYNCHRONIZATION.md) has 13 as the first possible split-prime norm magnitude after the unit cases in its real window. These examples identify why multiple projections or copies still need a common realization; they do not assign cycle lengths to the names of the missing branches.

A compact way to carry the three heights together is exact. A five-site tournament of height 11 flips to 13 (fixed-spine masks 6 and 38); a four-site tournament of height 3 flips to 5 (masks 1 and 5). Their ordered join gives the four joint readings \(33,39,55,65\). Its mixed difference is 4, because the heights multiply. This connects the old 33 and 55 common factor 11 to the 13 move without pretending that the two edits add independently. It also explains why this clean multiplicative grammar does not exhibit a unit crossing at 55.
