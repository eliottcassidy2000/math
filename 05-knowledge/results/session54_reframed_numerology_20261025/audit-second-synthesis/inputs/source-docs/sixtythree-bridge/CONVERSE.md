# Stronger bridge: converse-equivariant F64 multiplicative carrier

## Extended symmetry

Let iota send a deleted arc (u->v) to (-v->-u). It preserves the Paley arc set, is an involution, and conjugates vertex translation t:x->x+1 to t^-1 while commuting with m:x->2x. Thus <t,m,iota> is C7 semidirect C6 of order 42. The involution swaps two-source with two-sink and common-out with common-in, while sending the star at c to the star at -c.

The only iota-fixed blocker is the star at 0. In contrast, ordinary vertex negation on nonempty even subsets has seven fixed points. More strongly, ANY F2-linear involution on a six-dimensional space has at least seven nonzero fixed vectors: in characteristic two, (L-I)^2=0 implies rank(L-I)<=3, hence nullity>=3. Therefore no extension of this blocker action to a six-dimensional binary linear representation can realize converse linearly, regardless of which G-set bijection is chosen.

## Multiplicative repair

Choose a primitive element g of F64×, put alpha=g^9 of order seven, and write nonzero field elements as g^e for e mod63. Define the action

    t: e -> e+9       (x -> alpha*x),
    m: e -> 16e       (x -> x^16),
    iota: e -> -e     (x -> x^-1).

Since 16 congruent 2 mod7, this has exactly the required conjugation relations. These transformations form a group of order42: seven translations by multiples of nine times the six distinct multipliers generated by16 and -1 mod63.

Use the same intrinsic five blocker types as README.md. Define Psi(B)=g^e by:

- Star at c: e=9c.
- Common-out center c: e=9c+21.
- Common-in center c: e=9c+42.
- Two-source pair ordered u->v, with v-u=2^k and k in {0,1,2}: e=9u+16^k.
- Two-sink pair ordered u->v, with v-u=2^k: e=9v-16^k.

All exponents are modulo63; vertex coordinates are modulo7, which does not affect multiples of nine.

### Bijection and equivariance proof

The star family covers exactly e congruent0 mod9. The common-out/in families cover e congruent3,6 mod9. The source family covers residues1,7,4 mod9; the sink family covers8,2,5. Within each residue class, varying the translate covers its seven exponents exactly once. Thus Psi is a bijection to all F64×.

Translation by b adds9b in all five formulas. Multiplication of vertices by2 multiplies exponents by16: 16*9 congruent18 mod63, 16*21 congruent21, 16*42 congruent42, and k increments cyclically. The involution takes source pair u->v to sink pair -v->-u; the sink formula gives -9u-16^k, the negative of the original exponent. It also swaps common-out at c with common-in at -c, because -(9c+21) congruent-9c+42. Stars transform correctly as well. Hence Psi is equivariant for all42 transformations.

## The 7*phi(d) structure

The quotient F64×/F8× has order nine. Equivalently phase is x^7 in the ninth roots of unity, with exponent e mod9. Its strata of phase orders d=1,3,9 have sizes

    7*phi(1)=7,   7*phi(3)=14,   7*phi(9)=42.

These are exactly the star, common-neighbor Hall, and source/sink-pair blocker orbits under the extended group. The combined multiplier iota*m acts on e mod9 by -16 congruent2 mod9, a generator of (Z/9)× of order six. Thus the six-phase unit action is genuinely structural here, not only an equality of counts.

## Information boundary

This multiplicative carrier is different from the binary augmentation module. Even before adding converse, it is not a linear G-module identification: translation on the augmentation module has the two distinct irreducible cubic characters of C7, whereas multiplication by alpha on F64 has two copies of the same cubic character, since F64 is two-dimensional over F8. They are nonetheless isomorphic as G-sets, as the explicit maps prove. Converse is inversion, not an F2-linear map; the fixed-point obstruction shows why this is necessary.

Native arc incidence, XOR, and Hamming distance are still not preserved. The old Sierpinski carrier still has the wrong fixed points. No HP counts beyond the named minima, no Collatz orbit dynamics, and no LRC predicate have been transported. The new positive result is a precise G42-set bridge, its phase-order stratification, and a sharp no-linear-converse theorem.
