Endpoint responses identify minimum blockers after scalar collapse

Status: PROVED conditional theorem and independently checked finite consequences.
This is a deduction from the pinned cycle-potential classification and endpoint
counts, not a new classification, an unconditional all-Paley theorem, or a
literature-priority claim. No previous packet was modified.

1. Exact endpoint response

Let T be a tournament, B a binary arc word, and suppose
  b_uv = a_u - d_v on every arc,  sum_v a_v - sum_v d_v = 2.
For any directed Hamiltonian path P=(v_1,...,v_n) with start s and end t,
  |B intersect P|
    = sum_{i=1}^{n-1}(a_{v_i}-d_{v_{i+1}})
    = sum_v a_v - a_t - (sum_v d_v - d_s)
    = 2 - a_t + d_s.
Thus paths with the same actual ordered endpoints have the same response;
the interior order is irrelevant. The formula is invariant under simultaneous
constant shifts of both potential lists. Binary potentials themselves are not
required; only binary arc values are. In a regular tournament with outdegree r,
|B|=r(sum a-sum d), so the classified minimum blockers of size 2r have the
required deficiency 2.

The formula does NOT imply B is a blocker. The classified mixed row/column
nonblockers have a zero-intersection endpoint witness. Nor does every binary
potential blocker have responses in {1,2}: P7's exceptional Hall families attain
3. These distinguish the general identity from the special signature theorem.

2. Scalar histograms collapse at P11

For P_q let E_st count HPs with start s and end t. The square-affine action gives
E_st=A on arcs s->t and E_st=B otherwise (s!=t), with zero diagonal. Each row
and each column sums to R=r(A+B). Total H=qR. We use A,B here as scalars, not
as the deletion word B in section 1.

For deletion of the outgoing arcs at a vertex pair U, intersection is
2-1_{t in U}; for incoming pair U it is 2-1_{s in U}; for the full incident
star at v it is 2-1_{s=v}-1_{t=v}. In each case exactly 2R paths meet the
blocker once, and H-2R meet it twice. In the star case the two endpoint events
are disjoint because an HP has distinct endpoints.

At P11, A=628,B=1101,r=5, R=8645,H=95095. The audited complete classification
has 55 outgoing pairs,55 incoming pairs,11 stars. ALL 121 minima therefore
share the scalar intersection histogram
  intersection 1:17290 paths; intersection 2:77805 paths.
No scalar histogram identification of these 121 objects is possible.

At P7, A=1,B=8,r=3,R=27,H=189. Its 21+21+7 basic minima share histogram
  intersection 1:54; intersection 2:135.
The 14 additional Hall minima instead have histogram
  intersection 1:57; intersection 2:129; intersection 3:3.
The checker enumerates all 189 actual P7 paths, independently reconstructs
integral potentials from each blocker on the bipartite support, and checks the
identity path by path. For P11 it independently recomputes the fixed-start
endpoint counts using subset DP, translates to every start, and evaluates all
121 endpoint-weighted histograms. It does not enumerate P11 deletion subsets.

3. Optimal fixed-observer star-pool theorem

Assume a tournament T on n>=5 vertices has an HP for EVERY ordered pair of
DISTINCT endpoints, and its complete minimum blocker family consists of the
outgoing pairs O_U, incoming pairs I_U (|U|=2), and stars S_v, these being n^2
distinct blockers. Fix observer vertex 0. Choose any one HP for each endpoint
pair 0->i and i->0, i!=0. These 2(n-1) chosen HPs form the observer star pool.
Repeated paths with equal endpoints cannot help by section 1.

For an HP query define its bit as 2 minus its blocker intersection. In these
three families the bit is respectively
  O_U: 1_{end in U}; I_U:1_{start in U}; S_v:1_{start=v}+1_{end=v}.
They really are bits since start and end differ. The requisite potential
representations are (a,d)=(1_U,0), (0,-1_U), and (1_v,-1_v).

Pick any w!=0. Omit BOTH queries 0->w and w->0, retain the remaining 2n-4.
Let L=V minus {0,w}, m=|L|=n-2>=3, and record the signature (x,y), where
x_i is the bit on 0->i, y_i the bit on i->0, i in L.
Write 0_m,1_m for constant vectors, and e_v for a retained unit vector.
The complete list is:
  O_U: (1_{U intersect L}, 1_{0 in U} 1_m).
  I_U: (1_{0 in U} 1_m, 1_{U intersect L}).
  S_0:(1_m,1_m); S_w:(0_m,0_m); S_v:(e_v,e_v) for v in L.
Here a vector 1_{U intersect L} is not all ones because |U|=2<m, unless
m=2, excluded. If 0 not in U its weight is 1 or 2; if 0 in U its weight is
0 or 1. This list is injective.

Explicit decoder (also implemented verbatim in verify.py):
- If x=y, it is a star: all ones identifies 0, all zeros identifies w,
  and the nonzero coordinate of a unit vector identifies a retained vertex.
- Otherwise, if y is constant and x is NOT all ones, it is outgoing pair.
  Its retained members are the ones of x; include 0 iff y is all ones.
  If this yields only one member, include w.
- Otherwise it is incoming pair. Its retained members are the ones of y;
  include 0 iff x is all ones; if only one member results, include w.
The exceptional constant patterns are handled correctly: (0_m,1_m) means
O_{0,w}, whereas (1_m,0_m) means I_{0,w}. No zero-member case occurs.

Lower bound WITHIN THIS FIXED POOL: if two terminal labels u,v!=0 are
unqueried, the distinct blockers O_{0,u} and O_{0,v} agree on every retained
query. Every reverse query has end 0, and every retained forward query has
end outside {u,v}; their bits agree in both cases. Therefore at most one
forward query may be omitted. Symmetrically I_{0,u},I_{0,v} show at most one
reverse query may be omitted. Fewer than 2n-4 queries omit at least three
of the 2n-2 pool elements, hence omit two in one direction, and fail.
This proves the sharp optimum 2n-4 inside the fixed-observer star pool.
Moreover this characterizes ALL optimal fixed subsets: the separate bounds
force exactly one forward and one reverse omission. Suppose the forward
omission is 0->u and the reverse omission is v->0 with u!=v. Then O_{u,v}
and S_v agree on every retained query: their forward vectors both have the
sole 1 at v, while their reverse vectors are zero (the v coordinate was
omitted). Hence an optimal separating subset must omit matching labels.
Conversely the decoder above proves every matching omission works. There are
exactly n-1 optimal subsets, one for each w!=0. This count concerns subsets
of the fixed pool of chosen paths, not the many choices of interior HP routes.
The proved bound concerns fixed separating subsets of this pool. It is NOT an
optimum over arbitrary HP query sets, and no adaptive-query lower bound is
claimed.

Boundary hostile n=4: with observer 0, omitted w=3 and retained labels 1,2,
O_{1,2} and I_{0,3} both give (x,y)=(11,00). Thus the algebraic construction
fails when m=2. This is a signature countermodel, NOT a claim of a four-vertex
tournament meeting the theorem's full hypotheses.

4. Scope and certificates

The signature theorem uses no regularity or Paley property beyond its stated
hypotheses. P11 satisfies them by the audited complete classification and
strict positivity of independently checked endpoint counts; its pool optimum
is 18 of the original 20 queries. P7 has extra minima, so the n^2-family
theorem is NOT applied to P7. Its exceptional Hall response may be -1 rather
than a bit. Larger certified Paley orders may use the theorem only after BOTH
complete three-family classification and all ordered endpoint HP existence
are established. A forward endpoint witness alone is insufficient for the
latter: check both endpoint orbits. No general Paley rank or endpoint assertion
is imported implicitly from the conditional criterion.

The checker verifies every omitted w and every family label for n=5,...,31,
including explicit decoding. It tests lower-bound indistinguishability witnesses
for every pair of omitted labels in both directions, every unequal forward/reverse
omission collision, and the n=4 hostile.
These are finite checks of an already proved algebraic theorem, not claims
that tournaments with its hypotheses exist at every integer n in that range.
