## A new worked special case: ten points in four dimensions

**Claim.** Any finite set of at most ten distinct points of positive diameter in Euclidean four-space partitions into at most five smaller-diameter pieces. Proof uses an exact finite C++ census and independent short geometric certificates; no priority or unrestricted claim.

Normalize diameter to one and let its diameter graph G have exactly the pairs at distance one. Proper coloring and smaller-diameter partitioning agree on a finite set. If five coloring fails, take a vertex-minimal obstruction, which is 6-vertex-critical. By [Gallai’s decomposition, in Stehlík's critical-graph paper](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0095895603000698), a vertex-critical graph with connected complement and chromatic number k has at least 2k−1 vertices. With at most ten vertices our obstruction is a join, with one factor for each component of its complement. Each factor is vertex-critical, and its chromatic number adds under joins. A connected-complement factor is a singleton or has chromatic number at least 3 (the only 2-critical graph is K₂).

Retain the [archive's forbidden-near-clique lemma](../borsuk_order_interfaces_20261014j.md): K₆ minus one edge cannot occur even as a non-induced subgraph. The four common vertices make a regular tetrahedron; the tips both live at the same length on its one-dimensional normal, where they either coincide or are farther apart than the diameter. This also excludes K₆.

Hence the possibilities are:

- A single 3-critical nonsingleton is an odd cycle (a nonbipartite graph contains a shortest induced odd cycle, and criticality forces equality). It joins three singletons. Three consecutive cycle vertices together with K₃ give K₆ minus one edge.
- A connected-complement 4-critical factor has 7 or 8 vertices and joins two singletons. Its own K₄ minus an edge would yield the forbidden K₆ minus an edge. The exact census below shows that every such critical factor has this near-clique.
- A connected-complement 5-critical factor has 9 vertices and joins a singleton. Its own K₅ minus an edge is forbidden. The census leaves seven isomorphism types.
- Two nonsingleton factors must both be 3-critical, and fit only as C₅ joined to C₅. An indecomposable 6-critical factor would need 11 vertices.

### Circle and rank certificates

**Circle lemma.** On a circle, if all pairwise minor angles are at most θ<2π/3, the sites lie in an arc of length at most θ and at most one pair can attain θ. Choose one site as angle zero and put all others in [−θ,θ]. If the span exceeds θ, to make the extreme pair's minor angle ≤θ the span would have to be at least 2π−θ, which is impossible since the span is at most 2θ. At chord length exactly the permitted diameter, only the extreme pair can qualify.

- No diameter triangle can have two disjoint diameter edges in its common neighborhood. The common unit-distance sites lie on a circle of radius sqrt(2/3) in the orthogonal two-plane through the triangle center. The pairwise diameter condition limits their angles below 2π/3, so the lemma applies. Four of the seven nine-vertex factors, after adding their apex, have this forbidden configuration.
- The join C₅ with C₅ is impossible. Cross distances all one force the two difference spans to be orthogonal, by subtracting the four squared cross distances. Both spans have dimension at least two, since a line set has at most one diameter edge. They thus have dimension two, and their complementary orthogonal affine planes intersect in one point. Taking it as origin, the cross distances show both sets lie on circles with squared radii summing to one. One radius is at least 1/sqrt(2); its angle limit is at most π/2, so that cycle cannot exist by the circle lemma.
- Move the apex to zero in each of the other three candidates. The other nine vectors have norm one, so the matrix M=2Gram−J has rank ≤5. The diagonal is one. Off diagonal it is zero on unit edges and **strictly positive** on nonedges of the *exact* diameter graph. In each remaining pattern there is a 6×6 minor with at least one supported determinant term, with all supported terms of the same permutation parity. Its determinant cannot vanish no matter the positive values, contradicting rank ≤5. Replay checks the minor signs, not approximate coordinates.

The census uses the exact complement. A k-critical n-vertex graph has minimum degree k−1, hence its complement maximum degree n−k. Up to relabeling put a maximum-degree vertex first, with its neighbors at the next available labels. The recursive generator tries every remaining pair subject to this degree limit, without using any geometry. The near-clique guard on the complement requires at least two edges in each k-set. The clique-cover recursion tries each clique containing the least remaining vertex and recurses on the complement of that clique. Require chromatic number exactly k and deletion of any vertex to lower it. Canonical forms are minima over every degree-compatible relabeling of each survivor. Thus this is a complete finite certificate under the stated conditions, not an empirical set sample.

    ./critical_blocks 7 4
