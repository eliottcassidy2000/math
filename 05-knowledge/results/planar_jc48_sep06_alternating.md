## 2. Elementary all-size group lemma

Let `T` be any collection of individual three-cycles on a finite set
`Omega`. Form the hypergraph whose edges are their three-point supports,
and let `C` run through its connected components, including isolated
vertices. Then

```text
<T> = product_C Alt(C).                                  (1)
```

Here `Alt(C)` acts trivially off `C`, and is trivial when `C` is a
singleton. Every nonsingleton component has at least three vertices.
In particular, a transitive group **generated by individual three-cycles**
is the full alternating group in that action. Merely containing a
three-cycle is insufficient.

Proof: each generator preserves every component and is even on it, so
one containment is immediate. A single edge generates its full `A3`.
Suppose the generated subgroup already contains `Alt(U)`, `|U|>=3`, and
an edge meets `U` but has new vertices.

If the edge has just one new vertex `z`, it supplies a cycle `(a b z)`
with `a,b in U`. Conjugation by `Alt(U)` is transitive on the unordered
pairs in `U`, including when `|U|=3`; taking inverses chooses either
orientation. Hence all cycles involving `z` and two old vertices occur,
and together with `Alt(U)` they generate `Alt(U union {z})`.

If the edge has two new vertices `x,y`, write its cycle as `(a x y)`.
Choose `b in U\{a}` and conjugate by an old alternating permutation
sending `a` to `b`, giving `(b x y)`. Direct composition gives

```text
(a x y)(b x y)^-1 = (a x b).
```

The preceding one-new-vertex case first adjoins `x`, then the original
cycle adjoins `y`. Grow a component along intersecting edges. The
resulting alternating subgroup acts only on that component. Applying
this independently to each component proves (1), including the direct
product statement. No classification of finite primitive groups, Jordan
theorem, or finite census is used in this proof.

## 3. Global normal generation and the actual boundary

Put `V=A2\S`. The proper-locus cover `F^-1(V) -> V` is connected:
its source is a nonempty Zariski-open subset of the irreducible complex
affine plane. Thus the global monodromy group acts transitively on six
letters. The smooth locus of the irreducible `S` is connected, so its
normal meridians are conjugate, up to changing positive orientation to
its inverse. Each has the single-three-cycle type supplied at the cusp.

These meridians normally generate `pi_1(V)`. To see this, contract a loop
in the simply connected ambient `A2`, perturb its bounding disk away
from the finite singular locus and transversely to `S_smooth`, and remove
small disks about the intersections. The punctured-disk relation expresses
the loop as a product of conjugated smooth meridians. Consequently its
monodromy image is generated by individual three-cycles. Formula (1)
and transitivity give

```text
G = A6.                                                   (2)
```

Let `A2 --j--> Xbar --pi--> A2` be the finite normal envelope. At the
generic point of `S`, a prime with ramification index `e` and residue
degree `f` contributes `f` cycles of length `e`. All ramified primes are
deleted, since `F` is etale. The inertia type `(3,1,1,1)` therefore forces
one ramified prime `D`, with `e_D=3,f_D=1`. Moreover the three fixed
labels are all actual, because `a=3`. There are no deleted unramified
primes left. Every boundary divisor maps onto `S`, and the boundary is
pure, so

```text
Xbar\j(A2)=D,
e_D=3,   f_D=1,   D^nu isomorphic to S^nu isomorphic to A1. (3)
```

The last isomorphism is the finite birational map between normal curves.
It is **not** an assertion that `D` itself is normal. The retained primes
can have residue-degree partitions `3`, `2+1`, or `1+1+1`; this argument
does not select one. Boundary primes and dicritical divisors correspond
under a resolved Stein factorization: a dicritical cannot be contracted
by its birational part, since the following finite map would then send
its image to a point. Conversely each boundary prime has its unique
strict transform. Thus (3) also gives one dicritical component, without
confusing irreducibility of the target curve with that stronger property.

There is more exact anatomy that needs no smoothness claim at the cusp.
At a node the two three-cycles commute. The actual fixed sets each have
size three and are disjoint, so the cycle supports are disjoint as well.
There are two three-sheet normal local components, with maps `(u,v) ->
(u^3,v)` after exchanging axes where necessary. Each has a single smooth
deleted branch, and the two points over the node are distinct and deleted.
At the cusp the local orbits have sizes five and one; only the singleton
point is retained. There is exactly one boundary point over the cusp.
Over every smooth point of `S` there is also just one boundary point.
It follows that `D^nu -> D` identifies no points, so it is a finite
bijective morphism and a homeomorphism in the complex topology. Hence

```text
chi_c(D)=1,   chi_c(Xbar)=2.                               (4)
```

The finite fibre cardinalities of `pi` are four at a smooth point of
`S`, two at a node, and two at the cusp. This also checks (4) directly:
`6N+4(-2N)+2N+2=2`. The five-sheet cusp point can still require a separate
analytic smoothness analysis; (3)–(4) do not supply it.

## 4. Divisor-class consequences

Localization of Weil divisor classes for the normal surface `Xbar` gives
that its class group is generated by `[D]`, since the complement is
factorial `A2`. There is no nonzero relation `n[D]=0`: a rational function
with divisor `nD` restricts to an invertible regular function on `A2`, by
normality and vanishing of every codimension-one valuation there. It is
therefore constant, and its divisor is zero. Thus

```text
Cl(Xbar)=Z[D].                                            (5)
```

The pullback of the target form `du wedge dv` is a nonzero rational
two-form on `Xbar`. It has no zero or pole on the retained affine plane,
and its order along `D` is `e_D-1=2` by characteristic-zero tame
ramification at the generic divisor. There are no other primes. As a
Weil canonical class,

```text
K_Xbar=2[D] in Cl(Xbar).                                  (6)
```

These statements do not assume `Xbar` is smooth, locally factorial, or
that `D` is Cartier. Replacing the Weil class group by a Picard group
would require that extra information. They are constraints on the actual
finite envelope, not evidence that such an affine surface and map exist.

## 5. A semilocal hostile and the literature boundary

```text

<sigma,tau>=A5 fixing 5,   <sigma,tau,eta>=A6,
sigma eta=eta sigma,
Fix(sigma)={1,2,5},   Fix(eta)={0,3,4}.
```

The first two permutations give the `(2,5)` cusp re-access word. The
third attaches the sixth label and supplies a commuting node pair with
zero actual intersection and zero missing overlap. Reusing that pair
for several formal nodes remains consistent with the Euler budget.
This is a simultaneous **semilocal** control, not a representation of
the complete fundamental group of any proposed algebraic curve. It
pinpoints the missing relations rather than asserting global realization.
