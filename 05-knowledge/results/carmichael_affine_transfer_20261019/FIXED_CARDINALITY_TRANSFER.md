## 1. Exact fixed-cardinality Fourier bound

Let g_1,...,g_n be labelled elements of a finite abelian group H. Labels may coincide as group elements. For 1<=k<n put

    rho=k/n,
    b_(n,k)=binom(n,k) rho^k (1-rho)^(n-k),
    Delta_chi=n-|sum_i chi(g_i)|.

Let B be a uniform k-subset of the labels and Y=product_(i in B) g_i. For every character chi,

    |E chi(Y)| <= b_(n,k)^(-1) exp(-rho(1-rho) Delta_chi).       (F1)

For the trivial character the right side may exceed one; the useful cases are nontrivial characters. If Delta_chi>=lambda for every nontrivial chi, then for every h in H,

    |P(Y=h)-1/|H||
      <= (|H|-1)/|H| * b_(n,k)^(-1) exp(-rho(1-rho)lambda).     (F2)

In particular, for |H|>1, if

    rho(1-rho)lambda >= log(2(|H|-1)/b_(n,k)),                 (F3)

then every h has at least binom(n,k)/(2|H|) preimages. The convenient stronger sufficient condition is

    rho(1-rho)lambda >= log(2|H|(n+1)).                       (F4)

When H is trivial all binom(n,k) subsets have the unique product, without a mixing condition.

Proof. Set z_i=chi(g_i) and

    F(theta)=product_i (1-rho+rho exp(i theta)z_i).

The coefficient of exp(ik theta) is b_(n,k) E chi(Y). For any theta,

    |F(theta)| <= exp[-rho(1-rho)
                         (n-Re(exp(i theta)sum_i z_i))]
               <= exp[-rho(1-rho)Delta_chi].

This uses exactly the modulus identity of paper equation (5.3), with a common phase retained rather than set to zero. Integrating the kth coefficient proves (F1); character orthogonality proves (F2). The binomial distribution with success parameter k/n has a mode at k, so b_(n,k)>=1/(n+1), proving (F4). No independence claim is made after conditioning. QED.

The identity

    n^2-|sum_i z_i|^2 = sum_(i<j)|z_i-z_j|^2                (F5)

shows that Delta is a pair-dispersion invariant. It vanishes exactly when all chi(g_i) coincide. Consequently the subgroup governing a fixed-size problem is generated by pair ratios g_i/g_j, not just by the g_i.

## 2. Affine pruning

Let P be N labelled elements of G, and D=max(1,ceil(log_2 |G|)). Put M=N/(2D). There are a subgroup H<=G, an element a in G, and a retained labelled subset T of size n>=N/2 contained in aH such that every nontrivial character chi of H has every value-fiber of size at most n-M on the normalized labels b_i=a^(-1)g_i.

Proof. Start with G and its identity coset. If a nontrivial chi is constant on all but fewer than M retained labels, retain that fiber. Choose a retained label as new representative; its fiber is a coset of ker chi. Replace the current subgroup by ker chi and remove the exceptional labels. Each strict step divides subgroup order by at least two. There are at most D steps and fewer than DM=N/2 deletions. On stopping the stated fiber condition holds. If the subgroup is trivial the condition is vacuous. QED.

This is the affine counterpart of paper Lemma 3.1. It prunes all poorly supported *constant phases*, whereas the paper prunes the phase 1.

## 3. Translated Fejer control and explicit constants

Let A be an ambient labelled family of Q elements of G containing P. Assume

    |sum_(g in A) xi(g)| <= A0                       (A*)

for every nontrivial character xi of G. Choose an integer R>=2 with

    R >= 64Q/N,          (R-1)A0 <= Q.              (R*)

For the affine-pruned T,a,H above, every nontrivial chi of H satisfies

    Delta_chi >= lambda,
    lambda = N/R^2 * min(2/D, 3/(4 pi^2)).          (F6)

Proof for low order. If chi has order r<R, distinct values have squared distance

    |z-w|^2 >= 4 sin^2(pi/r) >= 16/r^2 >= 16/R^2.

Every fiber has size <=n-M, hence the number of unequal unordered pairs is at least nM/2. Equation (F5), followed by n+|sum z_i|<=2n, gives

    Delta_chi >= 4M/R^2 = 2N/(D R^2).

For high order, extend chi from H to G. Its extension xi has order at least R. For any real phase phi, use the nonnegative translated Fejer polynomial

    F_R(exp(-i phi)xi(g))
      = 1+2 Re sum_(j=1)^(R-1)(1-j/R)exp(-ij phi)xi(g)^j.

All xi^j here are nontrivial, so its ambient sum is <=Q+(R-1)A0<=2Q. On the arc of angular radius 1/R about exp(i phi), F_R>=R/4, just as in the paper. Thus at most 8Q/R<=N/8 ambient labels lie in any such arc. Choose phi as the argument of sum_(T)xi(g), arbitrarily if that sum is zero. At least 3N/8 retained labels lie outside the arc. Their individual centered energy is >=2/(pi^2 R^2), giving

    Delta_chi >= 3N/(4 pi^2 R^2).

Taking the smaller branch proves (F6). QED.

The dependence on the extension order in the original proof is avoidable: one can split by ord(chi); the high-order extension automatically has at least that order. The explicit minimum is stronger than replacing both branches by N/(100DR^2). For D>=27 it is exactly 2N/(DR^2); no asymptotic theorem is changed merely by this constant improvement.

A broader weighted form survives. If nonnegative ambient weights w_g obey absolute Fourier bounds A_j for powers xi^j, then the right side of the Fejer sum is total weight plus 2 sum_(j<R)(1-j/R)A_j. Every translated arc bound follows with that quantity. Only positivity and upper bounds for these particular powers are needed, rather than a complete equidistribution theorem for T.

## 4. Ambient cancellation bounds the cardinality period

Suppose n>A0. Then the index d=[G:H] of the affine-pruned subgroup satisfies

    d <= (Q-A0)/(n-A0).                              (F7)

Proof. Orthogonality on G/H gives the exact ambient coset count

    # {g in A:g in aH}
      = (1/d) sum_(xi in H^perp) xi(a)^(-1)sum_(g in A)xi(g)
      <= Q/d+(d-1)A0/d.

The left side is at least n. Rearrangement proves (F7). QED.

Every k-subset of T has product in a^kH. The identity is therefore reachable only if a^k belongs to H. Its exact cardinality period h=ord(aH) divides d. Conversely, whenever h divides k and (F3) holds, at least binom(n,k)/(2|H|) retained k-subsets have product 1. More generally every target in a^kH has that lower bound. The statement is sufficient for attainability, not a claim that every compatible small k already attains every target.

This is the crucial repayment of the affine sidecar: ambient cancellation bounds the *quotient* cardinality obstruction, so pruning cannot hide an enormous forbidden-size period.

## 5. Conditional exact-size Carmichael compiler

Assume the supplied paper's analytic inputs (S),(A), with any fixed beta<1. Fix positive delta,sigma,eta satisfying

    delta<1/16,
    beta+2delta+2sigma<1,
    2delta+2sigma<eta<1-sigma.                       (F8)

Set P,A,N,Q,L,G as in paper (3.1), R=ceil(64Q/N), and D=max(1,ceil(log_2|G|)). Then for every sufficiently large real y there exist n>=N/2 retained primes, a subgroup H, a positive integer h=O(y^sigma), and an integer

    k=h floor(y^eta/h)=y^eta(1-o(1))                 (F9)

such that the number of Carmichael numbers formed from exactly k retained primes is at least

    binom(n,k)/(2|H|),                              (F10)

and all are at most X(y)=(5y)^(y^eta). In particular,

    liminf_(y->infinity) log C(X(y))/log X(y)
      >= 1-sigma-eta.                              (F11)

Proof. Retaining the logarithm in source (3.2), rather than absorbing it twice into y^sigma, gives D,log|G|=O(U0), where U0=y^delta log(5y). Also R<=325y^sigma and A0=y^(beta+2delta+sigma)=o(N). Hence (R*) and n>A0 hold. Equation (F7) gives h<=d<=3Q/N eventually, hence h=O(y^sigma). Because eta>sigma, k/y^eta tends to one. Also k/n tends to zero.

Equation (F6) implies lambda>=cN/(DR^2) for an absolute c>0. Thus

    (k/n)(1-k/n)lambda >= c' k/(DR^2)
                          >> y^(eta-delta-2sigma)/log y.

Its ratio to U0 tends to infinity because eta>2delta+2sigma. It therefore dominates log|H|+log(n+1)=O(U0), proving (F4). If H is trivial, (F10) is automatic. Since h divides k, the target identity belongs to a^kH.

For each identity-product subset B, m_B=product_(p in B)p is squarefree, composite for large y, and satisfies p-1|L|m_B-1 for every prime factor p. Korselt gives a Carmichael number. Distinct labelled prime subsets yield distinct integers by unique factorization. This is the paper's Proposition 5.2 compiler, with the fixed-size input replaced.

Finally binom(n,k)>=(n/k)^k, n>=y^(1-sigma)/2, and log|H|=O(U0) imply

    log C(X(y)) >= k[(1-sigma-eta)log y-log 2]-O(U0)-log 2.

Divide by y^eta log(5y), use k/y^eta->1 and U0=o(y^eta log y), and obtain (F11). The continuous increasing unbounded X(y) covers all sufficiently large X, as in source Section 6. QED.

The sharper scale condition eta>2delta+2sigma was supplied independently by the coordinating research lane and follows directly by retaining logarithms. The improvement over source (6.3) is the removal of the cardinality collar factor (1-nu)/(1+nu), the exact binomial entropy, and retention of sigma instead of a loose 2sigma. Letting admissible parameters shrink still yields the same exponent-one conclusion **conditional on (S),(A)**. It gives no new proof of those analytic inputs and no quantitative rate for their thresholds.

### Prescribing divisibility of the number of prime factors

For any fixed integer m>=1, replace (F9) by

    k=lcm(h,m) floor(y^eta/lcm(h,m)).

Then k/y^eta->1, and every counted Carmichael number has omega(m_B)=k divisible by m. The same conditional exponent-one conclusion holds for this subfamily. The inherited worker's independent G x C_m argument obtains this fixed-m consequence by a different route.

More generally m=m(y) may vary if m(y)=o(y^(eta-sigma)); then h m(y)=o(y^eta), so the same fixed-scale estimate and asymptotic bound hold with that size-divisibility requirement. This is a statement at the chosen scale y; it is not an unqualified global counting function for an arbitrary varying modulus.

### A quantitative supply-to-count tradeoff

The same proof gives an abstract scale law. Suppose N>=y^(alpha-o(1)), Q<=y^(q+o(1)), log|G|<=y^(d+o(1)), with q>=alpha>0 and d>=0. Assume the ambient error A0=o(N), and an injective arithmetic compiler sends each identity subset to the product of its selected atoms, each at most y^(kappa+o(1)), with kappa>0. For every fixed eta satisfying

    2d+2(q-alpha)<eta<alpha,

the exact-k construction has k=y^(eta+o(1)) and a counting exponent at least

    (alpha-eta)/kappa.

The quotient period has size at most y^(q-alpha+o(1)), so rounding does not change k at this scale. The proof is the same comparison k/(D R^2)>>log|G|+log N with R<=y^(q-alpha+o(1)), followed by binomial entropy. Taking eta down to its permitted infimum gives lower exponents arbitrarily close to

    (3alpha-2q-2d)/kappa,

when this quantity is positive. This is a conditional compiler law, not a new supply theorem. For the paper's scales q=kappa=1, d=delta and alpha=1-sigma it recovers the limiting available exponent 1-2delta-3sigma, approached from below. No assertion is made at the strict mixing boundary.

## 6. Limitations and decisive hostile tests

1. Without ambient control, h can be arbitrarily large: labels all equal a generator of C_q.
2. The group generated by labels is insufficient for fixed-size mixing: pair ratios are the correct affine carrier.
3. A large original energy E_chi does not imply Delta_chi>0.
4. Mod-p visibility does not imply a p-adic lift; the first-lift defect remains a separate membership gate.
5. Exact parity or a uniform terminal law does not establish the earlier nonlinear path or a prime-input model.
6. Fejer only transfers upper bounds from a positive ambient measure. Signed weights or arbitrary nonlinear subset statistics do not inherit the argument automatically.
