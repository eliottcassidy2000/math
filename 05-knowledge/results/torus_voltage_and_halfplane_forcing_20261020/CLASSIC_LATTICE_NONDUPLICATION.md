# Classic lattice nonduplication by finite-boundary counting

2026-10-20. **PROVED: classic elementary Følner/counting argument; no novelty
claim.** The finite controls below are checks of formulas, not the source of
the universal conclusion. This note proves only the two stated lattice-action
cases, not a universal plane-isometry or three-dimensional theorem.

## 1. Precise statement, with arbitrary pieces

Let X be either the square lattice Z² or the triangular lattice
{a+b exp(iπ/3): a,b∈Z}. There is no finite partition

    X = A_1 ⊔ ... ⊔ A_m

and assignment of each piece to one of two labelled copies of X such that its
image under an allowed motion partitions those two copies. The allowed motions
are translations for the square lattice, and all lattice-preserving
translations, rotations, and reflections for the triangular lattice.

Equivalently, divide the indices into I_0 and I_1 and require, for j=0,1,

    X = ⊔_{i∈I_j} g_i(A_i).

The pieces may be infinite, nonperiodic, and chosen completely arbitrarily.
There is no regularity, definability, or measurability hypothesis. On a
countable lattice every subset is measurable for counting measure, so calling
such lattice subsets literally “nonmeasurable” would be misleading. The point
is that the proof imposes no restriction whatsoever on the piece choices.

This is the familiar nonparadoxicality conclusion of the Følner boundary
argument. For its classical amenability context, see Wagon,
[*The Banach–Tarski Paradox*, Chapter 10](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/banachtarski-paradox/measures-in-groups/6F9AE5EC8448E16EEFBD9B9796F4FADF).
The proof here is self-contained.

## 2. The finite counting inequality

For any finite F⊂X, counting its two target copies gives

    2|F| = Σ_i |A_i ∩ g_i^(-1)(F)|.

For arbitrary subsets A and finite sets E,F,

    |A∩E| ≤ |A∩F| + |E\F|.

Sum this inequality over the disjoint pieces. Since they partition X,

    2|F| ≤ |F| + Σ_i |g_i^(-1)(F)\F|
          ≤ |F| + Σ_i |g_i^(-1)(F) Δ F|.                 (1)

Thus the proposed duplication would force the boundary-error sum to be at
least |F|. All quantities counted here are finite, regardless of the pieces.

## 3. Square lattice: exact box bounds

Take B_R={-R,...,R}² for an integer R≥0, and put L=2R+1. Then |B_R|=L².
For a translation vector t=(p,q), let x_+=max(x,0). Rectangle intersection
gives the exact one-sided defect

    |(B_R−t)\B_R| = L² − (L−|p|)_+(L−|q|)_+
                   ≤ L(|p|+|q|).                       (2)

For the inequality, if either displacement exceeds L, the right side is at
least L². Otherwise expand the product and drop the nonnegative |p||q| term.
The symmetric difference has exactly twice the one-sided cardinality,
because the two boxes have equal size.

Write S=Σ_i(|p_i|+|q_i|), a fixed finite integer. Combining (1) and (2),

    2L² ≤ L²+LS, hence L≤S.

Taking R=S makes L=2S+1>S, a contradiction. In particular, the argument
compares a quadratic interior with a linear boundary for a fixed finite list
of translations; it is not merely a finite-set cardinality objection.

An alternative explicit cutoff uses D=max_i max(|p_i|,|q_i|).
All preimages lie in B_(R+D), so disjointness of the original pieces gives
2|B_R|≤|B_(R+D)|. At R=3D this fails by the positive exact gap

    2(6D+1)² − (8D+1)² = 8D²+8D+1.

This includes D=0 and R=0.

## 4. Triangular lattice: rotation-invariant hexagonal boxes

Use coordinates (a,b) for a+b exp(iπ/3) and define

    ρ(a,b)=max(|a|,|b|,|a+b|),
    H_R={(a,b)∈Z²: ρ(a,b)≤R}.

The six unit steps are ±(1,0), ±(0,1), ±(1,−1). The hexagonal norm ρ is
subadditive directly from its formula.
For fixed b between −R and R there are 2R+1−|b| admissible values of a. Hence

    |H_R| = Σ_(b=−R)^R (2R+1−|b|) = 3R²+3R+1.          (3)

An isometry fixing the origin permutes the six nearest neighbours, so its
point-symmetry group is the order-twelve dihedral group D6, generated by

    rotation:   (a,b) ↦ (−b,a+b),
    reflection: (a,b) ↦ (a+b,−b).

Both permute the three absolute values defining ρ, so every symmetry fixes
H_R as a set. An allowed motion is g_i(x)=r_i x+t_i, with r_i∈D6 and t_i∈Z².
Consequently

    g_i^(-1)(H_R)=H_R−r_i^(-1)t_i,
    ρ(r_i^(-1)t_i)=ρ(t_i)=d_i.

Subadditivity puts this translated hexagon inside H_(R+d_i). Therefore its
one-sided boundary defect has the explicit bound

    |g_i^(-1)(H_R)\H_R|
      ≤ |H_(R+d_i)|−|H_R|
      = 6R d_i + 3d_i(d_i+1).                          (4)

Its symmetric difference is again twice the one-sided defect. For a fixed
finite list of motions, (1) and (4) would imply

    3R²+3R+1 ≤ 6R Σ_i d_i + 3Σ_i d_i(d_i+1).

The right side is linear in R and the left side quadratic, a contradiction
for sufficiently large R.

There is also a simple explicit cutoff independent of the number of pieces.
Put D=max_i d_i. Since every preimage lies in H_(R+D), disjointness gives
2|H_R|≤|H_(R+D)|. At R=3D, formula (3) instead gives

    2|H_(3D)|−|H_(4D)| = 6D²+6D+1 > 0.

Again D=0 is included. This completes the triangular-lattice proof.

## 5. Scope, quotient warning, and audit

The finite piece list makes every sum finite and bounds the translations
independently of R. The proof does not cover an unbounded family of motions,
and the proposed decomposition cannot vary with R. Nothing here restricts
the pieces to finite sets or to regular measurable regions. Conversely, this proof does not extend
the allowed motions to arbitrary plane isometries, prove a theorem about all
subsets of the plane, or settle a three-dimensional paradoxical decomposition.

The map q(a,b)=a+3b mod 7 from the [geometric packet](geometric_forcing_bridges.md)
identifies infinite period fibres to produce a seven-vertex K7 quotient. That
is a many-to-one identification, not a piecewise isometric bijection from one
lattice onto two labelled copies. Its chromatic increase therefore has no
interpretation as duplication. Keeping the period/voltage information repairs
the graph-lift question; it is a different issue from the boundary count here.

The proof was separately checked for the target-copy counting direction,
arbitrary-piece quantifier, translation sign, affine inverse, exact overlap,
D6 invariance, and the zero-displacement case. The independent finite checker
uses R=0,...,8 and translation coordinates −3,...,3: 441 square translation
cases, 441 hexagonal translation cases, and 5,292 hexagonal affine-isometry
cases. It also checks infinite-quadrant partition bookkeeping on the relevant
finite sets, and both explicit cutoff identities for D=0,...,20. Normal and
optimized Python outputs agree.
