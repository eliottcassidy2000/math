## 2. One matching is optimal at every cut

Fix an actual terminal u and a nonzero vector v, with the sign convention
v=max(v,-v) in lexicographic order. List the +v sources by
(arrival depth, source ID), and independently list the -v sources by the
same rule. Pair the kth sources for k<=min(total positive,total negative).
Different terminals and antipodal classes are treated separately.

**Chronological matching theorem.** If P_d and N_d are the counts of
arrived +v and -v sources, exactly min(P_d,N_d) of these pairs have
matured by d. No disjoint pairing of opposite sources can have more
mature pairs at d. Thus one fixed source pairing achieves the maximum
at every cut, not merely at the final cut.

Proof: any mature pair consumes one source from each arrived class,
so min(P_d,N_d) is an upper bound. The first min(P_d,N_d) sources of
both ordered lists are already present, so those rank-pairs attain it.
Ties are deterministic but do not change the cut counts. Pairing by
source ID can instead match an early source with a late partner while
both signs are already available from different pairs.

The same matching can be executed causally: keep a FIFO queue for each
sign in each terminal/axis class and match a new arrival with the oldest
waiting opposite source. A credit is emitted only when that second
member actually arrives. No future arrival or convergence oracle is
needed for the finite-cut matching theorem.

For a pair arriving at a<b, its exposed vector is the first member's
signed vector on[a,b), and zero outside that interval. Equal-depth
pairs have identically zero exposure. This interval retains the clock
sign; it is not inferred from the unsigned charge or a prime register.
Within fixed paired supports, rank matching also minimizes the number
of exposed pairs at every cut, because that number is the number of
arrived paired members minus twice the number of mature pairs.

The theorem optimizes only disjoint **opposite pairs of equal vectors**.
For example (1,0),(0,1),(-1,-1) sum to zero with no opposite pair. No
claim about maximal general zero packing, energy minimization or an
unbounded source family follows.

## 3. Exact residual credit, with each source spent once

At cut d, erase only the members of mature chronological pairs from the
arrived-new source list. Keep the original source IDs of everything
else. Let Z_d be the sum of norms of the erased members and R_d the sum
of norms of the retained members. For each terminal and axis, the
retained arrived members have only one sign. Therefore

    Z_d=sum_(u,axis v) 2 Q(v) min(P_(u,v,d),N_(u,v,d)),
    R_d=sum_(u,axis v) Q(v) |P_(u,v,d)-N_(u,v,d)|,
    A_d=R_d+Z_d.                                               (1)

Here A_d is the full arrived-new source norm. Let H_u be its unchanged
terminal vector and W_u the actual old arrived vector. Put

    C_d=sum_u[Q(H_u)-R_(u,d)+beta(W_u,H_u)].

The inherited owned interaction and full bank become

    I_d=C_d-Z_d,
    Delta E=A_d+I_d,
    Delta E<=153N  iff  C_d<=M_d+Z_d,   M_d=153N-A_d.            (2)

Proof: a mature opposite pair has zero vector, so removing it leaves H
unchanged. Its two source diagonals sum to2Q(v); subtracting arrived
source norms therefore changes the coherence by exactly that amount.
Equation(1) proves(2) by substitution. This is a source-level normal
form of the inherited criterion, not a new bound on C_d.
In particular, replacing another matching by the chronological one
increases the legally matured Z_d, but decreases the retained source
norm by the same amount and therefore increases C_d equally. The true
interaction I_d and the available full bank are unchanged. The optimum
is legal temporal reclamation, not an optimization of the actual energy.

The term Z_d is exactly the negative within-pair interaction already
owned by those two sources. It is **not** an additional copy of their
norms. A pair contributes only at its later arrival event; all pair
members are disjoint. Cross terms between distinct pairs, between
exposed intervals, with unpaired sources and with the old core remain
in C_d. Nothing is deleted from the old baseline. No earlier cut's
credit is added a second time at a later cut or scale.

## 4. What a signed common-core decomposition must prove

Partition new source labels into disjoint blocks, with arrived vectors
H_(b,u). For the same old core W, polarization gives

    Delta E=sum_b sum_u[Q(H_(b,u))+beta(W_u,H_(b,u))]
            +sum_(b<c) sum_u beta(H_(b,u),H_(c,u)).              (3)

Thus local block budgets compose if their sum is within the original
source bank **and the retained inter-block interaction is nonpositive**.
More generally that interaction needs its own uniquely owned budget.
Sharing the old core is legal in the bilinear term, since beta is
additive; charging its squared norm separately to every block is not.
Set-disjoint petals need not be orthogonal vectors.

A sharp scalar guard illustrates both sides. Suppose the fixed old core
in one terminal is -a v, a>=0, and r arrived petals each contribute v.
Their increment minus their arrived source norm is

    Q(v) r(r-2a-1).                                           (4)

For r>0 the arrived-budget condition holds exactly when r<=2a+1.
If the total petal count is at most2a+1 and the same old core is present
throughout, it holds at every petal arrival cut; additional outside
interactions still require(3). For a=1, every singleton has increment
-Q(v), but four petals together have increment8Q(v), above their
combined source norm4Q(v). Counting the four singleton benefits loses
the six petal-petal cross terms.

Even blocks that individually pay at **every** cut need not compose.
Each opposite pair has exposed energy at most its arrived source norm.
Three zero pairs exposing the same vector v simultaneously have energy
9Q(v), exceeding their entire six-source norm6Q(v). This is sharp for
this equal-exposure pattern: one pair passes, two meet the full budget,
and three fail. The completed packet is still zero.
