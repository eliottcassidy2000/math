## 1. Independent geometry proof

Over a field of characteristic other than2 containing i with i²=−1, the
specified linear change of coordinates is invertible: its denominators have
product2 and its determinant is nonzero. It sends each square equation to
u_jv_j=c². This is a scheme isomorphism, not merely a birational map.

The kernel of the Laurent-monomial map c→T, u_j→TX_j,
v_j→TX_j⁻¹ is exactly the ideal generated by u_jv_j−c². Indeed repeated
replacement of opposite pairs gives one monomial for each exponent vector
(n,m) with n≥Σ|m_j|. These images are distinct Laurent monomials, so there
can be no additional linear relation among normal forms. This proves the
semigroup identification in every characteristic for the *binomial* model.

Normality can be checked without importing an unspecified theorem. The
semigroup is the intersection of the lattice halfspaces
n−σ·m≥0, σ∈{−1,1}^r. Each corresponding halfspace semigroup ring is a
polynomial ring in one variable over an r-variable Laurent polynomial ring:
the defining linear functional is primitive, its coefficient on n being1.
It is integrally closed. Their intersection in the common Laurent fraction
field is precisely the stated semigroup algebra and is integrally closed.
Thus its Proj is normal. Its open c≠0 is exactly the r-torus; the boundary
support strata and their dimensions follow directly from u_jv_j=0.

For the original form over an odd finite field, normalization c=1 gives r
independent copies of a²+b²=2, each with q−χ(−1) solutions. If χ(−1)=−1,
the boundary c=0 has no rational points. If χ(−1)=1, the signed support
strata give Σ_{s=1}^r 2^s binom(r,s)(q−1)^{s−1} points. This proves the
whole formula, with no finite-enumeration extrapolation. The split and
nonsplit polynomials differ at coefficient q^{r−1} by2^r−2r, positive for
r≥3; infinite even/odd extension subsequences prohibit a common polynomial.
At r=1,2 direct expansion gives q+1 and(q+1)² respectively.

**Characteristic2 is a genuine scheme boundary, not an inconvenience.** The
original equations become(a_j+b_j)²=0. Their reduced support is P^r, with
1+q+⋯+q^r rational points, and the original scheme is nonreduced. In
particular the r=2 odd-field polynomial fails at q=2. This does not contradict
the integral binomial model: the comparison required inverting2 and adjoining i.

## 2. The singular-point obstruction is global, not merely coordinate-specific

In the chart u_1=1, the r=2 binomial model has local equation u_2v_2=c².
In odd characteristic its sole singularity in that chart is the origin.
The four noncentral coordinate charts give exactly four ordinary double
points. Conjugation exchanges u_j and v_j, so the four geometric singular
points occur in two conjugate pairs over Q and over every nonsplit F_q.
There are no rational singular points for those forms.

Suppose the Q-form admitted *any* normal split toric structure. Its finite
geometric singular locus is intrinsic and stable under the torus. A connected
torus cannot nontrivially permute a finite set, so all four points would be
fixed. A zero-dimensional orbit of a split toric variety is Spec Q (the
maximal-cone chart has its canonical rational fixed point). Therefore all
four singularities would be rational: contradiction. This is a valid global
obstruction to a split toric structure, not a claim that no torification or
no blue model exists. The same argument works over a nonsplit finite field.

The separate open and boundary extension-count formulas in the producer
draft sum correctly. Their χ(−1)-dependent zeta factors cancel, while the
marked singular subscheme retains zeta(1−T²)⁻² rather than(1−T)⁻⁴. Thus the
example really does exhibit nonisomorphic forms with identical total counts
over every finite extension. No reconstruction of geometry from zeta is used.

## 3. Interior branching obstructs the actual finite toric maps

At(c,a,b,d,e)=(1,0,√2,1,1), the radicands F and J vanish, whereas G and K
are2. The point belongs to the specified torus c≠0, even though the original
coordinate a vanishes. The tangent vector(δa,δb,δd,δe)=(0,0,1,−1) respects
both base equations and gives δF=2, δJ=−2. These are simple branch divisors
on the smooth torus. Adjoining the other unit square root does not remove
ramification; at every lift the relative differential of the vanishing root
is nonzero. The combined nine-root cover is likewise nonétale there.

For a finite dominant map of normal toric varieties, the real lattice map is
injective and the preimage of the zero cone is the zero cone. Consequently
the preimage of the target torus is the source torus. In characteristic zero
the finite-index lattice map induces an étale torus isogeny. This contradicts
the exhibited interior branch. The theorem is valid **relative to the
specified target torus and the actual coordinate-forgetting maps**. It does
not rule out a different torification, a different cover, or broad F1 descent.

## 4. Blue presentations: exact algebra, deliberately weak descent content

The pushout statement follows directly from assignments of generators:
assigning all nine generators subject to all six additive relations is the
same as assigning each hourglass while agreeing on the five shared ones.
Ringification gives the stated ordinary quotient rings and their tensor
product. This establishes affine-cone algebra gluing; no theorem about blue
Proj or affine-blue globalization is needed or approved.

For maps into the pointed initial-monoid blueprint, zero terms disappear
but multiplicities of1 remain ordinary integers. The base relations become
A+B=2C=D+E for0/1 values, forcing all five values to be0 or all to be1.
Each added root relation then forces the same value for its generator.
Thus exactly two assignments exist for each blueprint, and exactly one is
nonzero. This computation must not be replaced by Boolean/idempotent addition.
The independent checker exhausts all assignments, including all512 for M.

The toric model's literal monoid Hom-point count3^r and rank-zero count2r
also have distinct definitions: there is one monoid map for each face/orbit,

the one nonzero additive-blue assignment is legitimate only as a comparison

## 5. Graph seam theorem over every abelian group

Spanning-forest propagation uses only addition and inverse, not division.
It therefore supplies an integral natural isomorphism C_G(A)≅A^{|V|−c(G)}
for arbitrary abelian A, including torsion groups. Loops have label0 and
parallel edges retain their indexing. Fundamental cycle equations are both
necessary and sufficient by direct propagation.

For connected H1,H2 with intersection K, include *all shared vertices* when
counting k=c(K). Local potentials differ by one constant on each component
of K. Their ambiguity is a simultaneous constant. The resulting homomorphism
to A^k/diag(A) is well defined; its kernel is exactly global potential edge
vectors. Every class is realizable by potentials constant on each overlap
component and zero elsewhere. After fixing the representative with one
component0, that construction is a group-homomorphic section (change its
sign if the obstruction is defined in the opposite order). This proves the
split exact sequence over arbitrary groups, not just dimensions over fields.

The structural missing-condition rank is k−1. For finite nontrivial A every
obstruction class has the same number of preimages, proving the stated
fraction. This does not make square-constrained samples uniform. An
intersection consisting of one edge and four isolated shared vertices has
k=5, not1; the K3,3 two-Hamilton-path example therefore needs four cross-cycle
conditions. The independent finite tests use two three-edge stars sharing
three isolated leaves, recovering two independent obstructions.

## 6. Interpolation and magic scheme checks over arbitrary rings

The universal formulas u⊕v=u+v+tuv and ⊖u=−u/(1+tu) satisfy all group laws
as identities in the displayed localized polynomial rings. G_t is an open
subscheme of affine1-space over Z[t], hence smooth and flat; its products
are likewise smooth and flat. Forest propagation makes the graph subgroup
isomorphic to such a product *as a functor on all rings*, proving scheme and
base-change statements, including nonreduced rings and characteristic2.

For a signed cycle, let P_+ and P_- be the products of1+tz_e along positive
and negative edges. The group-word coordinate is

    ((P_+−P_-)/t) / P_-.

The inner quotient is a universal integer polynomial, not cancellation by t
in the evaluated ring; the outer denominator is a unit. Thus the divided
polynomial is exactly the group relation over every ring. Fundamental cycles
suffice; the quotient is the tree-coordinate localization, so t is a
nonzerodivisor and the claimed t-saturated closure statement is justified.
Only coherent bipartite orientations justify an alternating-even/odd notation;
the draft correctly uses positive/negative signed edge sets in general.

**Hostile beyond t=0:** in Z/4Z with t=2 and cycle labels(2,0,0,0), all1+tz
are units and both raw products equal1. The divided cycle expression is2,
not0. Thus the un-divided equation fails even at nonzero t when t is a zero
divisor. This strengthens the draft's essential arbitrary-ring warning.

For ordinary magic arrays, I independently generated all eight line
incidences and formed the seven differences against the top row. Their
Smith normal form is diag(1,1,1,1,1,1,0). More directly, using matrix row indices(0,1,2,3,5,6), corresponding to rows2,3,
columns1,2 and diagonals1,2, the six-by-six minor on cell
indices(1,3,5,6,7,8) has determinant1. The complementary free coordinates
are top-left, top-right, center. Solving these six relations integrally gives
exactly the draft's normal form; the seventh relation then vanishes
identically. This proves the three-parameter kernel over every ring/group,
without incorrectly dividing by2 or3. Applying that same integral group
identity to G_m and G_t proves the monomial torus and flat family claims.
The phrase “magic plane” here means a three-dimensional linear carrier,
not a two-dimensional affine scheme.

## 7. Ordinary-square pullback and gluing: what flatness does preserve

For any coefficient ring R, successive monic quotients r_i²−z_i yield a
free R-module with the512 squarefree root monomials as basis. This does not
require reducedness, a domain, characteristic≠2, nonzero entries, or generic
independence of the square classes. Composing with the already-flat carrier
therefore gives the claimed Z[t]-flat total scheme. The five-root base has
rank32, either two-root extension rank4, and their fiber product rank16 over
that base; all statements follow from tensoring the disjoint monic equations.
At t=0 this recovers the *affine-cone* Session58 root construction. No
projective family during the deformation is needed or supplied here.

At invertible t the equations concern products of1+tr_i², with those factors
inverted. They are not multiplicative-square magic equations in r_i².
Ordinary squaring is also not group doubling2r+tr². Retaining these facts is
essential to the approval. The family preserves equations and finite-free
covers, not squarehood under a logarithm, étaleness in characteristic2, or
rational distinct-positive sections between fibers.
