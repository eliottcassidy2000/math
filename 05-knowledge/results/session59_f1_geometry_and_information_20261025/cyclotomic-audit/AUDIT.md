## 1. Noninvertible power dynamics

On a fixed nonempty support, normalization makes the first present exponent
zero. A pure power map preserves that anchor, so the remaining exponents
really evolve by multiplication by `r` in `Z/m`, without requiring `r` to be
a unit. Consequently the equality of the `(t+n)`th and `t`th iterates is
exactly annihilation by `r^t(r^n-1)` in each relative coordinate. A cyclic
group has `gcd(m,a)` solutions to `a*x=0`, including `a=0`.

For any finite functional graph, equality of those iterates is equivalent to
tail at most `t` and eventual cycle length dividing `n`. Subtraction at
successive `t` isolates the exact tail; divisor Möbius inversion isolates the
exact cycle length. No Burnside argument is applied to a noninvertible map.
Division by `n` occurs only for periodic points (`t=0`), where it counts
actual cycles.

The theorem assumes `r≥1`, so its exponent `r^0` cannot cause an ambiguous
absorbing-zero expression. The `r=1` boundary gives `gcd(m,0)=m`, as required.
The fixed-point theorem separately handles `r=0` using `F_0(0)=0` and maps
all units to the identity unit. Absorbing zero is not the residue exponent 0.

The Jordan-divisor orbit formula also checks out: vectors generating the
unique order-`d` subgroup number `J_h(d)`; their stabilizer is the kernel of
the surjective map `U_m→U_d`; orbit size is `phi(d)`. This does not assert
that `U_m` acts freely. The `h=0` and `m=1` conventions are consistent.

## 2. Semilinear cycles and the projective quotient

With `sigma(i_j)=i_{j+1}`, equation (7) gives the recurrence

    a_{i_j}=b_{i_j}+r*a_{i_{j-1}}-t.

After the full cycle, its return equation is precisely

    (r^ell-1)*a_{i_0}=A_C*t-B_C mod m.

There are `g_C` initial values if the indicated divisibility holds, otherwise
none. All other values are then obtained **forward** by recurrence. No
inverse of `r` is used. This also proves independence of the choice of
cycle start, since each start counts the same complete cycle solutions.

Every fixed projective class on a nonempty support has exactly `m` distinct
homogeneous representatives. Each representative has exactly one scalar
`t`: equality at any present coordinate determines it. Thus there are
exactly `m` pairs `(a,t)` per fixed class. The shift is

    (a,t) -> (a+c, t+(r-1)*c).

This action is free on the **pair**, because it is already free on `a`.
It need not be free on `t`. In fact, each attained scalar occurs for
`gcd(m,r-1)` representatives and there are `m/gcd(m,r-1)` attained scalars.
Confusing these two actions would be a genuine error; the packet does not.
Hence dividing the total count of lifted pairs by `m` is valid even for a
nonunit `r`.

At `r=0`, each `g_C=1`, and each indicator is 1: formula (6) reduces to
`2^{number of cycles}-1`. In the polynomial `A_C`, the constant term 1
remains 1 on evaluation at zero. This is not exponentiation of monoid zero.
The original verifier implements these polynomials by Horner recurrence.
The audit computes explicit coefficient lists starting with 1, so neither
implementation needs to evaluate an ambiguous monoid `0^0`.

The composition law, quotient by constant diagonal twists, and invertible
semidirect-product action have the correct permutation orientation. Their
use of a group/Burnside argument is restricted to invertible actions.

## 3. Descent corollary

Write `M=q^d-1` and `L=M/(q-1)`. Since `sigma^d=id`, the map's `d`th iterate
is identity on raw vectors: the exponent `q^d` acts as 1 on every unit,
and zero stays zero. If `T(v)=lambda*v`, semilinearity gives
`lambda^L=1`. In cyclic exponents this says `(q-1)` divides the exponent of
`lambda`, which is exactly membership in the image of `c→c^{q-1}`.
Thus rescaling to a genuinely fixed vector is justified explicitly, not by
an unstated field theorem.

A permutation-cycle length `ell` divides `d`; its fixed vector coordinate
choices number `1+gcd(q^d-1,q^ell-1)=q^ell`. Their product is `q^k`. Exactly
`q-1` scalars take any genuinely fixed nonzero vector to another one, because
they satisfy `c^{q-1}=1`. Removing zero and dividing gives the stated count.

Nothing here requires `q` prime or a prime power. The separate assertion
that this is finite-field Frobenius when `q` is a prime power is correct.
The hypothesis on the permutation is material: `q=3,d=1,k=2,sigma=(12)`
has only two fixed points rather than four. This negative control is tested.

## 4. Pointed-monoid Hom convention

The packet consistently works with pointed monoids, zero-preserving maps,
and prime ideals containing zero. `Spec(C_m union {0})` then has exactly
the prime `{0}`. Its map to projective space lands in a chart. A chart map
is determined by independent ratio images; changing charts identifies
exactly proportional nonzero vectors. The homogeneous-vector quotient is
therefore a genuine point-set description, not merely a numerology claim.

For a target over `A_n` and no chosen base map, a chart map also has a
coefficient homomorphism `C_n→C_m`. Its generator image has exponent `a`
with `n*a=0 mod m`; there are `gcd(n,m)` choices. These choices are retained
under chart gluing and independent of the ratio choices. This proves the
extra coefficient factor. For base-preserving maps, that coefficient map
is fixed, so there is no extra factor. No arithmetic-additive structure
or incidence is being silently transported.

Primary-source checks on 2026-10-25 confirm that the frame/count background
and monomial symmetries are already in [Thas, §§4.3–4.4](https://backoffice.biblio.ugent.be/download/8589084/8603796),
while [Deitmar, §1](https://arxiv.org/html/math/0404185v7) uses the unpointed
convention with trivial monoid `{1}`. The packet's explicit separation of
these conventions is appropriate. No novelty or completeness-of-literature
claim has been independently established by this audit.
