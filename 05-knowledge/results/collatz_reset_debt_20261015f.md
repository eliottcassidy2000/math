## 3. Reroute A: discounted debt, including the identity stages

Let `rho=7/10`, `m=k+8`. P2 gives, for the same native maps,

\[
 Q(B_m)\le\rho^m Q(B_0)+
 (1-\rho)\sum_{j=1}^m\rho^{m-j}Q(R_j).                 \tag{2}
\]

It is sufficient to bound the **sum** in (2); no individual `7X` premise
is needed. If

\[
 (1-\rho)\sum_{j=1}^m\rho^{m-j}Q(R_j)
                  \le7X(1-\rho^m),                     \tag{3}
\]

the inherited `Q(B_0)<=8X^(3/2)`, `rho^2<1/2` and `8rho^8<1/2`
give `Q(B_m)<7.5X`. This is a sufficient implication at every scale,
not a proof of (3) at every scale.

The reanalysis rounds each square root **up to an integer** and performs
the entire convolution rationally. Identity padding uses the actual final
row norm as its residual: its cost is not zero. For every `k=8..23`, (3)
holds with these upward-rounded inputs. At the failing scale `k=23`,
after 15 reset stages and 16 identities, the rigorous upper bound is

\[
 Q(B_{31})\le
 \frac{119632466829136671710074741291377299093}
      {2000000000000000000000000000000}
       <7.131X<7.5X.                                   \tag{4}
\]

The early zero residuals and the later sub-seven residuals pay for the
three excessive intermediate stages. Universal (3) is the cleanest
surviving quantitative target for this unchanged controller.

## 4. A weighted lower-tail formulation of what must be bounded

For a nonzero old row put `h_I=(u_I-v_I)/u_I`, its fraction of cancelled
mass, and retain the finite positive measure

\[
 \nu=\sum_I\frac{u_I^2}{b}\,\delta_{h_I},\qquad
 \delta=1-\rho.
\]

Zero old rows contribute zero. P1 is exactly the identity

\[
 Q(R)^2=\int(1-h/\delta)_+^2\,d\nu(h)
   =\frac2{\delta^2}\int_0^\delta(\delta-s)\nu([0,s])\,ds. \tag{6}
\]

The second equality follows by integrating the quadratic kernel from each
atom's location to `delta`. It retains the original source weighting.
An unweighted count of weakly cancelling rows or unresolved terminals does
not give (6).

For example, a sufficient estimate is

\[
 \nu([0,s])\le CX^2(s/\delta)^\alpha\quad(0\le s\le\delta),
 \qquad\alpha\ge0,
\]

which yields

\[
       Q(R)^2\le\frac{2C}{(\alpha+1)(\alpha+2)}X^2.       \tag{7}
\]

For `7X`, it suffices that `2C<=49(alpha+1)(alpha+2)`. On the refuted
single stages such a strong estimate cannot hold. It is now a precise
candidate for **paired** maps, or a time-weighted version of (6) for (3).
The frozen output includes exact profiles at cancellation fractions
`1/10,1/5,1/4,3/10`; no independence or random-sign assumption is used.

## 5. Conductor moments cannot absorb a finite signed source packet for free

Let `mu=sum_{n in S} a_n delta_n` be a nonzero finite atomic measure on
distinct integer sources, with complex coefficients. Set
`A=sum|a_n|`, `B=sum|a_n|^2`. For

\[
 q\ge q_0:=\max(1,2+\max_{n\ne m\in S}v_3(n-m)),
\]

with `q_0=1` for a singleton, its primitive Fourier coefficients satisfy

\[
 \sum_{\substack{a\bmod3^q\\3\nmid a}}
       |\widehat\mu(a/3^q)|^2=2\,3^{q-1}B.              \tag{8}
\]

**Proof.** Expand the square. A diagonal term contributes `2*3^(q-1)`.
Every off-diagonal Ramanujan sum vanishes because
`v3(n-m)<q-1`. QED.

Since each coefficient has magnitude at most `A`,

\[
 \sum_{\operatorname{cond}(\xi)=q}|\widehat\mu(\xi)|
                      \ge 2\,3^{q-1}B/A.               \tag{9}
\]

Consequently its weighted Wiener norm, and every nonnegative conductor
moment of that norm, diverges for `R>=1/3`. Conversely the norm converges
for `0<R<1/3` by the upper bound `A` on each coefficient. This is the
sharp threshold for a nonzero finite atomic packet. It applies equally
after subtracting its Haar mean, which changes only the zero coefficient.

Signed cancellation and vanishing ordinary moments do not repair this:
`delta_0-2delta_1+delta_2` is an explicit hostile. The result applies to
the finite nonzero annular source rows as well as to point atoms. At the
critical operator radius `R_c>=1` in W2, the mismatch is therefore
structural. The conductor theorem remains valid for its regular inputs;
an approximation of a marked atomic input must pay its diverging price.

This does not rule out a signed, weaker, or source-dependent norm. It rules
out the particular attempted inference from the existing absolute
conductor-moment estimate. A replacement must exploit actual phase/fibre
relations, not only the marginal conductor histogram. For example, signed
masses `+5,-5` at a common terminal cancel; the same masses at two distinct
terminals do not. Their clock/depth marginals can be identical. This is
an abstract hostile to a proposed marginal-only proof, not a claim that
either arbitrary transport is realized by the fixed Collatz controller.

The common principle is operational: measure a defect **after the allowed

problems. In particular, a contraction of a regular Fourier density, a
successful restricted palette method, and a small integrated polynomial
have different consumers.

itself a bound: it can conceal increasing weighted obligations. Here the
number seven is a proposed budget, and grouping two operations changes
the payment without altering their actual clocks. No new identification
with finite-field group orders is being asserted.

The next substantive targets are: an all-scale discounted estimate (3);
an independently proved lower-tail estimate for paired maps; and a
