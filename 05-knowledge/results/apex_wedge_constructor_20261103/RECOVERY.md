## Typed bridges

1. **Two apex words -> edge difference.** Map by contracting their shared
   edge. Preserves the exact signed change in H. D alone loses the absolute
   counts and word identity. Retain both words; C1--C3 recover them from
   continuation observations. Cheapest test: independent subset DP at d=1.

2. **Word pairs -> exterior pairing.** Map (w,u) to (1,w) wedge (1,u).
   Preserves D/2. Passing to a Smith class loses Boolean coordinates and
   named ports. Retain the original coordinate basis and source hyperplane.
   Cheapest test: the nonzero strong rectangle curl.

3. **Source hyperplane -> paired groups.** Restrict B_m to the zero-source
   lattice. Preserves the integral alternating form on that sublattice.
   It deletes the linear boundary response. Retain the radical's source
   height d and the embedding; its height gives both torsion and center
   denominator. Cheapest test: m=6 yields paired order-two factors and a
   half-integral center. No Selmer realization is inferred.

4. **Literal runs -> tensor recurrence.** Split the gap automaton into a
   left Krylov state and right Krylov state. Preserves H at every run length.
   A scalar sequence loses the actual word. Retain the template and r;
   then ports and the native clock are explicit. Cheapest test: the
   exceptional first terms, which a tail-only formula would miss.

5. **Exact response -> residue controller.** Reduce C1 modulo a stated
   modulus. Preserves all future edge-jump residues. Loses unbounded source
   reconstruction; the residue observer is nevertheless invertible on its
   six coordinates over every modulus. Cheapest test: the unit Hankel
   determinant, not a sample prime.

## Research move for reuse

When a scalar count has a hard inverse, take a physical edge derivative
and ask whether two independent word charges can cancel. This succeeded
here because contraction leaves a geometric separation kernel. It produces
both an exact constructor and a smaller observable recurrence. It is not
a general route when the edge derivative still depends on uncontrolled
global subgraphs. We retain this as a local research move rather than adding
a META-PATTERNS entry without independent-thread evidence.
