# The integral pairing behind an apex edge reversal

**PROVED scoped.** G1--G5 describe the exact edge-jump response of A1 in
[THEORY.md](THEORY.md). Integral changes of basis classify this response
lattice; they need not send Boolean source words to Boolean source words.

## G1 A source coordinate turns the response into a wedge pairing

Set x=(1,w), y=(1,u), c=K^T 1+e_0+e_(m-1), and
\[
 B_m=\frac12\begin{pmatrix}0&c^T\\-c&K^T-K\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad S_m=(K^T-K)/2.
\]
Then
\[
 D(w,u)=2x^T B_m y.                                      \tag{G1}
\]
For m=0 set B_0=[0]. All entries are integral. Explicitly, the source row
is b_j=c_j/2=2^max(0,m-2-j), and
\[
 (S_m)_{ij}=\begin{cases}2^{j-i-2}&j>=i+2,\\0&j=i+1,\end{cases}
\]
above the diagonal; below it use antisymmetry. Expansion of A2 proves G1.
The response therefore factors through the exterior product x wedge y,
rather than through an ordinary scalar potential.

## G2 An exact integral peel at every size

Write J=[[0,1],[-1,0]]. For every m>=2 there is an explicitly supplied
unimodular congruence
\[
 B_m\simeq J\oplus B_{m-2},\qquad B_1=J,\quad B_0=[0].       \tag{G2}
\]
Here is the change of basis. Let p be the source coordinate and q the last
bit coordinate; B(p,q)=1. Replace every other coordinate vector v by
\[
 v'=v+B(q,v)p-B(p,v)q .
\]
The new vectors are orthogonal to p,q. Order them after p,q, placing the
last residual vector first. Substitution in the explicit entries of G1
gives B_(m-2) on that residual block. Adding integral multiples of p,q and
permuting a basis is unimodular. This proves G2 without a determinant guess.

For odd m, B_m is unimodular symplectic. For m=2l its radical is primitive
and generated by
\[
 g=(l-1;\ -1,1,\ 0,2,\ 1,3,\ldots,l-2,l).                 \tag{G3}
\]
The bit-coordinate pair at positions 2j,2j+1 is (j-1,j+1).
Direct substitution gives B_m g=0; the entry -1 makes g primitive.
Thus the full response lattice has no finite cokernel torsion.

## G3 Complete interaction cokernels after deleting the source coordinate

For odd m,
\[
 \operatorname{SNF}(S_m)=\operatorname{diag}(1^{m-1},0).
\]
For m=2, S_m=0. For even m=2l>=4,
\[
 \operatorname{SNF}(S_m)
   =\operatorname{diag}(1^{m-2},l-1,l-1),\qquad
 \operatorname{coker}S_m\cong(\mathbb Z/(l-1))^2.            \tag{G4}
\]

**Proof.** Let lambda be the primitive functional selecting the source
coordinate. S_m is the restriction of B_m to ker lambda.

When m is odd, the unimodular symplectic identification sends lambda to a
primitive vector v. Extend v to a symplectic basis (choose a vector pairing
to 1, and split that J block). On v-perp the remaining pairs are unimodular,
and v supplies one radical coordinate. This proves the odd case.

When m=2l, G2 splits the lattice as a unimodular symplectic summand W and
the primitive radical Zg. Put d=lambda(g)=l-1. The first peeled pair retains
p with lambda(p)=1, so lambda restricted to W is primitive. An integral
symplectic basis change in W makes it the coefficient of p_1. For d>0,
ker lambda has basis
\[
 q_1,\quad d p_1-g,\quad p_2,q_2,\ldots,p_l,q_l.
\]
Its alternating form is dJ, up to sign, plus l-1 unit J blocks. This proves
G4 including the paired discriminant form, not just its order. For d=0 the
first two coordinates are radical, giving S_2=0.

Consequently every cyclic paired group H_d=(Z/d)^2 occurs as this interaction
cokernel at base length m=2d+2, for the Boolean source family of order 2d+4.
It can also be measured entirely inside a strong family: replace each variable
word w by 1w0. The mixed rectangle derivative of D/2 on the interior bits
is exactly S_m, since its entries depend only on separation. These strong
sources have order 2d+6. Thus the coefficients come from actual graph counts,
not from treating an arbitrary integer weight as a Boolean edge.

At d=5,11,13 the variable lengths are 12,24,28. The group belongs to this
family's integer response lattice; it is not attached to one tournament as
its automorphism group or an arithmetic Selmer group. This is a concrete
new instance of the alternating reduction
used in the prior [two-port classification](../overnight_research_20261029/TWO_PORTS.md).

## G4 Oddness decides whether the affine source term has a center

The expression D/2 is b^T(u-w)+w^T S_m u. Translating both word coordinates
by a rational vector a removes the linear term exactly when S_m a=b.

For even m=2l>=4, G3 gives the unique solution
\[
 a=g_{\rm bits}/(l-1).
\]
Then w=a+v,u=a+z gives D/2=v^T S_m z. For m=6 the center is
(-1/2,1/2,0,1,1/2,3/2), an actual half-integral shift of the response grid.
This is an affine coordinate calculation, not an identification with the
Eisenstein integers. In general the necessary denominator is exactly l-1,
because g_bits contains -1.

For odd m there is no rational center: a solution would make (1,a) a radical
vector of the invertible B_m. For m=2, S_2=0 but b is nonzero, so there is
no center there either. Thus the rational center exists precisely at even
base lengths >=4.

## G5 The fifteen planes and the hourglass interaction

At m=3, B_3 modulo 2 is a nondegenerate alternating form on F2^4.
There are 15 isotropic planes: each of the 15 nonzero vectors is contained
in three such planes, and each plane contains three nonzero vectors.
Exactly three planes lie in the source-zero hyperplane x_0=0; the other
twelve meet the actual source chart x_0=1 in two points each.

For the latter planes the corresponding Boolean words have D divisible by
4. These word pairs are genuine sources, but the affine chart alone does
not impose the start-1/end-0 strongness guard. The 12+3 split is a typed
source-chart distinction, not a bijection to fifteen supersingular primes.

For four sources, the rectangle curl is
\[
 D(w_1,u_1)-D(w_1,u_0)-D(w_0,u_1)+D(w_0,u_0)
 =2(w_1-w_0)^T S_m(u_1-u_0).                              \tag{G5}
\]
Take m=5, w_0=u_0=10000, w_1=11000, u_1=10010. Every pair obeys the strong
guard and the curl is 2. This is the exact extra interaction term that an
ordinary potential model cannot represent. The prior
[diagonal-layer hourglass H1](../hourglass_triangular_dynamics_20261103/HOURGLASS.md#h1-a-rectanglehourglass-basis-with-no-torsion)
requires zero rectangle and hourglass curls for scalar potential differences.
G5 extends that vocabulary to a bilinear response without confusing the
four-vertex hourglass with K4.
