# What changed, and what did not

## The successful reframe

The request suggested a link between the two excluded odd counts and
symmetry/graphs. The first useful move was to distinguish four carriers:
ordered spanning paths, their endpoint fibres, coloured odd-cycle packings,
and ordinary graph traversal traces. Their cardinalities and group actions
are not interchangeable.

The inheritance pass mattered. The full-automorphism quotient H/|Aut| already
excluded7/21 by THM-4812, so searching for a counterexample there would repeat
a closed route. The actual new global result concerns every subgroup, plus
an exact symmetry-price gap below23. The dangerous subgroup indices7/21 can
only arise where the full quotient is1/3, and those low strata cannot support
seven-symmetry. Positivity and three block orders then sharpen the price bound
into a complete small-symmetry equality structure.

## Two different ways the forbidden values reappear

At an endpoint, the relevant group is the vertex stabilizer. A fibre can have
7 or21 orbits even though their sum cannot. The smallest witnesses are explicit,
and ordinal C3 padding turns them into nontrivial action-graph towers. These
are potential ingredients for future gluing, not witnesses of the forbidden
global counts.

For packings, the issue is not a boundary but a change of quotient measure.
OCF equates the raw cardinality to H, yet its automorphism action fixes the
empty object and other cycles. Ordinary orbits forget stabilizer weights.
Weighted mass recovers the Hamilton-path quotient; ordinary packing orbits
produce7/21. A tower with constant mass3 but increasing coarse counts makes
the lost coordinate visible at every scale.

These are complementary lessons. Keeping stabilizer size fixes one quotient,
but equal mass still does not identify groupoids or transition maps. The
explicit inverse-system tower distinguishes stable images from constant
levels. No algebraic-geometric theorem was needed to prove these examples.

## Graph connection with its exact loss ledger

There are two graph constructions, not one:

1. The path-action graph has Hamilton paths as vertices and generator actions
   as edges. Its component count is a specified orbit statistic. Its full
   unlabelled graph-automorphism group need not be the specified acting group.
2. The finite simple-graph encoding has tournament vertices and rigid arc
   gadgets. It preserves the full tournament automorphism group and decodes
   the tournament. Its admissible traces retain H, but its ordinary spanning
   graph paths do not. The pendant leaves make that latter count zero.

Thus the graph bridge is a genuine explicit object map, unlike merely matching
numbers. It does not turn Chan–Pak into an unconstrained graph Hamilton-path
spectrum problem or extend the earlier spanning-tree analogy into a bijection.

## Instrument and construction boundary

The exact prefix-orbit recurrence retains pointwise used-set stabilizers.
The source=>C3 hostile distinguishes coarse counts from mass; the C3=>K1
hostile distinguishes pointwise stabilizers from endpoint-state stabilizers.
Both are small enough to keep as permanent regression controls.

For Session231, cut-preserving group order divides every complete state
coordinate and every Hamilton-count difference between the specified
singleton-padded star closures sharing the same marked core (C,M). Unit-step constructions must break common
marked-core symmetry, but the inherited gcd filter already knew more. The
new viewpoint neither enlarges the twelve-bit windows nor places them over
arbitrary targets. A useful next step is a new physical interface/core family
or an endpoint-sensitive gluing operation with a proved target selector.

## Research portfolio close-out

- Direct progress: all-subgroup omission theorem and sharp low-symmetry strata.
- Niche progress: minimum-order endpoint witnesses and faithful graph carriers.
- Wildcard progress: coarse/mass stabilization contrast and exact orbit transfer.
- Refuted shortcuts: equinumerous sets need not have equivalent actions; a
  global forbidden count need not be forbidden in a fibre; a group-preserving
  graph encoding need not preserve ordinary spanning-path counts.
- Remaining frontier: unrestricted Chan–Pak completeness and arbitrary target
  placement are OPEN. No new universality, asymptotic coverage or external
  priority claim is made.

Used existing method cards: retain quotient sidecars, challenge the natural
carrier, test canonical hostiles, and explain failure mechanisms. This session
provides connected evidence for those cards, not a new generic method card
based on a single analogy.
