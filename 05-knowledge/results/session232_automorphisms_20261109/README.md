# Session 232: automorphism quotients and the 7/21 gaps

**Status: ACTIVE RESEARCH. No new theorem is claimed by this opening checkpoint.
Chan–Pak completeness remains OPEN.**

This session interprets “automorphism graphs” in three explicit ways: tournament
automorphism actions, ordinary graph realizations of those actions, and graphs
of continuation states. The targets and statistics must not be interchanged.

## Inheritance and portfolio

- **Anchor:** determine which automorphism quotients retain the forbidden
  Hamilton-path counts 7 and 21, and whether symmetry adds a constructive route.
  The closest mechanism is [THM-4812](../../../01-canon/theorems/THM-4812-presentation-fibres-7-and-21-are-absent-at-every-order-orbit-product-lemma-vertex-transitive-lemma-closed-window.md):
  the full-automorphism quotient H/|Aut| already omits both, with inherited
  independent finite-certificate audits. This result is not rediscovered here.
- **Niche:** graph encodings, subgroup indices, and the difference between
  invariant objects, ordinary orbits, and weighted action groupoids.
- **Wildcard:** finite gluing and stabilization methods inspired by the two
  supplied algebraic-geometry papers, with an explicit transfer contract.
- **Hostile:** C3 has H=3 but only one Hamilton-path orbit; an ordinary graph's
  automorphism order is not its Hamilton-path count. Divisibility is not a
  sufficient construction criterion.
- **Corrected near miss:** [THM-4892](../../../01-canon/theorems/THM-4892-tournament-sites-plus-construction.md)
  already treats OCF packings as sheaf sections, but does not identify those
  sections equivariantly with Hamiltonian paths or claim Chan–Pak progress.
- **Least-used sidecar:** stabilizer, endpoint/cut state, and overlap transport.
  [Session231](../session231_overnight_completion_20261109/README.md)'s growing
  intervals do not establish universal placement; fixed-core context absorption
  remains a required hostile test.

## Concept board

1. Raw Hamilton paths: cardinality H, SCC product, forbidden 7/21.
2. Free path actions: subgroup quotient H/|G|, index data, full group sidecar.
3. Ordinary graphs: group-preserving encoding versus count-preserving encoding.
4. Colored odd-cycle packings: OCF equality of cardinalities versus action data.
5. Continuation states: full physical interface versus scalar gain and cut symmetry.
6. Finite gluing/limits: retain transition maps before quotienting or stabilizing.

Every proposed connection records its source, target, map, preserved predicate,
loss, sidecar and cheapest decisive test. Independent review precedes promotion.
