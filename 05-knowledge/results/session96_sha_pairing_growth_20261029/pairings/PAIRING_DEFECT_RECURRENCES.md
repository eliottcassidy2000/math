# The square/twice-square law as a marked finite-pairing recurrence

**Status:** cited arithmetic input; independently proved finite algebra and finite exact tests. No proof of Shafarevich–Tate finiteness or new external-priority claim is made.

## 1. Exact arithmetic input, with the finiteness boundary

For a principally polarized abelian variety over a global field, the Cassels–Tate pairing on Sha modulo its maximal divisible subgroup is nondegenerate and antisymmetric. If that quotient is finite, it is isomorphic to T×T or C2×T×T. The choice is controlled by the characteristic element c, defined by b(x,x)=b(x,c): b(c,c) is respectively0 or1/2. The given pairing is alternating precisely when c vanishes in the quotient. A rational divisor inducing the polarization suffices; elliptic curves satisfy this condition. Square order alone does not force the given pairing to alternate. For Jacobians, the defect is the parity of places lacking a divisor of degree g−1. These are Poonen–Stoll, Theorem8, Corollaries7,9,12, not new claims. [Primary paper](https://math.mit.edu/~poonen/papers/sha.pdf).

Neither finite n-torsion nor a nondegenerate pairing on the nondivisible quotient proves that Sha is finite. A nonzero divisible subgroup is invisible to that quotient. The remaining sections analyze a **finite** input G and do not remove this arithmetic hypothesis.

## 2. The finite marked carrier and its one-bit operation

Let G be a finite abelian group and let b:G×G->Q/Z be a perfect antisymmetric bilinear pairing. Perfection means G maps isomorphically to its character group by b. Antisymmetry gives 2b(x,x)=0. Set

    delta(x)=0 if b(x,x)=0, and delta(x)=1 if b(x,x)=1/2.

Because cross terms cancel, delta:G->F2 is a homomorphism. There is a unique c in G such that

    b(x,c)=delta(x)/2  for every x.

Perfection implies 2c=0. Define epsilon=delta(c) and the endomorphism

    f(x)=x+delta(x)c.

This is a fully specified operation on the marked carrier (G,b,c), not a manipulation of its cardinality. Direct expansion gives

    f²=identity if epsilon=0;
    f²=f if epsilon=1.

The repaired pairing beta(x,y)=b(x,f(y)) is alternating: beta(x,x)=b(x,x)+delta(x)b(x,c)=0 in Q/Z. It is bilinear, and alternating bilinear forms are antisymmetric.

If epsilon=0, f is invertible and beta is perfect. When c=0 it is the identity; otherwise it is an involution. If epsilon=1, f has kernel {0,c}, image c-perp, and beta has radical {0,c}. The original b restricts to a perfect alternating pairing on c-perp. Indeed delta vanishes there, the line generated by c is nondegenerate, and

    G=<c> orthogonal-direct-sum c-perp.

Thus the operation is reversible in the even-defect case and genuinely loses a two-element fibre in the odd-defect case. Merely retaining epsilon loses more: it forgets which character delta and which element c perform the correction.

### Two minimal hostile controls

1. On G=F2², take b(x,y)=(x1y1+x2y2)/2. Then c=(1,1), epsilon=0 and |G|=4, but b is not alternating. Its repair is the alternating cross pairing. Thus 'square order' is weaker than 'the original pairing is alternating'.
2. C4 has square order4 but admits no perfect antisymmetric pairing: its generator's self-pairing has order at most2, so twice that generator is in the radical. In contrast C2×C2 does admit one. Cardinality alone does not characterize the allowable groups.

## 3. Constructive hyperbolic peeling and a recurrence

For a perfect alternating pairing on a nonzero finite G, choose x of maximum order m. Its character b(x,-) also has order m, so choose y with b(x,y)=1/m. The maximum-order condition makes y have order m. The subgroup H=<x,y> is C_m×C_m and its restricted pairing is perfect. Thus

    G=H orthogonal-direct-sum H-perp,
    |G|=m² |H-perp|.

To check the direct sum, an element ax+by pairing trivially with x and y has a=b=0 modulo m. The restriction map G->Hom(H,Q/Z) is surjective because restriction from the finite character group is surjective; its kernel is H-perp. Therefore every element has a unique H+H-perp decomposition. Repeating terminates and reconstructs paired cyclic factors.

Apply this to beta in the epsilon=0 case, or to c-perp in the epsilon=1 case. The output is a list m1,...,mr with

    |G|=2^epsilon (product_i mi)²,
    G ≅ C2^epsilon × product_i (C_mi × C_mi).

The group isomorphism here is not a canonical choice of factors, and beta need not equal the original b when epsilon=0. The procedure supplies an actual finite certificate, not only a square test. A recurrence can retain the marked residual pairing and update

    (epsilon,R,G_residual) -> (epsilon,mR,H-perp),

after the one-time odd-defect split. Each step reduces the finite group size by m². No version of this termination argument applies before a finite input group has been justified.

## 4. Orthogonal products have a binary carry

Write a finite paired group's size uniquely as 2^epsilon R², epsilon in{0,1}, R a positive integer. For orthogonal direct sums,

    c_(G⊕H)=(c_G,c_H),
    epsilon_(G⊕H)=epsilon_G XOR epsilon_H,
    R_(G⊕H)=2^(epsilon_G epsilon_H) R_G R_H.

The last formula is the elementary carry 1+1=0 modulo2 together with a factor4 in the square part. The update on pairs is

    (epsilon,R) * (eta,S) = (epsilon XOR eta, 2^(epsilon eta)RS).

It is associative because both sides encode the same product order; directly its exponent identity is

    epsilon*eta + (epsilon XOR eta)*theta
      = eta*theta + epsilon*(eta XOR theta)

for three bits. Doubling an odd-defect object cancels its cardinality defect, but does not automatically make the **original orthogonal-sum pairing** alternating: c_(G⊕G)=(c,c) is still nonzero when c is. This distinction is essential.

There are two different recursion modes:

* Adjoin the anisotropic C2 line with pairing b(1,1)=1/2: |G| ->2|G| and epsilon toggles; the square-root factor doubles only when the old epsilon is1.
* Adjoin a hyperbolic C_m² plane: |G| ->m²|G| with epsilon unchanged.

On exponent-two groups, adjoining a hyperbolic plane raises vector-space dimension by2. Orthogonally duplicating an entire group doubles that dimension and squares its order. This gives precise '+2 versus ×2' operations in this finite pairing category. A claimed bridge to tournament recursion must provide an actual object map preserving these operations; shared formulas alone do not supply one.

## 5. A local isomorphism-type generating recurrence

Fix a prime p. A finite abelian p-group carrying a perfect alternating pairing has each cyclic multiplicity even. Conversely, pairing equal cyclic factors constructs such a form. Consequently the number of possible underlying groups of order p^(2n) is the partition number p(n), and the formal size-exponent inventory is

    A_p(t)=product_(j>=1) (1-t^(2j))^(-1).

At p=2, allowing either defect gives

    B_2(t)=(1+t) A_2(t).

The odd-defect term adds a single C2, so it is disjoint from the even-defect family. This counts **underlying group isomorphism types**, not isometry types of pairings, principal polarizations, abelian varieties, or actual Sha realizations. For degree cutoff D, the product uses only j<=D/2 and obeys the ordinary partition dynamic program. Keeping the c-mark and the pairing refines this inventory and can distinguish objects with the same group type.

## 6. The local-condition boundary and the next honest arithmetic target

Morgan–Smith construct Cassels–Tate pairings from short exact sequences of finite Galois modules of order prime to the field characteristic, supplied with local conditions. Their kernel descriptions retain the images of the middle Selmer groups. This is a useful source-preserving interface: a candidate recurrence for descent data must transport the local conditions and these images, not just module orders or a binary defect. [Primary paper, abstract and Theorem1.3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.08530).

The finite algebra above suggests a testable next step: for a specified descent exact sequence, calculate its pairing matrix and characteristic element, then compare the corrected alternating quotient with the actual localization kernels. It does not supply a bound on the number or exponents of Sha's cyclic factors. The conjectural finiteness step remains outside the proved recursion.

It tests the binary carry associativity, hostile C4/F2² examples, and the finite partition inventory. Assertions remain active under `python -O` by explicit exceptions.
