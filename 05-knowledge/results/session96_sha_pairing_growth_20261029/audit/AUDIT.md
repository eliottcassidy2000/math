## 1. Arithmetic theorem versus finite algebra

The primary source [Poonen–Stoll](https://math.mit.edu/~poonen/papers/sha.pdf),
Theorem8 and Corollary9, supports the square/twice-square statements for a
finite Sha or finite quotient by its maximal divisible subgroup. The
principal polarization is essential to the stated self-pairing. Its
antisymmetry is weaker than alternation; the characteristic element controls
that difference. A nonzero isotropic characteristic element is compatible
with square order. Nothing in this theorem bounds the divisible subgroup or
makes the quotient finite.

The finite algebra report correctly keeps these hypotheses. The map
f(x)=x+delta(x)c is an involution when delta(c)=0 and a projection with
kernel generated by c when delta(c)=1. Repairing the pairing can change the
original marked form. In the latter case its orthogonal complement is a
perfect alternating group. Hyperbolic peeling is valid after a finite input
has been supplied. It is not an argument that an unknown arithmetic input
must terminate.

The binary carry law and the partition generating series are correctly
scoped to group orders or underlying abstract group isomorphism types.
They do not count pairings, polarizations or arithmetic realizations.
The same square group can carry inequivalent marked pairing data, while a
cyclic group of square order need not carry any perfect antisymmetric form.

Independent enumeration tests every nonsingular symmetric F2 matrix in
sizes1 through4:481 forms in total. It checks the characteristic identity,
repair power, image size, alternating repair and perfect odd-defect
complement. In particular I2 has a nonzero isotropic characteristic element,
whereas the hyperbolic matrix has zero characteristic element.

## 2. Tournament lattice transports

The tournament construction gives an integral skew matrix, not merely an
antisymmetric Q/Z-valued form. Its inverse is skew over Q, so the finite
linking form is alternating. At even order its reduction modulo2 is J-I,
whose square is identity. Its determinant is therefore an odd square.
This construction cannot generate the2-primary defect. Adding a C2 line
would be an additional abstract object, not a consequence of the tournament
recursion or an arithmetic realization.

All32,834 labelled tournaments on2,4,6 vertices were independently checked
for odd Pfaffian. The determinant sets are respectively{1},{1,9}, and
{1,9,25,49,81}. Independent rational determinants and inverse forms were
checked for all orders2/4 and a declared deterministic sample at order6.

Endpoint padding has the stated integral congruence to M directsum J2;
the unit block contributes no discriminant. For skew-Sylvester doubling,
the explicit quotient map is valid, and its transported form is
-z^T M(I-2M²)^(-1)w modulo Z. The denominator matrix by itself has a different
natural symmetric form. Both the quotient and the named M action matter.
The proof's column-elimination wording was clarified during audit; the
identity D(M)(-y,-y)=(-(2M+I)y,y) makes the transport literal.

The self-order-join hostile is independently verified: a four-vertex
matrix with group C3² produces a join with determinant81, nullity2 modulo3,
and exponent9. The group is C9², not C3^4. The named port being trivial is
a sufficient condition for the integral direct-sum shear, not claimed
necessary for every possible abstract isomorphism. Cardinality squaring
alone demonstrably loses extension data.

## 3. The finite-field/F1 boundary

The symplectic flag and Lagrangian counts concern explicitly alternating
forms. The direct-sum Lagrangian formula correctly classifies a subspace by
its intersections with the two summands and an anti-symplectic isomorphism
between the residual quotient spaces. The residual spaces both have
dimension2r; their graph sector is a torsor under Sp_(2r), not a canonical
identification.

Independent polynomial arithmetic checks the full composition formula for
0<=m,n<=4, including positivity after q=1+t, exact vanishing order r, and
leading coefficient2^(m+n-r)r!binom(m,r)binom(n,r). Literal subspace
enumeration in dimension4 gives:

* F2:15 Lagrangians,9 separable and6 mixed;
* F3:40 Lagrangians,16 separable and24 mixed.

The raw symplectic group count vanishes at q=1; the normalized count or
flag count gives the Weyl-group size. The report does not treat q=1 as a
finite field or assign a variable q to Sha. Its thin specialization erases
all positive coupling-rank sectors, rather than proving they do not exist.
This is a controlled finite-field comparison, not an F1 model of an
abelian variety or its local-global obstruction.

## 4. The fixed-prime stopping certificate

For a p-primary torsion group with finite p-torsion, multiplication by p
injects each successive torsion quotient into the preceding one. The
nonnegative logarithmic increments therefore decrease and eventually
stabilize. The resulting affine-tail cardinality formula is valid without
assuming a classification theorem. A zero increment is an exact positive
certificate of fixed-prime finiteness; a growing finite prefix is not a
negative certificate.

The Selmer-ratio inequality has the correct bound directions. A certified
rank lower bound r0 and exact torsion ratio tau_n give a lower bound for the
actual consecutive Selmer ratio. A certified upper bound for the numerator
and lower bound for the denominator give its upper bound. When those meet,
all inequalities are equalities: the actual rank equals r0 and the Sha
layers plateau. The n=0 normalization and torsion correction are explicit.
An unsupported rank guess cannot replace the lower bound.

The finite-level perfect pairings on (Z/p^n)^2 scale by p under the standard
inclusions; they are not isometries. Inside the ambient stage E, the
restricted pairing has radical size p^(2min(n,E-n)); it is zero when E>=2n.
Thus a bounded window of groups, maps and pairings can agree with a divisible
infinite model. These models are not claimed to be realized as Sha groups.
The separate infinite-prime-support obstruction is also necessary: fixed-prime
finiteness alone does not imply global finiteness.

The independent tower checker tests monotone profiles, bound directions,
lower-rank inputs, n=0, and literal restricted-pairing radicals. It proves no
arithmetic certification algorithm always reaches the required bound.

## 5. The actual two-descent calibration

For E/Q:y²=x³-x, the cited full rational two-torsion descent map is supported
by [Morgan–Paterson, §7.2, equation7.5](https://eprints.gla.ac.uk/261997/1/261997.pdf).
The local support argument retains the branch exceptions. For odd primes,
a nonintegral x has even valuation, while an integral x allows at most one
of x,x-1,x+1 to have positive valuation; the product square condition makes
that valuation even.

Independent enumeration of primitive projective coordinates modulo8 leaves
exactly the four displayed real-surviving squareclass triples. Their counts
are128 for(1,1,1),128 for(-1,-1,1),256 for(1,2,2), and256 for(-1,-2,2).
The four other real-compatible triples have no primitive solution modulo8.
The four survivors are actual distinct Kummer images of O and the three
rational two-torsion points. Consequently the lower bound from actual points
matches the upper Selmer bound. There is no circular use of a guessed rank.
Mordell–Weil finite generation gives rank zero; Sha[2]=0 then kills the entire
2-primary torsion component. Odd-primary parts are not computed by this
argument. The calibration is a reproduced classical example, not a new
Sha finiteness theorem.
