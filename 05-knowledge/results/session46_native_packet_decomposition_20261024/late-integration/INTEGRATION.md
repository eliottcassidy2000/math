## 3. A proved conditional multi-target extension

Incoming MULTITARGET.md supplies a useful extension of our common-pair rule.
Let t distinct targets have one common paired-charge label in a finite abelian
group G, with source owners outside that target set. Suppose every mixture of
the supplied old/new actual records is compatible with all retained shared pins,
full owner/kernel records, local guards and normalized-resource capacities,
including the immutable remainder. Endpoints have equal INTEGER target margins
and equal physical-row current signatures.

For each changed slot, draw an edge from its old target to its new target,
labelled by the actual source charge. Equal target margins make this a balanced
directed multigraph. Split its edges into simple directed cycles. Each cycle
uses at most t slots and already preserves every integer margin. Its remaining
vector of target-owned charges sums to zero, so belongs to

    Gamma = ker(G^t -> G), isomorphic to G^(t-1),

where the last target coordinate is reconstructed as the negative sum of the
others. Source rows are safe because the target labels agree. Extract minimal
zero-sum packets of these cycle charges. At most D(Gamma) cycles are needed in
one packet, so atomic support is at most

    t D(G^(t-1)).

Every changed slot is used once, and the all-mixture native hypothesis supplies
actual compatibility and injection at every step. For an abelian p-group,
D(G^(t-1))=1+(t-1)(D(G)-1), by the classical formula proved independently in our
decomposition-theory packet. For the original G with D(G)=132, this gives 264,
789 and 1,576 for t=2,3,4 respectively. These are conditional upper bounds, not
claimed sharp native widths or order-independent constants.

Integer margins must be solved first. A multiple of a modulus is still an
incorrect count of actual target paths. Cycle extraction handles these integer
constraints; finite-group extraction handles the remaining target-owned currents.
If mixtures are not compatible, arbitrary subsets of cycles need not be legal.
The full shared-coordinate/incidence encoding and Graver theorem remain the
appropriate fallback, without the same small charge-only bound.

This is consistent with the abstract cyclic-margin hostile in our theory:
when G is trivial the bound is t, and an abstract t-target cyclic interface can
require all t changes. The common-pair bound was never a bound independent of
the number or incidence pattern of targets.

## 4. Arithmetic has a third target; the pinned paths do not

The complete original marked label table has exactly the equal-charge fiber

    {570, 1965, 2234}, with label (1,1,0,1;18).

Our checker verifies the entire 3,528-node table, not just those three nodes.
But the authenticated full 28-source common-clock supply has no path targeting 570.
It has 56 U/V paths plus the one 1923-to-1559 extra path. Thus a real third node in
the correct charge fiber is not a licensed third native choice under the old pins.

An inherited complete source census gives a stronger, still narrow boundary.
Its 700 source/target-pair menus exhaust 3,528 canonical merged source
representatives and 1,102,392 actual Hamiltonian paths. None involves target
570, while exactly 28 menus have target pair {1965,2234}, with the same 28
source owners as this session's supply. The census requires genuine four-word
normalized black resources, excludes source-target loops, uses two distinct
equal-charge targets, and retains the same actual first and last owners plus
at least one common indexed internal arc at clocks k,k+1 with 1<=k<=5, in a
fixed canonical source frame. Its separate endpoint-fiber audit establishes
completeness in that grammar; this note scans the saved census rather than
rerunning that large enumeration.

This can be summarized by a grammar-dependent pin-compatible target graph:
its vertices are the three equal-charge targets, and an edge means at least
one source menu exists under the audited endpoint/indexed-arc black-transition
grammar. Its exact graph is the single edge 1965--2234, witnessed by 28 source
menus, together with isolated vertex 570.

There is a conditional topology-sensitive refinement of the cycle proof. Let C
be an active nontrivial component of the authenticated pin-compatible target
graph, and let ell_C be the maximum length of a simple directed cycle in its
bidirected version, including two-cycles. Under the same all-mixture,
source-outside-target, current and resource hypotheses, one may use support

    ell_C D(G^(|C|-1))

within C, then take the maximum over active components. Same-target changes
are handled separately as support-one events; an isolated vertex needs no
cycle-length convention. Indeed the actual directed transition multigraph
splits by these components, and endpoint current equality makes each component's
total target-charge vector zero separately. Decompose into simple cycles of
length at most ell_C, then extract zero-sum packets in G^(|C|-1) just as before.
No slot moves twice. This is a proof-level refinement, not a claim of sharpness,
efficient longest-cycle computation or additional native supply.

For the present single edge plus isolated vertex, the only nontrivial component
has |C|=ell_C=2. Its bound is therefore 264. Listing three equal-charge vertices
does not supply a usable new 789-slot three-target mechanism when the grammar
has no transition incident to the third vertex.

That isolation is not isolation in the original metagraph. The independent
late audit verifies that node 570 has distinct converse classes 732/736, fibre
count 590, and 213 distinct original neighbors with total black weight 590,
no blue contribution and no loop. It has genuine incident resources, but no
transition menu joining it to its equal-charge peers under the combined
retained grammar. This does not identify which individual pin or resource
requirement causes the failure; that would require a separate weakened-grammar
comparison.

Thus merely selecting a different original source cannot provide a target-570
transition while retaining all those conditions. A successful extension must
change at least one of the specified menu conditions or the ambient setting,
then authenticate its shared records and available resources anew. This is not
a claim that no Hamiltonian path targets 570, or that general native wiring is
impossible. The multi-target theorem can use a richer authenticated interface,
but neither its group formula nor a new source label creates that interface.
