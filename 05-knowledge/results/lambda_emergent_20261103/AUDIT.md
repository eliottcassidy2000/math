**Same-agent proof review with independent computational paths.** No Lean
formalization or independent-agent audit is claimed.

The new all-order claims are JC1--JC4, PL1--PL3 and RR1--RR5, with proofs in
their named files. Their finite certificates use exact integers/rationals,
explicit row-action conventions, and checks that remain active under Python
-O. No result is promoted from a finite prefix without an operator identity,
induction, structural classification or a proved insertion argument.

## Hostiles and repaired exploratory mistakes

Inherited81/27 and55->111 receipts are pinned and remain outside any claimed
scalar classification. A four-phase primitive guard obstruction is retained.
The exceptional q=0 boundary changes the doubling offset from1 to3.
The ready grid has genuine count collisions already among wider one-apex
word banks; its scalar formula is not an isomorphism-class enumeration.

A cheap exploratory probe found two Hamilton cycles in R5, not three.
The stronger invariant is three Hamilton starts at each vertex. The proof
uses that rooted quantity. No false cycle-count claim was published.
Another tempting shortcut was rejected: the parent of an exceptional
indegree-two ready root need not be transitive. Only deletion of the root
and its two in-neighbours is transitive. RR2 therefore uses the actual
five-word language, and RR3 uses strong-parent q>s to exclude that case.

## Actual consequences and limits

There is now a positive old-edge readiness repair and an infinite preparation
grid, an exact minimal linear observer for a specified return language, and
a common full-phase primitive legalizer. These are different interfaces.
Neither count-changing readiness repair nor modularly neutral long words
give a uniform exact-H inverse. The general eighteen-polynomial language,
arbitrary re-marking, native complement and joint-port attachments are not
replaced by six scalars. Full ternary closure and spectrum completeness
remain OPEN. No consequence for Collatz, LRC or Sha finiteness is claimed.
