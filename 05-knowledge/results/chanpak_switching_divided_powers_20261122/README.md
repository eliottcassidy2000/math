# What vertex switches and single arc flips can select

**Chan–Pak remains OPEN.** Chan and Pak credit the conjecture to bof and Gordon Royle. The new results give exact predictions for the ordinary number \(H(T)\) of Hamiltonian paths of a tournament. One prescribes the gap between counts across a physical arc reversal. Another determines how much of a whole switching class must be sampled to recover its counts modulo a given integer. A third identifies a Latin-square permutation that decides both square-sum path connectivity and a tournament correlation. [Chan and Pak, Conjecture 6.2](https://www.math.ucla.edu/~pak/papers/DominoPermanent16.pdf)

## The remaining absolute-count problem

Take \(d=1\). At sufficiently large odd orders the one-apex construction has only four possible normalized positive-gap addresses, and all their upper counts are divisible by three. At sufficiently large even orders the same family can make *both* counts avoid any prescribed finite set of primes. The reason is visible in binary coordinates: an odd order forces the two addresses onto opposite sides of a power of two. Equal-width addresses at even order allow a common prefix to vary while keeping the gap fixed.

For fixed \(M\), prescribed residues still leave gaps of order \(\sqrt X\) in the set of lower endpoints. This explains why an all-modulus local theorem can coexist with a sparse set of actual adjacent counts. The inherited star construction instead reaches all heights on certain fixed rays, including all admissible targets of canonical defect at most 4096. Its unbounded obligation is to select a pair of actual tagged tournaments with the necessary intercept and slope. The switching theorem restricts the residue data of such sources and supplies small exact tests; an arbitrary-target selection rule is still missing. ## Which symmetry preserves which information?

* **Switching at vertices.** The edge signs across a cut have common endpoint factors. After a diagonal change of coordinates the adjacency perturbation has rank two. The resulting determinant is *quadratic* in switch indicators. In the squarefree vertex algebra its \(j\)-th power about the constant term carries \(j!\) because its disjoint pieces can be ordered freely. Transposing a tournament identifies the two endpoint series modulo two and supplies the extra divisibility in HYP-9669. The order-nine example realizes every odd residue modulo 24, including both residues of the two absolute holes. [Proof](../../../01-canon/theorems/THM-5313-switching-derivatives-of-hamiltonian-paths-and-cycles.md) · [Direct reconstruction](check_switches.py)
* **Forgetting an endpoint.** Weighting tournaments by \(H\) gives uniform matching-row parities. Conditioning the marked path on its endpoints closing into a cycle detects the single-cycle relation of two Latin rows. Marking other cycle types recovers all relative-permutation cycle lengths. A related incoming result separates otherwise indistinguishable self-converse hosts using a marked center and graded endpoint counts. These operations have different domains; each identifies exactly which extra observation distinguishes its objects. [Matching rows](../chanpak_matching_parity_faces_20261122/CYCLE-ROWS.md) · [Graded center probe](../chanpak_matching_folds_branching_20261122/CENTER-PROBE.md)
* **Composing reflections.** Earlier work read the 3–4–5 path on \(1,\ldots,15\) from a translation modulo 16. Two primitive triples sharing one even leg supply two reflection matchings and a common third matching. Trading on a chosen set of alternating cycles preserves the Latin constraints, while a gcd counts the translation orbits. Folding the two shores turns those orbits into a spanning path or a path and residual cycles. This is the concrete map from three integer lengths to the component test. [Earlier reflection and rooted ternary constructions](../chanpak_berggren_square_bridge_20261117/README.md) · [New correspondence](LATIN-CYCLE-TYPE.md)

## Outside sources and the exponent question

The pinned Latin-square writeup represents rows as perfect matchings and corrects the unequal numbers of ways to complete a partial history. For path-marked tournaments a path contains at most one full matching row. Exchanging one prescribed pair gives exactly the two histories needed in the exceptional case, producing a rejection-free sampler. Its proof counts completions directly. [Pinned Mira Cult writeup](https://github.com/Mira-Cult/math/blob/30ddaaad27b48df2002225c3e3f40cffd02c5253/notes/combinatorics/exact-latin-square-sampling/README.md) · [Tournament sampler](../chanpak_matching_parity_faces_20261122/README.md)

Pasten and Vidaux's Lemma 4.1 uses a formal elliptic-group coordinate to approximate a prescribed integer at the finitely many primes dividing a modulus. Their number-field theorem additionally assumes abc and a positive-rank elliptic curve whose rank stays fixed under extension. The comparable step here is the fixed-modulus one-arc decoder. Its independent real-height estimate exposes the remaining error in an absolute target; the number-field theorem contributes no assumption to the tournament proof. [Pasten and Vidaux, Theorem 1.1 and Lemma 4.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.24748)

Alpöge's supplied remark describes seeking an arithmetic substitute for multiplicity and proposes iterated theta lifts in its RH announcement. The specific question it suggests here is which operation on finite combinatorial objects supplies multiplicity after labels are forgotten. In the switching proof the witnesses are the \(j!\) orders of \(j\) disjoint vertex supports; the proof counts them within a nilpotent ring. This comparison uses the remark as motivation and imports no RH or GRH assertion. [Alpöge, remark, pp. 1 and 3](https://alpo.ge/remark.pdf)

The Fermat and Jacobian comparisons distinguish three different meanings of dimension. The plane Fermat curves \(x^e+y^e=z^e\) all have dimension one; their degree and genus \((e-1)(e-2)/2\) vary. The conic permits rational Euclid parameters, and FLT excludes a positive integral triple for \(e>2\). In the complex constant-Jacobian problem, adjoining identity coordinates carries a counterexample to each higher ambient dimension; the planar case stays open. In the switching problem there is a proved conversion of exponent into combinatorial dimension: a prime \(p\) bounds the number of independent vertex switches that can interact modulo \(p\), with the exact maximum in THM-5313. The three statements have different mechanisms and quantifiers.
