# Session 329: activation is not regeneration

The numbers around 30,000 mark a real change in what geometry a construction can afford, but not a single universal transition. This session separates **activation** (the first time a piece fits), **compatibility** (the first time its boundary data can match the job), and **regeneration** (a legal move returns the boundary data it will need next).

## Chan–Pak: change the interface, not merely its scale

The inherited slope-99 chart cannot use an order-seven strong component before 54,277. In the two-sided triangle cut there is a different near-30,000 chamber: grades 4+7 and 5+6 fit, but 4+8, 5+7 and 6+6 first enter at 37,467, 39,675 and 40,851. The two-sided output can be prime (30,347) or seven times a prime (29,477) using a four-core and two different seven-cores. The exact path language has a crucial term in which both reservoirs are split; omitting it badly understates the threshold. Moving one neutral vertex between the reservoirs changes the output by an exact two-by-two determinant. See [the two-sided activation](TWO_SIDED_ACTIVATION.md).

There are also two infinite limitations. [A single seam with bounded source SCC order](BOUNDED_GRADE.md) remains sparse even with arbitrarily many components. And a finite seed bank iterating Fermat blocks, with arbitrary choices between the existing root and the newest terminal, misses almost all primes and almost all seven-times-primes: consecutive resets trap the computation in a common divisor, while avoiding them leaves sublinear growth in the number of legal itineraries. See [the two-port cutoff obstruction](FERMAT_RETURN_CUTOFF.md). These results concern specific construction languages; neither is a new globally forbidden count.

A further [small-source frontier](replay_fermat_frontier.py) puts the one-block scene on the same scale: with the three blocks of counts 3, 5 and 17 and strong source order at most 8, the absolute maximum is 28,503. Thus by 30,000 that particular construction actually needs a nine-vertex strong source, despite six- and seven-vertex components still being central in the other charts.

## Square sums: an actual repeatable local repair

The archived 25-fold gluing of a square-sum path with endpoints 1 and 3 is stronger than a recurrence for length. [Square-sum switch theorem](SQUARE_SUM_SWITCH.md) identifies exactly which block choices are compatible, which choices survive in an output parity observation, and an independent exchange cube that reappears at every scale. One exchange lives on a reflection hexagon of edge labels 16, 25 and 36 whose first possible height is 23. Its first use in the anchored spanning template is a separate question; the order-35 seeds give a repeatable use. A [related four-cycle calculation](SQUARE_REFLECTION_HEIGHT.md) gives an exact first positive height of 99 for the scaled identity 4+196=100+100, distinct from the universal first four-cycle at 46. This is a concrete model for proving that a spare operation really returns a boundary state and does not spend its freedom only once.

## Hadwiger–Nelson: record what fails to come back

In odd-degree unit-distance carriers, cross-edges between additive cosets are exact translation matchings. The new [holonomy test](HN_COSET_HOLONOMY.md) tells when translations can make such a patch a direct product and when traversing a loop returns with a displacement. It finds a sharp non-product example and gives a reason to discard flat contexts as candidate coloring obstructions under stated hypotheses. It does not supply a seventh-color witness. The [older radius-2 work](../hn_type_m_hinge_rotations_and_forced_radii_20261014.md) already exhibited the analogous size trap at another chromatic level: two radius-forcing pieces were jointly 4-colorable at 31,729 vertices, while the matched M3/rotated-P1 interface at 48,121 was not. The new holonomy test is a structural filter in a different carrier, not a replacement proof for those older solver results.

## Next proof obligation

The object to cover is an **absolute target together with its next usable interface**, not a count or residue alone. An all-scale cutoff would follow from a sound target-decreasing inverse whose outputs cover every odd target above a finite initial window and whose predecessors are physically available in the required states. The two-port theorem rules out closing this obligation by terminal resets alone. The transferable idea from the square-sum exchange is to leave one reserve independently switchable at the end of a return; the two-sided determinant identifies where to look for such a switch in tournaments. What remains is a coverage theorem for those switches at prescribed targets, not just equality, phase overlap, or a longer checked interval.
