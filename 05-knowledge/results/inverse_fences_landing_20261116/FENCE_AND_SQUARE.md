# Fence discretization and an exact square-only seam repair

Two notions of a small graph matter here. First, a shifted unit lattice turns a whole physical fence into exactly one bond of a contact graph. Second, two modular reflections turn all edges of a sum graph into class motion and a small list of components. In both cases an ambiguity in a scalar count is removed by keeping the *interface edge*.

## Random lattice certificates for a subclass of Friedman's fences

[Friedman's maximum-area problem](https://erich-friedman.github.io/packing/fence/) asks for the largest total area enclosed by unit fences with endpoints on other fences, noncrossing interiors, and individual fields of area at most one. The archived [corner lemma](../../../01-canon/theorems/THM-4509-fence-corner-lemma-and-density-bound.md) and [per-fence study](../procgen_fence2_20260926_per_fence_accounting.md) distinguish aggregate angle relaxations from length accounting. Here is a sharp finite envelope for a restricted class.

**Theorem.** Suppose all fences are horizontal or vertical and each bounded field has horizontal and vertical span at most one. Then \(A\le K(n):=\max\{k\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}:2k+\lceil 2\sqrt k\rceil\le n\}\). At the budgets \(n=2k+\lceil2\sqrt k\rceil\), the bound is attained by the archived quasi-square \(k\)-omino construction (THM-4505, F5).

**Proof.** Translate \(\mathbb Z^2\) by a uniformly random vector in \([0,1)^2\). Outside a measure-zero event, each field contains at most one sample point, by the span constraints and openness, and each unit fence crosses exactly one nearest-neighbor lattice bond (one line orthogonal to the fence, with exactly one containing bond). If \(S\) is the set of sampled points in bounded fields and \(k=|S|\), every lattice bond incident with \(S\) crosses a fence: its endpoints belong to distinct fields or one outside. As no fence crosses two lattice bonds, these incidences give an injection of bonds into fences by choosing one fence from each nonempty crossing set. For the union of unit cells centered at \(S\), let \(P\) count its boundary unit edges. Then the number of incident bonds is \(2k+P/2\). With \(r\) occupied rows and \(c\) occupied columns, \(P\ge2(r+c)\) and \(rc\ge k\), so \(n\ge 2k+\lceil2\sqrt k\rceil\). Thus \(k\le K(n)\) for almost every shift. Averaging over shifts gives \(\mathbb E k=A\).

For two fixed unoriented directions meeting at angle \(\theta\), take the linear map sending the two unit direction vectors to coordinate unit vectors. Areas divide by \(|\sin\theta|\) and all fence lengths in their respective directions stay one. If every transformed field has each coordinate span at most one, the bound is \(A\le|\sin\theta|K(n)\), again sharp at the lattice budgets. This does not handle unrestricted long thin fields or arbitrary directions.

For a weaker but unconditional two-direction bound, in the transformed orthogonal coordinates rectilinear perimeter is at least \(4\sqrt{\text{area}}\) (sweep across both coordinate projections on each component, then sum). Each original field of area \(a\le1\) therefore has perimeter at least \(4\sqrt{a/|\sin\theta|}\ge4a/\sqrt{|\sin\theta|}\); the exposed perimeter of the bounded union is at least \(4\sqrt{A/|\sin\theta|}\). The sum of the bounded faces' perimeters plus that exposed boundary is at most \(2n\); hence \(A+\sqrt A\le n\sqrt{|\sin\theta|}/2\). A \(k\times k\) square grid attains this latter bound in the right-angle case.

## The whole orbit packet, not only its primitive case

The [archived reflection theorem](../../../01-canon/theorems/THM-4510-sum-graphs-as-reflection-orbits-pythagorean-zigzags.md) finds the three-square chain at \(15\) and generalizes it to primitive Pythagorean triples. The corresponding exact decomposition for the whole scaled family is:

Take integers \(0<s<m\), put \(d=\gcd(s,m)\), \(L=m/d\), and on \(1,\ldots,m-1\) allow the sums \(s,m,m+s\). The graph has one path on \(L-1\) vertices, a second path on \(L\) vertices if \(d\) is even, and \(\lfloor(d-1)/2\rfloor\) cycles of length \(2L\). A single vertex is counted as a path.

Indeed, use residues in \(\mathbb Z/m\): the edges are the matchings \(R_0(x)=-x\), \(R_s(x)=s-x\), omitting loops, then remove the vertex 0. The product \(R_sR_0\) is translation by \(s\), which traverses the full class modulo \(d\), and a reflection swaps class \(a\) with \(-a\). Paired classes \(a\ne-a\) give a connected 2-regular cycle of size \(2L\). For \(a=0\), the two involutions on the second coordinate \(t\in\mathbb Z/L\) are \(-t\) and \(s/d-t\). They have two fixed incidences in all: one each if \(L\) is odd, two and zero respectively if \(L\) is even (then \(s/d\) is odd). This connected component is a path with 0 as an endpoint. For \(d\) even the class \(a=d/2\) similarly has involutions \(-1-t\) and \(s/d-1-t\); their fixed-incidence counts are one each for \(L\) odd, zero and two for \(L\) even. It is the remaining path and does not contain 0.

With \(d=1,s=9,m=16\) the path at 15 starts at 8 and ends at 9: the reflection fixed point 8 survives, while 9 is exposed by removal of 0. With \(s,m\) the square legs of a primitive triple, \(m+s\) is the hypotenuse square, and \(d=1\). Multiplying a primitive triple by \(g>1\) makes \(d=g^2\); it produces entire disjoint cycles rather than missing local edges. At least \(\lfloor d/2\rfloor\) edges across the original components are needed by any Hamiltonian repair.

## An optimal four-square repair for infinitely many nonprimitive triples

Let \(d>1\) be odd, write \(f=(d-1)/2\), and choose \(u>0\) with \(\gcd(u,d)=1\) and \(fu<s\). Then **a single extra target \(m+u\)** supplies a Hamiltonian path using exactly \(f\) extra edges, the minimum possible. Start by traversing the old path so its endpoint next to deleted 0, namely \(s\), is last. Put \(y_0=s\). For \(i=1,\ldots,f\), enter the cycle on classes \(\pm iu\) at \(x_i=m-s+iu\) from \(y_{i-1}\), then traverse it to \(y_i=s-iu\) along the complement of the old \(R_0\) edge \(x_i y_i\). Indeed \(x_i+y_i=m\) and \(y_{i-1}+x_i=m+u\). All labels are in range by \(fu<s\); the cycles are distinct and exhaustive because \(u\) is a unit mod \(d\).

In particular, take a primitive Pythagorean triple \((a,b,c)\) with \(a<b\), scale by a fixed odd \(g\ge3\), and set \(s=(ga)^2, m=(gb)^2, u=2gb+1, d=g^2\). The new target \(m+u=(gb+1)^2\) is itself square and \(u\) is a unit modulo \(g^2\). For infinitely many balanced primitive triples the size guard \(fu<s\) holds: take Euclid parameters \(U=2V+1\), \(V\) even and growing, and sort the two legs. Both legs have quadratic growth in \(V\), so \(s\) has quartic growth and \(u\) quadratic. Example \((24,45,51)\) from \((8,15,17)\), \(g=3\), has \(s=576,m=2025,d=9,u=91\). The old graph consists of one 224-vertex path and four 450-cycles; four seams of the new square sum \(2116=46^2\) produce one path on 2024 vertices. Here four is optimal as a count of added cross-component edges, not a claim that four symbols are necessary if one may replace the three original symbols.

For even \(d\), the same \(u=1\) repair joins all cycle components while leaving the second path on the self-class \(d/2\). Join its actual endpoint \(e\) to the final \(y_i\) using the extra target \(e+y_i\). At most two new target values and exactly \(d/2\) cross-component edges suffice; neither new target is asserted square. The difference is an actual endpoint obstruction at the self-class, not a density effect.

The transfer to tournament work is methodological: counting how many pieces are available is not enough; preserve concrete free endpoints, as the cycle cut above does, or preserve an injective bond/segment map, as the lattice theorem does. These undirected constructions do not implement a Hamiltonian-count inverse lift in tournaments.

### Counting rates in this explicit subfamily

With fixed odd \(g\ge3\) and \(V\) even, \(V\ge4\), the output sizes are
\[
N=(g(4V^2+2V))^2-1.
\]
For that \(g\), only finitely many \(V\) fail the seam guard, and the number of these sizes at most \(X\) is \(X^{1/4}/(4\sqrt g)+O_g(1)\). Allowing \(g\) to vary under the guard changes the exponent: the number of valid pairs \((g,V)\) is \(\Theta(X^{3/8})\), and the number of distinct resulting sizes is \(X^{3/8+o(1)}\). Indeed the size constraint bounds \(g\) by \(O(\sqrt X/V^2)\), while the seam guard bounds it by \(O(V^2)\). Summing the smaller bound over \(V\), split at \(V=X^{1/8}\), gives the upper estimate. For the lower estimate, use \(V\) even in \([X^{1/8},2X^{1/8}]\) and \(g\) odd from 3 to a sufficiently small constant times \(X^{1/4}\); both upper constraints and the actual guard hold uniformly. Finally \(M=\sqrt{N+1}\) has the form \(g(4V^2+2V)\); each divisor of \(M\) is the leg value \(4V^2+2V\) for at most one \(V\). Each \(N\) therefore comes from at most \(\tau(M)=M^{o(1)}\) parameter pairs. This exponent describes the chosen repaired four-square family, not the full square-sum spectrum or the distribution of long Collatz transients.

### Endpoint range and the exact cost of projection collisions

In the odd-gcd repair with \(f>0\), the final endpoint is \(s-fu\). Thus choices of \(u\) have landing multiplicity one on endpoints inside this scheme, rather than merely producing a large aggregate number of walks. For scaled Pythagorean legs, any additional square \((gb+r)^2\) with \(r\ge1\) is usable if \(f r(2gb+r)<s\) and \(\gcd(r,g)=1\): since \(g\mid gb\), the modular unit condition on \(u=r(2gb+r)\) is exactly this gcd condition. The number of such choices is
\[
\frac{\varphi(g)}g \left(\sqrt{m+s/f}-\sqrt m\right)+O(2^{\omega(g)}).
\]
The strict boundary costs at most one, and the formula is just inclusion–exclusion for \(r\) coprime to \(g\) below the displayed upper limit. For fixed \(g\) and the balanced family this gives \(\Theta_g(V^2)\) distinct actual endpoints.

The lattice argument also isolates exactly what fails if faces can be large in their coordinate spans. For any finite orthogonal configuration define
\[
\Xi=\sum_{\text{fields }F}\bigl(|F\cap(F+e_x)|+|F\cap(F+e_y)|\bigr).
\]
On a generic translated lattice let \(L_{\rm same}\) be the number of lattice bonds whose two endpoints lie in the same bounded field. Every other lattice bond incident with \(S\) still crosses a fence, so \(g(k):=2k+\lceil2\sqrt k\rceil\le n+L_{\rm same}\), and averaging gives \(\mathbb E L_{\rm same}=\Xi\). For \(k>K(n)\), the successive gaps of \(g\) are at least two, so \(L_{\rm same}\ge2(k-K(n))-1\ge k-K(n)\). Hence \(A\le K(n)+\Xi\). When \(g(K(n)+1)-n\ge2\), replace \(\Xi\) by \(\Xi/2\). In particular the exact grid envelope still holds if each field has zero area overlap with both of its unit coordinate translates, a condition weaker than the original spans. This is a measured interface collision, not an assertion that such collisions can always be removed.

**Overlap correction without the span guard.** For axial fences, define the landing-overlap mass Xi as the sum, over bounded faces F, of area(F∩(F+e_x))+area(F∩(F+e_y)), where e_x and e_y are the unit axis vectors. For a lattice phase let L count nearest-neighbor sampled bonds whose endpoints lie in the same bounded face. All other bonds incident with sampled fields cross fences and inject as before. Thus 2k+ceil(2√k)≤n+L and E[L]=Xi by translation averaging. If K=K(n), then k−K≤L whenever k>K, since the first budget gap above n is at least one and all later ones at least two. Hence A≤K+Xi. If the first gap is at least two, the sharper bound A≤K+Xi/2 holds. In particular the exact grid envelope extends to any axial configuration with zero unit-translate overlap even if the coordinate spans exceed one. This isolates the geometric loss that comes from a phase counting multiple sites in one field.

Unlike the Collatz charging problem these rectangular orbit repairs have multiplicity one in the shift: the last endpoint is s−fu, so distinct admissible shifts never collide. For scaled square legs b'=gb, each extra square (b'+r)^2 is permitted when f r(2b'+r)<s and gcd(r,g)=1. Coprimality is equivalent to gcd(u,g²)=1 because u=r(2gb+r). The count of positive r below the exact real bound is (φ(g)/g)(√(m+s/f)−√m)+O(2^ω(g)) by inclusion–exclusion. Thus for fixed odd g the balanced examples give Θ_g(V²) distinct endpoints, not just many recipes for reaching a single endpoint.
