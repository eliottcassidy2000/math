# Planar shadows, five labels, and information carried by a lift

The synthesis is **marked projection**: specify the object, the data it
forgets, and which future readings still factor through it. Graph planarity,
a planar drawing with crossings, orientability of a covering surface, and
losslessness of a numerical observer are four distinct questions.

## 1. A5, K5, K3,3 and Petersen are actual transformations

Petersen is KG(5,2): vertices are the edges of K5 and disjointness is
adjacency. Equivalently it is the complement of the line graph of K5.
Label a vertex by the Sylow-3 subgroup of A5 on the complementary triple,
and an edge between ab and cd by the involution (ab)(cd). Thus its 15
edges are the 15 involutions of A5, equivariantly under conjugation.
Deleting vertex ab and suppressing its three former neighbours yields
K3,3: the surviving vertices are a-i and b-i for i among the other three
letters; original edges give unequal indices and the suppressed paths
give the three diagonal edges. Contracting any perfect matching gives
K5: a parallel edge in the cubic graph would require a triangle or a
4-cycle. There are six matchings, in equivariant bijection with
Sylow-5 subgroups; for cyclic labels mod 5 the invariant matching pairs
{i,i+1} with {i+2,i+4}. Any two matchings intersect in one edge, so the
same 15 edge handles also index pairs of six objects.

More finely, let s,t be the labels of two edges. If they meet then st
has order 3, and the 30 incident pairs exhaust that case. For disjoint
edges, delete both and suppress degree-two vertices. The result is:

| order of st | unordered pairs | projected cubic graph |
|---:|---:|---|
| 2 | 15 | K3,3 |
| 5 | 60 | triangular prism, planar |

The order-2 pairs are the commuting pairs in one of the five
Klein fours indexed by the fixed letter. The order-5 pairs lie in the
unique dihedral normalizer of the cyclic group generated by st.
On a pentagon there are two A5 orbits, indexed by the two possible
step lengths up to sign, each with 30 pairs. The planar prism forgets
that distinction. The verifier checks all 105 pairs and the two cubic
isomorphism types: a simple cubic 6-vertex graph has a 2-regular
complement, either C6 or two triangles. These give the prism and K3,3.
The labels do not make seam translations noncommutative; they remember
which edits were made on Petersen, where the product order is read.

This differs from an arbitrary projection of a 3D drawing. The
dodecahedral sphere's antipodal quotient is a 2:1 cover to Petersen
embedded in the projective plane, not a planar embedding of Petersen;
see [Zaslavsky's signed-graph treatment](https://people.math.binghamton.edu/zaslav/Tpapers/petersen.pdf).
Removing a point from the spherical lift gives a planar drawing of
the lift. Erasing its sheet labels is the operation that changes the
surface. The older Collatz Tait-sheet work explicitly shows its sign
fold is not this connected double cover.

## 2. Petersen labels the five-label switching moves

On n labels, take the F2 edge space of K_n modulo cuts: flipping a
cut is switching. The orientations are an affine space over this
quotient, dimension (n-1)(n-2)/2. For each fixed Hamilton frame P
there is a unique switched orientation with P directed forward:
prescribe switch differences along the spanning tree P, then propagate
from a starting label. The exact induced translation on the quotient
from complementing every free arc is v_P=[all edges except P].
Eligibility is automatic in this switching observer; on an actual
un-switched tournament it need not be. That difference matters.

For odd n, each edge-vector class has a unique Eulerian representative.
If D is the even-size set of its odd-degree vertices, add the cut D.
The degree parity of that cut is exactly D. A cut of an odd complete
graph can be Eulerian only when empty. At n=5, for v_P, D is the pair
of endpoints of P. The representative has four edges: the six free
edges, plus the six cut edges, less twice the four in their intersection.
It is Eulerian, hence a 4-cycle. All 15 squares occur by label symmetry,
each four times among the 60 undirected frames. Send a square to its
two missing diagonals: they are disjoint K5 edges, so this is precisely
a Petersen edge. This is not an inference from a coincidence of 15s.

At n=5 the switching move graph has two 32-vertex sheets by edge
parity. In either sheet, write z for the complete Eulerian graph
(the converse translation), and q(x)=|x|/2 mod 2 on Eulerian even
vectors. Its polar form is edge overlap mod 2. The cycle space is
orthogonal to cuts, and the unique Eulerian representative proves
the pairing on the cycle space is nondegenerate. Restricting to
the even vectors z-perp gives radical exactly <z>. Here q(z)=1;
thus each pair {x,x+z} contains exactly one q=0 state. The moves
are exactly the 15 *nonzero* q=0 states: all 15 squares have this
value, and q=0 has 16 states. Consequently each sheet projects
onto K16 after identifying converse, with exactly one of the two
possible destinations in every other converse pair. This is a
genuine graph double cover, a different one from dodecahedron → Petersen.
The quotient is the 4-dimensional symplectic vector space; the lost
coordinate is the quadratic choice of lift. In particular a triangle
of the quotient with vertices 0,u,v flips the sheet exactly when
the bilinear form on (u,v) is 1. The three edge lifts sum to z times
that pairing, by the polar identity for q. This names what is lost:
a loop can close in the shadow without closing in the actual lift.

All orders: differences v_P+v_P' span precisely the even edge
subspace before quotienting by cuts. Swap the first two of any
prescribed initial triple x,y,z to obtain the sum of the two edges
xz and yz; such pairs generate the even subspace because the line
graph of K_n is connected. A free complement has weight binom(n-1,2).
For even n a singleton cut is odd. For odd n every cut is even.
Hence there are two switching-move components exactly when n=1 mod 4,
otherwise one (n>=3). For n>=5 every component is nonplanar: select
four independent generators to obtain a Q4 subgraph, which has
32 edges on 16 vertices, more than the planar bipartite limit 28.
For n=1 mod 4 the lift also records a separate mod-8 bit:
q(z)=binom(n,2)/2 mod 2 is 1 exactly at n=5 mod 8.
These are structural statements about the full switching observer,
not classifications of the ordinary directed tournament metagraph.

## 3. A5 orbit atlases and the actual tiling metagraph

On five labels A5 acts on the 64 switching classes. Each nonidentity
involution flips the switching-invariant alternating triple sign on
a triple containing its fixed label and one swapped pair, so no
stabilizer has even order. No subgroup of order 15 exists (both
Sylow groups would be normal, yielding an element of order 15).
Thus stabilizers have sizes 1,3,5. A 3-cycle fixes four switching
classes: its four orbits of underlying edges yield 16 invariant
orientations, then quotient by four invariant cuts (three vertex
orbits). A 5-cycle also fixes four: two edge orbits and one invariant
cut, the zero cut. An invariant class has an invariant orientation
because a group of odd order admits affine averaging in characteristic
two; the difference space of switching vectors averages as usual.
Burnside gives (64+20*4+24*4)/60=4 orbits, with sizes 12,12,20,20.
A5 cannot act faithfully as arc automorphisms on a single tournament:
any nonidentity involution would reverse an arc between swapped labels.

Converse is free on these classes and preserves each orbit.
For the 5-cycle fixed classes, use inversion of the five cyclic
labels, an even permutation reversing the circulant. For the
3-cycle fixed classes, transpose two labels of the cycle and also
the two external labels. This reverses the internal triple and the
external pair; the uniform relations across the triple and the two
other vertices either already reverse when exchanged, or need just
the cut separating the triple from the pair. Therefore the 12-orbits
are A5/C5 (icosahedral vertices), and the 20-orbits A5/C3
(dodecahedral vertices). After converse they become two sets of
six Sylow-5 labels and two sets of ten Sylow-3 labels. Equipping
the 20-orbits with the dodecahedral adjacency gives the spherical
Petersen lift from section 1. This adjacency is transported from
geometry; it is NOT the switching-move adjacency of section 2.

A literal reading is the histogram of H over the 16 cut choices.
Both 12-orbits have histogram {1:5,9:5,11:5,15:1}; both 20-orbits
give {3:3,5:6,9:3,13:3,15:1}. The 9 and 15 overlap, so even exact
H cannot distinguish the two stabilizer types.

A separate exhaustive check projects the 64 five-vertex tilings
to tournament isomorphism *and converse* classes, remembering a
line for each complementary tiling pair. Dropping loops and
multiplicities gives ten vertices and 18 edges. It contains a unique
K5 and a unique K3,3 as literal subgraphs; the Petersen graph can be
constructed as disjointness on the ten edges of its intrinsic K5
(without pretending it is a spanning subgraph of the metagraph).
The **single-flip** graph instead has 21 edges and has neither
literal graph as a subgraph, though both are minors. The two
graphs share only 11 edges, so a change of move is not a refinement
by simply adding edges. The simple complement support's involution
acts as a double transposition on its K5, but loops themselves
break it. This is the difference between symmetry of a projected
diagram and symmetry of the complete data.

This simple complement support has nonorientable genus exactly one.
Remove its leaves (0,1,3 in the verifier) and contract edge 7–9.
The result is K6 minus the edge between the contracted vertex and 4.
Start with the antipodal icosahedral K6 triangulation of the
projective plane. Label the four surviving neighbours of the
contracted vertex so that {2,5} and {6,8} occupy consecutive sectors.
Split that vertex along the sectors, restore 7–9, and attach the
leaves. This is an embedding; its literal K5 already rules out the
sphere.

There are exactly three A5-equivariant identifications of the 15
Petersen edges with pairs of six Sylow-5 labels. Both actions are
A5/V4, and the normalizer of V4 is the order-12 A4 fixing its letter.
At each Petersen vertex one identification sends the star to a
matching on the six labels, namely the perfect-matching-pair map.
The other two send the star to a triangle: the stabilizer S3 has two
three-letter orbits, and its three size-three orbits of pairs of
letters are the two triangles and the invariant inter-orbit matching.
Indeed its involution fixes two labels (30 involution incidences
among the six D10 normalizers), so the action is two triples rather
than a single regular orbit. Equivariant self-bijections of the
star's S3 set of three are trivial. The replay verifies that both
triangle maps give projective-plane triangulations (vertex links C5,
two faces per edge, Euler characteristic 1) with Petersen as dual.
Their face sets partition the 20 triples of six labels. Same action,
different local geometry: confusing the three identifications would
turn triangle sides into three disjoint edges.

## 4. What a planar reader can and cannot prove

[Contact-shadow analysis](CONTACT.md) reconnects this to the exact
reader-price work. The one-vertex mixed contact bipartite graph
has positive weights from even deletion paths. Its total, plus the
deletion count, recovers H, so contracting both shores preserves H
perfectly. But two strong roots at H=69 have the same weight and
same nonplanar K3,3 support up to exchanging shores; the same
next attachment then reads 141 versus 153. A whole family has
this defect. Requiring the actual support to be planar yields
only O((log X)^4) distinct one-apex counts up to X. Conversely
one can have nonplanar contact with a repeatable ready root.
Planar shadows are not a universal exact-target construction.

There are two distinct usable projections: cut switches preserve
linear translations, not single-tournament H; weighted contact
collapse preserves H, not the next reader. A proposed proof by a
new reduced state ought to have **both** a lifting rule and a
continuation test. The next constructive target is to refine the
contact shadow with the smallest boundary data that distinguishes
its next reading, then use changing sources with the same retained
data to cover a whole absolute window. Neither the A5 symmetry nor
these bounds prove Chan–Pak.
