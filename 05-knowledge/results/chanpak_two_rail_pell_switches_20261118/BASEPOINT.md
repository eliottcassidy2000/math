# The missing six is a basepoint error

For b=2, the two-switch construction is not merely close to multiplying by seven. It is an exact multiplication on the excess above the transitive count:

    H11 − 1 = 7(H00 − 1).

It realizes the same 3–4–5 ray, but based at (1,1), not at (0,0). In mean and half-difference coordinates this reads `4D=3(M−1)` rather than `4D=3M`. As the lower count grows, the absolute error remains six, even while the slope converges. In particular, moving the origin from the transitive count to arithmetic zero is a genuine extra operation, not a normalization of the witness. The one-block no-go in this packet shows why a fixed uniform reader cannot buy that change on the entire ray.

This ties the two-rail experiment to the microscopic ladder 1→3→5→9: in both constructions the cleaner multiplicative coordinate is `H−1`. The microscopic extension doubles this excess, and the two switches together multiply it by seven on the specified family. Neither identity says an arbitrary path count can be multiplied by seven.
