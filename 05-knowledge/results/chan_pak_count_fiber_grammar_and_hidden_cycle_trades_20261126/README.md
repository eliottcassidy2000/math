# Chan–Pak as a classification of Hamiltonian-count fibers

The map \(T\mapsto H(T)\) forgets several different kinds of information. This note gives an exact finite core classification at every fixed height, full generating functions through 25, and examples where even the entire path-cover polynomial misses a change in cycle types, strong connectivity, automorphisms, or converse symmetry.

## 1. The finite core grammar

A tournament has a unique ordered list of strong components: the condensation is a transitive tournament, and every Hamiltonian path concatenates one path in each component in that fixed order. Its count is the product of component counts. Isomorphisms preserve the order, and its automorphism group is the direct product of the component groups. Conversely any ordered list of strong tournament isomorphism classes assembles uniquely by ordered join.

Delete the singleton components and call the remaining ordered list the core word \(w\). Let \(k=|w|\), and \(s(w)\) be its total vertex count. For fixed odd \(h\) the alphabet is finite: by [Busch's strong-tournament minimum](https://www.combinatorics.org/ojs/index.php/eljc/article/download/v13i1n3/pdf), a nontrivial strong component on \(m\) vertices satisfies \(H\ge5^{(m-1)/3}\), hence \(H\ge3^{m/3}\) for \(m\ge4\); at \(m=3\) its count is 3. A core of count \(h\) therefore uses at most \(\lfloor3\log_3 h\rfloor\) vertices. This gives a complete decision and classification algorithm for **each** input height, not a proof that the algorithm returns a witness except at the two gaps.

Write \(W_h\) for the finite list of ordered core words with product \(h\). This can be generated by listing strong types with counts dividing \(h\) (using the stronger per-count cutoff \(m\le1+3\log_5 H\)), then recursively appending types and dividing the residual count. The all-size ordinary generating function for isomorphism classes is
\[
F_h(t)=\sum_{w\in W_h}\frac{t^{s(w)}}{(1-t)^{k+1}}.
\]
Here the \(k+1\) slots are the gaps before, between, and after the strong components, filled by arbitrary singleton chains. The empty word gives \(F_1=1/(1-t)\), with the empty tournament included. In particular all minimum witnesses have no singleton padding; at each sufficiently large size a nonempty fiber has a counting polynomial of degree the maximum \(k\) over its words. For prime \(h\) a core word, if any, has exactly one component. At composite heights the factorization of \(h\) and the ordered component types can both be hidden.

For the user's fixed-path tiling model, a class contributes \(h/|\mathrm{Aut}(T)|\) tilings: normalization along the path identifies precisely the automorphism orbits, and the action on ordered paths is free. Replace each summand of \(F_h\) by its multiple \(h/\prod_{S\in w}|\mathrm{Aut}(S)|\) to obtain the total tiling generating function. The tiling size of a class is therefore not determined by \(h\) and the vertex count alone.

Converse reverses the component order and takes each component's converse. A self-converse tournament has a left half, its reversed-converse right half, and either no central component or one self-converse central component. With \(C_1(t)=1+t\) and \(C_e(t)\) for \(e>1\) the generating polynomial for self-converse strong types of count \(e\), the fixed-class series is
\[
B_h(t)=\sum_{d^2e=h} F_d(t^2) C_e(t).
\]
Thus for metagraph vertices formed by merging converse classes, the all-size series is \((F_h+B_h)/2\). This formula counts merged nodes, not their tiling mass.

## 2. The two empty fibers: where the budget breaks

For 7, a core must be one strong component; for 21 the only nontrivial product partition is \(3\cdot7\), already excluded once 7 is. The exact strong minima at sizes 3 through 8 are \(3,5,9,15,25,45\); for larger sizes the Busch bound already exceeds 25. In particular 7 would require at most four vertices, where the only strong counts are 3 and 5. The attempted count 21 would require at most six vertices. At size five the only strong counts are \(9,11,13,15\), so the last possible chamber is size six.

Here is a short structural certificate with a small finite check. In the [Rédei–Berge odd-cycle-pack formula](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.05569), six vertices allow only triangles, five-cycles, or a pair of vertex-disjoint triangles. Write their counts as \((a,b,d)\), so
\[
H=1+2(a+b+2d).
\]
Thus the forbidden count asks whether the cycle budget can be exactly 10. The certificate below comes from fixing a directed Hamiltonian 6-cycle, then varying only its nine chords. A strong tournament has such a cycle by Camion's theorem, so this loses no cases. The replay loops over all \(2^9\) assignments; it counts directed odd cycles once, starting each at its least label, and independently checks the weighted pack formula against a Hamiltonian subset DP at every retained isomorphism type.

| \((a,d)\) | possible \(b\) | \(b\) required for 21 |
|---|---|---:|
| (4,0) | 3, 4 | 6 |
| (4,1) | 2 | 4 |
| (5,0) | 4 | 5 |
| (5,1) | 4, 6 | 3 |
| (6,0) | 5, 6 | 4 |
| (6,1) | 6, 7 | 2 |
| (6,2) | 4, 6, 8 | 0 |
| (7,0) | 9 | 3 |
| (7,1) | 7, 9 | 1 |
| (7,2), (8,1), (8,2), (8,4) | irrelevant | negative |

At the closest row \((5,0)\), there are exactly four five-cycles and no disjoint triangle pair; an extra five-cycle would be needed. At \((5,1)\), adding the disjoint pair pushes the budget past 10 even with the minimum four five-cycles. Thus the restrictions that empty this fiber are joint restrictions: treating cycle species as independently available would not exclude 21. This is an alternative certificate for the known exclusions, not a proof that these are the only empty fibers.

## 3. A hierarchy of what an equal count can hide

Use \(\Phi_T(u_3,u_5,\dots)=\sum_\mathcal C\prod_{C\in\mathcal C}u_{|C|}\), where \(\mathcal C\) is a disjoint odd-cycle pack and the empty pack is included. It multiplies across ordered joins. The normalized path-cover polynomial is \(D_T(x)=\Phi_T(2x,2x^2,\dots)\); the colored path-cover count polynomial is \(P_T(z)=z^{|T|}D_T(z^{-2})\), and \(H=D_T(1)\). Thus one has three distinct levels: the value \(H\), all color evaluations (at fixed size), and the full cycle-pack profile. Even the last is not a complete invariant. In the following witnesses \((n,m)\) means the fixed \(0\to1\to\cdots\to n-1\to0\) cycle with the natural forward orientation of every chord except the bits of \(m\), with chord pairs in lexicographic order.

* **At 9**, the two minimum classes \((5,0),(5,1)\) are both self-converse and rigid, and are related by one arc reversal. Their identical full profile is \(1+3u_3+u_5\), but they are nonisomorphic. At size six there is also an ordered join of two directed triangles, with profile \((1+u_3)^2\). Thus the first nontrivial neutral arc can be invisible even to all odd-cycle-pack species, and at the next size the same height can conceal multiplicative decomposition.
* **At 17**, \((6,0)\) and \((6,11)\) have profiles \(1+4u_3+4u_5\) and \(1+4u_3+2u_5+u_3^2\). Both give \(D(x)=1+8x+8x^2\). Two five-cycles trade exactly against one disjoint pair of triangles, at every color count, not only at \(H\).
* **At 15**, put a source before \((5,4)\) and compare to the strong \((6,1)\). Both have six vertices and the same full profile \(1+4u_3+3u_5\), hence the same \(P_T\). Yet the former has automorphism order 3 and the latter order 1, giving respectively 5 and 15 tilings in their classes. Both witnesses belong to converse pairs: the padded witness and its converse are distinguished by source versus sink, and the strong witness fails converse isomorphism in the canonical-form test. At the minimum size five, both classes are self-converse, and their automorphism orders are 3 and 5, yielding class sizes 5 and 3.
* **At 23**, the strong profiles \(1+6u_3+5u_5\) and \(1+5u_3+4u_5+u_3^2\) have the same \(H\) but different color responses. This is the layer visible to the [earlier two-color midpoint test](../euler_zigzag_minimum_matching_and_hidden_flip_20261126/README.md), unlike the height-17 trade.

Each example propagates to any \(h\) obtained by multiplying its height by a realizable count: append the same tournament to both sides. Unique strong-component words allow right cancellation, and multiplication by a common nonzero polynomial preserves equality and inequality at the respective layers; automorphism and tiling-size ratios persist. The count-9 neutral arc remains just one reversal. The proof does not need an assumption that every quotient is realizable.

## 4. Complete class series through 25

The replay exhausts all cycle completions through size six and all size-seven completions with \(H\le25\), quotients by isomorphism (relabelings grouped by outdegree), and records both cycle-pack and automorphism data. The minimum bound cuts off larger strong components. Write \(R=1-t\). Excluding \(h=1\) above:

| \(h\) | \(F_h(t)\) |
|---:|---|
| 3 | \(t^3/R^2\) |
| 5 | \(t^4/R^2\) |
| 7, 21 | \(0\) |
| 9 | \(2t^5/R^2+t^6/R^3\) |
| 11, 13 | \(t^5/R^2\) each |
| 15 | \((2t^5+2t^6)/R^2+2t^7/R^3\) |
| 17, 19 | \(2t^6/R^2\) each |
| 23 | \(4t^6/R^2\) |
| 25 | \((2t^6+t^7)/R^2+t^8/R^3\) |

Different strong core types explain multiple smallest classes even at prime heights, e.g. 17 and 19. Multiplicativity generates another source of multiplicity, usually at larger sizes in this range. The constructive target left by Chan–Pak is to give a short core word, or one strong letter, at every allowed exact height; graded cycle budgets give a way to keep track of distinct completions without confusing their aggregate counts.
