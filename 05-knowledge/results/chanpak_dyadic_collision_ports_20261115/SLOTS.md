# Nonrotation slot charges and a half-order near-floor gap

Write f(n) for the minimum Hamiltonian-path count of a strong n-vertex tournament, and K=H−f(n). For n≥48 every positive excess satisfies **2K≥n−5**. All assertions concern strong tournaments at a fixed order, not the unrestricted Chan–Pak spectrum.

## 1. A slot charge from a nonrotation projection

For an exact-floor Busch split A,B at v, read their unique cycles after v as A_1,...,A_p and B_1,...,B_q. If the local surplus L is smaller than n−2 then every cross arc follows the Ferrers interleaving W, by [exact seam rigidity](../chanpak_exact_seams_douglas_20261115/README.md). For an interior slot of the A-list, define its charge as follows. For every Hamiltonian path of A, count one if, in the portion strictly before v, some consecutive edge goes backward across the slot in the A-list order. Count another if the same holds in its portion strictly after v. Take the sum over all those paths. The two exterior slots have charge zero by definition.

**Slot bound.** Fix A and sum each A slot charge times the number of B vertices in that slot of W. The result is at most L. One may also use the bound with A and B exchanged (we do not add those two symmetric inequalities).

For one A path and a B vertex B_j lying between the ranks of a consecutive backward step after v, take the B cycle rotation having exactly j vertices after v. In the anchored post-v shuffle DP, the step gives adjacent rows with entries 0,1 at column j (the cross bit is 1 when A beats B). At the second row both end states are positive: the upper row's 0 forces its A-end state positive, which persists down the column, while the lower row's 1 forces its B-end state positive. The defect propagates down to the final row in this column. Indeed if the A state is already at least two, it persists; otherwise with both states positive above, the next bit 0 sums them into the A state, while bit 1 preserves the A state and makes the B state positive from the adjacent left cell. Thus that rotation fiber has at least two post-v shuffles. The reflected before-v argument uses the B rotation with j−1 vertices after v: reverse both lists, take the converse, and swap the sides. For a fixed A path, distinct B vertices use different rotations within each half, and the two half charges add since a fiber of size xy contributes xy−1≥(x−1)+(y−1). This establishes the slot bound. Every rotation choice used is a valid factor Hamiltonian path; the surplus from broken projections is separate.

## 2. Exact-floor chain slot geometry

Use Moon–Yang special chains: each link is a nearly transitive tournament of order 3, 4 or 5, successive links share a junction, and arcs between their off-junction parts go forward in natural chain order. There is at most one order-5 link and at most two order-3 links, with no order-3 links when an order-5 link occurs. Write U⋆V for this concatenation. The product identity gives a bijection between factor Hamiltonian paths and paths of the chain. Explicitly, with common vertex t, paths P_U,t,Q_U and P_V,t,Q_V merge uniquely into P_U,P_V,t,Q_U,Q_V; concatenation is allowed because all cross-arcs go forward. Directed edges within one such portion stay consecutive. The unique chain cycle lists all junctions in reverse (including the two extreme marks) and then all interiors in natural order.

**Bottom root.** Let t be the shared vertex between the last link Q and the preceding chain P, and let v be the overall bottom root. The rooted cycle list is t followed by P’s cycle after t, then the interiors of Q. If P is nonempty, z denotes its last interior vertex, the predecessor of t on P’s cycle. Every slot between t and z is positive and costs at least H(P'), where P' removes the final link of P (cost 1 if no links remain). To see this, choose the final-link cycle rotation containing the edge z→t; the two ends of that edge are not its left junction, so arbitrary earlier P' paths preserve the edge at the exact join. Choose the Q cycle path starting at t. In the whole merge, the consecutive z→t step occurs before v and straddles every indicated slot. Conversely, all slots after z are neutral: all P vertices beat the Q interiors, which are transitive. If Q is the only link, every slot is neutral. Top root is the converse. For the bottom root every positive charge is at least H(A)/45: the two fixed link factors multiply to at most 45.

**Junction root.** For v shared between two links split the chain into L_0⋆R_0 at v. The rooted cycle reads the whole L_0 list followed by the whole R_0 list. Exact path merging never separates edges strictly on one side of v in either factor, so a positive slot within one block receives its smaller-factor charge times the other factor count. The middle slot is neutral because cross-arcs all go forward. Conversely, no additional backwards witness can straddle a previously neutral slot: every backwards edge is internal to one factor, with its endpoints on the same side of the projected path’s v if they were on the same side in the whole path. In particular each positive slot costs at least H(A)/45. All neutral interior slots lie in one central interval extending from the last interior of the left exterior chain (the chain after dropping its v-adjacent link) to the first interior of the right exterior chain, when those wings exist. In particular every neutral interior slot is **after all vertices of the left exterior chain** and **before all vertices of the right exterior chain** in this rooted cycle list. For an overall extreme root regard the absent half as empty.

Every neutral slot for a junction or extreme root gives a uniform cut X→Y in the off-root factor, where X precedes the slot and Y follows it. This follows directly from the bottom/top descriptions and the one-way split at a junction.

**Interior root.** Suppose v is an interior vertex of Q in a chain A=L_0⋆Q⋆R_0. The top and bottom marks of Q are t,u respectively. Put z equal to the last interior of L_0 if that wing exists, and t otherwise. Put w equal to the first interior of R_0 if that wing exists, and u otherwise. The W-list reads, in order, the later Q interiors, R_0 interiors, the marks from the far right down to the far left (in particular u and then t), L_0 interiors, and the earlier Q interiors.

If both wings exist, choose the Q path (u,t,all Q interiors). Choose L_0 ending with z,t and choose R_0 with a nonempty prefix before u starting at w. To enforce these, fix only the final L_0 link and initial R_0 link to cycle paths: for the former use its natural top-start path ending z,t; for the latter take its cycle rotation starting w and ending u. At the outer exact joins the required end adjacency or first vertex persists, regardless of all exterior paths. First merge Q with R_0 at u, then L_0 at t; the two prefixes juxtapose to give a consecutive z→w before v. Thus all slots between w and z are charged. If L_0 is absent and R_0 present, use Q’s path (all interiors,u,t) and the R_0 path beginning u,w (fix only its first link to its top-start cycle path), giving t→w after v. If R_0 alone is absent use the converse.

If later Q interiors exist, take Q’s path (earlier interiors,v,u,t,later interiors) and L_0 ending at t, achieved by fixing its final link as above. The step t→first later interior persists after merging both wings (the join at u leaves that step within a suffix, and there is no L_0 suffix after t). It is after v and covers the whole front tail, overlapping the central span covering down to t. By converse an earlier-interior tail is covered too. If there are no wings, the same tail steps cover every slot for order 4 or 5; for a lone triangle all slots are neutral and the remainder is transitive. Each witness for a multi-link chain fixes at most Q and one link of each wing; the product of these fixed counts is at most 9·5·5=225. Every interior slot for such an interior root therefore costs at least H(A)/225. For the single-link exceptions any positive slot charge is trivially ≥H(A)/225.

**Summary.** For any exact-floor A, every nonzero slot charge is at least H(A)/225, and neutral slots always have X→Y. In a large B at a junction or extreme root a low-surplus interleaving can place off-root A vertices only in the central neutral interval or outside the whole B list. For v interior all A vertices must be outside the whole B list if the surplus is smaller than H(B)/225.

## 3. A large wing cannot carry a faulty projection at a neutral cut

Assume n≥48, and suppose a strong tournament has 0<K<(n−5)/2. Outside the existing stability band K<2f(n)/25 the bound is automatic (using f(n+3)=5f(n)). Inside it, consider a deepest nonfloor node U of order m. Its multiplier w is at least 1+(n−m). If 9≤m<n, then the [linear cycle budget](CYCLE_BUDGET.md) on U gives
```
w K_U ≥ (m−2)(n−m+1)/3 ≥ 2(n−3)/3 > (n−5)/2.
```
The quadratic takes its minimum at an endpoint m=9 or n−1, and n≥48. If m≤8, parity gives wK_U≥2(n−7) instead. Thus the root has exact-floor factors, and its L is K. The unique-cycle Douglas bound rules out C=1 in this range.

The [broken projection argument](../chanpak_second_gap_20261115/README.md) for L<(n−1)/2 gives a bad Hamiltonian cycle with precisely one invalid projected A arc (A has order a≥4), followed across a B-block D; the B projection is valid. The number r of B−v vertices outside D satisfies r≤L+1−a, so a≤L+1 and b=n−a+1≥n−L. The seam is Ferrers. We use f(j)≥45j for j≥13 (check three bases j=13,14,15 then multiply by 5 upon adding three), so H(B)/45>L and also H(B)/225>L here: indeed b≥27, and f(j)≥225j for j≥17 by the same three-base check. Hence B slot localization applies.

If v is an interior B root, the whole B list lies in one A slot. This slot is neutral since b−1≥n−L−1>L and a positive charge would charge every B vertex. Thus write A’s off-root vertices as X→Y around this full B block. If both X,Y nonempty, the A bad cycle read from v must have all X before all Y: a Y→X consecutive projected step cannot be a valid arc, and can't be supported by a B block because Ferrers direction forbids a detour from Y through this block to X. A B segment cannot support a bad arc within X or Y (wrong side), across the single X→Y boundary (an actual arc), or at a root gap (which touches the first X or last Y, so wrong cross direction). Contradiction. If one group is empty the seam is one-way between the factors; all B vertices necessarily occur in one block because the cycle cannot cross in the reverse direction outside v. An invalid gap on A is then root-incident with D=B−v. Its strong-R replacement gives b−1>L, again a contradiction.

Suppose v is a B junction or extreme. Choose the longer B exterior wing P and reflect if necessary so it is left, attaching at its natural bottom t. This omits the v-adjacent link on either available side. The sum of their order costs is at most 7, so writing p=|P|−1,
```
p ≥ (n−a−7)/2.
```
In the rooted B list, the t plus P−t block begins at the very first slot after v and precedes all neutral interior slots. Every vertex of P−t therefore sits in one A slot. If this slot had positive charge, it would give at least p extras in valid projection fibers. These are disjoint from the ℓ=a+r−1 broken rotations of the bad cycle. Therefore L≥p+ℓ≥(n+a+2r−9)/2≥(n−5)/2, since a≥4. Thus the slot is neutral.

Moreover D must meet P−t, because
```
p−r ≥ (n+a−9−2L)/2 > 0.
```
Again write X,Y for the two sides of this neutral A slot. If both nonempty, the bad A projection must have no Y→X consecutive step: it is not a valid A arc (neutrality gives X→Y), and Ferrers chronology forbids detouring from a later A vertex through any consecutive B segment to an earlier A vertex. Thus the A list has X before Y. No invalid gap can include a P vertex: that wing lies strictly between the two groups, so an internal gap must cross X→Y, which is valid; and a root gap neighboring first X or last Y has the wrong cross direction. This contradicts D meeting P−t.

If all A are before P−t, slot localization forces them before the entire B list; here P has at least two links, and all its interior rooted slots before its last interior carry positive charge. Then the whole seam is one-way, contradicted by the same root-gap strong-R argument. The only remaining possibility has all off-root A vertices after P. Then P−t uniformly beats the rest of T−t: its arcs to A−v follow chronology, and its arcs to B beyond t follow the natural chain join. Removing P−t leaves a strong T' formed from A and the remaining B chain meeting in v. The new split P,T' is one-way, and its floor product is f(n): the remaining leaf budget is the old floor multiset with the removed links of P discarded (any submultiset of the admissible 3/4/5 floor multiset has exact floor product, by the cost and exponent formula).

If T' is nonfloor, Busch multiplication gives K≥2H(P). Otherwise the [one-way surplus formula](../chanpak_oneway_seams_20261115/README.md) gives either K=0 or K≥(5/9)H(P): the left factor P is marked at its overall bottom t and has no bad-prefix covers there, so its only remaining coefficient is H(P)−E_P(t)≥(5/9)H(P). Here p≥(n−a−7)/2≥(n−L−8)/2>n/4−11/4, so H(P)≥10(p+1)>9L/5 for n≥48 (check f(10),f(11),f(12), then recurse). Either lower bound contradicts 0<K<(n−5)/2. This proves the claimed fixed-order bound.
