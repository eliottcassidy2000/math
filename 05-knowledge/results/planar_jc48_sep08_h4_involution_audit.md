## 3. Arbitrary subgroups and the quotient direction

Let T be the common sixty-element reflection class. For every proper
subgroup H of W, K=<H intersect T> lies in H, contains every element
of H intersect T, and is therefore a proper reflection subgroup with

    K intersect T=H intersect T.

This is an exact equality. The argument does not assume H itself is
generated by reflections, parabolic, maximal, a point stabilizer in
the real representation, or faithful in a later permutation action.
The classification hence bounds |H intersect T| by16. Alternatively,
the direct closed-set enumeration suffices, as checked in §4 below.

For a transitive action write its labels as left cosets W/H. A coset
gH is fixed by a reflection s precisely when g^-1sg lies in H. Each
reflection in H has |C_W(s)| such conjugators, so the fixed-coset
count is |H intersect T|*|C_W(s)|/|H|. Dividing by the degree
D=|W|/|H| gives exactly

    |Fix(s)|/D=|H intersect T|/60.

This proves the ratio without a faithfulness hypothesis. D>=2 means
H is proper; an empty reflection intersection is allowed.

An involutive Artin H4 meridian representation factors from the
Artin group through its quotient obtained by imposing the four
generator squares. That quotient is the stated Coxeter W(H4), so
there is a homomorphism *from W onto the actual image*. Extra
relations or a nontrivial kernel do not invalidate the transitive
coset argument. There is no unsupported reverse map from an
arbitrary finite image to the Coxeter group.

    1=ND+(-2N-2)k+sum n_i+N(2k-D)+sum omega
