## 2. The actual forest and sheet consumer

Write `D_v={x mod 1: ||vx||<1/14}`. At a translated grid
`x_j=(j+alpha)/t`, let `S_j` be the set of dangerous tail labels. If `F` is
any forest on the actual seven labels, its induced edges on `S_j` number at
most `max(|S_j|-1,0)`. Summing gives

```
number of weak-safe grid points
  >= t - sum_i |D_ui on the grid| + sum_{ij in F}|D_ui intersect D_uj|.
```

For `d_i=gcd(t,u_i)`, the first sum is at most
`sum_i d_i ceil(t/(7d_i))`. Define its excess over t by `E_t(d)`.
Consequently any actual forest of uniform pair credits with total strictly
larger than `E_t(d)` guarantees at least one weak-safe lift at every alpha.
All credits are nonnegative, so a maximum spanning tree of **known actual**
pair lower bounds is legitimate. This does not replace the MINIMUM tree used
when only independently possible edge labels of an unknown graph are known.

For a pair write `(u,v)=D(p,q)`, `gcd(p,q)=1`, `e=gcd(t,D)`, `n=t/e`.
Multiplication by `D/e` permutes the n residue labels; every residue occurs e
times. Therefore its count is

```
e * C_{n,p,q}((D/e)alpha),
C_{n,p,q}(beta) = #{0<=j<n: (j+beta)/n in D_p intersect D_q}.
```

The uniform credit is exactly `e min_beta C_{n,p,q}(beta)`; the common phase
multiplier cannot be dropped in a pointwise identity, but disappears from
this all-translation minimum. This calculation applies to every primitive
ratio, including endpoint products outside the 5855-edge strict atlas.

An actual path `i--k--j` with `u_i/u_k=a/b`, `u_j/u_k=c/d` has endpoint ratio
`ad/(bc)`. Primitive normalization of `(a/b,1,c/d)` gives an integer triple
R with gcd one. Its actual common multiplier h satisfies
`gcd(t,h)=gcd(d_i,d_k,d_j)`. Thus the path recovers both the endpoint ratio
and its exact sheet gcd; non-clock factors of h cannot change a uniform
credit. On three labels any two of the three pairs form a forest, so the sum
of the largest two certified pair credits is valid.

This is the connection contract: actual directed path -> actual endpoint
