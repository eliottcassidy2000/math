## Theorem and coordinate dictionary

Fix p>=5 prime, k>=2, and R=(Z/p^k)[delta]/(delta^2-Delta), where v_p(Delta)=1. Put m=2k, h=(p-1)/2, n=p^(k-1), and dimension N=p^k-1. The clock is g=(1+delta)/(1-delta). The named action O_k=C_n x C_p is generated by scalar 1+p and alpha(delta)=(1+p^(k-1))delta.

There is a common unsigned gauge in which:

1. Each valuation d=0,...,m-1 has h named cycles of length p^j, j=ceil((m-d)/2)-1. Representatives are delta^d u(1+p)^i, u=1,...,h and i modulo p^j.
2. The two top valuations d=0,1 have shifts a and a+p^(k-2)b respectively. Every lower block has shift a.
3. A nontrivial ideal cut delta^r, 1<=r<m, is a module cut iff every block with d<r has original cyclic period dividing p^c, c=floor((r-d-1)/2). Blocks d>=r are unrestricted.

**Proof of representatives and gauge.** In R/(delta^ell), nontrivial principal scalar displacements have even delta-valuation, while nontrivial powers of g have odd valuation: respectively 2+2v_p(a) and 1+2v_p(t), until zero. These standard binomial valuations hold for p>=5. Thus the principal scalar and clock subgroups intersect trivially. Their orders are p^(ceil(ell/2)-1) and p^floor(ell/2), whose product is the size p^(ell-1) of the principal unit group. They commute and therefore give its direct product. Choosing u=1,...,h selects one from each antipodal residue pair. At valuation d use ell=m-d; the representatives above consequently enumerate each antipodal clock orbit once.

The leading nonzero delta-adic residue chooses the positive side u rather than -u. Scalar 1+p and alpha preserve this residue, so the gauge is invariant under both generators. Addition by delta^r preserves it whenever d<r, so the same zero assignment belongs to EVERY ideal-cut locus; it is not a separate incompatible gauge for each cut.

**Proof of action.** Alpha fixes even powers of delta and multiplies odd powers by 1+p^(k-1), modulo the relevant truncation. On d=1 this equals (1+p)^(p^(k-2)) modulo p^k. On d>=2 its displacement vanishes. Hence precisely the named top-B shift is exceptional.

**Proof of cuts.** A delta^r coset module requires the orientation on R outside delta^r to descend to R/(delta^r), with the inside unrestricted. In the quotient, valuation d<r retains exactly the same u and scalar coordinate i modulo p^(ceil((r-d)/2)-1). Thus descent is exactly the stated period condition, with no signed offset or identification between different d or u. Conversely these conditions give descent and hence every ideal coset is a module. By the inherited canonical ideal-module theorem, a binary bank is prime iff NONE of these cuts holds. This last equivalence is the explicitly inherited dependency, not derived from period counting alone.

## Exact finite-state completion counts

Order blocks as in112: h top-A, h top-B, then lengths p^j for j=0,...,k-2, each with h copies of valuation m-2j-2 followed by h of valuation m-2j-1. Copies are named and not permuted.

Retain the 112 block stabilizer H, one of k+2 possibilities, and S subset {1,...,m-1}, the cuts compatible with completed blocks. Start with full S. For a block at valuation d and original period p^v, replace

    S by {r in S : d>=r or v<=floor((r-d-1)/2)}.

If the image of H on this block consists of shifts divisible by p^q, the exact-period orbit count is

    W_v / p^max(0,v-q),
    W_0=2; W_v=2^(p^v)-2^(p^(v-1)) for v>0.

For v<=q, H already fixes the word and remains unchanged. For v>q impose shift divisible by p^v, using the 112 subgroup transition. This differs from blindly treating packet period 1 as original period p^q.

Let T(i,H,S) be the number of prime suffix orbits. Terminal T equals 1 iff S is empty, otherwise 0. Sum each exact-period orbit count times its successor T. This is legitimate blockwise stabilizer decomposition: each canonical current-block representative has exactly the stated stabilizer and cut update, so all representatives of a period class share their continuation count. No independence of the shared scalar a is assumed.

## Threshold counts and constructive indexing

For block length L=p^j, integer threshold t, and v<=q, put

    B_v=2^(p^v), R_v=(2^L-1)/(B_v-1),
    E_v(t)=B_v-clamp(ceil(t/R_v),0,B_v).

Words of period dividing p^v are exactly the numerical repetitions x R_v, 0<=x<B_v. Thus E_v counts such words >=t; the exact-period count is E_0 or E_v-E_(v-1). These words are fixed under H, so each is one orbit.

For v>q, pack p^q binary digits into one symbol. The alphabet is A=2^(p^q), the packet-word length is p^(j-q), and original period p^v is equivalent to exact packet period p^(v-q). Use the 112 threshold automaton `necklace_ge_counts` for these positive packet exponents. Its period 1 entry is NOT used; it has been replaced by the finer original-period counts above.

Multiply the numbers of current representatives below t in each period class by their suffix T values. This gives an exact cumulative count. Binary search selects each block; subtract its preceding count and update H,S. Ranking sums the same preceding counts. Terminal S empty enforces primeness, and the same ordered block lexicographic canonical representative as 112 is used. The two routines are mutual inverses on the prime O_k orbits.

## Polynomial bit cost

There are O(pk) blocks, k+2 stabilizers, at most 2^(2k-1) masks, and at most k period classes per transition. Since p>=5,

    2^(2k-1) <= (N+1)^(log_5 4).

Thus the table size and its exact-integer construction cost are polynomial in N. Counts have O(N) bits. Every threshold automaton has at most N states and uses alphabet weights symbolically, never enumerating its potentially exponential alphabet. Its powers and traces have O(N)-bit entries (a coarse polynomial bit bound also suffices); polynomially many arithmetic operations suffice. Across one rank/unrank query there are O(N) threshold evaluations from the binary searches. Explicit word checks, period recovery, and subgroup transitions are polynomial as well. No enumeration of 2^N banks is used in production rank/unrank. This proves polynomial bit cost in the explicit binary bank dimension. No assertion of polynomial complexity in k, nor a Q-colored tournament theorem, is made.
