## A1. The three prime triples have exact, different definitions

| Set | Defining predicate and mechanism | What the set does not encode |

for odd n>=9, a=(n+3)/2, b=(n−3)/2 give

    F_a L_b = F_n + 2(−1)^b,

## A2. Exact modular context for the native sibling generator

Use U(n)=oddpart(3n+1) on positive odd integers and S(n)=4n+1. Then

    3S^j(n)+1 = 4^j(3n+1),       U(S^j(n))=U(n).             (1)

Thus all these integers share their next accelerated state, independently
of whether that state is known to reach1. In source-index coordinates
n=2m+1, the sibling map is F(m)=4m+2, and

    F^j(m)=4^j m+2(4^j−1)/3,
    F^j(m)−m=(3m+2)(4^j−1)/3.                              (2)

For an odd modulus q, F is a permutation, and the exact period at m is

    ord_{3q/gcd(3m+2,3q)}(4),                              (3)

with order1 at modulus1. Indeed q divides (2) iff 3q divides
(3m+2)(4^j−1); cancel their gcd. Since 3m+2 is prime to3,
q=3 gives period3. For primes p!=2,3 the map is conjugate to multiplication
by4 via m -> m+2/3 modulo p. Its fixed residue is −2/3; every other residue
has period ord_p(4).

| Modulus | Possible source periods |
|---|---|
| 3 | 3 |
| 5 | 1,2 |
| 7 | 1,3 |
| 11 | 1,5 |
| 13 | 1,6 |
| 105=3·5·7 | 3,6 |
| 195=3·5·13 | 3,6 |
| 1365=3·5·7·13 | 3,6 |
| 33=3·11 | 3,15 |

The two requested odd triples meet in the exact identity

    (4^6−1)/3 = 1365 = 3·5·7·13,
    4^6−1 = (2^6−1)(2^6+1) = 63·65.                      (4)

Consequently F^6 is the identity modulo105 and195 for **every source**.
This is a genuine common-future map, not a conclusion from the triples'
cardinalities. The 3-adic division in (2) matters: ord_3(4)=1 does not make
the affine map have period1 modulo3. The missing factor3 comes from the
affine translation, exactly the coordinate lost in slope-only reasoning.

The prime2 behaves differently. For every j>=1,

    v2(F^j(m)−m)=v2(3m+2),                                (5)

because (4^j−1)/3 is odd. Thus modulo2^a a source returns iff
3m+2=0 mod2^a; that residue is already fixed. Every residue eventually
enters it, since 3F^j(m)+2=4^j(3m+2). Modulo66 this contraction is combined
with periods3 or15 modulo33. This explains a precise difference between
{2,3,11} and the two all-odd triples: a contracting modular coordinate
versus entirely permutational coordinates. It does not claim conjugacy
of the corresponding prime classifications.

## A3. Choose context so the first alias is part of the source certificate

The prior is

    nu_r(m)=(m+1)^(−r)−(m+2)^(−r),          r>0.

Fix a source m and j>=1, and choose the **source-adapted modulus**

    q=q_j(m)=(3m+2)(4^j−1)/3.                              (6)

It satisfies q>m, tends to infinity with j, and m+q=F^j(m).
These contexts can be genuinely nested: choose j=1,2,4,8,..., since
q_{2j}(m)=(4^j+1)q_j(m). Every source remains marked in every cell and
each refinement has its own authenticated first alias. This does not
assume any ROOT score is positive. The sequence is generally not dyadic.
The prior's elementary alias
estimates hold for every integer modulus, so they need no new Haar claim.
All measurements below concern ordinary positive integer source indices.

Let 0<=V(k)<=1 be a specified score field. Suppose a checked native rule
supplies the domination

    V(m+q)<=gamma V(m),                 0<=gamma<=1.        (7)

For the ROOT indicator, gamma=1 by (1). An important second application
is the inherited fixed-price score f_theta, 0<theta<1. On a rooted n>1 it
is theta^K(1−theta)^L, with K=sum floor((a_i−1)/2), L=tau−1; on an
unrooted source it is0, and f_theta(1)=1. Appending j sibling steps changes
the first valuation by2j and leaves the accelerated future unchanged.
Hence

    f_theta(S^j(n))=theta^j f_theta(n),                    (8)

including n=1 by direct first-hit calculation. Thus gamma=theta^j is
known without knowing K,L or deciding rootedness. The beta-integrated
score W does **not** inherit an exact constant multiplier: integration
retains its theta dependence. It does satisfy W(S^j(n))<=W(n), so gamma=1
is valid for the domination (7). Its stronger existing section inequalities
remain available separately.

Write

    M_q = sum_{k>=0} nu_r(m+kq) V(m+kq),
    A = nu_r(m)+gamma nu_r(m+q).

Then M_q<=A V(m)+R_q, and all remaining terms have k>=2.
There is equality when (7) is an equality. The elementary
integral kernel gives

    0 <= R_q <= r q^(−r−1) sum_{k>=2} k^(−r−1).

Separating k=2 and bounding the rest by the integral from2 to infinity yields

    R_q <= (r+2)/(2^(r+1) q^(r+1)).                        (9)

Therefore any **independently justified** lower measurement L_q<=M_q gives

    V(m) >= max(0, [L_q−(r+2)/(2^(r+1)q^(r+1))]/A).        (10)

For the new r=6 prior this is

    V(m) >= max(0, [L_q−1/(16q^7)] /
                         [nu_6(m)+gamma nu_6(m+q)]).       (11)

The rational error constant has improved from7 to1/16, a factor112, at
the **same chosen modulus**. This is a scoped improvement using (7), not
a claim about every fixed dyadic context or every score. In particular,
L_q>1/(16q^7) is now a sufficient independent positivity target along the
cofinal sequence (6). It is not supplied by this theorem.

If every remaining alias score is at most B_0, multiply the error term
by B_0. For f_theta one can use B_0=theta, since a first-hit
certificate for n>1 has K>=1. For the beta-integrated W, the inherited bound
W(n)<=1/3 for n>1 gives the directly applicable floor

    W(2m+1) >= max(0, [L_q−1/(48q^7)] /
                                [nu_6(m)+nu_6(m+q)]),     (11b)

where L_q is a lower measurement of the nu_6-weighted W scores in the
same cell. Here W is evaluated on odd integers, while the generic V above
is indexed by m. Every off-source alias is nonroot as an *integer label*
(greater than1); this does not assert that it lies outside the ROOT basin.
The bound on W applies whether it is rooted or not.

For fixed m and q=q_j(m)->infinity, the exact remaining capacity satisfies

    q^7 sum_{k>=2} nu_6(m+kq) -> 6(zeta(7)−1).             (12)

The removed “1” is the first term of the Dirichlet series, corresponding
to an actual witnessed sibling. This is the substantive zeta connection:
**native coalescence deletes a known alias from the error series**.
No assertion about the rationality of zeta(7) is used.

### A general finite-context compiler

Suppose C is a finite set in {m+kq:k>=0}, containing m, and checked rules
supply V(c)<=gamma_c V(m), gamma_m=1, 0<=gamma_c<=1. Put

    A_C=sum_{c in C} nu_r(c) gamma_c,
    B_C=sum_{c in C, c!=m} nu_r(c).

If U_q bounds all off-source prior mass, then

    V(m) >= max(0, [L_q−U_q+B_C]/A_C).                     (13)

Each included address is counted once, even if it has several receipts.
For gamma_c=1, enlarging C weakly improves this lower bound at fixed
L_q,U_q: writing a=nu_r(m), B=B_C, the unclipped expression is
(L_q−U_q+B)/(a+B), with derivative
(a−L_q+U_q)/(a+B)^2>=0 because L_q<=M_q<=a+U_q.
For other multipliers, the exact formula (13) remains valid; that particular
monotonicity argument is not asserted without additional conditions.

The whole sibling subsequence F^{tj}(m) stays in the same cell (6), at
alias indices 0,1,4^j+1,4^(2j)+4^j+1,... . Finite prefixes therefore give
further exact improvements via (13). Their rapid growth explains why the
first alias is the main gain for a steep prior. Taking a countable cohort
requires its convergent sums, not counting infinitely many descriptions
as independent mass.

**Hostile and scope.** If the source and its witnessed cohort are unrooted,
all their scores vanish. Even certifying every other alias gives at most
the remaining capacity in (9), so cannot pass (10). This checks the
inequality direction and shows precisely what remains to be proved.
The adversarial score field is a test of this measurement interface, not
a constructed counterexample to the fixed Collatz operator. Existing
MISTAKE-entries prohibit promoting such an observer obstruction into a
universal impossibility theorem.

### Which further aliases can a fixed common-future word bank absorb?

There is a sharp boundary, obtained by specializing the inherited affine
word law rather than declaring all route switching impossible. Fix j and
an alias index k, put B=4^j, and let n=2m+1 vary. Its kth alias in (6) is

    n_k=A_k n+(A_k−1)/3,       A_k=1+k(B−1).                (23)

A fixed pair of native valuation words has rational affine endpoint maps
with slopes 3^ell/2^a. If their endpoints agree at n_k and n for two
distinct sources, the affine maps agree identically. Their slope ratio
forces A_k=3^u 2^v for integers u,v. But A_k is a positive integer prime
to3, so u=0 and A_k=2^v with v>=0. Since A_k=1 modulo2^(2j)−1, the order
of2 modulo that number is2j; hence v=2jt. Therefore

    k=(B^t−1)/(B−1),       t>=0.                          (24)

Conversely (24) gives n_k=S^(jt)(n), so the one-step sibling identity
supplies common-future words on every fixed first-valuation cylinder.
Thus (24) classifies exactly the alias positions where a fixed word pair
can agree identically on an infinite source family. For every other k,
each fixed word pair can authenticate at most one source. A finite bank
of them cannot cover infinitely many sources at that alias.

In particular aliases2 and3 cannot be filled uniformly by a fixed finite
bank in this fixed context family. At j=1 their first successors are
7U(n) and5U(n), respectively; a prime multiplier survives instead of
cancelling. This pinpoints the next reroute: use genuinely source-dependent
word selection, alter the context family, or obtain an analytic joint
measurement. Countably many word pairs can still cover all integers;
this lemma does not exclude that possibility or prove noncoalescence of
any positive pair. The carry and actual native guards remain necessary
where the slope obstruction vanishes.

## A4. Why ordinary prime sieving cannot replace the native receipt

For an arbitrary fixed q>m, set W_d=M_{dq}, and let P be squarefree.
Knowing all W_d for d|P determines, by Boolean inclusion–exclusion, the
score mass in each signature

    {k>=0: p|k exactly for p in J},        J subset {p:p|P}.

The source k=0 has the all-divisible signature, shared by every multiple
of P. Its signature mass is exactly W_P. In the relaxed model of arbitrary
scores in[0,1], all these measurements together yield the sharp interval

    max(0,[W_P−E_{Pq}(m)]/nu_r(m)) <= V(m)
                 <= min(1,W_P/nu_r(m)).                   (14)

Other signatures cannot improve it: leave their scores unchanged and
assign one constant score to all nonzero aliases in the all-divisible
cell. Their total capacity is E_{Pq}; every value allowed by (14) is
attained. Actual Collatz equations or prescribed score covariances are
additional information, exactly what A3 contributes.

More generally, any periodic kernel f of period P with f(0)=1 also has
f(kP)=1. Even a signed kernel has positive error capacity at least
E_{Pq}; for a nonnegative kernel the unique minimal Boolean choice is
f(k)=1_{P|k}. Thus a complicated finite wheel cannot beat its fully refined
cell at fixed retained congruence information.

There is also a useful positive identity, with its source loss visible:

    sum_{d|P} mu(d) W_d
       = sum_{k>=1, gcd(k,P)=1} nu_r(m+kq) V(m+kq).         (15)

The coefficient of V(m) is sum_{d|P}mu(d)=0 when P>1. Sieving away the
multiples of every selected prime deletes the source itself. Restoring it
requires an additional marked-source term, not an uncharged convention.

The corresponding unweighted tail constants are exact Euler products:

    sum_{k>=1, gcd(k,P)=1} k^(−s)
       = zeta(s) product_{p|P}(1−p^(−s)),  s>1.           (16)

At s=7, the three proposed triples give P=66,105,195. They fit the same
formula without privileging any set of three primes. At s=2 the full
product is 1/zeta(2)=6/pi^2; this is the squarefree-density constant.
Here s=7 weights *alias indices*, whereas squarefree density tests p^2|n
under uniform integer sampling. Matching Euler products does not identify
the probability spaces. Equation (16) follows from absolute convergence
and unique prime factorization; it makes no irrationality assumption.

## A5. Odd zeta values: the useful arithmetic analogue and its bill

The old bound7/q^7 was already a rational envelope for an exact
6 zeta(7) asymptotic constant. For any K>=1 the same integral test supplies
an entirely rational bound

    E_q(m) <= [r sum_{k=1}^K k^(−r−1)+K^(−r)]/q^(r+1).    (17)

At r=6,K=2 this is97/(16q^7). The much stronger (11) uses native
information; merely estimating zeta(7) more accurately does not achieve it.

For integer r and q>m+2, expansion of the same kernel gives the convergent
identity, with a=m+1,b=m+2,

    E_q(m)=sum_{h>=1} (−1)^(h+1) binom(r+h−1,h)
                         (b^h−a^h) zeta(r+h)/q^(r+h).    (18)

This explains the full adjacent zeta ladder: r=6 starts with zeta7,
zeta8,zeta9, not exclusively odd values. A reflected analytic companion

    sum_{k>=1} [(kq+a)^(−r)−(kq+b)^(−r)
                    +(kq−b)^(−r)−(kq−a)^(−r)]

cancels the even h coefficients. For even r it contains only odd zeta
values, with coefficients2 binom(r+h−1,h)(b^h−a^h), h odd. All four
arguments are positive under the displayed q condition. This is an exact
parity projection of an analytic kernel. The reflected half is not an
extra family of positive Collatz sources in the original one-sided cell.

The relevant primary arithmetic model is
[Zudilin, Proposition5 and Theorem3, pp.34–35](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0206176):
a cleared-denominator growth exponent must be strictly smaller than the
analytic decay exponent. The stated theorem concerns at least one of
zeta5,zeta7,zeta9,zeta11. It does not single out zeta7.
The repo's [odd-zeta audit, §2](odd_zeta_parallels_petersen_20261001.md)
already found a proposed improvement whose claimed prime-valuation gain
exceeded its elementary maximum. We retain that correction instead of
reusing its numerical constants.

For Collatz, the parallel obligation is not simply “a nonzero readout.”
It is a *positive readout above the complete alias and measurement-error
bill*, such as (11). Irrationality gives no sign or source-dependent rate
for L_q. Nor are the unknown infinite score sums proved to lie in a
specified rational lattice. A denominator argument must establish that
lattice for the actual readout before using integer separation. The
[existing shell-denominator audit](collatz_shell_denominator_budget_20261005.md)
already explains why integral polynomial coefficients alone do not do so.
No recently posted individual odd-zeta claim is assumed here.

## A6. Which probability space do the prime factors live in?

A precise bridge to the incoming arithmetic weights is available. Put
s=r+1 and normalize the off-source prior weights, without ROOT filtering:

    P_q(k)=nu_r(m+kq)/E_q(m), k>=1,
    pi_s(k)=k^(−s)/zeta(s).

The integral kernel bounds the ratio of nu_r(m+kq) to
r q^(−s) k^(−s) between c=(1+(m+2)/q)^(−s) and1. After normalization,
P_q(k)>=c pi_s(k) for every k. Therefore their total-variation distance is

    TV(P_q,pi_s) <= 1−(1+(m+2)/q)^(−s).                   (25)

This proves convergence with a source-dependent rate, rather than reading
independence off a finite residue histogram. In the limiting zeta law,
for each finite set of primes the valuations are independent geometric
variables:

    Pr(v_p(K)=a)=(1−p^(−s))p^(−sa),   a>=0.               (26)

To prove it, fix the chosen valuations, sum k^(−s) over the remaining
prime factors, and use the absolutely convergent Euler product. Thus
Pr(p|K)=p^(−s), not1/p. Conditioning this law on squarefreeness gives
Pr(p|K)=1/(p^s+1), and squarefree probability1/zeta(2s).
These follow by replacing each geometric factor with1+p^(−s).
The incoming [arithmetic conditioning note](arithmetic_context_laws_20261011d.md)
has1/(p+1) for uniformly sampled squarefree integers. It is the same
factorization operation under a different measure, not the same weights.

Native removal of alias1 conditions pi_s on K>=2. Write
c_s=zeta(s)/(zeta(s)−1)>1. For distinct primes p,l,

    Pr(p|K | K>=2)=c_s p^(−s),
    Pr(pl|K | K>=2)=c_s (pl)^(−s).

Their covariance is c_s(1−c_s)(pl)^(−s)<0. The prime flags cease to be
independent after this conditioning, despite being independent before it.
The same proof as (25), now k>=2, gives total-variation error at most
1−(1+(m+2)/(2q))^(−s) for the remaining alias prior. ROOT reweighting
introduces the unknown V as a further factor; none of these prior laws
asserts a law for ROOT scores or a lower bound on their mass.

## G1. The star and the path-plus-isolate really are two reversible faces

The later incoming commit `45b05d69f2` independently supplies the operations
below and proves a stronger all-star result. We incorporate that current
result rather than present its four-vertex specialization as a new theorem.
Let Phi be the incoming tournament/even-graph type codec and Psi its inverse.
Its “even” predicate is automorphism-even, not even vertex degrees. Define
on its graph types

    J(G)=Phi(converse(Psi(G))),
    G diamond H=Phi(Psi(G) ordered-join Psi(H)).             (19)

These are all-order definitions relative to the cited count theorem used
by that codec. J is an involution, diamond is associative with empty graph
as unit, and

    J(G diamond H)=J(H) diamond J(G).                      (20)

This follows by conjugating the corresponding tournament identities.
Let a be the singleton graph and b=P3, the image of the directed triangle.
Then the codec gives

    a diamond b=K_{1,3},      b diamond a=P3 disjoint-union K1,
    J(K_{1,3})=P3 disjoint-union K1.                        (21)

Thus “two sides of the same coin” is exact as **transported converse**.
It is not graph complement: the complement of K1,3 is K3 plus an isolate.
Nor does it preserve ordinary connectedness. In fact the stronger
[converse/fusion packet, §4](tournament_converse_context_20261011d.md) proves
J(K_{1,r+2})=P3 disjoint-union rK1 for every r>=0. Its rank-zero atom
argument identifies the star explicitly. On labelled vertices, retain the
centre and the two path leaves; the remaining r edges can then be toggled
reversibly. The marker has exactly binom(r+2,2) possible values, a sharp
inverse multiplicity. Its order-six example also shows that general J
can change chromatic number, despite preserving bipartiteness on this fan
family. Those stronger results are inherited from the new checkpoint.
The representation is
reversible on types, while its familiar graph observables change.

Ordinary disjoint union and diamond have the same unit but do not satisfy
interchange. Setting the middle two arguments to the empty graph would
force a disjoint-union b=a diamond b, contradicted by (21). This supplies
an actual common carrier for an Eckmann–Hilton comparison and exhibits
exactly the failed hypothesis. Earlier
[difference-family operations](difference_family_operations_20261004.md)
had a different explicit interchange defect; the failures are not equated.

### Retaining interaction can make an update linear

In the marked two-bit section, edge parity is y+xy over F2. Retain the
lifted coordinates z=(1,x,y,xy). Then the two flips become linear maps

    flip_x: (1,x,y,xy) -> (1,1+x,y,y+xy),
    flip_y: (1,x,y,xy) -> (1,x,1+y,x+xy).                  (22)

The added coordinate xy is redundant as raw information, but necessary
for a linear readout of this parity observable: no affine function of
1,x,y has the four required values. Equation (22) applies on the four
valid lifted states; arbitrary vectors in F2^4 need not encode a graph.
This is a useful design distinction: store enough interaction coordinates
to make a desired operation simple, while retaining the validity equations.

A3 does the analogous operation in a different carrier: a source plus
one alias is bounded by one score variable only after its covariance
or domination (7) is authenticated. It retains a native predicate, not just two states
with similar counts. No graph-type correspondence supplies that covariance.

## N1. The two sets of four arithmetic formulas, with the observables fixed

Take N uniform on {1,...,X}; d(N) counts divisors, omega(N) distinct prime
factors, Omega(N) prime factors with multiplicity. The intended first
quartet can be made precise as follows.

| Observable | Correct scale / status |
|---|---|
| E[d(N)] | log X+2 gamma_E−1+O(X^(−1/2)); classical Dirichlet divisor mean |
| Pr(N prime) | pi(X)/X ~1/log X; prime number theorem |
| E[omega(N)], E[Omega(N)] | log log X+O(1); Hardy–Ramanujan normal order, Turan's variance method, Erdős–Kac Gaussian refinement |
| Pr(N squarefree) | 1/zeta(2)+O(X^(−1/2))=6/pi^2+O(X^(−1/2)) |

The divisor count and prime-factor count are different observables.
“Erdős–Turán” is not the usual name of the displayed prime-factor law.
Sources: [Tao's divisor derivation](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2011/07/23/erdos-divisor-bound/),
[prime number theorem notes](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2021/02/12/246b-notes-4-the-riemann-zeta-function-and-the-prime-number-theorem/),
and [elementary multiplicative notes, prime-factor mean and Exercise51](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2014/11/23/254a-notes-1-elementary-multiplicative-number-theory/).
The squarefree estimate follows directly from
mu(N)^2=sum_{d^2|N}mu(d), summing floors and bounding sum_{d>sqrt X}d^(−2).

The second quartet involves representation counts; a probability needs a
sampling model and a normalization.

| even=p+q | binary Goldbach conjecture for even N>2 |

### The exact additive/multiplicative bridge

For the prime indicator P(n), additive convolution gives

    R_2(N)=sum_{a+b=N}P(a)P(b),
    R_3(N)=sum_{a+b+c=N}P(a)P(b)P(c),
    R_L(N)=sum_{a+2b=N}P(a)P(b).

Dirichlet convolution instead gives d=1 *_D 1, with a*b=N as its incidence
relation. Ordinary power series turn additive convolution into products;
Dirichlet series turn multiplicative convolution into products. A common
word “product” hides different source relations. Passing n->log n converts
multiplication to addition on log-integers, not integer addition.

The exact rich carrier is the finite valuation vector (v_p(n))_p together
with the ordinary integer evaluation n=product p^(v_p(n)). Multiplication
is coordinate addition there. To perform n->3n+1 one must retain or recover
ordinary evaluation and its carries. Summaries omega, Omega, radical,
residue signature, or a prime-factor histogram discard information that
this additive operation consumes. This supplies a principled reason to
retain two operations and their conversion context rather than seek an
unjustified interchange law.
