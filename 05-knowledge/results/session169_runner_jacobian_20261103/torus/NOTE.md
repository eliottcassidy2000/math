# Flat signed-unit memory: torus covers, converse sheets, and square-cycle clocks

**Status: proved elementary constructions, conditional only on the explicit S168 sign-kernel theorem; finite exact controls supplied.** The main bridge is constructive. Every Cayley–Dickson level from the octonions onward contains two explicit abelian transport systems acting regularly on all signed units. One uses the minimum possible number of torus directions. The other preserves a binary observation that detects algebraic sign reversal, and therefore can extend S166's actual converse double cover. The same formulas turn S167's positive square-sum cycles into two-lap or four-lap clocks and then into scheduled, constant-profile tournament circuits.

These are new compositions of standard covering theory with the repository's inherited transport and tournament interfaces. No literature-priority claim is made. They do not prove the Lonely Runner Conjecture, classify all tournament metagraphs, identify formal sheets with tournament classes, or supply an intrinsic autonomous evolution on an unmarked tournament.

## 1. The two regular abelian transport systems

Use the inherited convention

    (a,b)(c,d) = (ac − conjugate(d)b, da + b conjugate(c)).

At level n write a signed basis unit as (s,y,t), meaning (−1)^s e_(y+2^(n−1)t), where s,t are bits and y belongs to F_2^(n−1). All sign arithmetic below is modulo two. Let G_n be the permutation group generated by positive basis left multiplications, and let Z reverse the global sign. Fix n≥3 and put δ(y)=[y≠0].

The inherited S168 kernel theorem says that every diagonal operation

    D_(g+ct)(s,y,t) = (s+g(y)+ct,y,t)

belongs to G_n exactly when the truth table g has even parity. In particular D_t and Z belong. The lower-level sign row f_u(y), defined by e_u e_y=(−1)^f_u(y)e_(u+y), has even parity because n−1≥2. S168 also gives

    L_(u,0)(s,y,t) = (s+f_u(y)+t c_u(y),y+u,t),
    c_u(y)=1+δ(y)+δ(y+u)  for u≠0,
    H:=L_(0,1)(s,y,t)=(s+δ(y)+t,y,t+1).

Define

    B_u(s,y,t) = (s+t(δ(y)+δ(y+u)),y+u,t),
    J(s,y,t)   = (s+δ(y),y,t+1).

For u≠0, B_u=L_(u,0) D_(f_u+t), where the rightmost operation acts first. Also B_0=identity and J=H D_t. Thus these are genuine elements of G_n, not additional generators silently adjoined to it.

**Theorem 1 (two flat completions).**

1. A_n=<B_u,H> is abelian, regular on all 2^(n+1) signed units, and isomorphic to C_2^(n−1)×C_4.
2. E_n=<B_u,J,Z> is abelian, regular on the same states, and isomorphic to C_2^(n+1).
3. Every abelian subgroup of G_n that is transitive on all signed units has one of these two abstract isomorphism types.

**Proof.** Change coordinates by the involution

    Q(s,y,t)=(q,y,t),    q=s+tδ(y).

Direct substitution gives

    Q B_u Q(q,y,t)=(q,y+u,t),
    Q H Q(q,y,t)=(q+t,y,t+1),
    Q J Q(q,y,t)=(q,y,t+1),
    Q Z Q(q,y,t)=(q+1,y,t).

Thus B_u B_v=B_(u+v), all B_u commute with H and J, and H is a four-cycle on (q,t), with H²=Z. The integer k=t+2q modulo four increases by one under H. This proves the first two claims, including regularity. Q is a coordinate change, not asserted to belong to G_n.

For the third claim, a faithful abelian transitive permutation group is regular: an element fixing one point fixes its entire orbit by commutation. Hence such a subgroup A has order 2^(n+1). Its index translations surject onto F_2^n, so its zero-translation kernel has order two. A nonidentity diagonal element in a regular group cannot fix any signed unit; its sign function must therefore be constantly one. The kernel is exactly {1,Z}. Every square lies in it. By the elementary classification of finite abelian groups, either all elements have order two, giving C_2^(n+1), or there is exactly one C_4 factor and n−1 C_2 factors. Two C_4 factors would make the square subgroup larger than {1,Z}. ∎

The low levels matter. G_1=C_4 works on four states. G_2=Q_8 is regular on eight states, and no proper subgroup is transitive. Consequently **no abelian transitive transport subgroup exists at the quaternion level**. The n≥3 restriction is not cosmetic.

## 2. Genuine torus covers and the extra cost of detecting sign

A connected finite cover of a d-torus has abelian transitive monodromy: its fundamental group is Z^d. Hence its permutation image is regular and generated by at most d elements. This is ordinary covering-space theory, not a new result.

**Corollary 2 (sharp torus dimension).** For n≥3, a connected torus cover with all 2^(n+1) signed units as sheets and monodromy contained in G_n requires d≥n. Equality is attained by

    R^n / (2Z^(n−1) × 4Z)  →  R^n / Z^n.

For integer winding w=(w_1,...,w_n), its sheet action is

    B_(w_1,...,w_(n−1) mod 2) H^(w_n).

This follows from Theorem 1: A_n needs n generators and E_n needs n+1. The displayed lattice quotient realizes A_n explicitly. The full nonabelian G_n cannot be the monodromy of a torus cover, however large the torus dimension. A graph cover carrying G_n does not automatically extend when commuting two-cells are attached.

Now require, in addition, a binary observation p of the sheets with the following two properties:

- Every transport changes p by a state-independent bit. Equivalently p is a map to a two-sheet quotient cover.
- p(Zu)=p(u)+1, so algebraic sign reversal is visible in that binary observation.

**Corollary 3 (one extra torus direction).** Under these additional requirements d≥n+1, and equality is attained by E_n and

    R^(n+1) / 2Z^(n+1)  →  R^(n+1) / Z^(n+1).

Indeed any binary quotient of a regular abelian action is a homomorphism to C_2. In A_n, Z=H², so every such homomorphism kills Z. Theorem 1 therefore forces E_n. For E_n the corrected sign p(s,y,t)=s+tδ(y) works: only Z changes it. Notice that raw sign s is generally not a transport-compatible observation. The required correction is explicit.

This separates two different meanings of “retain the sign.” A_n retains the full signed state, but its sign reversal cannot survive as a compatible two-sheet quotient. E_n retains both the full state and such a quotient.

## 3. A converse-compatible extension of the actual S166 black graph

S166's Q_1 retains the non-self-converse vertices and individual reflection-pairs of black lines. Its physical converse lift is a genuine two-sheet cover with binary edge voltage σ. Loops and parallel edges must remain distinct. Blue edges and self-converse vertices are not part of Q_1 and are not included in the following covering construction.

**Theorem 4 (prescribed binary quotient).** Let B be a connected multigraph, let its binary voltage σ have nonzero cycle class, and let β=|E|−|V|+1. For n≥3, a connected abelian G_n-transport extension on all signed units, whose binary quotient detects Z and agrees with σ, exists if and only if β≥n+1.

**Proof and construction.** Switch σ so every edge of a spanning tree has zero voltage. The β remaining voltage bits form a nonzero vector. Extend this vector to n+1 linearly independent binary characters on the cycle space. Put their values on the cotree edges, and zero on tree edges, obtaining voltages in E_n=F_2^(n+1). The resulting monodromy is all E_n, so the cover is connected. Projection to the first coordinate recovers the switched physical double cover. Undoing the original switching recovers σ itself. In signed-unit coordinates the first coordinate is q=s+tδ(y), and Z changes it. Conversely Theorem 1 and sign detection force E_n, which needs n+1 independent generators; a graph of cycle rank β has at most β fundamental-cycle generators. ∎

An entirely mechanical completion is useful. Pick a cotree edge p with σ_p=1. Give it vector (1,0,...,0). Pick n other cotree edges, give their vectors first coordinate σ_e and distinct remaining unit coordinates, and give every other cotree edge vector (σ_e,0,...,0). These vectors span E_n. Thus no search for an abstract embedding is needed.

At order six S166's verified Q_1 has 22 vertices, 157 edges, β=136, and nine negative loops. Its voltage is therefore nonzero. For n=3 Theorem 4 gives a **regular, converse-compatible** 16-sheet formal cover with 352 vertices and 2,512 edges. It maps eight-to-one onto the actual converse lift, which has 44 vertices and 314 edges. The checker reconstructs Q_1 from the 1,024 fixed-path flags and verifies this particular extension, including projection of each individual edge.

These sizes agree with S167's 16-sheet example, but the covers have different monodromy. S167 used the full 128-element octonion transport group and obtained a nonregular cover with only two deck transformations. Here the transport group and deck group both have order 16. The formal extra states are not claimed to be new tournament isomorphism classes. Only the prescribed two-sheet quotient is the physical S166 object.

## 4. Runner winding: a torus lap can hide four sheet states

For integer speeds v=(v_1,...,v_n) with gcd(v_i)=1, the base flow t↦tv mod Z^n has least positive period one. Lift it to the A_n torus of Section 2. Its least return time is

    ℓ(v)=lcm( 2/gcd(2,v_i) for i<n, 4/gcd(4,v_n) )
        =4 if v_n is odd, and 2 if v_n is even.

The second case uses primitivity: when v_n is even, at least one earlier speed is odd. This is a lattice calculation, not an equidistribution hypothesis. The function

    L_v(t)=min_i ||v_i t||

is unchanged by the lift. Every base loneliness configuration reappears every integer lap, while its lifted state returns only every ℓ(v) laps.

For v=(1,2,3), L_v has maximum 1/4, attained at t=1/4 and 3/4 modulo one. In the n=3 cover, the lifted sheet at consecutive integer laps from e_0 is

    +e_0, +e_5, −e_0, −e_5, +e_0.

Thus the same optimal lonely configuration at t=1/4, 5/4, 9/4, 13/4 has four distinct lifted states. Nothing about the maximum loneliness improves.

More generally for speeds (1,...,n), max L_v=1/(n+1). The n+1 circle points 0,t,...,nt have a neighboring gap at most 1/(n+1); their index difference is between 1 and n, proving the upper bound. The time t=1/(n+1) attains it. The lifted return multiplier is four for odd n and two for even n. This supplies explicit recurrent runner experiments at every n≥3 without claiming a result for arbitrary speed sets.

This mechanism counts **coordinate winding around the torus**, not crossings of loneliness-threshold walls. A fixed threshold-crossing cocycle may cancel under time reflection or obey a sharper two-lap restriction. The two encodings retain different data and must not be substituted for each other.

## 5. One honeycomb produces two-lap and four-lap arithmetic memory

Use S167's honeycomb with vertices (u,v,e), u,v integers, e a bit, and black-to-white edges

    a: (u,v,0) ↔ (u,v,1),
    b: (u,v,0) ↔ (u−1,v,1),
    c: (u,v,0) ↔ (u,v−1,1).

Keep n≥3 fixed. On the signed units with y either zero or the first bottom unit, use the commuting operations B=B_1 and H. They act regularly on the eight states ±e_0, ±e_1, ±e_h, ±e_(h+1), where h=2^(n−1). Give an a edge identity transport, a black-to-white b edge B^−1=B, and a black-to-white c edge H^−1. Reverse traversal uses the inverse.

The honeycomb position (u,v,e) then has unit state B^u H^v e_0. Its kernel in the translation lattice is exactly

    Λ_h = 2Z × 4Z.

The quotient honeycomb has 16 vertices and 24 edges. It is an eight-sheet cover of the two-vertex, three-edge quotient, and extends over the hexagonal torus cells because B and H commute. In contrast, using two noncommuting left multiplications would leave a nonidentity hexagon commutator. The construction is a flat toroidal memory, not arbitrary octonion multiplication assigned to undirected edges.

For arithmetic target sums a<b<c set p=b−a, q=c−a, g=gcd(p,q), r=q/g, s=p/g. Retain the **marked bipartite arithmetic lift**: its vertices are (z,e), with edges (z,0)–(d−z,1) for each target d in {a,b,c}. For an initial integer x, S167's covering map is

    (u,v,0) ↦ (x+pu+qv,0),
    (u,v,1) ↦ (a−x−pu−qv,1).

It identifies the marked component with the honeycomb quotient by the primitive translation

    k=(r,−s).

The layer-zero labels have residue x modulo g and the layer-one labels have residue a−x modulo g. Forgetting the layer is injective on this component when 2x≠a modulo g. Without that qualification the unmarked arithmetic graph need not be bipartite: targets (25,36,49) give the triangle 6–19–30–6. That triangle returns to its integer label after three steps but changes the retained layer. The return and shortest-winding statements below concern the marked lift; they pass to the unmarked graph under the disjoint-residue condition.

A primitive lap in the marked arithmetic lift has signed-unit transport B^r H^(−s). Since gcd(r,s)=1, its order is

    ℓ(k)=4 if s is odd, and 2 if s is even.                 (1)

There are no fixed-sheet exceptions here because the eight-state action is regular. S167's honeycomb distance formula is 2 max(|u|,|v|,|u+v|). Therefore the shortest nonzero winding closing both the marked arithmetic state and this unit register has length

    2r ℓ(k).                                              (2)

Indeed Zk∩Λ_h=Z(ℓ(k)k), and the distance to tk is 2|t|r. A geodesic achieves the bound. S167's three pinned tournament bits also return by this time: their translation kernel is 2Z², which contains Λ_h.

## 6. Positive square cycles and an explicit 88-step example

Specialize to S167's positive simple square-sum cycles, for every m≥3:

    a=(2m+1)², b=(2m+3)², c=(2m+5)²,
    g=8, r=2m+3, s=m+1, L=floor((4m²−4m−7)/2).

For j=0,...,r−1 put

    x_j=L+8(js mod r),
    y_j=a−x_j if x_j<L+q−p, and y_j=c−x_j otherwise.

The cyclic list x_0,y_0,...,x_(r−1),y_(r−1) has 4m+6 distinct positive vertices and winds by (2m+3,−m−1). Its edges alternate an a- or c-edge with a b-edge. The construction and positivity proof are inherited from S167. Here g=8 and a is odd, so 2L≠a modulo g automatically: the two layer residues are disjoint, and the marked component embeds in the unmarked integer graph. Combining this with (1) proves:

**Theorem 5 (square-cycle memory law).** The arithmetic cycle, together with the eight-state toroidal unit register, has exact period

    2(4m+6) if m is odd,
    4(4m+6) if m is even.

The arithmetic phase is retained in this state. Repeated laps are closed walks, not longer simple arithmetic cycles. The first lap is already a simple positive square-sum cycle.

For m=4 the targets are 81,121,169, and the 22-edge cycle is

    20,61,60,21,100,69,52,29,92,77,44,37,
    84,85,36,45,76,93,28,53,68,101,20.

Its winding is (11,−5), and its register states at successive completed laps are

    +e_0, +e_(h+1), −e_0, −e_(h+1), +e_0.

The pinned three-bit holonomy after one lap is (0,1,1), so those bits have returned after two laps, while the signed unit is still −e_0. Four laps, 88 arithmetic steps, return every retained state. For m=3 the 18-edge cycle instead has two-lap return, 36 steps. The checker verifies every family member 3≤m≤100 and every intermediate label, target sum, transport, and return time.

## 7. Compile the clock into actual equal-profile tournaments

On the eight-state register, write y as one bit. Its formulas reduce to

    B(s,y,t)=(s+t,y+1,t),
    H(s,y,t)=(s+y+t,y,t+1),
    H^−1(s,y,t)=(s+y+t+1,y,t+1).

Each is implemented by a conditional flip of the sign bit followed by one unconditional index-bit flip. The a move does nothing to the register.

For a complete finite clock let N=4m+6, k=ceil(log_2 N), and store the cycle phase i in k bits. The logical map on the active states 0≤i<N is

    (i,z) ↦ (i+1 mod N, τ_i(z)),

where τ_i is exactly the oriented register transport of the i-th colored edge of the displayed arithmetic cycle. Fix all states with i≥N. This is a permutation of all 2^(k+3) bit states. Its active state space splits into four cycles of length 2N for odd m, or two cycles of length 4N for even m. The other 8(2^k−N) states are fixed.

A fully specified reversible gate schedule can implement any such finite permutation: decompose it into endpoint transpositions, route each transposition along a coordinate-flip path in the Boolean cube, and swap along that path and back with the last edge used only once. Intermediate vertices are restored and the endpoints exchanged. Each adjacent transposition is a NOT of one bit controlled on the exact values of the other bits. This standard construction needs no unmentioned work bits. The checker constructs and exhaustively verifies the entire schedule for m=4: 1,074 controlled single-bit gates on eight bits, checked on all 256 inputs. This is an explicit schedule, not an optimal gate count.

Now replace each of the k+3 bits by S168's labelled six-vertex tournament module of mask 153 or 185. They differ by one designated arc, are strongly connected and nonisomorphic, and have common labelled-slot polynomial

    F(r)=r^6+12r^4+16r^2,    H=29.

Take their ordered ordinal sum. Every gate is a literal designated arc reversal on exactly the states satisfying its controls. All intermediate tournaments have the same complete path-cover profile

    F_total(r)=(r^6+12r^4+16r^2)^(k+3),
    H_total=29^(k+3).

For m=4 this is a 48-vertex family, with two logical 88-cycles, 176 active encoded tournaments, and 80 fixed logical states. The fixed gate schedule is external timing information; a logical step is one full circuit pass. These are actual scheduled arc-flip implementations, not single-edge embeddings of the arithmetic cycle, and not autonomous dynamics of the blue/black metagraph. The bit modules and phase/controller markers are necessary sidecars.
