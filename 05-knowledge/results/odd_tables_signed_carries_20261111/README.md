## Inheritance and concept board

The closest proved mechanism is the joint H13/C3 defect and exact q order
from the preceding packet. The hostile example is a switch of the correct
width attached to the wrong baseline. The corrected near miss is reducing
the core deletion count while increasing the future response. The least-used
sidecars are the actual root choice, the translation of an affine carry,
and separate observer moduli at different times of one trajectory.

The live board has six concepts: prescribed target, paired source response,
binary reverse address, signed3 carry, synchronized modular clock, and
prime-power information loss. Every positive connection is recorded with
its actual map and the retained data in the linked proofs.

The user's table convention is expressed with modulus m: the central
block has two copies each of N and3N+1 when m=4N+1, or N and3N−1 when
m=4N−1. Each central row sums to m. The mod-eight lanes retain the sign
and the edge clock. Prime tables make every nonzero row reversible; the
central rows alone are reversible for every odd m. The signed decoder is
inherited from the [central table proof](../central_modular_chanpak_20261110/central/PROOF.md)
and the [modular centre packet](../modular_centers_collatz_pell_20261111/PROOFS.md).

Known positive and negative Collatz cycles motivate comparing the two
signed sheets. Their known lists are not assumed exhaustive. No assertion
of Collatz convergence is a dependency of the tournament constructions.

The exact remaining gate is a **prescribed-target** selection law: terminal
padding reaches a useful rebase, but also changes the starting target.
The current searches therefore focus on source changes with a retained
baseline, not an increase in numerical coverage alone.
