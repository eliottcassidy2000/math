## Corrections

### THM-4622

1. **(2), lower bound.** "de Grey's 1581-vertex unit-distance graph (or Heule's 509-vertex descendant)".
   The 509-vertex 5-chromatic unit-distance graph is Jaan Parts's (arXiv:2010.12665, Geombinatorics
   2020); Heule's smallest is 510 (FoCM 2023 talk; 517/529/553 earlier). Corrected: "(or the 510-vertex
   graph of Heule / the 509-vertex graph of Parts)". The mathematics is unaffected.
2. **(5), absoluteness.** "For every countable conjugation-closed `L`, `χ(G(L))` is absolute ...
   Absoluteness is (2) applied to the computable graph `G(L)`." `G(L)` is computable only when `L` is
   computably presented; there are `2^(aleph_0)` countable subfields of `C` and only countably many
   computable ones, and "the same value in every transitive model" does not even parse for a field that
   is not definable. Corrected statement, two versions: (a) for `L ⊆ Q̄` given by a computable
   enumeration (every number field, the odd-`t` compositum, the Hilbert class fields of THM-4566), the
   sentence "`χ(G(L)) ≤ k`" is `Π^0_1` and has the same truth value in every transitive model of ZF, by
   (1) exactly as in (2); (b) for an arbitrary countable `L`, "`χ(G(L)) ≤ k`" is absolute between
   transitive models `M ⊆ N` of ZF with `L ∈ M` and `L` countable in `M` (finite subsets of `L` and
   `k`-colourability of finite graphs are absolute; (1) applies in both models). Also the clause "the
   residue colourings of THM-4558 (Lemma R) are choice-free" is a statement about number fields (where
   Lemma R lives), not about every countable `L`.
3. **Typing.** Statement (1) (countable de Bruijn-Erdős needs no choice) is folklore, not new: it is the
   standard König argument on the definable tree `k^(<ω)`, the only place choice enters de Bruijn-Erdős
   being uncountable vertex sets (Läuchli; BPI). Likewise the component structure of `UD(Q^2)` (cosets of
   the group generated by the rational unit vectors; denominators are products of primes `≡ 1 mod 4`) is
   classical (Woodall 1973 context). Type both KNOWN (the written-out proofs can stay); the genuinely
   new packaging is the `χ_alg` chain (2)-(4) and the `Sym(ω)` remark in (6), and (2)-(4) is itself
   "KNOWN in substance" as the status already says.
4. **(4), nit.** The distinctness conjuncts `(x_i, y_i) ≠ (x_j, y_j)` are unnecessary (harmless): a graph
   homomorphism `G → UD(R_alg^2)` already gives `χ(G) ≤ χ_alg`, and adjacent vertices cannot collide
   because `|x - y| = 1 ≠ 0`. Extra unit distances never hurt (the realised point set induces a
   supergraph of `G`). Both points are as the file says; recorded only because the brief asked.
5. **(6), nit.** "the components are dense in `R^2`" is asserted, not proved. It is true
   (`(0, 1/5) = 2[(3/5, 4/5) + (-3/5, 4/5) - (0, 1)] - (0, 1) ∈ Γ_Q`, and `(0, 1/25) ∈ Γ_Q` similarly; checked
   exactly), but the argument only needs that a coset of `Γ_Q ⊇ Z^2` is not contained in a line (the
   locus `|w + t_1 - t_2| = |w|` is a line).
6. **Reading, Shelah-Soifer.** "their example of axiom-dependence is a different distance graph on `R`"
   is accurate for the cited 2003 paper (vertex set `R`, `x ~ y` iff `x - y ∈ √2 + Q`; `χ = 2` in ZFC,
   uncountable in ZF + DC + LM). Add that Shelah-Soifer, *Axiom of choice and chromatic number: examples
   on the plane*, JCTA 105 (2004) 359-364, give distance graphs **on `R^2`** with the same
   axiom-dependence, so "OPEN" is specifically about the unit distance, not about distance graphs in the
   plane.
7. **Minor.** "`UD(R_alg^2)` ... decidable edge relation" and "`Π^0_1`" are right (real-algebraic
   equality is decidable). The bound `5 ≤ χ_alg` in ZF rests on the SAT verification of de Grey's graph
   (a finite computation, independently re-verified in the literature); say so in the status.

### THM-4623

1. **(2) is false as stated.** "There are `2^(ℵ_0)` such `h`" for every `m ≡ 4 (mod 6)`, `m ≠ 4`.
   Counterexample `m = 10` (`u = 20`, `v = 3`): every admissible value of `h` is a multiple of `3`
   (`h(u) = 3`, and `T^(-1)(3j) = {6j}` since `3j ≢ 4 (mod 6)`), so `h(n) = 2^(depth n) · 3` is the **only**
   map `h`. The same holds whenever `v = (m-1)/3 ≡ 0 (mod 3)`, i.e. `m ≡ 10 (mod 18)` (`m = 10, 28, 46,
   64, ...`): the count of `h` on the depth-22 truncation is exactly `1` for `m = 10, 28, 46, 64`, versus
   `6.7·10^115` for `m = 16` and `4.0·10^177` for `m = 22` (`audit_B_endomorphisms.py`). The proof's own
   remark "choosing the odd one whenever `(t_k - 1)/3 ≢ 0 (mod 3)`" handles later steps but misses
   `t_0 = v`. Corrected hypothesis: `m ≡ 4 (mod 6)` and `m ≢ 1 (mod 9)` (equivalently `m ≡ 4` or `16 (mod
   18)`), e.g. `m = 16, 22, 34, 40`. The conclusion `|End(N, T)| = 2^(ℵ_0)` survives (take `m = 16`).
2. **(2) silently assumes `m` is not periodic.** The proof uses "`T u = m ∉ anc(u) ∪ {u}` (else `u` would
   be periodic)" and "a common ancestor would visit `m` twice"; for a general `m ≡ 4 (mod 6)` the
   non-periodicity of `m` is the open cycle half of the Collatz conjecture. If `m` were periodic the
   conditions `h(u) = v`, `h ∘ T = T ∘ h` on `anc(u) ∋ u` would force `v = u`, so no `h` exists. Add the
   hypothesis "`m` not periodic (e.g. any `m` whose orbit reaches `1`)" — harmless for the conclusion.
3. **(2), rotation.** "`φ(c)` is periodic of period dividing `3`, i.e. in `{1, 2, 4}`" uses that
   `T^3 x = x` has only the solutions `1, 2, 4` in `N` (an odd step forces two even steps, `3n + 1 = 4n`;
   three even steps force `n = 0`). State it. (Checked for `n < 10^6`.)
4. **(3), the "adjoint" sentence is a misidentification (title, statement, proof, Reading).** "the
   transfer operator at level `L` is the transpose of `θ : V_L → V_(L+1)` restricted to level `L`
   (THM-4520's `M_L`), a square matrix, whose `K[t]`-module is torsion with the spectrum computed there"
   and the title's "its adjoint, the level transfer operator of THM-4520, is pure torsion at every level
   ... with all nontrivial eigenvalues of modulus `1/2`". THM-4520's operator `T_n` acts on `C^(L_n)`,
   `L_n = 2·3^(n-1)` (the units mod `3^n`: the 3-adic Fourier recursion of the Syracuse law), has no
   object called `M_L`, and is not the transpose of anything defined on `Z/2^L`; its eigenvalue moduli
   are `1/2 ∓ 2^(-L/2)/(2L)`, not `1/2` (exact `1/2` is the untwisted walk of the loosened-walk note).
   The actual transpose of `θ` restricted to level `L` (functions on `Z/2^L`, averaged back to level `L`)
   is the Terras/de Bruijn transition matrix `P_L(a → b) = ½ #{x ∈ {a, a + 2^L} : T x ≡ b}`, and
   `P_L^L = J/2^L` exactly (checked `L ≤ 8`): spectrum `{1, 0, ..., 0}`, minimal polynomial `t^L (t - 1)`.
   It is pure torsion, but with eigenvalue `0`, not `1/2`. Corrected: delete the identification with
   THM-4520, or replace by the `P_L` statement above.
5. **(1), nits.** Directed sets must be nonempty (otherwise (iv) fails and "dcpo" would need a bottom).
   "Collatz ⟺ `P` is a dcpo with a top" is correct but the dcpo clause is redundant: a top class is
   reachable from every point, which already excludes divergent orbits, so "Collatz ⟺ `P` has a greatest
   element". The Reading's "the dictionary places the two halves ... as (sober) and (top)" is therefore
   misleading: (top) alone is the whole conjecture. Honest split: no divergence ⟺ sober ⟺ dcpo; given
   that, "no other cycle" ⟺ `P` has a unique maximal element. The equivalences (i)-(iv), the
   irreducible-closed-set lemma, the `T_0` uniqueness and Scott = Alexandrov were re-derived and hold.
6. **(3), checked and fine.** Base change, CRT reduction to `(t - λ)^k`, density of period-`k` points
   with `k` prime to `m` in every residue class (cylinders; checked exactly: every class mod `2^4`
   contains period-`k` points for `k = 4, 5, 7, 9, 11`, every class mod `2^6` for `k = 6, 7, 11`), the
   dense orbit, and the height-one step all hold; characteristic zero is used only in "`nκ` takes
   infinitely many values" (in characteristic `p` the same conclusion follows from the transitivity of
   `T^p`, not claimed). The title's "t-primary torsion" should read "`(t - 1)`-primary".
7. **Status.** "PROVED (elementary)" → PROVED WITH CORRECTIONS 1-4; the title must drop the THM-4520
   clause and add the hypotheses of 1-2.
