# Minimal continuation observations at coarse and fine interfaces

**Proved.** The coarsest uniform-module interface has an exact minimal path-cover observable. At the other extreme, arbitrary labelled attachments recover the *entire old orientation*, even if only the signed difference across a marked old arc reversal is returned. Between these extremes there is a finite sufficient color-endpoint tensor. “Minimal” below means the exact equivalence relation of outputs under all permitted continuations, not a claim about optimal query complexity.

## 1. The exact color tensor and uniform-module minimality

Assign colors to the old vertices, with each outside vertex's contacts to a color uniform; an outside vertex may choose its direction independently for each color and each other outside vertex. The old vertices do not have to form modules by color internally. Let \(C_r((a_1,b_1),\ldots,(a_r,b_r))\) count ordered covers of the whole old block by \(r\) nonempty directed paths with the indicated start/end colors.

An outside continuation acts linearly on this tensor. Fix a permutation of the outside vertices and choose the occupied gaps (including before the first and after the last). Each occupied gap contains one maximal old run. Consecutive outside vertices not separated by a run must use an actual outside arc. For an occupied gap, the colors of its two endpoints must match the two exterior contacts, whenever present. For that skeleton sum the compatible color entries of \(C_r\). Every Hamilton path has exactly one such decomposition, so summing over all skeletons gives the exact continuation count. Tensor entries are invariant under permuting the ordered pairs of endpoint colors, as one may rename the run slots. For fixed \(k\) colors and old order \(n\) one thus needs at most \(\binom{n+k^2}{k^2}\) multiplicity-pattern coordinates, rather than a word of color pairs of unbounded length. For signed gaps across a marked old reversal, use the **difference** of the two old tensors with the same exact contraction. Sufficiency alone does not establish coordinate minimality for arbitrary intermediate color partitions.

When there is one color, the ordinary ordered nonempty cover counts \(C_1,\ldots,C_n\) are **exactly minimal**, for counts and signed gaps separately. Sufficiency is the contraction above. For necessity, for every \(r\ge2\) use the actual cyclic tournament quotient \(T\to I_{r-1}\to I'_{r-1}\to T\), with two exterior transitive blocks having \(r-1\) vertices apiece. Between successive visits to \(T\) both exterior blocks must occur. Thus no skeleton has more than \(r\) visits, and at least one has precisely \(r\), by alternating runs with successive available vertices in the two exterior blocks. The continuation therefore reads \(\sum_{j\le r}b_{rj}C_j\) with positive \(b_{rr}\). Beginning with the empty continuation \(C_1=H(T)\), the triangular system recovers every \(C_r\). The same triangular system recovers differences in the reversal problem. One may equivalently retain ordered covers allowing empty slots, since the binomial change of basis is triangular.

## 2. Fine labelled contacts recover one arc exactly

Let the old order be \(n\ge2\), and designate distinct old vertices \(p,i\). Use \(n-1\) new labelled vertices to form an alternating scaffold path on every vertex **except** \(p\), starting at old \(i\) and ending at a new vertex \(j\). Its \(d=2n-3\) consecutive arcs are all old/new and independently controlled. Write \(s_{uv}\) for \(+1\) when \(u\to v\) and \(-1\) otherwise, and use a variable \(z_e\in\{\pm1\}\) for each scaffold arc, measured in the forward scaffold direction. Keep all unqueried outside contacts fixed.

Take the alternating sum of the full counts over the \(2^d\) probes, with sign \(\prod_e z_e\). A Hamilton path contributes zero unless it contains every scaffold adjacency, by cancellation at an omitted variable. In the surviving paths the scaffold is consecutive and read forwards or backwards, with the only extra vertex \(p\) attached at one end. The signed sum from a forward scaffold is \(+1\) times the eligibility of that last edge, since each selected factor \((1+z_e)/2\) contributes one upon summing against \(z_e\); a reverse scaffold instead contributes \((-1)^d=-1\). Consequently the exact integer sum is
\[
 [p\to i]-[i\to p]+[j\to p]-[p\to j]
 =s_{pi}+s_{jp}.
\]
The second sign is a known exterior calibration. Thus the full old adjacency is the minimal exact equivalence type under fully labelled attachments, with an explicit separating probe family. If only signed gaps across an initially unknown marked reversal are observed, applying the same sum to the gaps reveals \(s^+_{pi}-s^-_{pi}\), identifying both endpoints and the direction of the mark.

## 3. Signed gaps alone recover all remaining orientation data

Once the marked edge \(a b\) is identified, any queried arc other than \(a b\) has an endpoint \(p\) outside this marked pair. Choose it as the omitted vertex; denote the other endpoint by \(i\). The old order is now at least three. Arrange the old vertices other than \(p\) in a line starting at \(i\), with \(a,b\) consecutive. Insert one new vertex between every other pair of consecutive old vertices, and append two new vertices at the end. This gives a scaffold from old \(i\) to new \(j\), using \(n-1\) new vertices; it has exactly one old-old edge, the seam, and one new-new edge. Independently toggle every scaffold edge **except** the marked old arc. Take the alternating sum of the observed signed gaps.

A path term surviving the gap must contain \(a b\); a term surviving the alternating sum must contain every other scaffold edge. Thus again it contains the full scaffold and merely places \(p\) at one end. The number of toggled edges is \(2n-4\), even, so their signs multiply to the same sign on reversal. However the difference of the two marked edge indicators changes sign on reversal. Consequently the total sum is
\[
 \varepsilon (s_{pi}+s_{jp}),
\]
where \(\varepsilon=+1\) if the forward scaffold uses the marked orientation of the plus host, and \(-1\) otherwise. The calibrated sum reads the actual old edge orientation, not merely an orientation difference. This recovers the whole labelled marked pair from the signed-gap observation alone. A pair of states with the same old gap or the same coarse endpoint counts need not remain indistinguishable under unrestricted labelled attachment.

## 4. The same separation can require every probe host to be strong

For \(n\ge8\), use \(n+1\) new vertices instead, placing two more consecutive new vertices at the end of each scaffold. This preserves the parity of its length and all the calibration formulas. To make every probe strong, first fix a spanning directed cycle in edges disjoint from the scaffold and with no old-old edges. Here is a direct construction. Select \(n\) of the new vertices. Arrange the old vertices in any cyclic order, then match the selected new vertices to the \(n\) gaps so that neither connecting edge is a scaffold edge. Each gap forbids at most four choices, since each bounding old vertex has scaffold degree at most two. Each new vertex is likewise forbidden in at most four gaps. The allowed incidence graph has minimum degree at least \(n/2\) on both sides, so has a perfect matching: a subset of at most \(n/2\) vertices meets enough neighbors, and a larger subset meets every vertex on the other side. This gives an alternating cycle avoiding the scaffold. Insert the remaining new vertex in a new-to-old gap of this cycle. There are \(n\) vertex-disjoint such edges, and at most four bad gaps by scaffold degree (in fact at most two). Orient the whole resultant cycle as fixed forward arcs in all queries. This makes every host strong, in both marked orientations, without affecting the alternating-sum calculation. For smaller \(n\), pad with known auxiliary vertices to reach eight “old” vertices before doing the same construction; no questioned adjacency of the original host is used. This proves the same minimal equivalence even when the physical probes themselves are required to be strong.

The nonstrong-probe versions in §§2–3 can even require the *outside block itself* to be transitive. In §2 none of its internal edges is toggled. In §3 exactly one outside–outside edge is toggled; put its endpoints consecutively in the fixed outside linear order, so the toggle merely interchanges those two neighbors in that order. This additional restriction is separate from the strong-host construction of §4.
