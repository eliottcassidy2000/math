## 2. Strict summand and multiplicand growth: complete fill needs an operation

The [strict-summand saturation theorem](../../summand_multiplicand_growth_20261111/summand/TOP_SUFFIX.md)

Our transfer is a construction discipline, not an identification of those
binary operations with Collatz. The macro-parent D(n)<n gives an actual

The macro tree can have three children at one vertex and is not the
ordinary binary preimage tree.

## 3. October compactness and finite currents: an exact height budget

The old [connectivity-from-rigidity note, Proposition2](../../collatz_connectivity_from_rigidity_20261001.md)
isolates an exact quantifier issue: finite residue views of a vertex occur
in the classical root component, but identifying the vertex itself needs
uniformly bounded integer representatives. Its local resemblance and
rigidity statements do not furnish that bound.

Model B now has the corresponding missing *physical* sidecar: an explicit
root path for each actual integer, with a uniform linear peak bound.
No compactness theorem or limit of changing representatives is needed.
In current language each path gives a nonnegative integral edge flow with
divergence delta_n−delta_1, supported in[1,12n+4]. Summing the named paths
for n≤N gives an exact finite demand flow, retaining its individual source
paths and with total edge use at most7N ceil(log_(3/2)N). The route, not
only its formal divergence, certifies reachability.

The ordinary graph keeps its genuine future boundary: a finite induced
component has one missing-successor sink or one contained cycle. Counting
these terminal objects is exact; declaring every boundary sink connected
to1 would be the unresolved step in model A. Thus the relaxed proof
exposes precisely which new edge pays for the finite-height certificate.
