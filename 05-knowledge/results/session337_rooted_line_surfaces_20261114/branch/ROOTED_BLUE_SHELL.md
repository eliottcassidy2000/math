# What the first blue branch can and cannot do

Use the **fixed directed Hamiltonian path tiling metagraph**, not the graph generated by individual arc flips. Orient the path on the order $0,1,\ldots,n-1$. Each tiling assigns the nonpath arcs. A line pairs the two tilings that reverse **all** nonpath arcs. It is blue when reflected path reversal fixes its tiling. Nodes are tournament isomorphism classes (here merged with their converses), with loops and multiplicities unless stated otherwise. This is the distinction in THM-643, THM-4729, THM-4731 and THM-787.

For $n\ge 3$ the transitive class has its unique directed Hamiltonian path, one tiling, and exactly one incident line, which is blue. The other end $T_n^*$ has the ascending path, all nonpath arcs descending and anti-automorphism $i\mapsto n-1-i$. For $n\le2$ there is no nontrivial tile complement to make a line between distinct classes. The original archive already proves $H(T_n^*)=h(n)$, with $h(1)=h(2)=1$, $h(3)=3$ and then tribonacci recursion; the older formula $1+2^{n-2}$ is not this neighbor's formula (it breaks at $n=7$). The blue presentation count of $T_n^*$ is $h(\lfloor n/2\rfloor+1)$ for $n\ge4$ (THM-4731; the order-three directed triangle has a nontrivial automorphism).

## Exact second shell: two reversed arcs per seam

In a Hamiltonian path of $T_n^*$ every ascent is by one. Maximal ascent runs are consecutive intervals, written in descending interval order because descent to an earlier position is the only other permitted step. A run transition from the last of a high interval to the first of the next low one exists unless both intervals are singletons. Thus the path is coded by the low-to-high list $L=(a_1,\ldots,a_r)$ of interval sizes, a composition with no adjacent ones. Since $T_n^*$ is rigid for $n\ge4$, there is no duplication at the presentation level. Its unique reversing anti-automorphism is axis reflection, so the path is blue precisely for palindromic $L$.

After complementing the tiling, take a converse (the node does not change). Equivalently, keep $T_n^*$ and reverse the marked path arcs. Start from the completely descending tournament. The initial $T_n^*$ reverses all natural successive pairs, and the second reversal cancels all those internal to a block. At each seam two rising arcs remain:
- maximum of low block $\to$ minimum of high block;
- minimum of low block $\to$ maximum of high block.

There are no other rising arcs. The two seam arcs are distinct because adjacent singleton blocks were excluded. Denote this tournament $S(L)$. This describes the entire second-shell neighbor multiset before identifying coincident class endpoints. Palindromic $L$ gives an actual blue line, not a speculative edge in a bookkeeping graph.

If $r\ge2$, the cyclic-triangle height of the new neighbor is
$$
 c_3(S(L))=2n-a_1-a_r-2-[a_1=1]-[a_r=1].
$$
Indeed there is no triangle in a block. A seam with sizes $x,y$ supports $x+y-2-[x=1]-[y=1]$ cyclic triples using two vertices on one side and one on the other. A triple occupying three consecutive blocks has to ascend twice and descend across the long edge, giving two choices if the middle size is at least two and four if it is a singleton. No other triples occur. Summing cancels the effect of every internal singleton. The one-part word is transitive, at height zero. Therefore, ignoring the return to the root, the exact blue second-step heights are
$$
 \left\{2n-2a-2: 2\le a\le\lfloor n/2\rfloor\right\}.
$$
The endpoint parameter $a=1$ duplicates the height of $a=2$. Every displayed height is witnessed by the two-block palindrome if $n=2a$, and by $(a,n-2a,a)$ otherwise. The first mate's own height is $n-2$ (the adjacent natural triples). Thus at odd order the second move, except the return, strictly increases cyclic triangles; at even order the balanced two-block word is level.

## Two exact metric rays, and a collision on the proposed surface

If $L=(a,b)$ with $a,b\ge2$, write the two exceptions as $u\to t$ (global min to max) and $v\to s$ (adjacent boundary pair). A Hamiltonian path has no rising arc only if it is descending, which fails at the boundary. With only $u\to t$ the intermediate vertices split independently into two descending runs but $v,s$ must split, giving $2^{n-3}$ paths. With only $v\to s$ there is one path, with all other high vertices first and all other low vertices last. With both there are $2^{n-4}$ paths in either order: each remaining high or low vertex chooses between its two possible descending runs. Hence
$$
 H(S(a,b))=1+2^{n-2}.
$$
So the old incorrect **first**-neighbor count is a genuine **second**-step count at even $n$ (with $a=b$), at the same triangle height as $T_n^*$.

For $L=(a,1,a)$ with $a\ge2$, deleting the middle singleton leaves a transitive spine. In its descending order the bit word for the hub (1 if the hub beats a site) is $1\,0^k\,1\,0\,1^k\,0$, where $k=a-2$. In a binary word starting 1 and ending 0, the total path count is
$$
 2+\#01_{\rm adjacent}+\sum_{i<j\,:,w_i=1,w_j=0}2^{j-i-1}.
$$
The two unbroken runs correspond to the hub at one end of the path. With both runs nonempty, the outgoing contact $i$ and incoming contact $j$ are either inverted as shown (all intervening sites independently choose a run), or form the only other possible arrangement, adjacent 0 then 1. Substitution gives
$$
 H(S(a,1,a))=1+6\cdot2^k+4\cdot4^k+2[k\ge1].
$$
At $n=9$, $T_9^*$ has $(c_3,H)=(7,105)$ and its blue mate $S(4,1,4)$ has $(8,91)$. This is a literal blue step for which the intransitivity height increases and the Hamiltonian count *decreases*.

There is an equality of three seemingly strong surface coordinates at order 8. The two blue mates from $(1,2,2,2,1)$ and $(2,2,2,2)$ both have $c_3=10$, $H=173$, and score multiset $(1,2,2,3,4,5,5,6)$. They are distinct even after merging converses: their sorted per-vertex cyclic-triangle incidences are $(2,2,3,3,4,4,6,6)$ and $(2,2,3,3,5,5,5,5)$ respectively. These incidences are preserved by taking a converse. Thus a $(c_3,H)$ drawing with degree data still folds genuine branches. The path-count equalities are independently verified by counting literal subsets and last vertices in the replay.

## A quantitative obstruction to shallow blue coverage

The union over all orders of path counts at blue distance at most two from the transitive root has **density zero**. Every non-root $S(L)$ on a palindromic composition is strong. The min-to-max rising seam arcs give a climb from the first block to every later block, and a maximum reaches every vertex within its block. For three or more blocks, any vertex of the last block reaches the first directly across a nonadjacent seam; every other vertex can climb into the last. For two blocks the palindrome has both sizes at least two; every high vertex still has a downward arc, since at most one of its arcs to the low block was reversed.

By [Busch’s 2006 strong-tournament bound](https://www.combinatorics.org/ojs/index.php/eljc/article/view/v13i1n3) (Theorem 1 and Corollary 1; exact versions also recorded in THM-4741), a strong tournament has $H\ge C5^{n/3}$ for a constant $C>0$. Thus a non-root count at most $X$ occurs here at $n\le 3\log_5 X+O(1)$. The number of blue presentations available at each order is $O(\rho^{n/2})$, where $\rho=1.839\ldots$ is the tribonacci growth constant. One may use THM-4731's exact blue count stated above. Alternatively, bounding all palindromic cuts by $2^{n/2}$ already suffices for sparseness. Summing the sharper bound over the possible orders yields
$$
 O\bigl(X^{\eta}\bigr),\qquad
 \eta=\frac{3\log\rho}{2\log5}<1.
$$
This rules out universal coverage from the first blue seam and its immediate branches, even if the order is allowed to vary. It does not rule out deeper blue moves, moves through black lines, or a different target-first construction.

## Faithful surface vs useful projection

The underlying simple rooted line graph (all colors, after merging converse classes and removing loops and parallel copies) is not a tree or even planar. At order five the root component already has 10 vertices and 18 simple edges; the blue-only root component has 8 vertices and 8 edges, hence also contains a cycle. At order six the full root component has 34 vertices and 173 simple edges. The orientable genus bound $e\le3v-6+6g$ gives $g\ge13$ even after throwing away multiplicities. At order seven the same exact construction gives 272 vertices and 6032 simple edges in the root component, raising the genus bound to 871. So a plane can portray triangle height or graph distance but cannot retain all the adjacencies without crossings. The cubic domination comparison is precisely about identifying which side information the projection forgets; here even tuple $(c_3,H,\text{scores})$ is not a node identifier.
