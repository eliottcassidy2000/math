# Source closure of the affine-x rational-flow group

2026-10-19. **PROVED, characteristic zero.** The finite-word group of
packet 130 has only the identity as a polynomial map, but its source
t-adic closure contains every polynomial shear fixing `t=0` pointwise.
In fact its entire closure has an explicit description. No historical
novelty claim is made.

## 1. Exact closure theorem

Let `A=K[x][[t]]`, where K has characteristic zero. The topology on source
maps is coordinatewise t-adic convergence: both coordinate differences
eventually belong to `t^N A` for every N. Coefficients of K carry the
discrete topology.

Let Γ be the group of finite words in the source scalar-time flows of

    H=axt²+f(t),   a∈K*,   f∈t²K[t].

Let Γ₀₁ be its subgroup generated by all scalar-time flows of just the
two fixed Hamiltonians

    H₀=xt²,   H₁=(x+1)t².

Then both groups have the same closure, namely

    C={ Φ_(L,g) : L∈K, g∈tK[[t]] },

    Φ_(L,g)(x,t)
       =(x(1+Lt)²+g(t), t/(1+Lt)).                    (1)

Every map in (1) is a continuous formal automorphism of A with Jacobian
one. Its inverse is another such map:

    Φ_(L,g)^−1=Φ_(−L,h),
    h(t)=−(1−Lt)²g(t/(1−Lt)).                        (2)

Consequently the polynomial maps in this closure are exactly

    (x+p(t),t),   p∈tK[t].                           (3)

By the finite-word result of
[packet 130](planar_affine_x_rational_word_rigidity_20261019.md), the
intersection of Γ, and hence of Γ₀₁, with polynomial maps is only the
identity. The closure in the present statement is the ordinary source
t-adic closure of these particular groups.

## 2. Return loops and dense shears

For `c∈K`, write `H_c=(x+c)t²`. In canonical coordinates
`z=1/t,w=xt²`, apply the H₀ flow at time b, followed by the H_c flow
at time −b. The point-map composition is

    (z,w) ↦ (z,w+c[(z+b)^−2−z^−2]),

and therefore, in source coordinates, it is the shear

    (x,t) ↦ (x+c r_b(t),t),
    r_b(t)=(1+bt)^−2−1.                              (4)

The sign and order follow directly from
`dz/ds=1,dw/ds=−d(c/z²)/dz`: the first flow translates z by b, and
the second returns z to its initial value. Formula (4) is the original
source exponential expanded at `t=0`.

With `c=1`, these loops belong to Γ₀₁. Their inverse shears have
correction `−r_b`, and shear composition adds corrections. Thus every
finite integer linear combination of the `r_b`, for `b∈K`, is realized
inside Γ₀₁.

For `n≥1` and `c∈K`, form the finite correction

    q_(n,c)(t)
      =Σ_(j=0)^(n−1) (−1)^(n−1−j) binom(n−1,j)
                       [r_(j+c)(t)−r_j(t)].          (5)

This is a finite integer combination of return loops, so its shear
belongs to Γ₀₁. Expand

    r_b(t)=Σ_(m≥1) (−1)^m(m+1)b^m t^m.

The coefficient operator in (5) is the finite difference
`Δ_1^(n−1)Δ_c` in b, evaluated at zero. It annihilates `b^m` for `m<n`
and sends `b^n` to `n!c`. Hence

    q_(n,c)(t)=(−1)^n(n+1)n!c·t^n+O(t^(n+1)).        (6)

All denominators needed to prescribe this leading coefficient are
invertible in K.

Given any `h∈tK[[t]]`, construct rational corrections `p_n` recursively.
Start with `p_0=0`. If `p_(n−1)≡h mod t^n`, let

    d_n=[t^n](h−p_(n−1)),
    c_n=d_n/[(−1)^n(n+1)n!],
    p_n=p_(n−1)+q_(n,c_n).

Then `p_n≡h mod t^(n+1)`, and `(x+p_n(t),t)` is a finite Γ₀₁ word.
It converges to `(x+h(t),t)`. Moreover each added factor differs from
the identity by order at least n, so this is an actual convergent infinite
product of finite return-loop words. No roots, extension of K, or
coefficient-amplitude Hamiltonians beyond H₀ and H₁ are required.

## 3. Proof of the entire closure description

Packet 130 gives every Γ word in the form (1), with its correction a
rational function regular at `t=0` and belonging to `tK[[t]]`.

Suppose a sequence of these words converges in the source topology.
The coefficient of `t²` in its second coordinate is `−L`. Convergence
forces these L values eventually to be exactly constant. After that,
the second coordinate is fixed, the coefficient of x in the first
coordinate is fixed, and the remaining one-variable correction converges
to an element of `tK[[t]]`. Thus every limit belongs to C. The t-adic
topology has a countable neighborhood basis, so this sequential argument
also describes the topological closure.

Conversely, take `Φ_(L,g)∈C` and set `h(t)=g(t)/(1+Lt)²`.
Section 2 approximates the shear `(x+h(t),t)` by Γ₀₁ words. Apply the
H₀ time-L map after each approximating shear. Since this latter map is

    (x,t) ↦ (x(1+Lt)²,t/(1+Lt)),

the resulting words converge to `Φ_(L,g)`. Hence

    C ⊂ closure(Γ₀₁) ⊂ closure(Γ) ⊂ C,

proving both closure equalities.

Finally, if a map (1) is polynomial, `t/(1+Lt)` forces `L=0` by
coprimality. Its first coordinate is then polynomial precisely when
`g∈tK[t]`. This proves (3).

## 4. An explicit finite-word sequence converging to x+t

For `N≥1`, let `H_N^harm=Σ_(j=1)^N 1/j`, and put

    c_(0,N)=H_N^harm/2,
    c_(j,N)=(−1)^j binom(N,j)/(2j),  1≤j≤N,

    g_N(t)=Σ_(j=0)^N c_(j,N)(1+jt)^−2.               (7)

The elementary binomial identities give

    Σ_j c_(j,N)=0,
    Σ_j c_(j,N)j=−1/2,
    Σ_j c_(j,N)j^m=0,  2≤m≤N.

For the first identity, use
`Σ_(j=1)^N (−1)^(j−1)binom(N,j)/j=H_N^harm`; the latter identity
follows by induction on N, since its successive difference is `1/N`.
The higher moment identities are the vanishing Nth finite difference
of polynomials of degree below N. Expansion of (7) therefore gives

    g_N(t)=t−[(N+2)N!/2]t^(N+1)+O(t^(N+2)).          (8)

Since the coefficients in (7) sum to zero,

    g_N(t)=Σ_(j=1)^N c_(j,N) r_j(t).

Thus `(x+g_N(t),t)` is the product of N loops (4), with
`b=j,c=c_(j,N)`, and is a finite Γ word. This explicit sequence uses
varying H_c; it is separate from the two-fixed-Hamiltonian construction
in section 2. Every g_N is nonpolynomial: at each distinct point
`t=−1/j`, its jth summand has a nonzero double pole that no other
summand can cancel. Nevertheless (8) proves

    (x+g_N(t),t) → (x+t,t).

Its successive quotient shears have corrections
`g_(N+1)−g_N∈t^(N+1)K[[t]]`, giving another explicit convergent
infinite-product presentation.

## 5. Why finite principal-part conservation does not survive

In canonical coordinates the correction corresponding to (7) is

    G_N(z)=z^−2 g_N(1/z)
          =Σ_(j=0)^N c_(j,N)/(z+j)².

Each finite G_N has zero total coefficient at every principal-part
order: at order two this is `Σ_j c_(j,N)=0`, and all other orders
are absent. But its expansion at infinity converges to `z^−3`, whose
order-three principal-part coefficient at zero is 1. Therefore the
finite-pole coefficient-sum invariants from packet 130 are not continuous
in the source topology. This explains the change in the polynomial
intersection without contradicting finite-word rigidity.

This result does not identify this source topology or this generated
group with a joint-boundary completion or with any other previously
studied closure. It does not classify arbitrary Hamiltonian groups or
resolve the planar Jacobian conjecture. All statements use explicit
source identities and finite-difference algebra, not a numerical census.
