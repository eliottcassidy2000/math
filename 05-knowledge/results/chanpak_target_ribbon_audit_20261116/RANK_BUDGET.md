# What rank monotonicity can pay for, and what the transitive rays cannot

## Uniform constant-rank segments would suffice

Let d be the dimension of the transfer, 61 in the convention after the first vertex. For a bottleneck-free word, the rank of the full prefix-product matrix is nonincreasing and at least 2. Thus there are at most d−2 strict drops. Suppose there are constants ρ>1 and 0<c≤1 such that on **every physical rank-constant stretch** of length L,
```
H(after) ≥ c·ρ^L·H(before).
```
Delete the individual extension letters that cause rank drops. At most d−1 constant-rank stretches remain. Each deleted letter preserves or increases H by the odd-cycle packing formula: existing disjoint-cycle configurations are still available after adjoining the vertex. Multiplying the stretch bounds yields
```
H(final) ≥ c^(d−1)·ρ^(N−d+2)·H(initial),
```
where N is the number of appended letters. The same reasoning can be used with a positive potential if its endpoint comparison with H is available. This isolates the missing ingredient as the uniform estimate on rank-constant stretches. A drop budget and a finite list of letters alone do not give it. The exact [rank alias](RANK_CERTIFICATE_STATUS.md) rules out one particular short deterministic rank update.

## Second independent check of sharp transitive growth; a failed shortcut beyond it

Run `python check_transitive_cone.py`. In the 119 raw-state transfer, use exact BFS from the empty profile, accepting nonempty extensions only while H=1. The queue closes on **99** nonempty profile vectors. This is all-order because H is monotone and H=1 exactly for transitive tournaments (a cyclic tournament contains a directed triangle whose term already raises H to at least 3). Thus no transitive-prefix profile can enter from a branch pruned earlier. The 792 complete one-letter extensions of those 99 profiles have maximum H=39. This checks the independent six-label proof of [sharp transitive growth](POSITIVE_ENVELOPE.md).

However the cone generated by all 99 of those profiles is **not** the full reachable cone. The physical ribbon word (0,0,2) is a directed triangle with H=3. An integer linear form stored in `TRANSITIVE_CONE_SEPARATOR.json` is nonnegative on every transitive ray but evaluates to −7 on this word. All dot products, and the complete generation of the 99 vectors, are replayed without a floating linear program. Thus the attractive shortcut “prove the 39 estimate on transitive prefixes, then extend by their conic span” is false. It refutes that extension method, not the proposed all-prefix estimate itself.

## Strong, equal-length rank aliases

There is also an exact obstruction with canonical, same-length encodings and **strong** actual ribbons. In the 61-dimensional model after the first vertex, take the appended words u=(0,2,7,0,2), v=(0,1,7,0,2). They share the three-letter suffix and both have rank 3. Append letter 1 to both: the product ranks are now 3 and 2 respectively. Direct subset-DP checks that the four realized strong tournaments have H=27, 17, 51, 33 (u,v,u1,v1). The rational minors are 36, 32, −108, 72. Run `python check_strong_rank_alias.py` for the exact quotient, rational factorization, and independent physical count checks; `STRONG_RANK_ALIAS.json` is the fixed receipt. This avoids any inference from distinct lengths, nonstrong boundaries, or unused early gap bits.
