# Compatibility as a piece-overlap pushout

**Validated generalization.** This is a standard finite-set universal property applied to the parity invariant, not a claim of categorical novelty or a realizability-topos construction.

## Precise hypotheses

Let S be the common output labels of two traversals. Let A and B index their nonempty pieces, and let `a:S->A`, `b:S->B` record which piece contains each shared label. Fix a common reference parity `pi:S->F2`. Assume every piece has a constant effective shift relative to pi: in the first traversal, label x has parity `pi(x)+u(a(x))`, and in the second it has parity `pi(x)+v(b(x))`.

In the affine-lift setting, pi is the common source-position parity. A detached source1 head has reference parity1; its effective shift is its actual output parity minus1. A low singleton can use reference parity0. The affine ownership proof supplies the unique common-label indexing.

Then compatibility on S is equivalent to

`u(a(x))=v(b(x))` for every x in S.

No condition is imposed by a label occurring in only one traversal.

## Graph and universal-property formulations

Form a bipartite graph with vertices the tagged disjoint union A+B and one edge joining a(x) to b(x) for each x in S. Multiple edges are harmless. The displayed equations say exactly that the assigned bit is constant on each connected component.

The connected-component set is the finite-set pushout `A coproduct_S B`: it is the quotient of A+B by the equivalence relation generated by those edge identifications. Therefore there is a canonical bijection

`Maps(A coproduct_S B,F2)`

`= Maps(A,F2) x_(Maps(S,F2)) Maps(B,F2)`.

Pointwise addition makes these spaces F2-vector spaces. If the graph has k components, the compatibility space has dimension k and2^k assignments. Isolated piece vertices count as components; they have no compatibility restriction from shared labels.

**Important qualification:** these are all abstract shift assignments satisfying common-label parity agreement. Arbitrary assignments need not be realizable by atom orderings with the prescribed lengths, square connectors, endpoints or interval ownership. The graph criterion does not replace those separate checks. In particular, positional count identities can constrain actual realizable assignments even when an isolated vertex has no graph edge.

The common reference pi is also essential. If reference parities disagree between the two traversals, the edge equations are signed affine equations instead. The plain pushout formulation applies after a common reference has been established, as it is for compatible B input pairs.

## Why detached heads add genuine freedom

For a known prefix cut after source1, the head's common-label set is exactly{1}; the tail's is the remaining common source labels. Affine bands keep these owners distinct. The overlap graph thus has two separate components: head-to-head and tail-to-tail. Their effective shifts are independent under the compatibility criterion. The older requirement that those two components carry the same shift imposed an additional, unnecessary equation.

For h low singletons, Q offset blocks and k such fixed prefix cuts, the source-piece matching graph has h+Q+k components (assuming every tail has common labels, as for inputs of size at least42). The old within-word linkage reduced the abstract coloring space by k equations. This count concerns parity compatibility alone, not free choices available to a Hamiltonian constructor.

For a nonprefix cut, the two traversals' pieces may contain different shared-label subsets. A piece on one side can overlap several pieces on the other, connecting components and forcing their shifts equal. Without proving those overlap sets, it is unsafe to treat all pieces as independently colored. The graph formulation explains both the valid prefix-head relaxation and this limitation.

## Composition and categorical scope

Disjoint unions of independent overlap diagrams give products of coloring spaces, because a function on a disjoint union is exactly a pair of functions. Gluing several diagrams is independent of the order in which the same complete set of overlap identifications is imposed: quotient by the generated equivalence relation, or equivalently take the colimit of that fixed diagram. This is the precise associative-gluing statement; omitting an overlap relation is not justified by associativity.

One can linearize without changing the result: `Maps(A,F2)` is the dual of the free F2-vector space on A, and compatibility is the annihilator of the span of edge differences `e_a-e_b`. The quotient has one basis vector per connected component. This supplies a concrete link to familiar categorical linear algebra. It does not identify disjoint-union gluing with tensor product, assert that the coproduct monoidal structure is closed, or build a realizability topos. Any connection to closed symmetric monoidal categories is through ordinary linearization and its standard Hom/tensor setting, not a new mathematical claim.

## Independent finite controls

`test_overlap.py` exhaustively checks finite piece sets up to three pieces on each side and up to three shared-label incidences, comparing all binary assignments with connected-component colorings. It also checks detached-head versus mixed-overlap examples, isolated vertices, disjoint-union factorization, and order-independent closure of a fixed relation set. These controls supplement the elementary proof above.
