## 2. A small Hamiltonian space already exists for every CNF

For a formula with `n` variables and `m` clauses, let `q_j(x)` be the
indicator that clause `j` is false and put `H(x)=sum_j q_j(x)`.
Work first over the rationals in the function space on `{0,1}^n`.
Let `M_H` multiply a function pointwise by `H`.

**S1 (PROVED).** The cyclic space generated by the constant function is

\[
 W_H=\operatorname{span}\{1,H,H^2,\ldots\},\qquad
 \dim W_H=|H(\{0,1\}^n)|\le m+1.                        \tag{1}
\]

Indeed the values lie in `0,...,m`; interpolation gives independence on
the attained values and an annihilating polynomial of that degree.
Moreover

\[
 p_0(H)=\prod_{j=1}^m(1-H/j)=1_{\{H=0\}},\qquad
 \#\mathrm{SAT}=\sum_x p_0(H(x)).                        \tag{2}
\]

The empty product handles `m=0`. Thus even the exact zero-energy projector
belongs to a polynomial-dimensional cyclic space. This fact by itself is
not an efficient SAT algorithm. The quotient
`Q[t]/prod_(j=0)^m(t-j)` is easy to write, but includes an abstract zero
energy whether or not zero is attained by the given formula. Its marked
evaluation map, actual annihilator, or multiplicity readout carries the
missing information. In particular, an efficient oracle for all required
moments `sum_x H(x)^j`, `0<=j<=m`, would compute the exact satisfying count
by (2). Writing that oracle as a linear functional does not construct it.

An invertible Walsh change of basis preserves these statements. It does
not make dense vectors sparse. For `r` disjoint variable triples, put all
seven strict clauses that exclude every triple assignment except `111`
on each triple. This is a `3r`-variable, `7r`-clause formula with one
satisfying assignment. Its `H` takes exactly `r+1` values, yet its
zero-energy projector has **all `2^(3r)` Walsh coefficients nonzero**.
This is an explicit support-size obstruction for a representation that
lists those coefficients, not a lower bound against every SAT algorithm.

### One generator and changing generators have different closure costs

Take instead one clause on each of `r` disjoint triples. All `2^r`
violation patterns occur. The cyclic space of `H=sum q_j` has dimension
`r+1`, but the joint cyclic space containing `1` and invariant under
every `M_(q_j)` has dimension `2^r`.

**Proof.** Applying products of `M_(q_j)` and `I-M_(q_j)` to `1` gives
the `2^r` nonzero indicators of the individual violation patterns.
They have disjoint supports and hence are independent. They also span
the whole algebra of functions of that pattern. QED.

This is the exact coordinate lost by collapsing individual clause states
to their total energy. It is useful when considering changing reset
generators or boundary contexts: closure under one summary operator is
weaker than closure under all operations the proof later uses.

### Two different finite-field failures

If the characteristic is `p>m`, the energy values and the denominators in
(2) remain distinct and invertible. But a scalar count can still vanish:
one strict clause on three variables has seven solutions, whose count is
zero in `F_7`, although `7>m=1`. The projector is nonzero; its sum is zero.
Conversely, taking seven copies of every one of the eight strict clauses
on three variables gives an UNSAT formula with `H(x)=7` everywhere.
In characteristic seven its Hamiltonian vanishes everywhere. These are
different defects: count cancellation and energy wrapping.

The usual Walsh transform over an odd field is invertible because its
square is `2^n I`; in characteristic two that argument fails. A specific
algorithm can avoid these problems by using a suitable representation,
field and readout. Its actual proof and bit complexity must establish that.
The examples above are tests, not a verdict on the inaccessible paper.

## 3. A safe singleton extends 457 of the marked boundary patterns

Keep exactly the eight pentagon/rhombus ports `B` in `Q(zeta55)`, with
their nine unit edges, from
[THM-4614, three-colourable contexts](../../01-canon/theorems/THM-4614-three-colourable-contexts-cannot-change-the-six-colour-port-relation-in-q-zeta55.md).
The audited global geometric census gives at most two port neighbours per
exterior vertex, and just nine possible exterior double-port vertices.
No new geometric point or edge is inferred here.

**H2 (PROVED).** Suppose a supplied proper four-colouring of `G-B`
has a class `C4` containing no neighbour of a port. Suppose another
class `C3` has a colour `c` absent from the prescribed boundary colours
on all its port neighbours. Then that proper six-colouring of `B`
extends to `G`.

**Proof.** Give `C3` the singleton palette `{c}`. On the other five
colours give `C1,C2` disjoint pairs and `C4` the remaining singleton.
There are `binom(5,2)binom(3,2)=30` choices. A vertex in `C1` or `C2`
can block one only if its two differently coloured port neighbours
exhaust that pair. Each such blocker removes three choices; fewer than
ten blockers leave at least one choice. There are at most nine in the
whole field. The singleton classes are safe by their stated hypotheses.
Choosing an available colour at each vertex respects exterior edges
because the class palettes are disjoint. QED.

In particular, if the supplied four-colouring uses at most three classes
on `N(B)`, every prescribed boundary pattern using at most five colours
extends: choose an unused colour for `c`. The exact bare relation has
`30,180,247,113` patterns using `3,4,5,6` colours respectively. Therefore
**all 457 patterns using at most five colours survive this whole class
of exterior contexts**. Only the 113 six-colour patterns can be removed.

For one of those 113 to fail extension, every four-colouring with at most
three interface classes must have **each** interface class seeing all six
boundary colours; a missing colour in any class invokes H2. This is a
necessary search screen, not a forcing gadget. A four-chromatic unit graph
in this field has not been constructed here. The previous
[anchored-profile screens](six_colour_interface_profile_20261015f.md)
remain valid and handle the case of only two interface classes more strongly.

The control exhausts all 167,960 nine-element blocker banks among the
twenty class/pair types. The minimum number of surviving palettes is
exactly three. Its separate restricted-growth enumeration of the actual
port graph reproduces all 570 patterns. These are checks of the sufficient
palette mechanism, not an enumeration of geometric exterior contexts.

## 4. Inverse clocks expose the boundary of a finite compression

For an actual planar Keller pair, let `M=A/D(A)` and use the source
operators `E,t=f-a` from
[the inverse-clock theorem](keller_inverse_clock_20261015f.md). They satisfy
`[E,t]=I`; `E` is bijective, and its explicit polynomial representative
inverse has a factorially small Volterra orbit on a fixed compact set.
Returning that orbit to its original class still costs `E^m`.

Let `P` project onto any finite-dimensional subspace `W` of the actual
module, `Q=I-P`, and write `e=PEP|W`, `z=PtP|W`. Direct expansion gives

\[
 [e,z]=I_W-L,\quad
 L=(PEQtP-PtQEP)|_W,\quad \operatorname{tr}L=\dim W.     \tag{3}
\]

The last identity follows from the zero trace of a finite commutator.
A nonzero finite subspace cannot be invariant under both source operators.
An artificial projected pair can conceal this only by losing the term `L`.
On polynomials of degree below `d`, projected differentiation and
multiplication by `x` give the familiar rank-one defect
`L=d e_(d-1)e_(d-1)^T`. Its rank is one, but its trace is the full dimension.

This boundary mechanism is **already present, in a stronger exact
Laguerre-quotient form, in THM-2856**. Equation (3) transports the lesson
to the actual inverse-clock module; it is not a new claim that no
finite-dimensional Weyl module exists. It also does not exclude the
different successful certificate in
[finite dual-field closure](jc_bilinear_reconstruction_20261013a.md):
that certificate uses actual source polynomials invariant under the
*commuting dual derivations*, not a nonzero cokernel subspace invariant
under the noncommuting pair `E,t`.

### Price an imperfect intertwiner before iterating

For changing operators `A_j`, compressed operators `a_j`, and lifts `J_j`,
set `L_j=A_j J_(j-1)-J_j a_j`. The exact standard telescoping identity is

\[
 A_m\cdots A_1J_0-J_m a_m\cdots a_1
 =\sum_{j=1}^m A_m\cdots A_{j+1}L_j a_{j-1}\cdots a_1. \tag{4}
\]

It is valid with empty products equal to the identity. A norm estimate
must pay the *actual* operators on both sides of each defect. Collatz P1
has a specific positive cone and contraction bound that gives geometric
discounts. An inverse transverse clock can amplify its defect through
the return operator. A Boolean colouring relation needs an actual lift
of each boundary state, not merely a small scalar error. A Fourier
coordinate change has zero intertwining defect when kept in full, but
its compressed readout and representation size are separate costs.

These are operational connections, not equivalences of the prize problems.
The common test is whether a proposed compression commutes with the *next
needed operation* and still supplies the original consumer.
