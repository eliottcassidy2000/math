COMPLETE MERGED METAGRAPH REALIZABILITY:
A COORDINATE-TRANSPORT CERTIFICATE, A LOCAL FOREST TEST,
AND A COHERENT-CUBE COUNTEREXAMPLE

Status: PROVED for the all-order certificate, local sufficient criterion,
Boolean/Fourier lift theorem, and move-count identity. FINITE-EXACT for the
local classifier through order 8 and the order-5 hostile. An all-order
connectivity theorem for the local moves is not claimed.

1. Scope and inheritance

A complete merged metagraph retains tournament classes modulo converse,
all fixed-Hamiltonian-path tilings, and all literal complement lines. Loops
consume two half-edges and parallel lines remain separate. This is the span
of THM-796, with the Hamiltonian-path/automorphism fibres of THM-781. It is
not the quotient of the full single-arc-flip graph.

The preceding distance/Walsh and endpoint-transport work gave necessary
arrangement laws, not sufficient criteria. The present contribution closes
one precise gap: it gives a finite exact constructive certificate, verified
without an atlas or enumeration of all labelled tournaments. A more local
forest certificate is sufficient at every order and is finite-exact through
order 8. Neither result makes the scalar count laws sufficient or efficient
in the size of a compressed node-count description.

Burnside counting, Fourier idempotence, and permutation isomorphism witnesses
are standard tools. Their specialization and combination here, together with
the new counterexample, are the results being recorded. THM-801/809 concern
other finite recursive codecs; their finite injectivity is not treated as an
all-order realizability theorem.

2. The precise input

Fix n >= 3 and put m = (n-1)(n-2)/2 and N = 2^m. Use the path

    0 -> 1 -> ... -> n-1.

Let X = {0,1}^m. Its coordinates are the pairs (i,j) with i+2 <= j, in
lexicographic order. A coordinate bit 1 means i -> j. The adjacent arcs are
fixed by the displayed path. Write T(x) for the resulting tournament.

Complement is Cx = x XOR (N-1). Reflection r permutes coordinate (i,j) to
(n-1-j,n-1-i), without complementing its bit. The true merged class map is
q(x) = [T(x)] modulo vertex relabelling and all-arcs converse.

A candidate expanded wiring consists of:

  - a set H of N half-edges;
  - a fixed-point-free involution c on H, whose two-orbits are literal lines;
  - a surjection pi:H -> U, assigning half-edges to nonempty candidate nodes;
  - one blue/black colour for each c-pair.

Thus loops and parallel lines are intrinsic. A compressed coloured weighted
multigraph can first be expanded this way, counting each loop twice. The
candidate is realizable when there is an isomorphism of this coloured span
to U_true <- X -> X/<C>. Node names need not already be canonical codes.
Additional requested sidecars, such as all-distance matrices, can be checked
against the same coordinate assignment.

3. The exact number of merged nodes without a tournament atlas

For a partition lambda = (lambda_1,...,lambda_t) of n, let a_d be the number
of parts equal to d, and define

    z_lambda = product_d d^(a_d) a_d!,
    c(lambda) = sum_i floor(lambda_i/2)
                + sum_(i<j) gcd(lambda_i,lambda_j).

Define two partition sums:

    A_n = sum_(all parts of lambda odd) 2^c(lambda) / z_lambda,

    S_n = sum_(lambda has at most one part 1,
               and every other part is 2 modulo 4)
          2^c(lambda) / z_lambda.

Then A_n is the number of ordinary tournament isomorphism classes, S_n is
the number of self-converse ordinary classes, and

    K_n = (A_n + S_n)/2                                      (3.1)

is the exact number of converse-merged nodes.

Proof. Apply Burnside to the action of S_n times the two-element converse
group on all orientations of the complete graph. A permutation of cycle
type lambda has c(lambda) orbits on unordered vertex pairs: floor(a/2)
within a cycle of length a, and gcd(a,b) between cycles of lengths a,b.
A compatible pair orbit contributes one free orientation bit.

For the ordinary action, compatibility holds exactly when every vertex cycle
is odd. An even cycle gives an antipodal pair whose endpoints are exchanged
by a power of the permutation, contradicting an invariant orientation.
If every cycle is odd, no such pair-orbit reversal occurs.

For the converse-twisted action, the square of the permutation must be a
tournament automorphism. Hence all vertex cycle lengths are odd or twice
odd. An odd cycle of length at least 3 is impossible: its length-th power
fixes its vertices while applying converse, contradicting every internal
arc. Two fixed vertices are impossible for the same reason. The remaining
possibilities are precisely zero or one fixed vertex and cycles twice odd.
They are sufficient. Within a length-2a cycle with a odd, the antipodal pair
orbit has odd length a and swaps endpoints, so its swap and converse signs
cancel. Every other within-cycle orbit has even length and no final swap.
Cross-cycle pair orbits have even length and no endpoint swap. Thus all
orbits are compatible.

There are n!/z_lambda permutations of a given cycle type. Burnside gives
(3.1). The twisted sum is S_n because every self-converse ordinary orbit
contributes exactly n! pairs (T,sigma) satisfying sigma(T^op)=T, and every
non-self-converse orbit contributes zero. QED.

Small values, obtained by the partition formula:

    n       3      4      5       6        7         8          9
    A_n     2      4     12      56      456      6880     191536
    S_n     2      2      8      12       88       176       2752
    K_n     2      3     10      34      272      3528      97144

The formula enumerates integer partitions, not tournaments. A separate
implementation checks its signed unordered-pair orbit interpretation for
every vertex permutation through n=6.

4. The exact coordinate-transport certificate

THEOREM 1. The candidate wiring in Section 2 is realizable if and only if
it has the following certificate.

  (a) |U| = K_n.

  (b) There is a bijection x:H -> X such that

          x(c h) = C x(h),
          colour(h) is blue iff r x(h) = x(h).

  (c) For each u in U there is one labelled n-vertex tournament R_u.
      For every h with pi(h)=u there are a permutation p_h of {0,...,n-1}
      and a bit epsilon_h such that, for every i<j,

          arc_T(x(h))(i,j)
            = arc_R_u(p_h(i),p_h(j)) XOR epsilon_h.           (4.1)

The value epsilon_h=1 means converse, not tile complement.

Proof of sufficiency. Condition (4.1) puts every candidate fibre inside a
single true converse-merged class. It therefore defines a map

    alpha:U -> U_true,   alpha(u)=[R_u],

with alpha(pi(h))=q(x(h)). Every tournament has a directed Hamiltonian path:
insert a new vertex into a path immediately before its first out-neighbour,
or append it if there is no out-neighbour. The predecessor, when present,
beats the inserted vertex. Induction gives a path through all vertices.
Normalizing that path shows that q:X -> U_true is surjective.

Because x is a bijection, alpha is surjective. By (a), its domain and range
have the same finite size K_n, so alpha is bijective. Consequently each
candidate fibre equals one entire true merged fibre, rather than a proper
subset, a merger of classes, or a split class. Condition (b) identifies the
literal line pairing and its colour. This is the required isomorphism of
spans. Necessity follows by taking actual coordinates, one representative
per class, and isomorphisms/converse-isomorphisms for its tilings. QED.

This is a genuine completeness argument. It replaces pairwise proofs that
representatives are non-isomorphic with global cube coverage and the exact
Burnside node count. Its verifier never searches for an isomorphism: it
checks the supplied permutations and their individual arc equalities.

If the node-count condition is removed, refinements of true fibres may pass.
If the transporter condition is removed, even the much stronger coherent
cube and endpoint constraints can pass on a false wiring; Section 8 gives
an explicit example.

Certificate size is

    O(N(m+n log n) + |U| n^2) bits,

hence O(N n^2). The transporter portion alone uses O(N n log n) bits.
Verification uses O(N n^2) arc and bit checks, coordinate-bijection checking,
and the partition sum. The certificate is exponential in n, but polynomial-
size for an explicit weighted matrix or explicitly listed weighted multigraph:
if k=K_n, then N<=k^2 and n^2=O(log k), giving O(k^2 log k) bits. Verification
is also polynomial in the binary input length, with preliminary mass and
node-count rejection tests before expansion. This does not provide a
polynomial-time method to find a certificate or recognize a candidate.
More succinct descriptions with unlisted nodes require a separate analysis.

For the size bound, the orbit count gives k>=2^binom(n,2)/(2n!). Hence

    k^2/N >= 2^((n-1)(n+2)/2)/(4(n!)^2).

The right side exceeds one at n=7 and increases by the factor
2^(n+1)/(n+1)^2 thereafter; n=3..6 are checked by the displayed table.
The same lower bound gives n^2=O(log k). For a binary-encoded input, first
sum its half-edge mass S and check that S is a power of two with
bitlength(S)-1=m, before forming 2^m. Then reject if 2^binom(n,2)>2kn!.
Passing these tests already bounds N polynomially in the explicit input
size. Exact integer Burnside sums and all subsequent certificate checks
consequently take polynomial bit time. COMPLEXITY_ADDENDUM.txt supplies the
full bit-complexity argument and a separate reduction to four-coloured
simple graph isomorphism. Neither result claims polynomial-time recognition.

No uniqueness of coordinates, representatives, or transporters is asserted.
Many choices differ by markings, automorphisms, and graph symmetries. The
result does not say that graph counts determine wiring.

5. A finite SAT / integer formulation

Theorem 1 is an explicit finite satisfiability criterion. Use:

  - m coordinate bits x_(h,e) for each half-edge;
  - representative arc bits r_(u,a,b), with r_(u,b,a)=1-r_(u,a,b);
  - permutation-matrix bits P_(h,i,a), exactly one 1 in each row and column;
  - a converse bit epsilon_h.

Impose coordinate distinctness, the complement equations on paired
half-edges, and the reflection-fixed colour test. For each i<j and a!=b,

    P_(h,i,a)=P_(h,j,b)=1
      implies arc_T(x(h))(i,j)=r_(pi(h),a,b) XOR epsilon_h.

The left side is a fixed 1 for adjacent path positions and the corresponding
coordinate bit otherwise. These are elementary Boolean constraints; an
integer formulation uses 0/1 variables and standard linearizations of the
finite AND/XOR clauses. Together with |U|=K_n they are exactly Theorem 1.
One need not include a separate non-isomorphism constraint between nodes.

This formulation is stated exactly, not advertised as an efficient solver.
The included code verifies constructive witnesses rather than calling a SAT
package. The full positive certificates for n=3,4,5 are supplied.

6. A more local sufficient certificate

A legal rightward relocation (a,b), with 0<=a<b-1<=n-2, changes the marked
word (0,...,n-1) to

    (0,...,a-1,a+1,...,b,a,b+1,...,n-1).

It remains a directed path precisely when the following existing arcs hold:

    b -> a;
    a-1 -> a+1, if a>0;
    a -> b+1, if b<n-1.                                    (6.1)

These are at most three distinct nonpath coordinates. Re-mark the tournament
along this word to obtain a new tiling. Every such move preserves ordinary
isomorphism class. Reflection preserves converse-merged class. Treat move
edges as undirected; a leftward relocation is the inverse of a rightward one.

THEOREM 2. Conditions (a) and (b) of Theorem 1, together with the following
forest condition, are sufficient at every n:

  In each candidate node fibre, supply a spanning tree whose edges are
  legal one-vertex relocations or reflection in the coordinate cube.

Indeed, every tree edge preserves the true merged class. Hence the entire
fibre belongs to one class, and the same surjection/cardinality argument
from Theorem 1 proves equality of fibres. A tree edge is certified by its
relocation pair and direction, or by the reflection symbol. No unknown
vertex permutation or representative tournament is needed.

The criterion is all-order sufficient. Its necessity for every actual fibre
at every n would require a further connectivity theorem, which is open here.
The finite computations below prove necessity through n=8, on the tiling
fibres HP(T)/Aut(T). They do not automatically prove connectivity of the
larger labelled Hamiltonian-path reconfiguration graph.

A useful independent count check is

    total valid rightward relocations over X
      = N (n^2+n-4)/16.                                    (6.2)

Self-moves are included. There is one endpoint-to-endpoint relocation with
one required bit, 2(n-3) one-endpoint relocations with two required bits,
and binom(n-3,2) internal relocations with three required bits. Summing
N/2 + 2(n-3)N/4 + binom(n-3,2)N/8 proves (6.2).

The standalone classifier unions all legal relocation edges, then all
reflection edges. Each component is contained in a true class by construction.
If the resulting component count equals the independent Burnside count,
then every component is an entire class. This certifies classification
without importing a tournament atlas or running isomorphism searches.

The exact scan is:

    n    tilings     ordinary components    merged components    valid moves
    3          2               2                    2                    1
    4          8               4                    3                    8
    5         64              12                   10                  104
    6       1024              56                   34                 2432
    7      32768             456                  272               106496
    8    2097152            6880                 3528              8912896

All ordinary component sizes are odd. Reflection-fixed ordinary component
counts are 2,2,8,12,88,176. Both checks agree with the independent orbit
formula and the inherited HP/Aut parity law. The order-8 statement scans all
2,097,152 fixed-path tilings, not merely a sample or the complement lines.

A separate direct-arc implementation checks the entire partition through
n=7, agrees with the C++ bit-mask implementation, and independently agrees
with ordinary permutation/converse canonicalization. A second complete n=8 C++ implementation reconstructs the tournament arcs
literally, tests each path word directly, and forms its normalization from
those arcs. It uses no precomputed affine coordinate transforms or move

of the moves, both complete scans, the Burnside count, and the exact

7. Exact coherent Fourier lifting still does not enforce tournament fibres

For clarity, the cube-only gap can also be closed exactly. Let W_d be proposed
ordered half-edge distance matrices, d=0,...,m. Set

    K_j(d)=sum_a (-1)^a binom(d,a) binom(m-d,j-a),
    Q_j=sum_d K_j(d) W_d.

A partition of X with these distance matrices exists if and only if there
are integer numbers Z_u(S), one for every node and coordinate subset S,
satisfying

    sum_u Z_u(S)=N if S is empty, and 0 otherwise;            (7.1)
    sum_S Z_u(S) Z_u(S XOR T)=N Z_u(T), for every u,T;         (7.2)
    Q_j(u,v)=sum_(|S|=j) Z_u(S)Z_v(S).                       (7.3)

Prescribed masses are imposed by Z_u(empty)=M_u>0. To require reflection-
invariant fibres also impose Z_u(rS)=Z_u(S). To require coloured complement
counts, reconstruct f below and check their blue and black sub-sums.

Proof. Necessity is the Walsh transform of Boolean fibre indicators.
Conversely define

    f_u(x)=N^(-1) sum_S Z_u(S) (-1)^(|S intersect x|).

Equation (7.2) gives f_u(x)^2=f_u(x); hence each f_u is 0 or 1. Equation (7.1)
gives sum_u f_u=1, so these are exactly a partition's indicators. Equation
(7.3) gives its distance matrices by the invertible Krawtchouk transform.
Reflection follows by Fourier inversion. QED.

Thus compatible Fourier columns plus their Boolean convolution equations
are sufficient for cube realization, unlike PSD/rank tests on level Gram
matrices alone. They are NOT sufficient for tournament-fibre realization.
The following counterexample meets even this exact cube criterion.

8. The stronger order-5 hostile

Use the coordinate convention of Section 2. Actual merged canonical codes,
meaning least full-arc code under permutations and converse, are

    (0,2,4,5,8,10,12,40,41,76),

with masses

    (1,2,10,1,9,9,11,5,13,3).

Start with the actual node assignment q:X -> U. Define q' by exchanging
only the node labels at the two tilings 4 and 26. Every other assignment
stays unchanged. Both tilings are reflection-fixed and have odd weight.
Their relevant data are

    tiling       node code     complement      complement node code
       4             12            59                    8
      26             41            37                   10.

Nodes 12 and 41 both have sorted score profile (1,2,2,2,3). Nodes 8 and 10
both have sorted score profile (1,1,2,3,3). The four nodes are distinct.
The changed blue line multiplicities are

    node pair        old     new
       8,12           1       0
       8,41           0       1
      10,12           1       2
      10,41           1       0.

Every black line stays unchanged. This is a genuine new coloured weighted
wiring, not merely a relabelling: masses allow only four possible global
node permutations, and each fails the complete blue/black matrix comparison.
The exact verifier checks all four.

The hostile preserves:

  - every node mass, blue degree, black degree, colour type, and loop count;
  - reflection invariance and the complete black incidence matrix;
  - the exact colour-resolved endpoint-current inventories;
  - the stronger colour-resolved destination folded-score-profile rows;
  - every node's signed top Walsh charge;
  - all necessary integral-Gram, PSD, reflection-rank, diagonal-congruence,
    and covariance identities of the distance/Walsh lift;
  - the full Boolean convolution equations (7.1)-(7.3).

For the last two items, compute all W_d and Z from q'. This is an actual
Boolean partition of the actual cube, so every coherent cube identity holds.
The endpoint statements follow because both ends of the switch remain
inside the same respective score shells, hence have the same cyclic-triangle
values too. The code independently recomputes those rows from Hamiltonian
paths and automorphisms of the ORIGINAL tournament representatives, not only
from the new wiring. The top charge is unchanged because the exchanged
coordinates have equal parity.

In the displayed node order, both charge vectors are

    (1,-2,2,-1,-5,7,3,5,-11,1).

The true Gram ranks are (1,4,8,7,7,4,1); the hostile ranks are
(1,4,9,8,7,4,1), within the same reflection caps (1,4,9,12,9,4,1).
Both local covariance matrices have rank 9. Actual class-specific distance
profiles and actual ranks are not asserted to stay unchanged; the point is
that all the general coherent lift laws still hold.

Yet q' is not a tournament-fibre partition. In T(4), the marked word

    (0,4,1,2,3)

is a directed Hamiltonian path, and normalizing along it gives tiling 9.
Thus T(4) and T(9) are isomorphic, both with true merged code 12. But q'(4)
is code 41 and q'(9) is code 12. This directly breaks path transport. The
word is itself the inverse of the legal rightward relocation (1,4) starting
at tiling 9, so the local forest mechanism detects the missing relation too.
An exhaustive 120-permutation check confirms that no isomorphism or converse-
isomorphism sends T(4) to the code-41 representative.

Because the hostile coloured weighted graph is not isomorphic to the actual
one, changing the cube coordinate certificate cannot rescue it as an abstract
complete wiring. This is stronger than merely rejecting one failed choice
of transporters.

The witness verifier is self-contained and imports no existing atlas; it
checks supplied permutations without searching. The demonstration harness
generates its positive examples by exhaustive permutation/converse orbits.
No efficient generation claim is being made. The harness checks
full positive coordinate-transport certificates for n=3,4,5,6, including
6,48,640,15360 individual arc equalities respectively, the signed Burnside
pair-orbit computation through n=6, the hostile, the exact Fourier Boolean
completion, and the independent endpoint inventories. Positive JSON witness
files are included for n=3,4,5. The local classifier and direct-arc checker
supply the further controls described in Section 6.

The useful next theorem is now sharply stated: are the legal one-vertex
relocations connected on every ordinary HP(T)/Aut(T) tiling fibre? The
computational answer is yes through n=8. Proving it all-order would make
the local forest mechanism an all-order necessary-and-sufficient local
transport characterization, with no arbitrary permutation witnesses.
It is not needed for the all-order sufficient theorem already proved.

Preserved/lost boundary: the coordinate-transport certificate retains the
literal tiling, complement partner, reflection colour, and tournament
isomorphism orbit. Walsh Gram levels alone lose cross-level phases; exact
Boolean Fourier completion restores the partition but still loses its
compatibility with tournament re-marking. Endpoint score inventories restore
some row information but not which same-score node receives a line. The
hostile isolates exactly that surviving loss. No inference to unrelated
research problems, graph-count uniqueness, or polynomial-time recognition
of compressed wiring is made.
