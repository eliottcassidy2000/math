  "For the size bound, the orbit count gives k>=2^{binom(n,2)}/(2n!). Thus
  k^2/N>=2^{(n-1)(n+2)/2}/(4(n!)^2). The right side exceeds one at n=7 and
  increases by a factor 2^{n+1}/(n+1)^2 thereafter; n=3..6 are checked by
  the displayed table. The same lower bound gives n^2=O(log k).
  For a binary-encoded input, first sum its half-edge mass S and check that
  S is a power of two with bitlength(S)-1=m, before forming 2^m. Then reject
  if 2^{binom(n,2)}>2kn!. Passing these tests already bounds N polynomially
  in the explicit input size. Exact integer Burnside sums and all subsequent
  certificate checks consequently take polynomial bit time."
