## 1. Inheritance and the proposed change of target

The anchor is **universal coverage by a weaker, honestly typed certificate**,
followed by progressively stronger checks. The niche is the distinction
between prime-factor balance and labelled-vertex balance. The wildcard is
a root-certified positive integer approximating even a negative-cycle
trajectory to arbitrary finite binary and ternary precision.

Closest proved mechanisms:

Canonical hostile: the `3x-1` cycle `5 -> 7 -> 5` has a weak multiplicative
certificate for 5. Corrected near miss: a product of grounded certificates
does not give a grounded certificate for the product of their sources.
Least-used sidecar: the **signed multiset of exact unmatched endpoints**.

Concept board: **universal weak coverage / labelled incidence / factorization
defect / distinguished prime 3 / grounded obligations / local versus ordinary
height**. The new results refine an inherited route; no finite density or

## 2. Universal coverage really is available at the weak level

Put

\[
U(u)=\frac{3u+1}{2^{a(u)}},\qquad a(u)=v_2(3u+1)
\]

on positive odd integers. A **weak receipt for an odd rational r** is a
finite multiset `c` of actual labelled edges `u -> U(u)`, with nonnegative
integer multiplicities, such that

\[
V(c):=\prod_u\left(\frac{u}{U(u)}\right)^{c(u)}=r.\tag{W}
\]

Applegate--Lagarias, [The 3x+1 semigroup, Theorem 1.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0411140),
prove that the semigroup generated by `2` and `(2k+1)/(3k+2)` is exactly
the positive reduced rationals whose denominator is not divisible by 3.

**Proposition W1 (universal odd weak coverage).** Every positive odd integer
has a weak receipt. More generally, every positive rational with odd
numerator and denominator and denominator coprime to 3 has one.

**Proof.** In a representation supplied by that theorem, rewrite each
odd-source generator as

\[
\frac{u}{(3u+1)/2}=2^{1-a(u)}\frac{u}{U(u)}.
\]

The target has 2-adic valuation zero, so the explicit powers of two cancel
exactly. What remains is (W), with the same nonnegative edge multiplicities.
For a general positive integer retain its initial halving word and apply
this to its odd part. This proves coverage of a weaker certificate type,
not coverage by routes to one. Enumerating all finite edge multisets and
checking exact rational products gives a total weak-receipt search by W1;
no efficiency bound is claimed. □

Thus the information-rich objects can be the fibres

\[
\mathcal C_n=\{(n,c):c\text{ satisfies (W) with }r=n\}.
\]

Every positive odd integer has at least one object above it. Multiplication
is `(n,c) * (m,e) = (nm,c+e)` and projects to ordinary multiplication.
The object also stores labelled endpoints, a chosen actual prefix, open
deficit obligations, and any independent root receipts that discharge them.
Those extra fields must be checked; nonempty fibres alone prove only W1.

## 3. The missing information has a free, explicit coordinate system

Let `V` be the free abelian group on symbols `[u]` for positive odd integers.
Define augmentation `epsilon([u])=1`, prime content
`Lambda([u])=(v_p(u))_(p odd)`, and

\[
\partial c=\sum_u c(u)([u]-[U(u)]),\qquad
D_n(c)=\partial c-[n]+[1].\tag{D}
\]

For a weak receipt, `D_n(c)` lies in
`K = ker(Lambda) intersect ker(epsilon)`.
It measures **integer endpoints which prime-factor cancellation erased**.

**Proposition D1 (normal form).** For each odd composite `u`, put

\[
K_u=[u]-\sum_{p\mid u}v_p(u)[p]+(\Omega(u)-1)[1].
\]

The `K_u` form a free abelian basis for `K`. Equivalently, `K` is generated
by the symmetric factorization relations

\[
R(a,b)=[ab]+[1]-[a]-[b].\tag{F}
\]

**Proof.** Subtract `sum_(u composite) D(u) K_u` from any `D in K`.
The remainder is supported on primes and 1. Its prime coordinates vanish
by `Lambda(D)=0`, and its coordinate at 1 vanishes by augmentation.
Independence follows by reading the coefficient of each composite `[u]`.
Successively splitting `u` into prime factors expresses `K_u` as a sum of
relations (F); conversely each (F) lies in K. □

This makes the defect finitely encodable as the **composite coordinates**
of `D`, without pretending that the infinite universe of possible labels
has become bounded. Keep the edges too: a defect alone does not verify
legal transitions or nonnegative multiplicities.

**Proposition D2 (exact fusion law).**

\[
D_{nm}(c+e)=D_n(c)+D_m(e)-R(n,m),\tag{C}
\]

and

\[
R(a,b)+R(ab,c)=R(b,c)+R(a,bc).
\]

Both follow by cancellation of basis symbols. The second identity is the
associative cocycle law for the cost of fusing separate sources. In
particular, even when both input defects vanish, the product defect is
`-R(n,m)`, nonzero for `n,m>1`. Multiplication of receipts is perfectly
associative; it is not composition of single-source paths.

Example: `3 -> 5 -> 1` gives a receipt for 3. Taking `k` copies gives a
receipt for every `3^k`, supported on just two edge types, but

\[
D_{3^k}=k[3]-[3^k]-(k-1)[1].
\]

The infinite family is weakly covered with constant support. Its remaining
problem is fusion, not a missing prime factor. Likewise receipts for 5
and 13 use `5 -> 1` and `13 -> 5 -> 1`; their product certifies 65 weakly
and leaves `[5]+[13]-[65]-[1]`.

The cross-domain map is precise: **labelled edge incidence -> prime
valuation vectors**. It preserves products, loses endpoint identity,
and needs `D` as sidecar. The cheap hostile is the product of two grounded
paths. This is the same type of quotient loss as an exact controller
translation with its source guard omitted; the objects and lost coordinates
are different, so no numerical coincidence is being asserted.

## 4. Strengthening without sacrificing universal prefix coverage

**Proposition A1 (arbitrary actual-prefix anchoring).** For any positive
odd `n` and any finite actual prefix `n -> ... -> d`, there exists a weak
receipt for n containing that prefix with its full edge multiplicities.

**Proof.** The prefix product is `n/d`; append a weak receipt for d from
W1. This works even if convergence of n is unknown. Prefixes may be cut
at their first root visit when ROOT rather than weak coverage is intended. □

**Proposition A2 (the distinguished 3-budget).** Every weak receipt obeys

\[
\sum_u c(u)v_3(u)=v_3(n),\tag{3}
\]

since every `U(u)` is coprime to 3. For `3|n`, a receipt supplied by A1
with a nonempty prefix has `c(n)=1`, no other source divisible by 3, and
`partial c(n)=1`: the first edge spends the entire 3-budget and there is
no edge into n. In particular, this source-surplus refinement is itself
universally attainable on positive odd multiples of 3.

That target class is sufficient for full convergence: for any positive odd
3-unit v, choose `a>=1` with `2^a v = 1 mod 9`. Then
`u=(2^a v-1)/3` is positive, odd, divisible by 3, and `U(u)=v`.
All such u being rooted would therefore root every v. This is a sufficiency
reduction, not a proof that its premise holds.

**Proposition A3 (why neutral addition cannot do every repair).** A
nonnegative neutral packet `h` with `V(h)=1` uses only 3-unit sources,
so its boundary is zero at every label divisible by 3. Consequently,
appending any number of neutral packets to the two-edge receipt for
`3^k`, `k>1`, can never produce a surplus at `3^k` or remove its surplus
at 3. It cannot turn that receipt into a root path for `3^k`.

**Reroute:** source-anchor before simplifying; or replace edges using
signed differences of receipts while verifying that the resulting stock
remains nonnegative. Blindly accumulating multiplicatively neutral
corrections cannot solve this interface problem.

Neutral packets do have nontrivial power on the 3-unit sector. The
reciprocal of the published wild-5 certificate (Applegate--Lagarias,
Table 3), after acceleration, is the edge multiset

`{7:2, 11:1, 17:1, 55:1, 65:1, 83:1}` with product `1/5`.
Adding `5 -> 1` gives a packet of product one whose boundary is

\[
2[7]+[55]+[65]+[5]-[11]-[13]-[49]-[125]-[1].
\]

It preserves prime content while changing genuine endpoint obligations.
The exact checker verifies this identity independently of the paper.

## 5. A sufficient lifting rule with named rooted deficits

**Proposition G1 (grounded-deficit rule).** Let c be any finite nonnegative
multiset of edges of a deterministic map, and let R be a set with supplied
root receipts. If `partial c(n)>0` and every vertex with negative boundary
belongs to R, then the actual trajectory from n reaches R using edges in c.
This does not require (W).

**Proof.** Suppose the supported trajectory from n misses R. If it stops
because its next edge has no stock, let S be its visited vertices including
that terminal vertex. If it repeats, let S be all vertices visited through
the repeated cycle. In either case no positive-stock edge leaves S. Thus
`sum_(v in S) partial c(v)` is minus the incoming stock from outside S and
is at most zero. But no vertex in S has negative boundary, and n has
positive boundary, a contradiction. Determinism then extracts the route;
cut it at its first visit to R and append the supplied root receipt. □

For a weak receipt and R={1}, this condition collapses to zero defect:
the product of surplus labels equals n, already includes n, and cannot
include another label greater than one. With a larger independently
grounded R, nonzero defects are allowed. The audit adds the neutral packet
above to a literal receipt for 7; it has deficits `{1,11,13,49,125}`, all
checked rooted, and extracts `7 -> 11`. This demonstrates the rule's type,
not a newly covered integer or a shortening of its known proof.

**Corollary G2.** A finite nonnegative c with
`partial c=[n]-[1]` exists iff n reaches 1. The forward implication is
G1 for n>1, with n=1 immediate; the reverse is the literal path.
Extra cycles are harmless only when
they do not conceal an ungrounded obligation.

The negative-sheet control uses `U_-(u)=oddpart(3u-1)`:

\[
\frac{25}{37}\frac{37}{55}\frac{11}{1}=5,
\qquad 5\longmapsto7\longmapsto5.
\]

The weak certificate is real; it is not a route from 5. Adding the full
cycle introduces an outgoing edge at 5 but leaves boundary(5)=0. This
distinguishes a marked edge from a source surplus and is rejected by G1.

For a fixed finite stock, G1 gives a cheap exact graph-cut test. Its success
always contains a literal route; it does not bypass trajectory legality.
The possible advantage is proving a uniform symbolic stock identity,
rather than enumerating individual trajectories. Universal grounded
deficit coverage is still **OPEN**.

## 6. The first fusion probe gives a useful exact boundary

Let `delta(u)=[u]-[U(u)]`. Direct cancellation gives

\[
R(a,b)=\delta(ab)-\delta(a)-\delta(b)
 +R(U(a),U(b))+[U(ab)]-[U(a)U(b)].\tag{T}
\]

This transports a fusion problem forward, but creates a **comparison of
two specific integer endpoints**, not merely a formal factor relation.

**Proposition F1.** For all positive odd `a,b>1`,
`U(ab) != U(a)U(b)`. Indeed,

\[
3<\frac{(3a+1)(3b+1)}{3ab+1}<4.
\]

The lower difference is `3a+3b-2>0`; the upper difference is
`3(a-1)(b-1)>0`. Equality of the two odd images would make this ratio a
power of two, impossible between 3 and 4. If
`k=v2(3a+1)+v2(3b+1)-v2(3ab+1)`, then

\[
U(ab)<U(a)U(b)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad k\le1;
\]

the reverse strict inequality is equivalent to `k>=2`. The trivial factor
1 is the equality boundary. Thus synchronous one-step multiplicative
fusion is ruled out entirely, while blocks of different lengths or
checked common futures remain available. Formula (T) has negative edge
coefficients: using it as a rewrite requires a separate stock check.

**Proposition F2 (a checked infinite fusion family).** For any positive
odd `a>1`, choose `k>=1` with `4^k=1 mod 3a` and put

\[
b=4^k+\frac{4^k-1}{3a}.
\]

Then b is an odd integer greater than one, and

\[
ab=4^k a+\frac{4^k-1}{3},\qquad
3ab+1=4^k(3a+1),\qquad U(ab)=U(a).
\]

The first valuation costs `2k+v2(3a+1)`. All positive multiples of
`ord_(3a)(4)` are admissible k, so there are infinitely many such products.
A supplied root certificate for a therefore becomes a root certificate
for ab by increasing only its first valuation by 2k. No root certificate
for b is needed. In particular, `a=3, k=3t` gives

\[
b=\frac{10\cdot64^t-1}{9},\qquad
ab=\frac{10\cdot64^t-1}{3},\qquad
ab\xrightarrow{(6t+1,4)}1.
\]

The first products are `213=3*71`, `13653=3*4551`, and
`873813=3*291271`. This is the inherited `S(u)=4u+1`
[sibling inverse ray](collatz_join_shields_and_lifts_20261004.md) in
multiplicative coordinates, not a new Collatz basin. It provides an actual
successful rule after F1's obstruction. Given weak input receipts for a
and b and a grounded receipt for a, replace the input stocks by this
checked root path for ab; the replacement may remove 3-source edges, as
A3 requires. This is a verified replacement, not a neutral addition.

## 7. A second universal structure: rooted shadows with explicit source error

Extend U to odd negative integers using the same valuation formula. Take
any actual length-m prefix of an odd integer n, positive or negative:

\[
d=U^m(n)=\frac{Pn+B}{Q},\qquad P=3^m,\ Q=2^A.
\]

Fix `h>=0`. Since 4 generates the principal units modulo `3^(m+h+1)`,
choose e in the unique class modulo `3^(m+h)` satisfying

\[
4^e=3d+1\pmod{3^{m+h+1}}.
\]

Take e arbitrarily large in that class, with `e>=2`,
`2e>v2(3d+1)`, and define

\[
r=\frac{Q(4^e-1)-3B}{3P}>0.\tag{S}
\]

**Proposition S1.** These infinitely many positive odd r have the same
first m exact valuations as n, satisfy `r=n mod 3^h`, and reach 1 on the
next odd step with valuation `2e`. Moreover

\[
r-n=\frac{Q(4^e-(3d+1))}{3P},\qquad
v_2(r-n)=A+v_2(3d+1).\tag{E}
\]

**Proof.** The congruence makes (S) integral and (E) divisible by `3^h`.
Its numerator has exact 2-adic valuation `A+v2(3d+1)` because `2e` is
larger. Hence `r=n mod 2^(A+1)`. At each prefix step the perturbation,
after the prior divisions, remains divisible by more than the valuation
being tested, so all m valuations are unchanged. The new endpoint is
`(4^e-1)/3`, an odd integer greater than one, whose next valuation is
exactly `2e` and whose image is 1. Positivity of r implies positivity of
all preceding states. None can be 1 earlier, because U fixes 1 whereas
the m-th state is greater than 1. The generator assertion follows from
`v3(4^(3^j)-1)=j+1` and the size of the principal-unit group. □

This sharpens the inherited hub construction by retaining the target's
actual prefix, a prescribed ternary source residue, and the exact binary
position of the first source difference. It does not create a route from n.

For example, n=27, m=2, h=1 gives e=4 and r=75, with word `(1,2,8)`.
More revealingly, n=-17, m=2, h=1 gives e=5 and r=151, with word
`(1,1,10)`: the positive root-certified shadow agrees with the negative
cycle for the prescribed prefix and modulo 3. The construction works to
arbitrary depths, not just in this example.

Consequently rooted positives meet every odd residue class modulo
`2^k 3^h`, while local agreement alone cannot distinguish a positive root
from a negative cycle. The needed strengthening is **ordinary source
control**. If `r_j=n mod M_j`, `M_j -> infinity`, and `|r_j-n|<M_j`, then
`r_j=n` exactly. Nothing above supplies that inequality; increasing e
usually moves the constructed sources away in ordinary size.

## 8. Helper questions, with their first decisive tests

1. **Can universal coverage survive retaining a genuine trajectory prefix?**
   Yes, at the weak level: A1 proves it for every finite prefix. Test the
   remainder's labelled boundary next, rather than only extending prefixes.
2. **Can neutral packets repair any defect?** No: A3 gives an exact
   invariant at all labels divisible by 3. Use source anchoring and signed,
   nonnegative-stock-preserving replacements. On the remaining 3-unit
   sector, classify which defects are reachable with a fixed finite packet
   library, and exhibit a separating integer linear functional when not.
3. **What is the smallest extra coordinate that prime content omitted?**
   D1 answers algebraically: one integer per composite label appearing in
   the defect. Next test whether useful parametrized families occupy a
   small submodule stable under checked controller substitutions. A finite
   census or low rank alone would not give that closure.
4. **Can fusion be made into a proof-producing rewrite system?** Use (C)
   for bookkeeping and (T) for transport, with exact endpoint joins and
   stock checks. F1 rules out synchronous one-step fusion; F2 supplies an
   infinite successful family through a checked common future. Next search
   for families outside this inherited inverse ray, with terminal obligations
   decreasing under a separately proved rank; do not assume rooted numbers
   are closed under ordinary multiplication.
5. **Can G1 give a better induction interface?** Ask for source-anchored
   weak receipts for multiples of 3 whose negative ports all have checked
   receipts to a fixed grounded set or smaller induction targets. This is
   a precise sufficient target. It remains OPEN and a solution must control
   all symbolic parameters, including Mersenne and negative-cycle shadows.
6. **Can residue-complete rooted shadows become exact-source certificates?**
   S1 proves universal local coverage and exposes the mismatch (E).
   Optimize the least permitted e against ordinary source height, or
   replace the terminal inverse ray by a portfolio of rooted seeds. The
   cheap hostile is n=-17: any argument using only the local prefix and
   congruences will wrongly accept its positive shadows as its own proof.

The resulting ladder is nonempty weak fibre -> marked actual prefix ->
source surplus where the 3-budget supplies it -> grounded deficits ->
extracted ROOT. The first three levels have the stated universal theorems;
the universal passage to grounded deficits is the remaining target.
