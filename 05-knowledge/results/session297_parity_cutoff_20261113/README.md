# Session 297: which loops remember the origin?

Three exact comparisons, with different carriers.

1. [Cyclic Hamilton frames](CYCLIC.md): changing the cut on a marked
   Hamilton cycle gives a torsor of origins, but can change its blue
   color. A reflection-compatible cyclic class has exactly one blue
   rooted tiling at odd order and two at even order. Explicit stabilizer
   formulas prove that all cyclic classes are self-converse through order
   five, and that non-self-converse classes exist at every order from
   six onward. At even order the antipodal-origin pairing reorganizes
   the blue lines into a graph of odd degree on self-converse nodes.
2. [Goldbach, gap coordinates and phase curves](BRIDGES.md): local
   Goldbach and Lemoine multiplicities agree on every odd prime-power
   wheel after doubling one coordinate; the map does not take primes
   to primes. The two gaps {3,10} and the greedy-inverse cycle {4,11}
   have conjugate integer reflections, not interchangeable physical
   realizations. The ball-volume recurrence produces an exact moving
   threshold as the radius varies. MRB provides a different comparison:
   a small residual can cross zero when its kernel changes slightly.
3. [From finite loops to exact cutoffs](CUTOFF.md): a physical modular
   witness within one modulus of its target is already exact. Without
   that control, congruence density, multiplicative closure and all even
   differences can coexist with infinitely many holes. An exact
   finite-state physical digit language would give a cutoff precisely
   when its trimmed failure graph is acyclic; two independent radix
   descriptions plus congruence density would force eventual coverage.

The **cycle-marked** phase transition is unconditional. The radix
cutoff and two-clock routes are conditional; no complete physical
language or sufficient uniform lift bound was obtained. Chan–Pak
remains open. The accompanying cyclic checker enumerates chord masks
independently of the formulas at orders 3–8.
