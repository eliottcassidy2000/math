# Tournament Hamiltonian-path spectrum: history and proof frontier

Let H(T) be the actual number of directed Hamiltonian paths in a finite tournament, counting ordered vertex sequences, and S its range. Rédei's parity theorem places S in the positive odd integers. The assertion S = odd positive integers minus {7,21} remains OPEN. This report distinguishes established exclusion, finite certificates, and all-order construction; it does not assert novelty.

## Exclusion and prior art

`math/01-canon/theorems/THM-1370-h-spectrum-omits-7-21-all-n.md` gives a proof for all orders and explicitly corrects an earlier novelty claim: the 2016 MathOverflow discussion https://mathoverflow.net/questions/232751/the-number-of-hamiltonian-paths-in-a-tournament already states the omissions and strong-order argument. A tournament's strong components are totally ordered and every Hamiltonian path visits them consecutively, so H is the product of their H values. A path in T-v has a legal insertion slot for v; selecting one such extension gives an injection and H(T)>=H(T-v). Moon's induced-strong-subtournament theorem makes the strong-order minima nondecreasing. Small strong spectra give minima f(3)=3,f(4)=5,f(5)=9,f(6)=15,f(7)=25, and 21 is absent at order 6. Thus neither 7 nor 21 can be the count of a strong component, and factorization shows that neither can be a total count. Alternatively Busch's published lower bound H(S)>=5^((n-1)/3) handles strong n>=5 for 7 and n>=7 for 21; the remaining finite cases must still be verified. See `math/05-knowledge/results/tournament_path_defects_20261014e.md` and https://www.combinatorics.org/ojs/index.php/eljc/article/view/v13i1n3 .

This only proves two holes. Search absence, fixed-order gaps and rising floor do not prove that all other odd integers occur. The transcript in `math/05-knowledge/results/hp_spectrum_gaps_kps_S128c106.out` is explicit: n<=6 was exhausted while the larger near-transitive and random searches were not exhaustive and only certify attainment. `math/01-canon/theorems/THM-1370-h-spectrum-omits-7-21-all-n.md` records the correction of the false 'exactly two' inference and the false 7*3^k gap tower. Independently checked here by subset DP: adjacency-bit rows (238,220,216,16,225,206,8,72) form a valid 8-vertex tournament with H=63. No claim of exhaustive computation is made for this check.

## Finite progress and false extrapolations

THM-1370's verified augmentation inventory reports all odd values through 609 other than 7 and 21 attained by order 8; padding with source/sink preserves H. Its strong minima 3,5,9,15,25,45 refute the guessed Leonardo 2F(n)-1 floor at n=8: 41 versus actual 45. The later `math/05-knowledge/results/h_spectrum_multiplicities_20261021.md` separates Moon's known chain formula, Busch's minimum theorem, Moon–Yang characterization, and new finite inventory. It assembles strong witnesses through 80,265 except 1,7,21; finite numerical coverage does not extend by induction without an explicit construction.

`math/01-canon/theorems/THM-4104-selected-order-eleven-strong-ear-solid-interval.md` gives a selected, independently audited finite image containing every odd 429..80,265; combined with multiplicative reduction it reports every allowed odd through 80,405. Its evaluator uses full endpoint and broken-edge data. This is a selected lower image, not a complete order-11 census. Exact controls audit finite statements rather than certify an infinite spectrum.

## Exact reduction and why it is useful

`math/01-canon/theorems/THM-4094-hamiltonian-matching-deficit-and-two-prime-lane-completeness.md` proves that global completeness is equivalent to strong witnesses for both:
* p for every odd prime p != 7;
* 7p for every odd prime p != 3 (including p=7).
Necessity follows by factoring path counts over strong components and H != 7. Sufficiency uses ordinary prime factors plus strong witnesses 49 and 343 for 7-adic exponent >=2; for a single 7 one pairs it with some p !=3 via 7p, and the residual case 7*3^a for a>=2 uses strong 63 and additional 3s. Strong 49,63,343 are finite certified atoms. This turns a vague all-odds existence problem into two precise strong constructions, but neither lane is proved for all primes.

The same theorem's exact deletion identity is H(T)-H(T-v)=sum_P(a_v(P)-1)+O_v, with a_v(P) the number of insertion slots into P and O_v the number of orphan paths that become invalid on deleting v. The triangle C3 already has an orphan. Consequently monotonicity or a scalar H recurrence alone misses genuine paths, invalidating naive insertion arguments.

`math/05-knowledge/results/session25_spectrum_wiring_20261021/session25-spectrum/PROOF.md` proves that every parity-admissible residue modulo every fixed M is realized by strong tournaments with feedback vertex number one, via binary words (1^L 0^L)^t and an exact state (H,A,last bit), choosing 2^L=1 mod the odd part of M and 2^L=0 mod its 2-power part. Its family is extremely sparse, O(log X log log X) values <=X. Thus finite congruence completeness does not prove numerical completeness, and no obstruction depending on a fixed modulus can distinguish 7 or 21 from all realizable values. Check the proof and its local verifier, not just the numerical outputs.

## Proof strategy and decisive tests

1. Seek an explicit, invertible arithmetic construction in either prime lane, ideally with a decreasing size measure and fully retained insertion boundary state. An inductive interval-filling lemma must show that attainable child values cover a provably expanding overlapping interval, not just exhibit large finite intervals.
2. Study the restricted one-feedback-vertex word family using the exact update (H,A,p)->(H,2A+1,1) for appending 1 and (H+A+p,2A,0) for appending 0. Strongness holds for words beginning 1 and ending 0. Exact representation algorithms or an invariant/lower-density obstruction for this family would be meaningful partial advances, but its modular universality says nothing by itself about exact universal representation.
3. For general ears retain Start, End, and broken-adjacency kernel Q(a,b), as in THM-4104, and prove reusable structured cut families yield full integer intervals with overlap. A selected-bank census cannot yield that theorem alone.
4. Target the weaker statements 'all sufficiently large primes in each lane' and an explicit finite exceptional list with independently checkable witness certificates. Multiplication then resolves all odd integers except a finite exceptional set; proving this set exactly {7,21} still requires resolving every surviving finite candidate.

## Sessions 149 and 150: verified packets, progress and limitations

Session 149 ([published index](https://github.com/eliottcassidy2000/math/blob/9b75ef18bff76bd9e4ecb9fb6e8711f7d91c4a3c/00-navigation/S149.md)) proves the one-apex formula already developed in earlier sources by classifying increasing runs around the apex. For a strong one-apex word of chain length m, the first 1 and last 0 contribute at least 2^(m-2), hence H>=2+2^(m-2). Thus membership at any fixed target is decidable by finitely many words. This proves H=13 has no strong one-apex witness, although 13 is globally attained. The family cannot independently solve the exact spectrum. A separately checked two-apex H=80,269 witness was already within earlier published finite coverage, so is not a range record. The m<=22 enumeration and the CSV are restricted-family data, not global absence evidence.

Session 150 ([published index](https://github.com/eliottcassidy2000/math/blob/f48af4d9486ca9b390c635fae04c94262733b77b/00-navigation/S150.md)) extends the finite certified prefix **through 100,001**, beyond THM-4104's 80,405 range: every positive odd integer in that range except 7 and 21 is attained. the Session 150 100,001 certificate describes the conditional last step: strong order-12 witnesses cover prime and 7-times-prime targets in 90,003..100,001; other targets factor into admissible odd counts in the inherited <=90,001 prefix, and ordinal sums realize products. The conclusion depends on the chained 90,001, 84,259 and THM-4104 certificates. The fixed-seed candidate pool was a search, not a census; independent subset-DP/strongness witness checks and direct product checks make the finite result auditable, not an infinite proof. See also the Session 150 status packet for current source and priority boundaries.

Session 150's adaptive rooted insertion starts at C3, repeatedly chooses any existing vertex v and adjoins x with v→x→T−v. Exhaustive pruning through H<=199 yields every odd count 3..199 except 7,21; a separate verifier independently replays 97 witness histories. The completeness of that *bounded family enumeration* rests on proved strict H-growth under each insertion, not on replay alone; it is not induction beyond 199. Every output has a last inserted vertex of indegree one, so the process cannot generate tournaments of minimum indegree >=2, though their counts may still be generated by other tournaments. See the Session 150 adaptive packet.

Additional Session 150 results provide actual all-order partial mathematics, not global completeness: (i) a one-apex-derived injective strong family gives A(X)>sqrt(X)/4 for X>=4, where A counts distinct realized H values <=X. This is not positive density and no literature-priority claim is made (`induction/RESULT.md`). (ii) Fixed-root block insertions trace exponential rays a+b2^k. A proved Dirichlet/modular argument shows any finite set of such rays misses infinitely many simultaneous p and 7p targets; even their ordinal products do not fill those prime lanes when 7 is absent (`prime_induction/RESULT.md`). This blocks only a finite fixed-ray approach, not adaptive roots or infinitely many seeds. (iii) No fixed uniform tournament substitution can obey H(Q[v←T])=aH(T)+b for every T when b!=0, blocking universal H→H+2 wrappers. It does not exclude restricted families, nonuniform interfaces, adaptive quotients, or profile-dependent recurrences (`obstructions/RESULT.md`). These arguments and the one-apex result are earlier Session 149/150 work, not Session 151 discoveries.

The published indices above identify immutable commits and the corresponding packets. The present synthesis independently rechecked only the H=63 witness, not the bulk of the prior certificate chain.

## Additional focused survey: cycle polynomials and ear propagation

The Rédei–Berge odd-cycle formula is a distinct exact route: H(T)=sum over collections C of vertex-disjoint directed odd cycles of 2^|C|. Equivalently H=I(Ω,2) for the independence polynomial of the odd-cycle intersection graph Ω. See `math/01-canon/theorems/THM-1425-the-ocf-is-the-weight-two-shifted-pascal-identity.md` and `THM-4114-ocf-mobius-positivity-tropical-defect-layer-and-opposite-ear-cut-curvature.md`. It implies parity but does not say which independent-set polynomials are realizable by tournament cycle systems; free choice of coefficients would be unjustified. THM-4114 develops induced-set Möbius positivity and ear-cut curvature, preserving cycle conflict data. Cycle-count and disjoint-packing terms both matter.

An earlier ear-response program goes further than proposing interval overlap. `THM-4118-ear-response-lattice-and-stateful-unit-component-intervals.md` proves cut-response values have an exact additive lattice and connected cut components whose permitted steps are 0 or 2 give solid odd intervals; touching intervals glue. But lattice span 2 alone does not force solidity: its stated 544 strong labelled order-five parents all have span 2 yet nonsolid images. An all-order connected-components/overlap mechanism remains open. `THM-4111-uniform-ear-average-and-recursive-selected-bank-growth.md` proves the all-cut sum 2^(n-2)((n+3)H+F_1), F_1 counting orderings with exactly one bad adjacency, and unbounded selected-bank maxima. `THM-4115` strengthens the maximum bound using cut variance, and `THM-4127` obtains Johnson-slice bounds. None proves expanding solid intervals; moment growth is not coverage. `THM-4099-squarefree-gap-transfer-and-mixed-insertion-boundary.md` retains multivertex insertions via a squarefree gap polynomial; proper-face H profiles fail to determine the mixed term. These are inherited mechanisms, not Session 151 discoveries.

The word fold in `math/05-knowledge/results/tournament_tail_kernels_20261021b/README.md` names an exact terminal linear-functional fold for tournament-enumeration cycle-polynomial computations. It is computational compression, not a proof that the range of H is complete. A targeted FRACTRAN search located `math/05-knowledge/results/collatz_procgen_20260922_barrier_atlas.md`, where it concerns universality of arithmetic/Collatz-like dynamical encodings; it does not establish a tournament count construction or transfer theorem. These are algorithms or analogies, not spectrum proofs.
