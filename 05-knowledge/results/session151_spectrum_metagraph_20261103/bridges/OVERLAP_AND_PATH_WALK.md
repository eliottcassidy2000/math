# Path-overlap bridge for a merged metagraph line

## Scope and prior art

All statements below are elementary deductions for labelled finite tournaments. The line convention is the path-preserving chord complement used in THM-643 and THM-4729. THM-4729 already establishes the closed/open line dichotomy and equivalence of the line conventions after converse merging. THM-082 already gives directed-edge deletion/contraction, and `05-knowledge/results/metagraph_spectrum_torus_session16_20261020.md` already records an H-weighted reversible path walk and class masses H/|Aut| (there for its specified continuation rule). The overlap cancellation and cyclic-rotation decomposition below are a direct structural refinement; no claim of literature novelty is made.

## Exact involution and cancellation

Let n≥3, let T have directed Hamiltonian path P=(v1,...,vn), and let K=K_P(T) retain P's n−1 edges while reversing all other arcs. Thus K=T^op xor E(P). For any Hamiltonian path Q let r_P(Q) be the number of its undirected edges contained in P, and h_r(T,P) count such Q in T with r_P(Q)=r.

1. T and K have exactly the directed arcs of P in common, so their only common Hamiltonian path is P: the only spanning directed path using these n−1 arcs is P itself.
2. Reversing the vertex sequence maps every T-path with r=0 bijectively to a K-path with r=0, because every arc involved was reversed. Consequently h_0(T,P)=h_0(K,P). This is a bijection between disjoint sets of sequences, not a common-path identity. Also h_{n−1}=1 on both sides.
3. For n≥3, h_{n−2}(T,P) is n−1 if P is closed (vn→v1), and 0 otherwise. Indeed deleting the one missing P edge cuts P into two oriented intervals; the only order of these blocks distinct from P is the cyclic rotation across the cut, and it requires vn→v1. The n−1 resulting paths are distinct. K has the opposite closure status because v1vn is a chord.

It follows exactly that, denoting the closed and open sides by C and O,

H(C)−H(O) = n−1 + Σ_{r=1}^{n−3} (h_r(C,P)−h_r(O,P)).

The sum is empty for n=3. This formula does **not** establish H(C)≥H(O): the correction can have either sign, and no sign estimate follows from this identity. Nor does it imply an interval, completeness, or any classification of the Hamiltonian-path spectrum. When n=1 or 2 there are no chords, so K=T; the closed/open chord dichotomy and the displayed formula do not apply.

## Reflection and folding

In P's coordinates, let ρ reverse the vertex order and put σ(T)=ρ(T^op). It preserves the forward base path and reverses the chord-grid coordinates with orientation reversal. Since complementing the tournament gives a bijection on Hamiltonian paths by sequence reversal and relabelling preserves them, H(σT)=H(T). Also σK=Kσ. Thus Δ(T)=H(T)−H(KT) satisfies Δ(σT)=Δ(T) and Δ(KT)=−Δ(T). For n≥3, σT=KT is impossible: σ preserves the apex chord orientation, whereas K reverses it. Thus the Klein group generated by σ and K has only four-element orbits and σ-fixed two-element orbits on marked tilings, as already recorded in THM-4732. A merged self-line may still arise through a different relabelling. Equality Δ=0 alone does not establish a loop. The identities hold whether σ fixes T (the blue condition) or not. Do not identify reversal of paths with an automorphism of T.

## Reversible line walk and exact masses

For each labelled T choose a Hamiltonian path uniformly and move to K_P(T), retaining the fixed vertex set. Rédei ensures H(T)>0. P remains a path of K_P(T) and K_P is involutive, so for any labelled T,U the number of paths P taking T to U equals the number taking U to T. Thus π(T) proportional to H(T) satisfies detailed balance. This holds even if the chain is reducible; no connectivity or uniqueness of the stationary distribution is asserted.

Writing m=binom(n,2), double counting (T,P) gives Σ_T H(T)=n!·2^{m−n+1} for n≥1. Therefore π(T)=H(T)/(n!·2^{m−n+1}). Under relabelling a class C contains n!/|Aut(C)| labelled tournaments, so its mass is H(C)/(|Aut(C)|·2^{m−n+1}). The converse-merged mass is the sum over its one or two isomorphism classes: κ H(C)/(|Aut(C)|·2^{m−n+1}), where κ=1 for self-converse classes and 2 otherwise. Relabelling and converse equivariance make the induced quotient walk well defined. This recovers the established tiling-fibre weighting for this exact involution walk, rather than claiming a new weighting.

For n=1,2 K is the identity and the walk stays put; the normalization and mass formulas remain valid. For n≥3, the set of common arcs of T and K recovers the underlying P, and its directions recover P itself; hence on labelled tournaments each distinct nontrivial transition corresponds to one path. Quotient edges can nevertheless carry multiple path presentations and loops, so simple-graph degrees must not replace these multiplicities.

## Reproducible finite check

`check_overlap.py` enumerates all fixed-base-path tilings at n=3,4,5,6 independently by brute-force permutations. It verifies the common-path assertion, explicit reversal bijection, rotational counts and signed decomposition, without inferring anything beyond this finite check. See `check_overlap.out`. The proofs above do not rely on the check.

## Closed side can have fewer paths

An explicit order-five counterexample to closed-side monotonicity is the tournament obtained from the transitive order 0<1<2<3<4 by reversing only 0→4, with P=(0,1,2,3,4). P is closed. The closed-side overlap profile (h_0,...,h_4) is (0,4,0,4,1), so H=9. Its open partner has profile (0,4,6,0,1), so H=11. The rotation term +4 is overtaken by the r=2 correction −6. This is a finite counterexample, not a claim about the general spectrum.

`check_walk.py` independently enumerates all labelled tournaments and directed paths for n=1..4 and verifies the transition involution, pairwise multiplicity balance, mass normalizer, reflection equivariance, and the small-order identity cases; see `check_walk.out`.
