# Complete finite-seed classification of strict product saturation

Date: 2026-11-11. Status: **PROVED**, using the inherited additive
[eventual saturation theorem](../../summand_multiplicand_growth_20261111/summand/TOP_SUFFIX.md).
The companion `classify.py` and `receipt.json` provide **FINITE-EXACT**
controls, not the source of the all-seed quantifier.

## Statement and the information retained

Let S be a nonempty finite set of integers at least 2. Define

    S_0 = S,
    S_(t+1) = S_t union {xy : x,y in S_t, x<y},
    C = union_(t>=0) S_t,       M_t = max S_t.

Saturation always means `S_t = C intersection [1,M_t]`: it concerns
attainable integers, not all integers below the maximum. Both factors
must be available before the same round; ordinary graph reachability is
a different process.

**Classification theorem.** The following conditions are equivalent:

1. `S_t = C intersection [1,M_t]` for every sufficiently large t.
2. The prime-exponent vectors of the seeds have rational rank 1.
3. There are an integer c>=2 and positive integers e_s such that
   `s=c^(e_s)` for every s in S.

If the rank is at least 2, there are attainable integers X_t absent from
S_t for every sufficiently large t such that `X_t/M_t -> 0`. Thus the
failure persists, and the holes eventually occur arbitrarily far below
the numeric maximum. A singleton seed is saturated from round zero.

The prior
[two-seed theorem](../../summand_multiplicand_growth_20261111/multiplicand/SATURATION_OBSTRUCTION.md)
handled two seeds and jointly independent sets. The new case permits
arbitrary relations among any finite number of seeds. Its retained
coordinate is the exposed face or first positive layer of the full prime
valuation cone. Replacing that vector by the scalar logarithm would lose
the obstruction.

Inheritance board: strict two-parent gates / prime valuation cone /
minimum positive face layer / exact synchronous time / competing ray
maxima. Closest proved mechanism: the additive saturation theorem on one
ray. Hostile example: `{2,3,12}` lets the seed12 replace two multiplications
on the sequence `3,6,12,...`; an arbitrary chosen layer seed does not have
the claimed serial clock. The repair is the **largest** seed in that layer.
The previously unused sidecar is the complete finite family of first-layer
geometric progressions, proved below.

## 1. Faithful valuation coordinates and independent extreme rays

Let P be the finite set of primes dividing any seed. Write

    v(n) = (v_p(n))_(p in P),
    K = cone_Q>=0 {v(s):s in S}.

The valuation map is injective on the generated multiplicative monoid,
sends multiplication to vector addition, and preserves equality and thus
the distinct-parent gate. Numeric order is recovered from the vector by
`n=product_p p^(v_p(n))`; it is not replaced by coordinatewise order.

The rational cone K is pointed because every vector is nonnegative. If
its span has dimension at least 2, K has at least two distinct extreme
rays. Every extreme ray is generated by at least one seed vector. A sum
of vectors in K lies on an extreme ray only if all its nonzero summands
lie on that ray. Consequently, for every t, the restriction of S_t to a
ray is **exactly** the process generated by the seeds originally on that
ray. Interior seeds and other rays cannot create new ray elements.

For completeness, a rational linear functional phi exposing any chosen
extreme ray R can be taken with

    phi(v(s)) = 0  for seed vectors on R,
    phi(v(s)) > 0  for every other seed vector.                 (1)

One elementary construction intersects K with `sum_p x_p=1`. The point
on R is a vertex of the convex hull of the normalized seed vectors and
can be strictly separated from the convex hull of the other distinct
normalized vectors. Subtract the value at that vertex times
`sum_p x_p`. The finite strict inequalities have rational coefficients,
so the separator can be chosen rational; clearing denominators gives
integer phi. Nonnegativity is required on K, not on the whole ambient
orthant. This distinction allows negative coefficients in phi.

## 2. A singleton extreme ray has an exact linear-time layer

Suppose an extreme ray contains exactly one seed a. Its entire live
zero layer is `{a}` at every time: producing any other ray element would
require distinct already-live ray elements, and there is only one.

Choose phi as in (1) and put

    mu = min {phi(v(s)):s in S, phi(v(s))>0},
    Bset = {s in S:phi(v(s))=mu},       B = max Bset.

The positive minimum exists because the valuation rank is at least 2.
Every phi-value of a generated integer is a sum of seed phi-values.
Hence there are no positive values smaller than mu. A product in the
mu-layer must combine one mu-layer value with a zero-layer value; two
positive layers would have phi-value at least 2mu. Its zero-layer factor
can only be a. Conversely multiplying any mu-layer value by a is legal:
the two values have different phi-values. Induction gives the exact set

    {x in S_t:phi(v(x))=mu}
       = union_(b in Bset) {b*a^k:0<=k<=t}.                    (2)

Collisions between these progressions cause no problem. In particular,

    max {x in S_t:phi(v(x))=mu} = B*a^t.                       (3)

The next point

    X_t = B*a^(t+1)                                          (4)

belongs to C, is absent from S_t, and arrives exactly at round t+1.
This is a complete first-layer theorem, not a statement that (4) is the
least missing point of all C.

The two-seed subsystem `{a,B}` already has

    M_2 >= a*B*max(a,B) > a*B.

The fixed value aB stays live. Thus for t>=2 its distinct product with a
current maximum gives `M_(t+1)>=aB M_t`. Combining this with (4),

    X_t/M_t <= [a^2/max(a,B)] * B^(-(t-2)) -> 0.              (5)

For an entirely explicit persistent-failure threshold, choose any
integer q>=0 with `B^q > a^2/max(a,B)`. Every t>=2+q has `X_t<M_t`.
This proves the classification's failure direction whenever any extreme
ray has just one seed. It does not require joint independence of S.

**Hostile control.** For `{2,3,12}`, use `phi=v_3`, `a=2`, `mu=1`.
Here `Bset={3,12}` and the exact layer is
`{3*2^k,12*2^k:0<=k<=t}`. Starting at the largest seed B=12 gives the
correct missing point `12*2^(t+1)`. Starting at 3 and claiming that
`3*2^k` first appears at round k is false: for k>=2 it appears by round
k-2. The face-layer statement retains this seed aliasing exactly.

## 3. Multiple seeds on every extreme ray still cannot synchronize

It remains to consider rank at least 2 when every extreme ray contains
at least two distinct seeds. On a ray R let w_R be the primitive
nonnegative integer vector generating it and let

    c_R = product_p p^((w_R)_p).

Every integer vector on the ray is an integer multiple of w_R: if
`q*w_R` is integral for rational q, Bezout's identity for the entries of
the primitive vector forces q to be integral. Thus its seed values have
the form `c_R^e` for a finite set E_R of at least two distinct positive
integer exponents. The restricted synchronous process is exactly strict
additive growth from E_R. Let `d_R=gcd(E_R)` and let m_R(t) denote its
largest exponent.

The inherited additive theorem gives, on each ray, eventual saturation,
cofiniteness after division by d_R, and

    m_R(t+1)=2m_R(t)-d_R.                                  (6)

There are only finitely many rays. Choose one time T after all these
conclusions hold, with every m_R(T) beyond its ray's conductor. Put

    q_R=m_R(T)-d_R > 0,       Q_R=c_R^(q_R).

The exact numeric ray maximum is, for every j>=0,

    max(S_(T+j) on R) = c_R^(d_R) * Q_R^(2^j).              (7)

The integers Q_R are pairwise distinct. If Q_R=Q_R', then
`q_R*w_R=q_R'*w_R'` by unique prime factorization, so the two rays are
the same. Choose two distinct rays called slow and fast with `Q_s<Q_f`.
The next d_s-spaced exponent beyond the slow maximum is attainable by
cofiniteness and absent by maximality. Therefore

    X_(T+j) = c_s^(m_s(T+j)+d_s)
            = c_s^(2d_s) * Q_s^(2^j)                      (8)

is in C but not in S_(T+j), and

    X_(T+j)/M_(T+j)
       <= [c_s^(2d_s)/c_f^(d_f)] * (Q_s/Q_f)^(2^j)
       -> 0.                                             (9)

The proof uses an exact comparison of integers Q_s and Q_f; no numerical
comparison of logarithmic approximations is needed. A concrete threshold
is obtained by repeatedly squaring Q_s and Q_f until the right side of
(9) is less than 1. Interior seeds can only increase the full maximum;
they cannot fill the missing slow-ray value by the face property.

For example `{2,4,3,9}` has two populated rays. On each ray the exponent
seeds are `{1,2}` and `m(t)=2^t+1`. Thus the slow missing value is
`2^(2^t+2)`, whereas the 3-ray maximum is `3^(2^t+1)`. Already at t=0
the former is8 and the latter9; the ratio decreases thereafter. Each
ray saturates in its own order while their union never saturates in the
common numeric order. Mixed interior births do not change this conclusion.

## 4. Rank one is sufficient, and the exact finite classifier

If the seed vectors have rational rank 1, divide any one of them by the
gcd of its entries to obtain a primitive vector w. All other seed
vectors are positive integer multiples of w, by the same Bezout argument.
Writing `c=product_p p^(w_p)` gives `S={c^e:e in E}`. The map `e -> c^e`
is a bijection of the entire generated process: it preserves synchronous
births, distinct parents, and numeric order. For at least two seeds, the
additive saturation theorem therefore proves product saturation. For
one seed the process is constant and saturated. Conversely condition3
plainly implies rank1; Sections2–3 exclude every higher rank.

An exact finite decision procedure is consequently:

1. Factor the finitely many seed integers.
2. Form their prime-exponent matrix and compute its rational rank.
3. Return eventually saturated exactly when this rank is 1.

The algorithm decides the infinite-time property without simulating its
frontier. No efficiency claim for integer factorization is made. The
optional seed1 has valuation zero and creates no new birth because
`1*x=x`; removing it leaves the dynamics unchanged on nonunits. Therefore
for any nonempty finite set of **positive** integers the criterion is
valuation rank at most1, with `{1}` the trivial rank-zero case. Empty
seed sets are excluded because their maximum is undefined.

This theorem is a classification of this specific multiplicative
two-parent grammar. It supplies no implication that a family of tournament
Hamiltonian-path totals is saturated under its separate constructors.
That transfer would require a literal map preserving parent availability,
strict gates, target values, and synchronous time.
