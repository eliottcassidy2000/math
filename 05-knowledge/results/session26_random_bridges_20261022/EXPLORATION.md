## Six live concepts

1. **Continuation observability.** An object can be identified by all its
   future response experiments, not by its present scalar output. The
   one-apex observer and constrained digit reader make this exact.
2. **Intransitivity as a resource.** Odd cycles become cut capacities in
   the one-apex model; disjoint cyclic windows force a feedback budget
   across every actual metagraph edge.
3. **Root-forgetting multiplicity.** A marked deletion apex is not an
   unlabelled tournament. The number of allowable roots and automorphisms
   must be retained before counting classes or tilings.
4. **Signed spectral coordinates.** A positive observable can acquire
   signed coefficients after a transform. Runner height curvature and
   averaged attachment channels give independent exact examples.
5. **Arithmetic reachability versus observation.** The quadratic ideal
   determines which digit states are reachable, while the output kernel
   determines which can be distinguished. These are different quotients.
6. **Profinite visibility versus size.** Every allowable congruence class
   can occur even when a whole structural family omits a positive
   proportion of odd integers in the ordinary counting sense.

## The actual hypothesis/test loops

### Loop 1: replace scalar memory by future tests

The first thought was that the Hamiltonian scalar might be enough to
predict further apex-word insertions. Words 00 and 01 refute this:
both have H=1, but appending 0 yields 1 and 3. Restoring the binary register
and last bit produces an exact observer. Reachability and separating
suffixes then prove its minimal finite state counts. The next test
separated these physical states from the four-dimensional rational span of
Hankel rows: linear rank and deterministic memory are not interchangeable.

An independent digit-word route reached the same question from another
direction. Legal reset blocks generate all translations of a reachable
module. Its observable quotient gives the exact reader size. The
Fibonacci/Lucas difference becomes an ideal-norm calculation, with the
prime5 genuinely arising from the discriminant, rather than a numerical
analogy.

### Loop 2: turn intransitivity into a cut, then a partition

Expanding the apex recurrence yielded a weighted directed cut. Independent
odd-cycle counting gave the same weights. A tempting guess that the
modularly complete family might realize every allowable odd value then
failed: a sharp cut minimum excludes 13 at every order.

The same inversion cells form a Ferrers diagram, and their distances are
exactly hook lengths. Hook and rectangle extremizers explain the lower
and upper counts, rather than merely fitting them. This is a weighted
hook statistic, not the standard tableau hook-product formula.

### Loop 3: recover the population law, not just isolated gaps

Writing the cut as a product of a forward binary number and a reversed
complementary binary number leaves a uniformly bounded error at the
relevant scale. Disjoint endpoint prefixes give an exact coupling to two
independent uniform variables. The resulting product distribution proves
a counting law with square-root error, and therefore a positive lower
density of omitted odd values in the one-apex spectrum.

This does not assert that the density of the attained set exists.
Class-counting by H is restricted to strong cores: unrestricted transitive
padding gives infinitely many classes at any fixed realized H.

### Loop 4: pay the root-forgetting and wiring costs

The intersection of cyclic triangles classifies deletable apices.
That closes ordinary, self-converse and merged one-apex class counts.
Symmetric Hamiltonian words then give blue-incidence counts, not just
class counts.

Disjoint path triples force the feedback-number sum across every edge.
At order eight, the unique two-vertex hitting set {3,6} reduces all
remaining internal one-apex wiring to twelve configurations and their
companions. Actual edge orbits give two paths and a double edge.
At order nine and above the entire induced stratum is edgeless.

### Loop 5: average only after preserving the ports

For a fixed exceptional core, a Hamiltonian word splits into ordered core
paths and increasing base runs. Keeping every entry, exit and empty gap
gives an exact expected-count transform. It recovers the ordered cover
profile, but not the tournament itself. Its leading channels also
supply fixed-feedback class and tiling asymptotics.

A separate exact slot automaton retains those same ports without averaging.
It is an instrument for marked bounded-feedback presentations, not an
unrestricted tournament canonizer or a minimal-state theorem.

### Loop 6: repair the old analogy instead of inheriting it

The archive sweep found a truncated function incorrectly treated as
reciprocal-odd, a numerical Coxeter pattern written as a causal theorem,
and a correction banner that still repeated a later-retracted uniform
runner-denominator claim. Narrow repairs preserve the valid formulas and
historical inspiration while removing invalid implications.

The denominator hostile has a positive survivor: the family
{1,...,6,L,2L,...,7L}, L=lcm(1,...,Q), for integers Q>=14,
has exact maximum clearance 1/8,
yet no rational time of denominator at most Q is lonely. A generous
clearance margin is not a bounded clock certificate.

## An exact electrical-energy lens, including the missing field

This is a direct algebraic corollary of the cut formula, not an identification
of arbitrary tournaments with physical circuits.

For a binary apex word b of length m, put w_1=2 and
w_d=2^(d-1) for d>=2. Give the complete undirected graph on the base
vertices conductance w_|i-j|. Let L be its weighted graph Laplacian and put

    d_j = sum_(i>j) w_(i-j) - sum_(i<j) w_(j-i).

Then the exact identity is

    H(b) = 1 + (b^T L b)/2 + (d dot b)/2.

For each pair, twice the directed cut contribution is the squared voltage
difference plus its signed difference. Summing proves the formula.

The nonnegative quadratic term is an electrical cut energy. The linear
field is indispensable: words 10 and 01 have the same energy but H=3
and H=1. Forgetting the field therefore destroys the Hamiltonian count.
Complementing all bits preserves the energy and negates the field;
reversing the bit word with the same reference-order weights also negates
the field. Their composition
(reverse-complement) preserves H, as converse should.

In particular,

    H(b)+H(1-b) = 2+b^T L b,
    H(b)-H(1-b) = d dot b.

Under independent fair bits, the field averages to zero. Since
sum_(i<j) w_(j-i)=2^m-2 for m>=1,

    E H = 2^(m-2)+1/2.

This exactly agrees with the k=1 random-attachment transform. It does
not replace the actual word by its mean or impose a loneliness predicate
on a circuit. The map, preserved statistic, lost coordinate and hostile
are all explicit.

## Limits of the common viewpoint

These results do not show that all mathematics is secretly a tournament.
They show when a proposed representation has enough information for a
specific family of experiments. The successful bridges retain an actual
operation, an exact statistic, and a repair for the data being forgotten.

Full Hamiltonian-count attainment, unrestricted tournament enumeration,
complete full-metagraph wiring/bridges, LRC(14), the native 153N source
ledger, general Collatz and the six-color unit-distance forcing problem
remain outside the proved conclusions.
