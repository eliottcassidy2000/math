# Recovery map: roles of 101, 19, 79, 169

This is a source-and-operation map, not a claim that occurrences of the same
integer are equivalent. Searches had three waves: exact rational-cycle
phrases; isolated numbers in Collatz/tournament/clock notes; and older
Fourier, finite-field, square-sum, and geometric sidecars. Page numbers,
theorem IDs, dates, journal volume 79, and copied source trees were excluded
as mathematical evidence. Distinct files in one campaign are not counted
as distinct discoveries. The map is selective, not an exhaustive corpus
inventory. [source_hashes.json](source_hashes.json) pins the inspected sources.

Status words below report the source's scoped result or explicitly identify
an analogy/lead. Only the elementary claims used in README.md are proved or
recomputed by this packet; whole historical papers have not been re-audited.

## 101: six earlier campaigns and a changing arithmetic role

1. **September 21, squarefree guards — PROVED, inherited.**
   [Original squarefree note](../collatz_guards_20260921_squarefree.md):
   `128-27=101`, unit carry 19, fixed point `19/101`, phase numerators
   `19,79,169`, and the 4,985 cutoff. The denominator is an active return
   resonance. This supplies P1/P2's mechanism and the terminal-node control.
2. **September 25–26, sporadic cycles — corrected FINITE-EXACT evidence.**
   [Free/sporadic cycles](../procgen_sporadic_20260926_free_and_sporadic_cycles.md):
   a `3x+14303` cycle with minimum 101 has shortcut period 2,155 and 1,092
   odd steps. An earlier period-2,000 search missed it. Here 101 is a
   **minimum**, not a closure denominator. It warns against promoting our
   finite lift table to a theorem without the digit-lifting proof.
3. **September 27, Catalan/Ramsey — corrected graph distinction.**
   [Catalan/Ramsey note](../collatz_catalan_ramsey_20260927.md): P101 is a
   Paley **graph**, since 101 is 1 mod4. The earlier cosmetic tournament
   interpretation is corrected in the source. Our tournament uses native
   waiting times on an odd clock, not quadratic residues modulo 101.
4. **October 14, successor holonomy — PROVED guarded families.**
   [Successor holonomy](../collatz_successor_holonomy_20261014b.md): the family
   `(32*4^(10+27s)-101)/81` has a subtractive carry 101. A finite return does
   not determine the sign of real descent. Retaining the carry is directly
   useful; identifying this 101 with a denominator would be a type error.
5. **October 19, adjacent returns/reset — PROVED with OPEN coverage.**
   [Odd adjacent returns](../collatz_odd_adjacent_return_20261019.md) and
   [return-rank obstruction](../collatz_return_rank_obstruction_20261019.md)
   use `(81x+101)/512`, fixed point `101/431`, and finite repeated-block fuel.
   The roles have exchanged: 101 is the numerator, 431 the denominator.
   This is a real operation-level comparison with the present Hensel clocks.
6. **October 29, selective targets and denominator attachments — PROVED.**
   [Session 100](../session100_selective_collatz_targets_20261029/bijections/VALUATION_CONJUGACY_AND_INTEGER_SIDECAR.md),
   [our preceding recurrence packet](../collatz_fermat_reframes_20261029/README.md),
   and [Session 101](../session101_denominator_connections_20261029/squarefree/README.md)
   distinguish inverse prehistory (denominator persists), periodic reclosure
   after an append (denominator can change), and a finite filtered window
   (the 4,985/8,600 difference). These are three different operations.

The [existing 40-source provenance map](../session101_denominator_connections_20261029/history/PROVENANCE_MAP.md)
already audits this 101/35/175 lineage. We inherit that work instead of
claiming to rediscover it. The present new step is the all-exponent lift,
phase-dependent window formula, and timed-port algebra.

## 19: cycle value, phase obstruction, and genuine tournament asymmetry

1. **September 21:** numerator 19 of the 101-denominator cycle. Its role is
   the affine carry and the starting phase value, not a multiplier prime.
2. **September 22, Paley/Fano design — PROVED / FINITE-EXACT.**
   [Paley/Fano note](../collatz_mod6_20260922_paley_fano_octonion_design.md),
   S21–S24: P19 has a cyclic-triple 2-design; arithmetic-progression triples
   are cyclic because `19=3 mod8`. A non-cyclic Steiner triple system on
   19 vertices is found inside it, whereas progression blocks obstruct the
   proposed cyclic partition. This is an earlier example of incidence
   surviving while a requested symmetry fails. It does not identify that
   design with our three zero events.
3. **October 1, two-sheet clocks — FINITE-EXACT within stated range.**
   THM-4532
   [tournament-clocks-two-sheet-clocks-all-odd-tournaments-and-the-syracuse-clock](../../../01-canon/theorems/THM-4532-tournament-clocks-two-sheet-clocks-all-odd-tournaments-and-the-syracuse-clock.md)
   includes D19 on 38 vertices. Its anti-circulant two-sheet structure is a
   useful earlier model of a clock plus a named sheet. It is not our
   three-phase skew product, and the all-r all-odd claim remains separate.
4. **October 13, residue fields — corrected PROVED statement.**
   [Five manuscripts, section 4](../five_manuscripts_chowla_collatz_residue_bsd_20261013.md):
   19 and 79 have order 10 modulo 55, with fifth power -1. Thus the relevant
   conjugation-fixed residue fields of `Q(zeta_55)` have degree 10, and
   conjugation is the fifth Frobenius power. This is a genuine field/phase
   mechanism. It is not permission to replace `Z/p^e` by a field.
5. **October 18, first-arrival clocks — PROVED scoped identities.**
   THM-4690
   [first-arrival-ladder-port-and-integer-clock-decompositions-and-the-nineteen-adic-copy-obstruction](../../../01-canon/theorems/THM-4690-first-arrival-ladder-port-and-integer-clock-decompositions-and-the-nineteen-adic-copy-obstruction.md)
   separates literal clocks, port-diagonal energy, and merged energy, then
   gives a 19-adic obstruction to unit-distance copy connections. The first
   part is closely relevant: evaluating one signed spectral mode is not the
   same as retaining all arrival clocks. The 19-adic geometry is orthogonal
   evidence of an arithmetic obstruction, not a Collatz theorem.
6. **October 21, tournament spectra — FINITE-EXACT comparison.**
   [Tournament pseudoprimes and Camion tests](../tournament_pseudoprimes_camion_fermat_20261021.md)
   distinguishes rotational and Paley tournaments at order 19; rotational
   examples beat Paley in the reported circulant path-count census. Symmetry
   type, rather than merely vertex count, changes the response. Our native
   clock tournament is specifically rotational.
7. **Recovered exact probe in this packet:** the old `7x+169` cycle with
   least element 79 contains `361=19^2`. This makes 19 an exceptional
   square-prime in a second rational cycle. The full cycle and its local
   repair are recomputed here; it is an instance of the older family below.

## 79: at least six distinct dated lanes, with different meanings

1. **September 21:** the middle phase numerator in `19/101,79/101,169/101`.
2. **September 29, perfect-power clocks — PROVED dictionary and finite census.**
   [Necklace/fair-split note](../collatz_necklace_20260929_fair_splits_power_clocks_basins.md)
   and its [audit](../collatz_necklace_20260929_audit.md) list least element
   79 among the primitive `7x+169` cycles. Our exact instance is
   `79 ->361 ->337 ->79`, with valuations `(1,3,5)`. Scaling by 169 gives
   the rational 7-map used in P1/P2. This is the strongest recovered
   arithmetic connection among the requested numbers.
3. **September 30, rational-cycle census — FINITE-EXACT.**
   [Mediant tree, section 4](../collatz_mediant_tree_K_20260930.md): denominator
   79 was absent from the first word-length box but appears by a direct
   `3x+79` search (listed cycle minima include 1,7,233,265). The denominator
   is now 79 itself. This is another warning that an empty bounded word
   census is not nonexistence.
4. **October 13:** the degree-10 residue-field occurrence shared with 19,
   described above. The finite-field role is structurally different from
   the p-adic ring role.
5. **October 15, special primes — cited theory plus finite arithmetic.**
   [Special-prime note](../special_primes_curling_genus_zero_langlands_20261015.md)
   uses 79 as a nonexample to a proposed supersingular/Paley saturation
   pattern: the reported rational supersingular count is 5 against total 7.
   The same note explicitly labels the overlap of several special-prime
   lists as NUMEROLOGY. We do not turn these coincidences into dependencies.
6. **October 21:** in the tournament-spectrum note, 79 is a missing strong
   Hamiltonian-path count at order 9 in the stated finite census. This is a
   count at a fixed order, not a universal forbidden integer.
7. **October 26, power clocks — exact finite order.**
   [Clock arrows](../three_quantities_fortyfour_five_dudeney_fermat_clock_arrows_20261026.md)
   and its [audit](../three_quantities_fortyfour_five_dudeney_fermat_clock_arrows_20261026_audit.md)
   include 79 among the moduli where powers of 2 give a tournament connection
   set: `ord_79(2)=39` is odd and half of 78. In contrast `ord_19(2)=18`, so
   powers of 2 modulo 19 contain both directions. This is a useful real
   distinction between the two primes, independently of their membership
   in the same rational cycle.

## 169: seven dated mechanisms, not one universal square object

1. **September 17, Pell/Berggren geometry — PROVED inherited recurrence.**
   [Pythagorean semicircle note](../collatz_mod6_20260917_pythagorean_semicircle.md)
   has the near-isosceles triangle `(119,120,169)` on the Pell ray, with
   hypotenuse recurrence `c_(k+1)=6c_k-c_(k-1)`. Here 169 is a hypotenuse,
   not a Collatz state. The exact recurrence is useful as a comparison;
   there is no claimed intertwiner with our affine return.
2. **September 21:** `169=13^2` is the offending phase numerator requiring
   the `f+13` squarefree repair. This exact prime valuation, not merely its
   size, creates the local exception.
3. **September 22, square-sum paths — PROVED construction with sourced origin.**
   [Square-sum Hamiltonicity, section 6](../collatz_mod6_20260922_w6_square_sum_hamiltonicity.md)
   includes 169 among the certified junction sums in Gerbicz's 25-fold
   blow-up. The transferable mechanism is **checking actual junctions**;
   a common square value does not identify the graphs or dynamics.
4. **September 29, Eisenstein clock — exact identity.**
   The necklace note gives
   `512-343=169`, `8-7*zeta=(3-zeta)^2`, and `Norm(3-zeta)=13` for
   `zeta^2+zeta+1=0`. This explains why the 7-word's slope gap is a square
   and why its first 13-adic curvature occurs one level later. The
   identity is verified independently in the source audit; our return
   table and squarefree limit 63 are a new use of that existing family.
5. **October 7, digit-factorial dynamics — reported exact finite portrait.**
   [Completion synthesis](../collatz_completion_synthesis_20261007b.md)
   records the decimal cycle `(169,363601,1454)`. Its digit-histogram carrier
   evaluates the next step exactly; a Collatz valuation multiset does not.
   The shared 169 is numerical, but the contrast between lawful and unlawful
   quotient carriers is relevant. No map between these cycles is asserted.
6. **October 12, finite-field unit-distance graph — FINITE-EXACT bounds.**
   [Logic lenses, section 10](../logic_lenses_jc2_collatz_hn_20261012.md)
   uses `F_169` and its norm-one torus, with a reported 36-point independent
   set. Its colouring conclusion has its own status boundary; we do not
   inherit a new chromatic-number claim. This source suggested our decisive
   ring/field hostile: translation has order 13 in the field but order 169
   in `Z/169`. A cardinality-preserving coordinate change loses the clock.
7. **October 21, Camion–Wieferich arithmetic — FINITE-EXACT plus criterion.**
   [Camion note](../tournament_pseudoprimes_camion_fermat_20261021.md) and
   [audit](../tournament_pseudoprimes_audit_20261021.md) give 9 and 169 as the
   prime-square exceptions in their stated range. A higher prime valuation
   changes a tournament-count congruence. This is analogous to a p-adic
   lift changing our zero clock, but the defining arithmetic functions
   differ; no common Wieferich condition has been proved here.

## Older connection that changed the proof strategy

The July 25 bispectrum work is earlier and more directly useful than most
same-number hits. THM-2312
[sparse-root-bispectrum-positive-word-current](../../../01-canon/theorems/THM-2312-sparse-root-bispectrum-positive-word-current.md)
already separates relative phase from absolute root address. THM-2319
[crt-unit-bispectrum-needle-and-mixed-polarization-no-go](../../../01-canon/theorems/THM-2319-crt-unit-bispectrum-needle-and-mixed-polarization-no-go.md)
warns that primitive-character or separately averaged data do not ensure
same-source incidence. These are the exact guardrails behind P4 versus P5:
the timed product at one shared source restores a point indicator, while
the averaged three-deck only reconstructs a mask up to translation.

The connection map is therefore operational:

| Source | Target/map | Preserved | Lost or required sidecar | Decisive test |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Affine p-adic return | Clock index via digit lift | Actual return order | Prime-power precision and carry | All roots by independent powers |
| Combined native clock | Unlabelled alarm span | Linear alarm responses | Two pure phase modes | Rank `3p^e-2` |
| Alarm span | Algebra of timed products | Same-source incidence | Closure costs two dimensions | Two-port product is a point indicator |
| Mask | Averaged two-deck | Pair distances | Reflection and absolute phase | Mirror gives same two-deck |
| Mask | Averaged three-deck | Relative oriented shape | Absolute translation and terminal anchor | One third-port bit; translated hostile |
| `Z/169` | Any set bijection to `F_169` | Cardinality only | Integer carry, additive order | Period 169 versus 13 |
| Clock | Rotational tournament | Half-clock orientation | Exact gaps and integer valuation guards | Cyclic/transitive type reverses by e=4 |

The two-port point-indicator theorem is a positive closure result. It does
not automatically supply a small representation, a global Collatz rank, a
square-sum construction, or a general tournament reconstruction theorem.
