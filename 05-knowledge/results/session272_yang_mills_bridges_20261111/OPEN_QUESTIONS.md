# Five extension programs and their first decisive tests

**OPEN / CONDITIONAL**, tied to the pinned trusted construction. These are
research targets, not claims of solved Yang–Mills obligations.

## Q1. Full volume convergence and uniqueness

Define a common state space for fixed-volume continuum rows and all extracted
hierarchies, distinguishing separated distributions, contact prescriptions,
and selected phases. Find a countable observer algebra determining that
carrier. Prove volume comparison estimates or rigidity making every candidate
agree on that algebra. Then LM1 upgrades compactness to full convergence.

First test: does the proposed family distinguish two candidate extensions
that differ by a diagonal distribution? If it only sees separated values,
state uniqueness in that quotient. Separately test two distinct full-support
phase measures with identical chosen averages. Do not use common refinement
of arbitrary subsequences; even and odd indices need not intersect.

Useful positive target: prove that the compressed phase algebra is scalar by
zero variance of actual generators. Irreducibility within each phase is not
this assertion. Different blocking penalties, boundary conditions or field
normalizations require an additional universality comparison.

## Q2. Continuum probability measure

Specify whether the carrier is random gauge connections modulo gauge,
curvature distributions, local observable distributions, or a countable
coordinate model. A measure on one is not automatically a measure on another.
Push actual lattice variables to that common carrier. Prove tightness, joint
consistency and uniform integrability for the required moments. Establish
ordinary positive definiteness, determinacy and contact agreement. Check OS
positivity separately if reconstruction uses it.

First test: evaluate a finite Gram matrix for overlapping/repeated smeared
variables under the actual contact prescription. A negative principal minor
blocks that prescription's classical positive-moment route. Passing finitely
many minors is not a theorem. Zero-field/white-noise equality on separated
configurations is the indispensable hostile control.

## Q3. Uniform mass gap

Fix physical units and the parameters in which uniformity is sought. Seek a
common exponential rate on a dense family, or a low-energy visibility bound
with constants uniform in those parameters. Source polynomial softness is a
baseline and a useful error estimate, not the missing empty interval.

First test: compute probe normalizations and smallest observable overlaps in
a low-energy truncation. If their inverse grows like ε⁻¹ as in S2, a proposed
uniform bound is measuring weak coupling rather than excluding the state.
A numerical finite matrix can motivate a bound, not certify its continuum
uniformity. Finite positive gaps may shrink after either limit.

## Q4. Wilson loops and area law

Define growing-link-support lattice holonomies in actual representations;
compare their continuum approximations, including perimeter/cusp subtraction
where required. Relate them to the local insertion/OPE algebra by a proved
limit or approximation map. For rectangles, identify the charged-sector
transfer energy and prove a lower linear bound E_flux(R)≥σR−c together with
sufficient overlap/boundary control. Retain topology and global flux sectors.

First tests: reproduce both the disk and torus Z₂ formulas, retain the two
complementary sheets, and check noncontractible holonomies. A Gaussian
covariance ansatz must survive the equal-variance Rademacher hostile. For the
actual model, strong-coupling estimates at fixed spacing do not cover the
source's running β→∞ regime without a new scale argument.

## Q5. Single particles and energy–momentum spectrum

After constructing the required representation, examine the joint
translation PVM and its invariant-mass pushforward. Show a nonzero mass-shell
component with controlled local-observable overlaps, and specify isolation
from other masses/thresholds. Scattering or stability conclusions need their
own localization and spectral hypotheses.

First test: distinguish a pure continuous mass distribution above a positive
threshold from a distribution with an isolated atom. Both are compatible with
a vacuum gap. Distinguish an atom in invariant mass from an energy eigenvalue
at fixed total momentum. The UV family U1 gives the same logarithmic trace
coefficient in gapped, gapless and isolated-mass variants.

## Prioritized bridge conjectures, not new named canon claims

A. Actual phase/volume-uniform quantitative visibility for an energy-localized
physical probe family. Need a controlled infinite family if no finite bank
can detect every low-energy sector.

B. Candidate-limit rigidity of an operation-closed observer algebra, with
contact and phase factors separated before attempting uniqueness.

C. A positive and tight common field-coordinate realization of the specified
contact prescription, then identification of its Schwinger moments.

D. A flux-sector energy comparison compatible with weak-coupling blocking and
macroscopic loop geometry.

Each is intentionally stronger and more specific than a slogan. Countermodels
here identify exactly which additional claim a successful proof must use.
