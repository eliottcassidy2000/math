## 2. Scalar spectral calculus: what the exponent really means

Let H>=0 self-adjoint, P0 its zero-energy projection, and x perpendicular to ker H. Put ν_x(B)=<x,1_B(H)x>, F_x(u)=ν_x((0,u]), C_x(t)=<x,e^(−tH)x>.

**Lemma S1 (PROVED).** If F_x(u)<=M u² for 0<u<=1 and ||x||²=N, then for all t>0,

`C_x(t) <= 2M/t² + N e^(−t)`.

Indeed Tonelli gives `C_x(t)=t integral_0^infinity e^(−tu)F_x(u)du`; split at 1, bound the first part by its integral to infinity, and use F<=N on the remainder. Conversely C_x(t)<=K/t² for t>=1 implies F_x(u)<=e K u² for 0<u<=1, since `e^(−1)F_x(u)<=C_x(1/u)`. Thus these decay exponents encode the same low-energy softness, up to constants, not an empty interval.

**Hostile S1 (PROVED).** Take ν(dE)=2E 1_(0,1)(E)dE. Then F(u)=u² and

`C(t)=2[1−(1+t)e^(−t)]/t²`.

It is gapless and atomless, with exact quadratic softness. Realization: multiplication by E on L²((0,1),2E dE), x=1; adjoin a separate one-dimensional vacuum if desired. This is an abstract spectral model, not a claimed local relativistic field theory.

## 3. Uniform phase estimates plus irreducibility still need visibility

**Hostile S2 (PROVED; complete fixed-word quantifiers).** In phase n>=1 set ε_n=2^−n,

`H_n=diag(0,ε_n), Ω=e0, A_n=ε_n σx, B_n=σz`.

Every phase has a unique ground vector and the unital *-algebra generated by A_n,B_n is M₂(C); hence scalar commutant and cyclic Ω. For a fixed polynomial P in the two noncommuting symbols, each monomial contributing to the off-diagonal entry contains at least one A. For 0<ε<=1 all entries of A and B have modulus <=1, so

`|<e1,P(A_n,B_n)Ω>| <= c_P ε_n`,

where c_P is the sum of absolute coefficients of its monomials. Consequently its vacuum-subtracted spectral measure satisfies

`ν_(P,n)((0,u]) <= c_P² u²`,

uniformly in n, and `C_(P,n)(t)<=c_P² ε_n²e^(−tε_n)<=4c_P²e^(−2)t^−2` for t>0. Nevertheless gap(H_n)=ε_n→0. Normalizing A_nΩ to e1 costs ε_n^−1, exactly the missing uniform coordinate. These are not Wightman models and refute only inference from the displayed spectral/cyclicity/commutant properties in isolation.

**Hostile S3 (PROVED; even each-word exponential decay is insufficient).** On CΩ⊕ℓ²(N), let H e_n=2^−n e_n and HΩ=0. Generate an algebra with identity and finite matrix units joining Ω and the e_n. It is irreducible: commuting with all rank-one matrix units forces a scalar operator. Every finite word applied to Ω has finite support, so every such vector has some positive exponential decay rate and a quadratic low-energy bound. Their span is dense. H has no positive gap. The missing quantifier is a **common** rate, not merely density.

**Lemma S2 (PROVED, common-rate dense test criterion).** For a total set D in (ker H)^perp, suppose a common m>0 satisfies C_x(t)<=K_x e^(−mt) for all x∈D and all sufficiently large t (K_x and starting time may depend on x). Then spec(H)∩(0,m)=empty. For a<m, `e^(−at)ν_x((0,a])<=C_x(t)` implies ν_x((0,a])=0 by t→infinity. The bounded projection 1_(0,a](H) annihilates a total set, hence vanishes. Union over a<m proves the assertion. Uniformity of K_x is unnecessary; uniformity of m is essential.

**Lemma S3 (PROVED, low-energy observability upgrade).** Let x_1,...,x_k be fixed probes with ν_j((0,u])<=M_j u² for 0<u<=1. Suppose K>0 and the additional scale-uniform visibility estimate

`||1_(0,u](H)|| <= K Σ_j ν_j((0,u])`  for 0<u<=1

holds. Since a nonzero orthogonal projection has norm one, every u<min(1,(KΣM_j)^−1/2) has zero projection. Thus there is a spectral gap at least this minimum; if ΣM_j=0 the statement means gap at least 1. If KMsum<1, even projection through energy 1 vanishes. For phase families require the same K and the same probe list uniformly in phase. This is a sufficient condition, not asserted available in Yang–Mills. It can be very strong: in infinite rank a finite probe bank may have arbitrarily poor overlaps. In S2 one probe A_nΩ makes K at least ε_n^−2.

## 4. An exact Wilson laboratory: Z₂ sheets, cuts, and torons

Take a finite connected planar disk cellulation with F faces, link signs U_e∈{±1}, gauge changes U_vw→g_v U_vw g_w, and density proportional to exp(β Σ_p B_p), where B_p=product of links around p. For β>0 write q=tanh β∈(0,1). Uniform link counting pushes to independent plaquette signs, each with mean q: after gauge fixing a spanning tree the F remaining links give arbitrary face data bijectively. Therefore for a disk region S of a faces,

`W(∂S)=product_(p∈S) B_p`,  `E W(∂S)=q^a=exp(−σ a)`,  `σ=−log q>0`.

This is **PROVED** exact area law in this finite two-dimensional abelian toy, not four-dimensional nonabelian confinement.

On a torus with F faces, the image of link-to-plaquette map has precisely the constraint product_p B_p=1. Every allowed plaquette vector has the same number of preimages, with two independent flat holonomies (torons) surviving after gauge quotient. Hence the law of B is independent biased signs **conditioned** on total product +1. For S of a faces,

`E W(∂S) = (q^a+q^(F−a))/(1+q^F)`.

Proof: insert indicator `(1+product_all B)/2` into numerator and denominator. The two terms are the two complementary spanning sheets. The formula is invariant under a↔F−a, unlike q^a. For fixed a and F→infinity it tends to q^a; uniform relative error requires control of F−2a, since ratio to q^a is `(1+q^(F−2a))/(1+q^F)`. Macroscopic/complementary sheets cannot be silently ignored.

A noncontractible fundamental Wilson loop averages to zero: multiply all links crossing an appropriate dual cut by −1. This leaves all plaquettes and the action invariant and reverses that loop. Thus plaquette curvature plus contractible loops does not determine noncontractible holonomy. Retain two marked fundamental holonomies. At fixed plaquette curvature, differences of configurations are flat and their gauge classes form a relative H¹(T²;Z₂) torsor after choosing a reference configuration; a nonflat configuration does not itself define a canonical cohomology class. A fixed toron sector need not have this zero expectation.

The exact transfer operator on flux parity is

`T=((1+q)/2)I+((1−q)/2)X`, with eigenvalues 1,q.

Then `(T^a)_(even,even)=(1+q^a)/2` and `E parity_after_a=q^a`. T composes; a single selected entry does not generally compose without both channels. The torus correction is the closing-channel trace constraint, not a local change to β.

## 5. Gaussian/Pfaffian sidecar: covariance is not Wilson information

The inherited Pfaffian identity (THM-174, file cited in BRIDGES.md) is finite skew algebra, not a Gaussianity certificate for a field law. For an abelian centered Gaussian flux X_S with Var X_S=κ area(S),

`E exp(iX_S)=exp(−κ area(S)/2)`.

The mechanism is characteristic-function closure at cumulant order two. A centered Rademacher flux ±s has the same variance s² as N(0,s²), but loop expectations cos(s) and exp(−s²/2) differ, even in sign. Thus covariance or a determinant alone cannot prove an area law. In a nonabelian model path ordering and representation labels are additional coordinates. A Gaussian ansatz needs an actual field-law/characteristic-functional hypothesis; formal Wick/Pfaffian notation is not that hypothesis.

## 6. Particles are a different spectral question

Even a proved vacuum energy gap does not imply isolated particle masses. Multiplication by E on L²([m,m+1],dE), adjoined to a vacuum, is gapped and has no positive-energy eigenatoms. Conversely a measure can have an isolated atom plus continuum down to zero and be gapless. These are energy-spectrum separation examples only: in infinite-volume relativistic QFT a one-particle sector is a mass-shell component of the joint energy-momentum spectral measure (an atom of the invariant mass variable), not ordinarily an atom of total energy at all spatial momenta. The missing sidecar is the **joint translation PVM**, its Lorentz invariant mass-shell support, nonzero field overlaps, and suitable isolation/stability. Do not use a one-variable heat bound to count particle species.
