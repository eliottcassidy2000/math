# An exact ternary selector for cycle packings, and where a tournament forces extra cycles

**PROVED.** A compact model on specified directed cycles implements a target-selected recurrence for **every odd integer**, including the two perfect neighbors. A strong-order inequality explains exactly which simplification of this model fails as a Chan–Pak proof.

For a list of selected odd directed cycles let \(W\) sum \(2^{|I|}\) over disjoint sublists \(I\). A new cycle disjoint from all current cycles sends \(W\) to \(3W\). A cycle meeting every current cycle sends \(W\) to \(W+2\). These are identities of actual vertex supports. To encode a positive odd \(N\), start with 1 and use one multiplication by three followed by \(j\in\{0,1,2\}\) additions of two per digit. In reverse, take \(j\equiv 2N\bmod3\) in this range and replace \(N\) by \((N-2j)/3\). This terminates at 1 for every positive odd input greater than one: the inputs 3 and 5 go to 1, and from 7 onward the quotient is a smaller positive odd integer. Equivalently, if \(m=(N-1)/2\), the forward rule is \(m\mapsto3m+1+j\). This gives a precise sense in which ternary descent can navigate the target. The digit is chosen from the residue; there is no assertion about an orbit of the ordinary Collatz map.

**Physical realization of the chosen letters.** Give the isolated cycle at step \(i\) a private directed triangle through \(a_i\). The triangles are pairwise disjoint. Use a common vertex \(v\) for all cycles that should meet every previous letter. After \(s\ge1\) triangle letters have been added, give a new universal letter the tour
\[
       v,a_1,a_2,\ldots,a_s,\ [b],\ t,\ v .
\]
Here \(t\) is private to the letter and the extra private vertex \(b\) is used precisely when \(s\) is even, making the cycle length odd. Orient all named edges along these tours. Shared edges among tours have compatible directions. Thus this is a partial orientation extendible to a tournament by independently orienting the remaining pairs. The universal letter meets precisely the earlier triangles, and all other universal letters meet it at \(v\). Finally add one more universal letter, reserving its edge \(tv\). In one orientation this designated cycle is absent, in the other it is present; every earlier designated cycle survives. On the **designated** alphabet, a literal one-arc edit now reads \(N\longrightarrow N+2\) exactly. For \(N=1\) a single marked triangle suffices.

For \(N=E_{61}-1\) the inverse uses **76 triangle letters and 71 universal letters** before the final edit. Its selected-cycle readings on the two boards are exactly \(E_{61}-1\) and \(E_{61}+1\). Full tournament heights also count the unlisted directed odd cycles.

**Obstruction valid for every realization of this grammar.** After \(k\) ternary steps there are \(k\) pairwise vertex-disjoint odd cycles, in every implementation keeping its stated disjointness. Its largest possible upper target is \(3^{k+1}\), since \(k\) uses of \(N\mapsto3N+4\) from 1 give \(3^{k+1}-2\). In a literal unit flip above the two small component mergers, the nontrivial strongly connected component at each endpoint is unique and contains every directed cycle. Indeed an unchanged nontrivial component divides the two coprime heights, and the only unit flips merging components have endpoints \(1,3\) or \(3,5\). Thus the strong core must have at least \(3k\) vertices. Busch's nondecreasing minimum gives
\[
                   H\ \ge\ b(3k)=3\cdot5^{k-1}>3^{k+1}
                   \qquad(k\ge4).
\]
So **no four-digit or longer unit flip can contain this ternary alphabet while realizing its two readings as full Hamilton counts**. This rules out that proposed transfer even if one changes the lengths of the letters, the unused vertex pairs, the endpoint labels, or the order of the representing tournament. The obstruction is the number of mutually disjoint cycles produced by the multiplications. A physical digit that reuses old vertices while controlling their contribution remains a genuinely different possibility. More generally, let \(r\) count the nontrivial strong components containing a family of \(\nu\) vertex-disjoint directed cycles. If component \(i\) contains \(t_i\) such cycles then it has at least \(3t_i\) vertices. Therefore \(H\ge 5^\nu(3/5)^r\), even without a marked flip. On the exact upper target the [two certified prime factors](../chanpak_fermat_solids_seams_20261122/UPPER-PRIME-PROFILES.md) give \(r\le2\), whence \(\nu\le52\) at **every** order. The 76 disjoint letters already violate this upper bound on the upper endpoint alone. An actual 13-cycle contributes its specific occupied vertices to this accounting.
