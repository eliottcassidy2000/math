# One arc centered at the exponent-61 perfect number

Let \(H(T)\) count the directed Hamilton paths of a tournament, and let \(E_p=2^{p-1}(2^p-1)\). Reversing one arc in a strong tournament on 122 vertices gives two counts with average **exactly** \(E_{61}\equiv1\bmod3\). Their half-difference is \(498460498419343452915388399908552705\). The ideal adjacent pair \(E_{61}-1,E_{61}+1\) and the general Chan–Pak conjecture remain **OPEN**. [Explicit arc and counts](CENTER.md) · [Chan–Pak, §6.5](https://www.math.ucla.edu/~pak/papers/DominoPermanent16.pdf)

| Question | Result |
|---|---|
| Which targets choose the same physical arc? | **PROVED.** In one eight-site quotient, substituting two chains of length \(p-3\) centers the same flip at \(E_p\) for every \(p\ge4\). [Construction](CENTER.md) |
| Can arbitrary unequal chain lengths correct the half-difference? | **FINITE-EXACT.** Among strong quotients on at most ten sites with three designated chains visited at most twice and a flip between ordinary sites preserving these caps, exactly two endpoint pairs center at \(E_{61}\). The pair above is the narrower. **PROVED.** For fixed tensors the simultaneous exact midpoint and difference conditions reduce to finitely many integer linear systems. [Width classification and proof](CENTER-THREE.md) |
| Where are the exact lower endpoints? | **FINITE-EXACT.** With any number of such chains on strong quotients through ten sites, eight classes attain \(E_{61}-1\), including four new classes on 125 vertices. None attains \(E_{61}+1\). All physical flips from the lower classes and their count-preserving neighbors miss the upper. [Class census and general lower family](SEAM.md) |
| Is one exceptional vertex sufficient for a unit flip? | **PROVED.** There are infinitely many unit flips with midpoint \(1\bmod3\). At \(E_p\) for \(p\ge3\), each endpoint would need at least two vertex deletions to become transitive. The result allows arbitrary order and a flip anywhere. [Complete spine-arc spectrum](SPINE-ARC.md) |

## Which odd cycles fit together?

The tournament count weights each family of vertex-disjoint directed odd cycles by \(2^{\#\mathrm{cycles}}\). Reciprocal Latin matching rows retain exactly the physical slot occupied at every visit to a branch of a Moore graph. Thus one can test simplicity, orientation and joint disjointness for selected cycles as soon as the edge directions are supplied. [Grinberg–Stanley](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.05569) · [Matching-row decoder](LATIN-CYCLES.md)

- **PROVED.** A target-selected ternary recurrence realizes every positive odd value on designated directed cycles. For the exponent-61 lower target it calls for 76 disjoint triangles, while the full upper count permits at most 52 disjoint directed cycles at any order. This identifies the information that the Collatz-like selector loses in a tournament completion. [Ternary inverse and obstruction](TERNARY.md)
- **FINITE-EXACT.** Hoffman–Singleton has 60,984,000 disjoint pairs and 8,694,000 disjoint triples of induced 13-cycles. **CONDITIONAL on existence.** In degree 57 every disjoint packing of at most 65 induced 13-cycles extends. Orienting 66 such cycles consistently forces a sharp minimum of \(625^{66}\) Hamilton paths over tournament completions when just their cycle edges are prescribed. [Packings and completion theorem](PACKINGS.md)
- **CONDITIONAL on existence.** Any nine branches at any root of a degree-57 Moore graph are connected and contain odd cycles. Each two-coloring leaves at least 50 edges monochromatic. The extremal case for disconnected eight-branch extensions imposes a simultaneous binary affine labelling. The graph's existence remains **OPEN**. [Nine-branch mixing and its equality constraints](LATIN-CYCLES.md)
