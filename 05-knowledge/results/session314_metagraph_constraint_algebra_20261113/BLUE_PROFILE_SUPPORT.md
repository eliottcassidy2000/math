# Complete support of the blue score quotient

The [one-box rule](OBSTRUCTIONS.md) and [labelled cut theorem](BLUE_CUTS.md) together give a complete characterization of which score profiles are joined by a blue line. **This is a quotient by score profile, not by tournament class.** The separate alignment recurrence recovers edges at the finer class level.

Use $m=\lfloor n/2\rfloor$ and center the scores by $(n-1)/2$. Keep one absolute magnitude per complementary score pair; for even $n$ subtract $1/2$ from each magnitude to make it integral. Feasible profiles are determined by the symmetric Landau inequalities (and are realized by Eplett's theorem and the blue presentation law, as above). A profile is called strong when its score list has strict Landau inequalities for all proper nonempty initial segments; this is equivalent to its tournament being strong. The parity-compatible middle vertex at odd $n$ has centered score zero.

**Theorem, $n\ge3$.** Distinct feasible profiles are joined in blue exactly when they differ by one box and at least one is strong. At even order a same-profile blue line exists exactly when the profile is strong and has a zero shifted magnitude, i.e. contains the two central scores. At odd order there are no same-profile blue lines. A same-profile line in this theorem need not be a self-line of an individual tournament class.

Necessity is the endpoint score shift and the fact that two nonstrong nodes cannot be joined. For sufficiency, suppose the desired box move lowers a magnitude $j\ge1$ in a feasible profile. Put its positive score first and its complementary score last: they are $m+j$ and $m-j$ for odd $n$, and $m+j$ and $m-1-j$ for even $n$. Put all remaining scores in descending order between them, so the full word $s$ is mirror-symmetric. For the even same-profile case use $j=0$. Let $t=s-e_0+e_{n-1}$ be the required other score word. The profile of $t$ is more balanced (or unchanged in the even horizontal case); every proper Landau sum is at least that of the less balanced profile. Thus if at least one profile is strong, $t$ is strong.

We check the cut inequalities for this particular ordering of $s$. Given a subset $S$, call a backbone boundary an edge from a selected vertex to the next unselected vertex. Such boundary pairs are vertex-disjoint. Let $g$ count the boundaries with weakly descending scores, and $b$ those with ascending scores. Only the two extreme backbone edges may ascend. Let $L$ be the selected ends and $R$ the unselected ends of the $g$ good boundaries. The two sets $S\setminus L$ and $S\cup R$ have sizes $|S|-g$ and $|S|+g$. The average of their binomial lower bounds is $\binom{|S|}{2}+g^2/2$. Also the average of their $s$ sums is at most $s(S)$, since each removed score is at least its corresponding added score. For $b=0$, ordinary Landau gives slack at least $g^2/2$, which by integrality is at least $g$ (including $g=1$).

If $b>0$, the modified subsets remain nonempty and proper because an ascending boundary keeps one selected and one unselected endpoint, untouched by the good boundaries. Use **strict** Landau for $t$, giving at least one additional unit in both subsets. In the presence of a bad boundary at the first edge the first vertex remains selected in both modified subsets, so $t\le s$ on their sums. With a bad boundary at the last edge the last vertex stays unselected, again giving $t\le s$. When both occur, $t=s-1$ on both sums. Thus the average bound gains at least $b$ in total, yielding slack at least $g+b$ after integrality. This is exactly the needed cut inequality. Apply the signed-rounding theorem to obtain an actual blue tiling with the constructed labelled score word. The reverse line has profile $t$, as required.

Consequently the [Laurent recurrence](OBSTRUCTIONS.md) now supplies exact multiplicities on a score quotient whose **support** also has a closed structural description: one-box moves, the nonstrong exclusion, and the even central-score rule. The finite controls through order nineteen helped locate the sorted-interior construction; the proof has no order bound. It does not make the different tournament classes in a given score profile interchangeable.
