# Complete all-color score support by one-win transport

This extends the [blue score theorem](BLUE_PROFILE_SUPPORT.md) to arbitrary tournament scores. Use ordinary score multisets first and fold by converse at the end. A legal score multiset is exactly a Landau multiset. It is strong iff all proper Landau inequalities are strict.

**Theorem.** Two ordinary multisets appear at the ends of a path-reversal line iff one can be obtained from the other by a unit transfer from one score to another, and at least one multiset is strong. This includes identical multisets, which require a transfer between two scores differing by one. After identifying each multiset with its degree complement, the result gives the exact support of the combined merged score quotient. A pair with the same merged score profile can come from two distinct complementary ordinary multisets; fold only after applying the ordinary test.

Necessity: reversing the Hamiltonian path transfers one win from its initial to its final vertex. In the literal complement convention a line has a closed end and hence a strong end; taking the converse of that endpoint does not change strength. Path reversal gives the same merged line and also preserves this necessary condition for the two ordinary multisets under its own convention.

Sufficiency: choose the direction of the proposed transfer so its donor $a$ and recipient $b$ satisfy $a\ge b+1$. If the original direction fails this, reverse it using the updated scores. Put $a$ at the first path position and $b$ last, with every other score in descending order. The target score vector $t$ subtracts one from the first and adds one to the last. It is more balanced (or has the same multiset), so $t$ is strong when either profile is strong.

For any subset $S$, its selected-to-unselected backbone edges are disjoint. At most two are ascending in this ordering, both on the outer boundary of the path. Use the proof of the [blue profile support theorem](BLUE_PROFILE_SUPPORT.md): remove the selected ends of the $g$ weakly descending boundaries or add their unselected ends. Averaging the two Landau tests gives slack at least $g^2/2$, enough for $g$ by integrality. If an ascending boundary remains, both test sets are nonempty proper and strict Landau for $t$ gives one more unit. If both outer edges are ascending boundaries, $t$ loses one unit on each test set, giving a second extra unit. With only the first such boundary the first vertex stays selected; with only the last the last stays excluded; either way $t$ does not exceed $s$ on the two test sums. Thus the displayed order satisfies all backbone cut inequalities in [BLUE_CUTS](BLUE_CUTS.md). The ordinary capacitated matching argument suffices to orient the free arcs. Reversing the path yields the prescribed other scores.

For multiplicities, the [ordered score product](ALIGNMENT_RECURRENCE.md) already gives the combined kernel, and subtracting the blue score kernel after the same projection gives the black multiplicities. The theorem above describes combined support; it does not assert a score-only description of black support beyond that subtraction, or say that any pair of individual classes with these scores is adjacent.
