# Marked stability, the square-sum 19 obstruction, and three \(A_5\) carriers

## Stable padding is isometric and geodesically convex

Use the merged metagraph whose vertices identify isomorphic tournaments and converses, and whose edges come from a single arc reversal. Its distance is the minimum Hamming distance of orientation vectors over relabelings and optional converse: one inequality follows by projecting a cube path; the reverse follows by lifting each quotient edge from the currently chosen representative. Loops can be ignored when computing distance.

Define \(P_k(T)=TT_k\to T\to TT_k\), an ordinal join. A minimizing bijection between any two padded orientation vectors must send their designated first source \(s\) to the corresponding source \(t\). Otherwise take \(x\) mapped to \(t\) and interchange the images of \(s,x\). The mismatch on \(sx\) disappears; for each other vertex the old comparison paired \((1,b)\) to \((c,1)\), the new one pairs it to \((1,c)\), and \(|b-c|\le(1-b)+(1-c)\) for bits \(b,c\). Thus the swap strictly improves. Peel the matched sources, then do the analogous argument with zeros for sinks. Converse commutes with symmetric padding. Hence \(P_k\) is an isometric induced embedding. In any quotient geodesic between padded classes, the lifted endpoint must be a minimizing representative, so it aligns all these arcs. The lifted shortest cube path never flips coordinates on which endpoints agree. Consequently **every** merged geodesic between padded classes stays padded. Moreover \(H(P_k(T))=H(T)\) by ordinal path concatenation.

The [Bott/Collatz marked periodicity work](../collatz_bott_marked_periodicity_20261007d.md) distinguishes a lossless marked tensor corner from the stable homotopy class, which forgets arithmetic guards. Here the preserved datum is the complete arc-flip metric, not just a stable invariant or the Hamilton count; the literal symmetry at the two ends makes the converse action compatible. This is not an eightfold periodicity theorem for metagraphs. Compare the [creation decoder](../creation_decoder_20260925.md) and [Fano/Clifford matrices](../creation_fano_20260925.md) for older examples of keeping the marked coordinate through stabilization.

There is also a clean transitive boundary. Reversing a nonadjacent pair in \(TT_n\) makes one strong interval of size \(m\ge3\), with one reversed endpoint arc; all other SCCs remain singletons. Reversing an adjacent pair just relabels \(TT_n\). The nontrivial interval is self-converse, so a neighbor is classified by \(m\) and the unordered padding lengths \(\{a,b\}\), \(a+b=n-m\). Its Hamilton count is \(1+2^{m-2}\): all directed odd cycles contain the one reversed arc, with an odd subset of the \(m-2\) internal vertices, and the odd-cycle packing formula contributes twice for each. The degree is \(\lfloor(n-1)^2/4\rfloor\) for \(n\ge2\), with ordinary generating function \(z^3/((1-z)^2(1-z^2))\). The shell is exact; the squares at \(n=9,19\) are \(4^2,9^2\) for a transparent reason, not evidence of a square-sum isomorphism.

## The four, five, eight, nine roles in square sums

In the [small-graph zigzag](../procgen_smallgraph_20260926_small_graphs_arithmetic.md), the unique first nontrivial Hamilton path in \(Q_{15}\) runs from 8 to 9. The 8 is a missing self-pair \(8+8=4^2\); the 9 is itself a square, and its only available neighbor is 7. The edge \(4-5\) uses the target 9. Their other neighbors \(12,11\) use target 16; these vertices are internal, with both incidences forced. The edge \(1-3\) using target 4 is instead the one unused surplus edge. The zigzag condition comes from simultaneous Pell equations, not from \(9-8=3^2-2^3\); the Catalan reading becomes operative only in other target families where 8 and 9 are both allowed targets. See also the [three-chain founder and leaf recurrences](../collatz_mod6_20260922_w6_summand_closure_square_filter.md), [forced endpoint geometry](../crossroads_family_20260926_squares.md), and the [Ogg-level comparison](../monster_levels_square_sum_7_21_20261022.md) for the distinct entry/exit events at 9 and 25.

There is a short non-exhaustive obstruction at **19**. The leaves of \(Q_{19}\) are 16 and 18; their neighbors are 9 and 7. Vertex 9 has exactly the two neighbors 16 and 7. Thus any spanning path would have to contain the complete endpoint-to-endpoint path \(16-9-7-18\), excluding fifteen other vertices. This sharpens the mere leaf-budget test and the old exhaustive assertion in the [September computation](../collatz_mod6_20260922_w6_lean_paste_audit_w6.md). It is the negative counterpart of the successful forced-endpoint corridor at 15: retain the actual incidence, not just the count of leaves.

## \(A_5\): \(K_5\), ten \(K_{3,3}\) frames, and two ways to see Petersen

Let \(A_5\) act on five letters and on its six Sylow-5 subgroups. Each cyclic subgroup of order three acts without fixed points on the latter six (a Sylow-5 normalizer has order 10), so its two orbits form an unordered \(3+3\) partition, hence a spanning \(K_{3,3}\) frame. Distinct order-three subgroups cannot have the same partition: inside the permutations of the two triples they would generate a subgroup of order 9. There are ten such subgroups and ten partitions, hence a bijection. The fixed letters of the order-three subgroup in the five-point action give a pair, i.e. an edge of \(K_5\). Declare two frames adjacent when their fixed pairs are disjoint. This is Petersen, and equivalently the two frame generators generate \(A_5\) rather than \(A_4\). Indeed if the fixed pairs intersect, the generated group acts transitively on the other four, and its order is divisible by 3 and 4, hence it is \(A_4\). For disjoint fixed pairs it acts transitively on five, hence has order divisible by 15. Order 30 is impossible by simplicity; a subgroup of order 15 would have normal Sylow-5, hence lie in its order-10 normalizer. Thus it is \(A_5\). Pure overlap cannot recover this adjacency: all distinct frames share exactly five edges, from the \(1,2,2,1\) intersection cells.

An alternate Petersen realization needs a *mark*: write each frame as the line of its balanced signs in six coordinates. Distinct lines have absolute inner product 2 and squared norms 6. Mark one coordinate and orient each line with plus at that coordinate. Its other plus coordinates form a pair of the remaining five, and the inner product is negative exactly when the pairs are disjoint. This gives a marked Petersen graph. Changing the mark switches Seidel signs on the six frames separating the marks, equivalently on the four complementary frames. There are six distinct marked graphs, permuted transitively by \(A_5\). In fact these exhaust the degree-three members of the switching class: after switching on a subset of size \(s\le5\), degree three forces each inside vertex to have \((10-s)/2\) original neighbors outside, and each outside vertex to have \(s/2\) original neighbors inside. A nontrivial switch must have \(s=4\), with an independent four-set. In \(KG(5,2)\), four mutually intersecting pairs are a star (if three make a triangle, there is no fourth). The five stars give precisely the five nontrivial changes of mark. The canonical Petersen defined by fixed letter pairs is \(A_5\)-invariant and therefore is not any of these six marked graphs. Same underlying data, different required frames: an exact warning for scalarized metagraph adjacency.
