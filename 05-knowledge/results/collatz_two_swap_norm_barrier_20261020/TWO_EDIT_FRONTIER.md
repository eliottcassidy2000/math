# Two adjacent edits: exact sparse forms, norm barriers, and bounded census

20 October 2026. Status: **PROVED structural reductions and norm barriers; FINITE-EXACT census; OPEN unbounded two-edit exclusion.** No claim about the Collatz conjecture or historical priority is made.

## 1. The useful result and its boundary

The one-edit method does not extend by simply taking the same sub-three bound on the sparse norm. Two actual adjacent swaps have a complete labelled sparse description, but a legal mixed example already has a 2,3-free norm of 343 at weight 4, exceeding 3^4=81. Its actual carry content is only 1. A legal overlapping-chain family has raw norm growth asymptotic to 3^p. The latter statement does not settle the growth after removing powers of 3.

The [structural proof and exact sparse forms](symbolic/TWO_SWAP_SPARSE_FORMS_AND_BARRIER.md) give:

- Complete ordered gap tests, including cut crossings and overlapping paths.
- Undo, same-one displacement +2 or -2, and two-distinct-one displacements.
- A moving-anchor carry polynomial, so no unchanged-one assumption is needed at p=2.
- Six canonical integral norm representatives, with full prime-power divisibility.
- Complete two-edit residue legality at positive critical clocks.
- Exact stripped-norm counterexamples and a proved raw-norm asymptotic.

For p>=2 and source gaps 1 or 2, two same-direction moves of the same labelled one are impossible. They would require a source gap at least 3. In contrast, 110 -> 101 -> 011 is legal: it moves two different ones in a forced order. At other clocks with larger gaps, same-one displacement two must be retained. The p=1,q=2 circle has a separate coincident-edge wrap-around convention and is included directly in the census.

## 2. What the exact barriers do and do not refute

At p=4,q=7, with zero-based swap starts 0 then 3,

    0101011 -> 1001011 -> 1000111.

The canonical polynomial modulo Z^4-2 is

    H=Z^3+2Z^2-3Z+1,
    Res(Z^4-2,H)=343=7^3.

The final carry is 331 and D=47, so g=1. Its necklace distance from the balanced source is exactly two.

At p=28,q=45, an overlapping ++ chain with swap starts 9 then 8 gives

    Q=3-Z^27+Z^11-Z^10,
    |Res(Z^28-2,Q)|=23221006835657 > 3^28=22876792454961.

This norm is also 2,3-free, while the actual content is 1 and the necklace distance is exactly two. The integers are independently reproduced by a fraction-free determinant of the multiplication matrix in Z[Z]/(Z^p-2), not only by a symbolic resultant routine.

These examples refute a universal bound M<c^p with c<3 for these exact norm moduli at every critical two-edit shape. They do not refute an eventual stripped-norm estimate, a bound with a prefactor, a smaller actual-content bound, or two-edit cycle exclusion.

For the endpoint-chain family at unbounded critical coprime shapes, the structural proof establishes

    |Res(Z^p-2,Q_p)|^(1/p) -> 3.

The proof uses a uniformly nonvanishing two-torus logarithmic average. Therefore an eventual raw-norm sub-three exponential gap is impossible. However, the 3-adic valuation is not controlled here: at p=13,r=8 it is 5. No corresponding eventual conclusion about the 3-free modulus or actual content is claimed.

## 3. Exact definition of the edit universe

Every operation swaps unequal bits across a cyclic adjacent edge in the current intermediate word. Both operations must be actual. Undo paths, crossings of the original cut, overlapping swaps, and repeated/nonprimitive source shapes are retained.

There are two distinct notions:

1. A path consisting of exactly two actual swaps can return to the original word or its rotation class.
2. The necklace distance of an output is the minimum number of such swaps between the two rotation orbits.

For an output generated by two swaps, distance is determined exactly by membership in the source rotation orbit, then in the union of all rotations of one-swap outputs, otherwise it is two. Consequently an integer output along an undo path is not a distance-two counterexample.

The [negative-cycle boundary certificate](../collatz_one_swap_negative_exclusion_20261020/MINUS17_THREE_SWAP_BOUNDARY.md) proves that the genuine -17 cycle, with parity 11110111000 and shape (7,11), has exact necklace distance three. The current census explicitly checks that none of its rotations appears within zero, one, or two swaps. It remains a genuine integer cycle and a three-edit boundary, not a two-edit hostile.

## 4. Critical-shape census: deliberately not a complete residual universe

`probe_two_edits.py --max-p 100 --norm-max-p 32` checks both lengths

    q=floor(p log_2 3),    q=ceil(p log_2 3),

for every 1<=p<=100 with p<q, retaining all gcd classes. These lengths are calculated by exact integer bit lengths. There are:

| quantity | exact count |
|---|---:|
| shapes | 199 |
| ordered actual two-swap paths | 919272 |
| distinct final words, summed by shape | 458113 |
| integral final words, summed by shape | 12 |
| integral outputs of exact necklace distance two | 0 |

All twelve integral outputs are rotations or repetitions of the ordinary positive cycle or the -5 negative cycle. They have necklace distance zero. The standard path classes, with multiplicities, are 11816 undo, 2 sole-one wrap-around, 229817 two-plus, 229817 two-minus, and 447820 mixed paths.

This is only an exploratory critical-shape slice. The one-edit theorem forcing a candidate to a critical clock has not been proved for two edits and is not imported. In particular, this census is not called a complete residual check for an unbounded two-edit claim.

Floating root products in the receipt rank 7488 canonical norm patterns through p=32. They are explicitly exploratory only: no floating-point inequality controls any theorem or finite integrality decision. Exact resultants, not this ranking, establish the two barriers above.

A separate tuple-word implementation checks every row through p=40: 79 shapes, 59640 paths, and 29613 distinct outputs. It uses canonical necklace representatives rather than unions of bit-mask rotation orbits, and positional carries rather than the affine carry recurrence. Every count, maximum content, integer-hit count, and exact-distance-two output count agrees.

## 5. Broad all-clock control and the small-weight all-length consequence

`probe_all_shapes.py` checks every shape

    1<=p<=10,    p<q<=80,

without a critical-clock or coprimality filter. The exact totals are:

| quantity | exact count |
|---|---:|
| shapes | 745 |
| off-critical shapes | 726 |
| ordered actual two-swap paths | 102684 |
| distinct final words, summed by shape | 51793 |
| integral final words, summed by shape | 20 |
| integral outputs of exact necklace distance two | 0 |

Again every integral output has necklace distance zero. Their shapes are q=2p for 1<=p<=10, and (p,q)=(2,3),(4,6),(6,9),(8,12),(10,15). The second implementation independently reproduces every one of the 745 rows.

This broad window does imply an all-length small-weight conclusion: no exact-necklace-distance-two integral output occurs at 1<=p<=10. Indeed, every positive integer cycle satisfies

    2^q=product_(odd a in the cycle)(3+1/a)<=4^p,

so q<=2p<=20. A negative fixed point has D<0, so q<p log_2 3<2p<=20. Thus no possible integer clock of these weights lies beyond the verified q<=80 window. This is a finite-weight conclusion, not an unbounded two-edit theorem.

Off-critical content need not be tiny. For example, an exact-distance-two primitive output at (p,q)=(9,26) has g=6037, and a nonprimitive output at (8,78) has g=40132174419737. Both are nonintegral. Such controls prevent the critical examples from being mistaken for a general content bound.

Balanced returns and repeated-cycle controls are therefore retained rather than discarded as impossible.

The current norm modulus has lost that side information and already exhibits the stated barriers. There is no claimed two-edit exclusion beyond the explicitly closed small-weight range.
