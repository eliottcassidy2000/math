1. A proper module cannot meet two children of a cyclic three-block
   substitution. An omitted third-child name sees the two occupied children
   oppositely; after that child is forced inside, an omitted name in either
   other child also sees opposite orientations. Thus a root-containing
   original-module XOR family has exactly the converse root quotient.

2. The decomposition theorem applies to arbitrary original-module XOR,
   including crossing descendants. It gives scalar monotonicity and, in the
   equality case, hereditary complete q-profile equality on each physical
   child. No invalid sequential legality is assumed for crossing operations.

3. The uniform root query depends only on the three q profiles. The positive-
   square equal-profile theorem therefore applies unchanged after arbitrary
   descendant operations in the scalar-equal case. The query (0,0) handles
   the scalar-strict case exactly. The stated two-query battery is sound.

4. For unequal transitive child sizes, covered_quotient's cyclic-run start-
   aggregate identity was checked in its stated arbitrary-profile form.
   Its strict sign uses the original transitive profiles; hereditary equality
   supplies those same profiles to the new children. It does not require
   their physical interior order to remain fixed. The endpoint tomography
   identity gives an actual battery of 2|Ai| queries, and adding (0,0) gives
   at most 2|Ai|+1, as claimed. If all sizes agree, this sign argument is not
   used; the equal-profile positive-square theorem handles that branch.

5. Laminarity preserves current modularity and hence scalar H, so omission
   of the scalar query is valid only in that declared case. The theorem
   distinguishes the original core from each root-containing target; it
   does not prove pairwise injectivity over a larger orbit. The restriction
   to whole-core/ordered-SCC ambient scope is explicit and necessary with
   the dependencies used here.
