# Arbitrary original-module XOR reduces to the legal inversion class

Session 188. **Proved using classical Gallai decomposition:** simultaneously
reversing any family of modules of an original tournament cannot decrease any
induced-subset Hamilton count or any ordered path-cover count. Equality of the
full scalar count has an exact structural characterization. All surviving
full-response collision candidates lie in the legal sequential module-inversion
class; equality of full named two-port responses within that class is not
asserted or excluded by this reduction.

## 1. Operations and classical input

Fix a tournament T on a physical set V and a finite family F of modules of
**T itself**. Repetitions are reduced modulo two. Let U toggle an edge {x,y}
exactly when an odd number of members of F contain both ends. This is
simultaneous original-module XOR. The sets in F are not assumed to remain
modules if processed successively.

A strong module meets every module only by containment or disjointness. The
nonempty strong modules form a rooted inclusion tree. At each nonsingleton
node M, its maximal proper strong submodules M_1,...,M_k partition it. We use
the classical Gallai theorem: the quotient Q_M on these children is either
transitive or prime (has no nontrivial proper module).

A precise primary source is Boussaïri, Ille, Lopez, and Thomassé,
[*The C3-structure of the tournaments*](https://perso.ens-lyon.fr/stephan.thomasse/liste/bilt.pdf),
Theorem 4. Its Proposition 1 includes restriction and union properties of
modules. The paper's Theorem 2 also identifies equal cyclic-triple structures
with the class generated by reversals of currently valid modules. That latter
characterization is classical, not claimed as a discovery here. We give the
needed legal-sequence construction explicitly below.

Let C3(T) denote the set of physical triples inducing directed triangles.
Let q_h(T[S]) count ordered spanning covers of the named induced subset S by
h nonempty directed paths. The empty-subset convention is H(empty)=1.

## 2. Exact action on the original strong tree

**Lemma 1.** Every original strong module remains a module in U. At each
original strong node M:

* if Q_M is prime, its new quotient is Q_M or its whole converse;
* if Q_M is transitive, its new quotient is obtained by XOR reversals of
  intervals of that original child order, including possibly the full interval.

The original strong tree remains a valid recursive modular partition of U;
it need not remain the canonical strong tree when a new quotient is created.

**Proof.** For any original strong module M and any X in F, exactly one of the
following applies: X is disjoint from M, X is contained in M, or M is contained
in X. The first two cases do not alter M's exterior incidences. In the third,
for each fixed exterior vertex v either all edges from M to v are toggled
(v in X) or none are. Uniform exterior incidence is preserved.

Now inspect the children M_i of M. If X meets M but does not contain M, then
X is contained in M. If it is not contained in a single child, strongness of
each child forces X to be a union of entire children. Its child index set is
a module of Q_M. At a prime quotient this cannot be a nontrivial proper set.
At a transitive quotient it must be a consecutive interval. Sets X containing
M toggle every quotient edge, contributing one whole-quotient reversal each.
Sets inside one child do not alter quotient edges. This proves the cases.

Crucially, on a descendant M_i the induced operation is the multiset

    {X intersect M_i : X in F}, reduced modulo two.

Every nonempty intersection is a module of T[M_i]. An ancestor or a parent
interval containing M_i contributes a reversal of **all of M_i**, not zero.
This records all descendant reversal parities, including cancellations.
It makes the same description available recursively. QED.

Thus a parent interval can flip many descendant interiors, but only as whole
descendant reversals. It does not create an uncontrolled partial operation at
a prime quotient.

## 3. Positivity tools for substitution

For a nonempty module B at a fixed root z of a quotient Q, compressing the
nonempty B-runs in a Hamilton path gives

    H(Q[B]) = sum_h c_h(Q,z) q_h(B),
    c_h(Q,z)>=0,              c_1(Q,z)=H(Q)>0.              (1)

Therefore replacing a module by another with coordinatewise larger q_h cannot
lower H. If its q_1 strictly increases, H strictly increases. Other modules
may be held fixed and replaced one at a time, so no unproved factorization of
joint endpoint data is being used.

Two further elementary facts will be used:

1. Whole converse preserves every q_h: reverse each path and reverse its
   direction, keeping its ordered cover slot. Consequently reversing a module
   preserves the ambient H by (1).
2. For any quotient Q and fixed blocks B_i,

       H(Q^op[B_i]) = H(Q[B_i]).                           (2)

   Whole-converse Q[B_i] to obtain Q^op[B_i^op], which has the same H; then
   reverse each child module to restore B_i, again preserving H.

Ordered path covers also have the exact partition formula

    q_h(A)=sum_(ordered nonempty partition S_1,...,S_h)
                         product_j H(A[S_j]).              (3)

Thus hereditary Hamilton domination implies domination of every q_h on every
induced subset.

## 4. Hereditary monotonicity

**Theorem 1.** For every subset S of V and every allowed h,

    H(U[S]) >= H(T[S]),       q_h(U[S]) >= q_h(T[S]).        (4)

**Proof.** Induct on the original strong tree. A singleton is immediate.
At a node M fix S subset M, discard empty child intersections, and write
A_i=T[S intersect M_i], B_i=U[S intersect M_i]. By induction every q_h(B_i)
dominates q_h(A_i).

If the original Q_M is transitive, T[S] is an ordered join, so its Hamilton
count is product_i H(A_i). Whatever the new induced child quotient K is,
choose one Hamilton path of K and concatenate Hamilton paths in the B_i.
This gives

    H(U[S]) >= product_i H(B_i) >= product_i H(A_i)=H(T[S]).

If Q_M is prime, Lemma 1 says K is the original quotient restricted to the
occupied children, or its converse. Replace A_i by B_i one at a time in K.
Equation (1) gives monotonicity at each step. Equation (2) shows the possible
converse of K does not change the starting scalar count. Hence again
H(U[S])>=H(T[S]). This argument does not require the restricted quotient to
remain prime.

Since this is proved for every S at the node, (3) gives domination of every
q_h there as well, completing the induction. QED.

In particular simultaneous reversals of original overlapping modules need not
preserve scalar path counts, but they can never decrease them. This is not a
claim about arbitrary unrelated tournaments ordered only by their cyclic
triple sets.

## 5. Exact equality criterion

Call an original transitive strong-tree node **changed to cyclic** when its
new child quotient is nontransitive. The following conditions are equivalent:

1. H(U)=H(T).
2. No original transitive strong-tree node is changed to cyclic.
3. H(U[S])=H(T[S]) for every physical subset S.
4. q_h(U[S])=q_h(T[S]) for every S,h.
5. C3(U)=C3(T).
6. U can be reached from T by a sequence reversing modules that are valid at
   the moment each reversal is performed.

**Proof of 1–4.** If no transitive quotient becomes nontransitive, the induction
in Theorem 1 becomes equality: at transitive nodes both quotients are ordered
joins, perhaps in different child orders; at prime nodes (1)–(2) and equality
of the child profiles give equality. Thus 2 implies 3 and 4, and 4 implies 1.

Conversely, suppose some transitive node M acquires a nontransitive quotient.
That quotient has at least three Hamilton paths: start with a directed
triangle's three paths and insert other vertices by any fixed valid insertion
rule; deleting the inserted vertex recovers the original path. Therefore

    H(U[M]) >= 3 product_i H(U[M_i])
             >= 3 product_i H(T[M_i]) = 3H(T[M]).          (5)

This strict increase propagates to the root. At a prime ancestor, replace the
strict child's old profile first in the eventual quotient; (1) has strictly
positive coefficient c_1, other child replacements are nondecreasing, and (2)
handles a quotient converse. At a transitive ancestor, if its new quotient
is transitive the product is strictly larger; if nontransitive the same
product lower bound is still strictly larger than the original product.
Thus H(U)>H(T). This proves 1 implies 2.

**Cyclic triples.** Directly, every original cyclic triple survives any original
module XOR. Its intersection with an original module has size zero, one, or
three, since a directed triangle has no two-vertex module. The triple is
therefore either unchanged or wholly conversed. Hence C3(T) is contained in
C3(U), regardless of the order in which original sets are listed.

Condition 3 implies 5 because the Hamilton count on a triple is one or three
according as it is transitive or cyclic. If a transitive node becomes cyclic,
pick one vertex from each of three children forming a new quotient triangle.
That triple was transitive and is now cyclic. Thus 5 implies 2. This proves
equivalence with 5 without importing a general monotonicity theorem for C3.

**Legal inversion normal form.** Assume 2. Work recursively on the persistent
original partition tree. At a prime node, its new quotient is either unchanged
or conversed. To converse only the quotient, reverse the entire node and then
reverse every child block, restoring all child interiors. Each chosen set is
currently a module.

At a transitive node, its new quotient is a permutation of the child order.
Any adjacent block swap A->B can be performed by reversing the current union
A union B, then reversing A and B. The union is currently an interval and a
module; these three operations swap the blocks and preserve their interiors.
Adjacent swaps realize any desired child permutation. After arranging the
quotient, recursively make the required changes within each child. The child
sets remain modules throughout. This constructs the legal sequence and proves
2 implies 6. Every legal module reversal preserves H of each induced subset:
the intersection is still a module and (1) applies. Hence 6 implies 3. QED.

Condition 6 is a sequence of **current** modules. It is not asserted to be one
XOR of a laminar family of original modules. Arbitrary permutations at a
transitive node may need crossing original intervals or changing current
adjacencies.

## 6. Consequence for the full named-response search

With the two named ports x->y and all-zero attachment masks,

    F_T(0,0)=H(T).

Therefore an original-module XOR can have F_U=F_T only in the equivalent
equality cases of Section 5. Every case creating a new cyclic triple is
already excluded by this actual scalar query, with H(U)>H(T).

In the surviving case the original strong tree offers a normal form:

* prime quotients may be unchanged or conversed;
* transitive quotients may have their child blocks permuted;
* the same choices recur within the children.

All induced-subset H and all q_h then agree, but this does not make the full
named two-port responses equal. A nontrivial single module converse and
pairwise-disjoint simultaneous converses are excluded by the separate named
parity/ambient-transfer results. The same-session
`simultaneous_response/ANCHORED.md` further excludes every proper nested pair
and every laminar family with an exposed anchor. Transitive source modules
are excluded even under arbitrary replacement by the `interval_modules`
packet. The remaining full-function problem includes fully covered laminar
families inside nontransitive cores, coupled quotient changes not covered by
those theorems, and arbitrary legal inversion sequences. No open status is
being reasserted for the already settled nested-pair class.

This is a proved reduction, not a full-response collision, a proof that all
legal sequences are visible, or a theorem reconstructing arbitrary tournaments.
No cubic or quartic coordinate is discarded in the residual problem. The
interval packet retains complete degree-four nested and crossing controls;
the nested control preserves every old induced-subset H but has a nonzero
full named quartic contrast. This is exactly why scalar equality is a
reduction criterion rather than the final test.

## 7. Reproduction

`verify.py` enumerates every labeled tournament and every original-module XOR
family through order five. It constructs modules and strong partitions by
their definitions, not a decomposition library. It checks original strong
modules survive, quotient actions satisfy Lemma 1, every induced-subset q_h
is nondecreasing, and all structural/scalar/C3 equality criteria agree.
Tables are computed once per named tournament and replayed for each family.

Run normally and with `python -O`; the deterministic receipts must match.
Finite checks support the implementation. The induction and explicit legal
sequence above establish the unbounded statements.
