# Arbitrary ambient contexts preserve the odd-pair reversal cube

This is a separate corollary of the frozen local theorem in `PROOF.md` and the independently reviewed [even-order modulo-eight transfer](../mod8_transfer/PROOF.md). It changes neither source proof nor checker.

## Exact assumptions

Let M be a fixed named module in an arbitrary ambient tournament T. Its induced tournament is Q[B_1,...,B_q], where q>=3 is odd and every B_i is a fixed two-name transitive block. No primeness or strong-connectivity assumption is imposed on Q or the ambient quotient K=T/M. Exterior names and every incidence involving them remain fixed.

Consider the 2^(q+1) states obtained by independently choosing Q or Q^op on the child quotient and the orientation of each pair interior. These are exactly the states generated by reversing M and any selection of its q pair children: the whole-M operation toggles the quotient and every child bit, while a child operation toggles just that bit.

**Corollary.** All these ambient full named two-port responses are distinct. At most 2q^2+2q+1 adaptive queries, or 2q^2+3q+1 fixed queries, identify the state. These are upper bounds, not optimality claims.

## Physical root battery and unchanged trace

For each local coarse query in `PROOF.md` Section 6, use the same physical pair labels inside M. Add the two named ports with x->y, and give both ports M's original incidence to each exterior vertex: if an exterior vertex beats M, it beats both ports; if M beats it, both ports beat it. This assignment is independent of the unknown internal state.

Then M union {x,y} is a common module in each actual extension. Its order is 2q+2, hence even, and its rooted exterior quotient is K. The transfer theorem gives, for every queried attachment,

    ambient contrast = lambda * local contrast (mod 8),
    lambda=H(K[TT2]), with lambda odd.

The same lambda applies to every query: K is fixed, and the transfer coefficient depends only on K, not on the chosen internal attachment. It therefore also multiplies every finite difference and their trace. The local trace is four modulo eight, so the ambient trace is again four modulo eight. Thus the same at most 2q^2+q+1 coarse assignments distinguish the two quotient orientations in every ambient context.

Every pair child is still homogeneous to both ports in these root queries. Its internal converse preserves each actual count exactly by the run-reversal bijection, regardless of the exterior. Hence this root battery is individually independent of all pair-interior bits, not merely independent after taking a sum.

## Reading the child bits

Once the quotient orientation is known, each child pair has an actual lifted single-module query with contrast two modulo four. Give the ports that child's complete exterior incidences, including both the other children and the original exterior of M. The other pair children remain modules in this extension, so their reversals do not change the chosen count. One query therefore reads each child bit independently.

There are q child queries after the quotient orientation is learned, giving at most

    (2q^2+q+1)+q = 2q^2+2q+1

adaptive queries. A fixed battery includes child queries for each of the two possible quotient orientations, giving at most

    (2q^2+q+1)+2q = 2q^2+3q+1.

Repeated assignments may be merged, so these remain valid upper bounds. The two ports always retain their physical names and their fixed mutual orientation.

## What primeness adds, and what remains outside the claim

If Q is prime, the pair blocks are the maximal proper strong children of the induced core. The standard modular-decomposition description implies that every proper nontrivial module of that core lies inside one child; since a child has two names, it is the pair itself. Therefore the q pair children and the whole core are all of its nontrivial original modules. In that prime case, the displayed cube exhausts every original-module XOR family contained in M.

This exhaustion is an internal-core statement. It does not cover additional selected ambient modules crossing M or containing M, arbitrary modifications of the exterior, or modules newly created during a legal sequence. For nonprime Q, extra original core modules may exist, and only the stated quotient/pair cube is claimed. The even-order transfer does not remove the local theorem's odd-q or two-vertex-child hypotheses.

The argument uses literal two-port counts and exact finite differences. No cubic or quartic coordinate is dropped, and scalar or profile equality alone is never substituted for full-response equality.
