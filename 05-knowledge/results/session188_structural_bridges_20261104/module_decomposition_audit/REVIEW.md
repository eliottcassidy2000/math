## Strong-tree action

For an original strong module M, each selected original module X is nested with M or disjoint. If X contains M, every exterior vertex has either all M-incidences toggled or none, preserving uniformity. If X lies inside M, no exterior incidence changes. Thus all original strong blocks remain valid modules, without a claim that they remain canonical strong modules.

If an X lies in a node but spans several of its strong children, it is a union of entire children and its indices form a quotient module. At a prime quotient only the whole quotient can be toggled nontrivially. At a transitive quotient the quotient modules are exactly intervals. Whole-node reversals from selected ancestors must also be counted. The proof explicitly retains them as full descendant reversals, so it does not lose the nested parity that would invalidate the induction.

## Hereditary positivity and strictness

Reversing a quotient with fixed blocks preserves H: whole-converse the graph, then restore each disjoint child by an internal converse, which preserves every ordered path-cover count.

At a transitive source node, a Hamilton order of any target quotient gives the required concatenation injection. At a prime source node, child replacement uses nonnegative run coefficients and the possible quotient converse leaves the starting scalar count unchanged. This proves Hamilton domination on every physical subset. The ordered set-partition formula then gives the full q-profile domination needed by the next induction step. There is no circular reliance on full q-domination before hereditary H-domination has been established at the current node.

A newly nontransitive transitive-node quotient has at least three Hamilton orders. This gives a strict local scalar increase. At a prime ancestor, the positive one-run coefficient propagates the strictness while all other q-differences are nonnegative. Empty subsets contribute H=1 and can be taken as immediate base cases; this is a harmless presentational convention, not a missing substantive case.

## Equality and legal normal form

If every transitive quotient remains transitive, child-profile equality and quotient-converse invariance give hereditary equality. If a new cyclic quotient occurs, the strictness argument rules out full H equality. Every original directed triangle survives original-module XOR because its intersection with a module has size0,1 or3; a changed-to-cyclic quotient supplies a new triangle using one vertex from each of three child blocks.

The legal sequence is constructive and valid. At a prime node, reverse the whole node and then its child blocks to reverse only the quotient. At a transitive node, the current union of adjacent child blocks is a module; reversing that union and both individual blocks swaps their order while restoring their interiors. Child sets remain modules, so recursive internal changes are legal. Conversely, each current-module reversal preserves H on every induced subset by restriction and the run expansion. Thus all six equality conditions in the source agree in exactly the stated original-XOR universe.
