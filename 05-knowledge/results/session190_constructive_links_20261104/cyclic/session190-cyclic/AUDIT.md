## Independent structural count

Put P={0,1,2}, Q={3,4,5,6,7}, F={8,9}. The normalizer is Hol(C3) × Hol(C5) × S2, of order 240. The multiplier-even circulant graphs on either 3 or 5 vertices are only the empty graphs. A normalizer-even graph therefore has independent blocks P and Q, a cross-edge bit, and a two-vertex graph on F with four possible adjacency colours recording the neighbours in P,Q.

If the two colours differ, there are six unordered colour pairs and two choices of the F-edge. If they agree, only the absent F-edge is allowed, giving four choices. Thus there are 2(6·2+4)=32 normalizer-even representatives.

Merge vertices with identical open neighbourhoods. The resulting independent twin classes are intrinsic. The two large classes have sizes at least 3 and 5, unless they merge. They can have equal sizes only when both F vertices join the size-3 class, producing two size-5 classes. The only distinct such adjacent classes give K5,5. Apart from that case, the large classes are fixed by every automorphism; any possible swap of remaining singleton classes either merges false twins or would require the excluded equal-colour F-edge. Hence every other full automorphism group consists of permutations inside independent twin classes and has trivial orientation character.

There are no identifications of different normalizer representatives: odd-order elements act trivially on the twin quotient. If the large classes remain separate, a disjoint 3-cycle and 5-cycle have uniquely determined host classes, except that the two size-5 classes of K5,5 may be exchanged by an automorphism. If the large classes merge, there is one class of size at least 8 and all disjoint (3)(5) subgroups inside its symmetric group are conjugate. Thus each graph type contributes one normalizer orbit. Exactly one of the 32 is globally odd, leaving 31.

The tournament count is 2×16=32 normalizer orbits. There are no tournament multiplicities with only two fixed vertices; the independent exhaustive unrestricted-isomorphism calculation verifies this directly, without relying on the incoming general disjoint-support lemma.

## Orientation sign is not edge-permutation sign

For K5,5 with A={0,1,2,8,9}, B={3,4,5,6,7}, let sigma=(0 3)(1 4)(2 5)(6 8)(7 9). In the ordinary numerical vertex order, sigma reverses 13 edges, so the graph's orientation character is −1. With all edges instead referenced from A to B it reverses 25; the character is unchanged. On the 25 unordered edge sets, sigma fixes five edges and exchanges the other twenty in ten transpositions. Its **edge-permutation sign is +1**, which is not the required character.

## Exact equivariance and stochastic consequences

An S10-equivariant map from labelled tournaments to labelled even graphs satisfies Aut(T) ≤ Aut(f(T)). If its induced map on isomorphism types were injective, it would inject the 32 types containing a conjugate of H into the 31 graph types containing one. Therefore no such map exists. This rules out an equivariant map inducing a type bijection, not arbitrary equivariant maps that collapse types, and not unrestricted bijections between unlabelled types. On unlabelled types themselves, relabelling acts trivially.

The NOTE's stochastic consequence is correct conditional only on a nonnegative type-indexed matrix B with unit row and column sums. For any row subset A and column subset C,

    B(A,C-complement) − B(A-complement,C) = |A| − |C|.

For these H-symmetric type sets, the right side is 1. Under a uniform choice among the 32 tournament types followed by B-sampling, the probability of obtaining a graph type containing no conjugate of H is at least 1/32. This is not a claim about uniform labelled H-fixed inputs, nor an evaluation of the order-ten Walsh matrix. The earlier construction of that matrix is outside this audit's scope.
