**CITED boundary.** Bott's original theorem concerns stable homotopy: the unitary groups have period two; the orthogonal and symplectic groups are related by a four-degree shift and therefore each have period eight. This is a statement about stable homotopy groups, not eight finite labels. See [Bott, *The Stable Homotopy of the Classical Groups*, pp. 314–315, equations (1.5)–(1.7)](https://webhomes.maths.ed.ac.uk/~v1ranick/papers/bott4.pdf).

For the negative-square convention `Cl_(0,k)`, the associated algebraic periodicity is

\[
 Cl_{0,k+8}\cong Cl_{0,k}\otimes M_{16}(\mathbb R),
\tag{1}
\]

with the real matrix size increasing by sixteen, not by eight. The table has `Cl_(0,7)=M₈(R)⊕M₈(R)` and `Cl_(0,8)=M₁₆(R)`. These are associative algebras, unlike the octonions. See [Atiyah–Bott–Shapiro, *Clifford modules*, section 4, Table 1, and section 6, Proposition (6.8)](https://webhomes.maths.ed.ac.uk/~v1ranick/papers/abs.pdf). Below we explicitly construct the `k=8` matrix algebra and an arithmetic action; we do not derive a dynamical consequence from (1).

## 2. Seven octonion operators really preserve the Fano E8 lattice

Use the binary labels `x=0,...,7` for `F₂³`, with a fixed basis and dot-product identification. Let

\[
 C=\{(s+v\cdot x)_{x\in\mathbb F_2^3}:s\in\mathbb F_2,\ v\in\mathbb F_2^3\},
\quad
 M_C=\{z\in\mathbb Z^8:z\bmod2\in C\},
\quad \Lambda_C=M_C/\sqrt2.
\tag{2}
\]

The inherited proof identifies `Λ_C` with E8: C has dimension four and weights 0,4,8, and the displayed scale gives an even unimodular lattice. Its roots, written as integer vectors z before dividing by √2, are the sixteen vectors `±2e_x` and the 224 signed weight-four code supports.

Let O be the real octonions in the inherited [Cayley–Dickson convention](https://math.ucr.edu/home/baez/octonions/node5.html), with orthonormal basis `e_0=1,e_1,...,e_7`. For i=1,...,7 define the **linear operator** `L_i(z)=e_i z`.

**F1 (PROVED).** Each L_i is a signed coordinate permutation preserving Λ_C, and

\[
 L_i^2=-I_8,\qquad L_iL_j+L_jL_i=-2\delta_{ij}I_8.
\tag{3}
\]

Its unsigned coordinate action is `x→i XOR x`. Modulo two the signs disappear, leaving a translation of the binary cube. Translating the arguments of an affine function preserves C, so L_i preserves M_C and its scale Λ_C. A signed permutation is orthogonal.

For (3), octonion alternativity makes the associator alternating in its first two arguments. Thus

\[
 e_i(e_jz)+e_j(e_iz)=(e_ie_j+e_je_i)z=-2\delta_{ij}z.
\]

This is an actual representation of `Cl_(0,7)` on the eight-dimensional space O. It is not an identification of the algebra O with `Cl_(0,7)`, nor is `x→L_x` an algebra homomorphism. For example, in the fixed convention `e₁(e₂e₄)=-e₇`, whereas `(e₁e₂)e₄=e₇`.

## 3. The eighth Clifford generator and the arithmetic carry operator

On `O⊕O`, define

\[
 \Gamma_i=\begin{pmatrix}0&L_i\\L_i&0\end{pmatrix}\quad(1\le i\le7),
 \qquad J=\Gamma_8=\begin{pmatrix}0&I_8\\-I_8&0\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{4}
\]

**F2 (PROVED).** These eight operators preserve `Λ_C⊕Λ_C` and satisfy

\[
 \Gamma_i\Gamma_j+\Gamma_j\Gamma_i=-2\delta_{ij}I_{16}.
\tag{5}
\]

The lattice claim follows from F1 and swapping the two copies. The relations follow by block multiplication. The 256 ordered squarefree monomials in the Γ_i form an orthogonal basis of `M₁₆(R)` for the matrix inner product `tr(AᵀB)`. To prove orthogonality, every nonempty monomial has trace zero: conjugate it by a generator inside its support if its length is even, or by one outside if its length is odd. The conjugation changes its sign. Products of two different monomials reduce, up to sign, to a nonempty monomial. Each monomial is orthogonal and has squared matrix norm sixteen. Dimension then proves `Cl_(0,8)≅M₁₆(R)` in this representation.

Put

\[
 D=\operatorname{diag}(I_8,-I_8),\qquad S=DJ.
\]

In the chosen convention `Γ₁⋯Γ₈=-D`; this last convention-dependent sign is checked exactly. The arithmetic formulas below define D directly, so they do not depend on forgetting that sign.

For real parameters a,b and positive d, define the Clifford operator

\[
 A(a,b,d)=\frac{(a+d)I+(a-d)D+b(S+J)}{2d}
          =\frac1d\begin{pmatrix}aI_8&bI_8\\0&dI_8\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{6}
\]

This is an explicit four-term expression for the affine carry. Although I,D,S,J are lattice isometries, their linear combination (6) need not preserve the lattice or norm. A positive triangular matrix of this form is orthogonal only when a=d and b=0. Clifford algebra membership is not a contraction theorem.

Choose the weight-four codeword `h(x)=x₂`, the high binary coordinate, and the E8 root `ρ=h/√2`. Define the arithmetic carrier

\[
 \Psi(n)=(n\rho,\rho)\in\Lambda_C\oplus\Lambda_C,
 \qquad n\in\mathbb Z.
\tag{7}
\]

It is injective, with `n=<first component,ρ>/2`, and

\[
 A(a,b,d)\Psi(n)=\Psi\left(\frac{an+b}{d}\right),
 \qquad \|\Psi(n)\|^2=2(n^2+1).
\tag{8}
\]

The output of (8) belongs to `Λ_C⊕Λ_C` **if and only if `(an+b)/d` is an integer**. In the unscaled first component its coordinates are that scalar times a zero-one word with four ones, so integer coordinates force the scalar to be integral. Conversely an integer multiple of h has parity either zero or h, both in C. This primitive choice of ρ is important; an arbitrary nonprimitive lattice vector would give a weaker divisibility test.

For the shortcut maps

\[
 T_\sigma(n)=\begin{cases}n/2&n\text{ even},\\(3n+\sigma)/2&n\text{ odd},\end{cases}
 \qquad \sigma\in\{-1,+1\},
\tag{9}
\]

take a finite parity word `ε₀,...,ε_(L−1)`. Start at `(a,b,d)=(1,0,1)`. An even symbol doubles d; an odd symbol first replaces `(a,b)` by `(3a,3b+σd)` and then doubles d. Thus `d=2^L`, `a=3^(sum ε_i)`, and the word has endpoint `(an+b)/d`.

**F3 (PROVED).** For integer n, endpoint integrality is equivalent to every intermediate integer and the declared parity word being legal. Necessity follows by peeling the first step: divisibility of the final numerator by `2^L` forces the first numerator even, because all later multipliers are odd and later added offsets are even. Divide by two and apply the same argument to the tail. Sufficiency follows by direct iteration. Consequently lattice admissibility of (8), for the specified word operator, is an exact arithmetic guard. On positive inputs both signs in (9) stay positive.

This constructs a faithful lattice representation of guarded Collatz words. The entire n and the specified word operator remain present. Forgetting to eight basis labels or to a norm does not retain this guard.

## 4. The nonlinear three-bit carry switches E8 gluing over D8

The parent lane's three-bit map records the first three shortcut parities, not the numerical endpoint after three steps. For a residue x modulo eight, write `q_σ(x)=ε₀+2ε₁+4ε₂`. Direct arithmetic gives

\[
 q_+=[0,5,2,3,4,1,6,7],\qquad
 q_-=[0,7,6,1,4,3,2,5].
\tag{10}
\]

The plus map swaps coordinate labels 1 and 5. In bit coordinates,

\[
 q_+(x_0,x_1,x_2)=(x_0,x_1,x_2+x_0+x_0x_1),
\quad
 q_-(x_0,x_1,x_2)=(x_0,x_1+x_0,x_2+x_0+x_1).
\tag{11}
\]

Thus the minus map is linear, while the plus map has polar defect

\[
 q_+(x)+q_+(y)+q_+(x+y)
 =4(x_0y_1+x_1y_0),
\tag{12}
\]

where addition of labels in this equation means XOR. The root lane retains this central carry when transporting Fano incidence. Here its effect on the metric lattice can also be specified exactly.

Let P swap coordinates 1 and 5, put `C'=PC`, and let `Λ'=PΛ_C`.

**F4 (PROVED).** The intersection `C∩C'` has dimension three and consists of the affine functions independent of x₂. Therefore

\[
 [\Lambda_C:\Lambda_C\cap\Lambda']
 =[\Lambda':\Lambda_C\cap\Lambda']=2.
\tag{13}
\]

Indeed replacing x by q_+(x) in `s+v·x` introduces the quadratic term `v₂x₀x₁`; it remains affine exactly when `v₂=0`. Equivalently a codeword must agree at coordinates 1 and 5: otherwise its difference from its swap has weight two, impossible for a nonzero codeword of C.

Pair coordinates `(0,4),(1,5),(2,6),(3,7)` and apply

\[
 Q:\ (z_x,z_{x+4})/\sqrt2\ \longmapsto\
 ((z_x+z_{x+4})/2,(z_x-z_{x+4})/2).
\tag{14}
\]

For a vector in the intersection, parity is constant within each pair, so every output coordinate is an integer. Its coordinate sum is `sum_(x=0)^3 z_x`, even by the affine parity check on that plane. Thus the image is contained in D8. Both lattices have covolume two: the intersection has index two in unimodular E8, and D8 has index two in Z8. The inclusion is therefore equality.

In this chart, `QPQ⁻¹` negates just the difference coordinate of the pair `(1,5)`. Moreover

\[
 Q\Lambda_C=D_8\cup(D_8+\tfrac12\mathbf1),
\quad
 Q\Lambda'=D_8\cup(D_8+\tfrac12\mathbf1-e_j),
\tag{15}
\]

where j is that one difference coordinate. The nonlinear parity-address change exchanges the two spinor glue classes over the same D8 lattice. This is an exact orthogonal transport, not preservation of the original code. The lattices have 112 common roots and 128 other roots each: the common roots become `±e_i±e_j`, while the two half-coordinate root families are exchanged by the sign flip.

For comparison, q_- is an invertible linear change of binary labels, so it preserves C and Λ_C outright. Both signs therefore have explicit lattice behavior, with a genuine difference at the first nonlinear plus carry. This difference alone does not separate convergent from nonconvergent dynamics.

Transporting the seven L_i by P, and the eight Γ_i by `P⊕P`, preserves all their algebraic identities on the transported lattice. The arithmetic operator (6) commutes with `P⊕P`, since its blocks are scalar multiples of I8. Accordingly the square

\[
 \Psi(n)\xrightarrow{A}\Psi(m),\qquad
 (P\oplus P)\Psi(n)\xrightarrow{A}(P\oplus P)\Psi(m)
\]

commutes whenever the arithmetic guard holds. Our chosen high-coordinate root is moved to a root of `Λ'` outside `Λ_C`, so this transport is visible in the arithmetic carrier. It preserves the integer and its exact word; the code frame changes.

## 5. What quadratic and quartic norm certificates can prove

Set `Q(n)=||Ψ(n)||²/2=n²+1`. If a legal word sends positive n to `m=(an+b)/d`, then

\[
 Q(n)-Q(m)
 =\frac{((d-a)n-b)((d+a)n+b)}{d^2}.
\tag{16}
\]

The second factor is `d(n+m)>0`. Thus this sum-of-squares norm decreases **exactly when m<n**. Squaring it gives no stronger sign test:

\[
 Q(n)^2-Q(m)^2=(Q(n)-Q(m))(n^2+m^2+2).
\tag{17}
\]

For a slope-contracting word `a<d`, (16) is an exact norm certificate on the legal inputs satisfying `n>b/(d-a)`. This is a geometric packaging of the inherited affine descent threshold, not a new proof that every input reaches such a word. Bott stabilization by tensoring an operator with an identity matrix leaves this norm-sign issue unchanged.

**F5 (PROVED obstruction).** Fix finite integers `k≥0`, `L≥1`. Suppose a proposed potential has the form

\[
 V(n)=P_{n\bmod2^k}(n),
\tag{18}
\]

where each P_r is a real polynomial tending to +∞ on the positive real axis. Then V cannot strictly decrease after every L shortcut steps for all sufficiently large positive integers, for either choice of σ.

For arbitrarily large positive t take

\[
 n=2^{k+L}t-\sigma.
\]

The first L states are odd, and the exact recurrence `T_σ(n)+σ=3(n+σ)/2` gives

\[
 T_\sigma^L(n)=3^L2^kt-\sigma>n.
\tag{19}
\]

Both endpoints have the same residue `−σ mod 2^k`. The same polynomial is used at both, and it is strictly increasing beyond a finite threshold. Thus V increases on arbitrarily large legal examples. This covers coercive residue-dependent quadratic and quartic sum-of-squares candidates, not merely a single unweighted norm. It is not a general circuit lower bound or an obstruction to adaptive stopping times and unbounded arithmetic sidecars.

The smallest displayed three-bit examples in the test family are `63→215` for plus and `65→217` for minus, each after three odd shortcut steps with unchanged residue modulo eight. The minus cycle `5→7→10→5` also survives exactly in the lattice carrier. It forbids a strictly descending global rank that excludes this cycle from its terminal set, independently of how high a polynomial degree is allowed.

The constructive output is therefore a lattice guard, a Clifford expression for every affine word, and an exact repair of the first nonlinear Fano coordinate change. The unresolved obligation is an adaptive certificate selecting sufficiently contracting legal words for every positive plus input. Neither the finite Fano geometry, Bott periodicity, nor the quartic norm factorization supplies that selection.
