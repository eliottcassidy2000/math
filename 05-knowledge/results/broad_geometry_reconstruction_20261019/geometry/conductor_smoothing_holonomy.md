# The theta conductor has a two-holonomy smoothing certificate

Status: PROVED local algebra and line-bundle calculation; CONDITIONAL
application to any normal-crossing surface with the stated normalization
and conductor. This is a necessary smooth-total-space smoothing criterion,
not a sufficiency theorem and not a verification of the S6 manuscript.

## 1. From cotangent torsion to the deformation sheaf

THM-3955-node-cotangent-normalization-kernel-and-conductor-torsion and
THM-3957-triple-normal-crossing-cotangent-conductor-kernel-and-normalization-cokernel
recover data erased by normalizing a normal-crossing surface. A different
consumer of the same local presentation is the first-order deformation
sheaf

    T^1_W = sheaf Ext^1(Ω_W,O_W).

One must dualize the full locally free presentation of `Ω_W`, rather than
simply dualizing its torsion submodule.

For a hypersurface `B=R/(f)` in a smooth threefold, at an ordinary double
or triple crossing the conormal presentation is

    0 -> B --df--> B^3 -> Ω_B -> 0.

It is injective because the partial derivatives have no common annihilator
in the reduced coordinate-crossing ring. Applying `Hom_B(-,B)` gives

    T^1_B = B/(partial f/partial x,
                 partial f/partial y,
                 partial f/partial z).                  (1)

At a double point `f=xy`, this is `B/(x,y)`; at a triple point `f=xyz`, it
is `B/(xy,xz,yz)`. These are exactly the reduced double-locus rings. On
changing local hypersurface equations the generators differ by units, so
`T^1_W` is an invertible sheaf on the reduced singular curve `D`.

If `W` is the reduced central fibre of a map `f:X->disc` from a smooth
threefold, then `O_X(W)` is trivial, generated by the global fibre
equation. Applying the preceding dual presentation globally shows

    T^1_W = O_X(W)|_D = O_D.                              (2)

This proves the necessary d-semistability condition directly. It does not
prove that a d-semistable surface has a smoothing: additional global
obstructions can remain.

## 2. The triple-point contribution is exactly one at each endpoint

Let `C` be a smooth component of the double curve whose two preimages in
the normalization `nu:V->W` are `C_+` and `C_-`, each mapping isomorphically
to `C`. Suppose all its triple points are ordinary and distinct, and let
`Z` be their reduced divisor on `C`. Then

    T^1_W|_C = N_(C_+/V) tensor N_(C_-/V) tensor O_C(Z).   (3)

Here the two normal bundles are transported to `C` by the specified branch
identifications. Formula (3) retains those identifications; it does not
only record their degrees.

Away from triple points, a crossing has equation `xy=0`; multiplying
normal coordinates by `a,b` multiplies the smoothing coefficient by `ab`,
which gives the normal-bundle product. At a triple point use `xyz=0` with
`C` the `z`-axis. On the punctured axis the node equation is `xy=0` and
`xyz=epsilon` becomes `xy=epsilon/z`. Thus the triple-chart generator is
`1/z` times the double-chart generator. This contributes `O_C(P)` at the
triple point `P`. Repeating at all triple points proves (3).

For the hexagonal del Pezzo conductor in the manuscript, each pair of
opposite boundary curves consists of two `(-1)`-curves and its image has
two triple points. Thus, provided the stated normal-crossing gluing is
realized, each restriction has degree

    deg T^1_W|_C = -1-1+2 = 0.                            (4)

This numerical balance is necessary, but it does not yet prove (2).

The formula and its irreducible/nonnormal scope are consistent with
Schroeer and Siebert, *Irreducible degenerations of primary Kodaira
surfaces*, Section 2 and Lemma 2.3:
https://www.math.uni-duesseldorf.de/~schroeer/publications_pdf/kodaira.pdf
The argument above proves the specific formula used here independently.

## 3. Exact degree-zero Picard and cohomology calculation

Let `D_n` be the connected reduced seminormal curve made from `n>=2`
copies `C_i=P^1` by identifying their `0` points to one point `P`, and
their infinity points to one point `Q`, with ordinary seminormal
`n`-branch crossing structure at both points. For `n=3`, the local ring
at a crossing is the union-of-three-axes ring
`C[x,y,z]/(xy,xz,yz)`, precisely the triple-point conductor above.

An invertible sheaf `L` of multidegree zero is trivial on each `C_i`.
Choose trivializations on those components and bases of its fibres at
`P,Q`. Let their endpoint identifications have nonzero scalars `a_i,b_i`;
write `r_i=b_i/a_i`. Rescaling a component trivialization cancels in this
ratio; rescaling the two vertex bases multiplies every `r_i` by the same
nonzero constant. Therefore

    Pic^0(D_n) = (C*)^(n-1),
    [L] <-> (r_2/r_1,...,r_n/r_1).                       (5)

This is also a completeness statement: the `a_i,b_i` give all gluing data
for a line bundle on the seminormal curve, and the indicated changes are
exactly their trivialization gauge. In particular

    L is trivial iff r_1=...=r_n.                         (6)

A global section is constant on each `P^1`. If its values in the two
vertex fibres are `v_P,v_Q`, the conditions are `r_i v_P=v_Q` for all
`i`. Hence it is nonzero iff every ratio agrees. Thus

    h^0(D_n,L)=1 if L is trivial, and 0 otherwise.         (7)

For completeness, the normalization sequence for an invertible sheaf is

    0 -> L -> direct_sum_i O_(P^1) -> C_P^(n-1)
                                          + C_Q^(n-1) -> 0.

It gives `chi(L)=n-2(n-1)=2-n`. Combining with (7),

    h^1(D_n,L)=n-1 if L is trivial, and n-2 otherwise.     (8)

No planar multiple-point structure is intended: ordinary plane triple
points have a different local normalization defect. Seminormal crossing
structure is load-bearing in both the Picard and Euler calculations.

## 4. The two-holonomy certificate for the hexagon

For `D=D_3`, equations (4)-(6) show that `T^1_W` has two well-defined
multiplicative holonomies

    h_2=r_2/r_1, h_3=r_3/r_1.

A necessary condition for `W` to admit a one-parameter smoothing with
smooth total space is

    (h_2,h_3)=(1,1).                                     (9)

If either holonomy differs from one, then

    H^0(D,T^1_W)=0,

so no first-order deformation has a nonzero local smoothing section.
If both are one, `h^0=1` and `h^1=2`. The latter nonvanishing is a reminder
that this local compatibility certificate does not settle global
unobstructedness or existence.

A cheap explicit hostile is the degree-zero line bundle with endpoint
ratios `(r_1,r_2,r_3)=(1,2,1)`: each normalized component restriction is
trivial, but `h^0=0`. Positive controls are `(1,1,1)` and `(2,2,2)`; the
common factor is gauge, so both describe the trivial line bundle.

These are examples of line bundles on `D`. They do not assert that every
such bundle occurs as `T^1` of a surface obtained by gluing the particular
del Pezzo hexagon. That realization question is separate.

## 5. What this adds to the construction problem

The relevant diagram of information loss is now concrete:

    normal-crossing surface and branch identifications
       -> T^1 line bundle on its conductor
       -> restrictions to normalized conductor components
       -> three integer degrees.

The last two objects cannot recover the two holonomies in (9). Conversely,
a computation of the actual transition units for `T^1` gives a finite
certificate: multiply their ratios around the two independent cycles of
the theta graph and test equality to one.

This connects the cotangent/conductor lane to deformation theory and to
rank-one local systems on a graph. The source is the full hypersurface
cotangent presentation; the map is `sheaf Ext^1`, followed by restriction
and gluing. The preserved condition is local smoothability of the node or
triple crossing and the branchwise degree balance. The information lost
by normalization is the multiplicative graph holonomy. The required
sidecar is the two transition products, modulo component and vertex frame
changes. The decisive test is (9).

This is distinct from MISTAKE-480: no raw twist kernel is being declared an
analytic parameter. Formula (5) computes an actual line-bundle quotient.
Nor are we asserting that the two holonomies are independently adjustable
in the manuscript's gluing family.

If the paper's claimed smooth total space `N0` with global function `t`
has already been constructed, (2) forces both holonomies to equal one
without further work. The new use is earlier in the argument: compute
(9) directly from the boundary identifications before assuming such a
smooth total space exists. A nontrivial value would rule out that specific
candidate gluing; a trivial value closes one necessary compatibility gate
and no more.

## 6. The manuscript's explicit toric deck maps give both holonomies one

This necessary gate can be closed at the level of the manuscript's displayed
maps. No numerical period function or proper-discontinuity estimate is needed.

Use its cocharacter lattice `N'=Z^2 direct_sum Z`, its height-one A2 fan,
and `m0=(0,0,1)` in the character lattice. The toric function is

    t=chi^(m0).

Every primitive ray generator `(v,1)` has pairing one with `m0`.
In a maximal unimodular cone with coordinate characters dual to its three
ray generators, `m0` is the sum of those three characters. Consequently
`t=z1*z2*z3` exactly. At boundary points where one or more coordinates are
units this is the corresponding double or smooth local model. This builds
the smooth toric local charts and their common fibre equation upstairs;
it does not presuppose an analytic quotient by the infinite deck group.

The manuscript's deck action (Section 4, also its page-2 setup) is

    Psi_lambda = (c_lambda(t),1) * Phi_lambda,
    Phi_lambda induced by S_lambda(y,y3)
                          =(y+y3*B0*lambda,y3).

The character calculation is immediate:

    m0 composed with S_lambda=m0,
    chi^(m0)(c_lambda(t),1)=1,
    Psi_lambda^*t=t.                                     (10)

Thus `t` is fixed both by toric chart changes and by every deck generator.
The conormal frame `t mod t^2` of `Y0` in the smooth toric `Y` is a global
frame with trivial deck character. Its dual maps onto the local `T^1`
sheaf in (1), sending the dual frame to the constant class `1` in `O_D`.
Functoriality of the conormal presentation under an ambient automorphism
preserving `t` shows that this local `T^1` generator is also fixed.

Therefore the upstairs trivialization of `T^1` is equivariant with trivial
character. Provided the quotient locally identifies these normal-crossing charts
without stabilizers, as asserted in the manuscript, this trivialization
descends to the quotient central fibre and conductor. In the induced frames
the transition units are all exactly one. In particular the two cycle
products of Section 4 are

    (h_2,h_3)=(1,1).                                     (11)

This is an explicit equivariant-algebra certificate from the displayed
maps, rather than an inference from an assumed smooth quotient. It is
conditional on using those maps and on forming their asserted locally
unramified geometric quotient: it does not prove the action is free/proper, the quotient is
Hausdorff/compact, or the full global gluing is valid. If alternative
boundary identifications are proposed without an ambient t-preserving
lift, their `T^1` transition units must be recomputed and (11) need not
survive.

Thus this lane produces positive proof progress: the manuscript's toric
clutch formulas pass the necessary d-semistability/holonomy test. The
residual hard gates are analytic quotient formation and global topology,
not the branchwise degree balance or these two smoothing holonomies.

## 7. Reproducibility and literature scope

The exact checker `verify_geometry_extensions.py` contains a separate
six-equation endpoint evaluation complex for five positive/hostile ratio
triples. It verifies `(h^0,h^1)=(1,2)` at equal ratios and `(0,1)` otherwise.
The proof (5)-(8) works for every `n>=2` and all complex nonzero ratios.

The necessary condition (2) is the standard d-semistability obstruction,
not a new general smoothing theorem. Besides the primary Schroeer-Siebert
source above, see Nikolaos Tziolas, *Smoothings of Fano varieties with
normal crossing singularities*:
https://arxiv.org/abs/1005.0531 . Its Fano sufficiency results are not
applicable merely from the hypotheses of this note. The contribution here
is the explicit theta-conductor holonomy/cohomology certificate and its
placement alongside the repository's local conductor differential losses.
