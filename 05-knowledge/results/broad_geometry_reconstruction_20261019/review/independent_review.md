## 2. Seifert gauge quotient and admissibility

### 2.1 Independent proof of the group and torsion coordinate

Write the two gauge columns as k1=(1,a,0), k2=(1,0,b), and let g=gcd(a,b). The entries of their matrix have gcd 1. Its maximal minors are −a, b, and ab, whose gcd is g. Thus the cokernel is Z⊕Z/g.

The linear functional phi=(ab,−b,−a) has coefficient gcd g, so phi/g is primitive on Z³. It kills both columns and therefore induces a surjection from the quotient to Z. Its kernel is precisely the finite torsion summand.

The proposed representative tau=(0,a/g,−b/g) satisfies g·tau=k1−k2. Conversely, if r·tau=x k1+y k2, the first coordinate gives y=−x and the second gives r=gx. Thus tau has exact order g, not merely order dividing g.

Euclidean division of the second and third coordinates produces a unique gauge-normalized triple with 0≤ell1<a and 0≤ell2<b. Hence a fixed p has g classes when g divides p, and none otherwise. This is a statement about the specified labelled integer-presentation quotient. It does not classify analytic fillings or unmarked Seifert spaces.

### 2.2 A separately derived global count

There is an independent inclusion–exclusion identity:

C_(a,b)(p) = Σ_{d|a, e|b} μ(d)μ(e) gcd(a/d,b/e)
                         · 1_{de·gcd(a/d,b/e) divides p}.

Proof: impose d|x and e|y in bx+ay≡−p (mod ab). Put x=du, y=ev. The homomorphism from Z/(a/d)⊕Z/(b/e) to Z/(ab) is generated by bd and ae. Its image is the subgroup of multiples of

h=gcd(ab,bd,ae)=de·gcd(a/d,b/e).

Each nonempty fibre has cardinality h/(de)=gcd(a/d,b/e). Inclusion–exclusion over the nonunit prime divisors of x and y gives the formula. This is a classical Möbius calculation supplied here as an independent verification route, not a priority claim.

Factoring this identity primewise recovers the note's cases. If A>B, the term b0x in the divided congruence has unit x, while the other term is divisible by q. Thus P must equal B, and every unit y modulo q^B gives exactly one unit x modulo q^A. If A=B=e, the two forbidden residue classes for x coincide precisely when P>e; otherwise there are two. This yields q^(e−1)(q−1) or q^(e−1)(q−2), respectively.

The zero case p=0, the q=2 cancellation, negative p, and unequal valuations are all covered. In particular the count depends only on p modulo ab even though it is conveniently expressed using valuations of an integer representative.

### 2.3 Clutch cokernel and information boundary

For the displayed 2×2 matrix R, gcd(a,ell1)=1 already forces the first Smith divisor to be one. Its determinant is −p. Thus the clutch cokernel is cyclic of order |p| for p≠0 and is Z for p=0. The latter matrix has rank one because a is nonzero.

The three normalized triples for (a,b,p)=(5,5,10) are correct. Their common cyclic clutch group does not identify their labelled gauge classes. No analytic inequivalence follows merely from this arithmetic difference.

**Independent controls:** every ordered pair 2≤a,b≤24; 89,401 residue classes; 178,802 signed tests using −ab≤p<ab; 1,289 explicitly normalized torsion-orbit points. Direct pair enumeration, Möbius inclusion–exclusion, and the primewise formula all agree.

## 3. Exterior Smith factors and mapping-torus groups

### 3.1 Complete integral reduction

For N²=0 with rank N=2 on a rank-four lattice, K=ker N is saturated because L/K embeds into the torsion-free group L. The image of N is a finite-index subgroup of K. Splitting the free quotient L/K and applying Smith reduction to its induced map into K produces a lattice basis with Nf1=se1 and Nf2=te2, s|t.

On Λ²L, the only nonzero columns are the three printed in the note. Their entries have gcd s. All 2×2 minors are divisible by s², and one is exactly −s². The rank is two because the last column has an e1∧f2 component and another nonzero column lies on e1∧e2. Therefore the two Smith factors are s,s. No division by t is hidden in this argument.

Integral duality Λ³M≅M^(−T), with determinant one, preserves the Smith factors of M−I after multiplication by unimodular matrices. This gives s,t in degree three. The invariant ranks and all stated cokernels follow.

The family diag(1,d) is a valid unrestricted hostile family: degree-two Smith data stay constant while the degree-one torsion order grows with d. Rational invariant ranks cannot detect either Smith factor.

### 3.2 Wang sequence

The Wang exact sequence gives

0 → coker(Λ^k M−I) → H_k(Y_M;Z) → ker(Λ^(k−1)M−I) → 0.

The right term is free, so the extension splits as an abstract abelian group. Combining the independently verified ranks gives Betti numbers (1,3,6,6,3,1) and torsion exactly as printed. The splitting is noncanonical. Neither the cohomology ring nor the monodromy conjugacy class is reconstructed from these groups.

**Independent controls:** 56 shears with 1≤s≤7 and 1≤t/s≤8, each conjugated by a full unimodular 4×4 basis change mixing the kernel and complementary coordinates; all 280 exterior matrices. Smith factors were recovered from rational rank and gcds of minors, without a Smith-normal-form library call. This is stronger than checking only block-diagonal representatives, while still only a finite verification of the unrestricted proof.

## 4. What the toric consequences actually assume

I read THM-3991 itself rather than relying on its label. Its hypotheses include the lattice-periodic unimodular triangulation, vertices in the full lattice, prescribed translation of toric strata, a free proper action on an invariant neighborhood, and compact quotient central fibre. Their roles are essential:

- Vertex orbits give exactly d irreducible components only with the vertex/period-lattice identification.
- The finite toric stratification has compactly supported Euler characteristic equal to the number of top-simplex orbits.
- Unimodular normalized volume makes that count 2d in rank two.
- Compactness identifies compactly supported and ordinary Euler characteristics.

Under those hypotheses, d=[K:B(L/K)]=st, so the proposed formula chi(W)=2|Tors coker(M−I)| is correct. It is not a formula for arbitrary degenerations with the same rational monodromy type.

For top homology, let S be the union of the lower-dimensional singular/conductor strata. It has real dimension at most two. The complement U is the disjoint union of the d connected oriented smooth four-dimensional pieces of the irreducible components. The Borel–Moore exact sequence gives

0=H_4(S;Q) → H_4(W;Q) → H_4^BM(U;Q) → H_3(S;Q)=0.

Thus H_4(W;Q)=Q^d. Over Q, the universal-coefficient theorem gives dim H^4(W;Q)=d. A smooth complex two-torus has dim H^4=1. Whenever the stated proper degeneration supplies the usual specialization map in that direction, it cannot be injective for d>1. This rank argument does not compute an integral kernel, prove realization of a degeneration, or establish any global gluing.

The real A2 triangulated torus has cell counts (d,3d,2d) and Euler characteristic zero. The toric complex central fibre has Euler characteristic 2d. The independent verifier keeps these two Euler characteristics distinct.

The inference from a single nonzero-Euler fibre to the total threefold also retains the proper fibration and zero-Euler assumptions for every other stratum. Only then does sphere homology force st=1.

## 5. Deformation sheaf and theta holonomy

### 5.1 Local and global algebra

For xy=0 or xyz=0, the differential map in the hypersurface conormal presentation is injective: the relevant partial derivatives have zero common annihilator in the reduced crossing ring. Dualizing gives T¹=B/J_f, which is the reduced double-locus ring in these models.

If the crossing surface is a reduced fibre of a function on a smooth ambient threefold, the normal line bundle O_X(W) has the global fibre-equation frame. The dual conormal presentation therefore gives T¹≅O_D. This is the standard necessary d-semistability condition. It is not a sufficient smoothing theorem.

Along a smooth double-curve branch, the normal-bundle tensor product gives the node smoothing line. At an ordinary triple point, changing xyz to the punctured node equation xy changes the frame by 1/z, which adds O_C(P). Therefore the normal-product plus triple-divisor formula, including the +1 contribution at each endpoint, has the correct sign.

### 5.2 Seminormal conductor, not a planar triple point

For n copies of P¹ with their zeros identified and their infinities identified as ordinary seminormal n-branch points, normalized component trivializations and the two vertex frames leave exactly n−1 endpoint-ratio invariants. The result Pic^0(D_n)=(C*)^(n−1) is complete in that category.

A section is a system of constants obeying r_i v_P=v_Q. It is nonzero exactly when all r_i agree. The normalization quotient has length 2(n−1), so chi(L)=2−n. The stated h0 and h1 follow. The theta case n=3 has (h0,h1)=(1,2) for the trivial class and (0,1) otherwise.

The degree-zero hostile (1,2,1) is a valid line-bundle hostile. It has not been shown to occur as T¹ of every proposed hexagonal surface gluing. The text explicitly preserves that distinction.

### 5.3 Actual manuscript deck maps

The supplied paper's displayed action has a block shear fixing the last lattice coordinate, followed by a torus factor with last coordinate one. Consequently the height character m0=(0,0,1), the toric function t=χ^m0, its conormal frame t mod t², and the induced T¹ frame are fixed.

This is a genuine conditional positive certificate: the upstairs trivialization has trivial deck character. If the quotient is locally unramified and identifies those normal-crossing charts without stabilizers, the frame descends and both theta holonomies are one. The revised artifact states this quotient hypothesis explicitly, following review feedback. Merely having an arbitrary coarse quotient would not justify identifying its T¹ with the descended upstairs sheaf.

No numerical period value is needed for this character calculation. That does not prove the action is free/proper or that its quotient is Hausdorff or compact. The certificate closes a necessary compatibility test, not the analytic existence test.

**Independent controls:** 28 endpoint complexes for n=2,…,8, including common nonunit ratios and unequal/sign-mixed ratios; nine explicit block-shear character checks. These agree with the unrestricted proofs.

## 6. Line arrangements and nonlinear conductor information

For a reduced union of distinct lines, restriction of a homogeneous polynomial gives exactly the degree-q evaluation space E_q. Normalization allows independent coefficients on all branches; the value-equalizer allows arbitrary positive-degree branch coefficients but a common constant. Therefore the two defect-length formulas follow degree by degree.

The value-equalizer is the seminormalization here: it is finite, has the same residue fields and underlying point set as the union, and satisfies the square/cube criterion inside the normalization. Finiteness and eventual surjectivity follow from explicit products of linear separators. This is straight-line algebra; curved branches are not controlled by tangent directions alone.

After choosing a linear coordinate with value one on every direction, e_i∈E_q exactly when a degree-q branch-supported monomial exists. Multiplication by that coordinate propagates such separators to every larger degree. An ideal of the normalization product ring decomposes branchwise, proving the exact conductor exponent formula.

For the two explicit families, the old conic evaluations are univariate polynomials of every degree up to 2q. The extra quadratic xz−y² vanishes on all old directions but not on the displaced one. This proves the complete Hilbert-rank formulas, not merely the quadratic discrepancy.

The uniform matroid assertion follows from Vandermonde determinants and the exact secant-line computation at the displaced point. The all-r defect difference and conductor exponents follow by finite sums and interpolation. The original terminology “ambient-transverse lines in A³” was correctly replaced by distinct tangent directions and reduced pairwise intersections; the formulas did not depend on the inaccurate terminology.

The full numerical first-jet space E1 does recover all E_q by coordinatewise products. The information loss is passage to the abstract linear-dependence matroid, not passage from full tangent coordinates to higher powers. This boundary is explicitly correct.

**Independent controls:** both families for r=6,…,13; 1,972 triple determinants; 824 separator tests using deletion of evaluation rows, a different criterion from the producer's augmented-column test. All ranks, lengths and branch exponents agree.

### 6.1 Final sharpenings: the first possible size and complete six-branch dichotomy

The final Sections 3.6–3.7 of the producer note were independently reviewed after the initial draft of this report. They are sound.

For any U(3,r) configuration with 3≤r≤5, fix one projective point. Partition the at most four other points into groups of at most two and choose a line through each group avoiding the selected point. Uniformity guarantees avoidance for a two-point group; the field is infinite for singleton/empty choices. The product is a quadratic separator. Thus E2 is all of k^r. For r=3, E1 is already all of k^3. For r=4,5, no degree-one separator exists because the remaining points span k³. Therefore there is no defect or conductor variability before r=6 in this precise category.

For U(3,6), every five degree-two evaluation rows are independent by the same separator argument. Hence h2 is either five or six. It equals five exactly when a nonzero homogeneous quadratic vanishes at all six directions. Under no-three-collinear this conic cannot be reducible or a double line, so it is a smooth irreducible conic over the stated algebraically closed characteristic-zero field.

In the conic case the one-dimensional left annihilator of the evaluation matrix has every coefficient nonzero, because any five rows are independent. Thus no coordinate vector belongs to E2. Products of three lines covering the other five points in groups of sizes 2,2,1 provide degree-three separators. All branch conductor exponents are therefore three and the seminormal defect is four. Off the conic, E2=k^6, all conductor exponents are two, and the defect is three. This is a complete U(3,6) dichotomy, not only a property of the two chosen examples.

These mechanisms are classical graded evaluation/seminormalization/interpolation. The explicit uniform-matroid hostile family, complete six-branch dichotomy, and their repository transfer are valid scoped contributions; no literature-wide novelty or general-r extremality theorem has been established.

## 7. Critical characters and minimum repair

Pairing degree-zero divisors with c descends through the Laplacian quotient precisely when Lc=0 mod m. Adding a constant to c changes no degree-zero pairing, so choosing a root outside the indicated cut, or subtracting the root value, has no effect on this test.

For a bipartite 0/1 cut modulo two, the Laplacian syndrome is the weighted-degree parity. Adding parallel copies therefore produces exactly a T-join problem. In a minimum repair no edge needs two added copies, since deleting two copies preserves all parities and reduces cost. This argument also respects an involution when applied to each edge orbit.

There are eight odd vertices. Four added edges is the lower bound. Equality forces a perfect matching on those vertices; the two forced edges and remaining K_(2,2) give exactly the two displayed minimizers. The involution exchanges them. All valid repair sets have even size by summing the boundary over one bipartition class. Therefore an invariant repair costs at least six, and the displayed six-edge witness attains it.

The number 27 is an exact finite property of this printed graph. My independent parity-syndrome dynamic program gives cost counts (0,0,0,0,2,0,63) without symmetry and (0,0,0,0,0,0,27) with the involution. It does not use the producer's subset enumeration.

The critical groups were independently recovered from Bareiss determinants, ranks modulo prime divisors, and mod-4 kernel-lifting counts. No Smith routine was used in this control. The answers are:

- original: C_74748;
- four-edge repair: C_2⊕C_4⊕C_49156, order 393248;
- symmetric six-edge repair: C_4⊕C_4⊕C_46620, order 745920.

The repaired cut character is nonzero because its pairing with a divisor supported at vertices on opposite sides is one. The repair changes the integral graph and its Jacobian. It does not produce a character of the original graph or prove that the added copies are lawful dynamical transitions.

The harmonic-character and T-join mechanisms are classical; the exact application and symmetry premium are the scoped result.

## 8. Bounded-run moments, decoding, and sharpness

Telescoping the discrete difference (x+1)^n−x^n gives the triangular transform from the first 2k power moments to the 2k signed endpoint powers. The logarithm of A(T)/B(T) has precisely those coefficients. If two at-most-k-run sets share the moments, cross multiplication produces a polynomial of degree at most 2k that decays at infinity after cancellation to order 2k+1. It must vanish. Reduced monic numerator/denominator pairs are therefore equal.

The constructive equations are correct. At the minimal feasible common degree d, the rational approximant is unique: any two candidates have numerator cross difference of degree at most 2d≤2k and agree to order 2k+1 at zero, hence are identical; normalized reduced pairs then agree. The first successful degree equals the actual run count because the normalized numerator and denominator have no common factor and their maximum degree equals that count.

A zero endpoint makes exactly one reciprocal polynomial lose its top coefficient. The homogeneous common degree restores a factor T in the corresponding monic endpoint polynomial. Zero cannot occur on both entrance and exit lists because the endpoints of maximal separated runs are distinct. The correction is mathematically necessary and properly implemented.

On arbitrary input, solving a rational system alone does not establish recognition. Integer roots, distinct endpoints, strict interleaving, run budget, and the original moments must be checked. The artifact explicitly requires those checks.

The discrete two-run hostile has moment vectors (3,12,62,342) and (3,12,62,378), so four moments are sharp for k=2. The one-run statement is immediate. The continuous endpoint Jacobian is a signed Vandermonde matrix, so the first 2k−1 moments have a one-dimensional local level set in a strict endpoint chamber. This establishes the continuous lower bound but not an all-k integer lower bound. The latter is correctly left open.

**Independent controls:** all subsets of {−3,…,4} with at most k runs for k=1,2,3, yielding respectively 37, 163, and 247 full decodes; 192 zero-endpoint cases and 368 negative-endpoint cases across those universes. Exponential coefficients were computed by multiplying exponential-series factors rather than the producer's recurrence, and Padé coefficients were recovered from a full simultaneous numerator/denominator system. Signed Vandermonde determinants and truncated-row ranks were checked for k=1,…,6.

The exponential/Padé interval mechanism is classical. The finite-difference translation and precise ordered-mask application are sound, without implying graceful-label existence or a noise-stability theorem.
