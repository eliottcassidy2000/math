## 5. Signed components followed by neutral atoms: an exact structural theorem

This theorem is an explicit component-level extraction combining the inherited endpoint-interval and neutral-partition mechanisms. It is the signed-equality special case, not a new general Boolean-clause theorem or a new Davenport theorem.

**Theorem (binary signed-equality synchronization).** Let binary full-record slots have distinct, pairwise disjoint two-alternative reservations. Retain signed equality relations

    x_j = x_i xor epsilon_ij

on a finite graph, and additive guards in an abelian group G, including all required integer margin coordinates. Assume these are the only predicates on the authenticated product, or separately prove closure under the states below. Fix two accepted endpoints x,y.

Every connected relation component is either endpoint-fixed or differs at every one of its slots. Let E_1,...,E_m be the differing components, with actual costs

    s_j=|E_j|, c_j=sum_(i in E_j) literal_change_cost_i,

and let d_j in G be the complete additive increment of replacing that component's x tuple by its y tuple. Then:

1. The accepted endpoint interval consists exactly of unions J of these whole differing components with sum_(j in J)d_j=0.
2. Strict routes are exactly ordered partitions of{1,...,m} into nonempty zero-sum blocks.
3. An optimum strict route can be chosen so each event is an inclusion-minimal zero-sum block of components. Its literal cost is `(sum s_j,sum c_j)` over that event.

**Proof.** Along an edge, if one endpoint bit changes between x and y then its neighbor does too. This propagates over each component. Relative to x, a partial update continues to satisfy every edge exactly when the update indicator is constant on each component. Components outside the endpoint difference cannot change on a strict route. Every union of whole differing components satisfies all graph relations, regardless of cycles. Its guard changes by the displayed component sum, proving1. Strict binary routes have disjoint, endpoint-directed updates, giving2. Split any nonminimal zero-sum event into a proper nonempty zero-sum subset and its zero-sum remainder. Both intermediate arrivals are accepted by1, and neither slot nor clock bottleneck increases. Repeat to prove3. QED.

A connected graph's signed cycles must all have XOR sum0 for any assignment to exist. Once accepted endpoints are given, this consistency is automatic; cycles no longer control component size. The theorem identifies the correct synchronization unit before additive zero-sum decomposition.

### 5.1 Conditional size-independent bounds with the synchronization invoice retained

Suppose the differing components admit a supplied disjoint packetization P_1,...,P_l such that each packet is neutral for **every integer margin**. Let its physical support and clock cost be b_a,w_a, and its residual complete finite-group charge be q_a in Q. The total charge sum is zero.

Assume every union of these packets with charge zero reconstructs a compatible accepted full assembly. For pure signed-equality graphs this follows because packets are unions of whole relation components and every integer margin is already neutral. With extra relational factors this is an additional load-bearing closure hypothesis.

Partition the packet charge sequence into minimal zero-sum subsequences. Each has at most D(Q) terms. Executing these subsequences in any order is a strict route, with simultaneous per-event bounds

    slots <= sum of the largest min(D(Q),l) packet supports b_a,
    clocks <= sum of the largest min(D(Q),l) packet costs w_a.   (7)

Actual event invoices are the sums over the selected packet, so there is no unpaid boundary or reused port. The two upper envelopes in (7) are attained as bounds on the same constructed route, though neither is claimed sharp in every family. In particular, if b_a<=b then at most b D(Q) slots change per event. If every synchronized component is itself integer-margin-neutral, components may serve as packets directly. Otherwise one must exhibit a neutral grouping; bounded component size alone does not certify such a grouping.

For Q=C128 x C2^4, D(Q)=132. This value is inherited from Session49, not a new zero-sum theorem. A self-contained verification is: over F2 the augmentation ideal of F2[Q] is generated by t_0,...,t_4 with t_0^128=t_1^2=...=t_4^2=0, hence its132nd power vanishes. A zero-sumfree sequence of length132 would make product(1-X^q) zero while leaving identity coefficient1, a contradiction. The sequence of127 copies of the C128 basis generator and one copy of each of the four C2 generators is zero-sumfree of length131. Thus the constant is exactly132. The inherited theorem credits Olson's classical p-group formula; no new priority claim is made.

The classical number132 counts neutral **packets**, not physical slots, components, kernel phases, or spare resources. Relation synchronization may make one packet arbitrarily large. This is precisely why the charge bound cannot by itself prove all-size bounded-local native repair under arbitrary relational pins.

## 6. Treewidth one already allows unbounded indivisible changes

For n binary variables with only equalities x_1=x_2=...=x_n along a path, and a constant additive guard, the accepted family is{0^n,1^n}. Its relation graph has treewidth1 for n>=2. Its only genuine event changes n slots; with cost2 per slot it costs2n clocks. Thus neither acyclicity, bounded treewidth, nor zero charge prevents unbounded synchronization.

There is an equal-population variant on2m physical bits: impose inequality between each pair of neighbors on a path. The only assignments alternate0101... and1010.... Both have exactly m ones, identical constant charge, and differ on all2m slots. The graph still has treewidth1; every route between them requires the direct2m-slot event, costing4m at two clocks per slot. These are signed equalities and become pure equalities after a fixed alternating bit gauge, but that gauge changes the representation of the population margin. Do not erase that margin sidecar.

These two controls are abstract full-record data models. The all-size class-incidence-neutral paired-prefix carrier in Session49 can separately realize literal Boolean relations, but this packet does not import its guards into the order-eight numerical electrical bank, or claim a new all-order dyadic realization of these paths. The native evidence proved here remains the fixed82-slot carrier and one new relation.
