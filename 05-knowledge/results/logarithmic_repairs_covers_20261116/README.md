# Can a logarithmic repair close a dyadic ray?

1. [MRB tail and repair radius](MRB.md). Scaling the actual
   alternating remainder gives log H+o(1); it falls below the
   proved positive induced-growth tariff. More generally even a
   perfectly filled logarithmic neighborhood around each previous
   pressure-bank anchor cannot be dense, let alone cover all targets.
2. [27→53→105→209→417→833→1665 and the 27/63 contrast](RAY.md). A fixed
   one-apex run library cannot cover an infinite subset of any
   prime-mantissa ray 1+q·2^e. A factored 27 cannot embed into
   any 63, even with arbitrary extra vertices. Explicit changed
   cores extend the physical experiments beyond 105.
   [The two Collatz cylinders from 27 and 63](COALESCENCE.md)
   actually coalesce on the same affine progression.
3. [The 24-clock and 36-clock](CLOCKS.md). The six primes are
   exactly the support of the 24th Mersenne number. There are
   two exact cover shapes up to translation, with 48 coefficient
   classes per sign. The same wheel cannot cover both Brier signs.
   Cover phases reveal why no term of the 13 ray can use 63 as
   a multiplicative foundation, without choosing its exact witness.
4. [The 2024 square-sum graph](SQUARE.md). All edges of sum 1024
   connect its five pieces but provably cannot give a Hamilton
   path. Sum 529 gives a four-bridge repair and is minimal for
   that repair format. The missing partner at 1024 is −τ(4).
   The Golay and Leech/Q-series links are exact arithmetic, not
   claims that the graph is a quotient of those objects.

The common distinction is *existence of a legal contact* versus
*existence of a coordinated completion*. A factor on a finite
wheel, a connected quotient, or a small residual are not enough
to specify an exact tournament witness. Chan–Pak remains open.
