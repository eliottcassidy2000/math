# Prime-bank orbit counts from a twisted ideal-chain convolution

**Status: PROVED**, using the explicitly inherited canonical-module theorem;
**FINITE-EXACT** checks use an independent literal-ring signed-constraint
construction. The group is the specified Sylow-p bank action, not the full
normalizer and not full graph isomorphism. No literature-priority claim.

## 1. Source and target of the extension

The inherited mechanisms are:

- Session 110, `CHECKPOINT_01.md` §2: composite ramified Cayley banks have
  canonical ideal-coset modules and a unique prime outer quotient.
- Session 110, `CHECKPOINT_02.md` §1: the corresponding transitive-action
  composition is exact; it counts source-labelled banks, not graph types.
- Session 111, `BANK_RECURRENCE.md`: the actual shared-residue action
  `O_k=C_(p^(k-1)) x C_p` on `D_k=p^k-1` bank coordinates.
- This session, `counting/BANK_RANKING.md`: a small block-stabilizer transfer
  ranks unrestricted O_k bank orbits. It explicitly does not impose primeness.

The present extension counts **prime** O_k orbits at every depth without
scanning the 2^D_k bank states. Canonical decomposition is applied BEFORE
averaging, retaining the shared acting element on the quotient and kernel.
The kernel has one exceptional twist which cannot be discarded.

Fix p>=5, k>=2, and a simply ramified quadratic ring with
`delta^2=D`, `v_p(D)=1`. Write `R_m=O/(delta^m)` for all intermediate depths.
Use the Cayley clock `g=(1+delta)/(1-delta)`. The metallic clock in the cited
source generates the same principal norm-one group when its hypotheses hold.
Here p denotes the residue prime; D denotes the discriminant parameter, not
the bank dimension D_k.

The action on R_(2k) is generated by scalar multiplication by 1+p and
`alpha(delta)=(1+p^(k-1))delta`. Its effective bank action is O_k with
coordinates `(a,b)`, `a mod n=p^(k-1)`, `b mod p`. The scalar and automorphism
commute. All actions here have odd order, so no orientation-coordinate cycle
can force a sign contradiction. A fixed origin may be chosen to make the
whole bank action unsigned, but no unrecorded change of gauge is needed in
the literal signed-constraint checker.

### The ideal-module criterion in this slightly larger clock scope

The source proves that a composite invariant bank's canonical maximal
modules are cosets of a clock-invariant additive subgroup. Such a subgroup
is an O-ideal here as well. Indeed `delta=(g-1)/(g+1)`, and if the odd clock
order is q, the inverse of g+1 as an additive operator is

    (1-g+g^2-...+g^(q-1))/2.

Multiplication by 2 is invertible on the finite odd p-group. Therefore
clock invariance implies delta invariance. The ideals of the truncated
ramified discrete valuation ring are exactly its powers of delta. This
extends the stated canonical-module criterion to the Cayley clock used
here; it does not extend it to unrelated non-chain rings.

## 2. Scalar-fixed prime banks at every intermediate depth

Put h=(p-1)/2. For any integer a define

    c_m(a) = h sum_(l=1..m) gcd(a,p^floor((l-1)/2)),
    A_0(a)=1,   A_m(a)=2^(c_m(a)).                         (P1)

The convention is gcd(0,t)=t. Let P_m(a) count prime source-labelled banks
on R_m fixed by scalar multiplication by `(1+p)^a`. Then

    P_0(a)=0,
    P_m(a)=A_m(a)-sum_(r=1..m-1) P_r(a) A_(m-r)(a).      (P2)

In particular `P_1(a)=2^h`, independently of a.

**Proof of P1.** At valuation d, put l=m-d. There are h scalar-coordinate
cycles of length `p^(ceil(l/2)-1)=p^floor((l-1)/2)`. The scalar acts on each
by shift a, giving gcd(a,length) cycles. The valuation comparison between
an even scalar displacement and an odd clock displacement is the one used
in Session 111. An odd-order signed action has exactly 2^(number of cycles)
fixed binary assignments, proving P1.

**Proof of P2.** A nonprime bank has a unique canonical prime outer quotient
R_r and inner bank on `delta^r R_m`, canonically identified with R_(m-r)
by multiplication by delta^r. Scalar multiplication induces the SAME
scalar on both pieces. A composite bank is fixed iff both of these pieces
are fixed; the gluing has no section multiplicity. Sum over its unique
outer depth r. This proves P2. The multiplication of counts occurs for a
fixed action, not after averaging over scalar actions. QED.

## 3. The full shared-residue action has one twisted inner factor

For `(a,b) in O_k`, put

    c=p^(k-2),    a'=a+c*b mod n,
    C_k(a,b)=(p-1)sum_(j=0..k-2)gcd(a,p^j)
                  +h[gcd(a,n)+gcd(a',n)].

Its total fixed-bank count is `2^C_k(a,b)` by Session 111. Let F_k(a,b)
be its fixed **prime** bank count. Then the new closed formula is

    F_k(a,b) = 2^C_k(a,b)
       - P_1(a) A_(2k-1)(a')
       - sum_(r=2..2k-1) P_r(a) A_(2k-r)(a).              (P3)

Every term is an integer; it is nonnegative because it counts the exact
fixed prime set, not because of a numerical positivity assumption.

**Proof of the twist.** On every proper quotient R_r with r<=2k-1,
alpha is identity: `alpha(delta)-delta=p^(k-1)delta` has delta-valuation
2k-1. Thus the prime outer factor always sees scalar exponent a.

Identify an inner ideal `delta^r R_(2k)` with R_(2k-r). On its coordinate w,
the induced map is

    w -> (1+p)^a (1+p^(k-1))^(r*b) alpha^b(w).             (P4)

The alpha on w vanishes on this shorter ring. If r>=2, the extra scalar
also vanishes because its difference from1 has valuation at least 2k-2,
which is at least the inner ring's length 2k-r. For r=1, however, this
scalar survives on R_(2k-1). The binomial congruence

    (1+p)^(p^(k-2)) = 1+p^(k-1) mod p^k

turns its exponent into a'. This is the one exceptional inner factor in
P3. Applying the unique prime-outer decomposition exactly as in P2 proves
the result. QED.

The term r=1 is not optional bookkeeping. At p=5,k=2, the action `(a,b)=(0,1)`
fixes 65536 banks, all composite. Incorrectly replacing a' by a gives a
pseudo-count -65280 rather than zero. This is a sharp shared-action
hostile, not a reason to average the two factors independently.

## 4. Burnside and only k+2 aggregate terms

The exact number of prime O_k bank orbits is

    PrimeOrbits_(p,k) = (1/p^k) sum_(a mod n,b mod p) F_k(a,b).   (P5)

It can be computed using only k+2 action types:

1. For each v=0,...,k-3, take representative `(a,b)=(p^v,0)` with
   multiplicity `(p-1)p^(k-1-v)`.
2. Put c=p^(k-2). The remaining a are c*u. The pair `(u,u+b)` ranges
   freely over F_p^2. Its two zero/nonzero flags give four types,
   represented by `(a,a')` in `{0,c}^2`, with multiplicities respectively
   1, p-1, p-1, (p-1)^2.

For the first types, adding c*b does not change any relevant valuation,
so all quantities in P1-P3 are unchanged; these F equal P_(2k)(a).
For the last types all lower gcd values are maximal and the top ones
only record the displayed zero flags. Their multiplicities sum with the
first set to p^k. This proves the aggregation without assuming independent
shift residues at greater depth.

The convolution tables require polynomially many exact operations in k
and log p after valuation grouping. Integer lengths are O(D_k), since
all fixed counts are at most 2^D_k. Consequently this is polynomial time
in the explicit bank dimension D_k=p^k-1, including ordinary bit costs.
It is not polynomial in k alone; the integers being output can already
have Theta(p^k) bits. This result supplies a constrained COUNT, not a
rank/unrank algorithm for prime canonical representatives.

## 4b. Scalar valuation produces a short rational recurrence

Fix a nonzero integer a with `v=v_p(a)<infinity`, now allowing all depths m.
Put `c=2^(h*p^v)`. Formula P1 gives `A_m/A_(m-1)=c` for every m>=2v+1.
Hence, as formal power series,

    A(z)=sum_(m>=0)A_m z^m
        =sum_(m=0..2v-1)A_m z^m + A_(2v)z^(2v)/(1-cz),
    P(z)=sum_(m>=1)P_m z^m = 1-1/A(z).                    (P6)

For v>=1, define

    Q(z)=(1-cz)sum_(m=0..2v-1)A_m z^m + A_(2v)z^(2v).

It has degree exactly 2v: its highest coefficient is
`A_(2v)-c*A_(2v-1)`, which is nonzero because the last pre-tail ratio is
`2^(h*p^(v-1))<c`. Moreover `Q(1/c)=A_(2v)/c^(2v)>0`, so Q is coprime to
`Q-1+cz`. Therefore the scalar prime sequence has minimal eventual rational
recurrence order 2v. For v=0, `A(z)=1/(1-2^h z)` and `P(z)=2^h z`, so every
depth m>1 has zero scalar-fixed prime banks.

At v=1, writing `t=2^h` and `c=t^p`, the recurrence is particularly concrete:

    P_1=t, P_2=0,
    P_m=(c-t)P_(m-1)+t(c-t)P_(m-2),   m>=3.              (P7)

For p=5 this yields `P_5=17045913600`, exactly the independently inherited
uniformizer-depth-five scalar26 counterexample in Session110. Its next
term `P_6=17454749184000` is the new nontrivial fixed-prime row below.
Thus the old exception and the new all-depth count lie in one short exact
recurrence; they are not isolated failed small-depth extrapolations.

### An unbounded family of prime banks with extra scalar symmetry

Since t>1 and c>t, P7 gives `P_3=t^2(c-t)>0`, P_4>0, and then P_m>0 for
every m>=3. For every uniformizer depth m>=5 there is therefore a prime
bank fixed by scalar multiplication by `(1+p)^p`. This scalar is nonidentity
and has order

    p^(ceil(m/2)-2).

Indeed its difference from1 has p-adic valuation 2, and the scalar
characteristic of R_m is `p^ceil(m/2)`. It is not a clock power: a nontrivial
scalar displacement has even delta-valuation, whereas U1's clock
displacements have odd delta-valuation. This upgrades the old single
depth-five hostile to an all-depth family, with growing extra scalar order.

For even m=2k>=6, the inherited universal group H is contained in every
bank automorphism group. Its intersection with additive-affine maps is the
translation-clock group A by U6. Consequently its intersection with this
scalar subgroup is trivial. The scalar normalizes H, so the prime banks
just constructed have an automorphism subgroup of size

    |H| p^(k-2) = p^(4k+p-4).                            (P8)

This is a certified lower bound, not the full automorphism-group order or
an isomorphism classification. In particular, individual-bank automorphisms
can exceed the complete universal group by an unbounded factor as depth
grows. No contradiction arises: the universal group is the intersection
over all banks, not an upper bound on one chosen bank's group.

At p=5,k=3 the prime fixed-state histogram is instead

    100 actions: 0,
     16 actions: 17454749184000,
      8 actions: 19267255250108083754496000,
      1 action: 21267647932558502850733461135840768000.

The current unrestricted ranking transfer therefore does not become a
prime-ranking procedure merely by testing its final stabilizer state.
P3 does not prove that efficient prime ranking is impossible. A prime
prefix counter must additionally retain which ideal-cut equations remain
compatible with the prefix, or find another equivalent carrier. Whether
period-cap transfers accomplish that is a separate next step.

## 6. Independent verification and limits

Run `python3 prime_check.py` and `python3 -O prime_check.py`. The outputs
are byte-identical. The checker:

- builds the actual ring, clock orbits and antipodal orientation coordinates;
- derives each proper ideal-cut equation directly from constancy on its
  nonzero cosets, without using P1-P3;
- computes action-fixed and cut-fixed intersections using parity-aware
  disjoint sets, then inclusion-exclusion over EVERY proper cut;
- compares those independent prime fixed counts with P3 for EVERY group
  element in `(p,k,D)=(5,2,5),(5,2,10),(7,2,7),(5,3,5)`;
- compares the complete Burnside sums with the k+2-type aggregate;
- checks integrality and nonnegative action strata in 15 formula
  families, through k=8 for p=5, k=6 for p=7, and k=4 for p=13.

The prime criterion is exact only because all canonical modules in this
bank are ideal-coset modules and the ideals form the stated chain. In a
non-chain ring, the source's dual-number example requires joint embedding
and quotient information instead. The output counts O_k bank orbits,
not full normalizer classes, graph-isomorphism classes or new A000568 terms.
No complexity consequence is imported from the inspiration paper.
