# Concrete next steps

**All items below are OPEN, not dependencies silently assumed by a theorem.**

1. **Return a rooted arithmetic state.** Classify the physical image of
   (H,end,start,L,R) for one seam grammar. Find an operation that sends it
   back into the same controllable slice and has a target-decreasing inverse.
   Our explicit exponential surfaces do not establish this return property.
2. **Make interaction globally strong.** The four ten-vertex cells are strong,
   but their ordered sum is not strong beyond one cell. Derive a strong-chain
   interface rather than carrying the product law through an unsupported
   wrapper. Preserve enough rooted cover state to predict every new seam.
3. **Add prime kernels honestly.** Extend the response inventory from C3 to
   the prime five-vertex kernels and then a chosen larger prime alphabet.
   Use the actual port permutation action in Burnside; S3 symmetry of the
   C3 observable is not a universal quotient symmetry. A finite prime alphabet
   and its huge generated class count still need a scalar-coverage theorem.
4. **Close a chosen CA's state, or quantify its growth.** Length 2rt+2 block
   statistics suffice for horizon t, while pair statistics fail for Rule 110.
   For which rules and initial languages can a smaller horizon-independent
   interface be proved? Fixed finite rings supply no undecidability claim.
5. **Decode the regular tower word.** Formula (2) in dynamics/PROOF.md gives
   exact similarity invariants for every C3/Paley7 word. No collision was found
   through word length 14. Does the invariant recover the word in this grammar,
   or is there a first collision? This is not general tournament reconstruction.

For Chan–Pak the target remains actual integer realization, not residue
controllability, eventual affine formulas, exponentially many classes, or a
large finite sample. The sparse-closure bound makes that distinction quantitative.
