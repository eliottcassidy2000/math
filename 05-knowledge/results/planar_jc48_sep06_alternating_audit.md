## 2. Boundary anatomy and normalization

cycles of length `e`. The type `(3,1,1,1)` gives one ramified prime of
indices `(3,1)`. Actual count `a=3` exhausts every fixed label, so no
unramified prime is deleted. Pure boundary then gives exactly one prime

finite birational and hence `D^nu=S^nu=A1`; this does not make `D` normal.

At a node, the two actual fixed sets are complementary three-element
sets. Their three-cycle supports are disjoint. The two local normal
components are the smooth cyclic covers `(u,v)->(u^3,v)`, with exchanged
axes for the other component. They give two distinct deleted points.
At the cusp, the five-sheet orbit and singleton orbit give exactly one
deleted point and one retained point. The local orbit/point passage is
justified by finite normalization over a sufficiently small local target:
different points give disjoint normal local components of the complement
cover. There is also one deleted point at a smooth support point.

Consequently `D^nu->D` identifies no points, even over the nodes, and is
finite bijective. A finite bijection of complex algebraic curves is a
homeomorphism in their complex topology. Thus `chi_c(D)=1` and
`chi_c(Xbar)=chi_c(A2)+chi_c(D)=2`. The independent stratified calculation
