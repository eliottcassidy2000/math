# Root-preserving atomic ears and a count-decreasing inverse

**PROVED deduction from cited structure; FINITE-EXACT implementation controls.**
This is a grammar for actual strong tournaments, not an all-target existence
theorem. A root is retained physically; its numerical endpoint profile may
change. In particular 'strong entry' below does not mean unit readiness.

## The inherited mechanism

Write R_m for the nearly transitive tournament on m≥3 vertices: all arcs
point forward in its displayed order except last→first. Its count is
a_m=2^(m−2)+1. Busch's Lemma 1 says that a strong tournament which is not
nearly transitive is the union of two induced strong subtournaments meeting
in exactly one vertex. His path-merging lemma gives H(T)≥H(A)H(B) for such
a union. These are cited inputs, checked in the
[primary paper](https://www.combinatorics.org/ojs/index.php/eljc/article/download/v13i1n3/pdf/).
The exact equality/deficit refinement is inherited from
[THM-4745, exact gluings](../../../01-canon/theorems/THM-4745-exact-gluings-of-interval-chains-and-the-reduction-of-busch-equality-to-a-one-way-lemma.md).

## G1. One can peel a nearly transitive ear while keeping a specified root

**Theorem.** Let T be strong with at least three vertices and r a specified
vertex. Either T≅R_m, or
there are induced strong subtournaments A,B such that

    A≅R_m,  |B|≥3,  A∩B={v},  A∪B=T,  r∈B.

In particular the old root survives the peel, and

    H(B) ≤ H(T)/(2^(m−2)+1) ≤ H(T)/3.                 (G1)

**Proof.** Induct on the order. Take a Busch split C∪D with intersection
{w}, naming the sides so that r∈D. If C is nearly transitive, use A=C,
B=D. Otherwise apply the induction statement to C with protected root w.
It gives C=A∪C', with A nearly transitive, C' strong, w∈C', and A∩C'={v}.
The tournament B induced on C'∪D is strong because its two strong pieces
meet at w. Its intersection with A is just v: the only possible additional
intersection is w, and if w∈A then w=v. Also r∈B. The merge inequality
proves (G1). All subproblems in this argument have smaller order. QED.

This reassociation is the useful step: the parent need not remain in a
finite bank of dyadic families, and the shared vertex need not be r.
There is no assertion that two arbitrary protected vertices can both stay
in B, or that s,q,F remain unchanged during a peel.

## G2. The complete unbounded grammar

The objects are literal rooted strong graphs (T,r) of order at least three.
The instructions are:

1. Start with any R_m and any one of its vertices as r.
2. Choose an old vertex v, a rooted R_l with its root identified with v,
   and an arbitrary orientation for every pair between the new l−1 vertices
   and the old vertices other than v. Keep the old r.
3. Optionally apply a proved H-neutral preparation, with its changed marked
   profile and retained physical names stated explicitly.

Every attachment produces a strong tournament: the old core and the atom
are strong and intersect at v. Thus the next instruction has actual entry
at every step. G1 proves that instructions 1–2 alone reach every rooted
strong tournament. The atom family is itself generated by expanding the
middle vertex of C3 to a transitive block; its block length is unbounded.

An exact certificate records the parent graph, v, the atom order and root,
the entire cross-edge matrix, and the inherited root r. Restriction recovers
all those data. Full graph rows are sufficient for every subsequent endpoint
query; no fixed-dimensional observer for arbitrary cross matrices is claimed.
Ordinary SCC composition then gives all tournaments, with H multiplied
across components. The inherited prime/7p reduction is
[THM-4094](../../../01-canon/theorems/THM-4094-hamiltonian-matching-deficit-and-two-prime-lane-completeness.md).

Along every inverse chain H decreases by at least a factor three. For a
certificate with atom orders m_1,…,m_L,

    n=1+sum_i(m_i−1),      H(T)≥product_i(2^(m_i−2)+1).

Hence L≤floor(log_3 H(T)). The elementary atom inequality
2^(m−2)+1≥5^((m−1)/3) gives n≤1+3 log_5 H(T). For that inequality check
m=3,4,5 and advance by three: a_(m+3)=8a_m−7≥5a_m. This recovers the
coarse order bound by the same product mechanism as Busch's argument.

**The unresolved selector is explicit.** Given an integer N alone, select
an atom, a realizable smaller parent, its marked data and a cross matrix
whose *full* output is N. A count-decreasing parse of an already supplied
graph does not prove that every requested N has such a graph. This is the
same logical boundary as the inherited insertion/deficit correction.

## G3. Triangle-only ears have a sharper, different floor

Let L_3 start with any R_m and allow only R_3 ears thereafter. Its exact
minimum at order n is

    g(n)=3^((n−1)/2)              (n odd, n≥3),
    g(n)=5·3^((n−4)/2)           (n even, n≥4).       (G3)

Indeed a triangle ear multiplies the lower bound by three. Each starting
atom satisfies a_m≥g(m): check m=3,4 and use a_(m+2)=4a_m−3≥3a_m.
Equality is attained by the inherited interval chains of triangle blocks,
with one four-vertex block when n is even. Their exact product law is
[THM-4744, interval chains](../../../01-canon/theorems/THM-4744-interval-chains-attain-busch-and-are-the-extremal-strong-tournaments-through-order-twelve.md).

Thus L_3 cannot generate every strong graph. Already at order seven its
floor is 27; the unique strong count25 interval chain lies below it. This
is a class obstruction, not a count25 obstruction: two six-vertex classes
have count25 and belong to L_3. The [neutral entry](NEUTRAL.md) uses exactly
this opportunity to change cores at the same count.

The complete parser accepts 1,1,6,35,352 ordinary strong classes at orders
3,…,7, missing only that count25 class from the full head. The arbitrary-atom
rooted parser succeeds on all 2,618 rooted strong classes through seven,
with each parent count and retained root independently checked.

## The testable stronger hypothesis

**OPEN — triangle-ear value completeness.** Every odd N≥9 except21 has a
representative in L_3. This is stronger than the original spectrum claim
on this range; no equivalence in the reverse direction is asserted.
The present exact positive certificates give every such N≤2881. This does
not extend the known finite coverage range. One inherited root-class bank
and all 10,014,992 triangle cross matrices supplied the experiment.

The first value absent from this bounded output bank is 2883. It is not
claimed absent from L_3 or from tournaments at larger order. For negative
claims on a requested N, the parent bound H≤N/3 must be applied before
declaring the source search complete. The current parent bank is complete
for all strong counts below45, hence for triangle outputs below135.
