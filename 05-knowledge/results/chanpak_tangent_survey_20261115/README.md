## What to try next

The norm menus provide a target-indexed center, not arbitrary values of the two needed source coordinates (g=H(L)) and (Q=P_L(2)). A real inverse must take an integer candidate from the norm equation and **build the same source** with both coordinates. Pell multiplication alone cannot do this: in the 113 sheet an entire positive unit orbit meets the physical cone at most once.

The one-sided bank and mirror move provide exact literal switches, but the former has sparse digit support and mirroring it still leaves a common residue modulus (2g). The next constructive test is to find a second source family that tunes this residue without losing the forward continuation, and then check absolute interval overlap rather than separate local coverage.

The linked Python replays check literal counts, modular tables and inverse candidates. Finite tests verify the inputs to the all-order arguments; none is a count-range extension being offered as a proof of Chan–Pak.
