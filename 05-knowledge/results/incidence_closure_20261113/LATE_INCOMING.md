## Equal target, equal continuation, and literal composition

The incoming [readiness check](../session318_reservoir_descent_20261113/READINESS_CHECK.md)
proves that a particular all-k equal-H reservoir switch changes its two-slot
count by exactly 12-4*3^(k-2). Thus preserving H does not preserve even the
first continuation probe Q(2). Our full-Q pair is the next loss: preserving
all uniform-exterior slot counts still does not preserve native partners.
These give two distinct witnesses in a hierarchy of observation losses; no
claim is made that the two families are the same tournaments.

The [changing-reservoir construction](../session318_reservoir_descent_20261113/CHANGING_RESERVOIR.md)
keeps (h',s',E') fixed while changing an exposed-end coordinate, giving every
even gap and actual adjacent corridors with unbounded reservoir count. Its
whole specified chain-cut grammar nevertheless occupies at most 1/28 of the
odd targets asymptotically. Low-bit readiness conditions also constrain the
reversed end response: they cannot be used as independent selectors.

[Descent and glue](../session318_reservoir_descent_20261113/DESCENT_AND_GLUE.md)
puts the composition rule precisely. Literal moves A<-E->B and B<-F->C compose
through E x_B F. Projecting B to its Hamilton count gives the larger E x_H F,
which can contain false joins. A neutral repair must be supplied as an actual
witness, not inferred from equality of H or nonemptiness of its fibre. This
agrees with our observer-algebra closure: the retained intermediate state must
support the next operation. It also explains why all-order target selection
is a separate gate from fixed-order matrix closure.
