# Odd blue spectra and hollow-frame cube packets

Status: **PROVED**, for every odd order `n=2k+1>=3`. Exact independent
presentation checks cover all self-converse classes at `n=3,5,7,9`.
This is a theorem about tournament isomorphism classes and their blue degrees,
not an automorphism theorem for the actual antipodal blue multigraph.

## 1. The result and the missing mechanism

Let `SC_n` be the set of self-converse tournament isomorphism classes of
order `n`. Write `c3(T)` for the number of cyclic triples and `B(T)` for the
number of reflection-fixed fixed-Hamiltonian-path tilings in the class.
Equivalently, `B(T)` is its blue half-edge degree in the converse-merged
antipodal metagraph.

**Odd-order split-spectrum theorem.** For every odd `n>=3` and every integer `b`,

    #{T in SC_n : c3(T) even, B(T)=b}
      = #{T in SC_n : c3(T) odd, B(T)=b}.

There is a fixed-point-free class involution preserving `B` and reversing
`c3` parity. More strongly, the classes partition into **hollow-frame cube
packets**: every packet is an auxiliary cube `Q_r`, all its vertices have the
same `B`, and its two checkerboard shores are exactly the two `c3` parities.
Here `r>=1` and `r≡k (mod 2)`.

Consequently:

- Every blue-degree multiplicity is even at every odd `n>=3`.
- At `n≡1 (mod 4)`, `n>=5`, every blue-degree multiplicity is divisible by
  four, and its multiplicity on **each** triangle-parity shore is even.
- In polynomial form, `sum_[T in SC_n] (-1)^c3(T) z^B(T)=0`.

The missing coordinate was an anti-involution's **matching-completion
cube**, not blue bipartiteness. Bipartiteness alone does not equate degree
spectra; the construction below pairs actual tournament classes.

## 2. Anti-involutions lose no class information

Let `T` be self-converse and `G=Aut(T)`. The group `G` has odd order: an
automorphism of even order would have an involutory power, and a transposed
pair of that involution would have its arc reversed.

The automorphisms and anti-automorphisms form a group `G^±` of order `2|G|`.
A Sylow 2-subgroup has order two and is generated by an anti-involution
`rho`, since `G` has odd order. Any anti-involution has at most one fixed
vertex: two fixed vertices would have their mutual arc reversed. At odd
order it therefore has exactly one fixed vertex `f`, and `k` transposed pairs.

All anti-involutions are conjugate under `G`. Sylow conjugacy first gives
conjugacy under `G^±`; if a conjugator lies in the anti-coset, multiplying it
on the right by the source involution replaces it by an automorphism without
changing the conjugation.

Thus forgetting `rho` induces a **bijection on isomorphism classes**

    {(T,rho): rho an involutory anti-automorphism} / isomorphism
        -> SC_n.

The extra decoration is a useful carrier, but it creates no extra tournament
types. This step is essential: a transformation on decorated objects is not
automatically a transformation on the undecorated classes.

## 3. The hollow reflection frame

In each `rho` pair select the unique vertex `x_i` that beats `f`; put
`y_i=rho(x_i)`. Anti-symmetry forces

    x_i -> f -> y_i.

Delete the `k` matching arcs `{x_i,y_i}`, retaining the anti-involution and
all other arcs. Call the resulting object the **hollow reflection frame** `D`.
Its pair index set is `I={1,...,k}`.

A frame is encoded by two structures on `I`:

1. the tournament `A` induced on the `x_i`;
2. a simple undirected cross graph with
   `b_ij=1` iff `x_i -> y_j`, for `i!=j`.

The second relation is symmetric because anti-symmetry says
`x_i -> y_j` iff `x_j -> y_i`. The tournament on the `y_i` is the converse
of `A`. The arcs involving `f` are already fixed. Thus `(A,b)` is a complete
encoding of `D`, with no hidden cross-edge choices.

The frame itself recovers its markings: `f` is the unique vertex with no
missing adjacency, and the missing matching recovers `rho`. Thus the
markings are redundant. Let `H=Aut(D)=Aut(D,rho)`, equivalently the simultaneous automorphism group of
`(A,b)` on `I`. The central vertex and its in/out-neighborhoods are retained,
so a frame automorphism cannot interchange the two members of a pair.
Its action is a **pure permutation** of indices, not an affine bit flip.
Moreover it embeds faithfully in `Aut(A)`, because its action on the `x_i`
determines its action on the `y_i` and on `f`. Therefore

    |H| is odd.

Every `H`-orbit `O_1,...,O_r` on `I` has odd size. In particular `r>=1` and
`r≡k (mod2)`.

### The exact completion fibre

A completion is a vector `a in {0,1}^I`, with

    a_i=1 iff x_i -> y_i.

All `2^k` completions remain anti-symmetric under `rho`. Their decorated
isomorphism classes are exactly `{0,1}^I/H`. By the previous section this
is also precisely the set of **unmarked self-converse tournament classes
with this hollow-frame type**. An isomorphism between two completions that
does not initially respect `rho` can be composed with an automorphism of
the target so that it does; it then belongs to the hollow-frame group.
There are no additional identifications after forgetting the marking.

## 4. The blue degree is a centralizer quotient

Let `P_rho(T)` be the set of directed Hamiltonian vertex words

    P=(v_1,...,v_n),    rho(v_i)=v_(n+1-i).

These paths have center `f`. **No such path uses a matching arc**
`{x_i,y_i}`. If paired vertices were adjacent at positions `j,j+1`, their
positions would also sum to `n+1=2k+2`, but `2j+1` is odd. Thus the whole
set `P_rho(T)` depends only on `D`, not on its completion vector. Write
`h(D)=|P_rho(T)|`.

For completeness, `h(D)` is positive and odd. On all Hamiltonian paths,
`P -> reverse(rho(P))` is an involution. Rédei's oddness theorem for the
total Hamiltonian-path count therefore makes its fixed count `h(D)` odd.
This is THM-647's involutory-twist proof; it is not a new proof of Rédei.

**Centralizer fibre formula.** For every completion `T_a`,

    B(T_a) = h(D) / |C_Aut(T_a)(rho)| = h(D) / |H_a|,           (4.1)

where `H_a` is the stabilizer of `a` in `H`.

To prove the first equality, blue tilings are the `Aut(T_a)`-orbits of
Hamiltonian paths whose positional reversal is an anti-automorphism. Each
such positional reversal is an anti-involution, hence conjugate to `rho`.
Every blue path orbit therefore meets `P_rho(T_a)`. If two paths in
`P_rho(T_a)` differ by an automorphism, that automorphism conjugates their
positional reversal `rho` to itself, so it centralizes `rho`. Thus blue
path orbits correspond exactly to centralizer orbits in `P_rho(T_a)`.
The action is free because an automorphism fixing an ordered Hamiltonian
word fixes every vertex. Finally the centralizer is exactly `H_a`: erase
the matching arcs to obtain a frame automorphism, and retain them precisely
when the matching vector is fixed.

The denominator is important. In general `B(T)` is **not** `h(D)`, and the
full `|Aut(T)|` is not the denominator for this single-involution path set.
For `C3 -> 1 -> C3`, one has `B=1`, `h=3`, centralizer order `3`, and full
automorphism order `9`.

There is a useful stronger form. The full frame group `H` acts freely on
the unchanged path set `P_rho(D)`. Hence `b_0(D)=h(D)/|H|` is an odd
positive integer, and

    B(T_a) = b_0(D) |H·a|.                                  (4.2)

The blue degree in a hollow stratum is a fixed odd factor times the orbit
size of its matching-completion vector.

Within one hollow stratum the total blue mass is `b_0(D) 2^k`, and its
blue mass on each triangle-parity shore is `b_0(D) 2^(k-1)`. This follows
by summing orbit sizes in (4.2), or directly from the toggle pairing.


## 5. Triangle parity is diagonal parity

The action of `rho` pairs the vertex triples except for the `k` triples
`{x_i,f,y_i}`. Converse preserves cyclicness, so every nonfixed pair of
triples contributes an even number of cyclic triples. The fixed triple is
cyclic exactly when `a_i=0`. Consequently

    c3(T_a) ≡ k - sum_i a_i  (mod 2).                        (5.1)

An optional integer refinement is also exact. Let `d_i` be the outdegree
of `x_i` in the hollow frame, excluding its missing matching arc. No triple
contains two matching edges, so `c3` is affine on the matching cube:

    c3(T_a) = c3(T_0) + sum_i (2k-1-2d_i) a_i.              (5.2)

Indeed reversing `y_i -> x_i` changes the pair's scores from
`(d_i,2k-d_i)` to `(d_i+1,2k-d_i-1)`, and
`c3=binom(n,3)-sum_v binom(d^+(v),2)`. The coefficient is odd and is
constant on each `H`-orbit. Formula (5.1) is the parity shadow, with the
baseline parity `c3(T_0)≡k`.

## 6. Odd-orbit toggles and the cube packets

For an `H`-orbit `O_j` on `I`, define the **odd-orbit toggle**

    tau_j(a) = a XOR 1_(O_j).

Because `O_j` is `H`-invariant, this map commutes with `H` and

    H_(tau_j(a)) = H_a.

Equations (4.1) and (5.1) show that it preserves `B`, `h`, and the
centralizer order, while reversing `c3` parity because `|O_j|` is odd.
The hollow frame does not change.

All `r` toggles commute. They act **freely** on `{0,1}^I/H`, not merely on
the labelled completion cube. The reason is the orbit-parity vector

    p(a) = (sum_(i in O_j) a_i mod2)_(j=1,...,r).

It is constant on every `H`-orbit, hence defined on the quotient. Toggling
`O_j` changes precisely its `j`th coordinate. No nonempty combination of
toggles can fix a quotient class. Therefore every toggle orbit has exactly
`2^r` tournament classes and is a cube `Q_r`. All its vertices have equal
`B`, and precisely `2^(r-1)` lie on each triangle-parity shore. This proves
the theorem and the stated divisibilities.

### Intrinsic cubes versus a chosen single involution

The collection of all odd-orbit toggles is intrinsic to the hollow-frame
type, up to permuting the cube coordinates. To specify a **single** global
class involution, select one pair orbit equivariantly from each hollow-frame
type. One explicit convention is to compute a canonical rooted-frame code
for each pair and select the orbit with least code. Two roots have the
same code exactly when they lie in the same `H`-orbit, so this selects one
whole odd orbit and is independent of labels. It depends only on `D`, not
on the matching completion.

Changing the chosen anti-involution of `T` conjugates all of this structure
by an automorphism of `T`; consequently the target tournament class does
not change. Reapplying the construction may use the same `rho`, has the
same hollow frame and selected orbit, and returns `T`. Parity reversal
makes the class involution fixed-point-free.

A least-code convention supplies an actual well-defined involution; no
claim of a unique convention-free preferred matching is needed. The full
cube-packet structure needs no choice of one distinguished coordinate.

## 7. Exact checks and hostile controls

`verify_odd_pairing.py` uses two different presentations and imports no
producer/classifier module:

1. Enumerate all `k^2`-bit normalized frames **with** their matching bits,
   then quotient by the `k!` simultaneous pair permutations.
2. Independently enumerate every reflection-fixed fixed-path tiling, encode
   its center-polarized frame, and count its class directly.

For every class it checks the centralizer formula against the direct blue
count, the selected-orbit involution, the entire free toggle cube,
constant blue degree/centralizer/symmetric-path count, and balanced shores.
It also matches every SC class in the inherited full census at `n=3,5,7`.

| n | all SC classes | blue tilings | auxiliary cube packets |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| 3 | 2 | 2 | one Q1 |
| 5 | 8 | 16 | two Q2 |
| 7 | 88 | 512 | four Q1 and ten Q3 |
| 9 | 2752 | 65536 | sixteen Q2 and 168 Q4 |

The common order-nine shore spectrum is

    {1:12,3:14,5:26,7:36,9:50,11:26,13:42,15:66,17:56,
     19:120,21:60,23:120,25:120,27:88,29:136,31:136,
     33:138,35:64,37:48,39:18}.

This is a complete SC blue-spectrum census, not a census of all order-nine
tournaments or all order-nine antipodal edges. It enumerates two cubes of
size `2^16`, not the full fixed-path cube of size `2^28`.

### First failed candidates

`probe_switches.py` records two useful near misses against the inherited
full census:

- Switching every arc at the fixed vertex is a class involution but does
  not preserve `B`: it takes the transitive order-five class from `B=1` to
  `B=3`. At order seven it takes `B=1` to `B=7`.
- Flipping all matching arcs preserves `B` uniformly, but changes `c3`
  parity only when `k` is odd. It does not solve `n≡1 mod4` by itself.

The corrected move is to toggle an **odd automorphism orbit**, not to
assume that every canonical large switch has odd support.

### The odd-only 3-power cap also fails

The fresh order-nine census has maximum `B=39`, not at most
`3^floor((9-2)/2)=27`. The separate independent audit validates a single
witness by all `9!` vertex permutations, without relying on the census.
Its adjacency row masks are

    [356,273,274,263,169,206,158,15,240],

where bit `j` in row `i` means `i -> j`. The exact values are
`(H,|Aut|,mass,B,c3)=(2659,1,2659,39,28)`. Thus restricting the historical
3-power conjecture to odd orders does not repair it. Its corrected
all-order extremal replacement remains open. The witness and independent
full-permutation checker are in `audit_independent/independent_n9_cap_witness.*`.

### Not a symmetry of the actual antipodal graph

At order seven, the source `C3 -> 1 -> C3` is inherited census node `271`
and the all-three-matching-arcs toggle lands at node `267`:

    source: B=1, c3=2, H=9,   |Aut|=9, mass=1,  pure blue;
    target: B=1, c3=11,H=135, |Aut|=3, mass=45, mixed.

The source's actual antipodal blue neighbor is node `21`, with `B=5`, not
node `267`. Thus these auxiliary cubes do **not** in general preserve actual
blue adjacency, purity, total fibre mass, total Hamiltonian-path count, full
automorphism order, score sequence, or the integer value of `c3`.

The theorem is stronger than a numerical equality but weaker than an
edge-respecting symmetry of the existing metagraph. It supplies a new
class-level coordinate system explaining the blue-degree histograms.

## 8. Reproduction, dependencies, and boundaries

From the workspace containing the sibling census directory:

    python3 session14-odd-blue-pairing/verify_odd_pairing.py
    python3 session14-odd-blue-pairing/probe_switches.py
    python3 session14-odd-blue-pairing/build_n5_squares.py

The main verifier also runs standalone from its own directory; the inherited
cross-check is optional and only runs when its file path exists. Core output
is `verification.json`, with every class and partner in `pairing_n*.json`.
After publication, supply the sibling full census explicitly with
`--inherited ../merged_metagraph_black_quotient_bridges_20261020` to the
main verifier, and `--census` with the same directory to the hostile probe.
The square-data extractor requires the main verifier's inherited cross-check.

The uniform proof uses elementary finite-group conjugacy, odd tournament
automorphism groups, the exact Hamiltonian-path orbit interpretation, and
Rédei for positivity/oddness of `h`. The equality mechanism and the cube
freeness are proved, not inferred from the finite checks. It does not use
the pure-blue atom classification or assume that pure-blue means rigid.

The one-vertex boundary is excluded: its lone class has even `c3` and
`B=1`, so no opposite-parity partner exists. The even-order extension is
false in general and the proof loses its fixed center, canonical pair
polarity, and matching-edge avoidance there. Neither a new LRC statement
nor a formula for the entire antipodal incidence graph is claimed.
