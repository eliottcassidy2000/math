# Heisenberg word orbits and prescribed-target core choice

This session asks for a physical Heisenberg action and for an exact host/second-core inverse in the [two-core anchor construction](../chanpak_quadratic_anchor_atlas_20261115/README.md). It achieves both in specified senses, but does not prove universal placement or Chan–Pak.

1. **[Weighted Heisenberg transfers](GAP_HEISENBERG.md).** One actual binary spine gives a triangular polynomial matrix under concatenation. The unweighted triangle count fills the full positive integer Heisenberg area interval. A modular quotient contains two independent Heisenberg factors at many moduli. The same unweighted area can hide exponentially different absolute path and cover counts.
2. **[An independent adjacency actuator](CLOCK_LIFTS.md).** For every modulus m coprime to 6, strong physical one-apex cores realize every joint residue pair (h,Q). A positive word acts as the identity at all three weighted evaluations but adds one adjacent pair. Combine it with a commutator that moves Q by a unit. The clocks can stay fixed modulo m, but they do not stay fixed as integers. At 3, the all-tournament identity Q≡(−1)^order h is a genuine physical parity guard for the inverse.
3. **[The cut-state Heisenberg quotient](CUT_HEISENBERG.md).** Suffixes 0 and 10 generate the full unit-slope Heisenberg group modulo every m coprime to 6, and are transitive on the cut-state grid modulo every odd m. The group loses a horizontal direction at 3, even allowing arbitrary ordinal blocks with unit count. No exact central step keeps the absolute start weight in the transitive suffix grammar: that coordinate encodes the whole word.
4. **[Choosing a host from the absolute target](ANCHOR_DECODER.md).** Two transitive cores and any binary-cut host admit a complete O(sqrt(N))-candidate inverse. In the gap-separated subfamily, the target's high digits directly choose the literal host via bit reversal. The low digits must still equal a specific checksum. When the middle core is arbitrary but fixed, its true h and Q change the step and shift of the admissible high quotients.
5. **[A growing reservoir of exact checksums](QUOTIENT_RESERVOIR.md).** Combine two equal-Q core dictionaries: W has one fixed Q but h=5^i·19^j. At each of infinitely many prescribed high quotients, paired host/core choices give (2e+1)(e+1) distinct low checksums, with a direct decoding of the actual host and core from the low digits. The checksums are distinct, not consecutive. The anchor decoder also supplies exact +32 and −30 locks on two other window families; they cannot yet be combined into a step of 2.
6. **[An exact equal-h switch](PHYSICAL_PROFILE_SWITCH.md).** Two strong order-eight cores both have h=61 but have Q=1744 and 1864. This gives actual choices inside the bounded progression at fixed h, including an exact range of Q valuations at 2 and a coset at powers of 5 with unchanged integer h. It also has a literal factor inverse for this sparse set of profiles.
7. **[Why the obvious composition still falls short](HOST_CAPACITY.md).** Independent ratios in a fixed anchor bank do not fill the odds when both cores are ordinal sums of one-apex blocks. Even freeing the binary host misses at least 2% of odd targets in that alphabet if the middle core is cyclic, and at least 73% if both cores are cyclic. The proof uses a physical identification: the host's trailing ones can be absorbed into the middle core without changing the tournament.

The actionable distinction is between modular correction, fixing the high quotient, and repopulating the exact low checksum at that quotient. The two former can now be done with real words. The last would need to exploit the transitive middle-core lane, richer non-ordinal components, or a stronger-changing host. The equal-h switch is a small test case for that third task, not a proof that the resulting progression contains a core at every target.
