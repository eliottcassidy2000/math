# Removing most exceptional moduli and using adjacency as a 5-adic actuator

These extensions use the exact polynomial transfer of [GAP_HEISENBERG.md](GAP_HEISENBERG.md). The integer tournament word itself is never divided or inverted.

## Stronger result: every modulus coprime to 6

Let m>1 be coprime to 6 and set κ=φ(m). For t=2,3/2,1/2, both t and t−1 are units, so t^κ=1 and 1+t+...+t^(κ−1)=0 in Z/m. Therefore the **positive** word F=A^κ B^κ has identity transfer simultaneously at all three evaluation points. It starts A, ends B, and contributes exactly one adjacent AB. Appending F to any word ending B is consequently a pure h translation by +1 modulo m: it leaves Q and every diagonal clock unchanged. This is the extra actuator hidden by the earlier fixed-counter calculations below.

Now take L=1+κ, X=AA B^(L−2), Y=A B^(L−1). The same polynomial filters isolate the two horizontal eigenvalues 2 and 1/2 of the t=2 action, since each differs by a unit from all other eigenvalues **except** from its reciprocal partner channel t=1/2, where it is intentionally retained. In fact their differences from each other and from 3/2,2/3 have prime factors only 2 or 3. There is no need to distinguish 3/2 from 2/3 modulo m. The commutator Z of the two filtered lifts is central in all three projections, with unit entry at t=2 and unit entry at t=1/2 (the same filters pull out the reversed horizontal pair with unit entries there). It is zero at t=3/2. It preserves adjacency modulo m. Thus in the (h,Q) readout, Z changes Q by a unit and might also change h; F independently translates h by 1. Solve first for Q then for h, starting from X, and realize Z by positive representatives in the finite matrix group with the adjacency counter included. This gives **all** joint residues modulo every m coprime to 6, including mixed moduli divisible by 5, via strong physical tournaments. Both actuator words keep all diagonal clocks; only Z keeps the adjacency counter.

No theorem here turns F into an exact integer +1 move: geometric sums vanish only modulo m, and the unknown absolute error can grow with κ. This supplies a particularly sharp local-versus-absolute control.

## What can still be controlled with the adjacency counter frozen

**All moduli coprime to 30.** Given such an m>1, choose L=1+φ(m), X=AA B^(L−2), Y=A B^(L−1). These equal-length blocks each start A, end B, and have one adjacent AB. They differ only at position 1. The matrix V=XY^(-1) has horizontal entries (t^(-2),−t) at each t as before, and clock t^L=t modulo m by Euler. Thus the four filtered eigenvalues are now 2,1/2,3/2,2/3. All pairwise differences are units modulo m: the only prime factors in their numerators or denominators are 2,3,5. The same Lagrange filters and central commutators give the full two-factor Heisenberg projection and the same joint (h,Q) coverage as in the length-three proof. Block lengths may depend on m. No quantitative upper bound on absolute target size follows.

**All powers of 5.** Here take L=5 permanently: X=AABBB and Y=ABBBB. At modulus m=5^k the two eigenvalues from t=2 are distinct from one another and from both t=3/2 eigenvalues modulo 5; the latter two reduce to −1. Isolate each t=2 eigenvalue by a polynomial vanishing at all other eigenvalues as before. The denominator only compares the selected eigenvalue with each other one, and is a unit. We do not need to separate the two eigenvalues that reduce to −1. The commutator Z of the two resulting lifts has unit center at t=2, is central at all three evaluation points, and has zero counter. This gives an independent h control but not yet a second central control for Q.

Instead use D=X^4. Writing the matrices as [[1,a,c],[0,d,b],[0,0,1]], direct multiplication for t=2,3/2,1/2 respectively gives modulo 5:
```
D = (I+E13, I, I+E13; counter +4).
```
This includes the clock (d=1 at every channel). On right-multiplying a state by D modulo 5, h changes by 1+4=0, while Q changes by the unit 2^(n−1). Thus X alone supplies a second direction if we allow the adjacent-pair counter to move. It is a different actuator than the central commutator.

For each j≥1, D^(5^j) reduces at precision 5^(j+1) to identity plus 5^j times its three displayed central entries, and adds 4·5^j to the counter. To see this, write a component as I+A with A^2=0 mod 5. The binomial theorem gives (I+A)^5=I+5A mod 25 (in particular A^5=0 mod 25). Induct on j using the binomial expansion of a matrix already congruent to I mod 5^j. Also Z^(5^j) is precisely identity plus its scaled center, with zero counter. For j=0 this description is just the reduction modulo 5. At each step both moves fix the current precision, and their effect on the next (h,Q) digits is triangular and invertible: Z has unit h entry and D zero h entry and unit Q entry. The clock2=2^n is unchanged at that precision. Starting with X, lift to any joint residue pair modulo 5^k using these moves. Z has a positive block representative by finite group inversion; D is already positive. Every result comes from a strong one-apex tournament.

This gives another, digit-by-digit, construction at powers of 5. The first section above handles moduli mixing 5 with other primes different from 2 and 3. There is a genuine reason not to assume the same proof works at 3: for **every** tournament W of order n, Q(W)≡(−1)^n h(W) mod 3. In the odd-cycle packing expansion for Q, a packing of k cycles on s vertices contributes 2^(n−s+2k), which is (−1)^(n+k) mod 3 since s≡k mod 2, while its h contribution is 2^k≡(−1)^k. For the absolute profile equation N=L(Q−h)+Mh, the physical order parity therefore forces N≡(M+Lε)h mod 3 with ε=0 at even order and ε=1 at odd order. In particular a lane with 3|M+Lε is impossible for a target not divisible by 3. This is an exact early reject test for the bounded progression, not a guarantee when it passes.
