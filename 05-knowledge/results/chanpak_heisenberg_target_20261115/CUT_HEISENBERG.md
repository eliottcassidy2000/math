# Physical cut semigroup: exact odd-modulus Heisenberg law and its carry obstruction

**Scope.** All-order statements for the ordinal painted-block transfer of
[the four-coordinate cut state](../chanpak_selective_target_20261114/CUT_STATE.md).
The group lives in the reduced permutation image, not in the exact positive
extension monoid. The obstruction at 3 extends to arbitrary marked ordinal
blocks with invertible spine count; it need not survive actual recontacting
of previously inserted vertices.

## 1. Two physical words and a central witness

Let the signed start bias of the current marked spine vanish. Write
(h) for its path count, (S) for its total marked start weight, and
(N) for the path count after adding the apex. Put (x=S/h) and
(y=(N-S)/h), in the rationals or modulo an odd integer (m) with (h)
a unit. Paint a vertex 1 if the apex beats it, otherwise 0. The suffix
words 0 and 10, applied left to right, induce
```
A(x,y)=(2x,y),      B(x,y)=(4x+2,y−x).
```
Indeed 1 doubles (S) and adds (h), sets its bias to (h), and leaves
(N) fixed. A following 0 doubles (S), adds former (S+h) to (N), and
resets bias. Acting modulo odd (m), (A,B) are permutations of a finite
set, so each inverse is a positive power of the same physical word. All
subsequent group expressions modulo (m) therefore have positive-word
representatives satisfying the bias condition.

With composition rightmost first, set
```
C = A^(-2) B,
C_i = A^i C A^(-i) = (x+2^(i−1), y−2^(−i)x).
```
Unit slope maps (U(a,b,c):(x,y)\mapsto(x+a,y+b x+c)) satisfy
```
U(a,b,c) U(a',b',c') = U(a+a', b+b', c+c'+b a').
```
This is the ordinary Heisenberg group law. The two horizontal parameter
pairs of (C_0,C_1) have determinant (3/4); their commutator
([C_1,C_0]) translates (y) by (3/4). The noncommutative relation
```
C_{−1}² C_0^(−5) C_1² = U(0,0,7/2)
```
gives a second central translation, essential at 3. To check it, the
horizontal entries sum to zero (coefficients (2,-5,2)). The three
power constants sum to (-17/2) since (ab=-1/2) for each (C_i). The
composition cross terms, accumulating from right to left, are (10,2).
Consequently subtracting four copies of the commutator gives a pure
translation by (1/2), a unit modulo every odd (m).

**Orbit consequence.** Modulo every odd (m), the reduced physical action
is transitive on the full (m\times m) grid: set (x) with a power of
(C_0), then adjust (y) with the pure center. This is simultaneously
an orbit theorem for ((S,N)) at every fixed invertible spine (h).
It gives an explicit modular inverse construction, not just successful
sampling of scalar residues.

## 2. Exact group size and the exceptional prime

Every positive transitive suffix ending in 0 is a concatenation of
(1^k0), (k\ge0). For this block the map has
```
x' = 2^(k+1)x + 2^(k+1)−2,
y' = y − (2^k−1)x + c_k,
c_0=0,     c_k=2−2^k (k≥1).
```
For (k=1) this is (B). The constant follows from the last 0 using
(S/h=2^kx+2^k−1) and bias 1 for (k\ge1). On dividing the slope
(2^{k+1}) out by composing with (A^{-(k+1)}) at the end,
the horizontal parameters satisfy (b\equiv a\pmod3). This also follows
from the arbitrary-block result in §3.

For (<A,B>) modulo (m), the kernel of the slope map
is generated by the (C_i): move powers of (A) right and cancel their
net slope. Its horizontal image is already spanned by (C_0,C_1). If
(3\nmid m), their determinant is a unit. If (3\mid m), both are
on (b=a) modulo 3, and the displayed (2\times2) matrix has a
unit entry and determinant (3/4), hence its image has index exactly
three. Every (C_i) meets that congruence. The center is unrestricted
by §1. So the kernel is
```
all U(a,b,c),                              3∤m,
all U(a,b,c) with b≡a mod 3,                3|m.
```
The actual group is this kernel semidirect the slope group
(<2>), of size (m^3\operatorname{ord}_m(2)/\gcd(3,m)).
Adding all terminal-zero transitive words adds no elements, by the
normalized horizontal condition above. In particular for (m=3) the
group has order 18 and not even a subgroup of order 27, despite being
transitive on nine states. For (m\) coprime to 6, the unit-slope subgroup
is the full Heisenberg group over (Z/m). The distinction is
akin to [the repository's physical odometer / Heisenberg-shadow comparison](../../../01-canon/theorems/THM-2788-physical-modular-odometer-versus-heisenberg-bockstein-extension.md),
but here both the exact reduced group and its obstruction follow from
the tournament path transfer.

## 3. Why arbitrary marked ordinal blocks cannot repair the mod-3 defect

Let (R) have order (r), ordinary path count (a), total two-slot
cover count (q), start weights (s) from the marked vertices (V),
and end weights (t) into the unmarked complement. An endpoint weight
means the sum over nonempty paths with the specified endpoint of the
ordinary Hamilton counts of their complements. Write (eta(V)) for
the end weight into (V) itself. Then (t+eta(V)=q-a). The cut
transfer, when the initial bias is zero, has
```
x' = (q/a)x + s/a,
y' = y − (η(V)/a)x + c.
```
The constant is immaterial; if the new bias is not zero, append a final
0, which multiplies the two coefficients of (x') by 2, preserves the
coefficient of (x) in (y'), and only changes the constant of (y').
The resulting maps are terminal-zero.

We prove the two congruences
```
q ≡ (−1)^r a mod 3,       s ≡ (−1)^(r−1) η(V) mod 3.
```
Work in the squarefree ring on commuting (X_v), with (X_v²=0), let
(X) be the diagonal variable matrix and (D) the adjacency matrix of R.
Let (H(X)=1+\mathbf{1}^T(I-XD)^{-1}X\mathbf{1}), and let (E_v(X))
and (P_v(X)) be the corresponding path polynomials for paths ending
and starting at (v). Thus
(E_v=\mathbf{1}^T(I-XD)^{-1}X e_v); (P_v) is the same end polynomial
for the converse. From (D^T=J-I-D), the inverse for the converse
takes the form (I+XD+X-XJ). The diagonal (+X) term can be dropped
when multiplying the inverse by (X) on the right: every expansion
term using it is a walk with a repeated vertex at that immediate
diagonal step. Now write (M=I+XD). The rank-one geometric sum
for ((M-X\mathbf1\mathbf1^T)^{-1}X) gives
```
P_v(X) = −E_v(−X)/H(−X).
```
Summing over (v) and using converse invariance of the unmarked
path polynomial gives (H(X)H(-X)=1). Modulo 3, the Frobenius map
gives (H(X)^3=1) because all monomials besides 1 are nilpotent of
square zero. Thus (H(X)^2=H(-X)), proving the first congruence by
taking the full monomial coefficient. For the second, multiply
the formula for (P_v) by (H(X)) to get
(H(X)P_v(X)=-H(-X)E_v(-X)\pmod3); take the same coefficient and sum
over (v\in V).

For an append with normalized map (x'=d x+u, y'=y+b x+c),
unit (a\pmod3) implies (d=\pm1\pmod3) and
(u/d\equiv b\pmod3). These relations are independent of a
possible final 0. They are preserved under composition, since
(d^{-1}=d) modulo 3. Consequently every slope-one map in the reduced
mod-3 ordinal semigroup obeys the horizontal restriction (b=a).
Here the last (a) is the first Heisenberg coordinate, not (h(R)).
Genuine recontacts or a new set of carried coordinates are needed
to get full horizontal rank at 3 within this mechanism.

## 4. Why the orbit cannot by itself place an absolute count

For a transitive painted word (w) of length (L), the exact
normalized start weight is (2^L x + bin(w)), with
the first appended bit most significant. If (x\ge1), the half-open
ranges ([2^L x,2^L(x+1))) are disjoint for different (L);
within each (L) binary coding is injective. No two exact positive
words from such a source end at the same (x), even if lengths are
allowed to differ. In particular there is no nonidentity exact
central step preserving absolute (x) in this grammar. Negative
formal words yield (C_i) over the dyadics, but their positive
representatives only exist after reduction, where wrap is possible.
More generally a nonempty appended ordinal block has (q/a\ge r+1\ge2),
by splitting each full path at all its cuts, so a positive composition
of such normalized block matrices has slope strictly above 1. This
does not rule out an exact relative switch between two different
nontransitive blocks of equal slope, retention-changing moves, or
choosing a host from an absolute target.
