# Isolated-column Walsh switches for the physical two-address bank

Companion to the signed-table/mod-24 proof (TABLES.md).

## Arbitrary fixed dyadic precision by isolating pairs of active columns

Write the digits of an address $n$ as $b_i$, with the least significant digit at $i=0$. Expanding the recurrence gives the exact finite sum
$$
 p(n)=\sum_{i<j}\bigl(2^{j-i-1}+[j=i+1]\bigr)(1-b_i)b_j.
$$
The first term counts the prefix $n$ above each zero bit; the guard adds the extra copy of the adjacent product. For a pair of addresses with a fixed per-column sum (0, 1, or 2), call sum-1 columns active. In the sum $p(x)+p(y)$, an interaction between two active columns is the displayed weight times the xor of the two $x$-bits. An interaction involving a fixed 00 or 11 column is independent of the choice in any active column. This is exact and records the sidecar that the ordinary sum alone loses.

Conversely, for a fixed finite length, the ordinary sum and the sum of the reversed addresses **determine the per-column sums**. If two column-sum profiles agree on these two readouts, the difference polynomial $P(z)$ has coefficients in $\{-2,-1,0,1,2\}$ and roots $2,1/2$. If nonzero of degree $d$, the root $1/2$ forces the leading coefficient even by multiplying by $2^d$ and reducing modulo 2. The leading term at $2$ then has magnitude $2^{d+1}$, impossible to cancel with lower terms of total magnitude at most $2^{d+1}-2$. Thus the independent actions in this sector really occur in the active columns, not through hidden column carries.

Fix $s\ge2$. In a suffix of $B=O(s^2)$ bits, pin bit 0 to $x_0=1,y_0=0$. Put one pair of active columns at distance $\ell+1$ for each $\ell=1,\ldots,s-1$, all above bit $s$. Separate the different pairs, the high prefix, and bit 0 by distances at least $s+1$. All other low columns are fixed 00. In each pair pin one $x$-bit, and let the other vary freely with its complementary $y$-bit. Modulo $2^s$, interactions at distances at least $s+1$ have zero weight, so the variable contribution to $p(x)+p(y)$ is exactly an independent sum $\sum_{\ell=1}^{s-1}2^\ell t_\ell$, $t_\ell\in\{0,1\}$. The two actual addresses are individually fixed modulo $2^s$, so this steers every odd residue of $H_j$ while leaving $j$ fixed. All choices still have the same ordinary suffix sum and the same reverse-complement sum by construction. The mod-24 bracketing proof therefore applies with $2^B$ replacing 32, with modified common coefficients, and $j$ selected mod 3 as before. For each **fixed** $s$, the result is two full physical windows at distance $O_s(N^{1/3})$ whose selected members are $N$ modulo $3\cdot2^s$. This does not control the constants for a modulus competitive with the absolute residual, nor does it assert simultaneous independent steering modulo arbitrary odd factors.

For a concrete placement start at column s+1; with a pair of length ell+1, start the next pair s+1 positions after the last one. Begin the high prefix at the same spacing after the final pair. Thus B=(s+1)+(s−1)(s+2)+s(s−1)/2 = (3s²+3s−2)/2, and enumerating the switches is unnecessary: the 2-adic difference divided by 45 gives their binary digits. In the physical bracketing statement the numbers of suffix bits and constant coefficients increase with s. The exponent statement is fixed-precision, not uniform. In particular a construction of width on the order of s² cannot simply be substituted with s on the order of log N while retaining positive high addresses.

For the dependence on precision, put $h=2^B$, $L=\sqrt{N/(108h^2)}$, and choose the same high complement width $g\asymp L^{1/3}$. The constant linear-in-$U$ coefficient is $O(h^2)$ since the two suffix sums are each $O(h)$. Its coefficient is positive, and the variation with imbalance $d$ is $O(h^2|d|)$; the remaining complement-checksum error is $O(g+U/g)$, and the bounded suffix intercept is $O(h^2)$. Taking $U\ge L+1$ handles that intercept uniformly as $L\to\infty$. The two opposite ends of the $d$-interval can still use $d=O(\sqrt{Lg})$ with absolute constants, since the quadratic coefficient is $432h^2$. Adjacent jumps are therefore $O(h^2 L^{2/3})=O(h^{4/3}N^{1/3})$, with an absolute constant for sufficiently large $L$. This makes sub-logarithmic growing precision possible, but its cost vastly exceeds the resulting modulus. It does not give exact placement.
