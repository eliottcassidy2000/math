## 1. General odd-group criterion

Let G be a nontrivial finite abelian group of odd order, A an automorphism, and a Cayley-tournament connection set mean S disjoint from -S with union G\{0}. Write X(A) for the sets with AS=S.

**Theorem 1.** X(A) is nonempty iff ord(A) is odd. In that case

    |X(A)| = 2^(N(A)/2),
    N(A) = (1/ord(A)) sum_j (|ker(A^j-I)|-1).

**Proof.** If A has even order d, B=A^(d/2) is a nonidentity involution. Choose y with By≠y and set x=y-By≠0. Then Bx=-x, impossible in an invariant connection set. Conversely if A has odd order, none of its nonzero orbits can contain both x and -x: the induced permutation would force -1 to have odd order on x. Negation therefore pairs orbits, and each pair gives one independent choice. Burnside gives N(A).

**Theorem 2 (reversal criterion).** There exists S with AS=-S iff

    ord(A)=2m with m odd, and A^m=-I.

When these hold, the reversing connection sets are **exactly X(A²)**, hence number 2^(N(A²)/2).

**Proof.** AS=-S makes A² preserve S, so Theorem 1 forces ord(A²) odd. A cannot have odd order (iterate AS=-S an odd number of times). Thus ord(A)=2m with m odd. B=A^m reverses S. A nonzero fixed vector of B would be impossible. Since B is an involution, B(x+Bx)=x+Bx, whence x+Bx=0 for all x; B=-I. Conversely A=-A^(m+1), and m+1 is even, so every A²-invariant S satisfies AS=-S.

**Hostile control.** Over F3², diag(1,-1) has no scalar -I in its cyclic subgroup, yet admits neither invariant nor reversing tournaments. Thus “-I is absent from the acting subgroup” alone is NOT sufficient: individual orbits may be self-negative. Nor is “A has even order” sufficient for reversal.

## 2. Exact Fibonacci/Pisano classification, including composite n

Let n>1 be odd, Q=[[1,1],[1,0]] modulo n, and P=ord(Q)=pi(n). For any integer r>=0 let d=P/gcd(P,r). Then

* Q^r preserves some tournament iff d is odd.
* Q^r reverses some tournament iff d≡2 mod4 AND Q^(rd/2)=-I modulo n.

In particular **Q itself never preserves or reverses a Cayley tournament on (Z/n)²**. Its determinant -1 forces even order, excluding preservation; if reversal were possible, d=2m with m odd and Q^m=-I would give determinant -1=1 modulo odd n>1.

For a more explicit CRT statement write n=product p_i^k_i and a_i=v2(pi(p_i^k_i)). Every a_i>=1. Then Q^r preserves iff 2^max(a_i) divides r. It reverses iff

    all a_i equal the same a>=2, and v2(r)=a-1.

Here r=0 is preserving, never reversing.

**Local proof.** The odd-prime-power order has the same 2-part as the order modulo p, because the reduction kernel is a p-group. For split p≠5, the roots α,β satisfy αβ=-1. If their joint order has 2-part at least4, both roots have the same 2-part, and the unique involution in <Q> is -I. If its 2-part is2, that involution has determinant -1 and is not -I. For inert p, the field unit group's involution is -1, and the norm -1 forces order divisible4. At p=5 the double root is3 of order4 and Q mod5 has order20, again giving involution -I. A matrix involution lifting -I modulo an odd prime is -I: (B-I) is invertible and (B-I)(B+I)=0. Finally CRT's global half-order involution is identity in a component whose local 2-part is smaller, precluding scalar -I. This proves the stated uniformity condition.

**Exact count without orbit enumeration.** For t>=1 put

    u_t = gcd(F_(t+1)-1, F_t, F_(t-1)-1),
    v_t = abs(1+(-1)^t-L_t)/u_t.

The integer Smith invariants of Q^t-I are u_t,v_t. Consequently

    K_n(t)=gcd(n,u_t) gcd(n,v_t),     K_n(0)=n²,
    N(Q^r)=(1/d) sum_(j=0)^(d-1) (K_n(rj)-1).

Use this N in Theorem 1, or replace r by2r and d by d/2 in Theorem 2. These are labelled connection-set counts, not graph-isomorphism counts. The formula allows v_t=0 with gcd(n,0)=n.

## 3. Observer conjugation makes a free Klein quotient

In R=(Z/n)[mu]/(mu²-a mu-1), n odd, let H be an odd-order cyclic group of norm-one units. Let X be H-invariant connection sets, and C the quadratic conjugation mu↦a-mu. Since CgC=g^-1, C acts on X. Reversal J:S↦-S also acts and commutes with C.

**Theorem 3.** The Klein group generated by C and J acts freely on X. Thus the count after identifying precisely these observers and global reversal is |X|/4=2^(N(H)/2-2).

**Proof.** J has no fixed connection set. C and -C are nonidentity involutions of the additive group (C fixes1, while C(mu)=a-mu≠mu since2 is a unit). By Theorem 1 neither can preserve a connection set. Fixed points of CJ would mean (-C)S=S, equally impossible. All three nonidentity actions are fixed-point-free. This proves the count and in particular N(H)/2>=2.

This quotient is **only by the specified observer/reversal group**; it is not a count up to arbitrary group automorphisms or tournament isomorphisms. It applies directly to the known U1 bank without rebranding that bank as new.

## 4. Ramified theorem, independent audit, and attachment recurrence

**Theorem 4 (simple ramification).** Let a be an integer, D=a²+4, and let p be an odd prime with v_p(D)=1. Then p≡1 (mod4), hence p>=5. In O=Z_p[mu], where mu²=a mu+1, put delta=2mu-a and g=mu⁴. For every k>=1, the number of g-invariant Cayley tournaments on the additive group of O/p^k is

    2^(p^k-1).

There are B_k=p^k-1 antipodal nonzero orbit pairs. For k>=2 every lower orbit pair has exactly p upper child pairs, and the reduction kernel contributes p−1 new pairs. Thus B_k=pB_(k−1)+(p−1), and a fixed lower orientation has exactly 2^(p−1) coherent lifts.

**Proof.** Since a²=-4 modulo p, p does not divide a and -1 is a square modulo p, proving p≡1 (mod4). The polynomial delta²-D is Eisenstein, making O a ramified quadratic DVR with uniformizer delta and v_delta(p)=2. The identity

    g-1=mu⁴-1=a mu² delta

implies v_delta(g-1)=1. For u of positive delta-valuation, raising 1+u to an exponent prime to p preserves that valuation; raising to p increases it by2, since p u is the unique lowest-valuation binomial term when p>=5. Therefore

    v_delta(g^j-1)=1+2v_p(j).

A nonzero x of remaining delta-depth r in O/p^k is fixed by g^j exactly when 1+2v_p(j)>=r; its orbit length is p^floor(r/2). There are (p−1)p^(r−1) elements of this depth. Summing their orbit counts at depths2l−1 and2l gives2(p−1)p^(l−1); summing l=1,...,k gives2(p^k−1) nonzero orbits. All orbit lengths are odd, so negation pairs them freely, proving the exponent. Under reduction, every lift of a nonzero element retains its valuation and gains two remaining-depth units. Orbit length hence grows by p; a lower orbit of size L has p²L preimage elements and exactly p upper orbits. The kernel depths1,2 supply2(p−1) orbits. This proves the recurrence and coherent-lift statement. ∎


For the p=5 ramified extension, the formula is independently confirmed here: for g=phi^4, the invariant exponent at modulus5^k is B_k=5^k-1, for k=1,2,3 by direct signed constraints.

The recurrence has the following exact justification. In the ramified quadratic DVR take uniformizer sqrt5. For nonzero x modulo5^k let r be its remaining uniformizer depth. The shell size is4·5^(r-1) and its orbit size is5^floor(r/2). Pairing r=2l-1 and2l gives8·5^(l-1) nonzero orbits, so 2(5^k-1) orbits and B_k=5^k-1 antipodal pairs.

For k>=2, reduction to modulus5^(k-1) preserves the valuation of each lift of a nonzero vector, so remaining depth increases by2 and its orbit length multiplies by5. An orbit of length L downstairs has25L points in its full preimage and hence exactly5 upper orbits. Negation respects projection, giving five upper antipodal orbit pairs for each lower pair. The reduction kernel has shells r=1,2, hence8 orbits or4 pairs. Therefore

    B_k=5B_(k-1)+4.

Fixing a lower tournament and requiring every non-kernel difference upstairs to have the lower orientation forces all five children of each parent pair to agree and leaves exactly four independent kernel bits: **16 coherent lifts per specified lower tournament**. Unrestricted tournaments also have four extra relative bits per parent pair. This is a statement about coherence of connection sets on differences outside the kernel, not an unrestricted tournament quotient.
