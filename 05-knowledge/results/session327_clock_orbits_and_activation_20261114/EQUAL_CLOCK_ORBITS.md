# A full residue orbit of H at one exact Q

A **switch** consists of two actual tournament sources with unequal H but exactly equal two-slot count Q. Ordinal products multiply H and Q. Therefore if a switch has responses (a,q),(b,q) with a,b prime to a modulus M, each of its copies multiplies the selectable H residue by an extra b/a (relative to choosing the first source). The exact Q is untouched. This makes an actual unit-group orbit at one clock, not two independently prescribed congruences.

The first two rows use source data already covered in [S326](../session326_exact_source_profiles_20261114/SAME_Q_SWITCHES.md) and that session's exact searches; what is important here is their joint generator action. Every a,b is a unit at 2,3,11.

**Theorem.** For every M=2^e·3^f·11^g (exponents nonnegative), there is one positive exact slot budget Q_M at which every unit residue for H modulo M occurs among literal sources. The source is an ordinal sum of the four switches, not asserted strong. Any additional common singleton padding multiplies all these equal Q by the same power of two.

At the base modulus 8·9·121=8712, the four ratios b/a are (6565,5231,8045,761), and their successive subgroup sizes are 330,660,1320,2640. These exhaust the unit group: successive quotients have orders (330,2,2,2). The short replay provides the whole finite certificate.

To lift to all higher powers, use that the units modulo 2^e, e≥3, are C₂×C_(2^(e−2)); at an odd prime p the units modulo p^f are cyclic of order (p−1)p^(f−1). Reduction to 8,9,121 retains the order-p quotient of every cyclic prime-power factor of its respective unit group, including the 2- and 5-primary parts at 11. Consequently every homomorphism of the higher unit group to a group of prime order kills the kernel of reduction to 8712: on a matching prime-power factor it factors through reduction of that factor modulo its prime, and on other factors it vanishes. If the subgroup generated by the switches were proper it would be contained in a maximal subgroup of prime index (the group is finite abelian). Its reductions are full, contradicting such a homomorphism. Exponents smaller than the base are covered by projection from a larger exponent.

For a literal construction at any M, form the successive subgroups G₀={1}⊂G₁⊂⋯⊂G₄ and let d_i be the order of ratio i modulo G_(i−1). Take d_i−1 copies of each switch; choosing its second factor j_i times gives every coset j_i=0,…,d_i−1. All choices share Q_M=∏q_i^(d_i−1), and H ranges over one unit multiple of the whole group, hence over the whole group. At M=19008, the common phase-and-mod-three period of the 99 bank, the quotient orders are (720,2,2,2). The clocks can be very large: this is why exact budget equality and arbitrary H residues still do not lift a *prescribed absolute* target.

## 2. Small complementary phases and the number 1386

The full orbit is overkill for one phase. Combine just the (5,19) and (23,79) switches. The left choice twice and the right choice twice give the literal common-Q pair

    (H,Q)=(115,303104), (1501,303104).

Their difference in H is 1386=7·198, so H₂≡H₁+198 mod396. They are the two complementary phase choices of [the lattice test](CLOCK_LATTICE.md), implemented at one physical slot budget. The return of 1386 here has an exact explanation: (19·79)−(5·23)=1386. It is not evidence that the earlier coefficient-1386 affine rail is the same operation.

There is also the sign pair using the (73,85) and (5,19) switches:

    (H,Q)=(365,278528), (1615,278528),    H₁+H₂=1980=5·396.

These are exact minimum common-clock costs *in this four-switch palette* for their respective residue ratios 199 and −1 modulo396. To verify the scoped minimum, use the residue graph whose edges multiply by one of the four ratios or its inverse; edge costs multiply the shared Q by its q_i. Shortest multiplicative paths from 1 give 303104 and 278528. Such a path corresponds to choosing opposite orientations in the relevant switches; extra copies chosen identically never improve the clock. No minimality over all tournaments is asserted.

## 3. Scope of the inverse gain

A fixed budget with a full unit orbit can eliminate modular incompatibility on the H side without moving the Q coordinate. It does not make the integer H small enough, and reducing or changing the budget to hit N exactly is a different task. Even within a row built from four switches, at most ∏(t_i+1) H values can be selected using t_i copies; the price Q=∏q_i^t_i is exponential in total copy number. If the row carries at least φ(M) distinct H residues, AM–GM gives Σt_i≥4(φ(M)^(1/4)−1). Thus full residue supply is mathematically real but should not be mistaken for a logarithmically sized source compiler for a target with a prescribed budget. This is the physical distinction between changing a digit, changing a phase, and changing an absolute clock.
