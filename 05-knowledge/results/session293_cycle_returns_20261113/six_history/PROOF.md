## 2. A different rational map with exact period six

Let `A=[[0,−3],[1,3]]` and `g(x)=−3/(x+3)` on the projective rational line.
Direct multiplication gives `A⁶=−27 I`, while the ratio of its eigenvalues is
a primitive sixth root. Every proper nontrivial power has the same two fixed
points as `g`, the nonrational roots of `x²+3x+3`. Consequently every rational
point has exact period six. A sample is

`0 → −1 → −3/2 → −2 → −3 → infinity → 0`.

This is a Möbius map, not the quadratic above, and says nothing about rational
periods of arbitrary quadratic maps. Its projective return does NOT return
an ordinary vector: its gain is `−27`. No real scalar rescaling of `A` makes
its sixth power the identity, because a real sixth power is nonnegative.

## 3. A mass-preserving quadratic lift

The gain can be removed lawfully on a different carrier. On symmetric
matrices `U=[[u,v],[v,w]]`, set `C(U)=A U Aᵀ/3`. In coordinates:

```
C = [[0, 0, 3], [0, −1, −3], [1/3, 2, 3]].
```

Then `C⁶=I`, and its characteristic polynomial is
`(X−1)(X²−X+1)`. The following six rank-one positive-semidefinite matrices,
written as `(u,v,w)`, are cyclically permuted:

```
v0=(3, 0, 0)       v1=(0, 0, 1)
v2=(3,−3, 3)       v3=(9,−6, 4)
v4=(12,−6,3)       v5=(9,−3, 1).
```

They span the three-dimensional carrier. The functional
`L(u,v,w)=u+3v+3w` is invariant and satisfies `L(vi)=3` for every i.
It is strictly positive on nonzero positive-semidefinite matrices: its
representing quadratic form has matrix `[[1,3/2],[3/2,3]]`, positive definite.
Thus the cone generated by these six rays supports an exact positive-mass
six-step return. The middle coordinate may be negative; it is not itself a
count. The six ray coefficients ARE nonnegative hidden coordinates.

Let `D` have columns `vi` and let `P e_i=e_(i+1 mod6)`. Then
`D P = C D`, `rank(D)=3`, `L D=(3,3,3,3,3,3)`.
This gives the precise architecture `6 hidden positive states → 3 observed
modes`, with no scale or sign loss after six steps.

## 4. Six hidden states are necessary under conserved positive mass

**Theorem.** Suppose `M` is a nonnegative real `d×d` matrix, `w` is a strictly
positive row vector with `w M=w`, and `D₀` is a surjection to the carrier above
with `D₀ M=C D₀`. Then `d≥6`. The construction in section 3 attains equality.
If in addition `d=6` and `M` has integer entries, `M` is a six-cycle permutation
matrix after reindexing.

**Proof.** The quotient identity implies the primitive sixth eigenvalue
`zeta=exp(pi i/3)` occurs in `M`. Positive mass permits diagonal similarity
to a column-stochastic matrix, whose transpose is row-stochastic. For a
complex eigenvector of that transpose at `zeta`, choose a component of
maximum modulus. Equality in the triangle inequality shows every outgoing
positive transition stays among maximum-modulus components and advances
phase by the same factor `zeta`. A directed cycle in this nonempty finite
closed subgraph has length divisible by six. Taking a simple directed cycle
therefore uses at least six vertices.

If `d=6`, this cycle uses all vertices. Their six phases are distinct, so each
vertex can transition only to the unique vertex at the required next phase.
Thus `M` is a weighted six-cycle. Its weights multiply to one, by positive
mass conservation. If all are positive integers, each equals one. QED.

A faithful five-state permutation action of `C₆` DOES exist, using a disjoint
2-cycle and 3-cycle. It does not contradict the theorem: its eigenvalues have
orders 1,2,3, never 6. It cannot retain the primitive sixth observer mode.
Thus the necessary sidecar is the actual cyclotomic mode, not abstract group
order. Four/five states fail for this carrier, though period four is possible
in other rational dynamical systems.

The integer conclusion exposes a useful cost: at the minimum six states,
exact full positive-mass renewal cannot branch. A branching count mechanism
must enlarge the carrier, cease to conserve all positive mass, or return
only on a restricted reachable family. The theorem is not a claim about
signed matrices, one scalar readout, or infinitely many hidden states.

## 5. Transfer to the current constructive problem

The source is the quadratic lift above; the target is a proposed finite
nonnegative endpoint-transfer model. The map would have to send the six
positive ray states to actual realizable endpoint profiles and preserve both
transition and the positive full-count functional. Neither map nor physical
six-state operation has been constructed here.

The useful design question is therefore not "does the ratio return after
six moves?" but:

1. Which primitive return mode is retained by the full endpoint observer?
2. What positive mass measures total Hamiltonian counts, rather than ratios?
3. Is the proposed return branching, and if so where are the additional
   hidden states required by the theorem?
4. Which directed cycle-opening changes those hidden coordinates, and which
   compensating physical operation restores them without changing the target?

The six-ray model is a positive control for exact transport, not a proof of
Chan–Pak. It also explains why compressing to three observed modes does not
license a three-state nonnegative source construction.

## 6. Cyclotomic observer grammar and balanced hidden replacements

The preceding example is an instance of standard cyclic representation
algebra, here packaged as an exact positive-source design test. For any
`q≥2`, let the observed carrier be `Q ⊕ Q[X]/(Phi_q)` and observe the q
nonnegative cycle states by

`e_j ↦ (1, X^j mod Phi_q), 0≤j<q`.

The observed transition is identity on the mass coordinate and multiplication
by X on the second summand. It has `1+phi(q)` rational modes; the observer is
surjective. The primitive q mode and positive conserved mass force at least
q hidden states by exactly the maximum-modulus argument above. Thus
`6→3`, `12→5`, `24→9`, `30→9` are precise compression/minimum-positive-source
pairs. They are not identifications of earlier repository state spaces.

For an integral target `(a,r)` with canonical polynomial r of degree less
than `phi(q)`, the exact integer observer image is

`a ≡ r(1) mod Phi_q(1)`.

Indeed every source polynomial has `f=r+Phi_q h`, hence
`f(1)=r(1)+Phi_q(1)h(1)`. Conversely the constant choice
`h=(a−r(1))/Phi_q(1)` has `degree f≤phi(q)≤q−1` and realizes the target.
This proves the lattice index `Phi_q(1)` without a residue search. This value
is p for q a power of p and 1 when q has at least two distinct prime factors.
The assertion is about signed integer source coefficients; nonnegative
realizability still requires the cone and semigroup constraints.

The integral kernel consists exactly of coefficient vectors of

`(X−1)Phi_q(X) h(X), degree h<q−phi(q)−1`.

To prove it, use `Phi_q | f` and `f(1)=0`; since `Phi_q(1)≠0`, the quotient
is divisible by `X−1`. Monic division stays integral.

For q=6 the basic relation is

`v3 + 2v1 = 2v2 + v0`.

Both sides have three nonnegative hidden tokens and the identical full
three-mode observer (hence the same positive mass). This is an exact
balanced hidden replacement, not merely a ratio collision. Its cyclic
translates remain valid. There are simpler opposite-pair replacements:

`v0+v3 = v1+v4 = v2+v5`.

These equations give a constructive small test bed for repeated compensation:
use only a replacement whose subtractive side is actually present, retain
all three observed coordinates, and keep the six nonnegative inventories.
There is no claim that the displayed moves connect every nonnegative fibre,
or that they correspond to tournament operations. The latter is exactly the
physical realization obligation. The benefit over an unconstrained signed
identity is that each move now has an explicit nonnegative availability guard.

## 7. Where branching can return: the cyclic-class requirement

Here is the positive extension rather than only a minimum-state obstruction.
Let R be an irreducible row-stochastic matrix on d states. It has a primitive
q-th root zeta as an eigenvalue if and only if there is a partition into q
nonempty classes `V0,...,V(q−1)` such that every positive transition from Vi
goes to V(i+1 mod q).

For necessity, take `Rz=zeta z` and propagate equality in the maximum-modulus
triangle inequality through irreducibility. Every component has the same
nonzero modulus. Along every positive edge, the component phase advances by
zeta. A path from one fixed vertex therefore places each component in one
of the q phases; the phase assignment is path-independent because the
component is fixed. Following paths produces all q phases. For sufficiency,
assign component `zeta^i` on Vi and substitute into the row-stochastic sum.

This is the elementary peripheral-spectrum / cyclic-class theorem, not a
new Perron–Frobenius claim. It shows how to generalize the six-state model:
branch among multiple states of the next phase, but never skip a phase or
mix it with a different phase. At d=q the transitions are deterministic;
with d>q they may genuinely branch. Without irreducibility, apply the
statement to a closed communicating class supporting the peripheral mode;
no global partition or every-state return is asserted for transient states.

A proposed normalized endpoint-transfer operator should therefore expose
these phase classes and its stationary renewal rule before a rational-cycle
analogy is used. A stochastic normalization alone preserves probability,
not the unnormalized Hamiltonian count. Its normalization multiplier must
also have product one along the proposed return (or be physically compensated).
A growing path-prefix DAG itself is not a recurrent Markov chain. The states
in this proposed comparison are interfaces across operations, not tournament
vertices artificially declared to form a stochastic cycle.
