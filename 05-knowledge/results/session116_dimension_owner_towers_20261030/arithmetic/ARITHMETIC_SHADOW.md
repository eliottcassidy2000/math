# The characteristic-four shadow and the extreme-Walsh unit subgroup

**Status: PROVED**, with independent finite-exact checks below. Novelty claimed only relative to the cited repository results; no literature priority or major-conjecture closure is claimed.

## Inheritance and actual new content

Session112 `norms/NORM_SIGNS.md` proves the signed-XOR rectangle criterion, the 16 coherent dimension-eight tables, their divisor-sum bank, and a five-equation impossibility certificate in dimension sixteen. Session115 `quadratic/ODD_EIGHTH_PHASES.md` classifies the one-point affine phases by their Walsh peak. We retain those theorems, rather than count them as new.

The new assertions here are: (i) the exact **integral defect ideal** and hence complete coefficient-ring/modulus classification; (ii) a dimension-uniform **multiplicative group structure** on the previously classified one-point phases, including its exact index in all arithmetic units; and (iii) the equality of its explicit character with Hamming weight modulo four. This is an actual map joining the two previous constructions, not a coincidence of the number eight.

## 1. Exact universal defect ideal

Put V=F₂^d, N=2^d. Fix any binary table F and define an integral bilinear product

    e_a e_b = (−1)^F(a,b) e_(a+b),       Q(x)=Σ_a x_a².

Let D_F(x,y)=Q(xy)−Q(x)Q(y). Write I_coeff for the ideal of Z generated by its polynomial coefficients, and I_values for the ideal generated by all its values at integral x,y.

**Theorem 1.** Both ideals coincide, and are exactly

    I_coeff = I_values = (0)  if F composes Q over R,
                          (4)  otherwise.

Here R in this display means the real numbers. No associativity assumption is made.

**Proof.** Pure-square terms cancel. The only remaining monomials are x_a x_(a+t) y_b y_(b+t), t≠0, with coefficient

    2[(−1)^(F(a,b)+F(a+t,b+t))
      +(−1)^(F(a,b+t)+F(a+t,b))] ∈ {−4,0,4}.

Choose each unordered row pair and column pair only once. This exhausts all collisions because the index product is XOR. Thus the coefficient ideal is zero or (4), and every value is divisible by four. If a coefficient is nonzero, set x=e_a+e_(a+t), y=e_b+e_(b+t). There is only that mixed monomial left after pure-square cancellation, so D_F(x,y)=±4. Hence the value ideal is also exactly (4). □

**Corollary 1: all coefficient rings.** For a commutative unital ring R, this *fixed* signed table satisfies Q(xy)=Q(x)Q(y) for all R-valued x,y iff either it is a real norm-composing table or 4·1_R=0. Sufficiency follows coefficientwise; necessity uses the same sparse vectors with entries 0,1. Thus this is both a formal-polynomial and a pointwise classification, even over finite rings.

In particular an incoherent table composes modulo a positive integer m iff m divides 4. This is not just a prime-characteristic statement: characteristic four works too.

For normalized tables F(0,b)=F(a,0)=0, the inherited five-equation obstruction gives:

* d≤3: coherent tables exist and compose over every coefficient ring;
* d≥4: **every** such table composes over R iff 4·1_R=0.

At d≥4 this does not say the algebras become associative, alternative, division algebras, or have a nondegenerate positive norm. It says only that this particular norm identity survives in the characteristic-four shadow. Modulo eight, the sparse ±4 witness always detects an incoherent table.

### Exact arithmetic-bank refinement

For the Session112 seven-prime bank with bits (A,B,C,D,E,F,G) and

    m=19^A 2^B 59^C 29^D 5^E 89^F 7^G,

Theorem 1 upgrades its predicate to

    defect ideal=(0) iff sigma(m)/30 is an integer square;
    defect ideal=(4) otherwise.

Thus reduction modulo four erases **every** three-bit squareclass syndrome in that bank. Retaining the integer coefficient, or reducing modulo eight, restores a sharp yes/no norm-composition test. This statement is only for this fixed bank and its prescribed table map; it is not a criterion for arbitrary integers.

## 2. The parity convolution norm

Let B_d=F₂[V] with convolution *, and identify an element a with its binary coefficient function. Let wt(a) be its Hamming weight. Define

    n₄(a)=wt(a) mod 4 ∈ Z/4Z.

**Theorem 2.** For every d≥0 and every a,b∈B_d,

    n₄(a*b)=n₄(a)n₄(b).

**Proof.** Lift a,b to integral 0/1 vectors and use the unsigned XOR product. Every square of an integer is congruent modulo four to its parity bit. Therefore Q(xy) mod4 is wt(a*b) mod4; Q(x),Q(y) are wt(a),wt(b). Theorem 1 gives the identity. □

This is a multiplicative map, **not an additive ring homomorphism**. For example n₄(e₀+e₀)=0 but n₄(e₀)+n₄(e₀)=2.

The augmentation is aug(a)=wt(a) mod2. Since a*a=aug(a)e₀, an element is a unit iff its weight is odd, and every unit is its own inverse. Hence

    B_d^× ≅ (F₂)^(N−1),   |B_d^×|=2^(N−1).

On this group n₄ takes values {1,3}, a genuine multiplicative arithmetic character. It is nontrivial for d≥2 (use e₀+e₁+e₂), so its two fibers have size 2^(N−2).

### Complete modulus classification for the integer weights of units

**Theorem 2b (joint refinement with the parent lane).** Let J_d be the ideal of Z generated by

    wt(a*b)−wt(a)wt(b),   a,b∈B_d^×.

Then J_d=(0) for d=0,1; J_2=(8); and J_d=(4) for all d≥3. Equivalently, unit Hamming weight is universally multiplicative modulo every positive m at d≤1, precisely modulo divisors of8 at d2, and precisely modulo divisors of4 at d≥3.

**Proof.** At d≤1 every unit is a basis vector, so every weight is1. At d2 the units have weights1 or3. Weight1 units translate supports. The mod4 character forces the product of any two weight3 units to have weight1, since1 is the only available unit weight congruent to1 mod4. Thus every weight defect is0 or−8, and−8 occurs by squaring a weight3 unit. At d≥3 all defects are divisible by4 by Theorem2. The units with supports {0,e₁,e₂} and {0,e₁,e₃} have weight3 each, while their product has support {e₂,e₁+e₂,e₃,e₁+e₃,e₂+e₃}, of weight5. Their defect is5−9=−4. Embedding this three-coordinate subspace works in every higher d. □

This gives a different sharp dimension threshold: unit-weight multiplicativity mod8 fails already at d3 (dimension8), while real signed-XOR norm composition remains possible there and fails at d4. The parity map erases even integer coefficients; their squares can contribute4 mod8. Those lost carries explain why its mod8 test does not inherit real composition. **These thresholds must not be identified.**

## 3. The extreme-Walsh family is a subgroup, not just a spectrum census

Assume d≥3. Let A_d⊂B_d be the d+1-dimensional vector space of affine Boolean functions. As an additive subspace it is invariant under translation and hence an ideal for convolution.

**Lemma.** A_d*A_d=0.

**Proof.** A convolution coefficient counts modulo two the solutions of two affine binary equations in d variables. If consistent and independent there are 2^(d−2) solutions; if their rank is one or zero there are 2^(d−1) or 2^d. All are even for d≥3. The same conclusion holds when one function vanishes. □

Define

    H_d={f_(v,r,c)(x)=c+r·v+r·x+δ_v(x) : v,r∈V, c∈F₂}.

All additions in this definition are binary; convolution is a different operation.

**Theorem 3 (uniform complete group classification).** For d≥3,

    f_(v,r,c) * f_(w,s,e) = f_(v+w,r+s,c+e).

The parameters are unique, so H_d is a subgroup of B_d^× isomorphic to F₂^(2d+1). Moreover

    n₄(f_(v,r,c)) = (−1)^c mod4,
    [B_d^×:H_d] = 2^(2^d−2d−2).

In particular H₃ is the **entire** unit group; H₄ has index64. The arithmetic-character fibers within H_d each have exactly N² members.

**Proof.** First write f=e_v+a and g=e_w+b with affine a,b. By the lemma,

    f*g=e_(v+w)+T_v b+T_w a.

In the unreparametrized notation a(x)=ε+r·x, b(x)=η+s·x, the product affine term is

    ε+η+s·v+r·w+(r+s)·x.

Substituting ε=c+r·v, η=e+s·w produces exactly the asserted c+e formula. If two representations coincide, their affine difference is e_v+e_w. For v≠w this has weight2, while a nonconstant affine function has weight N/2≥4 and a nonzero constant has weight N≥8; impossible. If v=w, the affine functions coincide. Thus there are 2N² distinct elements and the law gives a group (identity δ₀).

For r≠0, the affine term has weight N/2, divisible by four, and the toggle at v changes its weight by (−1)^(ε+r·v)=(−1)^c. If r=0, the weights are1 or N−1 with the same residue. The index follows from |B_d^×| and |H_d|. Finally 2^d−2d−2 vanishes at d3 and strictly increases thereafter. □

### Precise link to the previous odd-eighth theorem

Session115 proves that the functions with a Walsh coefficient of magnitude N−2 are exactly ε+r·x+δ_v, with unique parameters for d≥3. Therefore **H_d is exactly that extremal-Walsh family**, now endowed with a rigorously classified convolution multiplication and arithmetic character.

At d3 its readings (N−W_f(u))/(2N) are the odd-eighth patterns already classified. At d4 the same group exists, but is only 1/64 of the odd-weight functions. The loss of odd-weight exhaustion is therefore an exact group-index transition, not merely one counterexample or a numerological 8→16 analogy.

This group multiplication is NOT the additive sign-gauge law from the octonion tables. Nor have we proved that it descends through every gauge-normalization quotient. The original character labels/base table remain necessary if one transports named Walsh readings.

### Sharp low-dimensional boundary

At d2 the lemma fails: for independent linear coordinate functions x₁,x₂, their convolution is the constant-one function. The unique-parameter theorem also fails (2N²=32 exceeds the 16 Boolean functions). Thus neither the additive parameter law nor the index formula is asserted below d3.

## 4. Map, preserved predicate, and information loss

1. **Signed integral XOR algebra → parity group algebra**, by reducing vector coefficients and forgetting signs mod2. Retains the XOR support and the norm's weight-mod4 shadow. Loses all signed rectangle syndromes, real composition, and associator data. Keeping a sparse integer defect or a mod8 lift is necessary to see the composition failure.
2. **One-point affine Boolean phase → group-algebra unit**, by using its truth table as coefficients. Retains the full phase (hence all Walsh data), and adds a convolution group law. The arithmetic character remembers exactly c, not (v,r); keeping (v,r) recovers the named Fourier coordinates.
3. **Fixed sigma bank → defect ideal**, by the Session112 prime/line-bit correspondence. Retains exact real composition versus defect-ideal(4). Mod4 then forgets this distinction completely.

There is no assertion that all norm-composing octonion tables equal H₃ as groups, that octonion multiplication is this commutative convolution, or that matching thresholds prove a causal equivalence between Hurwitz composition and Walsh extremality. The connection here is the explicit shared parity map, invariant, and exactly identified losses.
