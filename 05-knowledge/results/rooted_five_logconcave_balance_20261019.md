# A rooted five-point balance theorem from ideal-run log-concavity

2026-10-19. A proof for an infinite structural class, with no width, degree, or exterior-size bound. This is not a solution of the general 1/3–2/3 conjecture. The statement is proved below; its priority status has not been established.

## 1. Main theorem

For a finite poset $P$, let $I_P(t)$ be the set of elements incomparable with $t$. Suppose $x\parallel y$ and

\[
I_P(x)\cup I_P(y)=\{b,x,z,y,a\},                       \tag{1}
\]

where these five distinct elements induce exactly the relations generated by

\[
b<z<a,\qquad x<a,\qquad b<y.                         \tag{2}
\]

**Theorem.** One of the pairs $\{x,y\},\{x,z\},\{z,y\}$ is balanced in the uniform linear extensions of the whole poset $P$.

The theorem permits arbitrary finite exteriors, including unbounded incomparability degrees and width. In particular, it eliminates the saturated rooted-five residual from the companion seven-thin investigation. Its mechanism is an exact realizability constraint, rather than validity for arbitrary mixtures of conditioned templates.

## 2. Ideal-run tails are log-concave

We use Stanley's classical rank log-concavity theorem: for a fixed element $t$ of a finite poset, the number $N_i$ of linear extensions placing $t$ at rank $i$ satisfies $N_i^2\ge N_{i-1}N_{i+1}$. This is the case $k=1$ of Theorem 3.1 in R. P. Stanley, *Two Combinatorial Applications of the Aleksandrov–Fenchel Inequalities*, Journal of Combinatorial Theory A **31** (1981), 56–65, pp. 62–63. [Author-hosted primary source](https://math.mit.edu/~rstan/pubs/pubfiles/48.pdf).

### Lemma 1: the initial-ideal moment bound

Let $K$ be an ideal of a finite poset $B$. In a uniformly chosen linear extension of $B$, let $R$ be the number of initial entries belonging to $K$, stopping at the first entry outside $K$. If $K=B$, set $R=|B|$. Write $m=\mathbb E R$. Then

\[
\mathbb E\binom R2\le m^2,\qquad
\mathbb E\frac{(R+1)(R+2)}2\le(m+1)^2.                \tag{3}
\]

**Proof.** Adjoin a new minimal element $t$ below every point of $B\setminus K$ and incomparable with every point of $K$. This defines a poset because $K$ is an ideal: no path from $t$ through its complement can return to $K$.

For $j\ge0$, let $T_j$ count extensions of $B$ whose first $j$ letters belong to $K$. Inserting $t$ immediately after these $j$ letters, or deleting it, is a bijection between these extensions and extensions of the enlarged poset in which $t$ has rank $j+1$. Hence $T_j=N_{j+1}$ exactly, with no multiplicative or binomial factor.

Set $q_j=T_j/T_0=\Pr(R\ge j)$. Then $q_0=1$, and the positive support is precisely $0\le j\le |K|$: placing all of the ideal $K$ first is legal. Beyond it $q_j=0$. Stanley's theorem says the ratios $q_{j+1}/q_j$ decrease on the positive support. Consequently

\[
q_{i+j}\le q_iq_j\qquad(i,j\ge0),                    \tag{4}
\]

where the statement is automatic if an index is beyond the support. For positive indices within the support, multiply $j$ successive ratios starting at $i$ and compare them with those starting at zero.

The tail-sum identities now give

\[
m=\sum_{j\ge1}q_j,\qquad
\mathbb E\binom R2=\sum_{k\ge2}(k-1)q_k
=\sum_{i,j\ge1}q_{i+j}\le\sum_{i,j\ge1}q_iq_j=m^2.
\]

Finally $(R+1)(R+2)/2=1+2R+\binom R2$. This proves (3). The empty ideal and empty poset cases give $R=0$ and are included. $\square$

For an upset and its terminal run, the same statement follows by order duality.

### Lemma 2: nested-run parameters

If $0\le S\le R$ and

\[
W=\frac{(S+1)(2R-S+2)}2,
\]

then

\[
R+1\le W\le\frac{(R+1)(R+2)}2.                       \tag{5}
\]

Indeed the two differences are respectively $S(2R-S+1)/2$ and $(R-S)(R-S+1)/2$. Therefore, if $R$ is an initial ideal run or terminal upset run, (3) implies

\[
1+\mathbb ER\le\mathbb EW\le(1+\mathbb ER)^2.         \tag{6}
\]

## 3. The exact exterior law of the rooted neighborhood

Every point outside the five-point set in (1) is comparable with both roots. A mixed orientation would make $x,y$ comparable. Thus the exterior splits into $A$, below both roots, and $B$, above both roots; also $A<B$ pointwise.

No core point can lie below a point of $A$: it would then be below both roots and could not belong to their incomparability union. Dually no point of $B$ can lie below a core point. From (2), the only possible core incomparabilities of a lower exterior point are

\[
\varnothing,\quad\{b\},\quad\{b,z\},
\]

and those of an upper exterior point are

\[
\varnothing,\quad\{a\},\quad\{z,a\}.                  \tag{7}
\]

Fix a linear extension of $A$ and one of $B$, add their chain relations, and take transitive closure. No new core relation is created. Remove the all-comparable lower prefix and upper suffix. The remaining conditional poset has a lower chain with $k_1$ exceptions of type $\{b\}$ followed by $k_2$ of type $\{b,z\}$, and an upper chain with $\ell_2$ exceptions of type $\{z,a\}$ followed by $\ell_1$ of type $\{a\}$. Every lower point lies below every upper point.

The lower parameters depend only on the chosen lower word, and the upper parameters only on the upper word. Every pair of words is possible, by concatenating the lower word, any core extension, and the upper word. The removed tails have unique positions. Thus the actual core count measure is the sum of the conditional template count measures over all independently chosen pairs of internal exterior words.

For the lower word, $R_A=k_1+k_2$ is the terminal run of the upset $I_P(b)\cap A$, and $S_A=k_2$ the terminal run of the nested upset $I_P(z)\cap A$. For the upper word, $R_B=\ell_1+\ell_2$ is the initial run of the ideal $I_P(a)\cap B$, and $S_B=\ell_2$ the initial run of the nested ideal $I_P(z)\cap B$.

These identities remain exact after closure: a point in an exception run acquires a comparison with the relevant core vertex precisely when the fixed exterior word puts a nonexception beyond it on the appropriate side. No path through the core or opposite exterior can return and create additional comparisons.

Define the averaged parameters, over uniform internal exterior words, by

\[
\begin{aligned}
c&=\mathbb E(R_A+1),&
u&=\mathbb E\frac{(S_A+1)(2R_A-S_A+2)}2,\\
d&=\mathbb E(R_B+1),&
v&=\mathbb E\frac{(S_B+1)(2R_B-S_B+2)}2.
\end{aligned}                                         \tag{8}
\]

For an empty side use its one empty word. Lemmas 1–2 yield the decisive actual-law constraints

\[
1\le c\le u\le c^2,\qquad1\le d\le v\le d^2.          \tag{9}
\]

These are not assertions about the exterior-word marginals in a uniform extension of $P$. They concern uniform internal words, whose independent raw count summation is justified above.

## 4. Eleven exact core-order counts

For one fixed pair of exterior words, put

\[
c_0=k_1+k_2+1,\quad u_0=(k_2+1)(2k_1+k_2+2)/2,
\]
\[
d_0=\ell_1+\ell_2+1,\quad v_0=(\ell_2+1)(2\ell_1+\ell_2+2)/2.
\]

The counts of extensions inducing each core order are the following. Summing independently over the two exterior words replaces each parameter by its average in (8), with common factor $e(A)e(B)$.

| Core order | Core-order count divided by $e(A)e(B)$ |
|---|---:|
| $b\ x\ z\ y\ a$ | $cd$ |
| $b\ x\ z\ a\ y$ | $c$ |
| $b\ x\ y\ z\ a$ | $cv$ |
| $b\ z\ x\ y\ a$ | $ud$ |
| $b\ z\ x\ a\ y$ | $u$ |
| $b\ z\ y\ x\ a$ | $ud$ |
| $b\ y\ x\ z\ a$ | $cv$ |
| $b\ y\ z\ x\ a$ | $cd$ |
| $x\ b\ z\ y\ a$ | $d$ |
| $x\ b\ z\ a\ y$ | $1$ |
| $x\ b\ y\ z\ a$ | $v$ |

For example, if the core word starts $b,z$, the lower interleaving count is

\[
\sum_{j=0}^{k_2}(k_1+k_2-j+1)
=\frac{(k_2+1)(2k_1+k_2+2)}2=u_0;
\]

if it starts only with $b$ before a root, the count is $c_0$; if it starts with $x$, all lower letters must already precede it and the count is one. The upper counts are dual. The roots separate the two collar interleavings, so the products in the table are exact.

Let $E=e(P)/(e(A)e(B))$, and let $N_{st}$ be the count of $s<t$ divided by that same factor. These quantities may be rational. The table gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
E&=2cd+c+2cv+2ud+u+d+1+v,\\
N_{xy}&=cd+c+cv+ud+u+d+1+v,\\
N_{xz}&=cd+c+2cv+d+1+v,\\
N_{zy}&=cd+c+2ud+u+d+1.
\end{aligned}                                         \tag{10}
\]

## 5. Proof of the theorem

First, the exact identities

\[
2N_{xy}-E=c+u+d+1+v>0                                 \tag{11}
\]

and

\[
\begin{aligned}
14E-11(N_{xz}+N_{zy})={}&6[(c-1)(d-1)+(c-1)(v-1)\\
&+(u-1)(d-1)]+4(c+d-2)+9(u+v-2)\ge0                 \tag{12}
\end{aligned}
\]

show that $p(x<y)>1/2$ and $p(x<z)+p(z<y)\le14/11<4/3$.

If $N_{xy}\le2E/3$, then $x,y$ is balanced. Otherwise write

\[
C=c-1,\quad U=u-1,\quad D=d-1,\quad V=v-1.
\]

All four are nonnegative, and the moment constraints (9) imply

\[
C\le U\le C^2+2C,\qquad D\le V\le D^2+2D.            \tag{13}
\]

The assumed root imbalance gives

\[
0<3N_{xy}-2E=2-C-D-CD-CV-DU.                          \tag{14}
\]

In particular $C<2$ and $D<2$. Next,

\[
E-3N_{xz}=-CD-4CV-7C+2DU-D+3U-6V-10.
\]

Using the upper bound on $U$ and lower bound on $V$ from (13),

\[
\begin{aligned}
E-3N_{xz}
&\le(2D+3)C^2-(D+1)C-7D-10\\
&=(C-2)[3C+5+D(2C+3)]-D<0.                           \tag{15}
\end{aligned}
\]

The dual calculation gives $E-3N_{zy}<0$. Therefore both $p(x<z)$ and $p(z<y)$ exceed $1/3$. By (12) they cannot both exceed $2/3$, so at least one is balanced. This proves the theorem. $\square$

The proof also specifies a witness rule: use $x,y$ when its probability is at most $2/3$; otherwise use the smaller-probability member of $x<z$ and $z<y$, which lies in $(1/3,7/11]$.

## 6. A simpler one-sided corollary

If the lower exterior is empty or an ordinal prefix, then $c=u=1$, and

\[
E=5d+3v+3,\qquad N_{xy}=3d+2v+3,\qquad N_{zy}=4d+3.
\]

If $d\ge3$, the pair $x,y$ is balanced, since $3N_{xy}-2E=3-d\le0$ and (11) holds. If $1\le d<3$, then $z,y$ is balanced, because

\[
3N_{zy}-E=7d+6-3v\ge7d+6-3d^2=(3-d)(3d+2)>0,
\]
\[
2E-3N_{zy}=6v-2d-3\ge4d-3>0.
\]

Thus the one-sided witness uses just two pairs and no degree budget.

## 7. What the new constraint repairs

The earlier seven-thin template cone contains the raw measure $5\mu_{Q_5}+2\mu_{T(0,0,1,5)}$, in which every core incomparable pair is unbalanced. Its averaged upper parameters would be

\[
d=19/7,\qquad v=59/7,
\]

so $v-d^2=52/49>0$. It violates (9), and hence cannot be the actual exterior law of any rooted-five poset. This explains the previous cone obstruction without reclassifying it as a poset counterexample.

The method suggests a broader precise direction: retain rank-log-concave ideal-run laws, or their exact factorial-moment consequences, when forming local boundary cones. Arbitrary nonnegative profile mixtures can discard a genuinely decisive constraint even when all individual profile templates are legal posets.
