# Independent audit of the faithful swap compiler

**Verdict: PASS for the stated analytic results and complete finite certificates.**
The existence premise in the general upper compiler is essential. The
seven-column monotonicity claim applies to completed swap-and-affine moves;
it does not apply to every bare intermediate swap. The reciprocal hostile
concerns swaps of output zero, not arbitrary single transpositions.

This referee imports neither the producer nor a repository implementation.
The all-prime conclusions follow from the arguments below. The finite
conclusions are exhaustively checked in their declared universes, without
sampling or numerical arithmetic.

## 1. Cosets, word distance and the conditional upper compiler

Use the producer's composition convention: a permutation maps source columns
to physical values, and maps compose from right to left. For a representative
sigma, physically swapping sigma(i) and sigma(j) gives sigma tau_(ij).
Replacing sigma by h sigma does not change the target left coset, so the
graph is well defined. Normalizing the two distinct values at source labels
0 and 1 gives exactly one representative per affine coset.

A transposition word w reaches H pi from H sigma exactly when

    sigma w = h pi, hence w = sigma^-1 h pi.

The minimum number of transpositions representing a permutation is its
number of letters minus its number of cycles, including fixed points.
This proves the claimed minimum over h in H in both directions. Additional
intermediate affine maps change no minimum: conjugation carries a
transposition to another transposition. Right multiplication by gamma sends
an edge labelled tau to one labelled gamma^-1 tau gamma, so it is a graph
automorphism. These statements use all transpositions, rather than a
restricted adjacent-swap generating set.

The classical modular input is correctly attributed. Cooper's Proposition 4
(printed pages 5--6) gives a modular collinear triple through every point of
an odd-field permutation graph. The proof's slope-product contradiction is
valid: if all nonzero slopes were distinct their product would be -1,
whereas the permutation of numerators and denominators makes it 1.
This primary text was read directly: [Joshua Cooper, *Collinear Triple
Hypergraphs and the Finite Plane Kakeya Problem*](https://people.math.sc.edu/cooper/collinear.pdf).

For f=pi sigma^-1, let g be an affine map agreeing with f at three positions.
The permutation sigma^-1 g^-1 pi therefore fixes the corresponding three
source positions. If its remaining support has size m>0, its transposition
length is at most m-1, hence at most p-4. The identity case has length zero.
This proves the general diameter bound, with no use of integer collinearity
for the matching modular triple. The F4 Frobenius control correctly shows
why odd characteristic cannot simply be dropped.

Thus, given an existing successful column ordering, the operation space
allows reaching one within the stated budget. Neither the matching triple
nor the diameter argument supplies that successful ordering's existence.
The p=5 and p=7 metric assertions follow from the complete finite checks;
no formula beyond those primes is inferred.

## 2. Finite geometry and the monotone compiler

The independent geometry engine first constructs every integer line in the
p-by-p grid having at least three grid points. A line has a primitive
integer normal and a canonical sign. Its bit mask contains every grid point
on it. The triple count of a board is then

    sum over lines ell of binom(|board intersect ell|,3).

Every collinear triple belongs to exactly one such line, so this is an exact
alternative to the producer's determinant and anchored-direction routines.
It retains longer-line multiplicities as well as all axis lines.

The referee enumerates all 6, 120 and 5040 column permutations at p=3,5,7,
and independently enumerates the entire 5040-column reordered p=7 class:
**10206 native boards** in total. Their cosets are recovered by grouping the
full permutations, rather than taking the certificate's universe on trust.
Every saved orbit histogram, minimum, affine forbidden mask and attained
affine pair agrees. The p=5 fixed-row class has four successes; the original
p=7 fixed-row class has none and has minimum count one.

The graph is independently generated by exchanging physical values and
renormalizing. BFS from every vertex verifies its radius profile. Cycle
distance agrees from the identity to every coset and also on designated
nonidentity pairs, testing the composition orientation. At p=7 the profile
is (1,21,98); the strict bad minimum's neighborhood is exactly the reported
multiset. All orbit minima used here come from the independent native engine.

After the specified row swap, independent reverse BFS in the directed
nonincreasing-score graph gives exactly

    monotone distance 0,1,2,3: 1,21,91,7 vertices.

All 120 successor records are replayed as literal operations: take the
attaining affine realization, exchange the two current physical column
values, and then apply the recorded following affine map. The final values
equal the successor's attaining realization; the score does not increase
and the stored distance drops by one. Following each entire word ends at
the single successful orbit. All nine strict-descent stalls make an
equal-score first step.

**A substantive scope control:** 66 recorded bare swaps increase the native
triple count before their affine completion. This does not refute the
theorem; it confirms that its completed-move definition is necessary.

The displayed repair word yields exactly the saved point list. Its row and
column incidence degrees are two, its graph is connected on fourteen
vertices, and its integer triple count is zero. Restoring the original row
addresses while retaining its column assignment gives six triples. Finally,
all **20328** specified words with at most one ordinary row swap, at most
one ordinary column swap and arbitrary affine columns fail. This bank does
not include free affine row maps, as the producer explicitly states.

## 3. All-prime native support barrier

Row and column operations commute across shores. Within either shore,
conjugating swaps moves all free affine maps to the front without increasing
the number of swaps. Write the two maps as A_r tau_r and A_c tau_c, and
let R,C be the supports of the residual permutations. Then

    |R|+|C| <= 2(k_r+k_c).

A complete nonaxis modular line has exactly one point in each row and
column. At least p-|R|-|C| of its points survive the residual permutations
unchanged. Their images under the final affine maps are actual standard
integer representatives on one complete modular line ell.

I checked the inherited elementary tangent-lattice argument directly in
`05-knowledge/results/continuing4_20260906_wildcard_affine_columns.md`,
Sections 5--6. Its transformed lattice has index 2p. With
h=floor(sqrt(2p)), the (h+1)^2 integer points of [0,h]^2 yield two equal
cosets, and their inverse-transformed difference is a nonzero tangent vector
of L1 length at most h. A shortest vector is primitive: its coordinate gcd
is less than p and invertible modulo p, so division would otherwise shorten
it. The functional vx-uy takes one residue modulo p on ell and at most
|u|+|v| occupied integer levels in the p-by-p box. Each exact level is an
integer line, which can retain at most two points of a successful board.
This proves the stated linear lower bound on swaps. At zero swaps the
equal-parity midpoint argument separately excludes success for p>=5.

For the sharper deletion-deficit formula, **ell_j means the occupied level
lengths of the complete final affine-image line ell** just introduced, before
deleting the points affected by R or C. It is not necessary that these
lengths equal those of the untransformed initial line. With that precise
typing, at least sum_j (ell_j-2)_+ points must be deleted, while at most
|R|+|C| original points were affected. The inequality follows. This small
wording clarification was applied in the producer's final report, which I
reread. It requires no change to a mathematical statement or computation.

The same level argument directly bounds agreement of any successful
p-point permutation graph with any affine field permutation. Neither the
agreement bound nor the deletion deficit is a sufficient repair criterion:
moved points and other directions can still create bad triples.

The referee separately searches growing integer diamonds for short tangent
vectors. It verifies every one of the **3190** nonaxis modular lines at
p=5,7,11,13,17,19,23,29,31 and all **3572** genuinely safe retained subsets
of those lines at p=5,7. These are controls for, not substitutes for, the
all-prime proof.

## 4. Reciprocal decoder and its failure boundary

The classical completed reciprocal graph is correctly credited to Cooper,
Section 3 of the primary paper above. Its new native statements are valid.
A line meets xy=1 over the field in at most two points. Any modular triple
must therefore contain the origin and an antipodal conic pair. Their
standard representatives have determinant p(x-y), which vanishes only for
x=y and x^2=1. Thus exactly the one reported integer triple remains, for
every odd prime. For p>=5 the graph cannot contain a complete modular line.

After swapping output zero with a, the moved points are P=(0,a),Q=(b,0),
where ab=1 modulo p. For two untouched conic points, the modular determinant
through P, multiplied by yy', is

    (y'-y)(y+y'-a).

The distinct y values therefore sum to either a or a+p. Substitution in the
integer determinant gives respectively the two reported ordinary-product
and complemented-product equalities. Transposition proves the Q cases;
the line through P and Q is exactly ax+by=ab. No entirely untouched triple
is possible, and the three types are disjoint by their moved points.
This proves necessity and sufficiency, including the carries.

The independent referee enumerates each literal triple after each of the
**34** zero swaps at p=3,5,7,11,13, compares all individual event decisions,
checks all three type multiplicities, and matches every saved triple set.
It recovers successes [1], [1,4], [1], [], [1], respectively. Consequently
p=11 is the first odd-prime failure of the zero-output-swap proposal; it is
not a failure assertion for all transpositions. Modular false positives
are present in the bank, verifying the need for integer products rather
than congruences alone.
