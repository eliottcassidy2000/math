## 2. A transitive-root inflation identity

Let X be rooted at x. Write

    h=H(X),
    F=sum_(paths P ending at x) H(X−vertices(P)).

Append a beaten only by x. Then append b beaten only by a. Thus b beats
all of X, and a is its sole in-neighbor. Call this graph Y.

Replace b by a transitive block B_t of t>=1 vertices, all with the same
outside comparisons as b. Keep a distinguished first vertex r of B_t.
The complete local construction is `X`, the vertex a, and `B_t`, with
`a→B_t→X` and a's unchanged incidences with X.

**Lemma1.**

    H(Y_t)=h+2^t F.                                 (1)

Proof. For t1 the inherited two-birth formula gives h+2F. A Hamilton
path can use B_t in at most two runs, since its only outside entrance
is a. A path with two nonempty runs has the form

    first B-run → P → a → second B-run → Q,

where P is a nonempty path of X ending at x and Q is its complementary
path, possibly empty. These are counted by F. Assigning the t ordered
block vertices to two nonempty increasing runs gives2^t−2 choices.
The one-run contribution remains the t1 contribution. Therefore the
total is `(h+2F)+(2^t−2)F=h+2^tF`. QED.

The same ordered-run proof gives the more general substitution formula

    H(Y[b→R])=h H(R)+F Q_R(2),                     (2)

where Q_R(2) is the ordered spanning two-path-cover count with empty
slots allowed. This is a physical graph substitution, not independent
addition of two numerical counts.

For t>=2, the first block vertex r has sole in-neighbor a, and a still
has sole in-neighbor x. With E_X the number of full X paths ending at x,
its marked coordinates include

    H=h+2^tF,       E=E_X,
    S=2^(t-1)F,     V=H+h,
    d=h+2^(t-1)F.                                 (3)

Here V is the weighted end-cover sum at r. To prove (3), delete r.
The remaining graph has H=h+2^(t-1)F. Its Hamilton paths starting at a
must traverse the remaining transitive block before X, so there are h.
The indegree-one birth identities give S=d−h and V=H+h. Paths ending
at r must end their X segment at x, giving E=E_X.

## 6. A complete small extension gate, with the physical source retained

For a fixed old graph T of order n, the following finite menu contains
**every** extension by two new vertices in which a specified new root
has indegree one. Its sole predecessor is either the other newborn or
one of the n old vertices. There are exactly `(n+1)2^n` labelled
possibilities in this specified-root universe.

Let E_i be full paths ending at i, S_i full paths starting at i, F_i
weighted path-end covers at i, and K_ij ordered spanning two-path covers
whose first path ends at i and second starts at j, both nonempty.
Endpoint subset DP computes these quantities once per named source.

If the final root v is beaten only by the other newborn u, choose the
set I of old vertices beating u. The exact two-birth count is

    H_child=h+sum_(i in I)(E_i+F_i)
                  +sum_(i in I,j notin I) K_ij.    (10)

Proof. The first birth has the usual endpoint/cut expansion. Its
weighted end-cover sum at u is `h+sum_(i in I)F_i`; its full start count
is `sum_(j notin I)S_j`. The second indegree-one birth adds the former
and subtracts the latter. The start terms cancel, giving (10).

newborn then has an arbitrary cut on the old graph, subject only to
losing to v. Apply the exact one-birth endpoint quadratic to this
