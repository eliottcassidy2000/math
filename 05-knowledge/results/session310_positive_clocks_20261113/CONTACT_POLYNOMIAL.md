# The contact polynomial of a packed triangle quotient

Let a tournament T have disjoint directed triangles B₁,…,Bₖ. First restrict to their union (or partition the entire tournament by also including singleton blocks). For any nonempty set S of block indices, let w(S) be the total weight 2^(number of cycles) of those odd-cycle packings on the induced union of S whose contact graph on S is connected. A cycle joins every pair of blocks it meets; one-block graphs count as connected even with no cycle. In particular w({i})=3 for a directed triangle and 1 for a singleton.

**Exact composition.** On a union U of the declared blocks,

    H(T[U]) = sum over set partitions π of U of ∏_(S∈π) w(S).

In an odd-cycle packing, the contact components give its unique set partition. Each component carries its own packing, and the weights multiply; conversely any choice of such components glues because their vertex sets are disjoint. This is the positive [odd-cycle collection formula](../../../01-canon/theorems/THM-002-ocf.md) with the connected-support coordinate retained. Equivalently, every connected weight is determined by smaller induced unions: subtract every nonsingle-block partition term from H(T[S]). In particular, a lower bound obtained by discarding some contact types never relies on subtracting estimated quantities. The paper's graph on individual cycles and this graph on their touched modules are different objects.

### The first interactions: 8, 3132, and the Sierpiński quotient

For two packed triangles, the connected weight is H(Bᵢ∪Bⱼ)−9: it counts packings with at least one cross-cycle. For uniform cross-arcs it is zero. For any mixed pair it is ≥8, with equality in 18 of the 512 cross-arc orientations. The lower bound has a short proof. In the 3×3 matrix of cross-arcs, each nonconstant row or column makes exactly one mixed directed triangle, since the other block is a directed 3-cycle. A nonconstant matrix has at least two such lines: if all rows are constant and differ, all three columns are mixed; if exactly one row is mixed, at least one column is mixed; otherwise there are already two rows. The union is strong because both blocks are strong and there are cross-arcs in both directions. [Moon’s vertex-pancyclicity theorem](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/canadian-mathematical-bulletin/article/on-subtournaments-of-a-tournament/5D7CCBE66AB07BB3F90F771D1CD3CD08) therefore requires every vertex to lie on a directed 5-cycle, so there are at least two such cycles. Alongside the original disjoint triangle packing (weight 9), the two new triangles and two 5-cycles contribute at least eight more, by positivity. A cross-matrix with just one 1 attains equality, as checked below. The [companion replay](verify_contact.py) checks all 512 using literal permutations; the full spectrum is 9,17,23,27,29,31,33,37,41,43,45.

If three blocks have uniform pairwise incidences and a cyclic quotient, all two-block weights vanish. The full count is the known 3159 for C₃[C₃] ([THM-3134](../../../01-canon/theorems/THM-3134-tournament-endpoint-jet-and-c3-newton-profile-transform.md)), so the connected weight is 3132=116·27. One quick derivation uses ordered visit covers (3,6,6) in each directed triangle; once the starting quotient block and number of runs are fixed, the cyclic order of runs is forced. In the older [Sierpiński–Collatz note](../sierpinski_steiner_collatz_tournaments_20261022.md), 3159 describes an exactly uniform 3-adic substitution. The new question here is what **price** one such core imposes inside an arbitrary larger tournament, even with no 3-adic global symmetry.

Let ℋ have as edges the mixed pairs and the uniform cyclic triples of packed triangles, with respective weights H(pair)−9 and 3132. Keeping only these connected types and the singleton weights gives

    H(T)/3^k ≥ Σ_(matching M in ℋ) ∏_(S∈M) [w(S)/3^|S|].

One may use 8/9 for every mixed pair and 116 for every uniform cyclic triple. The inequality still applies if T has unpacked vertices: ignore them in the original odd-cycle packing sum. It sums **all** disjoint choices, so it strengthens the earlier filter based only on the size of one matching. For example, if there are a mixed pairs and b uniform cyclic triples, the single-edge terms already force H/3^k≥1+8a/9+116b. In particular, below 117·3^k there can be no uniform cyclic triple. If all cross-arcs are uniform, the quotient there must be transitive. The condition concerns the union of the packed triangles, not the incidences of unpacked vertices.

In the fully uniform case there is also the independent all-order inequality H(T)≥3^k H(Q), where Q is the quotient on the triangle blocks. For each quotient packing, lift each of its odd cycles by independently choosing one of three vertices in each touched block. The lifted cycles have exactly the same weights; untouched blocks contribute 3 each from the choices of including their internal triangle or not. Every quotient packing therefore contributes exactly 3^k times its weight, and no two choices coincide. Repeated visits to a block give further positive contributions; if the quotient has a directed triangle there is already strict inequality by the local 3159 versus 81 comparison (more simply there exists a directed odd cross-cycle with more than one vertex in a block, obtained by replacing one quotient vertex by a three-vertex internal directed path). For a transitive quotient equality holds.

### Cross-thread path and what this buys

- The [September runner residual hypergraph](../../../01-canon/theorems/THM-4309-lrc14-endpoint-595-support-threshold-residual-hypergraph-compression.md) counts a response only at its own tagged row. Here a numerical excess is attached to its **exact touched block set**, and only disjoint contacts can be multiplied. Merely listing high local excesses would overcount.
- The neighboring October work on [stopped tails and cycle-count modules](../stopped_tails_deficit_six_and_cycle_modules_20261021b.md) retains enough state to bound every future placement, rather than treating unresolved cases as absent. This contact bound similarly discards known nonnegative sectors while retaining their tags for later refinement.
- The earlier [Gilbreath/Fermat tower](../gilbreath_fermat_platonic_20260926.md) had a valuable separation: a parity kernel is not the exact leading value, and identical arithmetic counts need not imply identical local geometry. Here the familiar Fermat prime 17 is not used for its primality, and the factor 13 in 3159 is not used as a congruence. Their useful content is the two explicit local contact weights.
- Session 309 retains named transfer sites, not just powers of two in a response. The contact budget can reject candidate cores *before* inspecting those sites. It gives no guarantees that candidates surviving the bound have the right marked response or realize an arbitrary exact target.

This proves a reusable rank/weight filter and an exact expansion, not Chan–Pak coverage.
