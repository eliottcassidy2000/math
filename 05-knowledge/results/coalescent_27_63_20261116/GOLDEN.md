# Golden-ratio arithmetic produces an exact two-digit square-sum carry

Take consecutive Fibonacci numbers u>v>0 with opposite parity
and (u,v)≠(1,1). Write w=uv and ε=u²−uv−v². Cassini, or the
swap (u,v)→(u+v,u), gives ε=±1. The Euclid triple is

$$
(a,b,c)=(w+\varepsilon,2w,u^2+v^2).
$$

These are precisely the positive pairs u>v with |u²−uv−v²|=1
(subject to the opposite-parity condition). For v≥2, u<2v
since otherwise the form is ≥v². Replace (u,v) by (v,u−v)
to reverse the sign, decrease the maximum, and keep positive
coordinates. Eventually v=1, and the only u>1 at norm ±1
is 2. Thus the golden irrational slope supplies a complete
integer descent, not just a sequence of good approximations.

With old square sums s=a², m=b² and s+m=c², the reflection
description of the graph on 1…m−1 gives a single Hamilton path.
The folded orbit is generated by translation by s in Z/m;
the Pythagorean legs are coprime. The lift to the full cyclic
orbit has the following exact base-b digit action. Since w is
even, m=b² and

$$
s=1+b(w/2+\varepsilon).
$$

Writing x=l+bh with 0≤l,h<b, each translation advances the low
digit l by one and the high digit h by w/2+ε plus the single
carry from l=b−1, all reduced in their own digit. After
exactly b steps the constant high drift sums to zero modulo b
and the high digit advances by one. Thus the finite passage
through every level is forced by an integer carry.

At (u,v)=(2,1) we get w=2, a=3, b=4, m=16, s=9. The
same reflection chain is precisely the familiar 8-to-9 path
on 1…15. The next pair (3,2) gives (5,12,13), a path on
1…143, and (8,5) gives (39,80,89). Though u/v tends to the
golden ratio, the square-sum rotation number s/m tends to
1/4, not to the golden ratio. This is distinct from the
[golden Sturmian Collatz circle, THM-4717](../../../01-canon/theorems/THM-4717-sturmian-circles-of-the-collatz-map-explicit-three-smooth-ladder-golden-circle-and-the-critical-slope.md):
there the golden slope codes a genuine irrational rotation.
It is also distinct from the previous consecutive-*leg* Pell
family. Their common 3,4,5 example does not identify the rays.

For a Chan–Pak repair, the useful template is not "irrational
slopes fill intervals" but "solve the low address and show
that each full low cycle carries to the next absolute high
address." The square-sum proof supplies this carry explicitly;
it does not supply a corresponding tournament gadget.
