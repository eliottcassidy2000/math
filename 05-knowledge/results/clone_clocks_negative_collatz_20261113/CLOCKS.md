## 1. Single clocks of every digit (PROVED parity law; FINITE-EXACT census)

**Lemma 1.** In the run expansion at a module vertex `u`, `(i−1)!` divides `a_i`.

*Proof.* A word with `i` runs of `u` has `i−1` internal excursions. Each starts at an out-neighbour of `u` and
ends at an in-neighbour, so any permutation of them gives another legal word with the same prefix and
suffix. The excursions are nonempty and disjoint, so `S_{i−1}` acts freely. ∎

Call `(S,u)` a **ternary clock of digit σ** when `a_{≥4}=0` and `a_2=3a_3`. Then with `A=a_3`,

    H(S[u←M]) = A·P_M(3) − σ·H(M)   for every tournament M,      H_m = A·3^m − σ,

so every step of module growth is the exact map `H↦3H+2σ`. This holds because
`3K_1+3K_2+K_3=P(3)` and `a_1=3A−σ`.

**Theorem 2 (odd digits).** Every transitive-clone ternary clock has `A` even and `σ` odd. Its count is
constant modulo 4 along the clock. In `r=(H+1)/2` the clock is `r↦3r+(σ−1)` with an even shift. In
particular no clone clock realizes the C2 digits 0 or 2 (`3H`, `3H+4`) exactly.

*Proof.* `A=a_3` is even by Lemma 1. `H_1=3A−σ` is odd (Rédei), so σ is odd. `H_{m+1}−H_m=2A·3^m≡0 (mod 4)`. ∎

**Census (FINITE-EXACT).** The universe is every tournament class from `gentourng` of order `n≤9`, and every
vertex `u` with matching number 2. The number of rooted ternary clocks of any digit is `0, 4, 30, 206, 1309`
for `n=5,…,9`. Data: `data/tern_all_orders.txt.gz`, generated by `c/ternscan.c`. Digit 17 is the most frequent.
The observed digits are all odd, from −1 to 421. The digit-1 clocks number 14, with amplitudes `60` (×6),
`100` (×2), `156` (×2) and `180` (×4). This agrees with Session 197's minimum and adds the next amplitudes.
A near-transitive scan (`c/backscan.c`) covers vertex orders `0<…<n−1` with at most 5 reversed arcs at `n=10`
and at most 4 at `n=11`, counting labelled back-arc sets. It finds 24,210 and 5,187 rooted clocks. The
order-10 digit-1 amplitudes are `{60,100,156,180,260,300,468,540}`, all divisible by 4.

**Readiness screen (FINITE).** None of the 14 digit-1 clocks of order ≤9 has, at `m=1`, any vertex of C2 digit 1
in either birth convention. The same holds for the 402 distinct order-10 near-transitive digit-1 clocks. So
none of these clocks is ready even at its first member. Exact ternary repeatability and unit readiness have not
been observed together.

## 2. Two clocks: definitions and the P-form (PROVED)

Put a ternary module at `u` and a second module at `v`. Let `a_{ii'}` count words with `i` runs of `u` and `i'`
of `v`, where runs of the same symbol are never adjacent. Then

    H(S[u←M, v←N]) = Σ a_{ii'} K_i(M) K_{i'}(N) = Σ c_{mm'} P_M(m) P_N(m'),

where `c` is the binomial inverse of `a`. The support is finite. If `v→u`, then `i'≤|N^-(v)|+1` and
`i≤|N^-(u)|+i'`, and symmetrically otherwise. `certify_twoclock.complete_table` uses caps beyond these bounds
and checks that the caps are never reached. A **two-clock** has `a_{ii'}=0` for `i≥4` or `i'≥3`, and both sectors
`X_{i'}(M)=Σ_i a_{ii'}K_i(M)` ternary-shaped (`a_{2i'}=3a_{3i'}`). Writing `X_1=A·P_M(3)−σH(M)` and
`X_2=α·P_M(3)−σ_2H(M)`,

    H(j,k) = 3^j(α·2^k + A−2α) − σ_2·2^k − (σ−2σ_2),

and the ternary step `j↦j+1` has digit `σ+σ_2(2^k−2)`. The **C2 line** (exact `3H+2` for every `k`, the shape
needed for `3^j(45·2^k+8)−1`) is `(σ,σ_2)=(1,0)`, i.e. P-form `αP_M(3)P_N(2)+βP_M(3)H(N)−H(M)H(N)`.

## 3. A certified two-clock on the +1 anchor (PROVED by complete finite table + run expansion)

Let Π be the 9-vertex tournament with order `a,v,c,u,d0,d1,x0,f0,f1`. All arcs go forward except
`d0→a, d1→a, f0→c, f1→c`. The complete table is `a_{11..32}=(361,360,120 | 180,180,60)` (rows
`i'=1,2`). Hence

    H(Π[u←M, v←N]) = 60·P_M(3)·P_N(2) + H(M)·H(N)       for ALL tournaments M, N,
    H(Π[u←TT_j, v←TT_k]) = 1 + 60·2^k·3^j.

Literal Hamiltonian-path counts agree for all 20 module pairs from `TT1..TT4, C3`, at orders up to 15.
Deleting `x0` gives the converse of Session 197's displayed source (checked by explicit isomorphism), with `v` at its second vertex. Its P-form is
`30P_M(3)P_N(2)−H(M)P_N(2)+H(M)H(N)`, and `N=TT_1` recovers `60P_M(3)−H(M)`. So **one inserted transitive
vertex** moves the pair `(σ,σ_2)` from `(1,1)` to `(−1,0)`. In the block family of `scripts/block_family.py` (bundle sizes
`p,q≤3`, `x≤2` middle vertices), only `p=q=2` gives a two-clock: `(σ,σ_2)=(1,1)` at `x=0` and `(−1,0)` at `x=1,2`.

## 4. Negative-integer Collatz by one arc reversal (PROVED)

In Π, `v→u`, and `u, v` differ only at `c` (`c→u`, `v→c`). Take the last clone `w` of the `v`-block. It is beaten
by the other `v`-clones and beats the whole `u`-block, which is exactly the position of a first `u`-clone. Reversing
the single arc `w→c` therefore turns `Π[u←TT_j, v←TT_k]` into `Π[u←TT_{j+1}, v←TT_{k−1}]` (`k≥2`). By §3,

    H ↦ (3H−1)/2,   equivalently   T(−H) = −H'   (the 3x+1 step on the negative integers).

The two module growths are `H↦3H−2` and `H↦2H−1`, which are `x↦3x+2` and `x↦2x+1` on `x=−H`. So every member's
`3x−1` ascending run is a chain of single-arc reversals until the `v`-block has one vertex. Section 3 of
[COLLATZ](COLLATZ.md) explains the anchor bookkeeping. `verify.py` §3 checks every `j≤3`, `2≤k≤4` literally.

    H(S[u←TT_j, v←TT_k]) = 1 + (α·3^j − σ_2)·2^k        (all j, k ≥ 1).

## 7. The tail-law route to the C2 line lands on digit 3 (FINITE-EXACT)

The inherited tail law ([THM-5126 (A)](../../../01-canon/theorems/THM-5126-unit-offset-rays-two-adic-lines-isolation-of-7-and-21-extension-mean-law.md))
gives a direct way to put a dyadic clock on a digit-1 clock `B=S[u←TT_j]`. Add a root `r` beating exactly a set
`O⊆B`, and a chain of `k` vertices beaten by all of `B` and beating only `r`. Then

    H = c_0 + M·2^k,   c_0 = H(B) − Σ_{e<f in O} W_ef,   M = Σ_{y in O} U_B(y).

The C2 line needs `c_0=A'3^j−1` together with a pure response `M=α'3^j`. `scripts/tail_c2_multi.py` and
`scripts/tail_constants.py` screen all 416 digit-1 clocks (complete through order 9 plus the near-transitive
order-10 clocks) and every `O` with `|O|≤3`, at `j=1,2,3`:

* `|O|=1` never gives a pure response. The ternary-shaped responses have constants `−1` or `−2`.
* every pure response, 155 tails, occurs for `|O|=2`, and **every one has offset digit exactly 3**. These are the Ψ shape
  of §3b; Ψ itself is the tail `O={2,6}` over `S_60`.
* the observed pairs (offset constant, response constant) are `(−1,−2), (−1,−1), (−3,0), (1,−3), (−3,−2), (−3,−1)`.
  The C2 target `(−1,0)` is absent.

So in this tail family a pure response forces the offset constant to `−3`, i.e. a `v`-once digit of 3, never 1.
That is the C2-line exclusion seen through the tail law. Why the constant is pinned at `−3` is not explained
here. No universal linear identity among the single-bin coefficients of `H(B)`, `U_e`, `U_f` and `W_ef` exists:
`scripts/single_bin_identity.py` fits one on 40 random rooted tournaments with nonzero residual. Any explanation must
use the clock structure of `B`.

### 8.1 Amplitude classes (FINITE-EXACT)

Every digit-1 clock found has amplitude `A≡4 (mod 8)` (416/416 single clocks; all digit-1 slices of two-clocks);
every digit −1 clock has `A≡0 (mod 8)` (342/342). Equivalently a digit-1 clock count satisfies `H≡3 (mod 8)`:
`v_2(H+1)=2`, i.e. its `3x+1` orbit begins with exactly two ascents. Larger digits mix classes. **Consequence.**
If the law holds, every `k`-slice `α2^k+β` of a C2-line two-clock is `≡4 (mod 8)`, which forces
`β≡4 (mod 8)` and `α≡0 (mod 4)`. All pure two-clocks found (`σ_2=0`) have `β≡0 (mod 8)`. This is a sharp
necessary condition and a search filter, not an exclusion: `β≡4` does occur for other digit pairs.

### 8.2 Crossing structure and the I/O factorization (PROVED for the factorization)

Call an arc `o→i` with `o∈N^+(u)`, `i∈N^-(u)` a *crossing*. In the complete census every digit-1 clock has exactly
four crossings forming two disjoint 2-stars, two "cherries" `s_1,s_2→t_1` and `s_3,s_4→t_2` (208 clocks, plus their
208 converses). In the order-10 data, small digits (`−1,1,3,5,7`) occur only with four crossings. Digit-1 clocks
with five crossings do occur in the extension data, so "digit 1 ⇒ four crossings" is false in general.

For a two-cherry skeleton, write `I=N^-(u)`, `O=N^+(u)`. Every arc between `I` and `O` points `I→O` except the four
cherry arcs. **Lemma (I/O factorization).** `a_3 = 2·F·G`, where

    F = Σ_{(P_0,Q_1,Q_2)} H(P_0)·s_{t_1}(Q_1)·s_{t_2}(Q_2),    G = Σ_{(R_1,R_2,P_3)} e_{\{s_1,s_2\}}(R_1)·e_{\{s_3,s_4\}}(R_2)·H(P_3)

are sums over ordered partitions of `I` and of `O`. Here `Q_i`, `R_i` are nonempty, `s_t(Q)` counts Hamiltonian paths
of `T[Q]` starting at `t`, and `e_X(R)` those ending in `X`. *Proof.* A word with three `u`-runs has two internal
excursions. Each must cross back from `O` to `I`, and the only such arcs are cherry arcs, so the two excursions
use the two different targets. The prefix therefore stays in `I` and the suffix in `O`. Each excursion is an
`O`-path ending at a source of its target, the cherry arc, then an `I`-path starting at that target. Both orders of
the two excursions occur, which gives the factor 2. ∎ (Check: `S_60` gives `F=2`, `G=15`, `a_3=60`.) More
generally every word is an alternation of `I`-blocks and `O`-blocks. `O→I` steps use cherry arcs, and each
`I→O` step is a *slot* that may hold a `u`-run. An empty slot is a direct arc, which is forbidden only from a target
into its own source. So the P-form is an inclusion–exclusion over forbidden slots. This **slot calculus** is the
proof route for §8.3.

### 8.3 The wrap law (FINITE-EXACT; conjecture)

In the two-cherry family with near-transitive sides (`c/cherries.c`, §8.4), **every** two-clock attached to a
digit-1 clock through order 11 has `(σ,σ_2)=(1,1)` and `A=2α`:

    H(S[u←M, v←N]) = P_N(2)·(α·P_M(3) − H(M)) + H(M)·H(N),     i.e.   H = (α·3^j − 1)·2^k + 1.

Through order 11 each digit-1 clock has exactly one such `v`. It is a non-target in-neighbour of `u`, beaten by
exactly one cherry target (usually its unique in-neighbour). At order 12 some clocks gain a second `v` in the same
local position, with `σ_2=2` and still `A=2α` (48 lines, `H=(α3^j−2)2^k+3`). Across all certified digit-1
two-clocks `v` never lies on a crossing. Four crossings give `σ_2∈{1,2}`; five crossings give `σ_2∈{3,9}`.
**Conjecture (wrap law).** For a digit-1 clone clock, every dyadic two-clock vertex has `σ_2≥1`. Together with the observation that cloning a crossing vertex changes the crossing graph with `k`,
this would imply the C2-line exclusion. It would also explain it: a dyadic module attached to a digit-1 ternary
clock always goes *outside* it. That gives `H−1=2^k·(α3^j−1)`, the unit two-clocks of §6, whose slopes run along
the C2 line, and never `H+1=3^j·F(k)`.

**The duality this exposes.** Swapping `2↔3` and `+1↔−1` exchanges the two shapes. The unit two-clocks are
abundant: `H−1=2^k·Y_j`, ternary inside, dyadic outside. The C2 line `H+1=3^j·F(k)`, dyadic inside and ternary
outside, never appears. The C2 map survives, but on the slope `c=Y_j` of the unit line, not on the count.

**Anchor anomaly and a pre-registered prediction.** A pure two-clock (`σ_2=0`) has constant `d=−σ`, its
*anchor*, the 3-adic limit of `H` as `j→∞`. All but one of the positive-anchor families found have `β=0`, so
`H−d = α·2^k·3^j`, and both clock maps fix `d` (`H↦3H−2d`, `H↦2H−d`). The exception, from the order-13
extension run, is a certified order-12 family `H=3^j(1720·2^k−400)+5`. It has P-form
`1720P_3P_2−400P_3H(N)+5H(M)H(N)`: the C2-line shape with a negative `β`, anchored at 5. Moreover `d = 2H(T)−H(T')` for every member `T`, where `T'` doubles the `v`-block. Across all
universes of §8.4 the anchors, with the number of **distinct** `(A,α)` families, are `1` (68), `3` (53), `5` (52),
`9` (31), `11` (8), `13` (8), `15` (17), `17` (5), `19` (2), `23` (1), `25` (1), `27` (3), `29` (2), `33` (3), plus
`−3` (11), `−5` (1). **The anchor 7 never occurs**, although its neighbours 5 and 9 have 52 and 31 families. 21 and 31
are also absent, but there only one to three families would be expected. Every anchor that occurs is a
Hamiltonian path count; 7 and 21 are proved exclusions. The assertion that they
are the only missing positive odd counts remains the OPEN Chan–Pak conjecture.
*Prediction, registered before any larger search:* (P1) no pure two-clock is anchored at 7; (P2) none at 21;
(P3) anchor 31 appears once the universe grows. A refutation of P1 needs one table. The anchor is not
`H(S−u−v)` or any other deleted-subtournament count tried, so no mechanism is proposed.

### 8.5 Why kernel composition cannot manufacture the C2 line (FINITE + elementary)

[THM-5133](../../../01-canon/theorems/THM-5133-flip-and-spin-kernel-signature-sign-tournaments-of-twisted-algebras-and-the-pg32-extremal-theorems.md)
(kind-pasteur S23) has two-port kernels with at most two visits per port, and a hyperbolic one of order 5 with
`H(K[A,B]) = 3Π_2(A)Π_2(B) − H(A)H(B)`. That gives `H+1 = 3·2^{a+b}`, the −1 anchor in the dyadic×dyadic world.
Substituting a clone clock `A=Y[u←M]` into such a kernel's port gives the C2 line only if `H(A)` and `Π_2(A)` are
both ternary-shaped: `H(A)=λP_M(3)+μH(M)` and `Π_2(A)=ρP_M(3)+τH(M)`, with `(μ,τ)` solving the kernel's 2×2
system (for the order-5 kernel, `μ=1`, `τ=0`). `c/pi2scan.c` computes `Π_2` along all 1,549 census clocks
(`data/pi2_census_clocks.txt.gz`). **None has a ternary-shaped `Π_2`.** Two bins let the module be visited four or
more times, so `4^j,5^j,6^j` terms appear. Kernels that read only `H(A)` give ordinal-sum-like products, and those
cannot reach the C2 line because clone digits are odd. For the 1,549 scanned
clocks, this excludes the tested composition route through THM-5133's dyadic
kernels. It does not prove an all-order composition exclusion or that every
possible C2-line two-clock must be primitive. The −1 anchor
exists for dyadic×dyadic (THM-5133); for ternary×dyadic it is exactly the open question.
