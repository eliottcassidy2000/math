# Arbitrarily long tight GP/P2 prefixes in genuine reduced shared DAGs

**PROVED counterexample family for arbitrary reduced legal states.** This does not
refute a stronger theorem about *minimum* circuits or logarithmic-dimensional
affine dispersers. It gives no improved circuit lower bound. The row names and
state invariants refer to the pinned provisional [ternary-pair elimination
candidate](https://github.com/Layr-Labs/circuitbreaker-llm-verifier/blob/4a61f024a4993592d97b49ca7d00979029b7fece/submission/elimination.md).
We establish the explicit transitions below directly, without assuming the
candidate's full theorem.

## 1. One block, retaining the actual children

Use six independent parameter bits x,y,a,b,c,z. Introduce affine equations

    A=x+a, C=x+b,

and gates

    G=xy, K=G+z, B=yb, T=ac, H=AC,
    O1=K+B, O2=O1+T, O=O2+H.

All sums are XOR; all products are AND. The two equations can be obtained by
moving two initial XOR gates by the candidate's M preparation. There are ten
charged objects. Each object lies in the output ancestry. The only child of G
is K. The other occurrences matter:

- x has children G,A,C;
- y has children G,B;
- a has children A,T;
- b has children C,B;
- c has child T, and z has child K.

The affine system is invertible with identity auxiliary matrix. There are no
constant/unary/repeated-pin objects, unused gates, or isolated unpaired parameters
whose unique child is an equation. Every minimal AND has independent affine
parents. Thus D1-D4 and M are exhausted before choosing G. This is *syntactic
reduction*, not global minimum circuit size.

Apply GP at G, excluding the pair corner (x,y)=(1,1). On the remaining three
points G=0, so K=z. Exactly G and K disappear. No other gate becomes unary,
constant or affine-parent XOR. The pair x,y is now marked. Its x member has the
exactly two equation children A,C, and its y member has the one gate child B.
The change in (s,I,q,a) is

    GP: (-2,-2,+1,-1).

Now use P2 on x, fixing it to the safe value zero. Delete A and C by substituting
A=a and C=b. The partner y becomes an unrestricted used parameter; no other
parameter loses its last occurrence. Exactly two charged objects disappear:

    P2: (-2,+1,-1,0).

The resulting circuit computes

    ab+yb+ac+z

with six gates. Its parameters y,a,b,c,z are all used. Hence one cycle has exactly

    (Delta s, Delta I, Delta q, Delta a)=(-4,-1,0,-1).

For W=s+5I+3q this is loss nine for one unit of affine safe-core dimension, with
no surplus at all. In particular an argument that automatically charges a third
P2 deletion has a concrete shared-DAG counterexample: A and C are equations whose
literal substitutions do not kill their common descendant H.

## 2. Arbitrarily many consecutive cycles

Take k disjoint blocks and XOR their O outputs, using k-1 more gates. List all
A,C equations first, then all G gates, then their K gates, then the residual
B,T,H and output gates in topological order. There are

    n=6k, s=11k-1, I=6k, q=0, a=6k.

The first remaining nonlinear gate is always the next unprocessed G. Every
processed block retains its three nonlinear residual products, so output XORs
do not become affine-parent XOR gates and trigger extra preparation/deletion.
No new D1-D4 deletion or earlier paired row intervenes beyond the stated P2.
After j cycles, exactly

    s=11k-1-4j, I=6k-j, q=0, a=6k-j.

Thus this is a genuine prefix of the specified priority elimination strategy,
not an abstract row word that ignores DAG sharing. It realizes k consecutive
tight cycles and a prefix of length proportional to n. Any universal statement
that such a prefix has bounded length, or must gain positive surplus per cycle,
is false for arbitrary reduced circuits.

## 3. These are actual loop states, not merely syntactic states

The block's Boolean function is the quadratic polynomial

    Q=x+xa+xb+ab+yb+ac+xy+z.

Its alternating polar form has rank four. Its radical is generated by
(x,y,a,b,c,z)=(1,1,0,1,0,0) and (0,0,0,0,0,1). Q is zero on the first generator
and one on the second. For k blocks, the direct-sum polar form has rank 4k,
radical dimension 2k, and a nonzero linear restriction of Q to the radical.

**Quadratic-flat lemma.** If a quadratic Boolean function on N variables has
polar rank 2r and is nonconstant on its radical R, its largest constant affine
flat has dimension N-r-1.

Proof: the direction U of a constant affine flat is isotropic for the polar
form, and U intersect R is contained in the kernel of the nonzero radical
linear functional. Hence dim(U intersect R)<=dim R-1. The projection of U to
the nondegenerate symplectic quotient has dimension at most r. Together these
give dim U<=N-r-1. Conversely choose a Lagrangian in the quotient, lift it, and
add ker(Q|R). This gives an isotropic U of dimension N-r-1. Q|U is linear and
vanishes on U intersect R, so it is represented by the polar pairing with some
translation vector. The corresponding affine coset is constant. This proves
both existence and the upper bound. Here Q(0)=0; adding a constant changes no
part of the argument.

Consequently our k-block function has exact largest constant-flat dimension

    d=4k-1.

It is nonconstant on every affine flat of dimension greater than d. At the start
of cycle j+1, for 0<=j<k, the safe-core dimension is 6k-j>=5k+1>=d+2. The GP
step reduces that dimension by one; P2 preserves it. All requested steps satisfy
the candidate's actual loop guard, and every domain restriction is a legal
injective affine fixing or its stated three-point restriction. No contradiction
with the affine-disperser hypothesis is being hidden by selecting an easy
function and an impossibly small d.

## 4. Exact limitations and surviving obligations

The circuits are **not minimum circuits**. The original block also equals

    (x+b)(x+a+y)+ac+z,

which is computable with seven gates: three preparatory XORs, two ANDs and two
output XORs. Combining blocks gives an 8k-1-gate circuit, strictly smaller than
11k-1. Therefore this family does not refute a strengthened deletion strategy
that invokes global minimality, semantic resynthesis, or a more selective gate
choice. It identifies precisely an assumption such a strategy must use. Conversely, starting from a minimum original circuit does not by itself make every restricted intermediate circuit minimum; a proposed use of minimality at later states needs its own justification.

Also d=(2/3)n-1 here, not O(log n). The construction does not show a long tight
prefix in the low-d regime needed for a sharp asymptotic circuit coefficient.
The naive 4n-9(d+1) conclusion is negative on this hostile family; the family is
used to test structural assertions, not the numerical lower bound.

A universal pair-stock conservation identity still survives. If all rows have
the exact pair changes in the source, and N_R counts row R, then

    N_GP = N_P0+N_P2+N_PC+N_PE+N_AP1+2N_AP2 + q_final-q_initial.

This follows by summing Delta q. Our prefix, starting and ending at q=0, has
N_GP=N_P2=k and every other listed count zero, so it saturates that conservation
law. Pair inventory alone cannot forbid the tight GP/P2 word. The additional
obstruction must use minimum-circuit structure, stronger low-d information,
semantic redundancy, or a genuinely richer transition invariant.

## 5. Exact checks

`check_tight_cycles.py` constructs the actual shared DAGs for k=1,...,20. It
checks all charged-object counts, every distinct child set used in a transition,
absence of extra D1-D4/M reductions at the relevant stages, and exact GP/P2
changes. It evaluates all final inputs for k=1,2, checks every original and
GP-domain block assignment, and enumerates all 26,387 affine flats in six
variables to confirm exact d=3 for one block. The all-k proof uses the displayed
polar rank argument, not extrapolation of that census.

Run normally and with `python3 -O`; `normal.json` and `optimized.json` are
byte-identical. All guards are explicit exceptions, not removable assertions.
No candidate submission code was executed.
