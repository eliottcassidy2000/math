## 2. From the plane lower bound to a finite construction target

Under the accepted unrestricted lower bound, graph compactness gives a
finite non-five-colourable unit-distance graph. Vertex-minimal extraction
makes its chromatic number exactly six. Real-closed-field transfer supplies
algebraic coordinates while preserving distinct points and all induced
edge/nonedge relations. These are three separate steps. ZFC alone is not
the algebraic-coordinate argument.

The [critical witness packet](plane_algebraic_witness_20261011.md) gives the
proof and an exact certificate interface: coordinates, reconstructed graph,
noncolourability refutation, positive colouring, and every marked vertex
deletion colouring. A fair complete algebraic search terminates under the
accepted premise. Neither a useful size bound nor a six-chromatic coordinate
list is supplied by that existence argument. The implemented tests use the
four-chromatic Moser spindle as an exact control.

The two possible values have different finite consequences:

| Assumed plane value | Finite algebraic witness | Search and extension consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Six | A six-critical graph exists; a seven-chromatic unit graph does not. | Every finite six-colourable fragment can be recoloured to leave a colour free around a proposed new point. |
| Seven | A seven-critical graph exists, and deleting/extracting inside it gives a six-critical graph in the same number field. | A finite six-colourable fragment exists whose every colouring uses all six colours around a proposed new point. |

A search for a finite non-six-colourable graph halts in the seven case.
Failure to find one in a finite search does not establish six. In either
case some finite real number field already attains the plane's full value;
there is no degree bound or effective stabilization test here.

## 3. Compress the permitted future interactions, with reconstruction

The [boundary palette packet](boundary_palette_compression_20261011.md)
stores all equality partitions of colours on an ordered boundary that
extend to full proper k-colourings, together with one full lift for each.
For fragments with exactly the same boundary and disjoint interiors,
gluing is intersection of these relations. Forgetting a port is existential
projection; adding a boundary edge is a filter. These are exact recursive
operations when the complete relations and geometric sidecars are retained.

The small hostile is two paths on three shared ports. Each uses two boundary
colours, but they force different pairs of ports equal. Their union is a
triangle. Counts alone predict compatibility incorrectly; named equality
partitions detect the conflict. Positive lifts alone also do not certify
that an omitted state is impossible.

For a k-critical unit-distance graph, deletion of a vertex leaves every
(k-1)-colouring using all k-1 colours on its neighbour circle. The local
unit graph on that circle is bipartite: its edges move angle by +/-60
degrees. For k>=4 the remaining graph must impose the extra colours.
Consequently the six/seven question can be attacked through realizable
circle-boundary relations rather than arbitrary whole-graph enumeration.
The exact finite extension equivalence is proved in that packet; it permits
recolouring the entire finite fragment, not just extending a chosen colouring.

The [multiplication-inspired routing packet](multiplication_certificate_routing_20261011.md)
uses a different compression. It releases a materialized ROOT word after
its final consumer, retaining the immutable source-labelled recipe and
all output obligations. In the stated fixed-order, no-recomputation model,
the optimum peak slot count equals maximum overlap of closed value-lifetime
intervals. A supplied nine-node proof DAG falls from nine to three payload
slots and from 147 to 83 abstract word cells; all emitted certificates agree.
Program storage, verifier transients and emitted-output storage are excluded
from these metrics. This is neither a Python memory benchmark nor new source
coverage.

The inspected multiplication source is pinned at
[`12a4934d15006e8c10f9b2264f13f8f341be802b`](https://github.com/CrocSwap/integer-mult-bounds/commit/12a4934d15006e8c10f9b2264f13f8f341be802b).
The transferable mechanisms are common-frame retiming, reuse after last
consumption, preservation of physical labels, and coverage of both inherited
obligations and currently consumed frames. Its retained all-size and analytic
interfaces remain premises of the published bound. Our program transports
that accounting discipline, not the external complexity conclusion.

## 4. The incoming source measure explains what still needs proof

For source index m, the incoming renewal packet constructs the rational
atomic prior nu_r(m)=(m+1)^(-r)-(m+2)^(-r). For q>m, other indices in its
congruence cylinder contribute at most (r+1)/q^(r+1). At r=2 the alias bill
is 3/q^3, and q^3 times the true bill tends to 2*zeta(3). Every source has
a positive atom; refining one binary bit divides the proved bill by eight.

That settles source retention for this chosen measure. The needed extra
predicate is convergence: an independently justified ROOT mass above the
alias bill would force the marked source itself to be ROOT. Reusing only
certificates for other sources cannot cross the bound. Equation (1) is a
legitimate way to move a named child certificate onto that source; slot
reuse and positive atomic weights alone do not perform that move.

The common rule across the lanes is exact but modest:

| Object | What the compression preserves | What must remain separate |
|---|---|---|
| Graph boundary relation | Extension through declared ports at a fixed palette | Geometry, completeness, and a lift |
| Collatz common-future receipt | ROOT transport along exact guarded words | Child ROOT proof and original-source payment |
| Scheduled proof payloads | All emitted certificates in the declared schedule | Recipes, future consumers, total storage model |
| Atomic source cylinder | The marked atom with an explicit alias bound | Independent ROOT-supported lower measurement |

Graph noncolourability has finite witnesses by compactness. An infinite
nonterminating Collatz orbit need not have a finite certificate of its
nontermination. The finite-obstruction extraction argument therefore does
not transfer to Collatz merely because both use graphs or recursive rules.
