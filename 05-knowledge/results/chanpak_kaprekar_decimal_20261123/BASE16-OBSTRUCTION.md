# A second Kaprekar obstruction: base 16 cannot make its first jump

**PROVED scoped / FINITE-EXACT.** In width two, the even-base gap iterate sends \(1\) to \(B-1\). The corresponding actual heights would have to jump \((B-1)\to(B-1)^2\). In base 16 this would be \(15\to225\), and **there is no such single-arc edge at any order**.

## The singleton-endpoint valuation test

Suppose a reversal has endpoints that are singleton SCCs in the starting tournament. Let \(M\) be the tournament in the interval strictly between them. If \(M\) is empty, the edit just swaps singleton factors and preserves height. Otherwise that interval changes from an ordinal join of two endpoints around \(M\) to a cyclic sandwich; its height changes from \(H(M)\) to \(H(M)+Q_2(M)\). Other components contribute a common odd factor. The 2-adic valuation of the whole height difference is therefore \(v_2(Q_2(M))\). For \(|M|\ge2\), \(4\mid Q_2(M)\): in the [odd-cycle-packing expansion](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.05569) at two slots, a packing with \(u\) unused vertices and \(c\) cycles contributes \(2^{u+2c}\), and \(u+2c\ge2\). Consequently a difference with valuation one forces a single interior vertex, and the whole height ratio must be three. For first jumps in widths two with \(B\equiv0\bmod4\), the difference \((B-1)(B-2)\) has valuation one. Only \(B=4\) can have the required ratio. In particular the first jumps in bases 16 and 40 cannot be implemented by toggling two singleton SCC vertices. This alone does not rule out other edits in base 40.

## Why only twelve vertices need testing in base 16

A height-15 starting tournament has either a single strong 15-core (at order five or six), or a 3-core and a 5-core (orders three and four). The lower order of a 15-core follows from the order-four maximum of 5, and the upper order from [Busch’s bound](https://www.combinatorics.org/ojs/index.php/eljc/article/download/v13i1n3/pdf). An edit internal to one of these cores involves at most seven active vertices even with the other nontrivial core retained; singleton pads can be discarded. An edit between components with changed height merges the intervening SCC interval. If it misses all nontrivial components both endpoints are singleton, already ruled out. If it contains all the old nontrivial components, the merged component has height 225 and hence order at most 11. Otherwise it contains exactly one old core. Merging the 3-core must make a strong 45-core (order at most eight), leaving the untouched 5-core (order four). Merging the 5-core must make a strong 75-core (order at most nine), leaving the untouched triangle. In either case the marked edit fits on at most twelve vertices. Delete external inactive singleton pads, then pad again by ordinal singletons to order exactly twelve.

The C++ replay identifies two strong 15-core isomorphism classes at each of orders five and six by anchored enumeration and canonical relabelling, and unique 3- and 5-cores at their respective orders. Distribute singleton pads before and after the strong 15-core, or before, between and after the two nontrivial cores in either order. This gives 72 representatives. Test all 4752 single-arc reversals: no result has height 225.

Replay: `c++ -O3 -std=c++20 kaprekar-b16-obstruction.cpp -o /tmp/kaprekar-b16 && /tmp/kaprekar-b16`.

When (B+1) is prime, every unaffected SCC factor divides (B-1), since consecutive gaps (a,a') are coprime. Base four supplies a positive control: place a directed triangle before a transitive triple, and flip the triple's long arc to cycle between ordinary heights 3 and 9. The other base-16 orbit is not settled here.
