# A square-sum origin, the ports1/3/8, and canonical Fibonacci numerals

**Status: proved definitions, classical numeration, and independently verified finite graph boundaries.** No new number field, arbitrary Hamiltonian extension theorem, or historical-priority claim is made.

## The proposed origin has two precise interpretations

If G32 represents the first **positive** natural number1, the natural zero is G31. That zero is a nontrivial graph with exactly two disconnected spanning2-factors; the arrival of32 permits the unique Hamiltonian cycle. With graph objects G_(31+r), ordinary arithmetic is transported faithfully from r>=0. If G32 instead represents zero, use G_(32+r); both conventions and their opposite parity conventions are explicit in [ORIGIN_MODEL.md](origin_model/ORIGIN_MODEL.md).

The graph-order semigroup is another useful, distinct object: adjoining arithmetic zero to the Hamiltonian orders gives {0} union [32,infinity), minimally generated by32,...,63, with conductor32 and Frobenius number31. None of these arithmetic definitions is a graph-gluing operation.

## The triple has exact arithmetic structure

1,3,8 are the first three even-index Fibonacci weights. Their pairwise products plus1 are4,9,25, making a classical regular Diophantine D(1) triple. Because the first label is1, two product conditions are also the square-sum closing edges1–3 and1–8. But3+8=11: the graph triple is a fork, not a triangle.

The [numeration proof](numeration/PROOFS.md) develops the classical Chung–Graham system on weights1,3,8,21,...: digits0,1,2 with no factor2 followed by only1s and then2. Every nonnegative integer has a unique canonical expansion. Explicit positive carry rules terminate, and the expansion uniquely minimizes the number of even-Fibonacci summands. The legality/parity recognizer has eight live states and a rejecting sink; nine states are minimal for the stated leading-zero-allowed language.

The [Beatty and orbit supplement](numeration/BEATTY_BRIDGE.md) gives the exact canonical shift

    P(n)=ceil(beta*n), beta=(3+sqrt(5))/2,
    parent(n)=floor(n/beta)=3n-P(n).

The remainder is a legal digit. The internal coordinate rho=P(n)-beta*n lies in[0,1); appending digit d sends rho to(rho+d)/beta. Digit2 is legal exactly in the Free part rho<beta-2. This is an explicit arithmetic mechanism for retained phase, not a new square-sum adjacency law.

The integral norm C(n)=n^2-3nP(n)+P(n)^2 is preserved by the shift. Its norm-one orbit is exactly1,3,8,21,... . General shift orbits give D(C) triples; equal norm does not identify an orbit (5 and7 both have norm11). Canonicalization matters: raw22 and canonical100 both have value8, but their raw zero-shifts have different values22 and21.

## Three different structural beginnings

The repository uses **ninja / weak B pair** for a different interface from the original Gerbicz nice pair. Ninja pairs begin1,3 and end at8 for even order or10 for odd order; their first pair is42/43. Original nice pairs start1 and end at3 or8, and begin at41/42. Both preserve common-label positional parity. The [side-by-side taxonomy](pair_taxonomy/README.md) independently checks the small-order distinction and uses the previously proved weak-B induction, not a concurrent reserved theorem.

The [origin-pair certificate](origin_pairs/README.md) distinguishes:

1. **Order32:** the first square-sum Hamiltonian cycle, uniquely realized.
2. **Orders41/42:** the first original nice pair, with six normalized pairs and three shared positional-phase masks.
3. **Orders43/44:** the first realizable pure-insertion successor after32.

The32→33 transition already needs exactly two old-edge deletions. The40/41 nice-pair failure is detected by labels6,7,30,33; no projection to three labels suffices. Positional parity is not vertex-label parity, and the weak B pairs used by later constructors have different endpoints from these original nice pairs.

Indeed no original nice pair's two closed cycles can be related by pure insertion at any order; [the proof](origin_model/ORIGIN_MODEL.md) uses the immutable odd/even role of edge1–3. The graph can grow while its particular certificate must be rearranged.
