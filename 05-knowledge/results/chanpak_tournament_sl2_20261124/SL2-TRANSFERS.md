# Where SL₂ lives in a tournament path metagraph

**PROVED.** A tower of directed triangles realizes every real \(2\times2\) matrix as a one-layer weighted Hamilton-path transfer, and multiplication is literal path concatenation. On unweighted tournaments two specified one-arc reversals realize the two positive elementary shears on **framed paths** (paths that visit ordered layers contiguously and enter each at one of two specified vertices). The resulting framed transfer graph has the Calkin–Wilf tree as its projective positive orbit, the group \(\operatorname{SL}_2(\mathbb Z)\) generated by the matrices when actual inverses are allowed, and the groups \(\operatorname{PSL}_2(\mathbb F_p)\) for \(p\ge5\) as congruence quotients. None of these is asserted to be the automorphism group of a tournament or the full unframed metagraph.

## A physical two-state interface

Each layer is a directed triangle \(0\to1\to2\to0\). The entry port is 0 or 1. There is exactly one internal Hamilton path per entry: it follows the cycle, ending at 2 or 0 respectively. Denote these exits \(e_0=2,e_1=0\). Ordinally join the layers. On the four cross arcs from an exit \(e_i\) to entry \(j\) of the next layer put the independently chosen weight \(M_{ij}\); give weight zero to the other cross arcs at that boundary and one to internal arcs. Orient all interlayer arcs forward. Starting at port \(i\) in the first layer and ending at exit \(e_j\) in the last, the formal weighted sum over all Hamilton paths is
\[
 (M_1\cdots M_s)_{ij}.
\]
In an ordinal join a full path has no way to revisit or skip a layer. Inside each layer the initial vertex determines the full internal word. The third entry/exits are absent from the nonzero terms. Hence the identity is coefficient-wise over the integers, valid over any commutative ring, not a numerical analogy.

The same mechanism realizes arbitrary \(d\times d\) transfers: in each layer take \(\max(3,d)\) vertices on a directed cycle, give its arcs weight one, give all other internal arcs zero, and mark \(d\) entries with their corresponding predecessor exits. Thus it realizes \(\mathrm{SL}_d(\mathbb R)\) at the level of weighted composition, and \(\mathrm{SL}_d(K)\) over any field \(K\). Group inverses are transfers with signed weights when needed; they do not remove vertices from a tournament.

## Two actual unweighted arc choices

Without weights, start from the same ordinal layout and, at each consecutive layer pair, reverse **just** one of two contacts:
* reverse \(e_1\to0_{\mathrm{next}}\) to get \(U=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&1\\0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)\);
* reverse \(e_0\to1_{\mathrm{next}}\) to get \(L=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\1&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)\).

On framed words these reversals give exactly the listed transition matrices, since no framed word can traverse a backward interlayer arc. These are strong tournaments as soon as there are two layers: every layer is strong, and each consecutive pair has arcs both ways. Growing the labelled frame by a triangle and choosing either physical reversal gives a binary tree with one mark identifying each branch. Different words are distinct labelled frames, regardless of what an unlabelled quotient forgets.

On count columns the maps are \((x,y)\mapsto(x+y,y)\) and \((x,y)\mapsto(x,x+y)\); one can read products algebraically in the corresponding reverse chronological order. The root \((1,1)\) gives all positive coprime pairs once: compare the coordinates and subtract the smaller from the larger until equality. This is the [Calkin–Wilf/Stern traffic](https://arxiv.org/abs/1406.2054). The determinant is one in the transfer, not in an individual weighted path count.

**Lost coordinate:** blocks are no longer tournament modules after their cross marks reverse. Ordinary Hamilton paths can mix layers, as can paths with the third internal entry. Already on two triangles each reversal gives **17** ordinary paths, of which eight visit the triangles contiguously in the prescribed order and only three also satisfy the two-port frame. Thus the positive group action is an exact encoding in a selected path language, not a construction of arbitrary scalar counts in the Chan–Pak problem. Removing the frame without introducing extra paths is a separate problem.

## From real operations to an infinite family of finite simple quotients

At real weights the matrices \(U(t)=I+tE_{12},L(t)=I+tE_{21}\) generate \(\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb R)\) by elementary row reduction. Integer words using \(t=\pm1\) generate \(\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb Z)\). The unweighted positive words alone give the whole group modulo any \(m\): inverses are positive powers in the finite image. Over each \(\mathbb Z/p^a\) some entry in the first column is a unit; swap rows with the determinant-one elementary rotation if necessary, eliminate, and express \(\operatorname{diag}(u,u^{-1})\) as elementary matrices (for example multiply the two antidiagonals \(U(u)L(-u^{-1})U(u)\) and \(U(-1)L(1)U(-1)\)). Use CRT on the parameters of each elementary operation to perform it in one component and the identity in all others. This shows \(\operatorname{SL}_2(\mathbb Z/m)\) is generated, not just embedded in a larger group. In particular the modular framed transfer graphs, after quotienting scalar central matrices over a prime field, give all \(\mathrm{PSL}_2(\mathbb F_p)\) (\(p\ge5\), the classical simple family). The action on \(\mathbb P^1(\mathbb F_p)\) is faithful for the projective quotient, since a projective transformation fixing three points is the identity.

There are three different group locations:
1. **Individual tournament:** every automorphism group is odd (an involution would reverse the edge between exchanged vertices). In particular the even groups just described cannot be the automorphism group of a single board. The actual \(\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb R)\) has no nontrivial homomorphism to any finite permutation group: each additive real shear group is divisible abelian so its finite image is trivial, and the shears generate. Thus it cannot act nontrivially on each finite layer by any size-preserving permutations, even without continuity.
2. **Finite collection of contact states:** the [Golay edit space](../chanpak_h23_golay_chains_20261124/DODECAD-CHAIN.md) carries a genuine affine \(2^{12}:M_{24}\) metric action; its count gate retains \(M_{12}\) in the dodecad placement and no nonidentity action in the two-apex placement. The operation is relabeling or translating **edit words**, not composing path matrices. The [classification of the whole fixed-spine height stabilizer](SPINE-STABILIZER.md) is more restrictive still.
3. **Composition across sizes:** elementary matrices multiply as path transfers in growing frames. Reduction of that operation gives the infinite finite-group family; no sporadic group is needed as a generator. In the native two-slot construction the [determinant-seven seam](FAREY-SEVEN.md) gives a true projective map but only a thin set of physical slopes. Formal real completion and a dense stock of actual tournament states are not interchangeable.
