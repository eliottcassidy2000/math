# Cayley–Dickson signs as literal tournament bit circuits

**Status: elementary all-level proofs and finite exact certificates.** The one-sided multiplication-group order is prior mathematics: Kirshtein's Theorems 34–35 describe it. This note gives explicit Boolean coordinates, a degree hierarchy, and a scheduled implementation by literal tournament arc flips. It does not identify the algebra with a tournament, realize every multiplication as a single flip, or produce autonomous dynamics in the blue/black complement-line metagraph.

## 1. Coordinates that survive every doubling

Use exactly the S167 convention

    (a,b)(c,d)=(ac-conj(d)b, da+b conj(c)).

The standard basis of the real algebra after n doublings is indexed by V_n=F_2^n. Write

    e_a e_x = (-1)^{f_a(x)} e_(a+x),
    L_a(s,x)=(s+f_a(x),x+a).

Here s is a sign bit, all additions are modulo two, and the state (s,x) means (-1)^s e_x. This is a finite signed-basis action, not multiplication of arbitrary algebra elements. Every L_a is a permutation, even in dimensions where the algebra has zero divisors.

For n=d+1, split a=(u,r), x=(y,t), with u,y in F_2^d. Put delta(y)=1 if y is nonzero and 0 otherwise, and

    c_0(y)=0,
    c_u(y)=1+1_(y=0)+1_(y=u)   (u nonzero).

The Cayley–Dickson rule gives the exact recurrence

    f_(u,r)(y,t) = f_u(y)+r delta(y)+t(c_u(y)+r).       (1)

To check it, the four cases r,t=0,1 give, respectively, the old product, its reversed product, the old product times the conjugation sign of e_y, and the reversed product times that sign and -1. Distinct nonzero basis units anticommute; equal basis units commute. This gives c_u. Both statements also follow inductively from the displayed doubling rule.

The same induction proves

    f_a(x)+f_a(x+a)=1  (a nonzero),
    L_a^2=Z,  Z(s,x)=(s+1,x),  L_a^(-1)=ZL_a.        (2)

For the induction in (2), if u is nonzero, use c_u(y+u)=c_u(y) and delta(y)+delta(y+u)=1+c_u(y); if u=0, then r=1 and the identity follows immediately from (1).

## 2. An explicit kernel, not just its order

Let G_n be generated by all L_a. Its index action is a translation, so it maps onto V_n. For n>=3 its kernel is exactly

    K_n={D_(g,c): (s,y,t) -> (s+g(y)+ct,y,t),
                      sum_y g(y)=0, c in F_2}.        (3)

Thus g is an arbitrary even-parity truth table on d=n-1 input bits. In particular

    dim_F2 K_n=2^(n-1),
    |G_n|=2^(2^(n-1)+n).                              (4)

This recovers a known one-sided Cayley–Dickson loop multiplication-group order, rather than claiming that order as a discovery. The useful additional interface here is the explicit truth table in (3).

**Proof.** A diagonal sign function is conjugated by any L_a simply by translating its argument. The space in (3) is translation-invariant, including top-bit translations: adding a constant to g keeps its parity even because its domain has even size.

Consider the zero-translation defect

    D_(a,b)=L_(a+b)^(-1)L_aL_b,
    k_(a,b)(x)=f_b(x)+f_a(x+b)+f_(a+b)(x).             (5)

Write a=(u,r), b=(v,q). Formula (1) shows that the coefficient of t in k_(a,b)(y,t) is

    c_v(y)+c_u(y+v)+c_(u+v)(y)=c_u(v),                (6)

which is constant. The bottom-half truth table k_(a,b)(y,0) has even parity. Indeed every row f_u on F_2^d has even parity for d>=2: summing (1) over its last bit reduces to the even parity of c_u. In (5) the remaining delta contributions have total parity q+r+(r+q)=0, the c_u contribution has even parity, and constant contributions sum to zero. Thus every defect belongs to (3). Normality and (5) reduce any word to one element of (3) and one L_a, proving the upper bound.

For the reverse inclusion let h=(0,1), the new doubling index. Equation (2) gives the constant sign function 1. For u nonzero, direct substitution into (5) gives

    k_((u,0),h)(y,t)=c_u(y).

Consequently 1+c_u=1_(y=0)+1_(y=u) belongs to the kernel. These functions, as u varies over the nonzero lower indices, form a basis of all even-parity functions g. Finally choose distinct nonzero u,v; such a pair exists because d>=2. By (6) their defect equals g(y)+t with g even. Cancel the already available g to obtain t. This proves (3). Distinct translations have disjoint cosets, giving (4). QED.

The exceptional associative levels are |G_1|=4 and |G_2|=8, not the formula obtained by substituting n=2 into (4). All statements using (3) are restricted to n>=3.

A path summary that predicts transport on **every** initial signed basis state must distinguish the |G_n| permutations. Therefore it requires at least 2^(n-1)+n bits in the worst case. A single signed unit uses only n+1 bits. For n=3,4,5,6,7 the exact transport-memory requirements are 7,12,21,38,71 bits. This is a lower bound for universal summaries of these particular path operations, not a statement about all descriptions of Cayley–Dickson multiplication.

## 3. The doubling tower is a Boolean-degree tower

**Theorem.** For every n>=2 and every nonzero a, the Boolean polynomial f_a has algebraic degree exactly n-1.

Use the unique square-free polynomial over F_2 representing a truth table, usually called its algebraic normal form. The quaternion case n=2 is checked directly: the three nonzero rows are y, y+t, and t. For n=d+1>=3, write

    1_(y=0)=product_j(1+y_j),
    1_(y=u)=product_j(1+y_j+u_j).

Their degree-d terms cancel. The degree-(d-1) part of c_u is

    sum_j u_j product_(i != j) y_i.

Using (1), the degree-d homogeneous part of f_(u,r) is therefore

    r product_j y_j + t sum_j u_j product_(i != j) y_i.      (7)

It is nonzero whenever (u,r) is nonzero. All other terms have degree at most d by induction. This proves the theorem.

Hence complex and quaternion updates use linear sign logic, octonion updates require quadratic terms in these coordinates, sedenion updates require cubic terms, and each further doubling increases the maximum control degree by one.

The exact circuit is elementary. First implement s <- s+f_a(x): for each monomial in the normal form, apply a NOT to s controlled by the indicated index bits. Then flip the index bits in the support of a. Since f_a(0)=0 and its full degree-n monomial vanishes, at most 2^n-2 sign gates and n index gates suffice for n>=2. This is a gate-count upper bound, not an optimal circuit synthesis theorem.

There is a matching **restricted** degree obstruction: circuits using only sign-target gates of control degree at most q and unconditional translations of the index register cannot implement a nonzero L_a when q<n-1. Composing such gates only adds translated sign polynomials and cannot raise their degree beyond q. This restriction matters. With additional work bits, or index gates controlled by data, low-arity reversible gates can synthesize higher-degree operations; no general lower bound against Toffoli circuits is claimed.

## 4. Literal tournament hardware inside one profile fiber

S167 supplies two fixed labelled strong six-vertex tournaments

    B_0 = mask 153,   B_1 = mask 185.

Pairs are ordered 01,02,...,45 and a set bit means the smaller label points to the larger. The two masks differ in exactly the arc {1,2}. They are nonisomorphic and share the entire path-cover profile

    P(z)=29z+131z²+162z³+77z⁴+15z⁵+z⁶,
    F(r)=r⁶+12r⁴+16r².

Here p_j in P counts partitions into j directed nonempty paths and F(r)=sum_j p_j(r)_j counts r labelled path slots, empty slots allowed. The fixed masks are S167's class A_0 and a relabelled representative of A_2. Choosing this binary edge avoids the relabelling holonomy of the full ternary triangle.

For a bit vector b=(s,x_1,...,x_n), define the labelled tournament

    T_b=B_(b_0) ⊕ B_(b_1) ⊕ ... ⊕ B_(b_n),

where all cross-block arcs point from earlier blocks to later blocks. Then:

- A bit flip is literally one reversal of the designated internal {1,2} arc. No relabelling is performed.
- Distinct bit vectors give distinct isomorphism classes. The strong components are the ordered six-vertex blocks and B_0,B_1 are nonisomorphic.
- The class family induces the binary cube Q_(n+1). Internal reversals change at most one block; a cross-block reversal merges the intervening strong components and leaves this family.
- Every state has 6(n+1) vertices, H=29^(n+1), and F(r)=(r⁶+12r⁴+16r²)^(n+1).

The profile product follows by restricting every labelled path slot to the successive ordinal blocks. These are actual labelled tournaments and actual arc reversals, not extra observation-state labels attached to an unrelated base graph.

Apply the circuit in Section 3 to these blocks. Each active gate flips precisely one actual arc, conditional on the encoded control bits; each inactive gate leaves the tournament alone. All intermediate states remain in the common profile fiber. Thus the full signed-basis transport group has a literal, reversible, scheduled macro implementation.

If an external controller can evaluate the whole predicate f_a(x) at once, an even shorter schedule flips the sign arc exactly when f_a(x)=1, then flips the index arcs in the support of a. On a specified initial state this uses exactly

    weight(a)+f_a(x)

arc reversals, equal to the endpoint Hamming distance inside this labelled cube. With bounded-arity control gates the normal-form implementation instead supplies a fully specified gate decomposition and may use more reversals.

If strongly connected whole tournaments are required, the inherited S167 degree-separated cyclic wrapper gives order 30(n+1)+10. The guards and cross arcs are fixed, so the same literal core arc flips implement the circuit. The full profile remains common, although its Hamilton count is not asserted to equal 29^(n+1).

For the octonion example n=3, there are 16 distinct encoded states on 24 vertices, all with H=29^4=707281. The S167 associator transport L_3^(-1)L_1L_2 acts as a controlled flip of the sign block by the index-4 bit. It fixes the encoded e_0 and changes the encoded e_4 to the distinct encoded -e_4. Thus the former formal sheet defect becomes observable as a difference between endpoints of actual profile-preserving tournament flip itineraries.

This is a macro implementation under an explicitly supplied controller and schedule. The transported permutations generally do **not** preserve cube adjacency and are not automorphisms of the tournament flip graph. Nothing here specifies an intrinsic autonomous tournament evolution, a local CA rule on tournament vertices, or an identification with the blue/black complement-line metagraph.

## 5. Why the single-flip version is genuinely harder

On signed basis states, draw an undirected edge for every positive generator step L_a, a nonzero. Because L_a^(-1)=ZL_a, for a fixed starting signed state and a different basis index, the outgoing and incoming L_a edges reach the two possible signs at that index. No generator edge joins the two signs at the same index. Hence this state graph is exactly

    K_(2,2,...,2), with 2^n parts,

also called the cocktail-party graph. Its vertex and edge counts are 2^(n+1) and 2^(n+1)(2^n-1), and its clique number is 2^n.

For n>=2 this graph contains a triangle. The flip graph of globally fixed labelled tournaments is a binary cube and is bipartite. Therefore no injective implementation of all these generators by literal single-arc flips on fixed labelled tournaments exists at any tournament order. This obstruction applies even without a profile constraint.

If instead states are required to be distinct tournament **isomorphism classes**, triangles may appear through relabelling. An injective class realization of **all** left-generator steps by single tournament arc flips, while preserving a common profile, would require a common-profile clique of size at least 2^n. At the octonion level this means eight pairwise adjacent common-profile tournament classes. The S167 ternary Hamming construction has clique number three, so it cannot supply that realization for n>=2, however many ternary blocks are used. Our binary hardware intentionally realizes the generators as paths, not single edges.

This is a necessary condition and a barrier for the specific Cartesian construction. It is not a proof that large common-profile cliques cannot exist elsewhere in the tournament class graph.

The verifier uses the standard library, builds the multiplication table independently by a dense integer-vector implementation of the displayed Cayley–Dickson product, checks every basis product through n=5, tests every kernel defect and normal-form circuit through n=7, independently enumerates full permutation groups through n=4, verifies the exact kernel truth-table span, checks the physical six-vertex binary edge and its profiles by two algorithms, and verifies the octonion literal itineraries.
