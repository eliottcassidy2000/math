## 1. Finite critical values: the all-degree step is valid

The proof needs finitely many critical **values**, not finitely many critical
points. Over an algebraic closure of the coefficient field, the algebraic
critical locus of a nonconstant polynomial has finitely many irreducible
components. It has no two-dimensional component in characteristic zero.
On any curve component, the restriction of the polynomial has zero
differential. If it were transcendental, the component's function field would
be a finite separable extension of the one-variable field generated by that
restriction; its derivative could be extended to give derivative1, a
contradiction. Thus the value is constant on each component. Point components
cause no difficulty. These finitely many values lie in the algebraic closure
of the original constant field, and cannot equal the transcendental generic
value c.

This argument remains sound when a coefficient specialization creates a
critical curve. The independent source uses `J=p²y²`, whose two coordinate
axes are critical, as a control. Both axes have the single value0. No growing
critical-point resultant, no generic choice of coefficients of A, and no
claim that the entire critical locus is finite are required.

## 2. Geometric integrality and complete branch divisor

After dividing by the nonzero constant gamma, the equation is a monic cubic
`F=(y²-p³)(y+A/gamma)+c/(gamma p²)`.
At p=0 every root has valuation `-2/3`: a nonnegative valuation leaves the
constant-term pole unmatched, and at negative valuation only the cubic term
can balance it. Hence the cubic has no rational root over
`algebraic_closure(K(c))(p)`, since rational functions have integral
p-valuation. A cubic without a rational root is irreducible. This proves
geometric integrality, not merely irreducibility before extending constants.
The unnormalized equation `I-c` has no vertical component: its leading
y-coefficient is a nonzero constant times p², whereas its value at p=0 is-c.

The independent source constructs the cubic discriminant from a literal5x5
Sylvester determinant, retaining gamma. It obtains

```
disc_y(I-c)/p⁴
 =4p⁷(gamma²p³-A²)²
  +4Ac p²(9gamma²p³-A²)-27gamma²c².                   (1)
```

The constant term is nonzero. If A has degree at most1, the degree in p is13
with leading coefficient `4gamma⁴`. If `m=deg A>=2`, its degree is `4m+7`
with leading coefficient `4a_m⁴`. The odd exponent3 cannot equal2m, so no
specialization of the nonzero leading coefficient cancels this term.

A generic triple root would satisfy
`y=-A/(3gamma)` and `A²+3gamma²p³=0`. The latter is never identically zero:
a polynomial square cannot be a nonzero multiple of p³. Such p and y are
therefore algebraic over the coefficient field and cannot have invariant
value c. For a nontriple double root, translate it to0 and write the cubic
as `y²(y+q)`, q nonzero. Direct Sylvester differentiation gives first
discriminant variation `-4q³ V(0)`. Thus a repeated discriminant zero forces
both partial derivatives of I to vanish at value c, excluded by Section1.

Consequently all finite nonzero discriminant zeros are simple, with one
index-two ramification point each. Invertibility of the discriminant away
from these zeros, p=0, and infinity excludes hidden finite ramification.

## 3. Both boundary charts are complete

At zero use `p=r³`, `y=r^-2Y`. The unnormalized equation multiplied by r⁶
has initial polynomial `gamma Y³+c`. Its three geometric roots are simple,
and the cubic deck action is transitive. They descend to one point of
ramification index3, contributing2.

For `m<=1`, the infinity chart `p=r^-2`, `y=r^-3Y`, after multiplication by
r⁹, has initial polynomial `gamma Y(Y²-1)`. The two nonzero branches are
exchanged by `(r,Y)->(-r,-Y)`, and the zero branch is fixed. The transformed
equation may change by a scalar sign under the deck involution; its zero
locus is preserved. Downstairs there is one index-two point and one
unramified point.

For `m>=2`, multiplying by `r^(2m+6)` instead gives initial polynomial
`a_m(Y²-1)`. The same deck involution exchanges the two simple branches.
The missing third branch is present on the ordinary infinity parameter:
`p=q^-1`, `y=q^-mV`. After multiplication by `q^(3m)` its initial polynomial
is `V²(a_m+gamma V)`. The root `-a_m/gamma` has nonzero derivative
`a_m²/gamma`, and therefore is unramified. The degrees2 and1 exhaust the
degree-three cover. The infinity contribution is always1.

With n simple finite branch points, the ramification degree is `n+2+1`.
Characteristic-zero Riemann--Hurwitz yields `2g-2=-6+n+3`, so `g=(n-1)/2`.
This gives exactly the stated genera. The primary curve input is the
[Stacks Project Riemann--Hurwitz formula](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0C1B),
read together with the repository curve-input sidecar.

## 4. Rational-flow implication and scope boundaries

The invariant I remains in the actual universal carrier, and
`f(I)-f(0)` is divisible by `p²D`. The argument retains the function field
over **I**, not f(I), whose generic fibres can split. In the logarithmic chart

```
p=s²+tau, y=sp,
I=tau W(s)+O(tau²), W=s⁸(A(s²)+gamma s³).
```

The even polynomial A(s²) cannot cancel the nonzero odd term. If f starts
with nonconstant term `f_k I^k`, the first displacement of p at time lambda
is `2lambda k f_k s W^k tau^k`. The derivation raises tau order by at least k,
so later iterates cannot cancel this coefficient. Every positive time iterate
is nontrivial in characteristic zero.

A rational specialization would give a field embedding fixing K(I), hence
a nonconstant selfmap of the smooth proper generic curve. The direction is
contravariant and does not presuppose a rational inverse.
[Stacks, Theorem53.2.6](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0BY1)
The genus is greater than1, so Riemann--Hurwitz makes this map an
automorphism. Its geometric automorphism group is finite: the finite-type
automorphism group has no tangent sections because the tangent sheaf has
negative degree. This contradicts the nontrivial displacement of every
positive iterate. [Stacks, Section109.7](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0DST)
The inherited fixed-input source comparison is still required; arbitrary
completed elements are not identified with rational functions.

**A specific boundary control.** Allowing variable gamma changes the genus
calculation. For `A=0`, `gamma(p)=p²`, the normalized equation is
`y³-p³y+c/p⁴=0`. Its finite discriminant numerator is
`4p^17-27c²`, with17 simple zeros. Zero contributes2 and infinity contributes1,
so this particular outside-scope invariant has genus8, not the genus6 of
the constant-slope A=0 family. This is not a low-genus escape; it explicitly
prevents transferring the stated formula to arbitrary gamma(p).

Constant Hamiltonians, zero time, and composition with the inverse flow
remain exempt. No conclusion about arbitrary Keller maps is inferred.
