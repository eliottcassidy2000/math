# The physical 67 ↔ 69 step needs more than a transitive spine at that edge

Represent a labelled tournament by its outgoing row masks, zero-based vertices. The order-seven tournament
```
(86,124,24,113,96,69,4)
```
has 67 Hamilton paths. Reversing arc {2,5} changes the masks to (86,124,56,113,96,65,4) and the count to 69. Reversing {5,6} next gives (86,124,56,113,96,1,36), of count 71. Thus the counts are connected by literal unit moves, not merely separate witness choices. Deleting 2 and 5 from the original tournament leaves a five-vertex tournament of count 3, not a transitive spine.

**All orders exclusion with the two flipped endpoints as apices.** There is no pair of transitive-spine two-apex tournaments with counts {67,69} differing on their mutual apex arc only.

For any tournament, the count factors over its strongly connected components: they form a uniquely ordered forward sum, and every Hamilton path follows that order. If one edge reversal changes the count, one orientation has an SCC containing both endpoints. Otherwise no directed cycle using the edge exists in either orientation; all cycles and hence SCCs remain the same, and so do their internal counts. In the orientation with both endpoints in one SCC C, reversing an internal edge cannot merge C with outside SCCs, since all arcs between the original SCCs still point forward across that fixed ordering. All outside factors are unchanged. Their product divides gcd(67,69)=1. The two counts are therefore the counts of the restrictions to C, before and after reversal.

For a strong tournament of order k, the [Busch bound used in the archived small-count census](../actual_hamilton_multiplicities_20261021/session24-multiplicities/PROOF.md) is H≥5^((k−1)/3). Applied in the strong orientation of C, it implies k≤8 because 5^8>69^3. C still contains both apices, and restriction preserves the transitive spine, so there are at most six spine sites. The attached exact three-run enumeration tests all 5,461 four-letter tapes of widths zero through six and finds no pair {67,69}. The same checker counts the displayed unrestricted chain of levels 67,69,71 by independent subset dynamic programming on the literal row masks.

The obstruction is at the *changed edge*, not to realization of either number or even to a one-edge transition in the unrestricted tournament space. Allowing a nontransitive spine at the edit, or more ports chosen before the edit, is a concrete way to escape it; determinant scheduling on a transitive spine at that same edge cannot address this pair.
