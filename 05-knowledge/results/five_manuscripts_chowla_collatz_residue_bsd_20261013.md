## 0. Summary

1. **Chowla for Collatz is decided below the stopping time and is a coalescence statement beyond it (THM-4627).** For functionals of the Collatz orbit that depend only on the parity word up to the stopping time, ordinary two-point correlations along any two non-proportional affine forms converge to Haar expectations, because their coefficient-stopping truncations are periodic, with a finite exceptional-height correction (Terras); the limit is a computable dyadic series, not zero: for the stopping-time parity `s(n) = (-1)^sigma(n)` the correlation at every odd lag equals `-E[s | n odd] = -0.5317...`, at every lag `2 mod 4` it equals `+E[s | n odd]`, and at lags `0 mod 4` it is `3/4 + (1/4) E[s(n)s(n+h) | n = 3 mod 4]` (values `0.68`-`0.87`). The Chowla manuscript's hard step (transfer from independent residues to integer averages by bounded independence) is void here: coefficient-word events at depth `k` are exactly periodic modulo `2^k`; actual first descent retains the finite-height correction. The genuinely hard Collatz correlations are those of functionals that are not stopping-time measurable, and for them the answer is the opposite of Chowla: by THM-4581 (equal-time merging of `y` and `y+h` for almost every `2`-adic `y`) and periodicity, the integer orbits of `n` and `n+h` coincide from a common time on, with equal odd-step counts, for a set of `n` of density one, so `tau(n) = tau(n+h)` and `omega(n) = omega(n+h)` on a density-one set, and the two-point correlations of `(-1)^tau` and `(-1)^omega` tend to `+1` at every lag (conditionally on the orbits reaching one). The census confirms the mechanism exactly: at `N = 2^26` the correlation of `(-1)^tau` at lag `h` equals the fraction of pairs whose orbits coincide before reaching one to three decimals (`0.510` vs `0.508` at `h = 1`), the unmerged pairs contribute `-0.003`, and the merged fraction rises with `N` (`0.478, 0.495, 0.510` at `2^20, 2^23, 2^26`).
2. **Residue colourings of `Q(zeta_55)` cannot be a "leading projection" (THM-4628).** At a conjugation-fixed prime with residue field `F_(q^2)` the residue graph is the finite Euclidean unit graph `Cay(F_(q^2), N^1)`, its eigenvalues are minus the Kloosterman sums over `F_q`, and Hoffman's bound gives chromatic number at least `1 + (q+1)/(2 sqrt q)`: a lower bound of `7` at `109` (S24 had this one prime), then `12, 15, 16, 20, 21, 23, 24` at `439, 659, 769, 1319, 1429, 1759, 1979`, and at least `788, 2266, 27737` at the degree-ten residue fields of `19, 29, 79`, which S24 left unexamined. Every conjugation-fixed prime of `L` has even residue degree and `q >= 109`, so no residue colouring of `L` with six or fewer colours exists at any fixed prime. The Deligne-Drinfeld proof's degeneration works because its equations are linear and saturated lattices keep their dimension under reduction; colourings are not linear, their reduction modulo a prime is not flat, and the degenerate problem is *harder* than the original, which is why no residue "leading projection" can see the plane's number.
3. **The two BSD manuscripts separate "integral" from "unit" and kill unknown divisors by raising codimension.** Their reusable shape: a one-sided inequality `X_p(E) >= 0` from an Euler-system divisibility, a conservation identity `X_p(E) + X_p(E^D) = 0` from a Heegner index, hence equality; and unknown horizontal factors removed by adding tame variables until a simultaneous jump has codimension two. What remains open after them is exactly `q`-Selmer corank at least two at every prime, i.e. the rank-two wall where no Heegner class exists. The repository's own curves are classified: the fruit curve `y^2 = x^3 + 109x^2 + 224x` of the creation certificate is LMFDB 910.a4 (rank 1, torsion `Z/6`, analytic Sha 1) whereas the proposed square curve `y^2 = 2x^3 + 4x^2 + 1` has conductor364 and exact algebraic rank1. Its former assignment to43.a1 was false; no Sha or all-prime Selmer conclusion for it is retained from that entry.
4. **The planar Jacobian lane gets a typed test, not a theorem.** The Deligne-Drinfeld squeeze (degenerate, prove the leading projection injective, count dimensions from below) needs linear equations and a saturated lattice; the Keller equation is bilinear and gains solutions modulo `p`, and its leading projection (Abhyankar-Moh) has enormous fibres. What transfers is the ordering-comparison device (corrections monotone in a count on each side, identical count-preserving parts), which names a concrete test for the repository's Weyl/Poisson lane (section 6).

## 3. Collatz: Chowla-type correlations along affine forms (Anchor)

Notation: `T(n) = n/2` for even `n`, `(3n+1)/2` for odd `n`; `sigma(n)` the stopping time (least `k` with `T^k(n) < n`); `kappa(n)` Terras's coefficient stopping time (least `k` with `3^(a_k) < 2^k`, `a_k` the number of odd steps among the first `k`), which depends only on `n mod 2^k` and equals `sigma(n)` for all but finitely many `n` in each residue class; `tau(n)` the number of steps to reach `1`; `omega(n)` the number of odd steps on the way to `1`; `s = (-1)^sigma`, `t = (-1)^tau`, `lam = (-1)^omega`.

**3.1 Theorem (THM-4627, repaired part1).** Bounded stopping-time word functionals have ordinary affine two-point limits given by the periodic **coefficient-stopping** models. Actual first-descent truncations are not exactly periodic: sources1 and5 already differ at depth2. If `H_k` is the largest affine descent cutoff through depth k, the repaired error bound is
`||f||||g||[P_1(k)+P_2(k)+(4*2^k+4*H_k)/N]`, with the Haar coefficient-tail probabilities `P_i(k)`. See [the proof and exact controls](incoming_cylinder_prime_repair_20261013.md). Taking N to infinity first and then k proves the claimed limit. The earlier free horizon `k~log_2 N` is not justified by the bound without its height term. The manuscript's bounded-independence comparison is not an input to this repaired argument.

**Exact lag laws for the stopping-time parity (PROVED, elementary).** Let `m = lim E[s]` and `m_odd = lim E[s | n odd] = 1 + 2m` (even `n` have `sigma = 1`). Then for every lag `h`:
- `h` odd: `C(h) = -m_odd` (the pair has one even member, whose `s = -1`, and the other member is a uniformly distributed odd number);
- `h = 2 mod 4`: `C(h) = +m_odd` (both even: product `+1`; both odd: exactly one is `1 mod 4`, with `sigma = 2`, so the product is `s` of the `3 mod 4` member, and `E[s | 3 mod 4] = 2 m_odd - 1`);
- `h = 0 mod 4`: `C(h) = 3/4 + (1/4) E[s(n) s(n+h) | n = 3 mod 4]`, which depends on the `2`-adic digits of `h`.

**3.2 Theorem (THM-4627, part 2: coalescence makes the total stopping time locally constant; PROVED given THM-4581).** Fix `h >= 1`. (a) The set `M_h(K) = {n : T^k(n) = T^k(n+h) for some k <= K, with equal numbers of odd steps up to k}` has natural density equal to the Haar probability `mu_h(K)` of the same event for the pair `(y, y+h)`, `y` Haar on `Z_2` (the class-wide identity `(3^a - 3^(a'))n = 3^(a') h + c' - c` holds for a whole residue class modulo `2^k` iff `a = a'` and `c = 3^a h + c'`; when `a != a'` it holds for at most one `n` per class and step, a density-zero set). (c) Hence for a set of `n` of density one the orbits of `n` and `n+h` coincide from a common time on, with equal odd-step counts; for `n > 2^K` the merge at depth `<= K` happens before either orbit can reach `1` (since `T^j(n) >= n/2^j > 1` for `j <= K`), so `tau(n) = tau(n+h)` and `omega(n) = omega(n+h)` whenever either side is finite, and `tau(n) < infinity iff tau(n+h) < infinity`. (d) Consequently, if the orbits of all `n <= N` reach `1` (true for `N <= 2^68` by Barina's verification, CITED; in general under the Collatz conjecture), `liminf_N (1/N) sum_(n <= N) t(n) t(n+h) >= 2 mu_h(K) - 1` for every `K`, so the correlations of `t` and of `lam` along `(n, n+h)` tend to `+1`. ∎ The parity consequence survives even a merge inside the final cycle `1 -> 2 -> 1` (equal phase gives equal parity), but the density-one merges happen earlier, at bounded depth, so the stronger equality `tau(n) = tau(n+h)` holds.

## 4. Hadwiger-Nelson: residue colourings are Kloosterman-forced (Wildcard; THM-4628)

(e) Hence no residue colouring of `L` at any conjugation-fixed prime uses six or fewer colours; HYP-9247's "residue colourings need at least 7" holds at every fixed prime, not only at `109`, and the number grows like `sqrt(q)/2`.

**Why this is the right negative for the degeneration idea.** The Deligne-Drinfeld proof reduces a linear system to characteristic two, where its solution lattice, being saturated, keeps its dimension, and a leading projection becomes injective; the degenerate problem is *easier* and *faithful*. A colouring problem reduced modulo a prime becomes a colouring of a finite Euclidean graph whose chromatic number grows with the prime: the degenerate problem is *harder* and *unfaithful* (every periodic colouring is excluded long before the plane's number is reached), so there is no leading projection and no squeeze; the plane's `6` or `7` is invisible from every finite residue field of `L`. The hexagonal seven-colouring itself is periodic under an Eisenstein lattice, which is not a sublattice of `O_L` (`zeta_3` is not in `L`), consistent with (e).

**A one-line use of M1's rigidity.** The only additive relation among three unit vectors with all three sums of modulus one is the hexagon (`|u| = |v| = |u+v| = 1` forces the angle `2 pi/3`); this is the Steinberg-type rigidity behind S24's direction-class method (a support-three relation forces a rigid triangle), typed ANALOGY with M1's alternating pairs, no new consequence.

## 5. BSD: what the two manuscripts do, what remains, and the repository's curves (Niche)

**5.1 The architecture, typed.** Both manuscripts separate three things that rank-and-finiteness arguments conflate: (i) *integrality* of a normalized determinant coordinate (horizontal comparisons at height-one primes where `p` is inverted, allowing fixed clearing denominators); (ii) *residual concentration* supplying the remaining integrality at the closed point; (iii) *primitivity*, the unit statement, by an independent argument (a Pfaffian cofactor lift, a CM comparator, ray units, or theta-to-cusp comparisons on characteristic-zero divisors). The "integral difficulty" is stated plainly in M4 section 1.2: an Euler system produces a point or a determinant generator only after multiplication by an unknown integer, and that integer's two-adic valuation is exactly what the leading term needs. M5's figure 1 shows the divisor-removal step: after integrality and `p`-primitivity, every possible remaining factor is made regular in the tame variables; adding finitely many further tame variables makes simultaneous strict jumps of codimension at least two, while a divisor has codimension one, so no divisor survives; specialization of the added variables to zero is integral and a unit stays a unit.

**5.2 The twist-pair squeeze (candidate META-PATTERN, not promoted).** The single most portable move: an unknown integer `X` with a provable one-sided bound `X >= 0` and a provable conservation identity `X + X' = 0` against a partner `X'` that satisfies the same one-sided bound is zero. In M5 the partner is a quadratic twist `E^D` with `a(E) + a(E^D) = 1`, the identity comes from the Heegner index over `Q(sqrt D)` (section 2.4 there), the inequality from Kato's element; in M4 the partner is the split pair `(A_h, A_hk)`. Trigger: a quantity known to be integral but not to be a unit, with a natural involution whose fixed-point-free pairs conserve a sum. Counterindication: the inequality must hold for both members (M5's `X >= 0` is for non-CM curves, so CM curves of rank one are handled by the pair identity plus the CM rank-zero formula, a different route); and the identity must be exact, not up to a unit. Evidence outside BSD in this repository is not yet established; the pattern is recorded here as a candidate and proposed in the close-out letter, not added to META-PATTERNS.

**5.3 What is left after M4 and M5 (if they are right).** Their hypothesis is `s_q(E) <= 1` at *some* prime `q`. For an elliptic curve of analytic rank at most one, Gross-Zagier-Kolyvagin gives finite Sha, hence `s_q = rank` at every `q`, so the hypothesis holds; M5 would then prove the full BSD formula for every elliptic curve over `Q` of analytic rank at most one, with no residual or reduction hypotheses, superseding the hypotheses of Skinner-Urban, Jetchev-Skinner-Wan, Li-Tian-Yan-Zhu, Kriz-Li and the CM-specific results it cites. What remains is the set of curves with `s_q(E) >= 2` at every prime: conjecturally the rank-at-least-two curves. There the pair identity has no second member of analytic rank zero or one to compare with, Heegner points are torsion, and the "one-sided inequality" would need an Euler system in rank two (diagonal cycles of Darmon-Rotger give classes but no height formula controlling a second derivative); the manuscripts say nothing about this wall, and neither does this note beyond naming it. "Progress on BSD" from this repository's side can only be: (1) finite receipts for the hypothesis (the codex note's Selmer-growth certificates), (2) classification of the repository's curves under the hypothesis, and (3) the typed pattern of 5.2.

## 6. Planar Jacobian conjecture: the degeneration test, typed (Niche)

The repository's current form of `JC_2` is THM-4626: for a Keller map `F : A^3 -> A^3`, the dual field `V_j = d/dF_j` is surjective iff locally nilpotent iff `K[x] = R_j[F_j]`, and "surjective implies locally nilpotent" in dimension three is equivalent to `JC_2`; the first proof attempt there used a leading-coefficient filtration. M2's shape has four steps: (1) a filtration in which the relation's leading part can be tested in a *different* algebra with an integral incidence presentation; (2) an ordering comparison in which corrections are monotone in a count on each side and the count-preserving parts coincide, so the leading relation transfers without an independence assumption on the target; (3) deletion operators making the leading projection injective on reductions of rational solutions; (4) a dimension squeeze on saturated lattices. Step (4) needs linear equations: the reduction of a saturated lattice has the same dimension as the rational solution space, which is what makes the characteristic-two bound an upper bound for characteristic zero. The Keller equation `J(F, G) = 1` is bilinear in the pair, its solution set modulo `p` is strictly larger (`x + x^p`), and its leading projection in any weight (the top forms of a Keller pair are powers of a common form by Abhyankar-Moh in the standard grading; the repository's weight charts THM-4308 and THM-4380-4426 close rows one at a time precisely because the fibres of the projection are large) is far from injective. So (3)-(4) do not transfer. Step (2) does name a test: the repository's Weyl-algebra lane (DC(2), the reserved THM-4406 "Moyal anomaly absorption") compares the Jacobian relation with its Poisson degeneration; M2's device is to filter both sides by a count (here the `hbar`-order against the `y`-degree) and to prove that the star-product corrections never lower the count while the commutative corrections never raise it, keeping only identical count-preserving terms. PROPOSAL, no computation here. Status of the lane: `JC_2` OPEN, unchanged.
