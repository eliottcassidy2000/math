# Paired cubes of first births and the opposite endpoint of a smooth count fiber

This follows the [first-birth atlas](../chanpak_birth_atlas_metagraph_20261117/README.md). The background pass supplied paired labeled cubes and the smooth block computations. We show that the cubes can be chosen to survive the isomorphism and converse quotients, and that all the longest smooth words lie in one controlled height corridor.

## Large cubes that survive merging

A framed complement keeps a distinguished directed Hamiltonian path fixed and reverses all other arcs. A tournament of order n with H greater than the previous-order maximum is automatically a first-birth representative for its count. By [Divoux–Wang](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.23755v1#S10), the maximum at order n is Θ(n!/2^(n−1)); the ratio of the previous maximum to that scale is Θ(1/n).

Fix a frame and its first vertex v. For any fixed 0<c<1, choose d=floor(c n log(n)/2) free unordered pairs uniformly among the off-frame pairs not incident with v; log is natural. Lock the other off-frame arcs randomly and keep the frame forward. At v force exactly r=floor((n−2)/4) off-frame wins, uniformly among those pairs. All completions form a labeled cube of dimension d, and the framed complements form a second cube.

A permutation avoiding every frame pair as a consecutive unordered pair is robust if it also avoids every free pair and all its required arcs agree with the locked directions. The fraction of permutations avoiding frame adjacencies tends to e^(−2): for fixed j, including j frame edges collapses their runs into blocks with 2 choices per run, and the j-subsets are asymptotically disjoint edges. The j-th inclusion-exclusion term tends to 2^j/j!. Truncating by Bonferroni and then sending the truncation to infinity gives the limit. The probability of avoiding the free pairs, uniformly over such permutations, is at least
     
    binom(K−(n−1),d) / binom(K,d) = n^(−c+o(1)),

where K is the number of eligible unordered pairs. Agreement with the locked arcs costs 2^(−(n−1)) times a factor bounded below by a positive constant: at most two required arcs are incident with v, and their constrained orientations still have probability bounded away from zero. Thus the expected robust count is at least n!/2^(n−1) times n^(−c+o(1)). Robust paths survive every completion, and their reverses survive every framed mate. This exceeds the previous-order maximum by a factor n^(1−c+o(1)).

The following high-probability conditions can be imposed simultaneously without losing that expectation.

* The free degree of every vertex is O(log n), by a union bound on the hypergeometric tails (with a sufficiently large absolute coefficient). Off v, the independent locked orientations then put the outdegrees within n/10 of the middle **for all completions of both cubes**, by Chernoff and the O(log n) effect of free pairs and the two frame adjacencies. These failures have probability O(n^(-10)). The degree of v is r+1 in the first cube and n−1−r in the second. It is a unique low extreme in the first and a unique high extreme in the second. Their degrees differ by one from the opposite cubes' corresponding converse extreme, so no completion in one cube is isomorphic or converse-isomorphic to a completion in the other. Within a cube any isomorphism fixes v, and no two completions are converse-isomorphic.
* On the remaining N=n−1 vertices delete the frame and free pairs from the locked support; the missing degree is at most L=O(log n). For a nonidentity permutation σ fixing v, with support s in these N vertices, at least s(N−2)/2 unordered pairs are moved (subtract at most s/2 transposition pairs from the pairs touching the support). At least s(N−2)/2−2sL of these have both source and image locked. An isomorphism of any two completions must enforce agreement of the locked orientations on every such pair, up to the forced orientation sign of the relabeling. The equations are steps along pair orbits of σ; each path has as many independent equations as edges, and a cycle loses at most one. Thus at least half the equations are independent, or the system is inconsistent. The probability that a given nonidentity permutation works for *any* two completions is at most 2^(−sN/5) eventually. The same equations apply in the other cube because both locked sides are reversed. Summing over ≤N^s permutations per s gives exponentially small probability.
* The same rank bound holds if one locked relation is allowed to fail: delete that one equation, and take a union bound over at most n² choices. This excludes any nonidentity relabeling taking one member of a cube to within one arc flip of a second member. Indeed such a relabeling must first fix v, since the exceptional degree differs from every other degree by more than one.

The number of robust paths on a bad outcome is bounded by the maximum at order n (take any completion). The O(n^(-10)) exceptional probability therefore costs o(1) of the required expectation. Some good lock has robust count above the previous maximum.

**Conclusion.** For every sufficiently large n there are two disjoint induced d-cubes of ordinary single-arc adjacencies on first-birth isomorphism classes. Their 2^d framed-complement edges are distinct, loopless edges even in the actual converse-merged tiling metagraph, and their endpoints are all distinct merged nodes. The edges are not claimed to be blue. The ordinary cubes and the framed matching are different graphs on those nodes. This counts classes and line incidences, not distinct scalar counts or a density-one result for odd integers.

The two smallest illustrative paired controls used here are a framed 9↔11 edge at order 5 and 47↔91 at order 7. The previous maxima are 5 and 45, respectively (see the order-nine atlas); the replay verifies both literal pairs and each chord-complement convention.

## Exact last singleton-free order when the count is 3^a 5^b

Unlike first order, the **largest** order before adding singleton SCCs has an elementary formula for these smooth counts. For a nontrivial strong tournament of count 3^x 5^y, [Busch's theorem](https://www.combinatorics.org/ojs/index.php/eljc/article/view/v13i1n3) bounds its order by 1+3x log_5(3)+3y; this is strictly below 3x+4y whenever x+y≥2. For a single factor 3 or 5 the unique longest core is C₃ or the unique strong four-vertex class D₄. Since H multiplies over the ordinal SCC sequence, the greatest singleton-free order for H=3^a 5^b>1 is exactly 3a+4b. Every class at that order with no singleton SCC is an ordinal word of a copies of C₃ and b copies of D₄. There are binom(a+b,a) such words; converse reverses the word, so fixed words are exactly palindromes.

Inside this normalized longest-order fiber, ordinary one-arc flips cannot join distinct nodes. Internal flips stay in the block (or split it) and cannot increase H; an unchanged count inside C₃ or D₄ means the block is isomorphic to its old class. A cross flip merges the whole consecutive interval of SCCs between its endpoints. It strictly increases the count: all previous internal odd packs remain, and there is a new mixed directed triangle (including the interposed component if there is one, or a distinct vertex of a strong endpoint component). Every former packing remains available. For adjacent blocks the exact minimum exited local counts are 9→17, 15→29, 25→49 (verified across all cross flips of the literal blocks). Nonadjacent flips have local growth ratio at least 25/9: use Busch's exact minimum at order 9, and replacing a triangle by a four-block increases that bound by at least 5/3 for each added vertex, offsetting the changed source factor; adding another block only increases the ratio. Thus for words with at least two blocks, the smallest upward exit uses adjacent triangles if available, else an adjacent mixed pair if both types occur, else adjacent four-blocks.

Despite isolation at the exact level, the entire word family is connected in a **uniformly bounded count corridor**. Freeze the internal arcs of C₃ and D₄. The least threshold permitting an ordinary cross-arc flip path from C₃→D₄ to D₄→C₃ is 51: the replay exhausts all 4096 orientations, tests connectivity at thresholds 50 and 51, and gives a twelve-step path. Inside any larger fixed ordinal word this exchange multiplies all intermediate counts by the unchanged outside component product (the interval is adjacent). Sorting the word by repeated adjacent mixed exchanges therefore connects any two longest-order words for the same m=3^a 5^b through graphs with H at most (51/15)m. This constant is sharp for the individual frozen two-block exchange, not a lower bound for routes allowing internal edits or other blocks to move.

Run `python replay.py` for the literal framed complements, four adjacent exit minima and all 4096 exchange orientations. The infinite statements are proved above independently of the finite replay sizes.
