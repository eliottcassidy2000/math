# From a reflection matching to a shell, and from two reflections to a marked return

**Status:** PROVED physical recursion and transfer identities, using the inherited odd-cycle slot specialization; FINITE-EXACT replay. No universal Chan–Pak coverage is claimed. The operators are elementary recoveries; no priority claim.

## 1. Literal orientation and the single-reflection classification

On labels1..n, orient i→j for i<j, then reverse the arc {i,j} exactly when i+j=s, with i≠j. The sum condition gives a matching; a possible fixed point s/2 is a vertex, never a loop.

For2<=s<=2n its active interval is

[max(1,s−n), min(n,s−1)],

of length m=min(s−1,2n−s+1). Vertices outside this interval form initial and terminal transitive blocks, with all cross-arcs forward. Inside it the reversed pairs are nested about s/2.

Define the cyclic shell S(T) by adding a,b with b→a, a→T, T→b. Removing the two outer active vertices leaves exactly the same reflection construction on the interior. Therefore the active tournament is S^floor(m/2)(TT_(m mod2)). Padding outside does not change H. At the level of full slot polynomials it multiplies by q^(n−m).

Thus a single reflected square-sum target carries no square-specific arithmetic into this tournament construction: after the active interval length is specified, the answer is independent of s. Multiple overlapping target matchings are needed to retain their interaction.

## 2. Exact all-slot transfer

Write f(q)=P_q(T), with P_q counting ordered q-slot path covers and empty slots allowed. Then

P_q(S(T)) = Lf(q) = q²f(q)+q[f(q+1)−f(q−1)].       (2)

This includes empty T: f=1 and Lf=q² gives the two-vertex transitive tournament. For nonempty T, P_0=0, so

H(S(T))=H(T)+P_2(T).                                (3)

Proof using the inherited odd-cycle/singleton identity P_q=G_T(2q,q): every directed cycle meeting a or b meets both. Such an odd cycle is b→a→a directed path through an odd-sized nonempty subset U of T→b. It leaves an arbitrary odd-cycle packing on T\U. Consequently

P_q(S(T))=q²P_q(T)+2q sum_{|U| odd} H(T[U])P_q(T\U).

The ordered-slot convolution identity holds polynomially in q. Also P_(−1)(T[U])=(−1)^|U|H(T[U]), because every packing has number of cycles plus singletons congruent to |U| modulo2. Subtracting the convolutions for q+1 and q−1 gives twice the odd-subset sum, proving(2).

Padding one source/sink vertex acts by Df=qf. Their commutator is

(LD−DL)f(q)=q[f(q+1)+f(q−1)].                       (4)

For nonempty T its effect on H is P_2(T), not an arbitrary unit.

### All-word residue barrier

For |T|>=2, P_2(T) is divisible by4: pair complementary subsets in its defining sum, and use oddness of every induced tournament's H. There are 2^(|T|−1) complementary pairs, an even number. Every intermediate size in a word of S and source/sink padding remains at least2. Equations(3) and H(padding T)=H(T) therefore show

H(W(T)) ≡ H(T) (mod4)

for **every word W** in these operations. This is not an obstruction to more general tournament moves, and does not apply across different initial cores. The singleton boundary is important: shelling it changes1 to3.

The scalar recurrence does not close on H alone; the full polynomial is the needed continuation state. Its positive-coefficient update is explicit:

L(q^m)=q^(m+2)+2 sum_{r>=0,2r+1<=m} binomial(m,2r+1) q^(m−2r).

## 3. Two reflections give a genuinely marked, repeatable operation

Now reverse both target matchings i+j=n and i+j=n+1. Distinct targets never designate the same edge; vertices can belong to both matchings, and fixed points are still omitted. Call the tournament R_n and mark vertex n.

For n>=3, removing vertices1,n leaves R_(n−2) after subtracting1 from its labels. Relative to the ordinary cyclic shell, there is precisely one extra reversed arc: (n−1)→1 from target n. Thus R_n is generated by the following rooted operation B:

Given (T,v), add a,b with b→a, a→T\{v}, v→a, T→b, and mark b.

The two bases are R_1, a singleton, and R_2, the arc2→1 marked at2. This is an exact all-order recursion, not a guessed recurrence for the observed counts. It keeps the nonuniform contact that a single matching lacks.

Let S_v(T),E_v(T) count full paths starting/ending at v. Define

K_start(T,v)=sum_{U containing v} S_v(T[U])H(T\U),
K_end(T,v)=sum_{U containing v} E_v(T[U])H(T\U).

Then

H(B(T,v))=H(T)+P_2(T)+K_end−K_start−E_v−S_v.        (5)

Proof. Start with S(T) and reverse its a→v arc. Paths lost through a→v either start at a (S_v choices) or have b immediately before a; splitting the old vertices around b→a→v gives K_start choices. Paths gained through v→a have a nonempty old suffix before b, since a cannot go directly to b. Splitting at v→a gives K_end−E_v. Subtract the lost and add the gained counts to(3).

The imbalance in(5) is the exact marked coordinate destroyed by an unmarked shell description. It can change the mod4 sector: R_2 has H1 while R_4=B(R_2,root2) has H3. No contradiction to the all-word barrier occurs because B is not S or padding.

The direct counts R_1..R_12 are

1,1,1,3,9,29,101,383,1565,6849,31929,157819.

This gives a physical recursive family, not a selector for arbitrary counts. In particular, H/P_2 alone is insufficient to update(5); the marked weighted endpoint sums must be carried.
