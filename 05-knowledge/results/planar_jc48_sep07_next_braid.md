## 3. Exact root tubes and marked braid transport

The rational certifier retains the complete tube and endpoint matching
mechanism of the audited predecessor. For \(z\in\mathbb Q(i)\), put
\(L(z)=\max(|\Re z|,|\Im z|)\) and
\(A(z)=|\Re z|+|\Im z|\). At a base-segment start \(u_0\) with four
root centres \(z_i\), use pairwise disjoint radii
\[
 r_i=\frac1{32}\min_{j\ne i}L(z_i-z_j).
\]
If
\(F(u_0+h,z_i+w)=\sum B_{jk}h^jw^k\), and
\(d=A(u_1-u_0)\), the always-active exact gate is
\[
 L(B_{01})r_i>
 A(B_{00})+\sum_{k=2}^4A(B_{0k})r_i^k+
 \sum_{j=1}^6\sum_{k=0}^4A(B_{jk})d^jr_i^k.
\tag{5}
\]
Rouché against \(B_{01}w\) gives exactly one simple root in each fixed
disk throughout the segment. Since the polynomial is monic quartic,
these are all roots.

Every proposed endpoint centre moves by less than \(r_i/2\) in the
A-norm. A second gate isolates its actual endpoint root in radius
\(\min(r_i,r'_i)/64\), with \(r'_i\) the next segment's radius.
Thus the endpoint root lies in both successive disks, preserving labels.
The initial roots are isolated separately; both loops have the same
labelled base centres; the final unordered centre set equals the initial
set exactly. Convexity of the four disjoint disks then gives a homotopy
from the actual root strands to the rational polygonal strands, with
consistent endpoint displacements and a common base-fibre identification.

Projection by the orientation-preserving multiplier \(1+i/4\) gives
rational crossing times. The source checks all nondegeneracy conditions:
nonzero endpoint real differences, distinct crossing times, adjacency
of the crossing pair, and nonzero imaginary separation. A positive
letter has the initially left strand below the right strand at the
crossing. The target exact words are
\[
 W_+=\sigma_3\sigma_2\sigma_1\sigma_2^{-1}\sigma_3^{-1},
 \qquad
 W_-=\sigma_3^{-1}\sigma_2\sigma_3.
\tag{6}

The exact producer has verified (5) and every endpoint gate on 5,875

Here is the marked consumer, inherited with its hypotheses paid again.
The monic quartic gives a continuous section above a Cauchy root bound

one constant vertical basepoint larger than that bound. The section loop

Use meridian access stems below the ordered punctures. Under a positive

\(x_{\rm right}^{-1}x_{\rm left}x_{\rm right}\).
Representation coordinates transport contravariantly. Thus chronological

\[
 H_i^+:(a,b)\mapsto(aba^{-1},a),\qquad
 H_i^-:(a,b)\mapsto(b,b^{-1}ab).
\tag{7}
\]
This is the same fully audited convention as the predecessor.

## 4. Two relations give two meridian generators

This step holds in **any group**, with no permutation restriction.
Let the four marked meridian elements be \((m_1,m_2,m_3,m_4)\).
A tuple is fixed by an elementary positive half-twist if and only if
its two affected entries agree.

For \(W_+\), first apply \(H_3^+H_2^+\). The first two entries become
\[
 m_1,\qquad m_2m_3m_4m_3^{-1}m_2^{-1}.
\]
The central \(H_1^+\) fixes this tuple precisely when they agree;
the inverse suffix then restores the original coordinates. For
\(W_-\), the initial \(H_3^-\) makes the second and third entries
\(m_2,m_4\), so its central \(H_2^+\) fixes precisely when they agree.
Thus the two fixed-word conditions are equivalent to
\[
 m_2=m_4,\qquad
 m_1=m_4m_3m_4m_3^{-1}m_4^{-1}.
\tag{8}
\]
Both \(m_3\) and \(m_4\) are themselves positive curve meridians.
Since the four fibre meridians generate the affine group, (8) proves
that this group is generated by those two positive meridians. No cusp
or node braid, no full presentation, and no abstract infinity image is
used in this generation statement.

Two single three-cycles cannot generate a transitive group on six
labels. If their supports overlap, the union contains at most five
labels and its complement is fixed. If they are disjoint, each of the
two triples is invariant. This proves the required intransitivity.
The distinction is essential: disjoint three-cycles have no common
fixed label. A claim that every resulting group fixes a label would
be false.

A stronger all-degree bound was supplied independently by root. Let a
transitive permutation group on d labels be generated by r permutations,
each moving at most \(\delta\) labels, where \(d>1\) and
\(\delta\ge1\). Then
\[
 r(\delta-1)\ge d-1.
\tag{9}
\]
To prove this, replace each nontrivial cycle of each generator by a tree
on that cycle's support. The union graph has the same connected
components as the orbits of the generated group, so transitivity requires
at least d-1 edges. A nonidentity generator contributes its moved-label
count minus its number of nontrivial cycles, at most \(\delta-1\).
An identity contributes zero, which obeys the same bound. This proves
(9). If \(\delta=0\), all generators are identities, so transitivity on
more than one label is already impossible.

For a hypothetical Keller map of generic degree d with an irreducible
whole nonproperness curve, let a be the number of actual sheets over a
generic smooth point of that curve. The a retained local sheets are unramified and fixed
by a positive meridian. The meridian permutation therefore moves at most
\(\delta=d-a\) labels; any further fixed deleted sheets only strengthen
the bound. If the complement has r positive-meridian generators, its
transitive monodromy must satisfy (9). For r=2 and \(a\ge d/2\),
\(2(\delta-1)\le d-2\), a contradiction. Thus:

**Two-meridian consumer.** An irreducible whole nonproperness curve whose
complement is generated by two positive meridians cannot have a Keller passport
with generic retained count at least half its degree.

This does not require the meridian permutation to be one cycle. In
particular, it applies to the inherited irreducible, affine-line
normalized one-odd-cusp-plus-at-least-two-nodes passport: its Euler
ledger has \(n+\sum\omega_p=1\) and
\(\omega_p\ge\max(0,d-2a)\). Two nodes force \(d\le2a\).
This is a conditional consumer of the proved meridian generation,
not a claim that every such curve has meridional rank two. The curve (1)
meets the generation hypothesis through the two actual paths above.

The combinatorial constant is sharp independently of Keller geometry:
two \(\delta\)-cycles whose supports meet in exactly one label generate
a transitive action on \(2\delta-1\) labels. These are abstract positive
controls, not source maps. The frozen source checks the named instances
\(2\le\delta\le8\); the support-graph proof establishes all degrees.
