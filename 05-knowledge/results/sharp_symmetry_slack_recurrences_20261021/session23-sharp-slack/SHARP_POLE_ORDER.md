# Sharp pole order for every nonempty symmetry-slack layer

Date: 2026-10-21. Status: PROVED in the stated scopes, with separate independent mathematical audit PASS and FINITE-EXACT controls. The already reviewed canonical-word rationality packet remains frozen and unchanged. No historical-priority claim.

## 1. Claim and dependencies

Let f(T) be the minimum number of backward arcs in a vertex order, let s(T) be the number of vertices moved by Aut(T), and let δ(T)=3f(T)−s(T). Let F_d(z) count unlabelled tournaments of exact slack d by vertex count, including the empty tournament when d=0. Put p(z)=1−z−z^3.

Dependencies:
- The proved canonical maximal-W-module rationality theorem in `session23-connections/SYMMETRY_SLACK_RATIONALITY.md`, with W=SEQ(K1,C3). It gives a finite positive sum with long-word cutoff d and denominator dividing p(z)^(2d+1).
- The inherited actual-orbit bound and exceptional-set construction in `math/05-knowledge/results/continuation_width_symmetry_20261020/README.md`, Theorems 3–6. The present proof repeats the needed charge calculation and specifies the optimal order from which the exceptional set is rebuilt.

**Theorem.** The nonempty slack layers are exactly d in the numerical semigroup generated by3 and4, namely every nonnegative integer except1,2,5. For each such d, write uniquely

d=3a+4b,  a≥0, b in {0,1,2}.

Then the reduced denominator of F_d is exactly

p(z)^(m_d),  m_d=2a+b+1.                           (1)

Consequently its minimal eventual constant-coefficient recurrence has characteristic polynomial

(E^3−E^2−1)^(m_d),                                (2)

and exact order3m_d. If ρ is the positive zero of p, then

[z^n]F_d(z) ~ c_d n^(m_d−1)ρ^(−n),  c_d>0.         (3)

The leading constant is not evaluated here. The self-converse reduced denominator is exactly q(z)^(k_d), and the converse-merged reduced denominator is exactly p(z)^(m_d)q(z)^(k_d), where q(z)=p(z^2) and k_d=ceil(m_d/2); their proof and minimal recurrence orders are given in §8. Equivalent residue form:

- d=3q: m_d=2q+1;
- d=3q+1, q≥1: m_d=2q;
- d=3q+2, q≥2: m_d=2q−1.

Empty layers have zero series and are excluded from the recurrence-order statement. No earliest recurrence onset is asserted. This result sharpens the denominator exponent of the previously proved compiler; it does not enumerate its numerator or every configuration.

## 2. Orbit charges

Let G=Aut(T), and let O_i be its nontrivial vertex orbits. Every orbit has odd size ℓ_i≥3, and its induced tournament is regular. Define

c=sum_i (ℓ_i^2−1)/8,
D=3c−s(T)=sum_{ℓ_i≥5} q(ℓ_i),
q(ℓ)=3(ℓ^2−1)/8−ℓ.

The regular-orbit lower bound gives f(T)≥c. If e is the number of backward arcs between distinct actual G-orbits in an optimal order, internal orbit costs are at least c, so

0≤e≤f(T)−c=(d−D)/3.                              (4)

In particular D≤d and D≡d mod3. The first nonzero charges are q(5)=4, q(7)=11, q(9)=21; q is increasing for odd ℓ≥5.

Let R be the number of actual orbits of size at least five. Construct B exactly as in the inherited theorem: include every such large orbit and every orbit meeting an endpoint of an external backward arc. Beyond the R already marked orbits, each of the e external arcs adds at most two orbits. Thus, writing b(B) for the number of actual G-orbits contained in B,

b(B)≤R+2e≤R+2(d−D)/3.                            (5)

This counts orbits, not vertices. The old bound |B|≤2d remains valid but is not sharp enough for (1).

## 3. Rebuild B from a canonical block-optimal order

For the canonical maximal-W partition, write T=Q[W(w_i)]. Choose an optimal weighted quotient order and place each W block in its natural atom order, with each triangle optimally ordered. The representative-sampling straightening theorem proves that this is an optimal actual vertex order.

Call a canonical block long if n(w_i)>d. The proved rationality theorem gives weighted quotient cost C≤d, so no backward quotient arc touches a long block. Moreover the actual decorated quotient stabilizer fixes every long slot individually. Thus every long block is G-invariant.

Rebuild the exceptional set B using this particular optimal actual order. Every vertex of a long block is outside B:
- Since the long block is G-invariant, its actual orbits are its singleton letters and individual triangle atoms. None has size at least five.
- All cross-block arcs incident with the long block are forward.
- Inside the block, arcs between different atoms are forward; a triangle's sole backward arc is internal to that actual orbit.
So none of the block's actual orbits meets an external backward arc.

Now fix an actual orbit O contained in B and a long block M. Because M is a homogeneous module, the direction from a vertex x outside M to M is well-defined. Because G preserves M setwise and acts transitively on O, that direction is constant on O. All cross arcs incident with M are forward in the chosen order. Hence every vertex of O lies on the same side of M. **No actual B-orbit straddles a long block.** This uses both module homogeneity and actual orbit invariance; a score-class substitution would not suffice.

The inherited construction says that each gap between consecutive B vertices is a W-word with every outside arc agreeing with the displayed order. Such a gap is therefore a W-module. If two distinct long canonical blocks had no B vertex between them, they would lie in the same gap W-module and would merge, contradicting maximality. Thus between successive long blocks there is at least one B vertex.

Choose one such separator for each adjacent pair of long blocks. The chosen separators belong to distinct actual B-orbits: otherwise one common orbit would straddle an intervening long block, which was just excluded. Therefore, if L is the number of long slots,

L−1≤b(B),
L≤R+2(d−D)/3+1.                                  (6)

For L=0 the inequality is trivial. This proof applies to every actual long filling of every valid finite compiler configuration, so it bounds the exponent of every term in that positive compiler.

## 4. Optimize the orbit penalty

Rewrite (6) as

L≤(2d−P)/3+1,  P=2D−3R=sum_{ℓ_i≥5}(2q(ℓ_i)−3).  (7)

Each summand is at least5, with equality at ℓ=5.

If d≡0 mod3, P≥0, giving L≤2d/3+1.

If d≡1 mod3, D is nonzero, hence R≥1 and P≥5. This gives L≤(2d−2)/3.

If d≡2 mod3, either R≥2 and P≥10, or R=1. In the latter case the single charge satisfies q(ℓ)≡2 mod3; ℓ=5 is impossible because q(5)=4≡1, so ℓ≥7 and P≥2·11−3=19≥10. Hence L≤(2d−7)/3.

These are exactly the three upper bounds stated in §1. In the notation d=3a+4b, b in{0,1,2}, every compiler exponent is at most2a+b+1, and the already proved rationality formula gives a denominator dividing p^(2a+b+1).

The same charge argument excludes d=1,2,5: charges have minimum positive value4, and D must be at most d and congruent to d modulo3. None of these three d permits such D. No other emptiness is asserted by the upper-bound argument alone.

## 5. An injective family attains every upper bound

Let A(z)=1/p(z). The inherited slack-three strong family is

S(W)=C3[K1,W,K1],  n(W)≥2,

with W an arbitrary singleton/triangle word. It has f=t(W)+1 and s=3t(W), so its slack is3. Its endpoints are intrinsic; thus W is recovered from the strong tournament, and its series is

S_3(z)=z^2(A(z)−1−z).                              (8)

The excluded words W=empty and W=1 produce slack zero, not three. The regular five-tournament R5 has f=3,s=5 and slack4, giving atom series z^5.

For d=3a+4b with a≥0 and b in{0,1,2}, take an ordered join containing, in a fixed order, a copies of the variable strong family S(W) and b copies of R5. Put an arbitrary W-word before, between, and after these a+b exceptional strong components. The uniqueness of the ordered SCC decomposition, and the intrinsic recovery of each S(W)'s interior, make this an injective class family. Slack adds under ordered join, since feedback and full-group moved support both add across the unique SCC order. Its exact series is

z^(2a+5b)(A(z)−1−z)^a A(z)^(a+b+1).                (9)

Its pole at ρ has exact order2a+b+1, with positive leading coefficient. This proves that the full nonnegative class series F_d cannot have a smaller pole order. It also proves existence for every d in the semigroup generated by3 and4. Conversely this semigroup consists exactly of the nonnegative integers except1,2,5, already excluded above.

For d=0, formula(9) with a=b=0 is simply A(z), the known zero-slack classification. For positive d, the subfamily may not start at the smallest realizable vertex order; no minimal-size claim is made.

## 6. Reduced denominator and recurrence

The compiler upper bound and the positive subfamily lower bound prove an exact pole of order m_d at ρ. The polynomial p(z)=1−z−z^3 is irreducible over Q: its only possible rational roots are±1 and neither is a root. The reduced rational denominator already divides p^(m_d); a pole of order m_d at one root of the irreducible factor therefore forces the entire reduced denominator to equal p^(m_d), normalized to constant term one.

Reversing the denominator yields (E^3−E^2−1)^(m_d), of degree3m_d. This is the minimal eventual homogeneous constant-coefficient recurrence, not merely an annihilator. The unique smallest-modulus zero of p is its positive root, so the exact asymptotic(3) follows. The positivity argument avoids any coefficient-cancellation assumption at the dominant pole.

## 7. Scope

The new work is the actual-orbit separator bound and its exact pole-order consequence. It does not change the frozen canonical-compiler theorem. It does not provide a complete slack-six numerator, an efficient compiler, the earliest index of a recurrence, or any black-degree/metagraph connectivity statement. The scalar denominator proof does not identify individual Hamiltonian paths or marked ports.

No computations are used to infer the all-order exponent. A separate finite checker verifies the orbit-penalty optimization and attainable formulas over a bounded range as controls, not as proof.

## 8. Exact self-converse and converse-merged denominators

Let C_d count self-converse classes and M_d count converse-merged classes, and put q(z)=p(z^2)=1−z^2−z^6. For every nonempty slack layer, the sharpened long-slot bound and the proved palindrome-channel compiler give

denominator(C_d) divides q(z)^(k_d),  k_d=ceil(m_d/2).   (10)

We now give an injective self-converse family attaining this pole order. This construction avoids having to identify every maximal W-module of the constructed objects.

### Wrapper lemma

For any tournament U of order N≥2, set Wrap(U)=C3[K1,U,K1], with x→U→y→x. The vertex x has outdegree N. The only possible other vertex of this degree is a source of U, which is unique if it exists, and x beats it. Hence x is the uniquely recoverable source of the maximum-score class, whose size is one or two. Likewise y, of score one, is the uniquely recoverable sink of the minimum-score class. The two score classes are distinct for N≥2. Thus x and y are intrinsic, every automorphism fixes them, and

Aut(Wrap(U))=Aut(U) in its action on U,
f(Wrap(U))=f(U)+1,
δ(Wrap(U))=δ(U)+3.                                 (11)

The feedback identity is the weighted C3 substitution formula with block sizes1,N,1: its least backward cost is min(N,N,1)=1. The wrapper is strong and determines U after deleting its intrinsic endpoints. Converse swaps the endpoint roles and sends U to U^op, so Wrap(U) is self-converse exactly when U is self-converse. The restriction N≥2 is essential: U=K1 produces C3 with extra rotations and slack zero.

### Nested symmetric construction

Write d=3a+4b with b in{0,1,2}. Fix the cutoff d and require every variable W-word below to have more than d vertices. Start with

T_0=W_0 ▷ R5 ▷ W_1 ▷ ... ▷ R5 ▷ W_b,

containing exactly b copies of R5. For j=1,...,a define recursively

T_j=V_j ▷ Wrap(T_(j−1)) ▷ V'_j,                    (12)

where V_j,V'_j are new long W-words. The inner order is always at least two whenever a wrapper is applied, because its base contains a word longer than d and d≥3 if a>0. Feedback and moved support add over ordered SCC joins, so (11) gives δ(T_a)=3a+4b=d.

This family has exactly2a+b+1 independently chosen long words. It is injective: at each positive recursion depth, the unique non-W strong component is the wrapper; the outer W-words are recovered from the ordered SCC decomposition. Its intrinsic endpoints then recover T_(j−1). At depth zero, the b strong R5 SCCs distinguish the base gaps; for b=0 the sole remaining object is its W-word. This argument uses actual isomorphism structure, not an unverified core presentation.

For self-converse members, impose V'_j=reverse(V_j) at every recursion step and impose W_(b−i)=reverse(W_i) at the base. If a base word is central, require it to be palindromic. These conditions are necessary and sufficient by SCC order, the wrapper lemma, and self-converseness of R5.

Let L_d(z)=A(z)−[degree≤d]A(z), and let P_{>d}(z) be the long-palindrome series from the proved compiler. The resulting self-converse subfamily has exact series

z^(2a+5b) L_d(z^2)^(a+floor((b+1)/2))
             P_{>d}(z)^((b+1) mod2).              (13)

Since P(z)=(1+z+z^3)/q(z), the series(13) has a positive pole at sqrt(ρ) of exact order

a+ceil((b+1)/2)=ceil((2a+b+1)/2)=k_d.

Together with (10), this proves an exact pole of order k_d for C_d. The statement includes d=0: the base is a single palindrome word and differs from the full zero-slack channel only by its finite excluded head.

### Irreducibility and exact minimal recurrences

The polynomial q is irreducible over Q. Here is a short integer proof. Work with the monic polynomial r(z)=−q(z)=z^6+z^2−1. Modulo2,

r(z)=(z^3+z+1)^2,

and the cubic is irreducible because it has no root in F2. By Gauss's lemma any rational proper factorization of r must consist of two monic integer cubics, each reducing to z^3+z+1. The even symmetry z→−z exchanges the cubics: neither normalized monic cubic can be fixed by it, since such a cubic would be odd and have zero constant term. They therefore have the forms

z^3+A z^2+B z+C,   z^3−A z^2+B z−C.

Their reductions force A even and B odd. But the coefficient of z^4 in their product is2B−A^2=0, which forces B even. Contradiction.

Thus the reduced denominator of C_d is exactly q^(k_d). Its minimal eventual recurrence polynomial is

(E^6−E^4−1)^(k_d),  of exact order6k_d.             (14)

Finally p and q are coprime. A common root would satisfy z+z^3=1 and z^2+z^6=1, and squaring the first equation would imply2z^4=0, impossible. Since M_d=(F_d+C_d)/2, neither family's poles can cancel against the other. The merged reduced denominator and minimal eventual recurrence polynomial are exactly

p^(m_d) q^(k_d),
(E^3−E^2−1)^(m_d)(E^6−E^4−1)^(k_d),               (15)

of total order3m_d+6k_d. These claims do not specify the earliest recurrence onset. No single positive asymptotic constant for C_d across both parities is asserted: its dominant poles occur at both sqrt(ρ) and−sqrt(ρ), and parity can affect leading terms.

## 9. An explicit sufficient recurrence onset

The exact denominator degrees give linear recurrence orders in d. The finite compiler also bounds how many initial coefficients suffice before the homogeneous recurrence is guaranteed. This is a sufficient onset, not the earliest onset, and the required initial coefficients have not been enumerated for every layer.

For a nonempty layer d≥1, put H_d=d(4d+1)=4d^2+d. With coefficients indexed from a_0, safe homogeneous recurrence onsets are

ordinary:         n≥H_d+3m_d+1,
self-converse:    n≥H_d+6k_d+1,
converse-merged:  n≥H_d+3m_d+6k_d+1.                 (16)

Thus the sequence through one less than its stated onset determines the full sequence by its proved recurrence. This is a quadratic bound on the length of a sufficient finite prefix, not a polynomial-runtime claim for obtaining that prefix or for computing unrestricted A000568.

To prove it, consider a compiler configuration with q_core≤4d+1 quotient vertices, ℓ long slots, and short total vertex weight A. Since each short word has at most d vertices,

A≤d(q_core−ℓ).                                    (17)

The ordinary long tail is L_d=N_L/p, with deg(N_L)≤d+3. Clearing the exact denominator p^(m_d), a term has numerator degree at most

A+ℓ(d+3)+3(m_d−ℓ)=A+dℓ+3m_d≤H_d+3m_d.            (18)

For the converse channel, write ℓ=2r+j with j in{0,1}. A paired-word numerator has degree at most2d+6 over q, and a palindromic-tail numerator has degree at most d+6 over q. After clearing q^(k_d), the bound is

A+r(2d+6)+j(d+6)+6(k_d−r−j)
 =A+dℓ+6k_d≤H_d+6k_d.                             (19)

The exponents being cleared are nonnegative because ℓ≤m_d and ceil(ℓ/2)≤k_d. In the merged channel, bringing both summands to the exact common denominator p^(m_d)q^(k_d) bounds the numerator degree by H_d+3m_d+6k_d.

A rational series N(z)/D(z), with D(0)=1, satisfies the homogeneous coefficient recurrence from D at every index strictly greater than deg N, provided the required earlier coefficient indices are available. Here each bound is at least the denominator degree, so (16) is a valid ordinary recurrence onset with no negative-index convention required.

For d=0, the explicit series have sufficient onsets n≥3, n≥6 and n≥9 respectively. In particular the merged zero-slack numerator is1−z^2−z^4−z^6 over p q, so the order-nine recurrence applies from n=9. No smaller onset is claimed for any channel here.
