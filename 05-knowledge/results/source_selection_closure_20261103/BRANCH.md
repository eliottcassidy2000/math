## B2. A strict-growth lemma makes the small searches complete

Use row bitsets for outgoing arcs. A positive-polarity command is encoded
`3*mask+discard`: the newborn loses exactly to the retained slots selected by
mask, beats every other old vertex, then replaces slot 0 or 1, or is itself
forgotten when discard=2. Masks 1 and 2 give the six inherited adaptive
commands. Masks 1,2,3 give **nine proper-cut commands**.

**Lemma.** Adjoining a vertex with nonempty incoming and outgoing old
neighborhoods to a strong tournament preserves strongness and strictly
increases its Hamilton-path count. In fact `H_new>=H_old+2`.

Strongness follows using an old incoming neighbor, an old outgoing neighbor,
and old strong paths. For the count, every old Hamilton path admits at least
one legal position for the new vertex. Choose an old Hamilton path starting
at a vertex beaten by the newborn; such a path exists by cutting a directed
Hamilton cycle of the strong source. It visits the nonempty incoming side.
Besides insertion at the beginning, either its last vertex beats the newborn
or there is a later incoming-to-outgoing transition, giving a second legal
position. Deletion of the newborn distinguishes these extensions, so
`H_new>=H_old+1`; oddness of both tournament counts strengthens this to +2.
The directed Hamilton cycle existence is the usual strong-tournament fact;
in the present C3-generated searches it also persists directly by inserting
each proper-cut newborn at a cycle edge crossing the cut.

For an old source of order at least three, masks 1,2,3 are proper cuts.
Consequently every generated graph here stays strong. Rejecting H above a
bound loses no later return, and there are only finitely many remaining
words. Exact carrier merging is valid for the declared future commands,
not for arbitrary native transformations of the merged source graphs.
