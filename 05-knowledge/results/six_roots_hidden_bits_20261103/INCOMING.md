## Combining two boundary defects changes the bit budget

[WINGS](../defect_offset_dynamics_20261103/WINGS.md) gives actual responses

    H=2(xy+bc)−1,
    x=2^m(2^l−1)−2^(l−1)+2, b=2^(l−1)−1,

and similarly(y,c) from(n,k), with all four exponents positive. Integer
realizability requires b+1 a power of2 and(x+b−1)/(2b+1) a power of2>=2.
The actual exponents cannot be recovered merely from modular phases.

Modulo7, each wing has nine phase states(m,l mod3) but seven present
response pairs(x,b). Six fibres are singletons; the seventh has size3.

l-growth exposes all three choices. This is the same finite-boundary
mechanism as the golden reader's three-way zero collision, with a different
arithmetic map and operation language.

The incoming theorem proves81 distinct future phase states for two wings.
Our independent complete81-by81 growth census sharpens the memory reading:

| Current observation | Different observed values | Largest future-distinct fibre | Minimal uniform fixed-length tag |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
|Both wing pairs modulo7|49|9|4 bits|
|Only H modulo7|7|18|5 bits|

For the first row the worst fibre is the product of two three-way erased
phases. For the second, the full residue histogram at0,...,6 is

    (18,12,11,8,13,13,6).

All81 phase states are separated by future independent block growths,
so P1's criterion gives the lower bounds; labelling each finite fibre gives
the matching upper bounds for this modular machine. This does not assert

This is a precise way to unify the successful one/two-bit examples with
the growing-memory counterexamples: exceptional fibres combine, and a
coarser scalar may merge additional combinations. The memory cost must be
computed after specifying both the observation and the future operations.
