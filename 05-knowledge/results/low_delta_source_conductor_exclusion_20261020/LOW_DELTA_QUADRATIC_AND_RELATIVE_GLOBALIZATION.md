# Low-delta plane polynomial curves: quadratic coordinates and the first cubic boundary

20 October 2026. **PROVED; independently reviewed.** This packet combines the low-delta theorem with the pinned, independently reviewed cylinder-generator obstruction specified below.

## Outcome and exact scope

Let `k` be a field of characteristic zero and let

    A=k[f(y),g(y)] ⊂ B=k[y],   Frac(A)=k(y),
    δ=dim_k(B/A)<∞.

Then the numerical semigroup of degrees of nonzero elements of `A` is symmetric, with largest gap `2δ−1` when `δ>0`. In particular:

1. **δ≤2 implies that A contains a monic quadratic in y.** No immersion hypothesis is needed.
2. After an affine translation of y, every such algebra has the form

       A=k[t²,t p(t²)],   deg p=δ,
       conductor(A,B)=p(t²)B.

3. Over C, the immersed δ=2 cases are precisely two ordinary nodes or one ordinary tacnode in this normal form. The two pairs in the nodal case necessarily have the same sum in the original normalization coordinate.
4. The bound is sharp: `C[y³,y⁴+y]` is immersed, has δ=3 and conductor `(y³+1)²C[y]`, and contains no quadratic.
5. At δ=3 the exact algebraic alternatives are a quadratic model with `deg p=3`, or an algebra generated by elements of degrees 3 and 4.
6. For the actual primitive monic Keller source algebra, a quadratic over C(x) globalizes over C[x], using specialization injectivity and the one-line injectivity theorem. This does **not** require constant fiber delta or a constant conductor leading coefficient.
7. Combining this with the published cylinder-generator obstruction proves that a noninvertible Keller map under these monic/primitive hypotheses has generic source-conductor degree at least six. At degree exactly six only the nonquadratic cubic/quartic presentation over C(x) remains.

The symmetry result is classical; the proof below is a short self-contained trace/residue derivation suitable for the existing conductor packet. No historical novelty is claimed. No assertion here settles the planar Jacobian conjecture or excludes the remaining cubic/quartic case.

## 1. Elementary trace-dual conductor calculation

Assume first that f is nonconstant; otherwise interchange f and g. Scale f to be monic and write `u=f(y)`, `n=deg f`, `D=k[u]`, `L=k(y)`, `K=k(u)`. The extension `L/K` has degree n and is separable. Since `K(g)=L`, the monic minimal polynomial `R(u,v)` of g has v-degree n. Its coefficients lie in D because g is integral over the integrally closed ring D. Consequently B and A are free D-modules with bases

    1,y,...,y^(n−1),      1,g,...,g^(n−1),

respectively. For a D-lattice M in L, let

    M†={z∈L : Tr_(L/K)(zM)⊂D}.

For a monic polynomial H of degree n with simple roots and root θ, Lagrange interpolation gives

    Tr(h(θ)/H'(θ)) = coefficient of T^(n−1)
                     in the remainder of h(T) modulo H(T).

The resulting pairing on the power basis is anti-triangular with unit anti-diagonal. Hence it is perfect over D, and

    B†=(1/f')B,     A†=(1/R_v(f,g))A,     M††=M

for these lattices. Since A⊂B, we have B†⊂A†. Applying this inclusion to `1/f'` proves that

    c(y)=R_v(f(y),g(y))/f'(y) ∈ A⊂B.

This establishes polynomiality of c even without immersion. For b∈B, perfect duality then gives

    bB⊂A
      iff b A†⊂B†
      iff (b/c)A⊂B
      iff b/c∈B.

Thus the conductor is exactly `cB`.

Let M be the D-inclusion matrix from the power basis of A to that of B, and let `I=det M`. Smith normal form gives

    δ=deg_u I.

The discriminant change-of-basis identity and the monogenic discriminant formulas give, with cancelling signs,

    Norm_(L/K)(c)=I².

For every nonzero polynomial h(y),

    deg_u Norm_(L/K)(h)=deg_y h.

To see this, extend constants to an algebraic closure and factor h into linear factors; the norm of `y−r` is a nonzero constant times `f(r)−u`, of u-degree one. Therefore

    deg_y c=2δ.                                         (1)

This proof includes n=1, where A=B and δ=0.

## 2. The residue gap

For every a∈A, the monogenic trace-dual identity gives

    Tr_(L/K)(a/R_v(f,g))∈k[u].                           (2)

Since `du=f'(y)dy`, the rational differential

    ω_a = a(y)dy/c(y)

can also be written as `(a/R_v)du`. The polynomial map `u=f(y)` has exactly one normalization point over u=∞, namely y=∞. Compatibility of trace and residue therefore gives

    Res_(y=∞) ω_a
      = Res_(u=∞)(Tr_(L/K)(a/R_v)du)
      = 0.                                              (3)

The last equality follows from (2): a polynomial times du has no residue at infinity.

For completeness, the trace/residue compatibility needed here has an elementary local proof. After extension of constants and a formal change of uniformizer, a totally ramified characteristic-zero map at infinity is `v=z^n`, where v=1/u. Write `ω=h(z)dz=(h(z)/(n z^(n−1)))dv`. Taking the trace sums over `z↦ζz`. Only exponents divisible by n survive. The coefficient of v^(−1) in the resulting differential is exactly the coefficient of z^(−1) in h(z), so the residues agree. Residue is unchanged by the formal parameter change or constant extension.

If `deg a=deg c−1=2δ−1`, the Laurent expansion of `a(y)/c(y)` begins with a nonzero multiple of `1/y`; its residue at infinity is nonzero. This contradicts (3). Thus

    2δ−1 is not the degree of an element of A.            (4)

There is no global dualizing-generator ambiguity in this proof: the differential is the single explicitly specified `dy/c`, and its trace identity is global. Equivalently, `dy/c` is the canonical plane-curve dualizing generator; no unrelated local choices or nonconstant numerator units are being glued.

## 3. Degree semigroup and low-delta consequences

Let `Γ={deg a:0≠a∈A}`. Since products add degrees, Γ is a numerical semigroup. The number of its gaps equals δ: in each polynomial-degree filtration step, the dimension increases by one exactly when that degree occurs in A. Equivalently, choose a monic element in each occurring degree and reduce leading terms; the missing monomials form a vector-space basis of B/A.

If Γ has δ gaps and largest gap F, the map `s↦F−s` sends `Γ∩[0,F]` injectively into the gaps. Hence `F+1−δ≤δ`, or `F≤2δ−1`. Combining this with (4) gives `F=2δ−1`. Equality in the same counting argument shows that exactly one of s and F−s lies in Γ for each `0≤s≤F`; this is symmetry.

For δ=0, A=B and y²∈A. For δ=1, the only gap is 1, so degree 2 occurs. For δ=2, degree 3 is absent by (4). If no degree-2 polynomial existed, then degree 1 would also be absent: a nonconstant linear polynomial generates B. The three gaps 1,2,3 would contradict δ=2. Thus a quadratic exists. Scaling its leading coefficient gives a monic quadratic.

For δ=3, the largest gap is 5. If degree 2 occurs, the quadratic normal form below applies. Otherwise 1,2 are gaps. Symmetry forces 4,3 into Γ; the only gaps are 1,2,5, so `Γ=<3,4>`. Choose monic h,k∈A of degrees 3,4. Then `A₀=k[h,k]` has fraction field k(y): `[k(y):k(h)] =3` and `[k(y):k(k)] =4`, so the degree over the intermediate field k(h,k) divides both. Its basis `1,k,k²` over k[h] has degrees 0,4,8 modulo 3, respectively. Consequently `Γ(A₀)=<3,4>` and `dim_k(B/A₀)=3`. Since A₀⊂A and both have codimension 3, A₀=A.

Over any characteristic-zero field one may further translate y, scale h,k, and subtract constants and a multiple of h from k to write this second alternative as

    A=k[t³+a t, t⁴+b t²+c t].                          (5)

This is a statement about the algebra and a chosen generating presentation; it does not claim that arbitrary original generating pairs are related by a specified ambient automorphism.

## 4. Quadratic normal form and immersed δ≤2 classification

Complete the square in a monic quadratic. After a translation `t=y+β/2` and subtraction of a constant, `t²∈A`. Every a∈A has an even part in k[t²], already in A, so its odd part also belongs to A. Thus

    A=k[t²] ⊕ t I,

where `I={q∈k[z]:t q(t²)∈A}` is a nonzero ideal of the PID k[z]. Write `I=p(z)k[z]` with p monic. Then

    A=k[t²,t p(t²)],
    B/A ≅ t k[t²]/t p(t²)k[t²],
    δ=deg p.

For `b=e(t²)+t o(t²)`, the conditions b∈A and tb∈A are respectively p|o and p|e. Since B is generated by 1,t as a k[t²]-module, this proves

    conductor(A,B)=p(t²)B.

Over C, the parametrization `(t²,t p(t²))` is immersed exactly when `p(0)≠0`: its two derivatives can vanish together only at t=0. This property depends only on A, so it is equivalent to immersion for any generating pair of A.

At δ=1, immersion gives `p(z)=z−a` with a≠0, yielding one ordinary node. At δ=2:

- `p(z)=(z−a)(z−b)` with distinct nonzero a,b gives exactly two ordinary nodes, with normalization pairs `{±√a}` and `{±√b}`.
- `p(z)=(z−a)²` with a≠0 gives a single ordinary tacnode. Near its two preimages, z=t² is a local coordinate and the two branches are `w=±√z (z−a)²`, whose difference vanishes to exact order two.

These alternatives exhaust immersed δ=2 plane polynomial curves. Returning to y, both nodal pair sums are `−β`. Hence a generic abstract gluing of two pairs with unequal sums cannot be a two-generator plane polynomial algebra, even though each local singularity is an ordinary node.

## 5. Sharp immersed boundary and exact conductor

Take

    f=y³,     g=y⁴+y=y(f+1).

The derivative pair `(3y²,4y³+1)` has no common zero. Also `y=g/(f+1)` lies in the fraction field. The monic equation is

    R(u,v)=v³−u(u+1)³.

The conductor calculation gives

    c=R_v(f,g)/f'=3g²/(3y²)=(y³+1)².

As a C[f]-module, A has basis 1,g,g². Their degrees modulo 3 are 0,1,2, so no leading cancellation between these three summands is possible. Its degree semigroup is exactly `<3,4>`, with gaps 1,2,5. Thus δ=3 and there is no quadratic. The three distinct roots of y³=−1 all map to (−1,0); their tangent slopes are `g'/f'=−1/y²`, which are distinct. This is an ordinary triple point. No smaller positive delta provides a counterexample.

### A sharp boundary with only nodes

A second example, supplied by the root calculation, is

    f=y³−y,     g=y⁴,
    R(u,v)=v³−2v²+v−4u²v−u⁴,
    c=(y²−1)(y⁴+1).

Again the degree semigroup is `<3,4>`, δ=3, and the derivative pair has gcd one. This conductor is squarefree of degree six. The three collision pairs can be determined without radicals for each individual parameter. For a distinct pair (t,y), put `s=t+y`, `p=ty`. Equality of f-values gives `s²−p=1`, while equality of g-values gives `s(s²−2p)=0`. Thus the three pair sums are

    s=0, √2, −√2,       p=s²−1.

Each quadratic `T²−sT+p` has two distinct roots, and the pairs are disjoint: the first pair is `{1,−1}`, while the other two pair-quadratic polynomials multiply to `T⁴+1` and have no common root. At a collision, the tangent determinant is

    f'(t)g'(y)−f'(y)g'(t)
      =4(y−t)[3p²−(s²−p)]
      =8(y−t) ≠0.

Hence the image has exactly three ordinary nodes and no other singularities. A quadratic identifying a pair of distinct normalization parameters must have its linear coefficient equal to minus their sum. The three different sums therefore give a direct second proof that this algebra has no quadratic. The obstruction beyond δ=2 persists even with exclusively transverse double points; it is not peculiar to the triple-point example.

The failed implication beyond the theorem is: small conductor or immersion alone does not force a quadratic. At δ=3 the nonquadratic symmetric semigroup `<3,4>` becomes possible. The two-generator hypothesis, not merely local nodality or principal conductor in B, is essential below that boundary.

## 6. Relative quadratic globalization for actual Keller source rings

Let `R₀=C[x]`, `K₀=C(x)`, `B₀=R₀[y]`, and let `S=R₀[P,Q]⊂B₀` arise from an actual planar Keller map with P monic in y and `C(x,P,Q)=C(x,y)`. Use the monic-v relation `R(x,u,v)` from the existing actual-source packet. Its Section 1 proves, for every a∈C, that `R(a,u,v)` is the monic minimal polynomial of Q_a over C(P_a), of degree deg_y P. Therefore

    S/(x−a)S → B₀/(x−a)B₀

is injective for every a. It follows that B₀/S has no (x−a)-torsion, hence no R₀-torsion. Explicitly, if `(x−a)b∈S` for b∈B₀, the zero specialization of that element in B₀ implies it lies in `(x−a)S`, and cancellation gives b∈S. Factor a general nonzero base polynomial to obtain saturation for every such polynomial.

Suppose `S_K₀` contains `y²+β(x)y+γ(x)`. Subtract γ(x), clear denominators, and use saturation to divide the gcd of the two coefficients. We obtain

    α(x)y²+b(x)y∈S,     gcd(α,b)=1,     α≠0.

If α has a zero a, then b(a)≠0; specialization shows `b(a)y∈C[P_a,Q_a]`, so that algebra equals C[y]. The Keller map is consequently injective on the source line x=a. By Gwoździewicz's one-line injectivity theorem it is an automorphism.

Thus, for a noninvertible Keller map, α has no complex zero and must be constant. Dividing by it supplies the required global monic quadratic in S. An automorphism has S=B₀ and supplies a quadratic trivially. This proves globalization in all cases under the stated actual-source hypotheses.

### 6.1 Combined actual-source conductor exclusion

The additional dependency in this subsection is the independently reviewed and published [moving cylinder-generator obstruction](../cylinder_generator_period_obstructions_20261020/MOVING_HYPERELLIPTIC_GENERATOR_OBSTRUCTION.md), Theorem B, Proposition C, and Corollary D, pinned to repository commit `9605e62f324416ddb1a448c2e923d12e7806166f`. That packet proves the entire-generation Keller obstruction for

    C[x,y²,y g(x,y²)],    g(x,0)∈C*,

whenever `2 deg_w(g)+1` is prime, and recognizes this entire algebra from a principal conductor plus a monic quadratic coordinate. It explicitly permits degree-drop fibers.

**Corollary (generic conductor at least six, with a cubic/quartic boundary).** Let `F=(P,Q)` be a complex planar Keller map, let P be monic in y, and assume `C(x,P,Q)=C(x,y)`. Write `S=C[x,P,Q]⊂B₀=C[x,y]`, and let `cB₀` be its actual normalization conductor. If F is not a polynomial automorphism, then

    m=deg_y c ≥6.

If m=6, then `S⊗C[x]C(x)` contains no quadratic in y. Its degree semigroup is exactly `<3,4>`, and it is generated over C(x) by polynomials of degrees three and four. After an affine change of the normalization parameter over C(x) and a change of these chosen generators, its presentation is

    C(x)[t³+a(x)t, t⁴+b(x)t²+d(x)t],
    a(x), b(x), d(x)∈C(x).

**Proof.** The actual-source conductor identity gives `m=2δ_K`, where `δ_K=dim_C(x)(C(x)[y]/S_K)`. Generic degree zero would give a source line with normalization algebra C[y], hence invertibility by the one-line theorem. Thus a noninvertible F has positive even m. If m≤4, Sections 1–3 give a quadratic in S_K and Section 6 globalizes it to a monic quadratic in S. The source shear completing its square has Jacobian one. Proposition C of the pinned dependency, using the actual source's principal conductor, identifies the entire source ring after that shear with `C[x,y²,y g(x,y²)]`, with `g(x,0)∈C*` and `deg_w g=m/2`. For m=2 or 4, the odd exponents are respectively 3 or 5, both prime; Theorem B of that dependency contradicts the generating Keller pair. Hence m≥6.

If m=6 and a quadratic exists in S_K, the same globalization and recognition apply with `deg_w g=3`; Theorem B now applies because the odd exponent is 7, again a prime. This excludes the quadratic alternative. Section 3 therefore gives exactly the nonquadratic `<3,4>` alternative and a cubic/quartic generating presentation over C(x). QED.

The degree m here is the degree in the normalization variable of the source conductor, **not** the function-field degree of F. No global cubic/quartic presentation over C[x], further exclusion of that case, universal degree-eight bound, or resolution of the planar Jacobian conjecture is asserted. The initial quadratic criterion has been discharged through δ=2 only; the explicit δ=3 hostiles show why it cannot be discharged from immersion and codimension alone.
