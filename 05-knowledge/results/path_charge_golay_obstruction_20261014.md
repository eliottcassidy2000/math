# Positional path charge: a ternary Golay obstruction and a Fermat splitting theorem

Date: 2026-10-14. Status: proved algebraic statements and independently replayable finite certificates. The proposed universal positional-charge surjectivity statement is **false**. A Fermat-prime version remains an open question, with a structural reason to distinguish it and only the finite controls stated below.

## 1. The carrier and its counting purpose

Let p be an odd prime and let S select exactly one of each pair {s,-s} in F_p*. Write T_S for the cyclic tournament with u -> v when v-u belongs to S. Put

\[
 O=\mathbb Z[\zeta_p],\qquad
 \eta=1+\sum_{s\in S}\zeta_p^s,\qquad R_S=O/(\eta).
\]

The preceding [cyclotomic note](fermat_platonic_cyclotomic_orientation_20261014.md) proves

\[
 |R_S|=N(\eta)=\operatorname{disc}(T_S)
 =\frac{\det(I+K)}{2^{p-1}},\qquad K=A-A^{\mathsf T}.
\]

Here the norm is positive: conjugate pairs of the eigenvalues contribute positive real factors. In particular eta is nonzero. Translation acts freely on ordered Hamiltonian paths, and each orbit has a unique representative P=(0,v_1,...,v_{p-1}). Consequently there are H(T_S)/p normalized paths for every prime cyclic tournament. Consider their positional charge

\[
 C(P)=\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}i\zeta_p^{v_i}\pmod\eta. \tag{1}
\]

Surjectivity of (1) would give H(T_S)>=p disc(T_S) by a genuine finite-set carrier. The counterexample below refutes this particular universal carrier, not that counting inequality. The earlier [bounded-flip theorem](fermat_bounded_flip_norm_bound_20261014.md) obtains explicit inequalities by a different mechanism: short-polynomial Fourier energy versus odd-cycle-collection counts.

## 2. Bezout identity and the odd-order norm-divisor theorem

Because S and -S partition the nonzero residues,

\[
 \eta+\overline\eta
 =2+\sum_{j=1}^{p-1}\zeta_p^j=1. \tag{2}
\]

Thus the principal ideals (eta) and (bar eta) are comaximal. This is the key structural restriction on the quotient.

**Theorem.** If a rational prime ell divides N(eta), then ell is not p and ord_p(ell) is odd.

**Proof.** The unique prime ideal over p is generated by 1-zeta_p. Modulo this ideal,

\[
 \eta\equiv 1+|S|=(p+1)/2\not\equiv0\pmod p.
\]

Thus p does not divide the norm. Now let a prime ideal over ell contain eta, and let xi be the image of zeta_p in its residue field. Since ell differs from p, xi has exact order p. Put d=ord_p(ell). If d were even, the cyclic group F_p* would give ell^(d/2)=-1 mod p. Applying that power of Frobenius to eta(xi)=0 gives bar eta(xi)=0. This contradicts (2). Hence d is odd. QED.

Equivalently, the irreducible factors of Phi_p modulo ell occur in reciprocal Frobenius pairs when the order is odd. The reduction of eta can vanish on at most one member of each reciprocal pair. A self-reciprocal factor cannot divide eta, since Frobenius would preserve it while implementing conjugation. This explains both the norm restriction and why a residue quotient naturally carries a cyclic code. It does not assert that every ideal satisfying this restriction is realized by a tournament.

**Fermat corollary.** If p-1 is a power of 2, every rational prime divisor ell of disc(T_S) satisfies

\[
 \ell\equiv1\pmod {2p},\qquad \ell\ge2p+1. \tag{3}
\]

Indeed the only odd divisor of p-1 is 1, so ord_p(ell)=1. The prime ell is odd, yielding the congruence modulo 2p. This applies to every Fermat prime, without assuming the five known examples exhaust them.

Every such ell splits completely in the **ambient cyclotomic field**. The exact quotient can still have several primary factors and higher valuations. If (eta)=product q^(e_q), the Chinese remainder theorem gives

\[
 R_S\cong\prod_{\mathfrak q\mid(\eta)}O/\mathfrak q^{e_{\mathfrak q}}.
\]

In the Fermat case each factor over ell is isomorphic to Z/ell^(e_q)Z, because its unramified local degree is one. This does not make the whole quotient a field, squarefree, or cyclic. Conjugate primes cannot both occur. Condition (3) excludes the small-characteristic coefficient repetitions used in the p=11 counterexample; it does not prove surjectivity of (1).

### Connection to 1093 and 3511

The [Wieferich tournament-lift packet](wieferich_tournament_lifts_20261014.md) studies a different invariant: order lifting from p to p^2. Here only order parity is needed. Since ord_1093(2)=364 is even, every prime cyclic tournament at p=1093 has odd disc. Since ord_3511(2)=1755 is odd, characteristic 2 is allowed at p=3511. It actually occurs: for the Paley tournament at 3511,

\[
 \operatorname{disc}=((3511+1)/4)^{(3511-1)/2}
 =878^{1755},\qquad v_2(\operatorname{disc})=1755.
\]

The Paley identity follows from its nonconstant adjacency eigenvalues (-1 +/- sqrt(-p))/2, whose shifted conjugate pair has product (p+1)/4. The verifier checks the two orders and exact arithmetic; it does not construct a 3511-by-3511 matrix. Being Wieferich and having odd order of 2 are distinct conditions.

## 4. Paley 11: exact failure and failed endpoint repair

Take

\[
 p=11,\qquad S=\{2,6,7,8,10\},\qquad N(\eta)=243.
\]

This is the Paley tournament on the quadratic nonresidues. Its exact quotient is

\[
 R_S\cong\mathbb F_3[x]/(f),\qquad
 f=x^5+2x^3+x^2+2x+2. \tag{4}
\]

The polynomial f divides Phi_11 modulo 3 and eta(x) modulo 3. Since ord_11(3)=5, every irreducible factor of Phi_11 over F_3 has degree 5, so f is irreducible. Evaluation therefore maps O onto F_243 and kills eta. Both the kernel and (eta) have index 243, proving equality and (4).

Exact depth-first enumeration gives 8,645 normalized Hamiltonian paths (53,491 visited prefixes). Their charges occupy 241 of 243 residues; the missing elements are precisely the field constants 0 and 1. The fixed-field fibre counts for charges 0,1,2 are respectively 0,0,200.

The focused candidate repair

\[
 C_+(P)=C(P)+\zeta^{v_{10}}
\]

also occupies exactly 241 residues and misses 0 and 2. Its fixed-field fibre counts are 0,105,0. Thus adding the endpoint does not repair the carrier.

The order-five multiplier subgroup <3>={1,3,9,5,4} acts as Frobenius on (4). Its action on normalized ordered paths is free, so the fibres above fixed-field elements have sizes divisible by 5. This divisibility alone cannot explain the exclusions: the nonzero counts 200 and 105 obey the same divisibility. The decisive obstruction is the coefficient composition in the following code.

## 5. The Golay obstruction occurs before tournament edges

Let

\[
 \mathcal C=\{f(x)h(x):\deg h\le5\}\subset\mathbb F_3^{11},
\]

with coefficient vectors indexed by 0,...,10. This is a six-dimensional cyclic code. It is permutation-equivalent to the ternary Golay [11,6,5] code. The [official GUAVA manual, section 5.4-3](https://gap-packages.github.io/guava/doc/chap5_mj.html), documents the generator

\[
 h_G=x^5+x^4+2x^3+x^2+2.
\]

Our f equals 2 x^5 h_G(x^-1). More explicitly, length-11 coordinate reversal R(c)=x^10 c(x^-1) sends x^j h_G to 2 x^(5-j) f for 0<=j<=5, identifying the two codes. The proof below needs only the displayed generator, not a classification theorem for Golay codes.

**Quadratic identity.** Every c=(c_0,...,c_10) in this code satisfies

\[
 \sum_{v=0}^{10}c_v^2+\left(\sum_{v=0}^{10}c_v\right)^2=0
 \quad\text{in }\mathbb F_3. \tag{5}
\]

To prove it, take the six shifted generator rows f,xf,...,x^5f and extend each by minus its coefficient sum. Each unextended row has sum 2. The inner product of every pair of unextended generator rows, including a row with itself, is 2 modulo 3. Adding the extension coordinate changes this to 2+1=0. The six extended rows therefore have zero Gram matrix, and every linear combination is self-orthogonal. This is precisely (5).

Now take **any** permutation P=(0,v_1,...,v_10), without requiring tournament edges. Its charge polynomial beta has coefficient beta_(v_i)=i mod 3. Therefore

\[
 \beta_0=0,\quad
 (\#0,\#1,\#2)=(4,4,3),\quad
 \sum_v\beta_v=1,\quad\sum_v\beta_v^2=1
 \quad\text{in }\mathbb F_3.
\]

If its charge in (4) were a constant a in F_3, then beta-a would lie in C. Applying (5), and using beta_0=0, gives

\[
 1+a^2+(1-a)^2=0. \tag{6}
\]

Among a=0,1,2, only a=2 satisfies (6). Thus charges 0 and 1 are impossible for **every normalized vertex permutation**, already before Hamiltonian admissibility is imposed.

For the endpoint candidate beta_+=beta+x^(v_10), the endpoint coefficient changes from 1 to 2. Its square is unchanged modulo 3, while the coefficient sum changes to 2. Hence a constant charge must satisfy

\[
 1+a^2+(2-a)^2=0,
\]

whose only solution is a=1. This proves the failed repair's exclusions 0 and 2 at the same pre-tournament level.

### Complete enumeration corroboration

The frozen certificate contains all 729 codewords, in lexicographic order of the six coefficients of h. The independent verifier reconstructs them, checks (5) for every word, and checks their complete composition enumerator (18 composition classes). The ordinary weight enumerator is

## 6. Order-retaining local moves and their limitation

Suppose positions i,i+1,i+2 contain a directed triangle (a,b,c). Provided the boundary arcs remain valid, cyclically rotating this block gives exact increments

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 (a,b,c)\mapsto(b,c,a):&\quad\Delta C=2\zeta^a-\zeta^b-\zeta^c,\\
 (a,b,c)\mapsto(c,a,b):&\quad\Delta C=\zeta^a+\zeta^b-2\zeta^c.
 \end{aligned} \tag{7}
\]

For i>=1 these moves keep the starting vertex 0. The boundary condition is essential; an internal directed triangle need not admit either rotation inside a Hamiltonian path.

If an ordered Hamiltonian path closes to a directed Hamiltonian cycle, moving its first vertex v_0 to the end gives the **unnormalized** formula

\[
 C(\operatorname{left}P)=C(P)+p\zeta^{v_0}. \tag{8}
\]

The sign is plus: every other coefficient decreases by one, while the departed vertex receives coefficient p-1, and the sum of all p characters is zero. If P starts at 0 and its next vertex is v_1, renormalizing the new first vertex gives

\[
 C(\operatorname{normalize}(\operatorname{left}P))
 =\zeta^{-v_1}(C(P)+p). \tag{9}
\]

Multiplication by p is invertible in R_S by the norm-divisor theorem, but valid moves do not provide unrestricted affine translations of the quotient.

For an exact obstruction, in the p=17 example of section 3 take P=(0,16,15,...,1), whose charge is 76. Every consecutive triple is a directed triangle, yet neither rotation preserves its boundary arcs. For a block starting after position 0, the new incoming step would be -2 or -3, neither in S. For the initial block the outgoing boundary fails instead. Thus it has no consecutive triangle-rotation moves, even if one first allows a new start and then normalizes. It also closes to a constant-step Hamiltonian cycle, whose cyclic-start rotations all normalize to the original P. Adding (9) does not connect this isolated path to the certified surjective component. This is a failure of a particular connectivity argument, not of p=17 surjectivity.

## 7. What remains open, and what the next test should mean

The universal map (1) fails for an exact cyclic-code composition reason. Restricting the question to Fermat primes is justified by theorem (3), which forbids the small residue characteristic responsible for this example. It remains a question, not a consequence of splitting:

> For a Fermat prime p and any skew S, does the positional charge of normalized Hamiltonian paths cover O/(eta)?

The full first nontrivial Fermat sector has 16 prime-cyclic isomorphism classes at p=17. Only the one explicitly identified class is certified here. A finite extension should verify witnesses in the actual integral product/primary quotient rings, rather than replacing a composite quotient by a convenient field. Even a complete p=17 result would remain finite evidence.

A more structural next target precedes tournament constraints: characterize which coefficient compositions occur in cosets of (eta) after reduction at its prime ideals. For the proposed map, the relevant composition is forced by the position weights 0,...,p-1 and by the normalization beta_0=0. The Golay example shows exactly why this algebraic feasibility stage cannot be skipped. Once a residue passes that stage, Hamiltonian admissibility and the order-retaining moves (7)-(9) are separate constraints.
