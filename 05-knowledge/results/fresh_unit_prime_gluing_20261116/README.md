# A real fresh unit, a root-change barrier, and exact prime-gluing families

- [Literal bounded +2 readers](UNIT_READER.md). Matched right
  readers of count 109 give exactly +2 on an unbounded incoming
  family, with the *same full continuation*. Count-197 readers
  do the same in strong tournaments. At incoming H=17 the
  strong output pair is 7843/7845. Neither has multiplier 3
  or refreshes the unit for arbitrary repetition.
- [Root reset and its limits](BRIDGE_RESET.md). Right reroot
  with a triangle can escape the fixed-root 3-adic trap
  indefinitely. But any common rerooting macro sends an old
  scalar difference into a permanent deletion-count difference.
  A mixed-policy one-step pair of count-three companions gives
  synchronized +4 but not +2. [Exact scalar probes](OBSERVABLE.md)
  show why g cannot simply be removed from this bridge interface.
- [(J) on the actual induced inputs](PRIME_GLUE.md). Exclude
  (11,11), which would give forbidden 21. Transitive overlap
  cannot work for prime inputs, and at least four shared
  vertices are necessary. Four are sufficient for 13,13→25;
  there are distinct-prime examples and exact two-scale
  gluing on an infinite family of physical counts. More general
  port substitutions preserve the relation with the shared
  Hamilton count in place of the constant one.
- [Prime target lanes](J_TARGET_LANES.md). One two-scale
  template is locked to hosts divisible by 3 when its inputs
  are primes. An asymmetric exact template escapes: e.g.
  79+73−1=151 and 18439+18433−1=36871 are all-prime triples
  with literal induced witnesses. These are sparse families,
  not uniform prescribed-target gluing.
- [Half-index and ready seeds](SPINE_AND_HALF_INDEX.md).
  h(10)=21; the failed (11,11) row is the addition 5+5=10.
  The ternary carry needs prepared seeds at the children
  of the two holes, not just a verified scalar cutoff.
- [Archive synthesis](ARCHIVE.md). Exact future observers
  from the planar Jacobian work, terminal relation gluing
  and a C5 conflict from Hadwiger–Nelson, the 128/125
  rescaling of the 1024 identity, Fibonacci-box pairings,
  and the distinct meanings of 24 and 13 in Bernoulli
  denominators and Niemeier roots.

**Open:** combine a fresh bounded unit with exact factor-three
transport while keeping earlier digits freely selectable.
A fixed-root modulo-three invariant and the observed root-change
drift rule out the simple common-bridge approach. The positive
J families supply literal templates, not a universal prime- or
7p-target inversion. Chan–Pak remains open.
