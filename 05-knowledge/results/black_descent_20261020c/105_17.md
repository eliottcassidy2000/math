## 2. Three distinct 105-sets

Let `X` be the perfect matchings of `{0,...,7}`, with `7` marked. Deleting
the pair `{7,i}` gives a three-pair matching of the other six labels,
together with its unmatched label `i`. Thus

```text
X = {(i,M): i in {0,...,6}, M a perfect matching of the other six},
|X| = 7 * 15 = 105.
```

This is the inherited heptic involution carrier of cycle type `2,2,2,1`.
Let

```text
Y = {(T,L): |T|=3, L a perfect matching of the other four},
|Y| = choose(7,3) * 3 = 105.
```

Its element is exactly a seven-port closure with one all-distinct cubic
junction on `T` and two equality wires on `L`. These are different
`S7` sets. A transposition fixes 15 elements of `X`, but 25 of `Y`.
For `Y`, either both moved labels lie in the triple, giving `5*3=15`,
or they form a wire, giving `choose(5,3)=10`. Therefore there is no
`S7`-equivariant bijection.

The earlier root-and-pair carrier, of type `4,2,1`, is a third 105-set.
Its transposition fixed count is 35, as already checked in the 105 atlas.
These three tests distinguish the actions before attempting any transport.

There is nevertheless a canonical incidence:

```text
R[(i,M),(T,L)] = 1  iff L is a two-pair subset of M.
```

Equivalently, delete one pair from `M` and combine its two endpoints with
the unmatched label `i` to make the triple `T`. Every row and column has
three entries. The common refinement has 315 flags. This is the
unsigned containment matrix between the two highest matching levels of
`K7`. It retains a specific deletion flag rather than inventing a
one-to-one identification.

### The rooted transposition identity

Let `rho` act on the eight-label matching space, and put

```text
Z = sum_(a=0)^6 rho((a,7)).
```

Then, as integer matrices,

```text
R R^T = 2 I + Z.                                      (1)
```

Indeed a row has three self-incidences. Two different near-perfect
matchings share a lower matching exactly when one is obtained by moving
the unmatched label across one of the three matched pairs. Each has six
such neighbours. In `Z`, the transposition with the marked point's current
partner fixes the matching, and each other transposition produces one of
those six neighbours. This proves every matrix entry in (1).

`Z` is the usual rooted sum of transpositions often called a
Jucys--Murphy element; the entrywise proof needs no representation-theory
input. The marked point is necessary: (1) is `S7`-equivariant, not a
claim that the lower matching level naturally carries the whole `S8`.

The exact characteristic polynomial is

```text
char(R R^T) =
 t^14 (t-9) (t-7)^14 (t-5)^21 (t-4)^14 (t-2)^6 (t-1)^35.
```

In particular `rank_Q R=91`.

## 3. The same 14-dimensional kernel as eight-port colouring

For an eight-port matching `M`, retain the inherited ternary equality
function

```text
B_M(s) = product_({a,b} in M) [s(a)=s(b)].
```

The ordinary word-count Gram matrix is
`G[M,N]=3^(number of components of M union N)`. Its known spectrum is

```text
t^14 (t-945) (t-210)^20 (t-120)^14 (t-30)^56.
```

Split the eight labels into two four-sets and antisymmetrize the 24
cross-matchings. Its colouring function is a four-by-four determinant
of equality indicators and is zero because the colour space has
dimension three. The 35 such relations span a 14-dimensional space `D`.

**The same determinant relation annihilates `R`.** Fix a two-edge matching
`L`. If it is not cross-bipartite in the specified four-four split, no
cross-matching above it contributes. Otherwise, among its three completions
in `X`, exactly two are cross-bipartite. Their determinant signs are
opposite. The corresponding entry of the relation times `R` is zero.

Thus

```text
D subset ker(R^T) and D subset ker(G),
dim D=14,  rank R=rank G=91,
so ker(R^T)=ker(G)=D.                                  (2)
```

The rank equalities are independently certified by exact integer
characteristic polynomials and modular elimination at 101, with the
displayed integer relations providing rational upper bounds.

Consequently there is a well-defined, invertible linear map

```text
span_Q{B_M}  -->  im(R^T),
sum_M c_M B_M  |--> R^T c.                             (3)
```

This is stronger than an equality of ranks: it is the *same coefficient
kernel*. It transports every linear dependence among eight-port pairing
measurements to a concrete matching-level incidence.

It does not identify an eight-port boundary word with a seven-port word,
or transfer a colouring obstruction without transforming the tested
functional. Negative coefficients occur in the quotient relations.
In particular, the three neighbours of a row of `R` form a formal sum
of three different closure kernels, not automatically one realized
cubic gadget with a selector.

## 4. Composing with actual junction probes loses fourteen more coordinates

Let `A` be the `105 by 91` matrix of seven-port junction kernels on the
91 colour-permutation orbits with all three multiplicities odd. Its
rank is 84. The inherited seven blind functions are

```text
w_i = +1 on a (5,1,1) orbit where i is singleton;
      -1 on a (3,3,1) orbit where i is singleton;
       0 otherwise.
```

They satisfy `ker A=span{w_i}`. Their exact Gram matrix is particularly
simple:

```text
<w_i,w_j> = 16 if i=j, 1 otherwise,
W W^T = 15 I + J.                                     (4)
```

There are six `(5,1,1)` singleton-pair orbits and ten `(3,3,1)` orbits
contributing to the diagonal; two different ports overlap only on their
singleton-pair orbit. Hence this is the natural permutation module on
seven ports, split into one constant and six standard dimensions.

Now form `R A`. **Its rank is 70, not 84 or 91.** There is an explicit
description of every additional loss. Give each unordered port pair
`{i,j}` a symmetric rational weight `a_ij`, subject to

```text
sum_(j != i) a_ij = 0 for every i.                     (5)
```

This edge-weight space has dimension `21-7=14`. Define `u_a` by

```text
u_a(A_ij) = -8 a_ij,
u_a(B_i;U,V) = sum_({p,q} subset U) a_pq
             +sum_({p,q} subset V) a_pq,              (6)
```

where `A_ij` is the `(5,1,1)` orbit with singleton ports `i,j`, and
`B_i;U,V` has singleton `i` and monochromatic triples `U,V`.
Then

```text
ker(R A) = span{w_0,...,w_6} direct-sum {u_a : (5)}.    (7)
```

Here is the cancellation mechanism. For a row `(i,M)` of `R A`, the
accepted singleton pairs are its three matched pairs and its six pairs
incident to `i`. Thus the sum of their `a`-values is `sum_(e in M) a_e`.
Among accepted `(3,3,1)` words, each edge of `M` occurs twelve times
inside a monochromatic triple, and each other edge four times. Their
weighted sum is

```text
4 sum_all_edges a_e + 8 sum_(e in M) a_e
 = 8 sum_(e in M) a_e.
```

The factor `-8` in (6) cancels it. The two subspaces in (7) are disjoint:
if `a_ij` is proportional to `c_i+c_j`, condition (5) forces every `c_i`
to vanish. They provide 21 independent null vectors; the exact rank-70
lower bound makes this the whole kernel.

The distinction is usable. After replacing all 105 junction measurements
by the 105 sums prescribed by `R`, the seven inherited tree closures
alone no longer guarantee reconstruction. They can add at most seven
dimensions to rank 70. Extra measurements that detect the 14-space (6)
are required. This is an ambient tensor statement; it does not assert
that every null vector is a difference of realizable cubic-pole tensors.

## 6. Seventeen Lagrangians give seven matchings and ten Hamming pieces

This gives a second structural connection, independent of the rank
calculation.

Let

```text
V = {even subsets of ten labels}/complement,
<S,T> = |S intersect T| mod 2.
```

It is an eight-dimensional binary symplectic space. A Lagrangian is a
four-dimensional subspace. A *spread* is a collection of Lagrangians
partitioning the nonzero vectors. Each member contains 15 nonzero
vectors, so every spread has `255/15=17` members.

Existence is explicit. Identify `V` symplectically with `F16^2`, with form
`Tr(x y'+x' y)`. Take

```text
L_t = {(x,t x): x in F16}, t in F16,
L_infinity = {(0,y): y in F16}.                        (10)
```

Every member is isotropic, different members intersect only in zero,
and every nonzero vector belongs to one member. The script uses
`F16=F2[X]/(X^4+X+1)` and stores the full symplectic coordinate map.

Lift each Lagrangian to a self-dual binary length-ten code, as in the
Fifteen/ten-label packets. Every such code is either:

- a matching code, with exactly five weight-two words; or
- `H8+I2`, with exactly one weight-two word, its distinguished pair.

There are exactly 45 duad vectors in `V`, and the spread partitions them.
If its numbers of these two code types are `x,y`, then

```text
x+y=17,          5x+y=45,
so x=7, y=10.                                        (11)
```

**Spread theorem.** Every Lagrangian spread in this ten-label model
contains exactly seven matching codes and ten `H8+I2` codes.
The seven matchings are edge-disjoint in `K10`. Their union is
7-regular. The ten distinguished Hamming pairs form its complementary
2-factor.

There is a stronger incidence condition: **the union of any two of the
seven matching factors is a Hamiltonian ten-cycle**. Each component of
the union is an even alternating cycle, and its vertex set is a codeword
in both matching codes. If there were more than one component, this
would give a common nonzero vector in the quotient by complementation,
contradicting the spread condition. Thus these seven factors are a
partial perfect one-factorization of `K10`, not just edge-disjoint
matchings. This condition is necessary; no converse realization theorem
is asserted.

This is an actual `17 -> 7+10` structure, with the same matching/Hamming
primitive types as the inherited Fifteen atlas. It does not identify the
ten Hamming pieces with Petersen vertices plus an adjacency rule.

The explicit spread (10) gives the residual ten-cycle

```text
0--5--9--2--8--4--1--3--6--7--0.
```

Applying the symplectic transvection
`x -> x + <x,a>a` with retained quotient mask `a=23`
gives a residual `C4 disjoint-union C6`. Both complete spread witnesses,
the seven matchings, and all lifted codes are in the JSON receipt.
All 255 single nonzero transvections of the original spread give
180 ten-cycle and 75 four-plus-six-cycle cases. This is precisely a
255-operation test of one spread, **not** a census of all spreads or all
symplectic changes of coordinates.

A separate [deterministic walk probe](spread_walk_probe.py) now tests
10,000 visits from four walks of 2,500 transvections each, starting from
the same spread. Its [receipt](spread_walk_probe.json) gives 6,892 visits
with residual `C10` and 3,108 with `C4+C6`. It records the four seeds and
full first witnesses. Visits can repeat and are not a complete orbit
census. In particular, neither this test nor the 255-operation test
proves that all spread residuals are even. A residual `C5+C5` remains a
useful decisive target for a Petersen-like cubic completion.

There is a useful Petersen hostile already in the first example. Add
any of its seven matching factors to the residual `C10`. Each result is
a simple connected cubic graph with an explicit Hamiltonian cycle and a
three-edge-colouring: alternate two colours on that cycle and use the
third on the matching. None is Petersen, and none can have Petersen as
a minor: both have ten vertices and fifteen edges, so such a minor
would have to be an isomorphism. The spread structure supplies genuine
colourable cubic graphs; its seventeen objects do not force a snark.
