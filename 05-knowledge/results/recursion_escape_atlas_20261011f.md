# Recursion interfaces and an any-distance equality compiler

2026-10-11. **PROVED:** the conditional serial-spindle construction and its
collision-safe critical-edge reduction below. **FINITE-EXACT:** three supplied
two/three-colour seeds and their declared transports. The historical atlas
inherits each cited source's stated scope; it is not a new audit of every
source. **OPEN:** a six-equality seed in the real unit-distance plane, whether
its distinct ports are unit separated or at any other positive distance.

## 1. Inheritance and the object that has to survive

The closest constructive mechanism is the generalized spindle in
[THM-4558, residue colourings of field planes](../../01-canon/theorems/THM-4558-residue-colourings-of-field-planes-the-heegner-tower-is-at-most-four-chromatic.md),
together with the authenticated finite graph/refutation interface in
[finite algebraic witnesses](finite_algebraic_witness_20261007.md).
The canonical hostile is a large recursion that never leaves a field with an
inherited colouring bound. The corrected near miss is the former Heegner
rotation roadmap: MISTAKE-576 and the attribution/scope correction MISTAKE-579
are retained in THM-4558. More rotations or vertices alone do not raise a
chromatic number. The least-used sidecar here is the map from **each original
copy's vertices** into the actual union, including all coincidences.

The live board is: (i) named equality ports, (ii) complete boundary support,
(iii) ordered operation words, (iv) geometric incidence/field, and (v) a
finite authenticated contradiction. The anchor is a six-equality fragment;
subdivision/word recursions are the niche; rational curve recursion supplies a
wildcard test of whether algebraic closure also preserves the desired predicate.

For a finite unit-distance graph on distinct points, a **k-equality seed**
means a supplied proper k-colouring and two distinct named vertices that have
the same colour in every proper k-colouring. Equivalently, adding an
**abstract** edge between the ports gives a non-k-colourable graph. This
abstract test edge need not be unit. Its refutation authenticates equality;
it is not itself a geometric construction.

## 3. The incoming context-root mechanisms

The incoming [golden/taxicab boundary note](golden_taxicab_boundary_20261011.md)
has scoped, self-audited geometric claims, not a six-equality theorem. Its six
private leaves at one hidden centre have extension weight `6-j`, where j is
the number of distinct boundary colours. Consequently the star excludes only
the all-six-distinct boundary pattern; it does not force a chosen equality.
Golden and taxicab coordinates can realize that same relation without making
their arithmetic carriers interchangeable.

Its private-leaf compiler is a stronger useful interface: preserve a supplied
unit-distance core and choose rational points on each vertex's unit circle
avoiding finitely many intersections. A precoloured leaf assignment leaves
lists `A_v` on the core; extensions are exactly its proper list-colourings.
For two adjacent hidden vertices the count is
`|A||B|-|A intersect B|`. Equal list sizes or the total number of boundary
colours do not determine this count. The repeated construction supplies actual
geometric boundary relations, but useful boundary assignments must still be
enforced when gadgets are glued.

The concurrent [pencil normalizer](legendre_pencil_normalizer_20261011.md)
is inherited only in its declared birational symplectic scope: for
`I=x^2t^2+(1+x^2)t`, permitting `I composed with F=h(I)` does not escape its
four fixed-invariant maps. The elliptic j-invariant permits six base changes;
the differential multiplier eliminates the additional ones. Thus “same
invariant profile” and “same permitted transformation” differ. This is an
analogy of **missing sidecar**, not a map from elliptic recursions to plane
colouring.

## 4. Any positive equality distance is sufficient, conditionally

**Theorem.** Let F be a finite k-colourable unit-distance graph on n distinct
points in the real plane, with distinct forced-equal ports u,v at distance
`d>0`. Choose any integer `m>=1` with `R=md>=1/2`. There is a finite
non-k-colourable unit-distance graph on at most `2m(n-1)+1` points.

**Construction and proof.** Translate u to zero and put `h=v-u`. Place copies
of F at translates `jh`, for `j=0,...,m-1`. Their consecutive ports coincide,
so every hypothetical proper k-colouring equates all ports from 0 to `mh`.
Rotate the whole chain about 0 by a unit complex number rho satisfying

\[
 \operatorname{Re}\rho=1-\frac1{2m^2d^2},\qquad
 \operatorname{Im}\rho=\frac{\sqrt{4m^2d^2-1}}{2m^2d^2}.
\]

Then `|(rho-1)mh|=1`. The two chains share 0 and force their far tips equal.
Adding the actual unit edge between those tips gives a contradiction.
Each translated chain contributes at most `1+m(n-1)` points, and the two
chains share their initial point, proving the bound.

Each individual copy is injective and isometric. Additional coincidences
between different copies are retained, never split into fictitious vertices.
The hypothetical output colouring restricts to every original F, so those
coincidences cannot invalidate the implication. They can make a chain already
non-k-colourable; the proof does **not** assume intermediate colourability.
Unlisted additional unit incidences can only impose further restrictions.

If coordinates are real algebraic, all new coordinates remain real algebraic.
Relative to a field containing the old coordinates and `d^2`, the displayed
rotation needs at most the corresponding square-root extension. It need not
belong to the original field. The checker below deliberately accepts only
rotations represented exactly in its inherited Moser field; the general real
theorem is not limited to that implementation.

**Critical-edge corollary.** The following existential statements are
equivalent for a fixed positive k:

1. A finite unit-distance graph has chromatic number greater than k.
2. There is a k-colourable unit-distance graph with distinct unit-separated
   forced-equal ports and their unit edge omitted.
3. There is such a k-equality graph with ports at some distance d>0.

For 1 implies 2, delete edges from a finite non-k graph until it is edge-minimal
non-k. Deleting any retained edge makes it k-colourable, and its endpoints
must then agree in every k-colouring. Its chromatic number is exactly k+1:
after deletion a k-colouring exists, and restoring one edge requires at most
one new colour. Implication 2 implies 3 is immediate; 3 implies 1 is the
theorem. Isolated vertices may be retained or removed.

In particular, an arbitrary nonzero equality distance is **not a weaker
independent route** to a six-equality seed. It is another exact form of the
same existence problem. The compiler is useful once a seed has been supplied;
it does not manufacture one from subdivision counts or an elliptic invariant.

## 5. Source-authenticated compiler and hostile controls

`EqualitySeed` stores exact points, actual unit edges, two named ports, a
proper k-colouring, and a complete finite refutation after adding the abstract
port inequality. `verify_seed` rechecks all of them. `amplify` takes an exact
positive integer m and a supplied exact unit rotation, checks the tip distance,
deduplicates actual points and stores the source-to-union map of every copy.
`verify_amplification` reconstructs the whole recipe and verifies all seams.
Only the explicitly named `discover_seed` and critical-edge test helper use
finite colouring search; production verification/amplification do not search
for a new equality seed or invoke an unresolved mathematical oracle.

The implementation uses the inherited exact basis `1,a,b,ab`, where
`a=(1+i sqrt(3))/2`, `b=(5+i sqrt(11))/6`. It verifies three controls:

| Supplied seed | Chaining / rotation | Actual result |
|---|---|---|
| Two-edge path, ports0 and1/5; middle `1/10+3i sqrt(11)/10`; k=2 | m=3, `rho=-7/18+5i sqrt(11)/18` | 13 vertices,13 unit edges; independently refuted with2 colours |
| Diamond `0,1,a,1+a`, ports0 and1+a; k=3 | m=1, rho=b | Classical7-vertex/11-edge spindle; independent3-colour refutation |
| Path `0,a,1`, ports0 and1; k=2 | m=1, rho=a | 4 actual vertices, not nominal5: an extra coincidence is retained; a unit triangle already obstructs2 colours |

For each control, critical-edge extraction returns a supplied k-colourable
unit-port equality packet. The checker also transports each seed through all
six a-rotations and three translations, giving54 exact source-frame controls.
Twelve malformed packets test bool/float aliases, nonunit rotation, bad tip
distance, forged source colouring/refutation, nonunit source edge, altered
copy maps and deleted output edge. Small scalar, lattice and elliptic controls
exercise the atlas's operation distinctions; they are not new global claims.

## 6. The next productive distinction

For candidate recursions, first record the complete two-port colour support,
then the geometric copy maps and actual cross-edges. If the seed still permits
unequal port colours, the conditional theorem cannot be invoked. If the seed
is genuinely equal but its allowed rotations remain in a field with a
colouring bound, that field restriction must be reconciled with the explicit
rotation required by the theorem. This separates a missing equality relation
from a missing geometric realization; neither is repaired merely by more
iterations of a storage or subdivision operation.

The bounded test reaches its intended stopping point: an exact compiler with
source provenance and collision controls, and a historical map of what the
available recursions actually preserve. No six-equality or seven-chromatic
plane graph is produced.
