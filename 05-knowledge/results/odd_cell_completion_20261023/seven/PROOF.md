The new step is that **every nonuniform equitable attachment to a
regular seven-vertex cell has a trivial one-sided chart kernel**. Its

strictly weakening the earlier distinct-row/column hypothesis.

Moreover, two actual owners in one root cell force ordinary directed
equitable refinement to become discrete throughout its connected
nonuniform seven-cell support. This stronger conclusion does not follow
merely from absence of automorphisms in a general tournament.

Concept board: actual owner marks; chart kernels rather than raw repeated
rows; directed equitable partitions; nonuniform support components;
group base size versus refinement; preserved path order.

## 1. The only nonidentity automorphism cycle shapes

Let R be any regular tournament on seven vertices. Every automorphism
has odd order: an even-order automorphism would have an involution as a
power; a nonidentity involution swaps some two vertices and hence
reverses their directed arc, which is impossible.

The possible nonidentity odd cycle types on seven points are

    7; 5+1+1; 3+3+1; 3+1+1+1+1.

The five-cycle type is impossible. Either fixed vertex has a uniform
orientation to that five-cycle, and therefore has outdegree at most one
or at least five, contradicting regular outdegree three.

For the last type, each of the four fixed vertices has either zero or
three outgoing arcs to the three-cycle. Its outdegree in the induced
four-vertex tournament would consequently have to be zero or three.
A tournament has at most one vertex of outdegree zero and at most one
of outdegree three; four such vertices are impossible.

Thus every nonidentity automorphism is either a seven-cycle or has
cycle type **1+3+3**. In particular, no nonidentity automorphism fixes two
distinct vertices. The automorphism group acts freely on the 42 ordered
distinct pairs. Its order is odd and divides42, so is in

    {1,3,7,21}.                                           (1)

This statement does not use the exhaustive three-class census. Every
subgroup obtained by retaining further shared-attachment constraints
has the same two-point base bound.

## 2. The kernel theorem allows rectangular blocks

Let M be an a-by-seven zero-one matrix, with a>0, every row sum k, and
every column sum l. Let R be a regular-seven tournament on its columns.
Assume 0<k<7, so M is nonuniform. Then

    M(i,g(j))=M(i,j) for every i,j, g in Aut(R)

forces g to be the identity.

**Proof.** If g is a seven-cycle, each invariant row is constant, contrary
to 0<k<7. Otherwise g has two three-cycles and a unique fixed point z.
Every invariant row is a union of those orbits, so

    M(i,z) = k modulo3.

If k is two modulo3, no such row exists. Otherwise the fixed-point entry
is the same zero or one in every row. Consequently column z has sum
zero or a. Equal column sums then make every column zero or every
column one, again contradicting 0<k<7. QED.

This is a constant-row AND constant-column assertion. Equitability is
essential. A single arbitrary row invariant under an order-three
automorphism certainly exists; it is not an equitable attachment.

Now compare two supplied attachment systems with internal source and
target charts. If one source-side chart admits two compatible
seven-side charts f,g, their quotient is a nonidentity automorphism of
the target regular-seven cell fixing its block on that side. The theorem
rules this out. Thus **a chart at the other endpoint determines at most
one regular-seven chart**. If both ends are regular-seven cells, the
reverse implication holds too: compatibility is an actual partial
bijection, regardless of repeated binary rows or columns.

An empty relation correctly rejects that comparison; it does not mean
the individual tournament or a nonflat owner connection is invalid.
The identity comparison always retains its identity chart section.

## 3. A small classification of equitable partitions, not of tournaments

Every cell of an equitable partition of a tournament has odd cardinality:
its induced tournament has constant outdegree (|D|-1)/2. The possible
nontrivial proper equitable partitions of a regular-seven tournament
would therefore have sizes

    5+1+1; 3+1+1+1+1; or 3+3+1.

The first two are ruled out by exactly the fixed-vertex degree arguments
above, with equitability replacing invariance under an automorphism.
Hence the only possibility is **1+3+3**.

In that case the singleton beats exactly one triple and loses to the
other, since its total outdegree is three. The triples are directed C3s.
Their cross block has degree one in one direction and two in the other,
so it is a matching or the complement of a matching. Any matching
between two C3s conjugates their cyclic rotation groups to each other.
Simultaneous matching-compatible rotations give a nonidentity order-three
automorphism preserving all three cells setwise.

Two consequences follow without an enumeration:

1. **Any two distinct actual owners inside R force discrete equitable
   refinement.** A retained owner is a singleton, whereas the only
   nondiscrete proper stable partition has just one singleton.
2. Any proper nondiscrete equitable partition of R has a nonidentity
   cell-preserving automorphism. Thus, in this specific seven-vertex
   carrier, a proper stable colour partition without such an automorphism
   must be discrete. This inference is false for arbitrary larger
   tournaments, including inherited order31 controls.

## 4. Discreteness propagates across every nonuniform attachment

Suppose named cells X,Y belong to an equitable tournament partition, Y
induces a regular-seven tournament, and their block is nonuniform. Once
all actual owners in X are singleton colours, refinement makes every
stable cell in Y have identical columns toward those individual owners.

If Y were still nondiscrete, it could not remain one seven-cell because
the nonuniform equitable block has some distinct columns. Its induced
stable partition would therefore be 1+3+3. Section3 supplies a nonidentity
automorphism of Y preserving those colour cells, and hence fixing each
column word toward X. This contradicts Section2. Therefore Y becomes
discrete as well.

This propagation works from any positive-sized known cell X, not only a
seven-cell. The checker includes equitable 21-by-seven controls. It does
not assert that the reverse chart relation into an arbitrary large X is
functional.

For a finite tournament with a supplied equitable collection of named
regular-seven cells, form a graph joining two such cells precisely when
their block is nonuniform. In **each connected component**, individualize
two arbitrary actual vertices of any root cell. Root refinement becomes
discrete by Section3, then propagates along every graph path by the
preceding argument. All seven-cell vertices in that component are now
literal owners.

Thus two owners per nonuniform support component suffice at every total
tournament order. Uniform attachments cannot synchronize separate
components. Taking arbitrarily many uniformly joined Paley-seven
modules shows that no total constant owner budget follows from this
statement; each independent Paley module really needs two for pointwise
naming. Retaining only one of the two owner labels is also insufficient
for an isolated Paley-seven cell.

Here a cyclic outneighbourhood is an actual rooted four-vertex predicate.
Its total 0,4,7 distinguishes these three internal isotypes, but does not
recover any cross-block owner alignment. The group-of-three type has one
fixed owner; marking that vertex wastes the one-mark opportunity. Mark
one of its six moved vertices instead. Consequently a support component
containing a non-Paley regular-seven cell can be made discrete with one
suitable owner at that root. A Paley-only component can still be rigid
because of incompatible attachment symmetries; the universal two-owner
refinement rule is sufficient, not necessarily minimum there.

For exact pointwise chart reconstruction, a compatible global group
faithful on a root regular-seven cell has order1,3,7 or21. Its minimum
additional root-owner base size is respectively **0,1,1,2**. For order3,
choose a moved vertex; for order7, every vertex is free. An order21 group
is transitive (it contains a seven-cycle) and every point stabilizer has
order3, so one owner never suffices, while two always do.

This generalizes the group mechanism behind the inherited order31
counts 672/252/6 for zero/one/two extra owners. It does NOT reclassify

warning against conflating a trivial global chart group with colour
refinement. The present propagation theorem additionally needs a
discrete root or the specified intrinsic seven-cell structure.

If an actual Hamiltonian port P is supplied, choose required owners from

chosen owners when applying converse and reversing P; do not separately
rename the same cell at different attachments. The checker considers

## 6. Sharp next boundary and residual potentials

At nine vertices the kernel property fails. Let R9=C3[C3,C3,C3], and
attach two copies by the block I3 tensor J3. Every row and column sum is
three. Independent rotations of the three target triples preserve the
entire attachment, giving a one-sided kernel of order27. This is an
actual equitable tournament attachment, not a synthetic table. The
parent packet checks its complete chart relation and module refinement.

Therefore one must either refine such hidden modules or retain their
nonfunctional relations; indiscriminately extending the seven-cell rule
to arbitrary odd cell sizes is false.

For cells through seven, the new kernel theorem supplies precisely the
functional chart constraints needed by the inherited gluing rule. Once
joint charts are selected, the existing residual-cycle potential test
is applied **inside each branch**, followed by comparison of projected
baselines between branches. No credit, capacity, or owner may be pooled
across incompatible chart choices. The finite chart rule does not alone
prove a native metagraph table exists.
