## Scope and adversarial audit

- The supplied partition is named and equitable; actual cross blocks are
  retained. The canonicalizer does not infer them from anonymous margins.
- A nonempty set of comparison charts is distinct from a parallel-owner
  section. Balanced nonflat five-cell controls and the order31 controls
  preserve that distinction.
- Repeated binary profiles do not automatically give an internal tournament
  automorphism. Conversely, rigidity does not generally imply discrete
  equitable refinement. Both false implications have retained controls.
- The all-prime refinement extension uses FULL Aut(R)=C_p and rational
  irreducibility. A regular prime-eleven hostile defeats primality alone.
- The alignment quotient retains one shared cell rotation across every
  incident edge. The rigid/rigid tree has five distinct alignments; local
  edge classes alone are insufficient even on an acyclic support.
- The cell-preserving quotient, the converse operation, literal path ports
  and native modulo-eight checks are distinct layers. None is discarded
  because another layer has a unique chart or scalar value.
- Residual-potential reuse requires the same projected channel weights
  and a valid bijection of complete branch models. Different branch orbits
  still need baseline comparison. Extra shared-port linear constraints
  require the incoming coupled-move theorem, not a union of graph cycles.
- The zero Fibonacci slot has zero metric length but a real adjacency
  position. Interval products answer queries at declared ports; arbitrary
  future interior ports require refinement and cannot be recovered from
  a finite total product and total debt.
- Nine-cell networks retain 3m independent inner-triple rotations and
  need 3m owners. The all-size result is bounded cell size with stated
  inputs, not a constant observer for arbitrary tournaments.

No theorem is promoted from a reserved identifier. Universal tournament
metagraph wiring, black-core connectivity, and Collatz coverage remain OPEN.
