# Finite-dimensional normalized planar Hamiltonian Lie algebras are abelian

19 October 2026. **Self-contained proof; independent review pending.** Candidate supplied by the coordinating research session and verified here. No historical-novelty claim is made.

Let K have characteristic zero, R=K[[x,y]], and m=(x,y). Equip m^3 with the Poisson bracket

    {A,B}=A_x B_y-A_y B_x.

This is a Lie algebra because {m^a,m^b} is contained in m^(a+b-2). Constants have already been removed by taking m^3.

## Theorem

Every finite-dimensional K-Lie subalgebra L of m^3 is abelian.

More strongly, every nilpotent Lie subalgebra of m^3 is abelian, even if it is infinite-dimensional, provided nilpotence means that its lower central series terminates after a finite number of steps.

## Proof

First let L be finite-dimensional. The descending vector spaces L intersect m^n eventually stabilize. Their intersection is zero because the formal power-series ring is m-adically separated. Thus L intersect m^N=0 for some N. This is a bound on nonzero vanishing orders, not a polynomial-degree argument; it works for genuinely infinite formal series.

A bracket of k elements of m^3 has order at least k+2. The k-th lower central term of L therefore lies in L intersect m^(k+2), and is zero for sufficiently large k. Hence L is nilpotent.

Now let L be any nonzero nilpotent Lie subalgebra. Its last nonzero lower central term contains a nonzero central element Z. Z is nonconstant, so dZ is nonzero in characteristic zero. For every H in L,

    {Z,H}=0.

Pass to the fraction field Frac(K[[x,y]]). The gradient vectors dZ and dH are linearly dependent. Every dH is consequently in the same one-dimensional subspace spanned by dZ. Hence the gradients of any two H,G in L are dependent, and

    {H,G}=0.

The bracket vanishes in the fraction field and therefore in R. Thus L is abelian. The zero algebra is immediate.

## Consequences for polynomial flows

If H_1,...,H_r are polynomial Hamiltonians in m^3 and the Lie algebra they generate is finite-dimensional, then they pairwise Poisson commute. Every finite scalar-time word reduces exactly to

    exp(D_(sum_i lambda_i H_i)).

The single-Hamiltonian classification in the companion note then applies: the word is polynomial precisely when its combined Hamiltonian is a polynomial in a polynomial coordinate.

Conversely, two noncommuting normalized polynomial Hamiltonians generate an infinite-dimensional Lie algebra and a nonterminating lower central series. A finite-dimensional or finite-step-nilpotent BCH model cannot encode their full interaction.

This does not say that every specific noncommuting word has a nonpolynomial logarithm. Special words can cancel, and the earlier three-factor source construction gives a concrete such phenomenon. It excludes the blanket finite-dimensional/nilpotent closure mechanism, not word-specific cancellation identities.

## Sharp boundaries

1. The order-three condition is essential. The quadratic Hamiltonians x^2/2, xy and y^2/2 span a finite-dimensional nonabelian Lie algebra; for example {x^2/2,y^2/2}=xy. Their derivations have nonzero linear parts.

2. Solvability is insufficient. Let G=x^2 y and include x^n for every n>=3. Their span is an infinite-dimensional Lie algebra because

       {x^n,G}=n x^(n+1),       {x^m,x^n}=0.

   The algebra generated by x^3 and G is exactly this span. Its derived algebra is the span of x^n for n>=4, which is abelian. Thus it is metabelian, but its lower central series never terminates. Every generator still belongs to m^3.

3. In higher symplectic dimension the centralizer step fails: gradients commuting with dZ need not lie on one common line. This theorem uses the two-dimensional nature of the symplectic plane essentially.

## Research consequence

The next normalized polynomial-flow family beyond common-invariant models must permit genuinely infinite-dimensional noncommuting Lie structure. The displayed metabelian family is a controlled test case: it retains a simple abelian ideal while escaping every finite-step BCH truncation. Neither this obstruction nor the autonomous-flow classification supplies a reduction for arbitrary Keller maps.
