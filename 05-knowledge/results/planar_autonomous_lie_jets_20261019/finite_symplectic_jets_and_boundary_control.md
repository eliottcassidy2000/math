# Fixed-direction completion of planar symplectic jets and the missing boundary bound

19 October 2026. **Constructive proof; exact controls supplied; independent review pending.** Polynomial completion of symplectic jets is classical. No historical-novelty or minimal-word-length claim is made. This note gives a particularly explicit characteristic-zero two-variable construction, packs all correction degrees into a fixed list of shears, and identifies a precise global supplement that would make a local limit useful for the planar Jacobian problem.

## 1. A fixed list of N+2 shears suffices

Let K have characteristic zero, m=(x,y), and let F in K[[x,y]]^2 satisfy

    F=(x,y) modulo m^2,       det JF=1.

For every N>=2 there are N+2 polynomials f_0,...,f_(N+1) in u^2 K[u], each of degree at most N, such that the chronological composition of

    S_j(x,y)=(x+j f_j(x+j y), y-f_j(x+j y)),
    j=0,...,N+1,

agrees with F modulo m^(N+1). Each S_j is a polynomial automorphism with Jacobian one; its inverse replaces f_j by -f_j. Therefore the completion A_N is a polynomial automorphism with

    number of displayed factors <= N+2,
    deg A_N <= N^(N+2),       deg A_N^(-1) <= N^(N+2).

Zero factors can be omitted. These are uniform upper bounds, not asserted optimal degrees or optimal unrestricted factor counts. The directions 0,1,...,N+1 are fixed before knowing F. There are no roots or extension-field choices.

The noteworthy packing is that we update the coefficients of the same N+2 factors at every order; we do not append a fresh batch of factors at each order.

## 2. Proof by triangular coefficient lifting

First, ell_j=x+j y is fixed by S_j, which proves the inverse formula. Direct differentiation gives its Jacobian one.

Suppose that profiles f_j have already been chosen through degree r-1 so that their composition A agrees with F modulo m^r. The degree-r part (a_r,b_r) of F-A is divergence-free. Indeed, F and A are symplectic and have the same lower-order terms, so their Jacobian difference at degree r-1 is precisely

    partial_x a_r + partial_y b_r=0.

Euler's identity shows that the homogeneous polynomial

    H_(r+1)=(y a_r-x b_r)/(r+1)

satisfies H_y=a_r and -H_x=b_r.

The r+2 binary forms

    (x+j y)^(r+1),       j=0,...,r+1,

form a basis of the degree-(r+1) homogeneous polynomials. In the monomial basis x^(r+1-k)y^k their coefficient matrix is

    V_(k,j)=binom(r+1,k) j^k.

Its determinant is a nonzero product of binomial coefficients and the Vandermonde product of the distinct integers 0,...,r+1. This works over every characteristic-zero K. Solve uniquely

    H_(r+1)=sum_(j=0)^(r+1) c_j (x+j y)^(r+1).

Now add (r+1)c_j u^r to f_j, leaving the other profiles unchanged. At total degree r, changing the j-th shear changes the full composition by

    (r+1)c_j (x+j y)^r (j,-1).

Why does this remain true inside a long, already nontrivial word? Every other factor is identity modulo m^2. Substitution into a homogeneous degree-r correction changes it only modulo m^(r+1), and the derivative of every outer factor is I modulo m. Hence pre- and post-composition leave this leading correction unchanged. Simultaneous changes add at degree r, with cross-effects only in higher degree.

Their sum is exactly (a_r,b_r), so the new profiles match through degree r. Starting from all profiles zero and taking r=2,...,N proves the theorem.

Within this frozen-lower-coefficient lifting mechanism, N+2 distinct direction slots are sharp at the final linear stage: the space of degree-(N+1) Hamiltonians has dimension N+2, and each slot provides only one degree-N scalar coefficient. This is not a lower bound on arbitrary factorizations with movable directions or nonlinear use of lower-order parameters.

## 3. What this proves about the local-to-global gap

Let U be the subgroup generated by polynomial shears tangent to the identity in arbitrary linear directions. Its m-adic closure is the entire formal symplectic group tangent to the identity. Its polynomial members in that closure are precisely all normalized polynomial Keller maps: every such polynomial map has a formal inverse, and the construction matches each of its finite jets.

This statement is not the Jacobian conjecture. Rather, it explains why merely replacing one fixed source line by arbitrary directions and passing to point-adic closure cannot supply the missing global inverse. Such a closure already includes every normalized candidate.

The assertion concerns the germ at one point. It does not say that every finite collection of source points and prescribed target values can be interpolated by an automorphism: distinct source points with the same prescribed image would immediately obstruct that. Nor does it say that a finite jet determines no information when a total degree bound is separately fixed. If the total polynomial degree is known to be <=d, the d-jet already specifies the whole polynomial.

The elementary family H_M=y^3+x^M in the companion autonomous-flow note is an explicit warning independent of unknown Keller counterexamples: its nonpolynomial flow agrees through degree M-2 with a polynomial shear.

## 4. A global supplement that really closes the limit

Here is a precise sufficient condition, stated for any number of variables.

Let A_n be polynomial automorphisms fixing the origin and having identity derivative there. Suppose A_n converges m-adically to a polynomial map F. If all inverse coordinate polynomials A_n^(-1)_i belong to one fixed finite-dimensional K-vector space W of polynomials, then F is a polynomial automorphism.

Proof. Inversion is continuous on the formal tangent-to-identity group, coefficient by coefficient, so A_n^(-1) converges to the formal inverse of F. A finite-dimensional subspace W is m-adically closed: the descending spaces W intersect m^r eventually equal zero, by finite dimension and separatedness. Once r is large enough, reduction modulo m^r is injective on W. A convergent sequence in W therefore becomes literally constant once its r-jets stabilize. Each limiting inverse coordinate lies in W and is polynomial. The formal inverse identities are polynomial identities, hence hold globally. This proves the claim.

A uniform inverse-degree bound is the simplest instance: use all polynomials of degree <=B for W. More geometrically, choose one normal projective compactification X of affine space and one fixed effective Cartier divisor E supported on its boundary. If all inverse coordinate functions have pole divisor bounded by E, they lie in the finite-dimensional space H^0(X,O_X(E)), viewed as regular functions on the affine chart. The same argument applies. The compactification, boundary support and bound must be common to the entire sequence.

This is the useful next obligation for an approximation program: construct a common inverse pole bound. Existence of arbitrarily accurate finite jets, separate compactifications, or a different larger pole bound at every stage does not supply it. No such bound is established for arbitrary planar Keller maps here.

## 5. Literature and verification

Erik Løw, Jorge V. Pereira, Han Peters and Erlend F. Wold, *Polynomial Completion of Symplectic Jets and Surfaces Containing Involutive Lines*, Theorem 1 and Sections 2--4:
https://w3.impa.br/~jvp/symplectic.pdf

The primary PDF was read directly. It proves polynomial completion of symplectic jets using homogeneous Hamiltonians and shear decompositions; the present proof is an elementary two-variable implementation with fixed rational directions and coefficient packing. The finite-jet existence principle is attributed to this established literature, and not claimed as a new obstruction theorem in historical terms.

The exact checker constructs all profiles over Q for the time-one formal flow of H=x^3+y^3+x^2 y^2, for N=2,...,7, and verifies the full resulting word modulo m^(N+1). The saved profiles define genuine polynomial shear words without expanding their potentially huge full compositions. These finite checks supplement the proof; they do not establish the all-order statement by enumeration.
