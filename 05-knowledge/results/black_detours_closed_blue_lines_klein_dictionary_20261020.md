## 3. Literal black detours of every closed blue line, n <= 10

### 3.1 Lemma 1 (length parity; PROVED)

`K_Q(T)` flips exactly the pairs outside `E(Q)`. After `L` moves `T_L = T xor (A \ E_1) xor ... xor (A \ E_L)` with `A` the set of all pairs, and `K_P(T) = T xor (A \ E(P))`. Hence a detour of length `L` exists iff `E_1 xor ... xor E_L = E(P)` for odd `L` and `= A \ E(P)` for even `L` (and each `Q_i` is a directed black Hamiltonian path of `T_(i-1)`). `L = 1` forces `Q_1 = P`, which is blue, so it is impossible. For even `L`, `|A \ E(P)| = C(n-1, 2) <= L (n - 1)`, i.e. `n <= 2L + 2`: two moves need `n <= 6`, four moves `n <= 10`, six moves `n <= 14`. For `n >= 7` every black distance is odd and `>= 3`. (In the census no two-move detour occurs even at `n <= 6`.)

### 3.2 Lemma 2 (reduction; PROVED)

*All non-pure-blue classes lie in one black component iff (a) the line metagraph with the pure-blue nodes deleted is connected and (b) every blue line between two non-pure-blue classes has its two classes in one black component.* (a), (b) are necessary: the black graph is a subgraph of the pure-blue-deleted line metagraph (pure-blue nodes carry no black lines) and black-connected classes are black-connected. Conversely take a walk in the pure-blue-deleted line metagraph between two non-pure-blue classes and replace each blue line by a black walk given by (b). Literal detours certify (b) line by line with a labelled target, which is the lane's stronger currency; the class census of section 2 certifies (a) and (b) at `n <= 9` by union-find.

## 4. Lemma 3: pure-blue-touching lines live in the top two layers (PROVED, scoped)

*Let `n >= 6` and let the closed blue line `{t, kappa t}` have a pure-blue class at one end. Then the pure-blue end is the open member, and the layer of the line is `k = n - 2 - delta`, where `delta in {0, 1}` is the out-degree inside the bottom block of the last vertex of the pure-blue presentation's path, i.e. `delta = 0, 1` for a bottom block `K1, C3` of the palindromic stack (by the centre law, `T5` occurs only as the middle block of an odd stack and never at the bottom for `n >= 6`). In particular `k in {n - 2, n - 3}`. At `n = 5` the strong pure-blue class `T5` contributes its two closed presentations at `k = 2` and its open one at `k = 1`.*

Proof. For `n >= 6` a pure-blue class is non-strong (THM-4729 L3), hence fully open, so the pure-blue end is the open member `T = T_t'` with presentation `P` and the closed member is `K_P(T)`. A non-strong tournament is a stack of its strong components with all arcs downward, so every Hamiltonian path is a concatenation of Hamiltonian paths of the blocks in order, and the last vertex `z` of `P` has out-degree `delta` equal to its out-degree inside the bottom block. The blocks of a pure-blue tournament are strong pure-blue tournaments (THM-2454; palindromic stacks over `{K1, C3, T5}` by THM-2444/2454 to `n = 13` and THM-4730 for all `n`, with the caveat recorded in THM-4729's status), with `T5` only at the centre (THM-2454's centre law; concretely a mirrored pair of `T5` blocks is not pure-blue, since for a given Hamiltonian path of the upper copy only 5 of the 15 paths of the lower copy make the positional reversal an anti-isomorphism, whereas for a mirrored pair of `C3` blocks all 3 do), and the last vertex of a Hamiltonian path of `K1`, `C3` has out-degree `0`, `1`. Under `kappa` the tiles at `z` are flipped and `z` has no path arc out, so `d_(K_P T)(z) = (n - 2) - delta`, which is the layer of the line read at its closed member. The count of pure-blue-touching lines in layer `k` is therefore `sum tc(C)` over the pure-blue classes `C` whose bottom block has `delta = n - 2 - k`. At `n = 9`: bottom block `C3` for `(C3,1,1,1,C3)` and `(C3,C3,C3)` gives `1 + 1 = 2` lines at `k = 6`; bottom block `K1` for the other four gives `1 + 1 + 3 + 1 = 6` lines at `k = 7`, matching section 2; at `n = 10` the four pure-blue classes (`T_10`, `(1,C3,1,1,C3,1)`, `(1,1,C3,C3,1,1)`, `(C3,1,1,1,1,C3)`, THM-2444's `c(5) = 4`) give `3` lines at `k = 8` and `1` at `k = 7`, matching section 3.3. At `n = 5` the regular `T5` is strong and pure-blue with `HC(T5) = 2` (a Hamiltonian cycle of the circulant on `Z_5` with steps `{1, 2}` uses steps summing to `0 mod 5`, forcing all five steps equal), so it has two closed presentations (`k = 2`) and one open presentation whose closed partner has `k = 5 - 2 - 2 = 1`, and the two stacks `T_5`, `(1, C3, 1)` give `k = 3`: the `n = 5` row `1, 2, 2` of the census. QED.

## 5. The Klein dictionary: the 3-4-3 / 4-2-4 chart against complementation, converse and path reversal

### 5.1 The two Klein-sets

On the tilings of order `n` the Klein four-group `<kappa, sigma>` acts; by the ordered-graph trijection `kappa` is graph complementation (the line pairing `T <-> K_P(T)`), `sigma` is the converse together with path reversal (the staircase reflection), and `kappa sigma` is path reversal alone, `(T, P) -> (T, P^rev)`. Information-flow reading, which the owner asked for: the Hamiltonian path is an integer clock, a tile `(i, j)` is a message between the times `i < j` carried forward or backward in time; `kappa` reverses every message (charge conjugation), `sigma` reverses the clock and every message (time reversal composed with conjugation), `kappa sigma` reverses only the clock. On the proper divisors `D(N)` of `N = p^2 q r` the trijection reset's `C` (complement `d -> N/d`), `Q` (exchange of the two equal-exponent primes) and `CQ` act; `C` is the arithmetic charge conjugation, `Q` the arithmetic chirality flip.

Credit: the fixed-point-freeness of `kappa sigma` and the even-`k` obstruction are, concurrently and earlier by hours, the fixed-pair lemma and Theorem 1 of `divisor_reflection_resolution_20261021` (and section 1 of `reflection_context_20261020h`); their twelve-state resolution and capacity theorem are not reproduced here. Kept as this session's: the general-`N` criterion (ii)-(iii), the bijection counts and the two explicit dictionaries of K2, and the information-flow reading. mac-mini's THM-4733 (18:40, pre-audit) states the obstruction in both assignments of the two reflections (its Theorem C) and realises `D(p^2 q r)` as a fold of the four mirror-moved tiles of the order-five cube (its Theorem B); the odd-`k` embeddings and the general-`N` criterion are not there.

**Proposition K1 (PROVED).** (i) `kappa` and `kappa sigma` have no fixed tiling at any `n >= 2`, and `sigma` fixes exactly `2^e`. Hence the `<kappa, sigma>`-orbits on tilings are of two types only: the `2^(e-1)` blue lines `{t, kappa t}` (stabiliser `<sigma>`) and the `(2^m - 2^e)/4` black twin pairs `{t, kappa t, sigma t, kappa sigma t}` (free orbits; these are THM-4731's sigma-twins). (ii) Let `N` be a non-square with two distinct primes `q, r` of equal exponent and `Q` their exchange. On `D(N)`, `C` has no fixed point, `Q` fixes the divisors with equal `q`- and `r`-exponent, and `CQ` has a fixed point iff every prime other than `q, r` has even exponent in `N` (then the fixed points are the divisors with half exponents at those primes and complementary exponents at `q, r`). (iii) Consequently `D(N)` admits a `<C, Q> -> <kappa, sigma>`-equivariant injection into the tilings of some order iff some prime other than `q, r` has odd exponent; for `N = p^k q r` iff `k` is odd. For even `k` the obstruction is exactly the `CQ`-fixed pair (the `f = 0` class of the trijection reset's split `2k, 2, 2k`); for odd `k` the split is `2k+1, 0, 2k+1` and the embedding exists.

Proof. (i) `kappa t = t` would need every tile bit to equal its complement; `kappa sigma t = t` would need the `sigma`-fixed apex tile `(0, n-1)` to equal its complement; `Fix(sigma)` is the `e`-dimensional blue subspace (THM-643). Orbits of an abelian group of order 4 have stabiliser `1`, `<sigma>`, `<kappa>` or `<kappa sigma>`, and the last two are empty. (ii) In exponent coordinates `C(a_i) = (A_i - a_i)`, `Q` swaps the `q, r` coordinates; `C d = d` means `d^2 = N`; `CQ d = d` means `2 a_i = A_i` at every other prime and `b_q + b_r = B` at the pair. (iii) An equivariant injection must carry each orbit onto an orbit with the same stabiliser; tilings offer only stabilisers `1` and `<sigma>` (with `Q -> sigma`), in unbounded numbers as `n` grows, while a `<CQ>`-orbit exists on `D(N)` iff `CQ` has a fixed point. QED.

### 5.2 The eight non-central divisors are the order-4 tilings (PROVED; counts FINITE-EXACT)

**Proposition K2.** For `N = p^2 q r`, deleting the central pair `{pq, pr}` leaves a Klein-set isomorphic to the eight tilings of order 4: the two `Q`-fixed `C`-pairs `{p, pqr}` and `{p^2, qr}` are the two blue lines, the free orbit `{q, r, p^2 q, p^2 r}` is the black twin pair. There are exactly 32 equivariant bijections (`trijection_klein_dictionary_20261020.py`, all `8!` bijections tested): 4 read the sign of `f = a - b - c` as closedness (`f > 0` closed, `f < 0` open), 16 read the sign of `g = b - c` as the chirality `[tile (0,2) forward] - [tile (1,3) forward]` of a black tiling (`g = 0` on the blue tilings automatically), and exactly 2 read both:

    p^2 -> all tiles backward (T*_4, closed),  qr -> all tiles forward (T_4, open),
    p -> (0,2),(1,3) forward, apex backward (closed),  pqr -> apex forward only (open),
    q -> (0,2) and apex forward (open),  r -> (1,3) and apex forward (open),
    p^2 q -> (0,2) forward only (closed),  p^2 r -> (1,3) forward only (closed),

and the same with the two blue lines exchanged. In this dictionary `C` is complementation, `Q` is the converse with path reversal, `CQ` is path reversal, `sign f` is the closed/open bipartition of THM-4729 and `sign g` is the chirality of a black presentation; the height `|f| = 2` pair is the transitive line (the transitive node and its tribonacci partner), and the `|f| = 1` pair is the other blue line. Exactly 2 of the 32 bijections are divisibility-monotone into the Boolean cube of tilings (`d | d'` implies containment of forward-tile sets); they send `p` to the empty tiling and `pqr` to the full one, and neither reads either sign. No equivariant bijection is both sign-reading and monotone, and none is an order isomorphism (the three minimal divisors `p, q, r` against the unique minimal tiling).

So the answer to the owner's question is: *yes, the two reflections of the chart are graph complementation and the converse-with-path-reversal, with path reversal as their product, and a map preserving the group action and both sign observables exists for the eight non-central divisors; the full ten cannot be matched at any order because path reversal never fixes a presentation (the apex tile), whereas `CQ` fixes `pq` and `pr`; and the order structure cannot be carried by the same map as the signs.* The obstruction half agrees with both concurrent packets; the sign-reading dictionary, the monotone dictionary and the counts are new here. The even-exponent boundary of the trijection reset (central points exist iff `k` is even) is, in the tiling picture, the statement that a "pure time reversal" has no fixed clock.

### 5.3 What the dictionary predicts and does not

The dictionary is a Klein-set isomorphism, so it transports exactly the orbit structure and the two sign characters; it transports no counting beyond that (the divisor set is finite and fixed, the tilings grow as `2^m`). The genuinely tournament-side content it points at is the sign-of-`f` character on lines (closed/open, THM-4729) and the chirality character on black twin pairs (new terminology here, section 7): every black presentation `t` carries a chirality `+-1` (compare `t` with `sigma t` on the lexicographically first tile where they differ), reversed by `sigma` and by `kappa` and preserved by path reversal, so the two ends of a black line have opposite chirality and a black line is given the chirality of its closed member; the sigma-twin line (THM-4731 (3)) then has the opposite chirality, so every merged black multiplicity `2B` splits as `B + B`, another reading of the sigma-twin theorem.

## 7. Terminology

- **Literal black detour, black distance.** Defined in section 1; Lemma 1 gives the parity law, and `F_n` shows that distance 5 occurs (`n = 5, 7`).
- **Pure-blue-touching line; the top two layers.** A closed blue line with a pure-blue end; Lemma 3 places it in layers `n-2, n-3` by the bottom block `K1, C3` (`n >= 6`).
- **Path-poor partner.** An open member with few black Hamiltonian paths (black-move degree); the exceptions `F_5, F_7` have degrees 6 and 20.
- **The two-arc family `F_n`.** The closed blue line whose open member is the transitive tournament with `d -> 0` and `n-1 -> d` reversed, `d = (n-1)/2`; layer `n - 3`; black distance 5 at `n = 5, 7` and 3 at `n = 9, 11`.
- **Klein-set type of an orbit.** Its stabiliser in `<kappa, sigma>` (`1` or `<sigma>` on tilings; `1`, `<Q>`, `<CQ>` on divisors); **central pair** = the `<CQ>`-orbit of `D(p^k q r)`, `k` even.
- **Chirality of a black presentation.** `+-1` by comparing `t` with `sigma t` (zero on blue presentations); the tournament-side image of the sign of `g`.
- **Sign-reading versus monotone dictionaries.** Equivariant bijections that carry `(sign f, sign g)` to (closedness, chirality), versus those carrying divisibility to containment; disjoint at `n = 4`.
