# Seven canonical forms are seven sextic characters

**Status: proof candidate pending independent audit.** Classical abelian-cover
mechanisms are rederived here; no historical-priority claim. The exact magic
arrangement application and the connection contract are the purpose.

## 1. Scope and the even-character algebra

Use [Session59 T1](../../session59_f1_geometry_and_information_20261025/toric-shared-roots/TORIFICATION_OBSTRUCTION.md):
distinct spanning linear forms `L_0,...,L_(n-1)` over C, no point on four
branch lines, `n>=4`. Let `f:X_n -> P2` be their projective square-root cover,
and `Y_n` its minimal resolution. Write `G=F2^n/<(1,...,1)>` for its sign
deck group, and `H=O_X(1)`, so `f*O_P2(1)=O_X(2)`.

Characters of G are **even** subsets S of the n labels:
`chi_S(epsilon)=product_(i in S) epsilon_i`. There is an equivariant algebra
decomposition

    f_* O_X = direct_sum_(|S| even) O_P2(-|S|/2).                 (C1)

Here the summand has character chi_S. One direct construction uses the
graded algebra generated by `x_i`, `x_i^2=L_i`, with base linear forms of
degree two and `x_i` of degree one. It is free over the base polynomial ring
on the squarefree monomials `x_S`. The even Veronese has basis x_S with S
even, of base degree |S|/2. Taking relative Proj proves (C1), including its
multiplication rule

    x_S x_T = (product_(i in S intersect T) L_i) x_(S symmetric_difference T).

This construction does not assume independent numerical square values.
Independence of the generic squareclasses is the distinct-line valuation
argument in T1. The global projective sign, and thus even parity, is essential.

## 2. Genus and why six is the threshold

Finite duality gives `f_*omega_X=Hom(f_*O_X,omega_P2)`. Equivalently one can
compute H2 of (C1) and apply Serre duality. Rational double points preserve
H1(O) and canonical sections. Therefore

    H0(Y_n,K_Y)_(chi_S) = H0(P2,O(|S|/2-3)),
    dim = binom(|S|/2-1,2) for |S|>=6, and 0 otherwise.       (C2)

All line bundles on P2 have H1=0, so q(Y_n)=0. In particular

    p_g(Y_n)=sum_(even k>=6) binom(n,k) binom(k/2-1,2).

| n | Character contributions | p_g |
|---:|---|---:|
| 5 | none | 0 |
| 6 | one six-subset, one section | 1 |
| 7 | seven six-subsets, one section each | 7 |
| 8 | 28 six-subsets + one eight-subset with three sections | 31 |
| 9 | 84 six-subsets + nine eight-subsets with three sections | 111 |

Thus the seven at n=7 is an omission count `binom(7,6)`, and the six is twice
the anticanonical degree of P2. These explanations are compatible with the
adjunction equation `K_Y=pi*((n-6)H)`, but expose more structure than its degree.
At n=8 the naive rule "one form for each omitted coordinate" already fails.

For a completely independent representation check, diagonal adjunction
linearizes canonical sections as polynomial sections of degree n-6 **twisted
by** `det(epsilon)=product_i epsilon_i`. A monomial with exponent vector a
therefore has character with mask `(1+a_i mod 2)_i`. At n=7 the coordinate
section x_i has character

    chi_i = chi_(all labels except i),
    chi_i(single flip j) = +1 if j=i, and -1 otherwise.       (C3)

The determinant twist cannot be discarded: raw coordinate signs do not
descend to a linear representation of the projective sign group.

## 3. Two kinds of K3 quotient, joined by a diamond

For any six-subset S, the subalgebra `O + O(-3)x_S` defines the normal double
plane

    Z_S: w^2 = product_(j in S) L_j,                          (C4)

with w a section of O(3). Its branch curve has only ordinary double and triple
points. The local equations are A1 (`w^2=uv`) and D4
(`w^2=uv(u+v)`), respectively; these are rational double points. Double-cover
adjunction gives trivial canonical sheaf, and `O+O(-3)` gives q=0. Its minimal
resolution is consequently K3. The double-plane quotient is normal because
the branch divisor is reduced and its singular locus is finite.

At n=7, let S be all labels except i. There is a finite diagram of normal
models, with degrees printed on the arrows:

    X_7 --2--> X_(6,S)
      |             |
     32            16
      |             |
      v             v
     Z_S ----identity---- Z_S --2--> P2.

The map to Z_S is given by the product of the six retained root coordinates;
its group is ker chi_S. The map `X_(6,S)->Z_S` is the quotient by the
order-16 even-sign subgroup of `F2^6/<1>`. This subgroup fixes the unique
two-form, hence acts symplectically on the six-root K3 resolution. These are
actual function-field inclusions and finite maps of the normal models.
Passing to smooth resolutions may require further blowups to realize every
arrow as a morphism; no unramified or finite map of the minimal resolutions
is inferred. The [root-deletion packet](../root_deletion/) records the precise
degree-two smooth model and its branch corrections.

Pulling back the unique holomorphic two-form of each resolved Z_S gives the
seven different chi_S eigenspaces of H0(Y_7,K). They are nonzero under the
generically finite maps and form a direct sum. Thus the seven canonical
forms are furnished by seven K3 double-plane quotients, in addition to the
seven deletion K3s. This is a statement about forms and quotient maps, not a
product decomposition of surfaces or an integral Picard-lattice isometry.

## 4. An arithmetic replay that remembers every character

Let q be odd. The following point-count identity is valid for the displayed
root equations whenever the linear forms still span; it does **not** assert
good reduction of the characteristic-zero resolutions. For a point b of P2,
choose any homogeneous representative and put chi(0)=0. Then

    #f^-1(b)(F_q) = sum_(S even) chi(product_(i in S) L_i(b)).  (C5)

Proof: a projective root lift has squared vector lambda*L(b), lambda nonzero.
Count affine root vectors for each lambda and divide by q-1:

    1/(q-1) sum_lambda product_i (1+chi(lambda L_i(b))).

Expansion kills odd subsets and keeps even ones. Nonzero projective vectors
have free scalar action, including when some coordinates vanish. Summing (C5)
over b produces exact per-character contributions and a full root-surface
point count. These are counts of the singular projective model, not Y_n.

The independent check counts projective choices of three basis **root**
coordinates, then the square roots of each remaining forced linear combination
of their squares. It never expands characters. The saved receipt compares
both methods, and compares (C2) to diagonal complete-intersection monomials.

## 5. Connection contract and boundaries

- Source: labelled square-root surface with its projective sign action.
- Target: even-character algebra, canonical eigenspaces, and double-plane K3s.
- Map: S to the root product x_S and the subfield fixed by ker chi_S.
- Preserved: squareclass parity, branch divisors, holomorphic two-form character,
  and the exact finite-field character sum on the stated normal models.
- Lost: individual root signs under the quotient; positivity, distinctness,
  and integral lifting under finite-field reduction.
- Sidecar: branch incidence, squareclass coefficients and base field, label
  marking, exceptional curves, and canonical determinant twist.
- Hostiles: odd subsets are not projective characters; fourfold concurrency
  breaks the inherited resolution scope; n=8 introduces weight-eight forms;
  an actual integer-square point cannot be reconstructed from its characters.

The square-root/Hirzebruch-Kummer and six-line K3 constructions are classical.
Schaffler, [arXiv:1707.09732v2, section 5](https://arxiv.org/html/1707.09732v2#S5),
describes the six-general-line covers; we do not import its generic lattice
claims to our concurrent lines. Auel–Singer,
[arXiv:2609.09351v1, section 4.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.09351v1#S4.SS2),
already studies magic six-root K3s and associated double planes. Our direct
derivation retains ordinary triples and makes the seven-form decomposition
and the distinction between the two quotient degrees explicit. Their Picard
and derived-equivalence results are not used here.
