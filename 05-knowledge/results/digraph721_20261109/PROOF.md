# THM-5055: proof

Session mac-mini-2026-11-09-digraph721. Canon: THM-5055, HYP-9625.

## 0. Statement

**Notation.**
- d(G) is the number of dominating sets of a graph G. It is always odd (Brouwer-Csorba-Schrijver).
- G is **even** (von Brömssen) if no automorphism reverses an odd number of edges relative to a fixed reference
  orientation. This does not depend on the reference orientation (B2 of THM-4996).
- For an odd k, L(k) is its bit length, so 2^(L(k)-1) <= k < 2^L(k).
- M(n) = 2^n - n for odd n, and 2^n - n - 1 for even n.
- X(n) = {2^n - n - 2} for odd n >= 5, and X(n) is empty otherwise.
- F(n) = {odd k : 1 <= k <= M(n)} minus {3, 7, 13} minus X(n).
- E(n) = {d(G) : G even with exactly n vertices}. Isolated vertices are allowed, so E(n) is contained in E(n + 1).

**Theorem (THM-5055).** F(n) is contained in E(n) for every n >= 1.

This is a second, independent proof of the existence half of THM-5040 (opus-2026-11-09-S89), which proves the same
sharp statement. The two proofs share:
- the cone, padding and E_2-join steps;
- the leaf stable recursion d -> 3 * 2^(n-1) + d, which is R below.

They differ in how the values near powers of 2 are reached:
- THM-5040 uses a jump family and L-graphs;
- this proof uses offset moves of order shift 2 and 3, and a seed lemma (section 4).

**Corollary.**
- Combined with the exclusions of section 3, E(n) = F(n) for every n. This is THM-5040's statement; its exclusions
  are re-derived here.
- The d-values of even graphs are exactly the odd numbers other than 3, 7 and 13. This proves HYP-9575 (B).
- Let k be an odd number other than 3, 7 and 13, and put L = L(k). The least order of an even realiser of k is L,
  except that it is L + 1 when k > M(L) or k = 2^L - L - 2 with L odd >= 5.
  - Indeed k is at least 2^(L-1), which exceeds M(L - 1), so no realiser has fewer than L vertices.
  - k is in F(L + 1), since 2^L <= M(L + 1) and k < X(L + 1).

## 1. Conventions

- Graphs are finite and simple. **Padding** adds an isolated vertex.
- A vertex is **universal** if it is adjacent to all other vertices.
- A **pendant** is a vertex of degree 1.
- N[u] is the closed neighbourhood of u.
- A graph is:
  - **Seed2** if it has two pendants whose neighbours are not universal;
  - **Seed3** if it has a vertex u with |N[u]| = 3, that is, a vertex of degree 2;
  - **good** if it is even and both Seed2 and Seed3.
- A Seed2 graph on at least 3 vertices has no universal vertex. Such a vertex would be adjacent to the pendant, hence
  equal to its neighbour, which is not universal.
- Parity facts from THM-4996, Lemma B2:
  - swapping non-adjacent twins is even, and swapping adjacent twins is odd;
  - G is even iff each component is.

## 2. Moves

**Lemma 1 (padding).** G u K_1 is even iff G is even, and d(G u K_1) = d(G). Padding keeps the Seed2, Seed3 and
good properties. A pendant keeps its neighbour, and that neighbour does not become universal.

**Lemma 2 (cone; THM-4996, B2.3).** Let G have at least 2 vertices and no universal vertex. Then K_1 v G is even iff G
is even, and d(K_1 v G) = 2^|G| + d(G).

**Lemma 3 (E_2-join).** For every graph G on n vertices, E_2 v G is even iff G is even, and
d(E_2 v G) = 3 * 2^n - 2 + d(G).

*Proof.*
- **Value.** A set meeting both sides dominates; there are 3(2^n - 1) of these. A nonempty subset of E_2 dominates iff
  it is all of E_2 (1 set). A subset of V(G) dominates iff it dominates G (d(G) sets).
- **Evenness.** The co-components of E_2 v G are {a, b} (with connected complement K_2) and the co-components of G.
  Aut(E_2 v G) is the automorphism group of the complement, which is the disjoint union of K_2 and the complement of
  G. So it is generated by automorphisms of single co-components and by swaps of isomorphic co-components. Take a
  reference order in which every co-component is a contiguous block.
  - An automorphism of one co-component C (fixing the rest) reverses no cross edge.
    - If C = {a, b}, this is the swap of non-adjacent twins: even.
    - Otherwise its sign is its sign as an automorphism of G.
  - A swap of two isomorphic co-components of size s, placed in adjacent blocks, reverses exactly the s^2 cross edges
    between them (THM-4996, B2.4).
    - Inside G it is an automorphism of G with the same sign.
    - A swap of {a, b} with a co-component of G isomorphic to E_2 has s = 2: even.
  - Conversely, automorphisms of G extend to E_2 v G (fixing a and b) with the same sign. QED

**Lemma 4 (hub move).** Let G be a graph on n vertices, u a vertex with |N_G[u]| = r, and l >= 1. Let PH(G, u, l) be G
together with:
- a new vertex h (the hub), adjacent to every vertex of G except u;
- l new vertices (leaves), each adjacent only to h.

Then:
1. d(PH(G, u, l)) = d(G) + (2^r - 1) 2^(n + l - r).
2. Suppose G is even and h is the unique vertex of maximum degree in PH(G, u, l). Then PH(G, u, l) is even.
   - This uniqueness holds if l >= 2 (and n + l >= 3).
   - It also holds if l = 1, n >= 2 and G has no universal vertex other than possibly u.

*Proof.*
1. **Value.** Sort the dominating sets S by whether h is in S.
   - h in S: the leaves are free (2^l choices). Every vertex of G other than u is dominated by h. u must be dominated
     by S cap V(G), which must meet N_G[u]. This gives 2^l (2^n - 2^(n - r)) sets.
   - h not in S: every leaf must lie in S, and then h is dominated. S cap V(G) must dominate G. This gives d(G) sets.
2. **Degrees.** deg h = n - 1 + l. A vertex v != u of G has degree deg_G(v) + 1, which is at most n, with equality
   only when v is universal in G. deg u <= n - 1, and the leaves have degree 1. This gives the two sufficient
   conditions.
3. **Evenness.**
   - Every automorphism a fixes h. Hence it fixes u, the only vertex other than h that is not adjacent to h.
   - Let D be the set of vertices whose only neighbour is h: the leaves, together with the isolated vertices of G
     other than u. a maps D to itself.
   - Vertices of D are pairwise non-adjacent twins, so every permutation p of D (fixing everything else) is an even
     automorphism.
   - Choose p so that b = p a maps the leaves to the leaves. Then b preserves V(G), and b restricted to V(G) is an
     automorphism of G, which is even because G is even.
   - In a reference order with h first, b reverses no edge at h.
   - So b is even, and hence so is a = p^-1 b. QED

**Corollary 5 (offset moves).** Let G be good on n >= 3 vertices.
- **R.** Let R(G) = PH(G, p, 1) for a pendant p whose neighbour is not universal. Then R(G) is good on n + 2 vertices
  and d(R(G)) = d(G) + 3 * 2^(n - 1).
- **PH(3, 2).** For a vertex w with |N[w]| = 3, PH(G, w, 2) is good on n + 3 vertices with d = d(G) + 7 * 2^(n - 1).
- **Offsets are invariant.** Write d = 2^(n - 1) + c. Then R gives 2^((n+2) - 1) + c and PH(3, 2) gives
  2^((n+3) - 1) + c: the offset c = d - 2^(n - 1) is unchanged.
- **Seeds.**
  - For a Seed2 graph G (even), R(G) is good.
  - For a Seed3 graph G (even), PH(G, w, 2) is good.

*Proof.* The values come from Lemma 4 with (r, l) = (2, 1) and (3, 2). Evenness comes from Lemma 4.2:
- for R, G has no universal vertex (section 1);
- for PH(3, 2), l = 2.

Goodness of the output:
- **R(G).** p is still a pendant. Its neighbour q is not adjacent to the new leaf p', so q is not universal. p' is a
  pendant, and its neighbour h is not adjacent to p, so h is not universal. A second pendant p2 of G (p2 != p) is now
  adjacent to its neighbour and to h, so |N[p2]| = 3. This proves the Seed2 case too.
- **PH(G, w, 2).** The two new leaves are pendants whose neighbour h is not universal (h is not adjacent to w).
  |N[w]| = 3 is unchanged, because h is not adjacent to w. This proves the Seed3 case. QED

Brute-force checks (section 6):

- **Corollary 5.** `chaincheck.py` builds the seeds A and B (section 4) and applies random R / PH(3, 2) chains up to 15
  vertices. It checks good, even and the invariance of the offset in 217 tests, with 0 failures.

## 3. Exclusions (opus-2026-11-09-S89, THM-5040; re-derived)

**Deficit.** Def(G) = 2^n - 1 - d(G) is the number of nonempty non-dominating sets. A nonempty S fails to dominate iff
S is contained in V \ N[v] = N_cG(v) for some v, where cG is the complement of G. So Def(G) counts the distinct
nonempty subsets of complement neighbourhoods.

**Proposition 3.1.** For G even, d(G) <= M(n).

*Proof.* The singleton {u} fails to dominate iff u is not universal. Two universal vertices would be adjacent twins,
so an even graph has at most one. Hence Def >= n - 1 and d <= 2^n - n. Since d is odd, d <= M(n). QED

**Proposition 3.2 (THM-4996).** 3, 7 and 13 are not d-values of even graphs.

*Proof.* Up to isolated vertices, their only realisers are K_2, K_3 and K_4 - e (exhaustive census), and each has
adjacent twins. Since 3, 7 and 13 are prime, any realiser is a single nontrivial component plus isolated vertices,
because a component with d = 1 is edgeless. QED

**Proposition 3.3.** For odd n >= 5, 2^n - n - 2 is not in E(n).

*Proof.* Suppose G is even with Def(G) = n + 1, and let u0 in {0, 1} be the number of universal vertices.
- **Counting.** Non-singletons account for 1 + u0 of the counted sets. A vertex of complement degree >= 3 alone gives
  3 pairs and 1 triple. So cG has maximum degree <= 2: it is a union of paths and cycles. The counted non-singletons
  are the distinct pairs N_cG(v) over vertices of complement degree 2:
  - P_k (k >= 3) gives k - 2 distinct pairs;
  - C_3 gives 3, C_4 gives 2, and C_k (k >= 5) gives k.
  The isolated vertices of cG are the universal vertices of G.
- **u0 = 0.** cG = P_3 u matching, so G = (K_2 u K_1) v E_2 v ... v E_2. The ends of the K_2 are adjacent twins of G.
- **u0 = 1.** cG = K_1 u H u matching with H in {P_4, 2P_3, C_4}, so G = K_1 v cH v E_2 v ... v E_2.
  - The complement of C_4 is 2K_2, and the complement of 2P_3 contains K_2 u K_1 as a co-component. Either way G has
    adjacent twins.
  - The complement of P_4 is P_4. Its flip reverses all 3 edges, and as an automorphism of a single co-component it
    keeps this sign in G.
- So G is odd in every case. QED

Together with the theorem, E(n) = F(n).

## 4. Offsets and seeds

**Definition.** For an integer c, Gamma(c) is the set of n such that 2^(n-1) + c = d(G) for some good graph G on n
vertices.

**Lemma 6.** If n is in Gamma(c), then n + 2 and n + 3 are in Gamma(c) (Corollary 5). Consequently, if n0 and n0 + 1
are in Gamma(c), then every n >= n0 is.

**Seed constructions.** For an even graph X on s - 2 vertices (A) or s - 1 vertices (B):
- **(A)** K_1 v (X u E_2) u K_1 is even (Lemmas 1-2) and Seed2: the two E_2-vertices are pendants of the apex, which
  misses the extra K_1. It has s + 2 vertices and d = 2^s + d(X).
- **(B)** E_2 v (X u K_1) is even (Lemmas 1, 3) and Seed3: the K_1 sees exactly the two E_2-vertices. It has s + 2
  vertices and d = 3 * 2^s - 2 + d(X). Padding keeps both properties.

**Lemma 7 (fixed offsets; computation).** For c in {1, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13}, 8 and 9 lie in Gamma(c). Hence Gamma(c)
contains [8, infinity).

**Lemma 8 (seed lemma).** For every odd j >= 1, Gamma(-j) contains [L(j) + 8, infinity).

*Proof.* By strong induction on j.
- **L(j) <= 12 (computation).** For each such j there is n0 <= L(j) + 8 with n0 and n0 + 1 in Gamma(-j)
  (`evenclos`: max of n0 - L(j) is exactly 8). Apply Lemma 6.

Now let J = L(j) >= 13, so 2^(J-1) < j < 2^J. E(J - 1) and E(J - 2) contain F(J - 1) and F(J - 2); this is the theorem
at lower orders (see section 5 for the interleaving).

- **Case A: j > 3 * 2^(J-2).** Put x = 2^J - j, which is odd with 1 <= x < 2^(J-2).
  - **A1: x in F(J - 2).** Seed (A) with s = J and X realising x gives a Seed2 graph on J + 2 vertices with
    d = 2^J + x = 2^(J+1) - j. R makes it good on J + 4 vertices with offset -j, so J + 4 is in Gamma(-j). Lemma 6
    gives [J + 6, infinity).
  - **A2: x in {3, 7, 13}.** By Lemma 7, J + 1 is in Gamma(x) (J + 1 >= 14). Padding that good graph gives a good graph
    on J + 2 vertices with d = 2^J + x = 2^(J+1) - j. So J + 2 is in Gamma(-j), and Lemma 6 gives [J + 4, infinity).
  - **A3: otherwise.** Then x > M(J - 2) or x = X(J - 2). Put i = 2^(J-2) - x, so 1 <= i <= J (because
    2^(J-2) - M(J-2) <= J - 1 and 2^(J-2) - X(J-2) = J) and j = 3 * 2^(J-2) + i.
    - Since J - 1 >= L(J) + 8 >= L(i) + 8 for J >= 13, induction puts J - 1 in Gamma(-i). That is a good graph on
      J - 1 vertices with d = 2^(J-2) - i = x.
    - Padding twice gives a good graph on J + 1 vertices with d = x = 2^J - j.
    - So J + 1 is in Gamma(-j), and Lemma 6 gives [J + 3, infinity).
- **Case B: 2^(J-1) < j < 3 * 2^(J-2).** Put y = 2^J - j and z = y + 2, so 2^(J-2) + 2 < z < 2^(J-1) + 2.
  - **B1: z in F(J - 1).** Seed (B) with s = J and X realising z, padded once, gives a Seed3 graph on J + 3 vertices
    with d = 3 * 2^J + y = 2^(J+2) - j. PH(3, 2) makes it good on J + 6 vertices, so J + 6 is in Gamma(-j). Lemma 6
    gives [J + 8, infinity).
  - **B3: otherwise.** z > 13, so z > M(J - 1) or z = X(J - 1). Put i = j - 2^(J-1), which is odd.
    - z > M(J - 1) means y >= M(J - 1), hence i <= 2^(J-1) - M(J-1) <= J. If z = X(J - 1), then i = J + 3.
    - Since J >= L(J + 3) + 8 >= L(i) + 8 for J >= 13, induction puts J in Gamma(-i). That is a good graph on J
      vertices with d = 2^(J-1) - i = 2^J - j.
    - Padding once puts J + 1 in Gamma(-j), and Lemma 6 gives [J + 3, infinity).

In every case Gamma(-j) contains [J + 8, infinity). QED

`seedcases.py` re-checks the case split for 13 <= L(j) <= 15 against the computed Gamma tables:
- each of the 14,336 values of j falls in exactly one case (A1 7,140; A2 9; A3 19; B1 7,145; B3 23);
- each case's hypothesis holds.

## 5. Proof of the theorem

The proof is by strong induction on n.

**Base, n <= 12.** `evenclos` computes the closure of the exhaustive set of even graphs on <= 9 vertices under the
moves of section 2. It finds E(n) = F(n) for n <= 26 (0 missing, 0 extra).

**Step, n >= 13.** Assume F(m) is contained in E(m) for m < n. Lemmas 7 and 8 are available:
- Lemma 8 for j <= n + 1 uses the theorem only at orders <= L(n + 1) - 1, which is < n.

Let k be in F(n).

1. **k <= M(n - 1) and k not in X(n - 1).** Then k is in F(n - 1), which is contained in E(n - 1), which is
   contained in E(n).
2. **k in (M(n - 1), 2^(n-1)), or k in X(n - 1).** Write k = 2^(n-1) - j.
   - Then j <= 2^(n-1) - M(n-1) - 1 <= n - 1, or j = n + 1 for k = X(n - 1).
   - Since n >= L(n + 1) + 8 for n >= 12, Lemma 8 puts n in Gamma(-j). So k is the value of a good graph on n
     vertices.
3. **2^(n-1) < k <= 3 * 2^(n-2) - 3.** Write k = 2^(n-1) + x with 1 <= x <= 2^(n-2) - 3.
   - **x in F(n - 2).** Pad a realiser of x to n - 1 vertices. It has no universal vertex, so the cone (Lemma 2)
     realises k on n vertices.
   - **x > M(n - 2) or x in X(n - 2).** Then x = 2^(n-2) - j' with j' <= n. Since n - 1 >= L(n) + 8 for n >= 13,
     Lemma 8 gives a good, hence universal-free, graph on n - 1 vertices with value x. Cone over it.
   - **x in {3, 7, 13}.** Lemma 7 puts n in Gamma(x).
4. **3 * 2^(n-2) - 1 <= k <= M(n).** Put y = k - 3 * 2^(n-2) + 2, so 1 <= y <= M(n) - 3 * 2^(n-2) + 2 = M(n - 2).
   - **y in F(n - 2).** E_2 v (a realiser of y on n - 2 vertices) realises k (Lemma 3).
   - **y in {3, 7, 13}.** Then k = 3 * 2^(n-2) + c with c in {1, 5, 11}. Lemma 7 puts n - 1 in Gamma(c), giving a
     good, universal-free graph on n - 1 vertices with value 2^(n-2) + c. Its cone has value k.
   - **y in X(n - 2).** Then k = 2^n - n - 2 = X(n), which is not in F(n).

The four ranges cover every odd k <= M(n). QED

## 7. Remarks

1. **The shift-3 move is needed.**
   - **Four moves suffice up to n = 26.** Consider the closure of the exhaustive 9-vertex bases under four moves: the
     cone, the E_2-join, the cone over G u K_1, and R. It is exact for n <= 26 (`closure4.c`, `closure4.out`).
   - **They cannot suffice for all n.**
     - In that closure, for n >= 10, the value 2^(n-1) - 81 can enter the pendant set at order n only through R from
       2^(n-3) - 81 at order n - 2. The other moves produce, at order n, only values below 3 * 2^(n-2) or at least
       2^(n-1) + 1.
     - For even n the chain ends at 47 at order 8. 47 has no even realisation with a pendant vertex at any order: it is
       prime, so a realiser is one connected component plus isolated vertices, and none of its 3 connected even
       realisers (census) has a pendant. The same holds for 11 (1 connected even realiser) and 23 (2).
     - Hence for even n >= 84, where 2^(n-1) - 81 > M(n - 1), the four-move closure misses a value of F(n).
   - PH(3, 2) changes the parity of the order and removes this obstruction.
2. **Families.** The proof is a three-move numeration of the odd numbers by bits:
   - a cone over a padded graph writes a leading 10;
   - an E_2-join writes a leading 11, with a borrow of 2;
   - the hub moves R and PH(3, 2) carry a fixed offset c from one power of 2 to the next. They supply the values just
     below 2^(n-1), and near 2^(n-1) + {3, 7, 13} and 3 * 2^(n-2) + {1, 5, 11}, that cones and joins cannot reach.
   - The values just below a power of 2 are never cones or joins at any order (a cone on m vertices has
     d >= 2^(m-1), and a join d >= 3 * 2^(m-2)). They need prime graphs, which is exactly what the hub moves build.
3. **Comparison with tournaments.** RPGFD: even graphs and tournaments are equinumerous.
   - On even graphs the exceptional set {3, 7, 13} is now a theorem with sharp orders.
   - On tournaments the conjectured set {7, 21} (Chan-Pak Conjecture 6.2) remains open.
   - The shared 7 is an analogy only: no bijection carries d to H.
