# Session42 research map

The central question is not whether two objects have the same small numbers, but whether the proposed map retains the relations used by the target theorem.

1. A graph and its complement encode a tournament after an order is supplied. Forgetting the order is not an innocuous quotient. The meaningful larger universe is a weighted correspondence of quotient classes.
2. A Gaussian point's octant representative retains its D4 orbit. Norm alone can lose the quartic angular coordinate; independent orbit representatives lose additive relative phase. Arithmetic generation is stronger than an isometry quotient.
3. A square-sum path's ordered edge-sum word loses at most one binary choice. That precise loss forces an alternating divisor-block coloring, with its first square-sum realization at36.
4. A cyclic block transporter is a lattice coset only in genuine cyclic coordinates. Larger noncyclic groups require actual subgroup and kernel coordinates; all attachments must share the same vertex variables.
5. Birth-island allegiance is naturally a relation or a distribution until parent choices are made. Separate marginal colors can erase shared ancestry.
6. Fibonacci addresses and Berggren ancestry are actual ordered data. A finite color or a depth is not a substitute for the address; prior THM-3339 and THM-3382 already demonstrate positive bridges and information loss.

An all-size supplied-model rule is distinct from a compact, complete description of every native tournament port. Preserve actual endpoint ownership, path positions, converse branches, electrical guards and clocks at that boundary.
