# Chan–Pak: absolute carrier selection, twisted collisions, and the thirteen-unit cone

**All-order results; Chan–Pak remains open.** The following are independent proof routes. None treats a residue match, a finite census or a real-valued bracket as an exact tournament witness.

- **[Cone certificate](SUFFIX_CONES.md):** fixed cones indexed by three-unit suffixes replace the rotating image planes. The verified growth per vertex is at least 1.7349 for all thirteen units subject to the reduced rank condition, and at least 1.7699 for any mixture of the twelve units other than 0.1.123. All inequalities and representation identities are verified with integers. The full thirteen-unit rate 1.7699, and arbitrary non-unit span-three letters, are not settled.
- **[Twisted collisions](TWISTED_LATTICES.md):** in either physical row an ordinary increasing pair with decreasing reversed addresses cannot collide. Strip both common ends; the remaining exact equation is a lattice line. A split chosen to match its primitive slope gives a bounded-degree graph of signed-digit signatures. This proves sublinear collision mass for all differing spans below (0.4441228664−ε) times the digit length. Infinite exact rays show why small-span collisions cannot just be forbidden; the distant long-span estimate in HYP-9752 is still open.
- **[Placement by complementary carries](STEERING.md):** a prescribed target chooses a fixed-sum two-tape slice with an all-scale O(N^(1/3)) bracket, improving the earlier O(N^(3/8)) steering. The exact candidates in that slice are confined to a constant-size window found by two quadratic inversions. The number of targets that hit in their own selected slices is only O(X^(2/3)); so this particular selection rule needs another knob.
- **[Literal absolute-host compiler](bank_absolute_host.py):** takes a target, uses the exact digital inverse to find admissible tape coordinates, then returns the adjacency rows of the physical tournament. For example target 1,000,000,001 returns a 31-vertex tournament with addresses 804 and 103594 and a one-mark directed-triangle core. This is demonstration of target-driven construction, not a new verification range. For any fixed finite carrier bank the procedure finds an exact member or certifies that the bank cannot supply the target.
- **[A two-apex host for the seven lane](TWO_APEX_HOST.md):** the cofactor H/7 in the interval-seven construction has a product phase with geometric weights (3/4)^j. The prescribed cofactor chooses the phase digits, and an eight-bit boundary repair matches it **exactly mod 9** without losing the macroscopic approximation. This is about physical supply of the related host profile, not an improvement to the separate O(N^(1/3)) bracket or an absolute-equality theorem.

To reproduce, use `python verify_suffix_cone.py CONE13.json` and `python verify_suffix_cone.py CONE12.json` (NumPy and SymPy, assertions enabled). The controls for the two inverses are `python verify_steering.py` and `python verify_twisted_inverse.py`. To build a host from the repository's marked forms, run:

```
python bank_absolute_host.py 1000000001 ../chanpak_waves_20261113/scripts/carrier_forms_orders3_5.txt
```

The cofactor construction has Python slot-DP and C++17 modular state replays linked in its note. The remaining absolute-selection problem is to grow the repair supply without making the target-selected baseline drift by more than the repair window. For the row approach, small and oppositely ordered displacements are now understood; an absolute proof needs control of the same-direction, long-span portion.
