## 1. The two recursions as backward trees, and the exact identities

Both sequences are defined by "the value at `n` is the sum of the values at two earlier indices determined by the previous values": `a(n) = a(p) + a(q)` with `p = a(n-1)`, `q = n - a(n-1)`; `Q(n) = Q(p) + Q(q)` with `p = n - Q(n-1)`, `q = n - Q(n-2)`. In the near-half regime (a known limit for Conway, only numerical evidence here for Q), both backward indices are near `n/2`: in `u = log2 n` both steps are near `-1`. This is the one-sided case of the transfer law of THM-4629 (Collatz has steps `-1` and `+0.585`). Writing `d(n) = x(n)/n - 1/2`:

- Conway: `d(n) = (p/n) d(p) + (q/n) d(q)`, `p + q = n`. The deviation is a convex combination of two earlier deviations: no source term, exact conservation.
- Q: `d(n) = (p/n) d(p) + (q/n) d(q) - (1/2)[(1 - 1/n) d(n-1) + (1 - 2/n) d(n-2)] + 3/(4n)`. The backward indices overshoot by the previous deviations (`p + q = n + 3/2 - (n-1) d(n-1) - (n-2) d(n-2)`), which feeds the previous two deviations back with weight `-1/2`.

Both identities follow from the definitions in two lines and were checked to `1.1e-16` and `1.5e-16` for `n <= 2^22` (`meta_fibonacci_resonator_20261013.out`). Step statistics at `2^29`: Conway `log2(n/p)`, `log2(n/q)` have means `0.948`, `1.054` (approaching `1` from either side, `p = n/2 + n d` above and `q` below) and standard deviations `0.021`, `0.022`, shrinking with the amplitude; Q has means `1.0001` and standard deviations `0.014` for both. For Conway both indices lie in the CLOSED previous dyadic arc, whose upper endpoint has zero deviation. ## 2. Linearisation: the one-sided transfer law

Treat `d` as a function of scale and position. With both steps exactly one octave, Conway's identity says `d(u) = d(u-1)`: every mode `e^(2 pi i f u)` with integer `f` is undamped, the hump is exactly self-similar from generation to generation, and the only thing that can change it is the nonlinearity (the positions `x(1 +- 2d)` and weights `1/2 +- d` depend on `d`). Q's identity for a slowly varying component says `d = d_prev - d`, i.e. `d_k = d_(k-1)/2`: the smooth comb is damped by `1/2` per generation. For a component `e^(i omega n)` inside a generation the feedback is the filter `H(omega) = 1/(1 + e^(-i omega)/2 + e^(-2 i omega)/2)`, whose poles `z = (-1 +- i sqrt 7)/4` have modulus `1/sqrt 2` and argument `110.7 deg`: period `3.252`, gain `|H| = 2.1` at the resonance and `1/2` at `omega = 0`. The Yule-Walker autocorrelation of the AR(2) part `x_n = -x_(n-1)/2 - x_(n-2)/2 + e_n` is `-1/3, -1/3, +1/3, 0, -1/6, +1/12, +1/24, -1/16, +1/96, +0.026, -0.018, -0.004`.

So the three systems sort by step geometry (an analogy, not a theorem; the integer comb is the Fourier form of exact self-similarity): two-sided with irrational ratio (Collatz: lines at the near-returns, decay quadratic in the defect), one-sided conservative (Conway: undamped comb, smoothed only by itself), one-sided with feedback (Q: damped comb plus a resonator).
