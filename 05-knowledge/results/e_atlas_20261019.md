# A bounded E3/E4/E5 atlas

## Result and scope

There is no single object called `E3/E4/E5` throughout this repository. Four particularly useful, distinct readings are:

1. **Real-analytic Eisenstein values at s=3,4,5:** their constant Fourier coefficients contain ζ(5), ζ(7), ζ(9), with an intrinsic index rule.
2. **Extended root systems E3=A2+A1, E4=A4, E5=D5:** the corresponding del Pezzo exceptional-curve graphs are C6, Petersen, Clebsch. The E4 lattice is explicitly recoverable from the unsigned K5 incidence matrix of the Petersen boundary geometry.
3. **Endpoint-reversal tournaments:** E3, E4, E5 have 3,5,9 Hamiltonian paths. Their useful coordinates are Boolean subset addresses, with no established odd-zeta period attached.
4. **Eulerian-graph metagraphs:** their adjacency requires a spanning-tree choice, or the corrected canonical all-cycle envelope. They are different from the preceding tournaments.

A second exact bridge crosses the graph and dynamics lanes: **edge sums reconstruct vertex labels uniquely on a connected nonbipartite graph; a connected bipartite graph needs one root-label sidecar.** This organizes graceful-tree coordinates, square-sum graphs, Petersen/K5/K3,3, and quadratic three-cycles without asserting equivalence of those problems.

This is a bounded disambiguation and synthesis, not an exhaustive dictionary of every use of the letter E. Inherited results are cited below. The proofs supplied here establish the stated mathematical identities; they make no historical-priority claim. No irrationality improvement, graceful-tree existence theorem, or new global rational-period bound is claimed.

### 1.1 Endpoint subset addresses are exact, but their rotations are not graph automorphisms

For n≥3, every Hamiltonian path is either the increasing path or

`P_S = (S in increasing order), n−1, 0, ({1,…,n−2}\S in increasing order)`.

Indeed a path avoiding the reversed edge is increasing. Once that edge is used, its two remaining blocks must increase. Consequently H(E_n)=1+2^(n−2). This gives 3,5,9,17,33,65 for n=3,…,8, checked independently by permutation enumeration.

A cyclic permutation of the Boolean address positions transports the address set; it does not preserve the tournament arcs, since reconstruction re-sorts the blocks. E3 is the directed triangle. For n≥4 the tournament automorphism group is trivial. A quadratic three-cycle can be identified with this directed triangle after choosing a phase; this is only a combinatorial cycle identification, not a conjugacy of the ambient dynamical systems.

### 1.2 The even-graph repair must survive every proposed connection

For a spanning tree T of K_n, let B_T consist of its fundamental cycles. The graph on labelled Eulerian subgraphs is the cube Cay(Z_n,B_T), dim Z_n=binom(n−1,2). Its simple edge image on graph-isomorphism classes is E_(n,T), with loops removed.

The corrected theorem gives E_(n,T)=the union of k-cycle toggles for 3≤k≤diam(T)+1. The canonical envelope uses every simple cycle and equals a path-tree image. At n=4, a star gives P3 and a path gives K3. At n=5 the common seven-vertex set has 8 star-image edges and 16 envelope edges. A statement about “E_n adjacency” without this choice is underspecified.

## 2. Intrinsic analytic E3/E4/E5 → ζ(5),ζ(7),ζ(9)

### 2.1 Nonholomorphic Eisenstein scattering

Use the primitive, half-counted normalization, for z=x+iy and Re(s)>1:

\[
 E(z,s)=\frac12\sum_{(c,d)=1}\frac{y^s}{|cz+d|^{2s}}.
\]

Its constant Fourier coefficient is

\[
\int_0^1E(x+iy,s)\,dx=y^s+\varphi(s)y^{1-s},\qquad
\varphi(s)=\frac{\sqrt\pi\,\Gamma(s-\frac12)\zeta(2s-1)}{\Gamma(s)\zeta(2s)}.
\]

Thus, defining `E_r(z)=E(z,r)` explicitly,

\[
\varphi(3)=\frac{2835}{8}\frac{\zeta(5)}{\pi^5},\quad
\varphi(4)=\frac{23625}{8}\frac{\zeta(7)}{\pi^7},\quad
\varphi(5)=\frac{3274425}{128}\frac{\zeta(9)}{\pi^9}.
\]

The prefactors follow from Γ(r−1/2)/Γ(r) and Euler's even-zeta formula and are checked in rational arithmetic. The occurrence of 2r−1 is part of the scattering formula, not a fitted indexing choice: it is also the gap between the two constant-mode exponents r and 1−r. Integrating the norm-power kernel in one real coordinate changes its exponent from 2r to 2r−1. [Jordan Bell, *Nonholomorphic Eisenstein series*, definition and Theorems 1 and 3](https://jordanbell.info/LaTeX/mathematics/eisenstein/).

**Convention firewall.** Holomorphic full-modular forms of odd weight 3 or 5 vanish by the action of −I; holomorphic E4 is a different object. For the determinant-one binary form Q_z(m,n)=|mz+n|²/y, the Epstein sum Z_Q(s)=Σ_(m,n)≠0 Q_z(m,n)^(−s) satisfies Z_Q=2ζ(2s)E(z,s), by separating the common divisor. A rank-one Epstein sum gives 2a^(−s)ζ(2s); choosing s=r−1/2 to obtain odd weights is an explicit reindexing. The Eisenstein integer ring Z[ω] is yet another object.

### 2.2 Euler polynomials: a natural transform, with a different index

With generating function 2e^(xt)/(e^t+1),

\[
E_3=x^3-\tfrac32x^2+\tfrac14,\quad
E_4=x^4-2x^3+x,\quad
E_5=x^5-\tfrac52x^4+\tfrac52x^2-\tfrac12.
\]

The even Euler-polynomial Fourier expansion yields, for m≥1,

\[
\int_0^1\frac{E_{2m}(x)}{\sin\pi x}\,dx
 =(-1)^m\frac{4(2m)!}{\pi^{2m+1}}(1-2^{-2m-1})\zeta(2m+1).
\]

To derive the integral, use sin((2k+1)πx)/sin(πx)=1+2Σ_(j=1)^k cos(2jπx). Its integral is 1; the bound by 2k+1 justifies termwise integration. Thus the direct triple for ζ5,ζ7,ζ9 is **E4,E6,E8**, not literal E3,E4,E5. [NIST DLMF §24.8, formula 24.8.4](https://dlmf.nist.gov/24.8.E4).

For elementary symmetric functions of the convergent sequence a_n=n^(−s), s>1, e_k is the multiple zeta value ζ(s,…,s) of depth k, not generally ζ(2k−1). This is immediate by expanding the defining distinct-index sum and is another index firewall.

### 2.3 Graph periods provide an additional exact odd-zeta lane

Let W_n mean a wheel with **n spokes**, hence n+1 vertices. Write Ψ_G(α)=Σ_T ∏_(e∉T)α_e, summing over spanning trees. For E=2h, the normalization is P(G)=∫_(α_1,…,α_(E−1)>0) Ψ_G(α_1,…,α_(E−1),1)^(−2) dα_1⋯dα_(E−1), whenever convergent. Then

\[
P(W_n)=\binom{2n-2}{n-1}\zeta(2n-3).
\]

For n=4,5,6 these are 20ζ5,70ζ7,252ζ9. The corresponding zig-zag periods are 20ζ5,(441/8)ζ7,168ζ9. These are cited integral evaluations, not established by our finite coefficient tests. Wheels with more than four spokes are not φ4 graphs, whereas the zig-zag family supplies the φ4 version. [Brown–Schnetz, *The wheel classes*, Theorem 62 with k=1](https://msp.org/gt/2025/29-8/gt-v29-n8-p07-s.pdf); [Brown–Schnetz, *Proof of the zig-zag conjecture*, Theorem 1.1](https://www.ihes.fr/~brown/ZigZag.pdf).

A graph's occurrence elsewhere does not assign it this period: one must specify an integrand, normalization, convergence and any completion/decompletion. In particular, no wheel-period identity is claimed for the endpoint tournaments or for Petersen/K3,3 merely from their names or drawings.

## 3. Root E3/E4/E5 and the Petersen boundary lattice

### 3.1 A concrete marked del Pezzo model

For the blowup of r=3,4,5 general-position points of P², use Picard coordinates aH+Σb_iE_i, with pairing aa′−Σb_ib_i′ and canonical K=−3H+ΣE_i. The roots in K⊥ of square −2 have types E3=A2+A1, E4=A4, E5=D5. [Dolgachev–Duncan, *Fixed points of a finite subgroup of the plane Cremona group*, §2](https://content.algebraicgeometry.nl/2016-4/2016-4-021.pdf).

The (−1)-curve classes are E_i and H−E_i−E_j, together with 2H−ΣE_i when r=5. Their intersection-one graphs are respectively C6, Petersen, Clebsch: 6/10/16 vertices, 6/15/40 edges, degrees 2/3/5. [*Del Pezzo surfaces of degree four*, geometric background](https://smf.emath.fr/system/files/filepdf/MSMF_1989_2_37__1_0.pdf).

Here are elementary completeness bounds for the accompanying exact enumeration. A root satisfies Σb_i=−3a and Σb_i²=a²+2, so Cauchy gives (9−r)a²≤2r. Therefore |a|≤1 and |b_i|≤1. A (−1)-class satisfies Σb_i=1−3a and Σb_i²=a²+1, so (1−3a)²≤r(a²+1). This allows a=0,1 for r=3,4 and a=0,1,2 for r=5, yielding exactly the listed classes. The complete root counts are 8,20,40.

Deleting a marked exceptional vertex E_r and its neighbors leaves exactly the previous graph: drop the last Picard coordinate of the curves disjoint from E_r. This is an intersection-preserving C6←Petersen←Clebsch chain. The sidecar is the marked exceptional curve and Picard basis; an arbitrary graph identification alone does not choose a blowdown.

### 3.2 E4=A4 is explicit inside unsigned K5 incidence

For r=4, identify each exceptional curve E_i with the two-set {i,5}, and H−E_i−E_j with the complementary two-set in {1,2,3,4}. Curves meet exactly when these two-sets are disjoint. Thus their graph is KG(5,2), the Petersen graph, which is also the complement of L(K5).

Let B be the unsigned 5×10 vertex-edge incidence matrix of K5. On the ten pair labels the curve intersection matrix is

\[
Q=A_P-I=J_{10}-B^TB.
\]

The integral image of B is Λ={u∈Z⁵:Σu_i is even}, with form

\[
h(u,v)=\tfrac14(\Sigma u_i)(\Sigma v_i)-u\cdot v.
\]

The explicit Picard-to-pair-lattice isometry is

\[
(a,b_1,b_2,b_3,b_4)\longmapsto
(a+b_1,a+b_2,a+b_3,a+b_4,2a+\Sigma b_i).
\]

Its determinant has absolute value 2, so its image is all Λ. It sends K to (−2,−2,−2,−2,−2). Consequently h(u,K)=−Σu_i/2 and

\[
K^\perp=\{u\in\mathbb Z^5:\Sigma u_i=0\}=A_4(-1),
\]

with exactly the twenty roots e_i−e_j. This is a genuine integral-lattice identification of E4, not only a shared Petersen picture. The accompanying [boundary-graph study](odd_zeta_boundary_graphs_20261019.md) identifies the same lattice from the ten boundary divisors of Mbar(0,5), including the integral Keel relations. Our script independently checks the coordinate isometry, determinant, all curve-pair images and all twenty roots.

## 4. One exact reconstruction mechanism across the graph lanes

### Proposition: signless incidence and the missing root value

Let G be a connected loopless graph and work over a field of characteristic other than 2. Give each edge uv the sum q_uv=x_u+x_v. The signless incidence map B_+:x↦q has kernel zero if G is nonbipartite, and a one-dimensional kernel if G is bipartite.

**Proof.** In the kernel, x_v=−x_u across every edge. Along a rooted path x_v=(−1)^(path length)t. An odd cycle forces t=−t, hence t=0. If every cycle is even, the two bipartition classes carry t and −t, giving precisely one free parameter. For arbitrary q, propagating x_v=q_uv−x_u gives reconstruction; all unused edges are compatibility checks. An odd cycle determines t, while a bipartite graph requires a root value. ∎

Thus ranks are 10 for Petersen, 5 for K5, and 5 for the six-vertex K3,3. The distinction is parity, not nonplanarity. This is an organizing restatement of familiar incidence algebra and the repository's existing signless-potential lane, not a novelty claim. See [arithmetic crossroads, weighted incidence extension](crossroads10_20260926_arithmetic.md).

### 4.1 Graceful labels and square-sum labels share a decoder, not an existence theorem

For a bipartite tree with vertex labels f, the sign gauge x_A=f, x_B=−f converts edge sums into oriented label differences. Oriented-distinct differences need not have distinct absolute values: the path labels 0,1,2 give −1 and +1 under the bipartite orientation. This is the exact mirror obstruction recorded in [THM-2761](../../01-canon/theorems/THM-2761-graph-edge-sum-discriminant-codegree-factorization-and-graceful-sign-gauge.md).

For a graceful edge with endpoints a<b and difference d=b−a, its gap interval is {a+1,…,b}. The ramp first moment μ satisfies 2μ=d(a+b+1). Hence d and μ give the edge sum a+b=2μ/d−1, and the tree decoder then reconstructs vertex labels after a root value. The stronger ordered-gap reconstruction and recognition result is [THM-2793](../../01-canon/theorems/THM-2793-oriented-ramp-reference-and-rooted-graceful-gap-reconstruction.md). Positivity, injectivity, full label range and graceful differences still require their stated tests.

For the square-sum graph Q_n on {1,…,n}, q_uv is a perfect square on every edge. Q14 is a three-armed tree. Adding vertex 15 supplies the ear 1−15−10, creating the odd cycle 1−3−6−10−15−1. Accordingly the signless rank changes from 13 on 14 vertices to 15 on 15 vertices. Q15 has the unique Hamiltonian path, up to reversal,

`8,1,15,10,6,3,13,12,4,5,11,14,2,7,9`.

The unused edge is 1−3. The uniqueness result is inherited, not proved by the rank argument. [THM-4505, square-sum graph structure](../../01-canon/theorems/THM-4505-small-graphs-encoding-arithmetic-square-sum-zigzag-fences-collatz-alphabet.md).

The separate +2/doubling graph develops a K3,3 subdivision when 16 is inserted; this is not the same threshold or graph as Q15. [Seam threshold study](seam_threshold_20260925.md).

### 4.2 Quadratic three-cycles use the same odd-cycle inverse

For distinct x_0,x_1,x_2 with x_(i+1)=x_i²+c, put s_i=x_i+x_(i+1), cyclically. The triangle inverse is

\[
x_i=\frac{s_i-s_{i+1}+s_{i+2}}2.
\]

Moreover x_(i+2)−x_(i+1)=(x_(i+1)−x_i)s_i, so multiplication gives s_0s_1s_2=1. This is the **secant product**, whereas the derivative multiplier is 8x_0x_1x_2. They are different observers.

- At c=−29/16 the rational cycle is (−7/4,5/4,−1/4), with s=(−1/2,1,−2), secant product 1 and derivative multiplier 35/8.
- At c=−7/4, let P(x)=x³+x²/2−9x/4−1/8. Exactly f³(x)−x=(f(x)−x)P(x)². Its algebraic period-three cycle has derivative multiplier 1 and secant product 1. It is not a rational three-cycle. The rational cycle there is 1/2↔−3/2, of period two.

Productive next steps, each with an explicit failure criterion:

1. **Period-aware graph transport:** for any proposed Petersen→zeta map, transport both differential form and integration chamber and prove convergence. A graph isomorphism without those data fails the test.
2. **Marked blowdown compatibility:** compare the del Pezzo C6←Petersen←Clebsch lattice maps with actual boundary-stratum maps. Require the Picard class map, not just induced subgraphs.
3. **Arithmetic label reconstruction:** enumerate square assignments on a fixed small graph, decode by signless incidence, and filter for distinct integer labels in the required interval. This separates linear reconstruction from the hard arithmetic/existence constraint.
4. **Endpoint-to-period proposal:** only advance an E_n tournament/zeta claim after defining a non-arbitrary period attached to its ordered subset addresses. The matching small integers alone supply no such map.
