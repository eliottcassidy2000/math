# An all-depth ternary grammar misses almost every prime on both axes

A cyclic composition C₃[A,B,C] has all arcs A→B→C→A, with each block nonempty and internally unchanged. Consider arbitrary finite trees of these compositions over transitive blocks TT_k (arbitrary k≥1). One may also allow a fixed bounded number of exceptional leaves, each an arbitrary ordinal sum of one-apex tournaments (an apex over a transitive spine). There is no bound on the depth, number of ordinary leaves, sizes of leaf blocks, or on the number of one-apex blocks inside an exceptional leaf.

**Theorem.** The set of direct Hamiltonian counts of cyclic-rooted members of this grammar up to X has cardinality O_b(X^0.99), where b bounds the number of exceptional leaves. The same bound holds for quotients generated by the sevenfold readers from these sources whenever their read is ≥cP₂ for fixed c>0. It includes any finite bank and a reader bank with lower bound c·8^jP₂ at level j. For such sevenfold readers one may also take arbitrary ordinal sums of the grammar's sources before feeding them into the reader; every constituent may have up to b exceptional leaves. So the union of the ordinary prime and sevenfold-quotient axes for these strategies misses a relative density-one set of primes. This excludes the grammar, not those counts in unrestricted tournaments.

## Slot profile, the eight skeletons, and a stronger cost for branching

Let h=H and P=P₂, with two ordered path slots and empty slots allowed. Slot counts of every order are symmetric in the three blocks of a cyclic composition. A cyclic permutation does not change the quotient; a transposition reverses it. Converse of the whole graph leaves each slot count unchanged by reversing paths, and replacing a child by its converse also leaves its local path-slot counts unchanged. To see that child profiles suffice, write the block-symbol word for each slot, collapsing each internal run into a symbol. Choices inside a particular block are its ordered nonempty path covers for the required run count, recoverable from its slot counts by inclusion-exclusion for empty slots. Thus the entire grammar can be counted as a nonplane tree without losing even the higher slots needed in later nesting.

At the root of a cyclic composition, for each of the eight choices of whether each of the three blocks is visited once or twice there is a quotient path word. In a cyclic sequence of length 3+k the first k symbols repeat, and any k-subset of three symbols is consecutive in the cyclic order. This gives

    H(C₃[A,B,C]) ≥ (P_A−h_A)(P_B−h_B)(P_C−h_C) ≥ P_A P_B P_C / 8.

The local sum for one visit or two is h+(P−2h)=P−h. Splitting at either endpoint gives P≥2h.

There is a stronger structural bound for the two-slot cost. Choose independently a two-slot cover in each child. In a given global slot, whenever all three children appear, there are three ways to arrange its child pieces, rather than one. With one or two nonempty children there is at least one compatible order. Recover the local input by deleting other children's vertices; these constructions do not collide. Set r_i=h_i/P_i. Therefore

    P(C₃[A,B,C]) / (P_A P_B P_C)
      ≥ 1 + 4∏(1−r_i) + 4∏(1−2r_i).

The first two marginals are the same: a specified slot is empty with probability r_i. Both slots of a child are nonempty with probability 1−2r_i. Expand the two factors (1+2·indicator(all children present)) to get the joint term. For any nonempty child r_i≤1/2; for a cyclic-root child, order is at least three, so r_i≤1/4 by cutting a full path at all gaps. With 0,1,2,3 cyclic-root children the factors are at least 3/2,7/4,17/8,51/16, respectively.

## Tree weight contraction

Take sigma=99/100; charge each tree weight P(tree)^(−sigma). Let x=2^(−sigma), and for ordinary transitive leaves put L_j=x^j/(1−x^j) (sum of j-th powers of their weights). Write L=L_1, L^(2)=(L²+L_2)/2 and L^(3)=(L³+3LL_2+2L_3)/6 for the weights of unordered pairs and triples. Every cyclic-root tree has P≥12 by the first factor bound. So when the total ordinary cyclic-root weight of subtrees at smaller depth is T, the sum of weight-powers of order j for those live child trees is ≤b_0^(j−1) T, with b_0=12^(−sigma). Using the same unordered pair/triple formulas gives the root majorant

    F(T) = (2/3)^sigma L^(3)
         + (4/7)^sigma L^(2) T
         + (8/17)^sigma L(T²+b_0 T)/2
         + (16/51)^sigma (T³+3b_0 T²+2b_0² T)/6.

In this expression the L factor in the third term matters: the remaining child is a transitive leaf, and its weight sum exceeds one when sigma<1. Exact rational upper substitutions for x,b_0 and the four multipliers are respectively 504/1000,86/1000,670/1000,575/1000,475/1000,318/1000. Each is verified by a 99th versus 100th power comparison. With these substitutions F(4/5)<4/5. Thus all depths together have ordinary live weight at most 4/5.

## Exceptional leaves and all-depth mixed branching

The weighted number of possibilities for an exceptional leaf is finite. For a single one-apex active word of length m (first bit 1, last bit 0), the extreme inversion gives P≥2(3^(m−2)+1), and there are only 2^(m−2) words. This is the [one-apex span bound](../chanpak_eight_split_20261117/THIN_READERS.md), so the sum of P^(−sigma) is finite. Under ordinal sum all slot counts multiply, hence every full profile can be represented by an unordered multiset of active words and a singleton padding count. Summing multiset weights uses the convergent Euler product over active words together with the singleton geometric series. The representative can be restricted to one order of the ordinal components even if the actual ordinal tournaments are different.

With exactly one exceptional-bearing child at an internal node, assume that child is already cyclic-rooted. Its coefficient from the two ordinary children is at most

    B = (4/7)^sigma L^(2)
        + (8/17)^sigma L·(4/5)
        + (16/51)^sigma ((4/5)^2+b_0·(4/5))/2 < 1.

The same rational upper substitutions certify the inequality. If the special child is itself a leaf rather than cyclic-rooted, this one root layer gives a finite contribution even without the contraction. With at least two exceptional-bearing children, each has a smaller exceptional-leaf count. Induct on that count: the finitely many positive splits across two or three live children give a finite weight using the induction hypothesis (even treating all such children as ordered is a valid upper bound). The rest is a geometric series of coefficient B for a single continuing special branch. This proves that the total cost weight is finite for each bounded number of exceptional leaves.

For any cyclic root H≥P_A P_B P_C/8, and the child weight pools just shown are finite, so ∑ H^(−sigma) over cyclic-root syntax is finite. This proves the claimed cardinality bound for direct H. The cost weight directly proves it for the sevenfold readers (for a bank with exponentially growing lower bound, sum the additional geometric series). For arbitrary ordinal combinations before those readers, P multiplies, so the Euler product applies again. Relative density zero among primes follows from the prime number theorem. It does **not** extend the direct count bound to unwrapped arbitrary one-apex leaves, where H need not dominate P.

The phase transition here is structural. The eight local run patterns do not give eight freely programmable target residues. They give a lower bound on cost; the order-forgetting symmetry and that cost together leave too few genuinely different choices. A better grammar needs new interacting strong kernels, unbounded exceptional content, or a reader not costing a fixed fraction of P₂.
