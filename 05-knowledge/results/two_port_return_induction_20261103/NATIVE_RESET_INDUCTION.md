No closed prime/7p induction is asserted.** The all-order module reset has a

## N0. Inheritance, live questions, and carriers

The closest mechanism is the [common seventeen-query observer,
C1–C3](../adaptive_state_induction_20261103/COMMON_OBSERVER.md), valid for any
actual tournament with two named vertices. Its six-command adaptive source
language is smaller: [LIVE_PORTS L1](../adaptive_state_induction_20261103/LIVE_PORTS.md)
requires a C3 core and future-name width at most two. Its marked form also
requires that the specified final ordered pair was never forgotten. The
[unique ancestry theorem A1–A2](../adaptive_metagraph_reformulation_20261103/ANCESTRY.md)
makes these tests intrinsic, up to an initial C3 rotation.

The canonical hostile is the previous H37 rooted-profile pair. The corrected
near miss is using a Hamilton-count return as a source-language return. The
least-used sidecar is the legal **final ordered pair**, in addition to the
ordinary graph's ancestry. No chronological log or numerical coincidence is
used as a source certificate.

| Lane | Object and question | Preserved data / next guard |
|---|---|---|
| Anchor | Actual two-port programs and native returns | Physical names, both terminal slots, exact seventeen continuation counts |
| Anchor | Permanently divisible continuation states | An H-neutral reset must change the future observer, not merely the scalar H |

Write K_P for the native operation retaining the arcs of the actual directed
Hamilton path P and reversing every other arc. Every path below is checked
on its actual input. A birth at r adds x with r→x and x dominating every other
old vertex. Rows encode outgoing-neighbor bitsets. Native steps retain every
physical vertex name and the vertex count; an endpoint's reconstructed
ancestry may have a different seed or chronological order. Those alternative
construction certificates are recorded rather than identified with the old
creation history.

Every C3-adaptive endpoint here has at least four vertices and is rigid by
unique ancestry. Thus an H-neutral endpoint reset also preserves its ordinary
source weight H/|Aut|=H. Native edges retain their individual Hamilton-path
witnesses and multiplicities; a single ordinary edge is never substituted
for that incidence data.

Our independent probe imports no repository implementation. It reconstructs
graphs, peels legal last vertices, compiles retained slots, evaluates the
seventeen static queries by subset Hamilton DP, and checks selected counts by
literal permutation enumeration. It does not repeat the H999 saturation.

The two reported H283/H287 ears have respectively five and four incoming old

**Permanent append obstruction.** Adaptive birth preserves the irreducible
core: its unique inverse first deletes its new vertex, and then performs the
parent's unique peeling. If the core is C3, appending more roots cannot lower
an existing future-live width. At each old boundary the old suffix-root set
is a subset of the new suffix-root set. Thus no finite sequence of adaptive
births repairs any of these failed entries. The reset itself must change the
obstruction, or the source language must be enlarged.

The probe retains actual X vectors before and after these returns at physical
pairs (0,1) and (2,4). Their coordinates are legitimate continuation queries
even outside the generated source language. They are not entry certificates.

## N2. A genuine H9 branch repair, and the failed infinite extrapolation

For m≥1 let T_m be C3 on 0→1→2→0 followed by m births all at root 0; put
n=m+3. The vertices 3,…,n−1 form a transitive block, newest first. The word

    P_m=(3,2,0,n−1,n−2,…,4,1)

is Hamilton; the middle decreasing segment is empty when m=1. Its native
partner U_m is a newborn-chain descendant with chronological name order

    Q_m=(0,n−1,n−2,…,4,1,3,2).

The first three entries of Q_m form a directed triangle. For every i≥3,
Q_m[i] loses among earlier entries precisely to Q_m[i−1]. This proves the
graph map for every m by inspecting which consecutive P_m edges are retained;
all other edges reverse. Both graphs have legal final ordered ports **(2,0)**.
T_m retains the seed pair 2→0 throughout. U_m retains physical 0 and the
moving chain root, replacing the latter until it becomes physical 2.

The two count laws differ. The inherited rooted R/N maps give

    H(T_m)=2^(m+1)+1,
    u_0=3, u_1=5, u_2=9,
    u_m=u_(m−1)+u_(m−2)+u_(m−3),   u_m=H(U_m).

For the second law use the zero-defect newborn map on (h,s,q),
`(2h−2s+q,h−s,h)`, whose characteristic polynomial is
`x^3−x^2−x−1`. For the first, repeated R sends a balanced count h to 2h−1.
The exact maps and their source guards are in
[ADAPTIVE §2](../adaptive_metagraph_reformulation_20261103/ADAPTIVE.md).

Consequently the native map is H-neutral for m=1,2,3 and fails first at
m=4: **33→31**, followed by 65→57 and 129→105. If d_m=H(T_m)−u_m, then

    d_m=d_(m−1)+d_(m−2)+d_(m−3)+2^(m−2)−2.

With d_0,…,d_3=0 this proves strict inequality for every m≥4. The initially
tempting infinite neutral-reset claim is therefore **REFUTED**. Its survivor
is the all-order named graph map and the three finite neutral cases.

At m=2 the literal data are

    T=(26,4,1,6,14),   P=(3,2,0,4,1),
    U=K_P(T),         ports=(2,0), H=9.

The probe records U and both complete marked source certificates. Before
the reset, births at the two retained ports have counts (23,17); afterwards
they have counts (17,31). Thus this one H-neutral native move opens the
specific branch **3H+4=31** at physical root 0. The child remains in the
two-port language. This is a finite constructive repair, not a ternary
closure theorem.

## N3. Native selection does not extend as a linear map on the seventeen queries

Use the fixed physical ports (2,0) on every T_m. The transitive block
M={3,…,m+2} is never a retained port. Initially its only external outgoing
neighbors are 1 and 2; every vertex subsequently created by any of the twelve
two-port commands dominates M. Hence a Hamilton path after any such word has
at most three M-runs: at most two can exit to distinct old vertices 1 and 2,
and at most one additional run can end the whole path.

Fix the outside word. Maximal-run substitution gives a linear combination
of the ordered one-, two-, and three-path-cover counts of the transitive M:

    c_1=1,  c_2=2^m−2,  c_3=3^m−3·2^m+3.

The coefficients depend on the outside word, not on m. Thus every one of the
seventeen X coordinates lies in the span of 1,2^m,3^m. In particular the
four actual vectors satisfy

    X(T_4)−6X(T_3)+11X(T_2)−6X(T_1)=0.               (N3)

The corresponding native output counts satisfy instead

    31−6·17+11·9−6·5=−2.

Therefore **no fixed rational linear functional of X produces the H count
after this native path selector**, even on these four actual marked sources
and their actual marked reentries. A fortiori there is no fixed 17-by-17
linear extension of the observer for this selector. The literal query
vectors independently certify every coordinate of (N3).

This is not a claim that X fails to encode m nonlinearly; H(T_m) already
determines m. Nor is it a counterexample to future-H equivalence under the
twelve birth/discard commands. Native path selection is a different operation
and needs its own source/path law. The ordinary ancestry, selected Hamilton
path, and reconstructed final port program are retained here.

## N4. Proper modules cannot supply an external-port reset in this family

**Theorem.** Every proper homogeneous module of a C3-adaptive descendant is
transitive. Here “strongly connected module” means a module whose induced
tournament is strongly connected, not a strong module in the terminology of
modular decomposition.

Induct on a birth x at v from a strong parent T. Suppose a proper nontrivial
strongly connected module M contains x. It must contain v, since otherwise
x is a source within M. For each old y outside M, x→y; homogeneity gives
M−x→y. This partitions the old T into two nonempty sets with all edges in
one direction, contradicting its strongness. If x is outside M, M must avoid
v, because x distinguishes v from every other old vertex. M is then a proper
strongly connected module already in T, impossible by induction from C3.
Finally, any nontransitive module has a nontrivial strongly connected SCC;
the total order of its SCCs makes that SCC a proper homogeneous module of
the whole tournament, giving the same contradiction.

Reversing a transitive module produces an isomorphic tournament: reverse its
linear order and fix every external vertex. If both retained ports are
outside that module, this is a port-fixing isomorphism and preserves all
seventeen queries. Such a reset cannot change any allowed future H response.
This blocks our earlier full-cover-profile uniform-context lift as a source
of a nontrivial **external-port** repair inside the adaptive C3 family.

The internal-port qualification is essential. Sections N5–N7 exploit exactly
that omitted coordinate: a retained physical vertex can move from the newest
to an older position inside a transitive module.

Independent proof review by the spectrum lane passed this argument. The
probe additionally checks all 146 C3-adaptive ordinary source histories
through order seven (first root normalized to 0), finding 150 proper
nonsingleton modules and verifying that each is transitive.

## N6. Exact within-class native transport is a permutation group

Let T be a rigid named tournament. For every Hamilton path P such that
K_P(T) is isomorphic to T, retain the unique isomorphism
`f_P:T→K_P(T)`, a permutation of the fixed name set. Define

    G_T=<f_P : K_P(T) is isomorphic to T>.

**Theorem.** Native words whose every intermediate graph stays in the
ordinary class of T produce exactly the label permutations G_T. Under the
canonical identification with T, their action on a fixed physical ordered
port pair is the inverse permutation action. Therefore its reachable port
states are exactly its G_T orbit. Legal marked reentries are the intersection
of that orbit with the independently compiled terminal-pair set.

For proof, on a relabelled source σT the lifted word σP is legal and gives
σf_P T. Conversely every class-staying native step pulls back to one of the
listed P. Products give exactly the generated finite semigroup, which is a
group. Inverses are also literal: P remains Hamilton after K_P, so pulling
its return step back through f_P realizes f_P^{-1}. Rigidity makes the
identification unique; nonrigid sources require an automorphism/groupoid
sidecar and are not covered by this formulation. This class-staying theorem
does not classify return words that leave the ordinary class.

For T in N5 there are exactly two self-native paths, namely

    (0,3,5,4,1,2),  (5,1,2,0,3,4).

Their permutations are

    f=(4,3,5,1,2,0),  f^{-1}=(5,3,4,1,0,2).

Thus G_T is cyclic of order four. The physical reset in N5 is f²:

    f²=(2,1,0,3,5,4).

The pair orbit is {(3,5),(1,0),(3,4),(1,2)}. Exactly (3,5) and (3,4) are
legal terminal pairs for the canonical T; they have query content 3 and 1.
The two illegal phases explain the need for two native moves before marked
reentry. The native path/unique-isomorphism sidecars distinguish this graph
rewiring from merely declaring that a forgotten physical name is live again.

Cheap hostiles reject universal port transitivity: the first four-vertex
adaptive graph also has group C4, with three ordered-pair orbits. Both
five-vertex H9 classes have no self-native generator. The histories
(0,3,3,3) and (0,3,3,3,3), of counts 27 and 51, also have trivial self-native
groups. These statements exhaust only the named small sources' Hamilton
paths; they do not exclude class-leaving return gadgets.

## N7. An infinite factor-breaking reset, with the native clock separated

For k≥2 let T_k now denote the history (0,3,…,3), with k copies of root 3,
on k+4 vertices. This notation is local to this section. Put z=k+3 and retain
ports (3,z). The transitive leaf module is M={4,…,z}; z is its newest vertex.
Its count and observer content are

    h_k=3(2^k+1),    D(T_k;3,z)=3.

For the content statement, the rooted profile just before the final sealing
birth is three times the actual rooted C3 profile after k−2 fixed-root births.
The universal sealing map therefore makes every query divisible by three.
The current count and the next root-3 count are 3(2^k+1) and
3(2^(k+1)+1); their gcd is exactly three, proving equality.

Reverse all arcs within M, keeping all physical names and every external arc.
Call the result V_k. Transitive reversal preserves the ordinary graph class
and H. The fixed physical z is now the oldest leaf. The final ordered pair
(3,z) is legal: after the first birth at 0, retain 3; after the first leaf
birth retain it together with 3, and discard every later newborn. Equivalently
use chronological physical names (0,1,2,3,z,z−1,…,4), initial ports (0,1),
and commands (0,1,2,…,2), with k−1 copies of 2.

Apply the already audited [marked block law B1](../adaptive_state_induction_20261103/BLOCK_SWITCH.md)
to the initial rooted four-vertex profile (h,s,q,F)=(5,2,3,6). At the oldest
leaf A=k−1 and B=0. The next root-z birth has count

    p_k=6·2^k+2·3^k−1.                               (N7)

The exceptional B=0 correction s−q=−1 is essential. The root-3 child still
has count 3(2^(k+1)+1). The first two observed counts have gcd three, whereas
p_k≡−1 modulo three, so **D(V_k;3,z)=1 for every k≥2**. This proves an actual
all-order factor-breaking marked reset, not merely one scalar realization.

The first outputs p_k are 41,101,257,677,1841 for k=2,…,6. The last is
7·263; trial division certifies the first four and 263 as prime in the receipt.
These are exact witnesses only; no prime-value infinitude or prime/7p
closure of this exponential family is asserted.

The root chosen after the reset matters. By the exact sealing-gcd formula
in [ROOTED_RESETS R3](ROOTED_RESETS.md), re-forming an interface at root3
restores D3, whereas doing so at z keeps D1. The first profile is(h,3,3,h);
the second has gcd(h,q_z)=3 and F_z≡-1 modulo3. This supplies an exact
root-selection guard for the candidate induction.

The incoming [clone-slot identity](../session155_spectrum_20261103/primes/README.md)
is the general counting mechanism behind such exponential formulas. These
children can also be viewed as j=k-1 transitive clones of vertex4 in the
H41 host(10,4,1,54,39,71,31), giving12*2^j+6*3^j-1. Our added result is the
marked factor-breaking transport and its reentry certificate, not a new

Swapping only the latest two leaves is insufficient once k≥3. The explicit
two-path pattern in the receipt does that swap, together with 0↔2, and
returns to the same H at every k. Its retained newest name becomes only
penultimate, with response

    27·2^(k−2)+15−1_(k=2).

This breaks the factor only at k=2. For k≥3 all seventeen resulting queries
still have content three, as the sealing factor survives before that final
two-leaf rearrangement. The receipt directly checks k=2,…,6. We do not
extrapolate the H15 shortcut into a constant-size native gadget.

The whole-module reversal does have a certified **stabilized** native program:
apply the inherited [one-temporary-vertex arc compiler, METAGRAPH §5](../adaptive_metagraph_reformulation_20261103/METAGRAPH.md)
to each of the binomial(k,2) leaf arcs. It applies to every named tournament
of order at least two, using a traced Hamilton path or the reverse traced
case supplied by one-guard separation. The exact clocks are

    temporary births = deletions = binomial(k,2),
    native moves = k(k−1),       peak order = k+5.

Every old name and every nonleaf arc is preserved at the endpoint. Each
temporary cut and both Hamilton paths must be retained. These births need
not be adaptive births; intermediate H, width-two membership, and marked
entry are not claimed. The new result is the factor-breaking target family
and its source certificate, not a new single-arc compiler.

There cannot be a fixed-length **unstabilized** native implementation of
these literal module reversals for unbounded k. For L native steps, let E_i
be their undirected Hamilton spine edge sets. The final arc-change graph is

    D = (L mod 2) K_(k+4) XOR E_1 XOR ... XOR E_L.

At every vertex the symmetric difference of the spines has degree at most
2L. A literal leaf reversal has degree k−1 on leaf vertices and zero outside.
Consequently an even L requires k−1≤2L; an odd L requires k+3≤2L by looking
at an external vertex in the complemented change graph. This rules out a
uniform bound on L. It does not rule out other factor-breaking endpoint
graphs, a linear-length native program, or the stabilized compiler above.
