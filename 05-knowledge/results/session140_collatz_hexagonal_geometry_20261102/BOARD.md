## Cross-comparison

- Growth versus unit distance: exact unit edges do not impose a positive
  minimum separation on nonadjacent vertices. This is why the two existence
  and impossibility theorems are compatible.
- Degree versus radius: three ports are locally compatible with honeycomb,
  but exponential predecessor supply defeats quadratic lattice capacity.
- Color versus position: chromatic monotonicity under a graph map and exact
  integrability of displacement labels have different directions and uses.
- Rigidity versus geometry: recovery of an arithmetic vertex label, subject
  to the inherited 3-unit lemma, does not determine a drawing.
- Historical HN versus new graph proofs: the named-field descent and
  external six-bound retain inherited/CITED status; neither is a premise
  of the new Collatz growth theorem.

## False-claim anatomy

**Candidate:** one outgoing and at most two incoming ports force a unique
bounded-clock honeycomb or space-filling representation.
**Witness:** every component contains at least `2^r` vertices within linear
radius, while the lattice has quadratic capacity; the known three-cycle is
already incompatible with edge-for-edge honeycomb placement.
**First failed implication:** local degree was silently upgraded to global
metric capacity and a traversal.
**Survivor:** a faithful directed honeycomb subdivision exists.
**Repair:** keep branch markers, direction, component data and unbounded
routing clocks. This repairs representation, not convergence.

Method cards used: controlled forgetting/sidecar repair; exact hostile
examples; quantifier and map separation. The evidence is recorded locally;
no priority claim or global META-PATTERNS edit is made.
