## 0. Inheritance

[BOUNDARY B1](../../normseven_boundary_20261025/BOUNDARY.md) proves, for a UFD `R` and pairwise
nonassociate prime radicands `b_i`, that the fibre product `B` of two complementary character
algebras has conductor `(prod_{i in J} u_i) A` in the (normal) root algebra `A`, and
[CONTINUATION D2](../../normseven_boundary_20261025/CONTINUATION.md) that single-root tests detect it
("one-step compatibility"). The canonical hostile stated there is a repeated radicand `b_1=b_2=t`.
The corrected near miss is to treat `A` as normal after a twist: a twist `d_i` with a square
factor, or two twists sharing a prime, destroys normality. The least-used sidecar is the
**per-character fractional ideal** (the "port class"), invisible in a field-rank count.

## 1. Normalization theorem

Let `R` be a Noetherian normal domain with `2` invertible, `K = Frac(R)`, and for every height-one
prime `p` write `v_p` for its valuation. Let `b_1,...,b_n in R\{0}` have `F_2`-independent classes
in `K^x/K^{x2}`, `A = R[u_1,...,u_n]/(u_i^2-b_i)`, and for `z in F_2^n` put `b^z = prod_{z_i=1} b_i`,
`u_z = prod u_i^{z_i}`, and let `c_z` be the divisorial ideal with

    v_p(c_z) = floor( v_p(b^z) / 2 )      for every height-one p.       (1)

**Theorem N.** The integral closure of `A` in `A_K = K tensor A` is

    A' = direct_sum_z  c_z^{-1} u_z,     c_z^{-1} = {f in K : v_p(f) >= -v_p(c_z) for all p}.   (2)

*Proof.* The sign group `G = Hom(F_2^n, {±1})` acts on `A_K` by `K`-algebra automorphisms
`u_z -> chi(z) u_z`, preserving `A` and hence the integral closure `A'`. Since `2` is invertible,
the isotypic projection `pi_z = 2^{-n} sum_chi chi(z)^{-1} chi` maps `A'` into itself, so
`A' = direct_sum_z pi_z(A')` and `pi_z(A') = {f u_z : f u_z integral}`. For `z != 0`, `y = f u_z`
satisfies `y^2 = f^2 b^z in K`; `y` is integral iff `y^2` is integral iff `f^2 b^z in R` (normality),
iff `2 v_p(f) + v_p(b^z) >= 0` for all `p`, iff `v_p(f) >= -floor(v_p(b^z)/2)`, iff `f in c_z^{-1}`.
For `z = 0` the component is `R`. QED.

**Corollary N1 (recovery).** If the ideals `(b_i)` are squarefree and pairwise coprime, all `c_z = R`
and `A` is normal: this is the hypothesis under which BOUNDARY B1 was proved.

**Corollary N2 (mixed reduced roots).** `c_z is contained in (prod_{i in z} c_{e_i})^(**)`,
where ** is reflexive hull; over a Dedekind base the product is already invertible. The exact valuation identity is

    v_p(c_z) - sum_{i in z} v_p(c_{e_i}) = floor( #{i in z : v_p(b_i) odd} / 2 ).            (3)

So beyond the single reduced roots `c_{e_i}^{-1} u_i`, the normalization contains a **mixed**
reduced root `u_z / (extra)` exactly when at least two radicands in `z` have odd `p`-valuation.
Example over `R = Z[1/2]`, `b = (15, 21)`: `u_1 u_2 / 3` is integral ((uv/3)^2 = 35), while `u_1/3`,
`(u_1+u_2)/3`, `u_1u_2/9`, `(1+u_1u_2)/3` are not; the complete box census of elements with
denominators dividing 9 equals `R + R u_1 + R u_2 + R u_1u_2/3` (receipt `dedekind-normal.txt`).

**Corollary N3 (port classes).** Over a Dedekind `R`, the character component `A'_z = c_z^{-1} u_z`
is a free `R`-module iff `[c_z] = 0` in `Cl(R)`; the Steinitz class of `A'` is `-sum_z [c_z]`, so `A'`
can be free while individual ports are not. Example: `R = Z[sqrt(-21)][1/2]` has `Cl(R) = Z/2`
generated by `p_3 = (3, sqrt(-21))` (inverting `2` kills only `[p_2]`, and `x^2+21y^2 in {3, 6}` has
no solution). With the single twist `b = 3 sqrt(-21)`, `(b) = p_3^3 p_7`, so `c = p_3` is not
principal and `A' = R + p_3^{-1} u` is not free: `u sqrt(-21)/3` and `u(3+sqrt(-21))/3` are integral
(monic minimal polynomials `T^4+1029`, `T^4+84T^2+2100`), while `u/3` and `u(1+sqrt(-21))/3` are not
(`3T^4+7`, `3T^4+84T^2+3388`). No global "reduced root" `u/sqrt(p_3)` exists; it exists locally at
every prime. This is the conductor ideal class on a non-UFD normal base.

**Prime-2 hostile.** The hypothesis `2 in R^x` is necessary and is the only place where
cross-character elements appear: over `Z` with `b = (6, 10)`, `(u_1+u_2)/2` is integral
(characteristic polynomial `T^4 - 8T^2 + 1`) but is not in any `c_z^{-1}u_z`; with `b = (15, 21)`
it is not integral (`T^4 - 18T^2 + 9/4`). Do not transport Theorem N to residue characteristic 2.

## 2. Conductors with twists: the valuation matrix

**Theorem C.** `(A : A') = direct_sum_z d_z u_z` with

    v_p(d_z) = max( 0,  max_{z'} [ v_p(c_{z'}) - v_p(b^{z AND z'}) ] ).                        (4)

*Proof.* The conductor is a `G`-stable `R`-submodule of `A`, hence componentwise. For `f in R`,
`f u_z` multiplies `g u_{z'}` (`g in c_{z'}^{-1}`) to `f g b^{z AND z'} u_{z XOR z'}`, which lies in
`A` for all such `g` iff `f b^{z AND z'} in (R : c_{z'}^{-1}) = c_{z'}` (divisorial), iff
`v_p(f) >= v_p(c_{z'}) - v_p(b^{z AND z'})` for all `p`. QED.

For the fibre product `B = A_C tensor A_D = direct_sum_z R b^{e(z)} u_z` of BOUNDARY (2),

    v_p((B:A')_z) = max( v_p(b^{e(z)}),  max_{z'} [ v_p(b^{e(z XOR z')}) - v_p(b^{z AND z'}) + v_p(c_{z'}) ] ),   (5)

by the same computation with target `R b^{e(z XOR z')}`. When every `c_{z'} = R`, (5) is BOUNDARY
(4)–(6), `c_i(z) = 1 - z_i` on shared coordinates. The extra term `v_p(c_{z'})` is the twist's
contribution, prime by prime and character by character.

**Finite control.** For `R = Z[1/2]`, `b = (15, 21, 55)` (every pair shares a prime), the receipt
`matrix-normal.txt` verifies closure of `A'`, integrality and sharpness of every generator
`c_z^{-1}u_z`, and formula (4) for all eight components including minimality: the scalar component
is `d_0 = (15)`, `d_{010} = (5)`, `d_{100} = (3)`, all others `R`.

## 3. The compatibility test with twists, valuations, normalization and ports

CONTINUATION D2 says that for two complementary binary packets with squarefree coprime radicands,
`(B : A) = K_1`: membership in `B` plus the `n` single-root tests `u_i x in B`. After a twist the
object to test against is the normalization `A'`, and Theorem C gives the exact bank:

**Theorem T (corrected full bank).** `x in B` lies in `(B : A')` iff
`x · c_{z'}^{-1} u_{z'} subset B` for **every** character `z'`.
Do not drop unreduced characters solely because the original single-root
tests pass: D2 assumes disjoint prime support and does not justify that
reduction after arbitrary shared-prime twists. Mixed reduced roots also
need their own tests. The complete bank is exact; no minimal bank is claimed.
Equivalently, membership is the finite system of valuation inequalities (5), one per
(character `z`, height-one prime `p`). Over a non-UFD base the test is an ideal condition, not an
element condition, and its failure can be an ideal class rather than a missing factor.

**Correction control (2026-10-25).** Use a DVR with uniformizer pi and four
independent unit squareclasses a_i, radicands `(pi*a_1,pi^2*a_2,a_3,a_4)`.
Let C have binary basis (13,10), D basis (9,15), bit0 the first root.
They are complementary. For `x=pi^2*u_3`, x and all four `u_i*x` lie in B,
but `u_3*u_4*x` does not. This product test has `c_1100=R`: its radicand
is a unit. The coefficient valuation thresholds are 2 for the single-root
bank and 3 for that product. This refutes the former claimed implication,
not the full-bank theorem or normalization formula. See the separate
[exact control](../../twist_conductor_reverse_20261025/check_incoming.py).
The original N2 inclusion was also reversed: for b=(15,21), `c_11=(3)`
is contained in R, not conversely. In higher dimension the reflexive hull
is necessary; valuation comparisons alone do not prove containment in an
ordinary nonreflexive product ideal.

Illustration (receipt `matrix-normal.txt`, `b = (15,21,55)`): `x = u_1` passes all single-root tests
and the mixed test against `u_1u_2/3`; a scalar `x = a` passes all single-root tests but lies in the
conductor only if `15 | a`, because of the two mixed reduced roots `u_1u_2/3` and `u_1u_3/5`. The
single-root bank is blind to the normalization; the mixed bank is not.

What is preserved: the twists `d_i` enter only through `b_i = d_i L_i`; every statement is in
`p`-adic valuations; the normalization is explicit (2); the character labels (ports) index every
component. What is lost by the bare multiplicative monoid (BOUNDARY B4): the carry in (3), which is an
additive phenomenon of the exponent vectors, not of the monoid.

## 4. Boundaries

- `2` invertible (prime-2 hostile above); normal Noetherian base (valuation-theoretic ideals).
- Independence of the squareclasses: if `b^z` is a square for some `z != 0`, `A_K` is not a field and
  the character argument still gives (2), but the geometric reading changes.
- Theorem N is about integral closure, not about rational points: a non-principal `c_z` obstructs a
  global reduced root, not the existence of points.
- Not verified: an independent audit; the Dedekind example is checked by minimal polynomials only.
