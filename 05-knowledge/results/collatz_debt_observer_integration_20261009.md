## 2. A complete native-cell collision compiler

Let

\[
T(n)=\begin{cases}n/2,&n\equiv0\pmod2,\\(3n+1)/2,&n\equiv1\pmod2.\end{cases}
\]

A parity word $w=(\epsilon_0,\ldots,\epsilon_{K-1})$ has carrier

\[
T_w(n)=\frac{P_wn+B_w}{Q},\qquad Q=2^K,\qquad P_w=3^{\sum\epsilon_i}.
\]

Appending $\epsilon$ sends

\[
(P,Q,B)\longmapsto((1+2\epsilon)P,\ 2Q,\ (1+2\epsilon)B+\epsilon Q).
\]

Its exact native class is

\[
n\equiv r_w:=-B_wP_w^{-1}\pmod Q. \tag{1}
\]

Here the modulus is $2^K$, with **no extra endpoint-oddness bit**: these are half-step parity words. To prove sufficiency, reduce the cleared endpoint equation modulo 2. It forces the first parity. Divide its integral first step out and repeat. Necessity follows by composing actual branches. This is valid with the formal convention that $T$ continues on the 1–2 cycle; strict receipts require the additional stopping test in §4.

Fix an integer offset $e$, equal-length words $u,v$, and positive sources $x=y+e,y>0$. Their native classes are compatible exactly when

\[
r_v+e\equiv r_u\pmod Q. \tag{2}
\]

On that class, set

\[
D=P_u-P_v,\qquad C=P_ue+B_u-B_v.
\]

Then

\[
T_u(y+e)-T_v(y)=\frac{Dy+C}{Q}. \tag{3}
\]

**Collision trichotomy.** The complete positive-source collision set for these supplied words is exactly one of:

1. **EMPTY:** incompatible native cells; or $D=0,C\ne0$; or the candidate below is nonintegral, nonpositive in either starting coordinate, or outside the native cell.
2. **WHOLE CELL:** (2) holds and $D=C=0$. Retain every $y\equiv r_v\pmod Q$ with $y\ge\max(1,1-e)$.
3. **SINGLETON:** $D\ne0$ and $y_*=-C/D$ is an integer, both sources are positive, and $y_*\equiv r_v\pmod Q$. This one source is the entire collision set.

This is a decision procedure linear in the supplied word lengths, apart from exact-integer arithmetic. It is complete for this transcript and offset, not for all possible future words.

The terminal frame is

\[
T_u(y+e)=M T_v(y)+e',\quad
M=\frac{P_u}{P_v},\quad e'=\frac{P_ue+B_u-MB_v}{Q}. \tag{4}
\]

The whole-cell case is precisely $M=1,e'=0$. The singleton case is the separate evaluated equation $(M-1)T_v(y)+e'=0$. Thus structural absorption is sufficient for every member of one compatible cell, but it is not necessary for a meeting at a particular positive integer.

For a fixed offset and horizon $K$, the native cells of $y$ form a partition with at most $2^K$ leaves. Each nonstructural leaf contains at most one meeting source. For $K\ge1$, if both starts are odd, the offset is even and there are at most $2^{K-1}$ leaves. Since equal actual trajectories remain equal, all nonstructural first meetings by that horizon also lie in this finite exceptional set. The same leafwise classification applies to a finite adaptive prefix partition retaining both transcripts at every leaf; this observation does not supply a new probabilistic stopping theorem.

Taking the countable union over finite horizons gives a Haar-null exceptional set. That does **not** permit its removal from an ordinary integer theorem: the positive integers themselves are countable and Haar-null.

### Marked determinant reading

Put $G_w=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}P_w&B_w\\0&Q\end{smallmatrix}\right)$. Then

\[
\det\bigl(G_u(y+e,1)^T,\ G_v(y,1)^T\bigr)=Q(Dy+C). \tag{5}
\]

This is exact source incidence in the marked affine chart, connecting the carry/minor representation of THM-4604 to (3). Removing the evaluated columns, their chronology, or their native guards destroys the certificate. A trace or covariance is not a replacement for this determinant and its source.

## 3. A singleton receipt and a whole-cylinder receipt

For $x=23,y=7,e=16$, the first five half-step paths are

\[
23,35,53,80,40,20;\qquad7,11,17,26,13,20.
\]

The words are $u=(1,1,1,0,0)$, $v=(1,1,1,0,1)$. Their common source cell is $y\equiv7\pmod{32}$, but the endpoint difference is

\[
\frac{-54y+378}{32}.
\]

It vanishes **only at $y=7$**. The retained frame at the meeting is

\[
(M,e')=(1/3,40/3),\qquad20=(1/3)20+40/3.
\]

After two common halvings, the first common odd value is 5, at half-step time 7. The strict odd words are

\[
23\xrightarrow{(1,1,5)}5
\xleftarrow{(1,1,2,3)}7. \tag{6}
\]

The smaller child is 7. Supplying the actual ROOT word $(1,1,2,3,4)$ of 7 produces $(1,1,5,4)$ for 23. The compiler does not infer that suffix from smallness. The native lift $y=39,x=55$ preserves the five supplied parity words but does not meet at this horizon.

In contrast, take

\[
u=(1,1,0,0,1,0,0),\quad v=(1,0,1,0,0,0,1).
\]

Their carriers are $(27,128,31)$ and $(27,128,85)$. For every $t\ge0$,

\[
y=49+128t,\quad x=y+2,\quad T_u(x)=T_v(y)=11+27t. \tag{7}
\]

All prefixes of the least pair 51/49 exceed 1; every corresponding prefix is increasing in $t$, so this entire positive ray has no earlier ROOT. At $t=0$, the odd words are $x:(1,3,3)$, $y:(2,4,1)$. These fixed odd words hold exactly on the even-$t$ subcell $y\equiv49\pmod{256}$. At a general lift the endpoint can be even; append its actual shared halvings before converting to odd words. In particular, $x=179,y=177$ yield odd words $(1,3,4)/(2,4,2)$ and endpoint 19. Equation (7) is an all-height smaller-child dependency, **not** an all-height ROOT theorem for this ray.

## 4. Strict receipt export and supplied grounding

`compile_cell` classifies formal native trajectories. `export_join` additionally requires two positive odd starting sources, a strictly smaller child, actual replay of both supplied parity words, and no occurrence of 1 before the end of either path. It appends only the shared halvings needed to reach an odd common endpoint. Distances between consecutive odd states give the exact accelerated valuations.

The resulting `Join` retains both immutable sources, both half-step words, both odd words, the odd endpoint, the earliest equal half-step time, and the time of the **chosen odd endpoint after the supplied horizon** (`odd_meeting_time`). The latter is not generically the earliest common odd state. Extending the 51/49 input words to horizon 8 selects endpoint 17 at time 8, although their first common odd state was 11 at time 7. The 23/7 values 5 and 7 also show why the first equal half-step clock cannot be identified with an odd endpoint clock.

`discharge` authenticates the entire supplied child ROOT word, checks that the child's common-future prefix is its initial segment, and replaces that segment by the source prefix. Determinism and strict first-hit validation make the splice valid. No root search, ROOT oracle, or unstated child hypothesis is used. Public records are recomputed before use; boolean/float aliases and forged cells or joins are rejected.

## 5. Hidden residue precision is a consumable sidecar

For the standard Collatz pair frame $x=My+e$, a reference parity $j$ determines $i=(\bar Mj+\bar e)\bmod2$, with

\[
M'=M\frac{1+2i}{1+2j},\qquad
e'=\frac{(1+2i)e+i-M'j}{2}. \tag{8}
\]

Knowing $(M,e)\bmod2^K$ determines the next $K$ paired digits for a supplied driver word and hence the $K$-block increment law when that word is Haar-distributed. Each division reduces available precision. In general, knowing the post-block frame modulo $2^r$ requires the initial frame modulo $2^{K+r}$. Therefore the truncated frame is not an autonomous Markov state at unchanged precision.

For every $K\ge1$, the precision bill is sharp. Compare $(M,e)=(1,0)$ and $(1,2^K)$. For **every** length-$K$ reference word, their paired digits agree throughout the block and their debt increments are zero. In the second frame, however,

\[
e_K=\prod_{s=0}^{K-1}(1+2j_s)
\]

is odd and nonzero. The next coupling flips parity, while the absorbed frame continues identically. Both have zero $K$-block covariance; only one is absorbed.

This occurs on explicit positive pre-ROOT sources, without any probability argument. Take

\[
y=2^{K+1}-1,\qquad x=3\cdot2^K-1.
\]

Both first $K$ half-step digits are odd, and

\[
y_K=2\cdot3^K-1\text{ is odd},\qquad
x_K=3^{K+1}-1\text{ is even},\qquad x_K-y_K=3^K. \tag{9}
\]

These paths are increasing during the shared odd run and avoid ROOT. This does not contradict THM-4611: its finite block matrices are conditional laws with precision accounted for, and its global escape proof retains the actual hidden process. It does rule out reading a zero block matrix as a source-specific equality or terminal certificate.

## 6. Covariance can forget the oriented increment law

In base 5, take the translation-only multiplier list $(1,1,6,11,16)$, with independent nonunit multiplier vectors

\[
v_0=v_1=0,\quad v_2=e_1,\quad v_3=e_2,\quad v_4=e_3.
\]

For the two allowed couplings $\pi_\pm(j)=j\pm1\bmod5$, let $\xi_\pm(j)=v_{\pi_\pm(j)}-v_j$. Uniform $j$ gives zero mean and

\[
5C_+=5C_-=\begin{pmatrix}2&-1&0\\-1&2&-1\\0&-1&2\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\mathbb E[\xi_{+,1}^2\xi_{+,2}]=1/5,
\quad\mathbb E[\xi_{-,1}^2\xi_{-,2}]=-1/5. \tag{10}
\]

The reverse translation negates the increment law after reindexing, preserving its second moment but reversing this third mixed moment. This is a precise quotient loss, not a defect in a theorem using only quadratic expectations. A uniform form certificate can deliberately use the common covariance; replaying one ordinary source still requires its selected digits, carries, and source value.
