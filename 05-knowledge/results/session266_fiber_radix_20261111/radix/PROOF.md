# Radix growth, unipotent dynamics, and multiplicity tomography

Status: **PROVED** elementary statements below; original puzzle solution is **CITED** and independently derived, not a new discovery. The tournament connection is an explicit transfer mechanism, not an identification of plants with tournaments. No priority claim is made.

## 1. Timing and the puzzle

The original [26 October 2026 puzzle](https://www.theguardian.com/science/2026/oct/26/can-you-solve-it-a-magical-melody-from-the-mozart-of-math) starts before the first harvest on Day 1. A seed born on day d becomes a regular, coin-producing plant on day d+1. Plants persist; regular plants do not reproduce. The [published solution](https://www.theguardian.com/science/2026/oct/26/did-you-solve-it-a-magical-melody-from-the-mozart-of-math) fixes this timing explicitly.

Write R_t for regular plants ready at the start of day t+1, and C_t for coins after t harvests. Then R_0=b, C_0=c, and

    R_(t+1)=R_t+2a,
    C_(t+1)=C_t+R_t+a.

Consequently R_t=b+2at and C_t=at²+bt+c, by summing the arithmetic progression of daily earnings. End Day2 gives 4a+2b+c; end Day3 gives 9a+3b+c. The decimal inverse problem is 100a+10b+c=427 with digits a,b,c, so (a,b,c)=(4,2,7). This is uniqueness from digit bounds, not merely one solution of a linear equation. Starting at the *end* of Day1 instead would shift t to X−1 and is not the published convention.

## 2. Every base, and the exact uniqueness threshold

For any integer B≥2, impose 0≤a,b,c≤B−1. At Day B, C_B=aB²+bB+c, and division with remainder decodes the three initial quantities. Thus the range is exactly every integer from 0 to B³−1 once each.

More precisely, evaluation (a,b,c)↦at²+bt+c on these B³ digit vectors is injective for an integer day t≥1 **if and only if t≥B**. If t≥B, reduction modulo t distinguishes c, then b, then a. If t<B, the different states (0,1,0) and (0,0,t) collide. At t>B the range has gaps: the value B cannot be represented, since c<B and any nonzero b or a contributes at least t>B. Day B is therefore the unique positive day with both injectivity and a contiguous range beginning at zero. This extends unchanged to any degree d≥1 with B-bounded ordinary polynomial coefficients.

Two different base changes must not be confused:

- If the final numeral is **427 in base B**, it is valid for B≥8; at Day B the initial counts are still 4,2,7, while the integer final count changes to 4B²+2B+7.
- If the final count remains the integer **427**, at Day B the initial counts are its three base-B digits, provided 427<B³. For B=8,9,10,11,12,13,16 these are respectively (6,5,3), (5,2,4), (4,2,7), (3,5,9), (2,11,7), (2,6,11), (1,10,11).
- Merely changing the coefficient bound to B−1 while keeping Day10 does **not** give a base-B digit interpretation. The evaluation base is the day, not the coefficient-bound label.

## 3. Why the polynomial appears: a nilpotent algebra

The daily state matrix on the column (a,R,C) is

    U = [[1,0,0],[2,1,0],[1,1,1]] = I+N,
    N³=0,   N²≠0,
    U^t=I+tN+binom(t,2)N².

The algebra generated by N over Q is Q[ε]/(ε³), acting faithfully on this state space. Its binomial expansion gives C_t=c+(a+b)t+2a binom(t,2)=at²+bt+c. Mother plants implement a second discrete derivative; regular plants implement a first derivative; coins implement the value. The natural recursive coordinates are (c,a+b,2a), whereas decimal decoding uses the ordinary monomial coefficients (c,b,a). These are distinct bases of the same degree-two polynomial space.

Generalization: every polynomial p(t) with nonnegative integer ordinary coefficients has nonnegative integer forward differences d_j=Δ^j p(0). This follows from

    t^k = sum_(j=0)^k S(k,j) j! binom(t,j),

where S(k,j) counts set partitions. A persistent hierarchy with state d_0,...,d_d and simultaneous update d_j←d_j+d_(j+1) (top d_d unchanged) produces output p(t). Its matrix is I+J, J^(d+1)=0. This is a literal realization of polynomial growth by a nonnegative discrete system in every degree. For a prescribed polynomial, nonnegative forward differences are necessary and sufficient for this specific hierarchy with nonnegative initial populations. Nonnegativity of p on nonnegative integers alone is insufficient: (t−1)² has Δp(0)=−1.

Three consecutive coin observations also recover the original plant state, without any digit restriction:

    a=(C_(t+2)−2C_(t+1)+C_t)/2,
    b=C_(t+1)−C_t−a(2t+1),
    c=C_t−at²−bt.

A single bounded-digit measurement and several unbounded measurements are two genuinely different inverse mechanisms.

## 4. Carries are the kernel of evaluation

On integer coefficient lists, evaluation at B is the homomorphism Z[x]→Z, p↦p(B), with kernel (x−B). A local carry replaces coefficient c_j by c_j−B and c_(j+1) by c_(j+1)+1; this changes p by x^j(x−B), so preserves p(B). Conversely polynomial division by the monic x−B proves that every equality of evaluations is generated by these integer carry relations. Digit bounds choose a unique nonnegative representative of each nonnegative value, allowing an extra leading digit if necessary.

Example in decimal: (a,b,c)=(0,0,10) and (0,1,0) have equal value at Day10 but different trajectories at every positive day other than10. A numerical equality after evaluation does not preserve a recursive state. This is the exact obstruction to treating a Collatz count readout, or a scalar tournament Hamiltonian count, as a full interface for future operations.

The connection is algebraic and not an assertion that plant dynamics implements Collatz. A Collatz branch additionally chooses a valuation and divides by a power of2, whereas U is one fixed integral unipotent map.

## 5. Tournament singleton gaps give the same algebra

The core-word decomposition is inherited from [Session24, sections 1–2](https://github.com/eliottcassidy2000/math/blob/main/05-knowledge/results/actual_hamilton_multiplicities_20261021/session24-multiplicities/PROOF.md). Its finiteness and effective cutoff, and the new multiplicity inversion and convexity consequences, are developed in this session’s [fiber lane](../fibers/PROOF.md). The shared unipotent interpretation below is our present bridge.

Let W=(S_1,...,S_k) be a fixed ordered word of nontrivial strong tournament isomorphism classes, with total s vertices. Inserting singleton SCCs in its k+1 gaps yields exactly

    G_W(n)=binom(n−s+k,k)  (n≥s)

isomorphism classes on n vertices: choose a weak composition of n−s into k+1 gap sizes. The SCC decomposition recovers both W and the gap vector, so this has no hidden quotient or overcount. Every such tournament has the same Hamiltonian path count product_i H(S_i), since a path visits the SCCs in their unique forward order.

The generating function is z^s/(1−z)^(k+1). With m=n−s,

    Δ binom(m+k,k)=binom(m+k,k−1),
    Δ^(k+1) G_W(s+m)=0.

Thus the vector of G_W and its first k forward differences evolves by the same persistent hierarchy I+J as the generalized plant system. The source object is a gap-vector census; the target is a discrete jet. The preserved quantity is the entire size-dependent multiplicity function. Individual gap vectors and internal tournament arcs are discarded. W is the necessary sidecar for reconstructing the original family.

Summing over a **finite** core-word catalog of bounded length K gives a polynomial tail of degree at most K. Its coefficients in this shifted binomial basis are nonnegative core multiplicities, whereas its ordinary monomial coefficients need not be nonnegative integers. Therefore the naive instruction “read tournament multiplicities as base-B digits” is generally false without an additional bounded-coefficient coding choice. The honest output is a unipotent recurrence, not an automatic radix code.

### Exact finite-catalog tomography

Suppose an independently justified finite core catalog gives A(n)=sum_W G_W(n) for all n≥n_0, and K bounds the core word length. Then K+1 consecutive values A(n_0),...,A(n_0+K) determine the entire future size sequence via forward differences. A(n) is reconstructed by Newton interpolation

    A(n_0+t)=sum_(j=0)^K Δ^j A(n_0) binom(t,j).

This is a theorem conditional only on the finite-catalog hypothesis, not a proof that an arbitrary Hamiltonian fiber has already been completely enumerated. It is stronger than checking another hundred sizes once the structural catalog has been established, but samples alone cannot certify that catalog. Distinct catalogs can also have identical multiplicity polynomials, so recovering the scalar sequence is not recovering the tournaments.

### Converse retains a parity sector

For a self-converse core word W (reverse order and converse of each component returns W), converse reverses the k+1 singleton gap coordinates. If k=2r, a fixed gap vector has r paired gaps and one central gap. Its generating function in the singleton total m is (1−z²)^(-r)(1−z)^(-1). If k=2r+1, all r+1 pairs must match, giving (1−z²)^(-r−1). These are exact palindromic-gap counts. The converse orbit count of this family is (G_W+Fix_W)/2; paired non-self-converse words must instead be combined before taking the quotient.

Hence ordinary isomorphism-class padding has only eigenvalue+1, but converse-fixed padding introduces eigenvalue−1 and period2 quasipolynomials. This is a real reason a parity/doubling sector appears. It does not identify converse with a Collatz branch or prove anything about Collatz cycles. The minimal polynomial of the shift divides (E−1)^(k+1)(E+1)^(floor((k+1)/2)) for the combined orbit sequence, with a potentially smaller actual exponent.

## 6. Multiplication has a positive recursive realization

Two such discrete polynomial outputs p(t) and q(t), with state maps U and V, have product output p(t)q(t) on the tensor-product state driven by U⊗V, because (U⊗V)^t=U^t⊗V^t. This remains a nonnegative integer transition whenever U,V are. For degrees d,e the output has degree at most d+e, even though this direct state space has (d+1)(e+1) coordinates. The finite-difference hierarchy compresses the output to d+e+1 coordinates.

The positivity of multiplication in the binomial basis is explicit:

    binom(t,i) binom(t,j)
      = sum_(k=max(i,j))^(i+j)
          k!/((k−i)!(k−j)!(i+j−k)!) · binom(t,k).

Proof: count an ordered i-subset and j-subset of a t-set by the size k of their union, and then partition the union into the two exclusive parts and the intersection. Thus multiplication tables, discrete integration and population recursions fit the same positive algebra without requiring decimal notation.

For tournaments, ordinal composition multiplies Hamiltonian counts, but this does **not** automatically multiply unlabelled class multiplicities: different split points in the same SCC word can produce the same unmarked tournament. Retaining the cut as a mark makes ordered-factor multiplication faithful; forgetting that mark is exactly the quotient that must be counted. This is the sidecar needed before using the tensor-product analogy for tournament classes.

A further algebraic extension is negative radix. For B≥2, evaluation at −B is also injective on three digits 0,...,B−1; its image is the integer interval [−B²+B, B³−B²+B−1]. This follows by reducing modulo B and recursively dividing by −B, or by adjoining the B adjacent c-intervals for each b and then for each a. It describes p(−B), not a physical negative day: U^(−B) exists algebraically but need not preserve nonnegative plant populations. Consequently signed radix is a valid algebraic bridge, not a proof that negative and positive Collatz basins have the same dynamics.

## 7. What this lane contributes

The original puzzle is solved exactly and generalized to all bases. The new session bridge is a precise shared transfer algebra: persistent populations, SCC singleton-gap multiplicities and finite differences all realize nilpotent discrete integration. Radix evaluation is a different map, with carries as its kernel; confusing the state with that scalar loses future behavior. Converse adds a calculable parity sector.
