# Paired Collatz exponents: exact fresh-bit queries and random overlap

**Status:** PROVED conditional Haar-cylinder and covariance statements;
FINITE-EXACT controls. This does not establish recurrence of a synchronous
exponent difference, or convergence of all positive integer sources.

**Reproduction:** run
    python 04-computation/experiments/collatz_lazy_precision_innovations_20261007.py
and repeat with the interpreter option -O. Matching output is saved beside
this note. The script uses exact integers and rational probabilities.

## 1. Inherited setting and the coordinate retained

This sharpens the finite-coset flushing mechanism in the companion
[paired-exponent package](collatz_paired_exponent_information_20261007.md).
The closest hostile is dependence of two streams obtained from one source,
even when both individual streams have geometric marginal exponents.
The additional sidecar is the current odd residue, not only how many source
bits have been consumed.

Use normalized Haar probability on the odd 2-adic integers. Let

\[
Y_0=Y,\qquad X_0=\alpha2^vY+\beta,
\]

where \(\alpha\) is an odd integer, \(v\ge0\), and \(\beta\) is odd when
\(v>0\), even when \(v=0\). Both states are odd. Iterate

\[
U(x)=\frac{3x+1}{2^{v_2(3x+1)}}.
\]

We omit the countable null set where a future numerator is zero. ROOT is
not absorbing in this probability model: its valuation is 2. The resulting
statements are about the source distribution, not probability assigned
to an individual fixed positive integer.

Write \(a_i,b_j\) for the two valuation streams and
\(A_s=\sum_{i=1}^s a_i\), \(B_t=\sum_{j=1}^t b_j\), with empty sums 0.
The joint finite-history sigma-field is generated by the two ordered
prefixes, not just their sums.

## 2. Exact joint atoms

For a valuation word \(w\), its affine action is

\[
F_w(x)=\frac{3^{|w|}x+C_w}{2^{|w|_1}},
\]

and its exact odd-output cylinder is

\[
x\equiv
(2^{|w|_1}-C_w)3^{-|w|}
\pmod{2^{|w|_1+1}}.
\]

Final oddness implies all previous valuations by reverse induction.
Pulling the X-cylinder back along \(\alpha2^vY+\beta\), and intersecting
with the Y-cylinder, gives either the empty set or one odd source class

\[
Y\equiv c\pmod{2^{M+1}},
\qquad M=\max\{A_s,B_t-v\}.
\]

Its probability is \(2^{-M}\). Congruences whose modulus exponent is at
most \(v\) first have to agree with \(\beta\); otherwise the atom is empty.
This is checked before forming a pulled-back residue. The script recomputes
the atom from the two words and affine parameters; it does not trust an
externally supplied residue summary.

Conditioned on a nonempty atom, write \(Y=c+2^{M+1}Z\), with
\(Z\) Haar on \(\mathbb Z_2\). At the two current endpoints the numbers of
known bits beyond oddness are

\[
q_Y=M-A_s,\qquad q_X=M+v-B_t.
\]

Each endpoint is uniform on its corresponding odd class modulo
\(2^{q+1}\), because its remaining coefficient of \(Z\) is a power of2
times an odd unit. Both \(q\)'s are nonnegative and at least one is zero.

## 3. The lazy-query theorem

Choose either stream using only the observed joint history. Let its current
conditional class be \(x\equiv r\pmod{2^{q+1}}\), with \(r\) odd. Exactly
one of the following cases holds.

1. If \(z=(3r+1)\bmod2^{q+1}\ne0\), the next valuation is the known
   integer \(a=v_2(z)\le q\). No original-source precision is added.
2. If \(z=0\), the next valuation is
   \[
   a=q+G,\qquad \Pr(G=j\mid\text{entire observed history})=2^{-j},
   \quad j=1,2,\ldots.
   \]
   The original-source precision becomes exactly \(M+G\).

For the second case,
\((3x+1)/2^{q+1}\) is Haar on \(\mathbb Z_2\); its valuation is \(G-1\).
For either stream its old consumed precision is \(M-q\), so the new one
in the critical case is \(M-q+q+G=M+G\). The source atom is split into
classes with conditional masses \(2^{-G}\). In the noncritical case the
fixed increment is at most \(q\), and the other stream already accounts
for all \(M\) bits.

This is an exact adaptive innovation rule. Under a predictable interleaving,
fresh draws can be represented by an independent geometric input stream,
revealed only when the rule requests it. Stopping produces a stopped
sequence of these draws. Conditioning on its eventual stopping length
need not leave the observed draws independent or geometrically distributed.

The conditional entropy of a query, in bits, is exactly its expected
increase in \(M\): both are 0 for a determined query, and both are
\(\sum_{j\ge1}j2^{-j}=2\) for a fresh query. Starting with empty histories,
the fresh increments along a realized history sum to \(M\), and that
history's source-cylinder probability is \(2^{-M}\). A known predictable
schedule and retained affine/residue state therefore allow reconstruction
from the fresh draws; determined valuations need not be stored twice.
This is information compression, not a positive-integer payment theorem.

## 4. Two exact hostiles to stronger independence claims

First take \(X_0=6Y+1\). Then \(b_1=1\), and \(X_1=9Y+2\).
Modulo4, the conditions \(a_1=1\) and \(b_2=1\) are complementary.
The exact joint law is

\[
\Pr(a_1=a,b_2=b)=
\begin{cases}
2^{-a},&a\ge2,\ b=1,\\
2^{-b},&a=1,\ b\ge2,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]

Both marginals have mean 2 and variance 2. Nevertheless,
\(\mathbb E[a_1b_2]=3\), so their covariance is \(-1\).
The initial fixed step has already been flushed; this is a genuine
near-diagonal dependence, not merely a non-Haar marginal.

Second, condition on the X-prefix \((1)\). Compare the Y-prefixes
\((1,2)\) and \((2,1)\). Both have \(A=3,B=1,M=3,q_X=3\).
Their source classes are respectively 11 and 9 modulo 16. For the former,
the next X valuation is \(3+G\); for the latter it is deterministically 1.
Consumed sums, their difference, and the current precision budget are
therefore insufficient to determine the next transition law. The residue
distinguishes the two cases.

## 5. Correlation is supported on a random overlap event

For \(s,t\ge1\), condition on Y's prefix through \(s\) and X's prefix
through \(t-1\). On the event

\[
\mathcal C=\{B_{t-1}-v\ge A_s\},
\]

we have \(q_X=0\). The next X valuation is then a fresh geometric variable.
For every integrable, joint-history-measurable multiplier \(f\), whenever
the displayed expectation exists,

\[
\mathbb E[f(b_t-2)\mathbf1_{\mathcal C}]=0.
\]

This is a conditional expectation identity. It assumes no independence
of the two histories. In particular,

\[
\mathbb E[(a_s-2)(b_t-2)]
=\mathbb E[(a_s-2)(b_t-2)
\mathbf1_{\{A_s>B_{t-1}-v\}}].
\]

An odd class with \(v\) known bits reaches full odd Haar after at most
\(v\) steps: each noncritical step removes at least one known bit, and a
critical step removes the rest. Mixtures of already flushed classes remain
Haar, since U preserves odd Haar. Therefore \(b_t\) is geometrically
distributed for \(t\ge v+1\); every \(a_s\) is geometric.
For this range, their variance is 2 and fourth centered moment 38.
Two applications of Cauchy--Schwarz give

\[
|\operatorname{Cov}(a_s,b_t)|^2
\le \min\left\{4,\ 
38\,\Pr(A_s>B_{t-1}-v)\right\}.
\]

Neither the overlap probability nor the covariance is replaced by the
product of two marginal probabilities.

## 6. Executable tail bounds and off-diagonal decay

Let \(\mathrm{NB}(r)\) denote the sum of \(r\) independent positive
geometric variables with probabilities \(2^{-j}\);
\(\mathrm{NB}(0)=0\). Y's sum \(A_s\) has law \(\mathrm{NB}(s)\).
The initial X-law is uniform on one odd class of relative Haar density
\(2^{-v}\), so any event determined by its orbit has probability at most
\(2^v\) times its full-Haar probability.

For every integer \(H\ge0\),

\[
\Pr(A_s>B_{t-1}-v)
\le\min\left\{1,\
\Pr(\mathrm{NB}(s)>H)
+2^v\Pr(\mathrm{NB}(t-1)<v+H)\right\}.
\]

The second inequality is strict inside the probability. Exactly,

\[
\Pr(\mathrm{NB}(s)>H)=2^{-H}\sum_{j=0}^{s-1}\binom Hj,
\]

with out-of-range binomial coefficients zero. For \(L\ge1\),
\[
\Pr(\mathrm{NB}(r)<L)
=\Pr(\operatorname{Bin}(L-1,1/2)\ge r).
\]
For \(L=0\) this lower tail is zero; for \(r=0,L>0\) it is one.
The implementation and independent point-mass sums test these boundaries.

Fix \(v\) and \(\varepsilon>0\). The bound tends to zero exponentially in
\(s\), uniformly for \(t\ge(1+\varepsilon)s\) once \(s\) is large enough.
To see this without an independence assumption, put \(r=t-1\) and \(H=s+r\).
For some fixed \(\eta>0\), eventually \(r\ge(1+\eta)s\). Thus
\[
H\ge(2+\eta)s,\qquad
v+H-1\le\left(2-\frac{\eta}{1+\eta}\right)r+v-1.
\]
The geometric generating function is \(g(z)=z/(2-z)\), \(0<z<2\).
Markov's inequality bounds the first tail by
\(g(z_+)^s z_+^{-H}\), \(z_+>1\), and the second by
\(g(z_-)^r z_-^{-(v+H-1)}\), \(0<z_-<1\).
Choosing each \(z\) sufficiently close to 1 makes the corresponding
exponential base less than 1: the derivatives of
\(\log g(z)-c\log z\) at 1 are \(2-c\), with opposite signs for the two
thresholds. The remaining factors depend only on \(v,\varepsilon\).

At near-diagonal clocks these tail bounds can remain 1. The script retains
such examples rather than interpreting a failed estimate as decorrelation.
The theorem does not establish recurrence of \(A_t-B_t\), a negative
conditional drift for it, or a convergence result on individual sources.
The next missing input is control of the joint residue state on repeated
overlap returns.

The subsequent [overlap-kernel integration](collatz_overlap_kernel_integration_20261007.md)
sharpens the estimate to absolute covariance at most twice the overlap
probability. It also specifies why the full joint past cannot be the
conditioning field for a random law of a depth it already determines.

## 7. Finite-exact controls

The universe comprises five affine couplings
\((\alpha,v,\beta)=(3,1,1),(3,2,1),(-1,0,0),(5,0,2),(1,3,5)\);
both prefix lengths 0 through 2; and each listed valuation 1 through 3.
There are 223 compatible joint atoms. Both next-query choices give 280 fresh
and 166 determined queries. Each is independently checked on 32 signed odd
integer representatives of its source class. Fresh increments 1 through 5
have exact conditional masses \(2^{-j}\); no sampled frequency is used
as a general theorem.

Additional controls check all pairs \(1\le a,b\le6\) in the antiphase
example; negative-binomial tails against independent point-mass sums;
the consumed-sums hostile; and eight malformed-type or range inputs.
There are 19,667 explicit checks and no Python assert statements.
The output includes rational covariance bounds and deliberately unhelpful
near-diagonal cases. Normal and optimized runs agree.
