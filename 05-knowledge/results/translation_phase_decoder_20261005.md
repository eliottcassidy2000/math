# Translation receipts recover a five-letter guarded controller

2026-10-05. **PROVED:** exact translation-only decoding and finite membership
testing for the marked five-letter alphabet below; a finite precision packet
when its dyadic cost is retained; the complete native guard language; and
source-aware certificate recovery. **FINITE-EXACT:** the declared controls.
**OPEN:** arbitrary-source coverage and arbitrary-child root completion.

## 1. Recover the precise alphabet

The closest stored theorem is the two-letter full-carrier decoder in
[lossless payment carriers, section 3](lossless_payment_carriers_20261004.md).
The four-letter controller actually tested in
[adaptive credit potential, section 4](adaptive_credit_potential_20261004.md)
uses H, G, and the actual words A=1213 and B=1123. The newer
[paid guard budget](paid_guard_budget_20261005.md) supplies L. The prior notes
explicitly did not extend the H/G injectivity theorem to every enlarged
alphabet. This note proves the extension for these five specific operations.

Our board is **marked alphabet / ordered carry / dyadic cost / ternary
phase / native source guard / original-source payment**. The canonical
hostile is the same endpoint with opposite payment. The corrected near miss
is treating affine membership as native guard coverage. The least-used
sidecar is the primitive letter's translation residue rather than its slope.

Each primitive is \(F_i(x)=(3^{r_i}x+b_i)/2^{a_i}\):

| Letter | \(r_i\) | \(a_i\) | \(b_i\) | Native source | Translation modulo 9 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|---:|
| H | 6 | 10 | 669 | 155 mod 2048 | 3 |
| G | 2 | 3 | 5 | 11 mod 16 | 4 |
| A=1213 | 4 | 7 | 85 | 187 mod 256 | 2 |
| B=1123 | 4 | 7 | 73 | 7 mod 256 | 5 |
| L | 2 | 4 | -3 | 219 mod 256 | 6 |

H and L are common-future dependencies. G, A, and B are actual Collatz
words. These types remain distinct even though they share affine notation.
Translation modulo 9 means multiplication by the inverse of the dyadic
denominator in \(\mathbb Z/9\mathbb Z\).

## 2. One exact translation determines the entire formal word

For a chronological word \(w\), write
\[
F_w(x)=\frac{P_wx+B_w}{Q_w},\qquad
P_w=3^{R_w},\quad Q_w=2^{A_w},\quad\beta_w=B_w/Q_w.
\]
Every nonempty word has odd \(B_w\): appending a primitive gives
\(B_{wi}=3^{r_i}B_w+b_iQ_w\), whose second term is even once the prefix
is nonempty. The one-letter case has odd \(b_i\). Thus the reduced
denominator of \(\beta_w\) is exactly \(2^{A_w}\), even when its numerator
is negative. A nonempty word cannot have zero translation.

**Last-letter theorem.** Every slope \(3^{r_i}/2^{a_i}\) vanishes modulo 9,
so
\[
\beta_{wi}\equiv b_i2^{-a_i}\pmod9.
\]
The table's five residues are distinct. They identify the last letter
without knowing the slope or the source. Its exact inverse update is
\[
Q_w=Q_{wi}/2^{a_i},\qquad
B_w=\frac{B_{wi}-b_iQ_w}{3^{r_i}}.
\tag{1}
\]
The denominator cost strictly decreases by \(a_i\ge3\). Therefore peeling
terminates after at most \(A_w/3\) letters. The empty word is the single
translation zero with denominator one. This proves injectivity of the exact
translation representation, hence also of the full affine representation.
It includes the four-letter H/G/A/B statement and its fifth-letter extension.

The same procedure is a **finite membership test** on an arbitrary supplied
reduced dyadic rational. Reject a wrong type, a nonreduced numerator,
an unknown residue, an excessive denominator cost, failed divisibility in
(1), or a nonzero terminal integer. Re-encoding the returned word confirms
the original rational. The promise that the input was generated by a word
is not needed for termination or sound rejection. Integer and rational
typing is exact; floating aliases are rejected.

This is compression of redundant carrier fields: the slope, total clocks,
and marked word can be recovered from \(\beta\). It is not free information
compression. The rational has an unbounded numerator and denominator, and
can use more bits than an explicitly stored short word or a repeated-word
expression. The claimed benefit is recoverability and verification.

## 3. A bounded ternary packet with a retained cost

Let \(A\) be the exact dyadic denominator exponent and
\(M=\lfloor2A/3\rfloor\). Then the finite packet
\[
(A,\ \beta_w\bmod3^M)
\tag{2}
\]
also determines the formal word uniquely. Each primitive satisfies
\(r_i\le2a_i/3\). A nonempty valid remaining word has cost at least three,
so at least two ternary digits remain and its last-letter residue is visible.
After identifying the letter, perform
\[
t\longmapsto(2^{a_i}t-b_i)/3^{r_i}
          \pmod{3^{M-r_i}},\quad
A\longmapsto A-a_i.
\]
Check divisibility before division. The remaining precision is at least
\(\lfloor2(A-a_i)/3\rfloor\), so induction continues. At cost zero the
remaining phase must be zero. Re-encoding verifies the original packet.
This recognizes the finite code language, rather than claiming that every
possible phase packet is a word.

Fixed precision without a growing bound does not recover arbitrary history,
even when total cost is known. For \(s\ge\lceil m/2\rceil\), appending
\(G^s\) multiplies every difference of earlier translations by
\((9/8)^s\), making it invisible modulo \(3^m\). The equal-clock words
HG and GH already have different translations. Prepend enough H letters
to both, then append \(G^s\): the resulting histories are distinct,
have equal denominator costs and slopes, are funded, and have the same
translation phase modulo \(3^m\). Each has infinitely many native sources.
Packet (2) avoids this loss by scaling precision with the retained cost.

## 4. Exact guard recovery: LB is the only forbidden pair

For H, G, A, and B, the native guard is exactly that the affine endpoint
is an odd integer. For L, it is exactly that its endpoint is an integer
congruent to 11 modulo 16. In particular an L output is 11 modulo 16,
whereas B requires input 7 modulo 16. Thus every word containing LB has
empty native domain, although its formal translation still decodes uniquely.

**Complete native-domain theorem.** Every word avoiding LB has a nonempty
native source cylinder. Put
\[
(s,t)=\begin{cases}(16,11),&\text{if the word ends in L},\\
                   (2,1),&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]
Its exact guard is
\[
P_wn+B_w\equiv tQ_w\pmod{sQ_w}.
\tag{3}
\]
The empty word uses all positive odd sources, 1 modulo 2.

Proof is by prepending letters. For a non-L first letter, the tail's odd
input condition already supplies that letter's whole guard; pulling back
the tail congruence multiplies its modulus by the first denominator.
For an L first letter, a nonempty tail's first native input is 11 modulo
16 unless that tail begins B. Its modulus is at least 16. Thus every
non-B tail supplies L's extra condition; a B tail contradicts it. With
empty tail, retain L's endpoint condition 11 modulo 16 explicitly.
All multipliers are odd, so the congruence preimages exist uniquely.
This proves necessity, sufficiency, and the stated modulus at every length.
Positive members remain positive at each legal primitive.

Consequently translation-only recovery restores the missing guard bits:
decode the word, reject LB if present, rebuild \(P,Q,B\), and solve (3).
If the last letter is L, the modulus is \(16Q\), not \(2Q\). The hostile
\(L(27)=15\) satisfies the affine oddness condition but fails the native
guard; this three-bit loss is caught without storing a separate guard cache.

An exact domain automaton has only two remembered states: whether the last
letter was L. It filters syntax, not all positive integers. Arithmetic
source membership, payment, and a completed root proof remain separate.

## 5. Recover a source-aware paid receipt, not just a word

The new `recover_source` API takes the supplied positive odd source and
its exact translation. It reconstructs the full native guard, verifies that
the same source belongs, recovers every state, and checks the inherited
source-sensitive credit ledger: H earns two, L four, A/B earn two except
the B step from 7 earns one, and G spends one. When all prefix credits are
nonnegative, the inherited potential
\[
E(x,k)=(x+5)(9/8)^k
\]
is bounded relative to the unchanged source with factor \(243/256\) per
funding action. A requested paid receipt must be nonempty; the empty word
does not prove strict descent. No failed prefix budget is silently reset.

The `compile_join` API then produces actual source and terminal words with
a common endpoint. It prepends G/A/B's actual words, applies H's inherited
quarter-child substitution, and replaces L's child prefix \((1,2,a)\)
by \((1,2,1,1,a+2)\). When necessary it extends the shared future first.
Native H outputs are greater than one; native L outputs have two forced
nonroot steps 12. Thus these extensions never require padding a root.
Literal replay independently checks both resulting words and their first-hit
boundary. A supplied rooted terminal can consequently be attached using
the existing common-future certificate machinery; this note does not assume
every terminal is rooted.

**Alphabet boundary.** Adding the natural sixth generator
\(K(x)=(81x+53)/128\), on 219 modulo 256, destroys injectivity:
\[
K=G\circ L
\]
as complete guarded maps. Its translation \(53/128\) decodes as LG under
the five-letter convention. One must either keep that convention, or retain
an expression sidecar if a distinct primitive K is admitted. No generic
affine-alphabet injectivity is claimed.

## 6. A Fourier target phase does not decide source-relative payment

For a nonempty actual odd valuation word of length \(m\), let
\(P=3^m,Q=2^A\), and choose its canonical positive odd source
\(0<c<2Q\). Let \(d=(Pc+B)/Q\). Then
\[
0<d<2P,\qquad d\equiv BQ^{-1}=\beta\pmod P.
\tag{4}
\]
To prove the upper bound, the equally guarded negative source \(c-2Q\)
has endpoint \(d-2P\). Every signed negative actual Collatz step stays
negative, so \(d-2P<0\). Set
\(\bar\beta_P=(B Q^{-1}\bmod P)\), using the modular inverse of \(Q\).
Thus the primitive Fourier factor
\(\exp(2\pi i u\bar\beta_P/P)\), with \(3\nmid u\), records \(d\bmod P\); that phase even determines
the canonical odd \(d\) in \((0,2P)\). It does not determine \(c\) or payment.

This is an arithmetic word statement, allowing \(U(1)=1\); a first-hit
certificate separately stops at its first root. Modular reduction must
precede the ordinary exponential. For word (1), \(\beta=1/2\) but
\(\bar\beta_3=2\), so the phase exponent is \(2/3\), not \(1/6\).
An initial draft wrote the rational quotient directly inside the exponential;
this incorrect identification was repaired during the independent audit.

Exact hostiles are:

| Actual word | Canonical source \(c\) | Canonical target \(d\) | Payment |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| (1) | 3 | 5 | grows |
| (3) | 13 | 5 | decreases |
| (1,2) | 11 | 13 | grows |
| (1,8) | 739 | 13 | decreases |

Each pair has the same \(P\), the same full canonical target, and hence
the same Fourier phase, but opposite source-relative payment. Their exact
translations differ. The new exact decoder does not contradict this loss:
it retains all rational information in \(\beta\), and its fixed alphabet
is essential. Aggregating Fourier summands may lose further information.

There is a useful structural comparison with an Euler-product decoder.
Over characteristic zero, if
\(f(q)=\prod_{n\ge1}(1-q^n)^{e_n}\), formal logarithmic differentiation gives
\[
-[q^m]\,qf'(q)/f(q)=\sum_{d\mid m}d e_d.
\]
Möbius inversion recovers every exponent \(e_n\). This is a triangular
decoder for commutative multiplicities; it does not recover an ordered
noncommutative word. Here the distinct terminal residues and exact carry
retain order. Neither decoder, by itself, proves that an unrelated source
lies in a paid guard or a terminating basin.

## 7. Reproduction and finite scope

Run `python -B 04-computation/experiments/translation_phase_decoder_20261005.py`
and repeat with `-O`. The experiment has no default file mutation.
It checks all 19,531 formal words of lengths zero through six by both exact
translation and bounded-phase decoding. Independent inherited guard
intersection confirms 15,985 nonempty domains and 3,546 empty LB domains.
All native words of lengths one through four at lifts 0,1,7 give 2,085
literal source replays and actual common-future receipts, including 1,641
paid instances. Twelve increasing-precision hostiles preserve distinct
funded equal-clock histories while aliasing the declared fixed phase.
The nine malformed/reduced/precision/guard/budget controls include a forged
low-precision source and the empty strict-payment claim. Checks use explicit
exceptions and remain active under optimization.

The practical result is a replayable arithmetic receipt with a finite
decoder and exact guard recovery. Its finite domain automaton and its
successful source controls do not establish arbitrary positive coverage.

## 8. Incoming real-phase injectivity: a different quotient, with a decoder

Incoming commit `3cf1efc70`,
[Fourier experiments, section 4k](collatz_fourier_experiments_20261004.md),
uses the real dyadic phase
\[
\Phi_q(a_1,\ldots,a_m)
 =\sum_{i=1}^m q^{m-i}2^{-(a_i+\cdots+a_m)}\pmod1
\]
for a positive odd multiplier \(q\) and positive integer valuations.
This is the affine word's rational translation \(B/2^A\), reduced modulo
one, with \(A=\sum_i a_i\). It is not the modular ternary reduction in
sections 3 and 6. The incoming fixed-length injectivity has the following
constructive proof and a total recognition algorithm.

Write the real phase in reduced canonical form \(N/2^A\),
\(0<N<2^A\), with \(N\) odd. The total denominator cost is retained by
this reduced fraction. The additional input \(m\) is essential.
The full carry satisfies
\[
B=q^{m-1}+2^{a_1}B_{\rm tail},
\]
where the tail carry is odd and its cost is \(A-a_1\ge m-1\).
For \(m>1\), form
\[
Z=(N-q^{m-1})\bmod2^A.
\]
On a valid word, \(Z\ne0\) and \(v_2(Z)=a_1\), because the reduced
tail numerator is odd. Recover \(a_1\), require \(a_1\ge1\) and
\(A-a_1\ge m-1\), then replace
\[
(N,A,m)\longmapsto(Z/2^{a_1},\ A-a_1,\ m-1).
\]
At \(m=1\), the numerator must be one and the final valuation is \(A\).
Re-encoding verifies the original phase. Failed types, bounds, zero tails,
or base cases are rejected. Length decreases at each step, so this is a
total membership test, not a decoder requiring an image promise.
It proves injectivity for every fixed \(m\) and every positive odd \(q\),
and reconstructs the integer part of the original translation afterward.

Fixed length cannot be omitted, even if total cost is retained. At \(q=3\),
the one-letter word (2) has translation \(1/4\), whereas (1,1) has
translation \(5/4\). Both have cost two and real phase \(1/4\).
The supplied lengths one and two decode that same phase differently.

**No fixed-length antipodal pairs.** Distinct denominator costs give a
phase difference with reduced denominator \(2^{\max(A,A')}\); when they
differ, this denominator exceeds two. At the same cost, suppose two
numerators differed by \(2^{A-1}\) modulo \(2^A\). The corresponding
\(Z,Z'\) also differ by that quantity. If either nonzero \(Z\) had
valuation \(A-1\), the other would be zero and invalid. Otherwise their
valuations agree below \(A-1\), so peeling produces another antipodal
pair of the same shorter length and cost. At length one the phase at a
given cost is unique, a contradiction.

This also independently audits the incoming second-moment conclusion in
its precise random-start setting. For Haar-uniform \(R\in\mathbb Z_2\),
or Haar-uniform odd \(R\), define the absolutely convergent sum
\[
f_m(R)=\sum_{a_1,\ldots,a_m\ge1}2^{-\sum a_i}
                   \exp(2\pi i R\Phi_q(a)).
\]
Each summand is its finite dyadic character. Ordinary Haar averaging
keeps only equal phases. Odd Haar averaging additionally assigns minus
one to antipodal differences; the preceding lemma excludes them.
Therefore both averages give
\[
\mathbb E|f_m|^2=\sum_a4^{-\sum a_i}=3^{-m}.
\]
Absolute summability justifies interchanging the sums and integral.
Finite sums whose denominators divide \(2^M\) use uniform residues
modulo \(2^M\) instead. This audit does not extend to the incoming
fourth-moment numerical values, asymptotic typical rates, or guard coverage.

The phase types differ already on the earlier same-target hostiles:

| Word | Real phase \(\beta\bmod1\) | Modular-target angle \(\bar\beta_{3^m}/3^m\) |
|---|---|---|
| (1) | \(1/2\) | \(2/3\) |
| (3) | \(1/8\) | \(2/3\) |
| (1,2) | \(5/8\) | \(4/9\) |
| (1,8) | \(5/512\) | \(4/9\) |

The new `decode_real_phase` controls cover \(q=1,3,5,7,9,11\), lengths
one through five, and every valuation in 1 through 4: 8,184 round trips.
They check no antipodal pairs and 30 exact truncated second moments
\((85/256)^m\), without renormalizing the truncated path weights.
Six additional type/length/normalization/zero-tail/base-case hostiles are
rejected. This adds a reconstructible real-phase receipt; it does not add
a paid source family or establish a completed route for an arbitrary input.
