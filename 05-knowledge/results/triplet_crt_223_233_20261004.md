## 1. Two refinements of mod 6

The exact ring statement is

    Z/36 = Z/18 x_(Z/6) Z/12 = Z/9 x Z/4.                 (1)

The first product consists of pairs with the same reduction modulo 6.
Each such pair has exactly one preimage modulo 36. In coordinates:

| Modulus | Retained information |
|---|---|
| 36 | mod 9 and mod 4 |
| 18 | mod 9 and mod 2 |
| 12 | mod 3 and mod 4 |
| 6 | mod 3 and mod 2 |

Thus 18 retains an extra ternary digit and 12 an extra binary digit. Sending
an integer representative of a mod-18 class to mod 12 is ambiguous:
`1 = 19 mod18`, but they are `1` and `7 mod12`.

The unit squares modulo 18 are `H18={1,7,13}=<7>`, a cyclic group of order
3. Reduction modulo 9 gives `{1,7,4}`, the old square group `{1,4,7}`.
Reduction from units modulo 18 to units modulo 9 is an isomorphism: each
unit modulo 9 has a unique odd lift. Arbitrary valuation exponents are not
restricted to units, so this observation does not authorize discarding
their parity.

The full lift of the class 1 modulo 6 is

    H36={1,7,13,19,25,31}={1+6t : t in Z/6}.
    (1+6s)(1+6t) = 1+6(s+t) mod36.                       (2)

This is an explicit isomorphism from additive `Z/6` to a multiplicative
subgroup. Modulo 18 it retains `t mod3`; modulo 12 it retains `t mod2`.
The reason addition and multiplication meet is that `6^2=0 mod36`.

Over the integers the exact induced law is instead

    s odot t = s+t+6st.                                  (3)

The quadratic term is the next information to retain: for example
`7*7=49`, whereas its linearized representative is `13`, a difference of
36. They agree modulo 36 but disagree modulo 216. This gives a concrete
recursive refinement of an arithmetic family, without assuming an
interchange law or identifying two unrestricted operations.

The recovered triplets are isomorphic with specified choices of generator:

| Parameter t | H18: 7^t | H9: 7^t | QR7: 2^t | H3 in F19: 11^t |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 |
| 1 | 7 | 7 | 2 | 11 |
| 2 | 13 | 4 | 4 | 7 |

These are multiplicative group maps, not ring isomorphisms. In particular,
the field mod 7 and the ring mod 9 have different nonunit and incidence
structure. Within any of the order-three groups, squaring fixes the
identity and interchanges the other two members; multiplication by a
generator instead cycles all three. This explains two distinct operations
on the same triplet. The older root-ray classes `3,5,1 mod6` are three
different source classes; `1,7,13 mod18` is one valuation class lifted three
ways, so these are not interchangeable labels.

## 2. The joined clock acts on actual Collatz phases

For a positive odd n let `a=v2(3n+1)` and `U(n)=(3n+1)/2^a`. On an odd
prime p different from 3, the branch labelled by its **actual** valuation is

    C_(p,a)(x)=2^(-a)(3x+1) mod p.                       (4)

Since `ord19(2)=18` and `ord13(2)=12`, the simultaneous action on
`Z/19 x Z/13 = Z/247` has smallest exponent period **36**. This is a
functional reason for the CRT join (1), not just a common multiple.

| a modulo 36 | a modulo 18 | a modulo 12 | C_(19,a)(x) | C_(13,a)(x) |
|---:|---:|---:|---|---|
| 1 | 1 | 1 | 11x+10 | 8x+7 |
| 7 | 7 | 7 | 7x+15 | 5x+6 |
| 13 | 13 | 1 | x+13 | 8x+7 |
| 19 | 1 | 7 | 11x+10 | 5x+6 |
| 25 | 7 | 1 | 7x+15 | 8x+7 |
| 31 | 13 | 7 | x+13 | 5x+6 |

All entries are reduced in their column's field. Increasing a by 6 multiplies
the output by 11 modulo 19, but by -1 modulo 13:

    C_(19,a+6)=11 C_(19,a),   C_(13,a+6)=-C_(13,a).        (5)

Thus one six-step change combines an order-three rotation with an
order-two reversal. Six changes, or 36 halvings, restore both. The
`1,7,13` triple alone has affine orders `3,3,19` on F19: its third map
is a translation. It is not an order-three subgroup of affine maps.
The multiplicative triplet in section 1 and these affine orders have
different types.

**Guards are retained.** For any chosen x modulo 13 or 19 and a>=1,
the congruences

    n=x mod p,   3n+1=2^a mod 2^(a+1)

have positive odd solutions by CRT. Hence each table entry is realizable
as an actual branch, but x alone does not select it. Our computation
constructs and replays every such pair for `a=1..36`.

## 3. Why 233 supplies the golden 13-phase

Let `K=F_(233)[phi]/(phi^2-phi-1)`. The polynomial has no root modulo 233,
so K is a field of size `233^2`. Conjugation sends phi to `1-phi`, and

    N(a+b phi)=a^2+ab-b^2,       N(phi)=-1.

The exact Fibonacci identity `phi^k=F_k phi+F_(k-1)` gives

    F13=233,   phi^13=144,   phi^26=-1,   ord(phi)=52.
    u=-phi^2,                 ord(u)=13.                 (6)

For the last assertion, `u^13=-phi^26=1` and u is not 1. The norm-one
group has order `234=18*13`; the script checks all `233^2` elements and
constructs the full direct product explicitly:

    tau=42+77phi has order18,
    u=232+232phi has order13,
    {(tau^i)(u^j) : 0<=i<18, 0<=j<13}
      = {z in K : N(z)=1}.                               (7)

The choice of tau is a coordinate choice; u is specified by phi. An
order-18 group is not thereby the field F19 or its additive space.

The map `n -> u^n` is a genuine golden realization of the mod-13 observer:

    u^U(n) = (u (u^n)^3)^(2^(-a) mod13).                 (8)

This is exactly the second column of dynamics in section 2. Together
with the order-19 phase eta from the binary-field construction, the pair
`(eta^n,u^n)` retains `n mod247`, and the update requires `a mod36`.
This joins the old missing-19 phase to the Fibonacci prime by specified
maps and an explicit common valuation clock.

**A different clock remains different.** The Fibonacci *pair* has period
24 modulo each of 6,9,12,18,36. Sampling indices six apart gives

    indices:  1, 7, 13, 19, 25, ...
    values:   1,13,233,4181,75025,...
    mod36:    1,13, 17,   5,    1,... .                   (9)

That is a four-cycle, not a three-cycle. The same integer 13 is an index
in (9), an element order in (6), and a residue-observation modulus in (8);
the identities above explain those particular transfers.

For comparison, the golden polynomial is also irreducible modulo 223,
but there `ord(phi)=448` and `ord(-phi^2)=224=32*7`: the golden element
generates the entire norm-one group. The 223 and 233 constructions have
different periods even though their definitions agree.

## 4. A sextic/octic pair with a direct Collatz consequence

The [older 223/233 arithmetic comparison, section 7](crossroads10_20260926_arithmetic.md)
already computed the two subgroup indices. We independently reproduce
them and add the octic and sharp-return comparison:

| p | ord_p(2) | ord_p(3) | d=[F_p^*: <2>] | Golden norm-one size |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 223 | 37 | 222 | 6 | 224=32*7 |
| 233 | 29 | 232 | 8 | 234=18*13 |

At either prime set `H=<2>` and `C_i=3^i H`, indexed modulo d. Division
by any power of 2 preserves a coset. Enumerating every nonzero source
residue under `x -> 3x+1` gives exactly one missing nonzero-to-nonzero
transition:

    p=223: C5 cannot go to C3.
    p=233: C7 cannot go to C4.                            (10)

**Common mechanism.** H is the group of d-th powers. Its odd size implies
`-1 in C_(d/2)`. A transition from `C_(d-1)` to `C_(d/2)` would give
`h1+h2+1=0` for h1,h2 in H; conversely such an equation gives a transition.
It is exactly a nonzero-coordinate point of

    X^d+Y^d+Z^d=0 over F_p.                              (11)

The complete projective curves are empty in these two cases, checked by
independent projective-chart enumeration as well as the subgroup equation.
Every other nonzero coset pair occurs. This is a finite theorem at these
two primes, not a classification of all empty Fermat curves. THM-4489
supplies the inherited broader sextic classification separately.

Zero is a separate state: `0 -> C0`, and the single source `-1/3`, in
`C_(d/2-1)`, goes to zero. We do not discard zero when testing returns.

At 223 quadratic-residue differences orient the Paley tournament; at 233
they give an undirected graph, because `-1` is square there. The two coset
automata (10) have a common arithmetic definition without forcing them
into the same kind of tournament.

## 5. The 233 return budget is sharp at ten halvings

For a valuation word `w=(a1,...,ar)`, write `A=sum(ai)` and

    B_w=sum_(i=0..r-1) 3^(r-1-i) 2^(a1+...+ai).
    U_w(n)=(3^r n+B_w)/2^A.                              (12)

If p divides n, it divides the endpoint iff it divides B_w. The independent
composition census has **all 1023** positive words with A<=10, with no
trajectory filter. Exactly four have `233|B_w`:

| w | r | A | B_w | Descent threshold B_w/(2^A-3^r) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| (5,2,1) | 3 | 8 | 233 | 233/229 |
| (5,2,2) | 3 | 9 | 233 | 233/485 |
| (5,2,3) | 3 | 10 | 233 | 233/997 |
| (2,1,1,2,3,1) | 6 | 10 | 1631=7*233 | 1631/295 |

Each threshold is strictly less than 233. Therefore **every actual return
from a positive 233-multiple to a 233-multiple using at most ten halvings
strictly descends**, at arbitrary integer height. No assertion is made
that an arbitrary source has such a return.

The four guarded source progressions have least members and periods
`(55221,119296)`, `(234165,238592)`, `(114869,477184)`, and
`(365577,477184)`, in table order. They are disjoint and occupy the exact
fraction `1/128` of positive odd 233-multiples. A separate direct source
census checks all 1024 odd multiples in the common period 477184: exactly
eight qualify. Thus the rule starts at **55221** and applies to no source
in the earlier compiler benchmark `n<=10000`. This is a concrete boundary
on its immediate usefulness, rather than an assumed compiler improvement.

At A=11 the word `(1,3,1,1,1,3,1)` has r=7 and B=5359=23*233. It gives
the expanding **first** return

    707155 -> 1060733 -> 397775 -> 596663 -> 894995
           -> 1342493 -> 503435 -> 755153.                (13)

There are no intermediate 233-multiples. This establishes the exact
failure boundary, paralleling THM-4489's bound eight and failure at nine
for 223. Its slope `3^7/2^11` has gap `3^7-2^11=139`, the old -17-cycle
clock; its carry is different. The shared slope does not imply a shared
cycle or an integer fixed point.

The exact unreduced carry 233 occurs only for `(5,2,k)`, k>=1. Indeed the
last carry term bounds the preceding valuation sum by 7; the complete
finite prefix search leaves just `(5,2)`. The first word gives the rational
cycle `233/229 -> 29/229 -> 79/229 -> 233/229`, equivalently the integer
cycle `233 -> 29 -> 79 -> 233` of `3n+229` with those valuations. None
of `(5,2,k)` gives an integer 3n+1 cycle: its positive gap
`2^(7+k)-27` never divides 233. This does not exclude multiples of that carry.

The older golden-shaped gap `2^13-3^8=1631=7*233` is recovered here as the
carry of a *different* shape `(r,A)=(6,10)`. This supplies a precise
arithmetic cross-reference; it is not a map between the corresponding
cycles. The earlier torsion census's caution about its Fibonacci
interpretation remains intact.

## 6. The source values and the forgotten tournament connection

    223 ->335 ->503 ->755 ->1133 ->425,
    111 ->167 ->251 ->377 -> 283 ->425,
    233 ->175 ->263 ->395 -> 593 ->445 ->167

A much less obvious repo connection already has an actual combinatorial

    H00=233, H10=291, H01=123, H11=223;
    H11-H10-H01+H00=42.                                  (15)

We independently replay all four counts by enumerating the 8! orders.
The interaction counts two boundary contractions of a three-vertex block;

## 7. A useful enriched object and the next test

**PROPOSAL, built from the proved maps.** Attach to a source a typed record

    (n; eta^n, u^n; exact valuation word;
     r,A,B_w; zero-or-coset states at223 and233;
     checked common-future witnesses to smaller certified sources).       (16)

The projection to n is specified. The phase pair needs only `a mod36`
for a branch update; the exact a remains stored for guards and height.
The 223/233 coordinates can reject impossible residue transitions and
certify certain bounded-cost returns. Word composition is exact:

    (r,A,B) followed by (s,D,E)
       -> (r+s,A+D,3^s B+2^A E).                         (17)

Source: guarded integer orbit segments. Target: the record (16).
Preserved predicates: realized branch, modular phase, endpoint identity,
and strict descent when explicitly certified. Destroyed by the finite
projections: actual source height and unbounded halving cost. Required
sidecar: the exact word/carry and a well-founded dependency certificate.
Cheapest decisive test, performed here: find the exact source cylinders
where the 233 rule can apply. Their minimum 55221 proves that the old
10000-source-height benchmark cannot benefit directly. The next experiment
would expand that benchmark and track paths entering these cylinders, with
the expanding first return (13) as a compulsory hostile control.

This session proves the local rule and records its exact boundary; it does
not claim a compiler improvement from that rule. A useful alternative
direction is to keep the *first exit beyond the certified budget* as an
explicit debt state, analogous to the earlier reset-two debt. The open
question is whether these debt states admit uniformly checked switches
to smaller certified dependencies. More phase labels alone do not prove
that every positive source has such a certificate.
