## 2. Independent audit of the all-degree polynomial descent

I rederived the leading equation directly. For leading rows
`F=A(x)t^p+...`, `G=g_n(x)t^n+...`, n>=1, the coefficient at t-degree
p+n-1 in J(F,G) is

    n A' g_n-p A g_n'.

Because p+n-1>=2, the constant Jacobian makes it zero. In the field
C(x), this is precisely `(g_n^p/A^n)'=0`. Its value is a nonzero
complex constant; no coefficient parameter is assumed generic.

If n=ps, then `(g_n/A^s)^p` is a nonzero constant. The quotient is
itself a complex constant, since the p roots of that constant all lie
in C and C(x) is a field. Subtracting the corresponding constant
multiple of F^s therefore cancels the entire highest t-coefficient.
This is a permitted polynomial target shear for every s>=1, and it
preserves the exact Jacobian, including its nonzero value.

The integer degree strictly decreases at each step. A zero polynomial
cannot result because its Jacobian would vanish. A degree-zero mate
B(x) is also impossible: `-F_t B'` is either zero or has exact positive
source degree p-1. Thus termination leaves a positive n not divisible
by p. No search or upper bound on n is involved.

Primality now has its exact stated role. Since gcd(n,p)=1, the local
identity `p ord(g_n)=n ord(A)` forces each finite multiplicity of A to
be divisible by p. Factoring in C[x] and taking a scalar pth root
therefore gives A=V^p for a polynomial V!=0. Constant A is included.
A composite p with n sharing a divisor does not permit this inference;
that missing coprimality is correctly separated by the source control.

This proof uses neither a full Newton polygon nor a global canonical
root. The passage A=V^p says only that the leading coefficient has a
polynomial root. It does not construct a polynomial pth approximate
root of F or justify target substitution by such a root.
