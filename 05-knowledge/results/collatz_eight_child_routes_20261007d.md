**CONDITIONAL:** ROOT completion still requires the selected smaller child's

The inherited [eight-bit completion](collatz_eight_bit_completion_20261007c.md)

\(F=924745897\bmod2^{32}\). The

The anchor is the actual emitted child, the niche is modular prefix reading,

## 1. A finite comparison that finds longer rules

For a word \(w\), write
\[
 F_w(x)=\frac{P_wx+B_w}{Q_w},\qquad
 P_w=3^{|w|},\quad Q_w=2^{A_w}.
\]
The useful ordered invariant is
\[
 I_w=3B_w-3P_w+Q_w,\qquad
 I_\varnothing=-2,\qquad I_{w a}=3I_w+2^{A_w+a}. \tag{1}
\]
It retains the carry; a slope or cost alone cannot perform this comparison.

After the \(F-1\) initial ones of \(M_F\), the actual checkpoint is
\(x=2\cdot3^{F-1}-1\). To read its next valuations, it suffices to compute
this integer modulo \(2^b\). If \(3x+1\) is nonzero modulo \(2^b\), its
valuation below \(b\) is exact; division consumes exactly that many known
bits. A zero residue means insufficient precision, not a zero integer or
a ROOT hit. The reader returns only the prefix it can authenticate.

The explicit search used \(F=924745897\), precision 8192 bits, deletions
\(1\le D\le128\), and source-head lengths \(0\le r\le1024\).
It compared source length \(r\) with child length \(r+D\), retaining the
same full odd-step clock after their different initial one-runs.
Equality \(I_u=I_v\), an even cost difference \(A_u-A_v=2g\), and actual
terminal valuations \(c,c+2g\ge1\) certify the signed-ladder template.
There are 24 successful deletions in this declared search. Its shortest
source head has length 39 and supports both \(D=7\) and \(D=8\).
This is not a global minimality statement.

## 2. The exact packet

The source head is

    u = (2,2,4,1,3,3,1,3,3,1,1,1,2,2,3,3,1,2,1,1,2,1,1,2,
         1,3,2,3,2,3,3,3,1,3,4,1,1,3,2).

Its seven-bit partner is

    v7 = (4,2,3,2,2,1,1,2,2,1,1,2,2,2,1,2,1,1,2,1,1,1,2,1,
          2,1,1,2,3,1,1,2,3,2,2,1,1,1,5,1,1,1,2,2,3,1).

The eight-bit partner replaces the initial \(4\) of \(v_7\) by
\((2,2)\). Thus
\[
 |u|=39,\quad |v_D|=39+D,\quad A_u=81,\quad A_{v_D}=79,
 \qquad D\in\{7,8\}. \tag{2}
\]
Exact carrier arithmetic gives
\[
 F_{v_D}(-1)=4F_u(-1)+1.
\]
Together with the slope relation, this is the full identity
\[
 \boxed{F_{v_D}(y)=4F_u(x)+1
 \quad\hbox{whenever }x+1=3^D(y+1).} \tag{3}
\]
The script checks (3) both by rational carriers and the independent integer
identity \(I_u=I_{v_D}\).

Let \(Q=2^{81}\), \(P=3^{39}\), and
\[
 r_u=(Q-B_u)P^{-1}\bmod2Q.
\]
The exact native odd-source cell for \(u\) is \(x=r_u\bmod2Q\).
Pulling it back through \(x=2\cdot3^{F-1}-1\) gives
\[
 3^{F-1}\equiv(r_u+1)/2\pmod {2^{81}},
 \qquad
 \boxed{F\equiv924745897\pmod {2^{79}}}. \tag{4}
\]
The period is minimal: the order of 3 modulo \(2^{81}\) is \(2^{79}\).
Thus this is an iff for the native head, not merely a sufficient list of
examples. Inside the inherited exponent progression
\(F=924745897+2^{32}t\), the new condition is
\(t=0\bmod2^{47}\). Its relative natural density there is \(2^{-47}\).
It gives a genuine positive subprogression, not coverage of the entire
previous phase.

## 3. Ordinary-source payment and first-hit safety

The same identity supplies modest-size literal controls. Fix \(e\ge162\)
and put
\[
 t_0\equiv \frac{r_u+1}{2}\,3^{-e}\pmod {2^{81}},\qquad
 t=t_0+2^{81}s,\quad s\ge0,\qquad n=2^{e+1}t-1. \tag{5}
\]
The canonical \(t_0\) is positive odd. The first \(e\) valuations of \(n\)
are ones, and its next checkpoint \(x=2\cdot3^et-1\) is in the native
cell. Define \(Z=F_u(x)\), read the actual positive
\(c=v_2(3Z+1)\), and let \(z=(3Z+1)/2^c\).

For \(D=7\) or \(8\), the supplied child is
\[
 h_D=(n+1)/2^D-1.
\]
After its first \(e-D\) ones, its checkpoint \(y\) satisfies (3).
The actual common-future receipt is
\[
 n\xrightarrow{\,1^e,u,c\,}z,\qquad
 h_D\xrightarrow{\,1^{e-D},v_D,c+2\,}z. \tag{6}
\]

Here is the sufficient first-hit guard rather than an implicit assumption.
Every source prefix through the head has multiplicative coefficient at
least
\[
 (3/2)^e2^{-81}\ \ge\ (9/8)^{81}>1
\]
relative to the original source; the affine carries are positive. All
initial one-steps grow as well. Thus no source prefix through \(u\) is ROOT,
and \(Z>n\). Identity (3) makes the child-head endpoint \(4Z+1>1\) positive
odd. In a positive valuation word, final odd integrality forces every
earlier state to be integral and odd: a nonintegral dyadic state cannot
regain integrality, and an even intermediate would destroy it at the next
step. The positive child therefore follows its exact word and cannot have
hit ROOT earlier, because legal positive odd steps cannot leave ROOT.
The final edge may reach ROOT for the first time.

Both children are positive and strictly smaller than the immutable source.
The full source/child odd clocks in (6) are equal and their valuation costs
differ by \(D\). Source-size payment is therefore established independently
of any child ROOT premise. Every positive exponent in (4) already exceeds
the sufficient cutoff \(F-1\ge162\); the huge Mersenne sources need not be
materialized to apply this proof.

## 4. Composition and the next explicit obligation

The first 15 letters of \(u\) are exactly the inherited eight-bit partner
head. The next letter is 3, so the previous source terminal is 1. This
retains the actual middle word, and the existing receipt composer aligns
the two checked clocks without replacing the middle source.

Consequently the earlier parent phase refines to
\[
 K\equiv924745905\pmod {2^{79}},\qquad
 M_K\rightsquigarrow M_{K-8}\rightsquigarrow
 \begin{cases}M_{K-15},\\M_{K-16}.\end{cases} \tag{7}
\]
Each arrow is a common-future dependency, not necessarily a forward edge.
The parent package owns the direct combined packet and any larger inverse
budget consequences. Here sixteen modest ordinary-source controls check
the exact middle-prefix composition independently.

The eight-bit choice returns to the three-twos type:
\[
 F-8\equiv924745889\pmod {2^{79}},\qquad v_2(F-9)=5.
\]
It misses all 225 current \(J=3\) head phases. Their costs are at most 25,
so their periods divide \(2^{29}\), hence the displayed period. The complete
finite comparison therefore excludes the whole
new terminal progression from this particular bank, not only its least
point. This is a precise remaining obligation. No ROOT proof for
\(M_{924745889}\), or for every member of that progression, is inferred.
The seven-bit choice is retained as a separate valid child port, not counted
as independent guard coverage. Its child has a valuation-four reset, and
the inherited reset switch replaces that \(4\) by \((2,2)\) at the next
smaller child. This is exactly the displayed \(v_7\)-to-\(v_8\)
factorization; see
[the checked reset switch](checked_switch_phase19_20261004.md).

## 5. Production contract and reproducibility

The script exports exact exponent membership, guarded source residues,
head-endpoint residues with the full denominator precision, an ordinary
source constructor, and actual receipts. Literal materialization has an
explicit cap; the least huge Mersenne exponent is rejected by that API.
The symbolic reader instead uses modular exponentiation. Its finite
precision does not silently become a ROOT or continuation claim.

The comparison and tests use:

- all 128 deletion choices and 1025 source-head lengths stated above;
- 32 literal receipts at runs \(162,163,200,256\) and parameters
  \(0,1,7,31\), including both child choices;
- sixteen exact inherited-parent compositions;
- symbolic phase parameters \(0,1,2,9,10^{30}\), with moduli
  \(2,3,19,729,2^{128},3^{20}\cdot19\);
- malformed types, a changed child, a neighboring source outside the native
  guard, insufficient binary precision and materialization-cap hostiles.
