# How many root continuations detect the conductor?

2026-10-25. **PROVED** from B1/C1, with finite controls in
`check_continuation.py`. Internal proof review; no independent agent audit.
This is an algebraic continuation theorem, not a Collatz stopping theorem.

## D1. A finite continuation bank always suffices

Use C1's notation A, B, m and n. For d>=0 define

    K_d = {x in B : u^w x is in B for every w in N^n with |w|<=d}.

Then K_0=B, the K_d decrease, and their intersection is (B:A), the
largest A-submodule contained in B. Since u_i^m=b_i belongs to R,
every word is an R-multiple of a word with each exponent below m. Hence

    K_(n(m-1)) = (B:A).                              (1)

This is the exact continuation-core operation suggested by the incoming
[operation-complete observer framework, section 1](../session63_integral_observers_root_arrangements_20261025/PROPOSED_UNIFYING_THEORY.md).
Its source here is a coefficient lattice B inside the marked root algebra
A; the operations are multiplication by the specified roots. The preserved
predicate is membership in B after every continuation. Counts of characters
alone discard multiplication constants. Those constants and valuations
are the required additional data. The decisive test is a word that exits B.

For binary packets, (1) improves to

    K_(max(n-1,0)) = (B:A).                          (2)

**Proof of (2).** Fix a coefficient character z and a coordinate i, and
let t_i be the number of summands with nonzero i-th coordinate image.
C1 gives required valuation floor((t_i-z_i)/2). The summands can choose
their i-th bits independently, so their ordinary sum can be any integer
from 0 to t_i. Choose the largest such sum congruent to z_i modulo 2.
Its character w has w_i=z_i and attains the required valuation. The
testing word y=w+z thus has y_i=0 and length at most n-1. Each prime is
tested separately; one word need not attain all coordinate bounds.
Multiplication by a fixed word permutes characters, so distinct source
components cannot cancel. This proves the claim for arbitrary x, not
only monomials. The case n=1 has only one nonzero binary summand and B=A.

## D2. Two complementary binary packets need only one step

Under B1's hypotheses V=C direct_sum D over F_2,

    (B:A) = K_1 = B intersect intersection_i {x in A : u_i x in B}. (3)

Thus testing each of the six chart roots once is sufficient for the
norm-seven pair. The conclusion applies to the integral algebra on a
chart, including vanishing-root boundaries; it does not mean that
reducing all valuations to residue-field values preserves the test.

**Proof.** Let P,Q be the complementary linear projections and
e_i(z)=(Pz)_i (Qz)_i. B1 gives required conductor exponent 1-z_i at a
coordinate shared by C and D, and zero otherwise. Fix a shared i and
z_i=0. If e_i(z)=1, membership in B already supplies the needed prime.
Otherwise (Pz)_i=(Qz)_i=0. Because both coordinate images are nonzero,
the i-th row of P is neither zero nor the i-th coordinate row. Thus
there is j!=i with (P e_j)_i=1. Since P+Q=I, also (Q e_j)_i=1.
Consequently e_i(z+e_j)=1, while multiplication by u_j creates no
b_i carry. The single-root test forces the missing b_i divisibility.
This holds for every i,z, proving K_1 is contained in the conductor;
the opposite inclusion follows from its definition. QED.

There is no fixed-point iteration hidden in (3). For this particular
two-packet algebra, passing the initial one-step tests already implies
passing every longer word. With no shared coordinate, B=A and K_0
already suffices. Once a shared coordinate exists, K_0!=K_1.

## D3. Sharp delayed failures for many binary packets

For n>=2, in F_2^n with coordinate labels 0,...,n-1 take independent
one-dimensional packets generated by

    v_0=e_0,     v_j=e_0+e_j for 1<=j<n.

Only coordinate 0 is shared. The corresponding algebra is

    B=R[u_0, u_0 u_1, ..., u_0 u_(n-1)] inside A,
    (B:A)=u_0^(n-1) A.                              (4)

The marked element x=u_0^(n-2) belongs to K_(n-2), but

    x u_1 ... u_(n-1) is not in B.                  (5)

Hence bound (2) is sharp across this class. Failure can be delayed to
any prescribed finite depth by increasing the number of packets.

**Proof.** For a reduced word, let q count its roots among u_1,...,u_(n-1)
and let epsilon in {0,1} indicate u_0. The exponent of u_0 in the source
product is a=n-2+epsilon. To have those q other roots, the generators of
B require the least exponent of u_0 that is >=q and congruent to a
modulo 2. If the word has length at most n-2, q<=n-2<=a, so membership
holds. Longer words reduced using u_i^2=b_i only acquire R factors;
therefore the same conclusion holds for all words of that bounded
length, not only squarefree words. For (5), q=n-1 and a=n-2; the least
allowed exponent is n, exceeding a by two. Exactly one b_0 is missing.
Equation (4) is C1 with t_0=n and all other t_i=1. QED.

The first one-step hostile is n=3:
B=R[u_0,u_0u_1,u_0u_2], x=u_0. All three u_i x lie in B, but
u_1u_2x does not. This is not a failure of D2: three packets are present.
It is also not an unbounded obstruction within one fixed finite algebra;
the number of packets and coordinates grows with the delayed depth.
