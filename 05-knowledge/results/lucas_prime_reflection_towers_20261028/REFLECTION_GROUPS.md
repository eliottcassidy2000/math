## 1. A general fixed-point gap

**Proposition.** Let p be prime, p not in {11,23}. Suppose a permutation group
G on p points contains a p-cycle and an involution with f fixed points, where

    1 < f < sqrt(p).

Then G contains A_p.

The p-cycle makes G transitive and hence primitive. We use only the k=0
case of Theorem 1.2 of [Gareth Jones, Primitive permutation groups containing
a cycle](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1209.5169). If G does not contain A_p, it is
affine of degree p, projective of degree (q^d-1)/(q-1), or one of the
exceptional degree-11/23 groups. This imported classification uses the
classification of finite simple groups. The following exclusion argument
is given explicitly rather than assumed from small examples.

An affine involution x -> ax+b over F_p has a=-1 and exactly one fixed point.
For a projective degree p=[d]_q, primality forces d prime. Here
[j]_q=1+q+...+q^(j-1), with [0]_q=0.

If d=2, q=p-1 is an even prime power. A nonidentity linear projective
involution in characteristic two fixes exactly one line. A semilinear
involution with nontrivial field component requires q=s^2 and fixes
[2]_s=s+1>sqrt(q+1) lines, as justified below.

If d=2k+1 is odd, consider first a linear projective involution, represented
by A with A^2=cI. In odd characteristic, det(A)^2=c^d makes c a square;
rescaling gives A^2=I. Its eigenspaces have dimensions a,b with a+b=d.
The number of fixed lines is [a]_q+[b]_q, minimized at {a,b}={k,k+1},
and is at least [k+1]_q. In characteristic two, normalize A^2=I and
write A=I+N, N^2=0. Then dim ker N>=k+1 and the number of fixed lines
is again at least [k+1]_q. Finally

    [k+1]_q^2 > [2k+1]_q.

Expand both polynomials: every coefficient on the left is at least the
corresponding coefficient on the right, and some are strictly larger.

For a genuinely semilinear involution, its field part is the quadratic
automorphism sigma of F_(s^2)/F_s. Write it as S=A sigma. From S^2=cI
one gets c in F_s^*. Surjectivity of the finite-field norm permits scaling
S to have S^2=I. If theta is outside F_s, every vector v decomposes uniquely
as v=a+theta b with a,b fixed by S: take

    b=(v-Sv)/(theta-sigma(theta)),     a=v-theta b.

Thus the fixed vector space has dimension d over F_s. A stable line has a
fixed representative: the scalar in Sv=lambda v has norm one, and every
norm-one scalar is z/sigma(z), as follows by counting in the cyclic
multiplicative group. There are exactly [d]_s fixed projective points.
Since [d]_s^2>[d]_(s^2) for d>=2, this also exceeds sqrt(p).

All possible proper cases contradict the fixed-point gap. QED.

The hypothesis is sharp enough to need its exclusions: the inherited
eleven-word group is PSL(2,11) with f=3. A dihedral group of odd prime
degree has an involution with f=1 and need not contain A_p. We exclude
degree23 without claiming it actually supplies a counterexample.

## 2. Every odd Lucas-prime degree beyond eleven

Let F_0=0,F_1=1 and L_0=2,L_1=1, both satisfying the Fibonacci recurrence.
Let W_n consist of rooted binary cyclic words with no adjacent ones. Its
cardinality is L_n. Put n=2m+1>=5, and reflect positions by i -> -i.

The fixed words are independent sets on a folded path. The outermost
paired position consists of adjacent cycle vertices and is forced empty;
the remaining m positions form an ordinary path. Consequently

    f_n = F_(m+2).

This counts words, not rotation orbits. Cassini and addition formulas give

    L_(2m+1) - F_(m+2)^2 = 2 F_m F_(m-1) > 0.

For example, with a=F_m,b=F_(m+1), use
L_(2m+1)=5ab+(-1)^m and b^2-ab-a^2=(-1)^m. This proves the required
strict gap at every m>=2, without enumerating the words.

**Theorem.** If p=L_n is prime and n is odd with n>=7, then for **any
p-cycle T on W_n**, the group generated by T and reflection R is

    <T,R> = S_p  if n == 11 (mod 12),
             A_p otherwise.

Indeed p>=29, the fixed-point-gap proposition applies, and T is even.
The sign of R is (-1)^((p-F_(m+2))/2). A prime value here excludes
3|n, so n mod12 is 1,5,7,11. The Fibonacci and Lucas recurrences modulo4
give an odd reflection exactly in the last case. This determines the
entire group once A_p is known to be contained.

The result is independent of the chosen cyclic enumeration. In particular
it applies to arithmetic translation after the inherited golden bijection

    W_n -> Z[phi]/(phi^n-1) = F_p,       phi^2=phi+1.

The all-period bijection is proved and independently audited in
[Session74](../session74_eleven_mechanisms_20261027/session74-clocks/RESULT.md).
Our increasing-exponent word convention differs from its decreasing-exponent
convention by reversal of named positions, which is a bijection of W_n.
The inherited period-seven A29 certificate is therefore the first case of
this all-prime theorem, not evidence being extrapolated on its own.

| n | p=L_n | fixed words | generated group |
|---:|---:|---:|---|
|5|11|3|PSL(2,11) for the inherited arithmetic cycle|
|7|29|5|A29|
|11|199|13|S199|
|13|521|21|A521|
|17|3571|55|A3571|

The eleven-row is the previously checked exceptional action; it is not
asserted for every possible eleven-cycle. All rows with n>=7 are independent
of the cycle choice. These are symmetry classifications conditional on the
displayed Lucas number being prime, not a primality criterion.

## 3. Even indices: a related result with a different arithmetic carrier

If an even-index Lucas number L_n is prime, n is a power of two. For
n=2^k s with odd s>1 and k>=1, L_(2^k) is a proper divisor of L_n,
by factorization of x^s+y^s. Odd composite indices similarly have a proper
odd-index Lucas divisor. Thus prime values with n>=2 occur only at odd
prime indices or powers of two; the odd index3 gives the composite value4.

Let n=2^k, k>=3, and suppose p=L_n is prime. Use the edge-axis reflection
i -> 1-i. Both end pairs in its folded path are adjacent and forced empty;
the remaining m-2 positions, m=n/2, give f=F_m fixed words. Since m is even,

    p = L_(2m) = 5 F_m^2 + 2,

so 1<f<sqrt(p). For any p-cycle T,

    <T,R_edge> = A_p if k is odd, S_p if k is even.

Here p==3 mod4, and F_(2^(k-1)) is 3 mod4 for odd k and 1 mod4 for
even k. First examples: A47 at n=8 and S2207 at n=16.

**Carrier boundary:** at even n the golden quotient has L_n-2 elements and
merges three words at zero. It is not F_(L_n). This theorem uses an explicitly
chosen p-cycle on the words; it does not promote golden addition to that
cycle. The odd-index theorem has the natural arithmetic realization above.

## 4. What a robust metagraph must retain

The small object is the guarded word graph and its named operations. Its
unrestricted permutation closure is enormous from period7 onward. Retaining
a generator list is compact; listing every group element is not. An
equivalence relation stable under T and R has either one class or p singleton
classes, by primitivity. Hence any nonconstant exact **partition** quotient
stable under both operations must retain all p labels. This does not bound
linear, symbolic, circuit, or formula descriptions.

The preserved structure in this theorem is the permutation action. Arbitrary
T need not preserve toggles, unit distances, source weights, Hamilton paths,
or Collatz integer clocks. Those guards remain extra data. The result thus
explains why replacing a guarded arithmetic interface by its full symmetry
group can erase precisely the structure the metagraph was meant to study.
