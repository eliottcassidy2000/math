# Effective eventual translation for the C5 composition capacity

2026-10-31. **PROVED, scoped; classical effectiveness input explicitly cited.**
No unrestricted Ramsey bound or novelty priority is claimed.

## 1. The exact open question closed

Let D be the nonempty graphs generated by a singleton, binary join,
binary disjoint union, and substitution into C5 with all five children
nonempty. Define F(A,B) as the maximum order subject to omega<=A,
alpha<=B, for positive integers A,B. This is precisely the class and
capacity in `ramsey_neighbourhood_lifts_20261030/GRAPH_CAPACITY.md`.
In particular D is not silently replaced by its hereditary closure.

Retain its proved G2 inputs: s=w(A), where

    w(0)=0, w(1)=1,
    w(2h)=(5/2)w(h),
    w(2h+1)=(3/2)w(h)+w(h+1).

For L=2^ceil(log2 A), G2 proves F(A,B)<=s B and constructs an actual
D graph with omega=A, alpha=L, order=sL. Thus C=sL is an integer.

**Theorem 1 (all clique budgets).** For each positive A there is a
finite, computable N_A such that for every B>=N_A,

    F(A,B+L)=F(A,B)+C.                                      (1)

More precisely there are computable nonnegative rational penalties
c_0,...,c_(L-1) and an exact finite exceptional prefix such that

    F(A,B)=sB-c_(B mod L)   for every B>=N_A.                (2)

Writing s=p/q in lowest terms, the least positive eventual translation
period d satisfies q|d|L, so is a power of two. The least threshold for (1), the least period, its least
threshold, and the full exceptional prefix are algorithmically
computable. This does not assert that the period is always L or that
our general algorithm is efficient. No numerical thresholds beyond
the inherited first four rows are certified by the bounded checks.

### Proof of eventual translation

Write s=p/q in lowest terms. Disjoint union with the sharp L-attainer
is legal and gives

    F(A,B+L)>=F(A,B)+C.

Consequently E(B)=pB-qF(A,B) is a nonnegative integer and
E(B+L)<=E(B). On each of the L residue classes this sequence eventually
stabilizes: every strict decrease reduces it by at least q, because
pL=qC. There are finitely many residue classes, so a common threshold
exists. This proves (1) and (2), without any semilinearity assumption.

In fact E(B)=0 on positive multiples of L. For representatives
r=1,...,L, the number of strict improvements on that ray is at most
floor(E(r)/q). Since disjoint unions of r copies of K_A give F(A,r)>=Ar,
this is at most floor((s-A)r). This bounds the number of changes, NOT
their locations.

If a positive integer d is an eventual translation period, its increment
must be sd: divide the eventual arithmetic progression by B and use
F(A,B)/B -> s. Thus d is a period of the eventual deficit sequence.
On its L-periodic tail, this amounts to invariance of the residue word
under addition by d in Z/LZ. The stabilizer is a subgroup; its least
positive period divides L. Since sd is integral, q divides d. It can be recovered by testing the
finitely many divisors of L against the final residue word.

### A necessary warning about finite checks

Monotonicity does not certify that an observed plateau is final. For
any odd M>=3 the abstract superadditive integer function

    f_M(B)=2B-1 if B is odd and B<M; otherwise f_M(B)=2B

has slope bound 2B and a sharp attainer at L=2. Its odd residue can
remain flat in deficit for an arbitrarily long time and then improve.
Superadditivity follows directly: even+odd preserves or improves the
odd deficit, and odd+odd has zero deficit on the sum. This is not
claimed to be a D capacity. It shows why slope, sharpness, and a bound
on the number of drops do not themselves provide an effective cutoff.

## 2. Effective exact realizability, not scalar relaxation

We now supply the extra algorithmic bridge. Put

    S_A={(alpha(G),|G|): G in D and omega(G)<=A} subset N^2.

**Theorem 2.** S_A has an effectively constructible semilinear
representation for every A. Actual nonempty child realizability is
retained; denominator feasibility alone is never substituted for it.

A linear set is u+<v_1,...,v_t>_N; a semilinear set is a finite union
of such sets. We use the classical effective equivalence of semilinear
sets and Presburger-definable sets, and decidability of Presburger
sentences. These are Ginsburg--Spanier, *Semigroups, Presburger formulas,
and languages*, Pacific J. Math. 16(2), 1966, Theorems 1.2 and 1.3:
https://msp.org/pjm/1966/16-2/pjm-v16-n2-p09-p.pdf .
The source was inspected, including the explicit effective-conversion
statement on pp.286 and 288. The effectiveness here is classical;
the application to this exact recursive graph class is the deduction.

Induct on A. At A=1, S_1={(b,b):b>=1}. For A>1 construct Q_A as the
union of the following sets of connected-root attainable pairs:

* {(1,1)};
* for each a=1,...,A-1, all
  (max(b,c),n+m) with (b,n) in S_a and (c,m) in S_(A-a);
* for each positive 5-tuple a with a_i+a_(i+1)<=A (indices mod5), all
  (max_i(b_i+b_(i+2)), sum_i n_i), with (b_i,n_i) in S_(a_i).

Every a_i<=A-1, so the recursion is well-founded. Max is Presburger
expressible: z>=z_i for every i and z=z_i for some i. Finite unions,
products, linear images with these constraints, and projections are
effective semilinear operations. Therefore Q_A is effectively
semilinear.

Every member of Q_A is realized by a connected D graph with clique
number at most A. Conversely a D construction not rooted at union is
one of these types. For a join the actual child clique numbers add,
so one can choose a in the displayed range. For C5 substitution the
actual child clique numbers obey exactly the displayed adjacent-pair
constraints; its independence number is exactly the maximum over the
five nonadjacent child pairs. Flattening all top-level unions therefore
gives

    S_A = Q_A^* minus {(0,0)},                              (3)

where star means all finite vector sums, including the empty sum.
There is no circular call to S_A on the right before taking the star.

For completeness, star has an explicit finite semilinear construction.
If Q=union_(i=1)^m (u_i+<V_i>_N), then

    Q^* = {(0,0)} union
          union_(empty != I subset {1,...,m})
          (sum_(i in I)u_i + < {u_i:i in I} union union_(i in I)V_i >_N).
                                                               (4)

A sum using precisely the component indices I contains one base u_i
from each and arbitrary further bases and periods. Conversely place
all periods of a component into its first summand and use the extra
bases as additional summands. This proves both inclusions in (4),
including the activation guard that periods cannot occur before their
base component has been used. It finishes Theorem 2.

## 3. An actual terminating threshold algorithm

Let P_A(b,n) be the Presburger formula computed above for S_A. Define

    M_A(B,n) := (exists b<=B: P_A(b,n)) and
                not(exists b<=B, m>n: P_A(b,m)).

All quantifiers range over naturals. F(A,B) exists: the singleton is
feasible and the inherited bound n<=sB is finite. Hence for B>=1,
M_A(B,n) defines exactly n=F(A,B).

For N=1,2,... decide the Presburger sentence

    forall B,n,m:
      [B>=N and M_A(B,n) and M_A(B+L,m)] => m=n+C.          (5)

By Theorem 1 some N passes, so this search terminates. The first one
is the least threshold for period L. Compute F on B=1,...,N+L-1
(either by the formula or by the finite exact decomposition recurrence)
to obtain the exceptional prefix and final residue word. Test divisors
d of L on that word; the smallest invariant shift is the least period.
Its increment sd is automatically integral, since it is the difference
of integer capacities. A second search of (5) with d and sd gives
its least threshold. These are certified infinite-tail decisions, not
inferences from a long finite table.

Equivalent computation: the Presburger-definable set of failures of
(1) is finite, so quantifier elimination can compute it directly.
The empty failure set means N=1; otherwise N=1+max(failures).

No Presburger engine or full symbolic S_A compiler is implemented in
this packet. The algorithm is a mathematical effectiveness result.
`check_translation.py` supplies bounded, independent numerical and
literal graph controls, explicitly separated from the all-parameter
proof. The first four inherited exact rows give periods 1,2,4,4 with
threshold1. Each equals its sufficient L in these four controls.
