## Repairs and limitations

The phase-move replay initially compared Python tuples against their JSON
list encodings. Normalizing the comparison repaired the serialization
boundary without changing a numerical result. This mechanism was already
recorded in the preceding observer packet's audit; it is not a new
mathematical correction. The Fourier audit caught a stale relative link
and requested an explicit four-base-8-digit target bound; both were fixed.

An atomic simultaneous move requires accepted endpoints; it does not
assert accepted one-owner intermediate states. Three different costs are
kept separate: actual owner clocks, cyclic-cell phases, and hierarchical
frame coordinates. No one of them is silently charged as another.

The order-eight Fourier consumer preserves the **original** three-bit
phase. A paired rectangle can preserve an aggregate higher modulus while
changing each individual higher-level phase. The full modulus-256
character and full port ownership remain stronger tests. Repeated use of
one scarce actual path is not multiple available capacity.

The incoming all-size coset-width and native complexity boundaries remain
active. A small transfer representation does not imply a small coset list;
an exact Fourier expansion or residual tree need not be efficient. The
new return-tail theorem retains acceptance mass and source weight, while
native path-length and congestion obligations remain unproved.
