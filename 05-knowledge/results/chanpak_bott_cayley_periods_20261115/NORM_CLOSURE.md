# Algebraic closure is not physical closure

In the [previous norm ledger](../chanpak_collatz_norm_clock_20261115/NORM.md)
a physical profile is (x,y)=(q₂−H,H), with both coordinates odd.
Writing z=x+y sqrt(−2) makes the modified real part of a conjugate
product a literal cyclic join. But even **retaining** the missing
imaginary part does not give a closed family of physical profiles.

For profiles (a,b) and (c,d), the ordinary product has
imaginary coefficient ad+bc. The conjugate product has coefficient
bc−ad. Both coefficients are even, whereas the second coordinate
of every physical input profile is odd. For input orders at least
two, a≡−b and c≡−d modulo 4, so ad+bc≡2 modulo 4 and bc−ad≡0 modulo 4.
The norm identity can be restored algebraically while the lifted
state remains unavailable as a physical source. This is distinct
from forgetting which coordinate was lost.

## Two readers, complete dyadic support, almost no absolute support

Take a source of k ordinal triangles and s ordinal singleton pads.
Its h=3^k and q=12^k 2^s. The two literal cyclic joins with singleton
closing block and TT1 or TT2 as the other block give q+h and 3q−h.
For s≥L these become respectively 3^k and −3^k modulo 2^L.
For L≥3 these two cosets of the subgroup generated by 3 cover every
odd residue (the subgroup is the inverse image of 1,3 mod 8). Yet
both families combined give only O((log X)^2) values ≤X: the
parameters are nonnegative and each output is at least q, so
k=O(log X), s=O(log X). Here a finite reader bank removes the
dyadic obstruction exactly, without remotely solving placement.

For comparison, the unrestricted diagonal shape itself has
O(X log log X/log X) distinct outputs ≤X as X→∞. Indeed for
a source of order n, cutting two optional slots gives an injection
to a permutation and a cut, so q₂≤(n+1)n!. Thus diagonal count
D in [Y,2Y] forces n ≫ log Y/log log Y. The previous inequality
x≥n y confines its profile to x≤sqrt(2Y),
y ≪ sqrt(Y) log log Y/log Y. Count integer points in the box
and sum dyadic bands (the bands below sqrt(X) contribute at most
sqrt(X)). No sector theorem or Riemann hypothesis is needed for
this support bound.

These observations credit the concurrent background follow-up.
