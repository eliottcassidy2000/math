# A useful, qualified operator palette for {2,3,11}

Status: PROVED elementary corollary of the independently audited all-period
lift theorem. This does not explain why the odd-square doubling classification
selects the same prime set. The connection is an explicit choice of operations,
not an asserted equivalence between the two source predicates.

In the inherited period-five model R_5=F_11, phi=4 and the quadratic-residue
subgroup is H={1,3,4,5,9}. For each positive integer r, repeat a rooted
period-five source word r times and lift its label by J_(5,r). The label
projection back to R_5 is multiplication by r. Consequently:

- r=2: multiplication by2 is a bijection and exchanges H with -H. It reverses
  every Paley arc between distinct labels. It is not a power of the period-five
  time action, since powers of4 stay in H.
- r=3: multiplication by3 preserves every Paley arc and rotates each nonzero
  five-element orbit. Its order is5 and 3=4^4 modulo11. Thus its projected label
  action equals the fourth power of the one-step label action.
- r=11: the projected label action is zero, while all eleven lifted labels
  remain distinct. The lifted image is a square-zero ideal, not a field
  embedding or an eleven-cell quotient of identical source states.

## Mechanism and non-uniqueness

The complete trichotomy is quadratic-residue unit / nonresidue unit / zero
modulo11. Every other square, nonsquare, or multiple of11 supplies the same
respective TYPE. The particular arithmetic integers2,3,11 were inherited from
a different bracket problem. We leverage them here through a specified map
and documented losses, rather than claiming their repeated appearance proves
an unexplained universal structure.
