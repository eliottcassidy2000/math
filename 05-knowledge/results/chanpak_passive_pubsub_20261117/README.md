# Passive tournament interfaces and the arithmetic between strands

**Status: all-order structural theorems and explicit infinite families; no universal absolute selector.** The packet treats a subscriber as a literal surrounding tournament, not as a freely chosen linear functional. At every failure we change the hypothesis that caused it.

| Assumption to question | Result | What it changes |
|---|---|---|
| There is one canonical object a core must publish. | [Factorial observation theorem](observer/PROOF.md). Homogeneous readers detect exactly a bounded initial tuple of factorial-weighted covers. Modulo M, a finite tuple suffices for *every* exterior, at arbitrary depth. | A residue can flow unchanged through many constructions while the underlying core changes. Exact equality of too many topics is costly: same-order equal-H and equal-P₂ cores differ by multiples of 480 under every uniform observer, and 1440 is sharp at even order. |
| Keep H fixed and simply let the two-cover producer grow. | [Finite publisher cores](publisher/PROOF.md). At fixed H, the normalized unmarked slot polynomial has a finite menu and P₂ only has finitely many dyadic rays. | Further unmarked statistics alone do not make the menu richer with size. The same SCC analysis gives an exact eventual polynomial for the multiplicity of each fixed H. |
| To preserve a small repair, the input coordinates must agree separately. | [Physical reader steering](reader/PROOF.md). Two adjustable strong reader families and constant-gap examples cancel changes in H against changes in P₂, with arbitrarily large height. | Aim for a common *linear reading*. The ordinary two-run reader cannot have arbitrary coefficients, and repeated nesting necessarily raises the required number of runs. |
| If two repairs cancel individually, they will cancel separately inside a product. | [Power-sum synchronized changes](power_sum/PROOF.md). Ordinal products can cancel jointly by matching lower symmetric polynomials while their top products differ. | This yields exact gaps 512 and 4096 on all-height strands. The theorem applies at any number of modules, conditional only on finding a physical matching multiset. |
| If an observer cannot see a change, there is nothing left for the next observer. | [Nested marked switch](symmetry/PROOF.md). Two ports in one unchanged core, accessed by a fixed two-arc switch, hide the exact H difference on odd subscriber addresses and reveal +2 on even addresses. | Growing the same subscriber preserves a state that fresh root migration would erase. All odd residue phases for the lower end of the exact +2 pair are achievable; not all absolute values are claimed. |

The additive return-matching law and the factorial observation law together explain what is recursive: enclosing a block adds possible visits, while permutation symmetry of those visits removes them at a predictable rate modulo a fixed modulus. In exact arithmetic they do not disappear. This separates a stable residue channel from the still-uncontrolled absolute count.

The next constructive target is not a larger list of towers. Use one side of a two-run reader to prescribe the residue and an adaptive marked/correlated core to choose its absolute height *and* keep the +2 switch. The global obstacle is still exact target placement: existing modular cloaks can be enormous, fixed-core translated dyadic rails are sparse, and the proof does not say how to choose a sanctioned core for every target.
