## F1. Preserve the seven marked branch forms

Keep the earlier order

    L=(E,E+x,E-x,E+z,E-z,E+x+z,E-x-z),  s_i^2=d_i L_i,

and the same complementary simplex codes labelled
U=(1,2,4,5,6,3,7), D=(1,2,4,6,3,7,5).
Take the fixed-twist family

    d=(1,eta,1/2,1,1,1,delta),     eta,delta in Q^*.

On L_1=0 with E!=0, use E=1,x=-1,z=t. The squared root ratios are

    (1,0,1,1+t,1-t,t,delta(2-t)).                    (F1)

The value eta is retained as part of the surface, though its root vanishes
on this particular divisor. Choose the component s_2/s_0=+1. Its normalized
function field is

    Q(t)(sqrt(1+t),sqrt(1-t),sqrt(t),sqrt(delta(2-t))). (F2)

All four linear factors are independent squareclasses over Qbar(t):
each has a distinct simple finite zero. Thus the smooth projective curve
C_delta has geometric degree 16 over the t-line.

For generic t away from {-1,0,1,2,infinity}, the unit-character spaces of
the two marked quotients together are the annihilator of the sign vector

    h=(1,1,1,0)

on the four roots in (F2). This is a direct binary calculation with the
unchanged U,D labels. Therefore the relevant component of their normalized
ordinary product is Y_delta=C_delta/<h>, with function field generated by

    u^2=1-t^2,   v^2=t(1+t),   y^2=delta(2-t).        (F3)

No individual root is silently chosen. Over Q, rational points on both
quotients require exactly the three displayed square conditions in this
unit chart; the remaining lift is the common squareclass of 1+t,1-t,t.

## F2. Explicit genus-three chart and the residual lift

The first two equations of (F3) have the rational parametrization

    B(s)=1+4s^4,          A(s)=1-2s^2+4s^4,
    t=4s^2/B(s),  u=(1-4s^4)/B(s),
    v=2s(1+2s^2)/B(s).                              (F4)

On the overlap 1+t+u!=0 its inverse is s=v/(1+t+u), so this retains the
marked u,v values, not merely t. Direct substitution proves the identities.
Writing Y=yB(s), the third equation becomes

    Y_delta: Y^2=2delta A(s)B(s).                    (F5)

Both quartics are squarefree and coprime: their derivatives have no common
zero with the quartic, and B-A=2s^2. Thus (F5) is a smooth genus-three
hyperelliptic curve after projective normalization. The original lift adds

    Z^2=B(s),   W^2=2delta A(s),   Y=ZW.             (F6)

Indeed 1+t=(1+2s^2)^2/B(s); on the indicated unit chart its squareclass
is precisely B(s). Equations (F6) retain the chosen comparison torsor.
The genus-five cover C_delta -> Y_delta is unramified geometrically,
as shown by its inertia below. This does not make the torsor rationally
trivial at every rational point.

For delta=5/6, s=1 gives t=4/5, u=-3/5, v=6/5, y=1, Y=5.
The radicands in (F1) are

    (1,0,1,9/5,1/5,4/5,1).

Every unit character of each original quotient has a rational value:
use roots (3,1,2)*sqrt(5)/5 in slots 3,4,5 and the unchanged unit roots
in slots 0,2,6. Every surviving quotient character meets those three

## F3. Classify every rational full lift on this divisor

**Theorem.** In the family (F1), rational points on the original surface
above L_1=0 exist if and only if 2delta is a rational square. Every such
point has t=0. There are 16 projective rational points there, or 8 on
the chosen component s_2/s_0=+1. In particular delta=5/6 gives no rational
full lift anywhere on the divisor despite the quotient pair just exhibited.

**Proof.** Suppose first E!=0. The three values 1+t,1-t,t must be rational
squares. If t>0 and a^2=1+t,b^2=1-t,c^2=t, the rational right triangle
with legs a+b,a-b and hypotenuse 2 has area t=c^2 (take nonnegative
square roots, so the legs are positive). A nonzero rational right triangle
cannot have square area, by the descent below. Negative t is not a rational
square. Hence t=0. The remaining condition is s_6^2/s_0^2=2delta; all
other nonzero ratios have square 1. The four noncentral nonzero roots
have independent signs, giving 16 points once s_0=1. The chosen s_2=1
component has half of them. At E=0, x=0 and z!=0 would require both z
and -z to be rational squares in slots 3,4; impossible. QED.

For completeness, Fermat's classical square-area descent is short. Clear
denominators and divide out the common gcd to obtain a primitive integer
right triangle with square area and least possible hypotenuse. Write its
legs as 2mn,m^2-n^2, with m>n coprime and of opposite parity. Its area
mn(m+n)(m-n) is a square. The four factors are pairwise coprime, hence
m=a^2,n=b^2,m+n=c^2,m-n=d^2. Both c,d are odd, and
c^2-d^2=2b^2 implies b is even. The positive integers
(c+d)/2,(c-d)/2 form another right triangle of hypotenuse a and area
b^2/4, again a square. Its hypotenuse is smaller than m^2+n^2, a contradiction.
The parametrization of primitive Pythagorean triples is the classical
elementary input; the descent, not a finite search, proves the obstruction.

## F4. The Petersen graph indexes incompatible free gluing

The five branch ports t=(-1,1,0,2,infinity) in (F2) have inertia vectors

    v=(1,2,4,8,15) in F_2^4.                         (F7)

Their only nonempty zero-sum subset is all five. Thus the deck graph with
these five labelled increments is the folded five-cube (Clebsch graph).
The ten remaining nonzero vectors are uniquely v_a+v_b for unordered
port pairs {a,b}. They are the ten vertices of the Petersen graph:

    (v_a+v_b) ~ (v_c+v_d)
      iff {a,b} and {c,d} are disjoint
      iff (v_a+v_b)+(v_c+v_d) is a branch inertia.     (F8)

This is the exact binary circuit used by
[THM-4701, sections 3–4](../../../01-canon/theorems/THM-4701-kuratowski-graphs-the-petersen-graph-and-the-finite-unit-circle-graphs.md),
where the residue pentagon in F_16 supplies the same five vectors, and by
[THM-261](../../../01-canon/theorems/THM-261-petersen-root-orthogonality.md),
where K5 edges index positive A4 roots. The bridge here is the explicit
pair-sum map; Euclidean distance and individual branch locations are not
preserved by that map. K5 is the graph of branch-port pairs, not a subgraph
of the deck graph. The latter has at most two common neighbours for any
pair of distinct vertices: a difference has at most one unordered
representation as a sum of two distinct v_i. Hence it contains neither
K5 nor K3,3 as a subgraph. No minor or colouring conclusion follows.

Riemann–Hurwitz gives 2g(C_delta)-2=16(-2)+5*8=8, so g=5.
Each v_i fixes exactly eight points, while a pair-sum fixes none. The
quotients by these involutions therefore have genus 1 and 3 respectively.
For h=v_a+v_b, the two branch inertias become equal in the quotient;
that common involution fixes eight points on the genus-three quotient,
so it is hyperelliptic. Our marked h=7=8+15 corresponds exactly to
the pair {2,infinity}. These are classical Humbert quotient mechanisms;
[Hidalgo, arXiv:1705.09337v5, section 6](https://arxiv.org/html/1705.09337v5#S6)
lists the ten free involutions and their hyperelliptic quotients. The new
use here is their explicit realization in the fixed norm-seven boundary
and its arithmetic comparison torsor.

There is a stronger compatibility consequence. Two distinct pair-sum
involutions generate a free V4 precisely when their port pairs meet.
If the pairs meet, their sum is a third pair-sum; all three nonidentity
elements act freely and the common quotient has genus 2. If they are
disjoint, their sum is a branch inertia; the quotient is ramified and
has genus 1. Thus Petersen adjacency detects failure of free two-generator
gluing. There are ten free V4 subgroups, indexed by port triples.

Pairwise compatibility is still insufficient for three generators.
The three pair-sums on a three-leaf star are pairwise compatible, yet
their sum is the fifth branch inertia. A triangle of K5 instead has
sum zero and generates a free V4. This gives an exact meaning to the
star/triangle contrast: their pairwise overlap graphs agree, but their
binary dependency differs. The full labelled relation, not its graph
shadow, decides simultaneous freeness.

The arithmetic surface is not determined by (F7): changing delta preserves
this entire geometric graph while changing rational lifting. Twists and
branch coordinates therefore remain separate, indispensable data.

## F5. An elliptic quotient supplies the missing arithmetic coordinate

There is a useful repair which accepts controlled ramification in the
auxiliary quotient. Take the star subgroup generated by 3,5,9 in the
bit convention (F7). Its character annihilator is generated by 15, so
its quotient is the elliptic curve

    E_delta: R^2=delta*t*(1-t^2)*(2-t).                 (F9)

The four finite branch points -1,0,1,2 are distinct; infinity is unramified.
The star subgroup contains only the infinity inertia 15, explaining its
failure of free gluing without treating that failure as useless. The quartic
has the rational branch point t=0,R=0, so its smooth model is an elliptic
curve over Q for every nonzero rational delta.

The h=7 quotient Y_delta and this elliptic quotient together recover C_delta:

    C_delta = normalization(Y_delta x_(P^1_t) E_delta).  (F10)

This equality is for the marked connected function-field model. The character
space of Y is h-perpendicular of dimension three, whereas character 15 is
not perpendicular to h. Their compositum therefore has degree sixteen and
is exactly the full field (F2); the tensor product is generically a field.
Normalization remains necessary at branch intersections.

On the unit chart with t,u,v,y nonzero the reconstruction is literal:

    sqrt(t)=R/(u*y),
    sqrt(1+t)=v/sqrt(t),
    sqrt(1-t)=u/sqrt(1+t),
    sqrt(delta(2-t))=y.                              (F11)

Their squares and every original marked quotient value agree. Thus a rational
point of Y lifts rationally to C iff its **same t-port** has a rational R in
(F9). Choosing an unrelated rational point of the elliptic curve proves
nothing. For delta=5/6,t=4/5, the new square is 36/125: the elliptic port
detects exactly the missing sqrt(5). At t=0 and the other omitted ports use
the normalized local charts, not division in (F11).

Equivalently the residual cover Z^2=B(s) defines a nontrivial geometric
two-torsion divisor class on the smooth Y. If D_infinity is the degree-two
divisor of its two geometric points at infinity and P_B is the sum of the
four branch points above B(s)=0, then

    div(B)=2*(P_B-2D_infinity).                        (F12)

At a simple B-root the hyperelliptic parameter makes B vanish to order two;
at each infinity it has pole order four. The half-divisor has degree zero.
Its class is nontrivial, since otherwise B would be a square up to a geometric
constant and C->Y would split, contradicting the degree calculation.
Every valuation parity vanishes, yet the comparison torsor is not trivial.
This is the projective-curve version of the class-group information isolated
by THM-2685; over Q, constant squareclasses must also be retained.

The new test is consequently an exact genus-three/elliptic fibre product,
not a reduction of the full rational-point problem to an independent
elliptic-point search. It supplies a smaller auxiliary equation and a
specific two-torsion class while preserving t, the chosen quotient point,
the twist delta, and normalization. No planar Jacobian or plane-colouring
conclusion is implied by this change of carrier.

## F6. Retain the stronger five-factor decomposition and its twists

Use the earlier rational models

    E0: y^2=x^3-x,
    E1: y^2=(x+1)(x-1)(x-2),
    E2: y^2=(x-2)(x+2)(x-1),

and write E_i^[delta] for the twist obtained by multiplying the right side
by delta. The preceding packet proves their Q-identifications, not just
equal j-invariants. Changing only the radicand at 2 multiplies by delta
exactly those character products which include that radicand. Thus

    Jac(C_delta) ~_Q E0 x E0^[delta] x (E1^[delta])^2 x E2^[delta],
    Jac(Y_delta) ~_Q E0^[delta] x (E1^[delta])^2.         (F13)

Proof: the five differential characters are 7,11,13,14,15 in the order
of roots (1+t,1-t,t,delta(2-t)). They are the four triple products and
the full product. Their elliptic factors are respectively E0,
E1^[delta],E1^[delta],E0^[delta],E2^[delta]. Invariance under h=7 retains
exactly 11,13,14. The inherited independent-character differential argument
then gives both isogenies over Q, with the twists stated above.

For example the three elliptic maps from Y use the root products

    u*y,    v*y,    u*v*y/(1+t),

at t, 1-t, t-1 respectively in the stated E1,E1,E0 models. Their squared
values verify the original rational coefficient conventions directly.
These maps extend from their open formulas to the smooth projective curves.

The two-dimensional Prym of C_delta->Y_delta is Q-isogenous to
E0 x E2^[delta]. Its dimension must not be confused with the one extra
square root needed to generate the function field. In particular (F10)
does not assert that the elliptic auxiliary curve is the entire Prym, or
that an isogeny decomposition reconstructs individual rational points.
