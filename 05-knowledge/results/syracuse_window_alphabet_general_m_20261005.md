## 3. Theorem 1: condensation multiplicativity

**Theorem 1.** Let T be an m-tournament with strong components of sizes c_1, ..., c_r in the order of its condensation (dominating end first). In the generic alphabet (threshold patterns of valuation words, which is the exact alphabet for every m <= 12 on both sheets and m <= 17 on the plus sheet, section 7), T occurs as an m-window class if and only if every strong block T_i of size c_i >= 3 occurs as a strong c_i-window class. In particular the number of occurring classes with a given composition (c_1, ..., c_r) is the product over the blocks of the numbers R_(c_i) of occurring strong c_i-window classes, and the m-window alphabet is generated by the strong k-window alphabets, k <= m, under ordinal sum in every arrangement. For larger m the necessity half holds for the actual windows as well; the sufficiency half is OPEN at pinched windows, whose strong blocks may have no word-level realiser.

*Proof.* The window tournament contains the Hamiltonian path 0 -> 1 -> ... -> m-1 (the time arcs). The condensation of a tournament is transitive, so a Hamiltonian path visits the strong components in condensation order and never returns: each strong component is an interval [p, q] of the time order, and the components are ordered by position. The induced tournament on [p, q] is the window tournament of the sub-window (S^p x, ..., S^q x), whose chord bits are the generic bits of the sub-word (a_(p+1), ..., a_q); so every strong block of an occurring class is an occurring strong window class of its size (necessity). Conversely, given occurring strong words w_1, ..., w_r for blocks of sizes c_1, ..., c_r (empty words for singletons), form the word w_1, J, w_2, J, ..., J, w_r with every junction letter J >= t_(m-1). A chord inside a block sees only the block's letters, so the blocks keep their patterns; a chord across a junction has sum at least J >= t_(m-1) >= t_(its length), so it is a descent, i.e. directed from the earlier block to the later one. Hence the window tournament is the ordinal sum of the blocks in the given order with all cross arcs forward, which is the given class; the junction letter has positive density, so the class occurs with positive density. QED (checked exhaustively for all classes with m <= 8 in the script; the theory lens independently counted the non-strong occurring classes 3, 6, 13, 45, 111 for m = 4..8 as sums over compositions of products of R_k = 1, 1, 1, 2, 12, 19, 83 for k = 1, 3, ..., 8).

Consequences. (i) The occurring counts by L are products: at m = 8, L = 5 gives 12 = (4 + 2) arrangements x 2 strong 5-classes, L = 6 gives 36 = 3 x 12, L = 7 gives 38 = 2 x 19; at m = 7, L = 6 gives 24 = 2 x 12, L = 5 gives 6 = 3 x 2. (ii) Since the strong 3- and 4-window classes are the unique strong classes of those sizes, every class with all strong blocks of size at most 4 occurs, for every m. (iii) A class is missing iff some strong block of size at least 5 is a missing strong class, so the minimal L of a missing class is 5 for every m >= 5 (the S12 guess "missing implies L >= m-1" is refuted at m = 7: twelve missing classes have L = 5). (iv) Densities are not multiplicative (a junction need not be a cap letter), but the support is.

## 4. Theorem 2: the all-ascent window is Moon's tournament T'_m, Moon-extremal at every length, the window of the Mersenne seeds

The word 1^(m-1) (every valuation 1) has every window sum A(i,j) = j - i < t_(j-i), so every chord is backward: the window tournament is the Hamiltonian path 0 -> 1 -> ... -> m-1 together with all chords j -> i, j >= i+2, i.e. TT_m^op with its Hamiltonian path reversed, which is Moon's tournament T'_m (p_i -> p_j iff i = j-1 or i >= j+2; Moon 1966; Camion/Busch note, section 2). The Mersenne seed x = 2^p - 1 has valuation word (1, ..., 1, a_p) with p-1 ones and a_p = 1 + v_2(3^p - 1) (= 2 for odd p, 3 + v_2(p) for even p; S^j(2^p - 1) = 3^j 2^(p-j) - 1 for j < p), so its m-window is the all-ascent window for every m <= p, and for m = p+1 iff p is odd (a chord ending in the letter 2 has A = k+1 < t_k), which covers every Mersenne prime exponent p >= 3; x = 15 (word (1,1,1,5,4)) shows the even case fails at m = p+1. The all-ascent word is carried by the whole residue class x = 2^(m-1) t - 1, not only by Mersenne numbers; more generally the all-ascent words are those with every A(i,j) <= floor((j-i) log_2 3), whose exact densities are 5/16, 13/64, 17/128, 11/128, 57/1024, 37/1024, 3/128 for m = 4..10 (theory lens; the m = 4 value is the S12 set A_3 <= 4).

**Theorem 2 (KNOWN in (a), Moon 1966; independent proof here; (b)-(d) new or finite).** (a) The simple cycles of the all-ascent tournament T'_m are exactly the intervals i -> i+1 -> ... -> j -> i with j >= i+2; hence it has exactly m-k+1 cycles of each length k = 3, ..., m, meeting Moon's lower bound c_k >= m-k+1 (Moon 1966) at every length simultaneously, and in particular a unique Hamiltonian cycle. (b) Its score sequence is (1, 1, 2, 3, ..., m-3, m-2, m-2) and it is self-converse; its Hamiltonian path counts are 5, 9, 17, 31, 57 for m = 4..8 (FINITE-EXACT), following the tribonacci recursion h(m) = h(m-1) + h(m-2) + h(m-3) through m = 8 (the recursion for all m is CONJECTURE), and coinciding with the nearly transitive count 2^(m-2) + 1 only for m <= 6. (c) For m >= 5 it is not the nearly transitive tournament (TT_m with the arc 0 -> m-1 reversed), which has the same score sequence but C(m-2, k-2) cycles of length k (3, 6, 10 four-cycles at m = 5, 6, 7 against 2, 3, 4) and 2^(m-2) + 1 Hamiltonian paths; the nearly transitive tournament never occurs as a window class for m = 5..8. (d) T'_m is not characterised by its 3-cycle count alone: the strong m-classes with the minimum c_3 = m-2 number 1, 2, 4, 8 for m = 4..7, all with a unique Hamiltonian cycle (theory lens, FINITE-EXACT).

*Proof of (a), (b), (c).* (a) Let C be a simple cycle and i its least vertex. The only out-arc of i that does not go to a smaller vertex is the path arc i -> i+1, so C continues i -> i+1; from i+1 the out-arcs are i+2 and chords to vertices at most i-1, which are below the minimum, so C continues to i+2, and so on until some vertex j leaves by a chord j -> i' with i' < j-1. If i' > i then C must continue from i' upward through i'+1, ..., which are already on C: impossible for a simple cycle. So i' = i and C is the interval [i, j]. There are m-k+1 intervals of length k. (b) Vertex i beats i+1 (if i < m-1) and every vertex at most i-2: score [i <= m-2] + max(0, i-1); the map i -> m-1-i carries the tournament onto its converse. (c) The cycle counts are computed in the script. QED.

So the most frequent window shape for m = 5..8 (and, by census, at m = 9, 10) is Moon's T'_m, the strong tournament with the fewest cycles of every length (whether it is the unique strong class meeting Moon's bound at every length, and whether it stays the densest class for all m, are OPEN here), and it is the shape every Mersenne seed shows for m <= p. S11 already logged the trap that the nearly transitive tournament is Moon-extremal only at n = 4. In the S11 chain language, the window of maximal ascent is maximally cyclic (L = m) but minimally so (one cycle per interval): cycle content counts non-descents, and the all-ascent window has every chord a non-descent while its cycles are forced to be the intervals.

## 5. Theorems 3 and 4 and the fine structure: transitive windows, the near-identity score law, score separation, the exotic twins, the pattern counts

**Theorem 3 (transitive windows).** The m-window is transitive iff every two consecutive valuations sum to at least 4, i.e. iff every 2-step sub-window descends. *Proof.* The 2-step chords are chords, so the condition is necessary. If a_i + a_(i+1) >= 4 for all i then A(i,j) >= 2(j-i) for even j-i and >= 2(j-i) - 1 for odd j-i, which is at least t_(j-i) for every j-i >= 2 (equality at j-i = 2 and 3: 4 = t_2, 5 = t_3; for k >= 5, k(2 - log_2 3) >= 2). QED. The density of TT_m is therefore the probability that the capped letter sequence (masses 1/2, 1/4, 1/8, 1/8 for 1, 2, 3, >=4) has all consecutive sums at least 4: a 4-state transfer matrix with characteristic polynomial x(32x^3 - 16x^2 - 4x + 1)/32, so d(TT_m) ~ C rho^m with rho = 0.62030 the largest root of 32x^3 - 16x^2 - 4x + 1; exact values 11/32, 13/64, 33/256, 81/1024, 101/2048, 125/4096 for m = 4..9 (the script asserts the transfer-matrix value against the dynamic program; the theory lens continued to 621/32768, 385/32768, 1911/262144 for m = 10..12). Theorem 3 is exact for the actual windows at every m on both sheets, not only in the generic alphabet (proof auditor): if all consecutive sums are at least 4, every even-length chord is a chain of 2-step descents and every odd-length chord of length 2d+1 >= 5 satisfies x_(i+2d+1) < x_(i+3) < x_i, so exactness reduces to the lags 2 and 3, which the S12 Lemma settles on the plus sheet and the minus-sheet short-lag genericity (lag < 12) settles there; conversely a 2-step window with a_i + a_(i+1) <= 3 is a growth window, which always grows on the plus sheet (the sign law) and fails to grow on the minus sheet only at a lag-2 crossing, of which there is none. A useful companion (PROVED): the thresholds are superadditive, t_a + t_b >= t_(a+b), since floor(a alpha) + floor(b alpha) >= floor((a+b) alpha) - 1 for alpha = log_2 3; so two consecutive descents compose to a descent, and a descent followed by a letter >= 2 (= t_1) is a descent.

**Theorem 4 (score = rank + eps; the near-identity law; no regular window).** For a generic window (chords equal to value comparisons, which every word realises through a large representative) let rank(i) = #{k : x_k < x_i}. Then

    score(i) = rank(i) + eps(i),   eps(i) = [i <= m-2] - [x_(i-1) < x_i] - [x_(i+1) < x_i]  in {-1, 0, +1}

(the second indicator absent at i = 0, the third at i = m-1): eps = +1 at an interior valley and at an ascending start, -1 at an interior peak and at an ascending end, 0 otherwise. Consequently (i) no score value is taken by more than three vertices; (ii) the vertex carrying the window minimum has score at most 1 and the vertex carrying the maximum has score at least m-2; (iii) the sorted scores satisfy |s_(k) - k| <= 1 for every k ("near-identity"); (iv) the regular tournament never occurs for odd m >= 5, and the near-regular tournaments (scores (m/2 - 1)^(m/2) (m/2)^(m/2)) never occur for even m >= 6. *Proof.* Vertex i beats every non-adjacent j iff x_i > x_j (gauge DESC), beats i+1 and loses to i-1 by the time arcs; so score(i) counts the smaller non-adjacent values plus the time arc out of i, which is rank(i) minus the smaller adjacent values plus [i <= m-2]. Ranks are distinct, so (i)-(iii) follow; for (iv), s_(0) = (m-1)/2 resp. m/2 - 1 must be at most 1. QED (the lemma was found by the session's theory lens; the identity and the near-identity property were re-verified here on every capped word with m <= 7: differences in {-1, 0, +1} only, 569,722 of each sign and 6,069,669 zeros).

Near-identity Landau sequences on m vertices are in bijection with the peakless Motzkin paths of length m (steps s_(k) - k in {-1, 0, 1}, nonnegative partial sums by Landau, total zero, no up-step immediately followed by a down-step), counted by A004148: 4, 8, 17, 37, 82, 185, 423 for m = 4..10. The occurring score sequences are exactly the near-identity Landau sequences for m = 4..7 (4, 8, 17, 37 of the 4, 9, 22, 59 Landau sequences) and 81 of the 82 at m = 8, the unique unrealised near-identity sequence being (1,2,2,2,5,5,5,6) (theory lens, FINITE-EXACT; the counts 4, 8, 17, 37, 81 agree with the table's last column). So the alphabet is a section of the score map over the near-identity sequences through m = 7: the degree-one field of an occurring window is a peakless Motzkin path.

**Score separation.** At m = 5 the eight occurring classes have eight distinct score sequences, all nine score sequences except the regular one. This fails from m = 6 on by pigeonhole (25 occurring classes over the 17 near-identity sequences; 64 over 37; 194 over 81): at m = 6 the colliding sequences are (1,1,2,3,4,4) with three occurring classes (T'_6 of density 73/512 and the converse pair of density 7/256 each), (1,1,3,3,3,4) and (1,2,2,2,4,4) with two, and (1,2,2,3,3,4) with five of its twelve classes. By S11 Theorem 5 identifiability is block-local, so every collision lives inside a strong block of size at least 5; at m = 5 the two occurring strong classes lie in different fibres, which is why m = 5 still separates.

**The exotic twins (same score sequence, different class).** The first score fibres with more than one class are at n = 5: (1,1,2,3,3) with two classes and (1,2,2,2,3) with three (S11). The alphabet realises exactly one class in each: in (1,1,2,3,3) Moon's T'_5 (c_4 = 2) and never the nearly transitive tournament (c_4 = 3); in (1,2,2,2,3) the class with (c_4, c_5, H) = (3, 2, 13) and never the two with (4, 1, 11) and (3, 3, 15). By Ryser's theorem any two tournaments with the same score sequence are connected by reversals of 3-cycles (the Brualdi-Li interchange graph, regular because c_3 is a function of the scores); here the realised T'_5 becomes the missing nearly transitive one by reversing its middle interval 3-cycle (1, 2, 3) in the time labelling (the 3-cycle (0, 3, 4) in the S11 representative's labelling), and the realised (1,2,2,2,3)-class reaches one missing twin by reversing either of two of its four 3-cycles (the other two return the class to itself) while the third twin is at interchange distance exactly 2.
