# Odd-function lattices: complete arithmetic signed-cycle classification

**PROVED elementary synthesis with exact finite controls.** No literature-priority
claim, general Ramsey bound or major-conjecture resolution is asserted. The new
interface is a complete integral operator classification, not an identification of
all phenomena described by the word “odd.”

## 1. Inheritance and the precise object

[LEM-004, tournaments are odd functions](../../../../01-canon/theorems/LEM-004-tournaments-are-odd-functions.md)
uses antisymmetric signs on residue **differences** to encode circulant tournaments.
[Odd-order arithmetic, §2–3](../../odd_prime_function_cycle_20261018d.md) already
separates translation alternation from reflection oddness and retains nonunit
valuation strata. [THM-4792](../../../../01-canon/theorems/THM-4792-two-sheets-mod-odd-m-merged-centre-reflections-unit-shift-and-alternation-classification.md)
already gives the common-positive-order-valuation criterion for -1 to be a power
of 2, explicitly crediting the classical result of [Moree (1997)](https://www.impan.pl/en/publishing-house/journals-and-series/acta-arithmetica/all/80/3/109568/on-the-divisors-of-a-k-b-k).
We retain that attribution rather than claim the CRT criterion as new. The present
specialization allows any unit multiplier, includes the full odd-function lattice,
and classifies both inhomogeneous operators by their Smith factors.

Fix odd N>=3 and a unit u modulo N. Let

    L_N^- = {f:Z/N -> Z : f(-x)=-f(x)}.

Then f(0)=0, and the values on representatives 1,...,(N-1)/2 are a free integral
basis of coordinates. Its rank is R=(N-1)/2. Define

    (P_u f)(x)=f(ux),       A_-=I-P_u,       A_+=I+P_u.

Multiplication commutes with negation, so P_u preserves this lattice. It is a
**signed permutation** in these coordinates. A change of sign of a representative
is an integral unimodular basis change. A Fourier transform is not such a change.

## 2. Complete cycle decomposition, with all divisor strata

For each divisor d>1 of N, let o_d=ord_d(u), and let e_d assert that
-1 belongs to the subgroup generated by u modulo d. Define

    if e_d:    h_d=o_d/2, epsilon_d=-1, c_d=phi(d)/o_d;
    otherwise: h_d=o_d,   epsilon_d=+1, c_d=phi(d)/(2o_d).

**Theorem 1.** The signed permutation P_u on L_N^- has exactly c_d cycles of
length h_d and sign product epsilon_d from the residues of additive order d.
In particular sum_(d|N,d>1) c_d h_d = R.

Proof. A residue of additive order d has the form (N/d)a with a a unit modulo d.
Its ordinary multiplication orbit closes when u^j=1 modulo d and has length o_d.
After quotienting by x~-x, closure occurs at the first j with u^j=1 or -1. If -1
is attained, its first occurrence is o_d/2, since the cyclic subgroup has exactly
one element of order two. Returning to the negative representative contributes
sign -1. Otherwise closure first occurs at o_d with sign +1. There are phi(d)/2
negation pairs in this stratum, yielding the stated cycle count. Different additive
orders are disjoint and exhaustive. This proof does not discard nonunit residues.

## 3. Smith normal forms, exact kernels and integral obstructions

For one signed cycle of length h and sign epsilon, the equations for I-P reduce
by integral row/column operations to h-1 unit pivots and the final scalar 1-epsilon.
For I+P replace P by -P, whose cycle sign is (-1)^h epsilon. Thus the last scalar
is 1-(-1)^h epsilon. This is the signed-cycle lemma, also proved in the companion
cycle-operator paper; elementary recurrence elimination proves it directly.

For each choice of sign define

    delta_-(d)=epsilon_d,
    delta_+(d)=(-1)^h_d epsilon_d,
    z_± = sum_(delta_±(d)=+1) c_d,
    t_± = sum_(delta_±(d)=-1) c_d,
    C = sum_d c_d = z_±+t_±.

**Theorem 2 (complete integral classification).** The Smith form of A_± is

    diag( 1 repeated R-C times, 2 repeated t_± times,
          0 repeated z_± times ).

Consequently its integral kernel is free of rank z_±, and its integral cokernel is

    Z^(z_±) direct-sum (Z/2)^(t_±).

If z_±=0, its determinant has absolute value 2^C and its inverse has entries in
(1/2)Z. There is no odd-prime integral obstruction. Reduction modulo an odd q
removes every 2-obstruction, but leaves any free kernel. For every modulus q>=2,
on the reduction of the normalized lattice,

    #ker(A_± mod q)=q^(z_±) gcd(q,2)^(t_±).

The same count holds for each nonempty solution fiber of A_± f=b. To test whether
a fiber is empty, retain cycle signs, not only these dimensions: write its equation
as (I-Q)x=b with (Qx)_i=s_i x_(i+1), indices cyclic, eta=product_i s_i. Then

    (1-eta)x_0 = b_0+s_0 b_1+s_0s_1 b_2+... .

For eta=+1 that signed sum must vanish over Z (or modulo q); for eta=-1 it must
be even over Z (or divisible by gcd(q,2) modulo q). The remaining coordinates
follow recursively. For A_+ use s_i equal to minus the P_u cycle-edge sign.
This gives a constructive inhomogeneous solver and its exact obstruction.

**Even coefficient modulus warning.** L_N^- tensor Z/q means f(0)=0. The set of
*all* reflection-odd functions into Z/q also permits f(0) in the 2-torsion subgroup.
For even q this is an additional independent two-element coordinate. Both A_-
and A_+ kill that coordinate, so a full odd target must have b(0)=0 and every
solvable fiber gains gcd(q,2) choices. In characteristic two, signs collapse;
Boolean odd truth-table weight is a different notion, not this lattice. More
precisely, on the odd residue set an F_2-valued reflection-invariant function has
truth-weight parity f(0), since every nonzero pair contributes twice. Thus the
normalized lattice reductions have even weight. On a Boolean cube with its
additive-group negation, -x=x; over characteristic two the reflection-odd condition
is vacuous, while over torsion-free coefficients it forces zero. The underlying
involution and coefficient ring must both be specified.

For a concrete inhomogeneous obstruction, N=5,u=2 gives

    I+P = [[1,1],[-1,1]].

The target (1,0) has the unique rational solution (1/2,1/2), and no integral
solution. Nonsingularity over Q does not remove the one parity condition.

## 4. A four-way classification by prime order valuations

For each prime p dividing N put t_p=v_2(ord_p(u)). Prime powers do not change this
valuation: the order modulo p^a is ord_p(u) times a power of the odd prime p.
For an odd divisor d, -1 belongs to <u> modulo d exactly when the t_p over p|d
are all equal and positive. Indeed the condition at p^a is exponent congruence
j=o_(p^a)/2 modulo o_(p^a); CRT compatibility of these exponent congruences is
exactly equality of their positive 2-adic valuations. Equivalently use the unique
involution modulo each odd prime power and their order lcm. This is the inherited
classical compatibility mechanism, reproduced here only to establish the operator
criterion's hypotheses.

**Theorem 3.** Over Q (equivalently, with nonzero determinant over Z):

| prime valuation profile | I-P_u | I+P_u |
|---|---|---|
| all t_p=0 | singular | nonsingular |
| all t_p=1 | nonsingular | singular |
| all t_p equal to one t>=2 | nonsingular | nonsingular |
| t_p not all equal | singular | singular |

Proof. I-P is nonsingular precisely when every signed cycle is negative. This is
exactly -1 in <u> modulo N, which is the common-positive profile. For I+P a positive
cycle is allowed only when its length is odd; a negative cycle is allowed only
when its length is even. A common zero profile gives positive odd cycles on every
divisor. A common positive t gives negative cycles of length o_d/2, which are even
exactly when t>=2. If profiles differ, choose a divisor consisting of two primes
with different profiles. Its -1 condition fails, its order is even, and it gives
a positive even signed cycle, making I+P singular. I-P is already singular on
that same divisor. A prime with t_p=1 alone also forces I+P singular. These cases
are exhaustive.

For u=2, prior residue-class facts p=3,5,7 modulo8 select t=1,2,0 respectively;
primes p=1 modulo8 have varying t. For example 17 and73 both equal1 modulo8 but
have t=3 and0. Thus the four odd residue classes alone do not classify these
operators: the order valuation is the retained arithmetic datum.

## 5. Exact circulant-tournament consumer, and a hostile kernel

A sign function sigma on nonzero residues with sigma(-x)=-sigma(x) orients the
complete graph by differences. For alpha in {+1,-1}, impose

    sigma(ux)=alpha sigma(x).

On a signed cycle this has a full sign solution if and only if alpha^h=epsilon.
When it does, there are exactly two choices for that cycle.

**Corollary.** Multiplier u preserves some circulant tournament orientation if
and only if all t_p=0 (equivalently ord_N(u) is odd). It reverses some such
orientation if and only if all t_p=1. In each admissible case the exact number
of orientations on the fixed labelled residue set is 2^C. These are not counts
up to graph isomorphism. Translation symmetries remain automatic.

Proof. The preserving condition requires every epsilon positive. A prime with
even order supplies a negative stratum, whereas odd order on every prime makes
all strata positive. Reversal requires every (-1)^h epsilon=+1: a prime t=0
or t>=2 prevents it, while a common t=1 satisfies every divisor stratum.
This is stronger than merely finding a nonzero kernel vector: every coordinate
must be a unit sign. The orientation statement does not give a Ramsey bound.

**Hostile N=15,u=2.** The divisor strata d=3,5,15 contribute signed cycles
(h,epsilon)=(1,-1),(2,-1),(4,+1). Thus

    SNF(I-P)=diag(1,1,1,1,2,2,0),
    SNF(I+P)=diag(1,1,1,1,2,0,0).

Both operators have nonzero rational kernels, but neither kernel contains a
full sign vector. A preserving vector must vanish on the negative strata; a
reversing vector must vanish on the d=5 stratum. Forgetting those zero coordinates
would falsely certify a tournament symmetry. This is the exact lost-data warning.

## 6. Fourier interpretation without erasing the lattice

For real reflection-odd f, its discrete Fourier transform satisfies
hat f(-k)=-hat f(k), while reality gives hat f(-k)=conjugate(hat f(k)). Thus its
nonzero Fourier coefficients are purely imaginary, and the sine functions
sin(2*pi*k*x/N), 1<=k<=R, form a real basis. Multiplication acts by

    hat(P_u f)(k)=hat f(k*u^(-1)).

The dual frequency cycles have the same lengths and signs. A signed length-h
cycle has eigenvalues satisfying lambda^h=epsilon, so Theorem 3 also classifies
resonance at +1 and -1 in this sine sector. This is an exact arithmetic/odd-function/
cycle bridge. The complex Fourier change of basis is not unimodular over Z;
only the original signed residue-pair coordinates retain the Smith factors and
parity conditions. A spectral nonzero mode is not an integral or full-sign lift.

## 7. Verification

`check_odd_lattice.py` constructs the literal signed matrices independently of
the divisor formulas. It checks cycle data, valuation criteria, Smith factors by
integer unit-pivot row/column elimination, modular kernels by exhaustive vectors,
and full sign orientations by exhaustive sign words. Both normal and optimized
Python must give the same JSON receipt. All-size claims follow from the proofs,
not from extrapolating those finite universes. The inherited Moree criterion is
credited; no major open question or unrestricted circuit bound is solved here.
