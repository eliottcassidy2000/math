# Signed cycles: exact integer and modular obstructions

**PROVED elementary classification; no literature-priority claim.** This extends
[odd-order arithmetic, §2 and §4](../../odd_prime_function_cycle_20261018d.md),
which already classified homogeneous alternating functions and unsigned graph
kernels. The extension here is the full inhomogeneous integer problem, its Smith
normal form/cokernel, explicit reconstruction, and the signed-graph generalization.
It is not a new Ramsey bound, circuit lower bound, or Collatz convergence proof.

## 1. One signed cycle, with an explicit convention

Let the cycle have length L>=1 and signs q_0,...,q_(L-1) in {+1,-1}. Define

    (Qx)_i=q_i x_(i+1 mod L),    eta=product_i q_i.

For A=I-Q, consider Ax=b over the integers or modulo M>=2. Put

    lambda_0=1, lambda_j=product_(0<=h<j) q_h,
    B(b)=sum_(j=0..L-1) lambda_j b_j.

Successively substituting x_i=b_i+q_i x_(i+1) around the cycle gives exactly

    (1-eta)x_0=B(b).                                      (1)

Once x_0 is chosen, the other coordinates follow uniquely from the first L-1
edge equations. No solution is lost in this reduction.

**Smith theorem.** Over Z, A has Smith diagonal

    1,...,1,0   if eta=+1;
    1,...,1,2   if eta=-1.

There are L-1 unit entries. To prove this without a numerical inference, use the
first L-1 equations to eliminate x_1,...,x_(L-1), each with a unit coefficient
q_i. These are unimodular integer row/column operations. The final remaining
equation is (1), up to an overall sign. Thus the last invariant factor is
|1-eta|. This also handles L=1, where the matrix is simply [1-q_0].

Consequences over Z:

- eta=+1: a solution exists iff B(b)=0. The solution set is one integer translate
  of a primitive rank-one kernel, generated by a vector with coordinates ±1.
- eta=-1: a solution exists iff B(b) is even, equivalently sum_i b_i is even.
  It is then unique. The cokernel is Z/2; it is not a hidden odd-prime obstruction.

Consequences modulo M:

- Put g=gcd(M,1-eta), with gcd(M,0)=M. Solvability is exactly B(b)=0 modulo g.
- Every nonempty solution fiber has exactly g points; choose any of the g roots
  of (1) modulo M and reconstruct the remaining coordinates.
- The kernel and cokernel of A over Z/M both have cardinality g. Thus odd M
  removes the eta=-1 obstruction entirely, but not the eta=+1 free-mode constraint.

Since Q^L=eta I, the nonsingular case has the explicit rational inverse

    (I-Q)^(-1) = (I+Q+...+Q^(L-1))/2      when eta=-1.       (2)

Formula (2) is integral exactly for the right-hand sides described above. It is
also the inverse modulo every odd M, with 1/2 interpreted as the inverse of 2.
The denominator cannot be dismissed over Z or an even modulus.

For I+P, apply this theorem to Q=-P. A P-cycle of length L and sign product eps
therefore has eta=(-1)^L eps. In particular an ordinary unsigned odd cycle has
I+P invertible over Q, determinant 2, and one parity obstruction over Z. An even
cycle instead has a free kernel and an alternating-sum compatibility condition.
The determinant for I-P is 1-eps; for I+P it is 1-(-1)^L eps.

## 2. Arbitrary signed permutations

Decompose P into disjoint cycles. For each operator I±P let b be the number of
cycles with eta=+1 and o the number with eta=-1 under the convention above.
Writing N for the total number of coordinates, its full Smith normal form has

    N-b-o entries 1, o entries 2, b entries 0.

Its integer kernel is Z^b and integer cokernel is Z^b plus (Z/2)^o. Over Z/M,
the kernel and cokernel cardinalities are

    M^b gcd(M,2)^o.

An inhomogeneous equation is solvable exactly when its individual cycle
compatibility conditions hold; every soluble fiber has this same size. If b=0,
the absolute determinant is 2^o and the inverse is the direct sum of (2).
This is a complete classification, including fixed points and mixed cycle types.

## 3. Arithmetic specialization without losing residue strata

Let N>=1 and u be a unit modulo N. On all integer-valued functions on Z/N set
P_u f(x)=f(ux). Residues of additive order d, for each d|N, number phi(d). Each
has a multiplication orbit of length h_d=ord_d(u), with h_1=1. Consequently
there are exactly phi(d)/h_d cycles on this stratum, and the total is

    C(N,u)=sum_(d|N) phi(d)/ord_d(u).                       (3)

Proof: write a residue of additive order d as x=(N/d)a with gcd(a,d)=1.
u^j x=x modulo N is equivalent to u^j=1 modulo d. This is independent of a.
Thus no unit-only calculation has silently discarded the nonunit residues.

For I+P_u, the preceding theorem gives a factor 2 for every odd-length orbit,
and a zero invariant factor for every even-length orbit. In particular

    I+P_u is nonsingular over Q iff ord_N(u) is odd,
    det(I+P_u)=2^C(N,u) in that case.                       (4)

For N=1 the convention makes (4) valid. For N>1 necessity follows from the
orbit of the residue 1; sufficiency follows because ord_d(u) divides ord_N(u).
For I-P_u every orbit contributes a free mode, including the fixed residue 0.
So I-P_u is never nonsingular on the full function space.

The distinct reflection-odd lattice f(-x)=-f(x) is treated in the companion
odd-function specialization. It changes the cycles into signed quotient cycles;
one must not apply (4) unchanged to that smaller lattice.

## 4. Relation to the different meanings of oddness

- Odd cycle length: determines whether alternating signs return with a minus.
- Odd target modulus: makes 2 invertible, removing precisely the factor-two
  lattice obstruction, not all cycle compatibility equations.
- Odd integer N: makes reflection on Z/N have only the fixed point 0. It does
  not force every multiplication orbit to have odd length.
- Odd function under reflection: is a negative eigenspace of a different
  permutation, not synonymous with translation alternation or odd truth-table weight.

A minimal hostile is N=3,u=2: N is odd but the nonzero multiplication orbit has
length two, so I+P_u is singular on the full space. Another is the triangle
with integer right-hand side (1,0,0): its unique rational alternating solution
has half-integer coordinates, even though every odd-modulus version is soluble.

The exact common object is a signed transport operator and its integral
cokernel. The word 'odd' alone provides no valid transfer between these settings.

## 5. Reproduction

Run `python3 check_signed_integral.py` and `python3 -O check_signed_integral.py`.
Both executions match `receipt.json`. The checker uses literal matrix minors
and complete modular fibers, rather than calling a Smith-form library or using
producer symbolic eliminations as its oracle. Its universes are:

- all 510 signed cycles of lengths 1 through 8 for determinants/inverse identities;
- determinantal-divisor Smith checks through length 5;
- all 17,684 right-hand sides for cycle lengths at most 4 and moduli 2 through 5;
- every signed simple graph on at most four vertices, plus loop/parallel controls,
  totaling 766 matrices and 134,000 modular right-hand sides;
- all 1,966 unit actions on cyclic groups of orders 1 through 80.

These are finite regression checks. The all-size theorems follow from unit-pivot
elimination and orbit arguments above; they are not inferred from the finite bank.
