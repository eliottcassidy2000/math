# The 8-sheet Lonely Runner comparison

The exact split 8n=n+7n has a geometric property beyond multiplication. It gives two speeds whose phases move in opposite directions on the eight lifts of the same core time. The proof extends the one-exceptional-runner argument in [THM-760](../../../01-canon/theorems/THM-760-coprime-exceptional-runner-sheet-dodge.md), which places one extra phase on a uniformly spaced grid. Here two real constraints are correlated and must use the **same** lift.

Let V be a nonempty set of distinct positive integer speeds and n an odd positive integer. Suppose t₀ gives all speeds of V margin δ, where margin is distance from the phase to the nearest integer. Then some t=(t₀+k)/8, k=0,…,7 gives all speeds in 8V∪{n,7n} margin ≥min(δ,3/16). The 3/16 for the extra pair is sharp if the core time must stay fixed.

Indeed the core phases do not change. Let θ=n t₀ mod 1, and let x=nt mod 1. Since n is odd, x runs through the full shifted grid of spacing 1/8. The second extra phase is θ−x mod 1. The extra margin is thus the distance of x to the nearer of the two points 0 and θ on the time circle. The longer complementary arc has length at least 1/2, so its midpoint is at least 1/4 from both points. A grid point is within 1/16 of that midpoint; it has both margins at least 3/16. When θ=1/2, the grid is shifted by 1/16; the two midpoints 1/4 and 3/4 are both 1/16 off the grid, giving maximum exactly 3/16.

The same argument works for c lifts and any two positive exceptional speeds a,b coprime to c with a+b≡0 mod c; the bound is 1/4−1/(2c). The bound is sharp in the paired speeds n,(c−1)n whenever 4 divides c. For eightfold scaled cores of at least three speeds that satisfy their own Lonely Runner bound, the added pair therefore satisfies the bound for the enlarged instance. This is a structured family, not an unrestricted LRC claim.

The useful comparison with the tournament problem is the exact lifting condition: the eight candidates have a genuinely fixed core projection, but the joint forbidden phases are witnesses in one common lift. [THM-4160](../../../01-canon/theorems/THM-4160-anchored-haar-deletion-cover-and-content-tower.md) makes a larger-scale related distinction between a bounded pool of admissible repairs and verifying the actual joint cover. Here a tournament analogue would be a controllable lift of the *same* marked path-cover state, not two independently valid statements about H and a residue. The time-circle midpoint has no known tournament analogue that places an absolute count; the new induced lift provides a physical candidate family, not that selection theorem.
