# The circular first-birth family inside the merged metagraph

The [near-extremal first-birth construction](../families/PROOF.md) gives the circular tournaments of [Divoux and Wang](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.23755v1): at odd order n use arcs with positive circular difference less than n/2; at even order delete 0 from the circular tournament on n+1 vertices. Their asymptotics imply these counts are first born at order n for all sufficiently large n. We can locate this explicit family in the **actual** merged tiling metagraph, not just the auxiliary flip graph.

Take the cyclic vertex labels mod the odd parent order. Reflection v→−v reverses every arc; on even order it survives deletion of 0. The odd circular tournament has exactly n automorphisms, its rotations. Any automorphism fixing a vertex fixes the vertex's two neighborhoods setwise; each neighborhood is transitive in its circular order and thus fixed pointwise. On even order the two neighborhoods of the removed vertex are the two distinct outdegree classes of the surviving tournament, each transitive, so the automorphism group is trivial. In both cases the merged node is a single self-converse class.

There is an exact formula for its blue incidence by half-paths. Pair up nonfixed vertices as {v,−v}; let B select one vertex from each pair. For odd n, sum the number of Hamiltonian paths of the induced B whose last vertex x beats 0. For even n, sum those whose last vertex x beats −x. These sums are exactly the number of blue tilings. Indeed a reflection-symmetric full frame is forced by its first half, either joined through the fixed center 0 or through the arc x→−x. For odd n there are n different anti-automorphisms (each rotation times the chosen reflection), all conjugate. Counting reflection-symmetric frames over the n anti-automorphisms and dividing by the n rotations gives the claimed blue tiling count for any one reflection. For even n reflection is the only anti-automorphism and there is no factor to divide out.

Write m=floor(n/2). There are at most 2^m m! candidate half-paths. The total number of tilings is H/n in odd order or H in even order. As H is asymptotic to (cosh 1/cos 1) n!/2^(n−1), the fraction of blue incidences is at most O(n2^(n+m)m!/n!) and tends to zero. On the other hand the [closed/open rule](../../01-canon/theorems/THM-4729-closed-and-open-tilings-hamiltonian-cycle-bipartition-of-the-metagraph-lines-and-the-forced-arrangement.md) gives closed incidence fraction n·HC/H. Divoux–Wang prove HC∼(cosh 1/cos 1)(n−1)!/2^n for this same family, so that fraction tends to 1/2. Thus these explicit high first-birth nodes are self-converse, asymptotically half closed, and overwhelmingly incident to black lines. Compare the simple [one-back-arc family](PROOF.md), whose closed fraction tends to zero. A vanishing blue fraction is quite compatible with strong global symmetry.

This is an incidence formula; it does not say how the many black lines distribute among individual neighbor classes.
