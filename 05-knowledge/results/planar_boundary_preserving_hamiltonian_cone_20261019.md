# A larger boundary-preserving Hamiltonian family has only identity polynomial words

2026-10-19. **PROVED + INDEPENDENTLY REVIEWED using the cited fixed-line theorem:** the finite-word rigidity theorem below. The family strictly contains the universal carriers of [packet 110](planar_carrier_composition_rigidity_20261019.md). **OPEN:** nonidentity rational realizations, other Hamiltonian families, unrestricted infinite words, chart entry, and planar JC. No maximality or priority claim is made.

## 1. Statement in the original source coordinates

Let K have characteristic zero, q=1+x²t, and use {f,g}=f_xg_t−f_tg_x on K[x,t]. For arbitrary polynomials A_i∈K[s,τ], constants c_i, and scalar times λ_i, set

    H_i=c_i+t²q A_i(xt,t),   δ_i={−,H_i}.

Each exponential E_i=exp(λ_iδ_i) is well-defined on K[x][[t]]. Consider any finite word C=E_r⋯E_1, with no common invariant assumed.

**Theorem.** If C(x),C(t) both belong to K[x,t], then C(x)=x and C(t)=t. Thus the group generated by this larger family intersects the polynomial endomorphisms of the original source plane only in the identity. Words which cancel to the identity are permitted.

This is a strict enlargement. In the notation p=tq, y=xt²q, D=p³−y²=t³q², a universal carrier satisfies

    p²D R(p,y)=t²q A(xt,t),
    A(s,τ)=(s²+τ)³ R(s²+τ,s(s²+τ)).                (1)

For example A=1 gives H=t²q, outside the universal class because its t-order is two whereas every nonzero universal carrier has t-order at least five.

The only external theorem is Gwoździewicz's fixed-line Keller-map theorem, [*Injectivity on one line*, Theorem 1.1 / Lemma 2.3](https://arxiv.org/html/alg-geom/9305008), with exactly the characteristic-zero and algebraically closed hypotheses used after base change. Packet 110 checks that primary source and its role. No general Jacobian-conjecture assumption is made.

## 2. Two compatible completions

The source identity H_i−c_i∈t²K[x,t] gives

    δ_i x∈tK[x,t],   δ_i t∈t²K[x,t].

Hence δ_i raises source t-order at least one. Its exponential and every finite word define continuous automorphisms of K[x][[t]], fix t=0 pointwise, and preserve the source bracket. A polynomial realization therefore has determinant one and fixes the affine line pointwise.

For the second boundary use s=xt, τ=t, h=s²+τ. Then

    x=s/τ,   t=τ,   q=h/τ,
    H_i−c_i=τh A_i(s,τ),
    {f,g}=τ(f_sg_τ−f_τg_s).

In the polynomial-coefficient common ring

    B=K[s][[τ]][τ^(−1)],

the vector-field components are exactly

    δ_i s=τ(h A_i+τ A_i+τh (A_i)_τ),
    δ_i τ=−τ²(2s A_i+h (A_i)_s).                     (2)

They have τ-orders at least one and two, respectively. Therefore δ_i raises every integer τ-order at least one; E_i and finite words are continuous automorphisms of B. The source and common-ring operations agree under the injective substitution

    σ:B→K[x]((t)),   s↦xt, τ↦t.

Indeed s^aτ^b goes to x^a t^(a+b), distinct exponent pairs remain distinct, and every target coefficient receives finitely many terms because b is bounded below and a≥0. The derivations agree termwise, so convergence gives the same statement for exponentials and finite words. This is not an identification with K(s)((τ)), nor with arbitrary localized cusp completions.

## 3. Setwise boundary preservation with its induced differential equation

The second evaluation

    ε:B→K((s)),   τ↦−s², s↦s

is well-defined and continuous. A τ-tail beginning at order N evaluates into s-order at least 2N; localization in τ introduces only finitely many negative s powers.

Unlike the pointwise-fixed subfamily, (2) usually does not vanish under ε. Instead it gives the exact intertwining identity

    ε∘δ_i = v_i(s) d/ds ∘ ε,
    v_i(s)=s⁴ A_i(s,−s²).                            (3)

On s this follows from ε(δ_i s)=s⁴A_i(s,−s²). On τ it follows from ε(δ_i τ)=−2s⁵A_i(s,−s²), the derivative of −s² multiplied by v_i. The chain rule proves (3) on Laurent polynomials, and continuity extends it to B.

Write Δ_i=v_i(s)d/ds. Because ord_s v_i≥4 unless v_i=0, Δ_i raises every integer s-order by at least three. Its scalar exponential is well-defined on K((s)). Consequently

    ε E_i=exp(λ_iΔ_i) ε,
    ε C=(exp(λ_rΔ_r)⋯exp(λ_1Δ_1)) ε.                (4)

In particular ε(C(h))=0. This proves setwise preservation of the second boundary through a convergent induced flow. It requires no assertion that (h) is closed, no identification of ker ε with (h), and no q-divisibility inference from the source t-adic topology, where q is a unit.

## 4. A polynomial word must be the identity

Base change to an algebraic closure. A polynomial realization F=(X,T) has determinant one and fixes the line t=0 pointwise by §2. Gwoździewicz's theorem makes it a polynomial automorphism. It preserves the prime ideal (t), so T=at. The determinant gives a X_x=1. Pointwise line fixing then gives

    T=t,   X=x+f(t),   f∈Kbar[t], f(0)=0.             (5)

Now h=t+(xt)²=tq. The faithful σ comparison transports C(h) to T(1+X²T). Evaluating under ε, (4) implies

    0=(−s²)[1−s²(−1/s+f(−s²))²],
    0=f(−s²)[2−s f(−s²)].                           (6)

The second factor cannot vanish: its constant term is two. Since K((s)) is a domain, f(−s²)=0; substitution Kbar[t]→Kbar[s], t↦−s², is injective. Thus f=0. Polynomial identity descends to K. ∎

No common Hamiltonian invariant, commutativity, or finite-order argument is used. The obstruction is the simultaneous realization of an injectively fixed line and a preserved second boundary in a polynomial map.

## 5. Pointwise subfamily, moving-boundary example, and a necessary guard

For one generator at a nonzero scalar time, the induced boundary action is the identity exactly when A(s,−s²)=0. If v≠0 has initial term c s^m, m≥4, then

    exp(λv∂s)(s)−s=λc s^m+O(s^(m+1)),

so nonzero λ cannot give the identity. Polynomial division by the monic τ+s² shows A(s,−s²)=0 exactly when h divides A. Thus H−c∈τh²K[s,τ] is precisely the pointwise-fixed-boundary subfamily among these generators, while the exponent-one family preserves the boundary setwise. Finite words can cancel boundary actions and are not classified by this individual-generator criterion.

For A=1, H=t²q, the induced equation is ds/dλ=s⁴. Its formal solution is

    s(λ)=s(1−3λs³)^(−1/3).

This is nontrivial on the second boundary. It shows why replacing setwise preservation by pointwise fixedness would incorrectly exclude an actual member of the enlarged family.

The h factor cannot simply be discarded from this argument. The Hamiltonian H=τ²=t² still has well-defined source and common-ring flows and fixes t=0 pointwise, but its exact flow is the nonidentity polynomial shear

    (x,t)↦(x+2λt,t).

It does not preserve q=0. This is a counterexample to the weakened theorem with no second-boundary condition; it is not a claim that the present family is maximal among all possible rigid families.
