## 4. Marked relations force every meridian to agree

This algebraic implication was independently checked by
orthogonal_returns before path production, together with the direct
retained-sheet argument in §5. The complete source/path audit has now
passed independently as well.

The monic polynomial gives a continuous section outside a Cauchy root
bound over the entire u-plane. Over a compact disk filling any one of
(5), take a constant vertical basepoint larger than all root bounds.
Its section lift of the base loop is null in the full affine complement.
The regular bundle group is generated by fibre loops and section lifts
of regular-base loops. Restoring the critical fibres kills those lifts,
and an arbitrary loop in the full complement can be perturbed to miss
the finitely many critical vertical fibres. Consequently the four
positive fibre meridians surject onto the affine complement group, and
each actual braid fixes their tuple exactly, not just up to simultaneous
conjugacy. The four selected loops need not generate the regular base
group for this necessary-relation argument.

Use below-stem meridians and the same convention as the audited
predecessor. The geometric map of loops for a positive half twist is
the inverse Artin action; contravariant transport of representation
coordinates in chronological order is
\[
 H_i^+:(a,b)\mapsto(aba^{-1},a),\qquad
 H_i^-:(a,b)\mapsto(b,b^{-1}ab).
\tag{8}
\]
Fixedness of \(\sigma_i\), \(\sigma_i^2\), and
\(\sigma_i^3\) is respectively equality, commutation, and the braid
relation for its two affected entries. These equivalences hold in every
group. For example, the second entry of \(H^3(a,b)\) is
\(aba b^{-1}a^{-1}\), so fixedness implies \(aba=bab\);
substitution gives the converse as well.

Write the four original positive meridians \((a,b,c,d)\).

1. The smooth word is \(\sigma_3\sigma_1\sigma_3^{-1}=\sigma_1\).
   Its fixedness gives \(a=b\).
2. The node3 word \(\sigma_3^2\) gives \([c,d]=1\).
3. The node_left prefix \(\sigma_3\) exposes \(b,cdc^{-1}\)
   to its central square \(\sigma_2^2\). Thus
   \([b,cdc^{-1}]=1\), and step 2 reduces this to \([b,d]=1\).
4. In the cusp word, the disjoint generators \(\sigma_1\) and
   \(\sigma_3\) commute. Its middle six letters reorder exactly to
   \(\sigma_1^3\sigma_3^3\), leaving outer prefix
   \(\sigma_3\sigma_2\) and its inverse suffix. This prefix sends
   the tuple to
   \[
   (a,bcdc^{-1}b^{-1},b,c).
   \]
   **After** steps 2--3, this is \((a,d,b,c)\). Fixedness therefore
   gives \(ada=dad\) and \(bcb=cbc\).

Now \(a=b\) and \([b,d]=1\) imply \([a,d]=1\). A commuting
pair satisfying a braid relation must agree, so \(a=d\). From
\([c,d]=1\) it follows that \([b,c]=1\); the other braid relation
then gives \(b=c\). All four meridians are equal. The source checks
the literal free-group prefix identities, the commuting middle-letter
reordering, and that every constant tuple fixes all four words. There
is no permutation enumeration and no transitivity assumption in this
group reduction.

The affine group is therefore cyclic, generated by a positive meridian
\(\mu\). Its order is infinite: the reduced defining polynomial
\(F:\mathbb C^2\setminus C\to\mathbb C^*\) takes \(\mu\) to
a loop of winding one at a smooth point of C. This proves (2).

## 5. Whole-support consequence and its scope

Suppose that C were the whole irreducible nonproperness curve of a
nonautomorphic polynomial Keller map \(\Phi:\mathbb C^2\to\mathbb C^2\).
Its finite étale cover over the complement is connected, so the
generic-degree-d monodromy is transitive. For a nonautomorphic planar
Keller map \(d>1\). One can pay this standard fact directly: a
birational étale map is an open immersion by Zariski's Main Theorem.
Its image cannot omit a divisor, since pulling back that divisor's
irreducible equation would give a nonconstant unit on the source plane.
If its complement is finite, the inverse coordinate functions extend
over it by normality of the target plane. Those polynomial extensions
give a two-sided inverse, so the original map is an automorphism.

There is at least one retained sheet at a generic smooth point of C.
Indeed \(F\circ\Phi\) is a nonconstant polynomial because \(\Phi\)
is dominant. Its zero locus contains a divisor. Since \(\Phi\) is
étale, the image of that divisor has dimension one in the irreducible
curve C and is dense there. Thus the generic actual count \(a\) is
positive. Every positive meridian fixes each retained local sheet.

A transitive action generated by a single permutation is one cycle on
all d labels. It has no fixed label when \(d>1\). This contradicts
the retained sheet and the cyclicity proved above. This argument uses
neither the one-cusp Euler ledger nor a false extension of it to the
two-cusp domain. It excludes this literal whole-support target; it is
not a classification of other two-cusp sextics or a general Jacobian
conjecture claim.
