## Errors and precise repair boundaries

2. **REFUTED proof assertion: quadratic Kummer subextensions are always `F(sqrt(c))` for rational products of the original radicands.** With `e=3`, `F=Q(zeta_8)`, and sole radicand `c=4`, `F(4^(1/8))=F(2^(1/4))` is a nontrivial quadratic extension, whereas `sqrt(4^k)` is rational for every integer `k`. Nontriviality follows because a real fourth root of 2 in the abelian extension F/Q would generate a Galois extension of Q, while Q(2^(1/4))/Q is not normal. Correct Kummer theory concerns classes modulo `F*^8`, not only rational squareclasses.
3. **REFUTED literal stronger sentence in Theorem U:** not every Boolean rule becomes constant. The identity rule `phi(b)=b`, `c=1`, preserves S and hence both tournament types. It fails to be graph-valued. Correct dichotomy: at each collapse prime, a rule either is not graph-valued on these tournaments or maps both types to the same symmetric set. If even-graph-valued, this common set is empty.
4. **Missing small-prime exception:** `p=5` has one tournament type and only the empty even circulant. The constant-empty rule is a Gamma-equivariant type bijection. Therefore “exists exactly for p=3 mod4” is correct only with `p>5`; among all odd primes, add `p=5`.
5. **False symmetry rationale, repairable conclusion:** multiplication automorphisms are power maps `x -> x^j`, not multipliers `x -> ax`. A parameter-free formula in `(F_p^*, multiplication, S)` is automatically power-equivariant, not automatically Gamma-equivariant. However, if it also descends to a well-defined bijection on multiplier types, that bijection is equivariant for the power action on the type sets. Its power-orbits are precisely the Gamma-orbits of labelled sets. Thus the orbit-count obstruction still excludes such a type bijection.
6. **REFUTED under ordinary uniform first-order semantics:** the Paley rule is not definable by one fixed first-order formula with “one quantifier.” Defining squares does not define the needed parity selection. See the explicit parity reduction below. With a parity/counting operation allowed, the construction is perfectly valid; set-theoretic canonicity and equivariance do not require first-order definability.
7. **Unsupported scope expansion:** Theorem U concerns pointwise finite-stencil rules `1_f(S)(x)=phi(1_S(c_1x),...,1_S(c_kx))` for a fixed finite bank of constants. It does not cover arbitrary processing of entire dilated sets, arbitrary quantifiers, or all multiplicative formulas with named constants. Theorem D handles power-equivariant type maps. ## Replacement positive-radical compatibility lemma

**PROVED, using CITED Kummer correspondence and Chebotarev.** Let `n=2^e`, `e>=3`, and let `c_1,...,c_k` be positive rational numbers. There are infinitely many primes `p`, avoiding all numerators and denominators, such that

- `p = 1+n mod 2n`, equivalently `v_2(p-1)=e`;
- every `c_i` is an nth power modulo p.

### Cyclotomic exclusion

Put `F=Q(zeta_n)` and `M=F(c_1^(1/n),...,c_k^(1/n))`. We prove `zeta_(2n) notin M`.

Kummer correspondence identifies the subgroup of `F*/F*^n` whose elements become nth powers in M with the subgroup generated by the classes of the c_i. Thus, if `zeta_(2n)` belonged to M, its nth power `-1` would satisfy

`-1 = a b^n`, with `a in <c_1,...,c_k> subset Q_+` and `b in F*`.

Write `q=1/a>0`. Then `b^n=-q`. Set `alpha=b conjugate(b)`. Because F/Q is abelian, conjugation commutes with every embedding; hence every conjugate of alpha is strictly positive. Also `alpha^n=q^2`. Every conjugate is therefore the unique positive nth root of the same rational number. Consequently `alpha in Q_+`.

We have `alpha^(n/2)=q`, so

`(b^2/alpha)^(n/2)=-1`.

It follows that `b^2/alpha=zeta_n^j` for an odd integer j. Taking the norm from F to `F0=Q(zeta_(n/2))` gives

`Norm(b)^2 = alpha^2 (-zeta_(n/2))^j`.

Thus `(-zeta_(n/2))^j` is a square in F0. But `-zeta_(n/2)=zeta_(n/2)^(1+n/4)`, and `1+n/4` is odd for `e>=3`. Its odd power is a primitive `(n/2)`th root of unity. Any square root is a primitive nth root and generates F, which has degree 2 over F0. This is impossible. Hence `zeta_(2n) notin M`.

### Frobenius and the prime set

M/Q is Galois, being the splitting field of the finitely many polynomials `X^n-c_i` after adjoining `zeta_n`. Let `L=M(zeta_(2n))`. The preceding exclusion gives `[L:M]=2`. Its nontrivial automorphism sigma fixes M and sends `zeta_(2n)` to `-zeta_(2n)=zeta_(2n)^(1+n)`. Since its order-two kernel is normal in Gal(L/Q), sigma is central.

Chebotarev gives a positive-density set of unramified rational primes with Frobenius sigma. They split completely in M and satisfy `p=1+n mod2n`. For primes outside the radicands' numerators and denominators, complete splitting gives all c_i as nth powers modulo p. This proves the lemma. In fact the constructed prime set has density `1/[L:Q]`.

**Sharp lower boundary:** e=2 is not covered. For c=2, any p=5 mod8 has 2 a quadratic nonresidue, so 2 cannot be a fourth power; the claimed norm contradiction correctly disappears at that level.

## Repaired signed finite-stencil theorem

Fix nonzero integers `c_1,...,c_k`. The positive-radical lemma with `n=8` supplies infinitely many primes `p=9 mod16` for which every `|c_i|` lies in the odd-order subgroup `K=K_max(p)` of `F_p^*`.

Let S be a K-invariant tournament connection set. Write `b=1_S(x)` and `epsilon_i=0` for positive c_i, `epsilon_i=1` for negative c_i. Since S contains exactly one of x and -x,

`1_S(c_i x)=b XOR epsilon_i`.

Therefore every Boolean finite-stencil rule reduces on these sets to a unary function

`psi(b)=phi(b XOR epsilon_1,...,b XOR epsilon_k)`.

Its output is one of `empty`, `F_p^*`, S, or -S. The latter two are not symmetric connection sets. The two constant outputs do not depend on S. If even circulant graphs are required, only the empty graph is allowed: the complete graph is sign-odd (equivalently its full multiplier stabilizer fails the standard evenness criterion).

Meanwhile `F_p^*/K` is C8. An antipodal section is an 8-bit word with `s(t+4)=1-s(t)`, so there are 16. Any nontrivial cyclic translation subgroup contains translation by 4, which cannot fix a section. Every translation orbit therefore has size 8, giving exactly two multiplier types. Hence no finite-stencil rule can be an even-circulant type bijection at these primes.

This argument actually allows the Boolean table to depend on p: the finite bank of dilation constants, not prime-independence of the table, is the essential locality restriction. Rational constants work as well after excluding finitely many denominator primes. No claim is made here for an unbounded/prime-dependent bank or nonlocal quantified rules.

## Signed-set Burnside audit

**PROVED.** For a finite G-set with a free commuting involution, choose a reference section. For g stabilizing a subset X of pairs, the parity of reference-sheet changes along X equals the parity of flipped g-cycles; telescoping makes this independent of the reference section. Restricted to the stabilizer G_X it is a one-dimensional sign character.

For each g, sections fixed by g are counted by `2^(number of pair-cycles)` if no pair-cycle flips, and zero otherwise. The weighted count over g-invariant subsets X is the product over pair-cycles of `(1+cycle_sign)`, hence the same number.

The final averaging step deserves an explicit sentence in the source: regroup the weighted sum by G-orbits of subsets. The contribution of the orbit represented by X is

`(1/|G_X|) sum_(g in G_X) sgn_X(g)`.

It is 1 exactly when the sign character is trivial and 0 otherwise. This proves equality between section-orbits and G-even subset-orbits. There is no missing hypothesis beyond finiteness and the free commuting involution.

In the circulant application, identification of multiplier-even subsets with sign-even circulant graphs is the previously established THM-4721 criterion; it is inherited, not newly reproved by this audit.

## Paley construction and the p mod 4 dichotomy

### p=3 mod4

**PROVED.** The squares form a section R and every power automorphism preserves R. A multiplier either preserves R everywhere or swaps it with its complement. Thus, for every symmetric S stabilized by `x -> ax^j`, the sign is

`(-1)^((number of selected pairs) [a is a nonsquare])`.

The multiplier -1 stabilizes every graph and is a nonsquare. It follows that multiplier-even means an even number of selected pairs; such a set is automatically Gamma-even.

For a tournament S let X be its disagreement set relative to R. There are an odd number m of pairs. Exactly one of X and its complement has even cardinality. Choose it as beta(S). Under Gamma, X is transported either directly or with a global complement, so beta commutes with Gamma. Its labelled fibers are exactly `{S,-S}`. It is onto all even labelled graphs; on multiplier types it is a bijection, since beta(S')=a beta(S) implies S'=aS or S'=-aS. Thus no equinumerosity theorem is needed for surjectivity here.

### p=1 mod4, p>=13

**PROVED.** The power `j=(p+1)/2=1+(p-1)/2` is odd and coprime to p-1. The map `x -> x^j=x chi(x)` fixes each antipodal class, and flips exactly the nonsquare classes.

Choose two distinct classes `{+/-x}`, `{+/-y}`, with x square and y nonsquare, excluding `y=+/-i x` where `i^2=-1`. Such a choice exists for p>=13: there are at least three nonsquare classes, and at most one is excluded. Their union S has multiplier stabilizer exactly `{+/-1}`. Indeed a larger stabilizer would act freely on the two pairs and therefore have order 4, forcing their ratio to be +/-i. The involution -1 flips two selected classes, so S is multiplier-even. The displayed power map flips just its nonsquare class, so S is Gamma-odd.

The multiplier group H is normal in Gamma. The sign character is compatible with conjugation; consequently H-even subsets are Gamma-invariant, and Gamma-even subsets form a proper Gamma-invariant subfamily. Signed-set Burnside then gives

`#(Gamma-orbits of tournaments) = #(Gamma-orbits of Gamma-even subsets) < #(Gamma-orbits of H-even subsets)`.

This excludes Gamma-equivariant orbit-bijections. It also excludes a power-equivariant map that descends to a well-defined bijection on H-types, because the quotient power actions' orbits equal the displayed Gamma-orbits. This is the correct route for parameter-free multiplicative formulas.

### Boundary p=5

**FINITE-EXACT and elementary.** There are two pairs. Empty is even; a one-pair set is odd under -1; both pairs give the complete graph, which is odd under a multiplier of order 4. All four tournament sections form a single multiplier orbit. The constant-empty map is therefore a Gamma-equivariant type bijection. The corrected set-theoretic equivariance dichotomy is: among odd primes, such maps exist for p=3 mod4 and for p=5, and fail for p=1 mod4 with p>=13.

## First-order definability correction

Use independent disagreement bits `x_1,...,x_m` relative to the Paley section, with m odd. The Paley output bit at pair i is

`beta_i = x_i XOR (x_1 XOR ... XOR x_m) = XOR_(j != i) x_j`.

Fix a representative of pair i and fix x_i. A fixed first-order formula over the multiplicative structure with unary predicate S, evaluated at that representative, expands to a constant-depth polynomial-size AND/OR/NOT circuit in the remaining tournament bits: replace each quantifier by an AND/OR over the p-1 elements, and each S-atom by one input bit or its complement. Group operations and equality are fixed data, not oracle gates depending on the input. Formula depth and number of variables are fixed, so the depth is constant and size is polynomial in p, hence in m.

If that formula defined beta uniformly for all primes p=3 mod4, these circuits would compute parity on m-1 independent bits for unbounded m. This contradicts the nonuniform constant-depth polynomial-size parity lower bound. Thus the ordinary uniform first-order interpretation of the source's “one quantifier” claim is false. The circuit lower bound applies at every sufficiently large input length, so restricting to prime-derived lengths does not avoid it.

This does not exclude the set-theoretic construction, a formula language with a parity quantifier, or other prospective first-order type bijections. It corrects the claimed definability of this particular beta. Nor does it establish the stronger global hypothesis about all formulas with named constants.
