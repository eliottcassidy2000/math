## 0. Summary of what is new

1. **Chess has a four-coordinate irreversibility potential** `Phi = (pieces, pawns, pawn distance to promotion, castling rights)` that every legal move weakly decreases in lexicographic order and that is constant exactly on the non-capturing, non-pawn moves that touch no castling right (Theorem 1). A game makes at most **118** pawn-moves-or-captures (proved via a per-file lemma checked exhaustively) and at most **122** `Phi`-decreasing moves, so it visits at most **123 strata**. On a stratum the pawn placement is frozen, so the pawn-conditioned visibility graph of the third note is a *stratum invariant*.
2. **Verification decomposes along strata** (Theorem 2): a win/draw/loss labelling is the true value function iff terminals are right, every stratum-leaving move is evaluated in an already verified lower stratum, and the wins claimed inside a stratum carry a ranking. This is Pavlov's capture-quiet theorem with "material class" refined to "stratum"; in KPvK the 331,352-position class becomes 48 two-king models of at most 7,004 positions each (1,968,490 king moves stay inside strata; 157,140 pawn pushes leave them, every one towards promotion).
3. **Local consistency is one-sidedly spurious** (Proposition 4.5): a terminal-correct labelling satisfying the Bellman equation everywhere never under-claims a win or loss; it can only over-claim wins on cycles. So the "false fixed point" danger is exactly the absence of a ranking, never a missed draw.
4. **A fortress is a trap, and traps are computable from a predicate without any tablebase** (Theorem 3 and section 5): the largest closed subset of any candidate region is the greatest fixed point of the defender's safety operator; its members are proved at least draws by closure alone. The extractor applied to the whole KPvK class returns exactly the drawn set of the independent retrograde solve (108,788 positions). The rook-pawn corner predicate (2,507 positions) and the wrong-bishop corner predicate (72,720 positions) are themselves closed; the "square of the pawn" predicate is not (64,222 of its 77,736 drawn positions survive); in KRvKB the safe-corner region contains a certified fortress of 1,237,588 positions while the wrong-corner region keeps 214,316. In KRvKN the four central squares alone keep 11.4% with no quiet attacker-to-move state, while "king in the central 4x4, knight within two squares" keeps 81.2% (1,769,224 states) with 542,280 quiet ones: a fortress predicate has to contain the defender's manoeuvring room.
5. **"Outcome first, optionality second" is sound for the defender and unsound for the attacker** (Theorem 4): any value-preserving policy holds a draw, but in KRvK the policy "among winning moves maximise mobility" mates from only 1,720 of the 175,168 winning white-to-move positions and cycles from the other 173,448; maximising the number of winning continuations instead fails almost as badly (2,817 mates, 172,351 cycles). Winning needs a ranking, and optionality may only break ties below it.
6. **The pawn-conditioned visibility atlas is digital topology** (Lemma V1): rook-ray components are the 4-connected components of the complement of the pawn set, bishop-ray components of colour `c` are the diagonal-step components of the colour-`c` squares minus the colour-`c` pawns, queen components are the 8-connected ones. "Visibility cuts" are the classical 8-connected separating curves.
7. **The axiom is a statement about the tempo on the symmetric diagonal** (section 2 and 8). The correct value-negating involution of the full game is the rank reflection composed with the colour swap (the second note's 180-degree rotation is not a symmetry of positions with castling rights). On boards fixed by such an involution the mover's value is a sign, the *tempo*; strategy stealing proves the tempo is never negative in chess-with-passing; A0 says it is not negative at the root of real chess. The Syzygy census of the symmetric 4-man diagonals (KPvKP exhaustive on the rank-reflection diagonal: 2,184 boards, mover wins 450, draws 642, mutual zugzwangs 0; seeded samples of the rotation diagonal and of KRvKR, KQvKQ) shows that on the pawn-only rank diagonal reachable by tablebases the tempo is never negative (Conjecture C1, HYP-9246), while seeded samples of the rotation diagonal and of the rook and queen diagonals show no mutual zugzwang either and a mover's winning share growing with the material's range (28%, 84%, 88%).
8. **Material lattice counts** of the second note are confirmed (72, 648, 419,904) and extended with promotions (26,298 per side; 691,584,804 joint signatures in the lattice, before legality).

## 2. The game, its values, the axiom

**2.1 States and values.** A state is `s = (placement, side to move, castling rights, en-passant square, half-move clock, repetition record)`. Under the automatic 75-move and fivefold-repetition rules the game graph is finite, so by Zermelo every state has a value `V(s) in {-1, 0, +1}` (White's view) and `v(s) = V(s)` if White is to move, `-V(s)` otherwise (mover's view). Write `G_inf` for the game without the move-count and repetition rules, with infinite play counted as a draw; it is the game the retrograde tables of this note solve. The two games differ only on *cursed wins* (forced mates that need more than 50 moves between irreversible events); none of the classes solved here has any (the longest wins are 10, 16, 19 and 19 moves to the class boundary). A trap in `G_inf` (section 4) certifies at least a draw in rule-chess as well, because the extra rules only end games as draws.

**2.2 Axiom A0 (the owner's).** `V(s0) >= 0` for the initial state `s0` with White to move. By determinacy this says exactly: *the maximal White trap contains the root* (White has a strategy that never gets mated). It leaves the dichotomy `V(s0) in {0, +1}`.

**2.3 What A0 asserts, exactly.** Let `sigma` be the rank reflection `(f, r) -> (f, 9-r)` composed with the colour swap, the swap of the side to move, the swap of the two sides' castling rights and the reflection of the en-passant square. Proposition 2.1 (section 8) shows `v(sigma s) = v(s)` for every legal state, i.e. `V(sigma s) = -V(s)`; and `sigma` fixes the board of `s0`. Hence `V(s0, Black to move) = -V(s0, White to move)`: the null-move position has the opposite value. In *chess with passing* (either side may pass; everything else unchanged) every `sigma`-fixed board `B` has `v(B, mover) >= 0`: if the mover lost, the mover would pass, and the opponent would face `sigma(B, mover)`, which the opponent then loses by symmetry. So in chess-with-passing the root is at least a draw for White by strategy stealing, and A0 says that deleting the pass from real chess does not change the sign at the root: **A0 is the statement "there is no zugzwang at the root"**. The two games are not comparable in general (passing adds options to both sides), so A0 is a genuine axiom and not a consequence of the symmetry; section 8 shows the sign does go negative on symmetric boards of small endgames.

**2.4 The bracket analogy (typed ANALOGY).** The owner compares A0's bracket `{draw, White win}` with the plane-colouring bracket `{6, 7}` (the repository currently phrases the plane problem as a six/seven alternative; see the 2026-10-11 entries of the results index). The shape that transfers is the logical type of the two remaining certificates: the lower alternative is certified by a *global closed object* (a 6-colouring of the plane; a Black trap containing `s0`, section 4), the upper alternative by a *finite well-founded witness* (a finite unit-distance graph with chromatic number 7, by de Bruijn-Erdos; a White ranking from `s0`, i.e. a finite winning strategy tree). Nothing quantitative transfers; this is typed ANALOGY only.

## 3. Irreversibility strata

**Definition.** For a state `s` let `N(s)` be the number of pieces on the board (kings included), `P(s)` the number of pawns, `A(s) = sum over pawns of the number of ranks still to travel` (a White pawn on rank `r` contributes `8 - r`, a Black pawn `r - 1`), `C(s)` the number of castling rights still held (0-4). `Phi(s) = (N, P, A, C)` with the lexicographic order. The **stratum** of `s` is the triple (material signature with bishop colours, pawn placement with colours, castling rights).

**Theorem 1 (the potential; PROVED).** For every legal move `s -> s'`: `Phi(s') <= Phi(s)`, with equality iff the move is a non-capturing move of a knight, bishop, queen, king or rook that forfeits no castling right. Equality holds iff the move stays in the stratum. The `Phi`-decreasing moves are: captures (`N` drops), pawn pushes (`A` drops, or `P` drops at promotion), castling and right-forfeiting king/rook moves (`C` drops).
*Proof.* A capture removes a piece (`N` drops; whatever else changes is lower in the order). A non-capturing pawn move keeps `N`; a promotion lowers `P`; a push keeps `P` and lowers `A` by the number of ranks advanced (1 or 2). A non-capturing, non-pawn move keeps `N, P, A`; it changes `C` iff it is a castling move or a king/rook move from a square that still carried a right, and then `C` drops. Castling rights are never regained and pawns never move backwards, so no coordinate ever rises. The en-passant flag lives one ply and only appears after a double push, which already lowered `A`. ∎

**Lemma 3.1 (per file; PROVED, model checked exhaustively).** Consider the two pawns that start on one file. If neither of them ever captures, they stay on their file and the total number of moves they make in the whole game is at most 10 (at most 4 if neither is captured). *Proof.* Two pawns on one file never pass each other. If neither is captured they end adjacent, the White one on rank `r`, the Black one on `r+1`, after `(r-2) + (7-(r+1)) = 4` moves. If the Black pawn is captured on rank `r_b` while the White pawn stands on `r_w <= r_b - 1`, the Black pawn made at most `7 - r_b <= 6 - r_w <= 4` moves and the White pawn makes at most 6 in all; symmetrically for the other order. The exhaustive single-file model in `strata_census.py` (all sequences of single and double steps, with an outside capture of either pawn at any moment) returns the maxima 10 and 4. ∎

**Theorem 1' (the 118 bound; PROVED).** A game of chess contains at most 118 moves that are pawn moves or captures, hence at most 122 `Phi`-decreasing moves, hence it visits at most 123 strata. *Proof.* Let `k` be the number of starting files on which at least one of the two pawns captures at some time. Those `k` pawns make `k` distinct captures, so captures by non-pawns number at most `30 - k`. Pawn moves on the other `8 - k` files are at most 10 per file by Lemma 3.1, and at most 12 on each of the `k` files (6 per pawn). The number of pawn-moves-or-captures is therefore at most `10(8-k) + 12k + (30-k) = 110 + k <= 118`. Castling-right losses not coinciding with a pawn move or capture are at most 4. ∎ Labelle's longest-game analysis uses exactly 118 events and reaches 5898.5 moves (claimed 50-move rule) and 8848.5 (automatic 75-move rule); attainment of 118 is CITED there, the inequality is proved here.

**Corollary 3.2 (the pawn mask is a stratum invariant; PROVED).** On a stratum the pawn placement `P` is constant, hence so is the whole multilayer visibility graph `G(P)` of the third note (section 7). Every move that changes `G(P)` is `Phi`-decreasing, hence happens at most 122 times per game.

**Theorem 2 (stratum decomposition of value verification; PROVED).** Let `L` assign to every legal state of `G_inf` a value in `{+1, 0, -1}` (mover's view). Then `L = v` iff
(T) `L` is correct on terminal states (checkmate `-1`, stalemate and dead positions `0`);
(B) at every non-terminal state the Bellman equation `L(s) = max over legal moves of -L(s')` holds, where successors in lower strata are read from strata verified earlier (the strata form a finite DAG under `Phi`, so this order exists);
(R) inside each stratum, every state with `L(s) = +1` carries a ranking: a function `rho` into the naturals with, at mover-wins, a move to a state `s'` with `L(s') = -1` and `rho(s') < rho(s)` (or a mate in one), and, at `L(s) = -1` states, `rho(s') < rho(s)` for every move. Equivalently, the states labelled `+1`/`-1` are the least fixed point of the win/loss operator relative to the boundary labels.
Here (R) reads: at every state with `L(s) = +1` some move leads to a state `s'` with `L(s') = -1` that is a terminal, a lower-stratum state, or has `rho(s') < rho(s)`; at every non-terminal state with `L(s) = -1` every move leads to a state with `L = +1` that is a lower-stratum state or has smaller `rho`.
*Proof.* `v` satisfies (T), (B) and (R) with `rho = DTM`. Conversely, induct along the stratum DAG, assuming `L = v` on all lower strata. Inside the stratum, induction on `rho` (lower-stratum and terminal successors are base cases) gives `v(s) = L(s)` at every state labelled `+-1`: at `L(s) = +1` the move of (R) reaches a state known to have `v = -1`; at `L(s) = -1` the state is a mate or every move reaches a state known to have `v = +1`. If `L(s) = 0` but `v(s) != 0`, Proposition 4.5 below (which uses (T) and (B) only, the lower strata serving as verified boundary) gives `L(s) = v(s) != 0`, a contradiction. ∎ This is Pavlov's capture-quiet decomposition (arXiv:2604.07907: terminal / capture / quiet positions, with capture positions checked against verified smaller sub-models) with the material class refined to the stratum: pawn pushes and castling-right losses are moved from "quiet" to "boundary". The sub-models shrink accordingly.

**KPvK by strata (FINITE-EXACT, `solve_small.py`).** The class has 331,352 legal states and 2,125,630 internal moves; 1,968,490 of them are king moves inside a stratum and 157,140 are pawn pushes between strata, every one of which advances the pawn (checked). The 48 strata have between 6,822 and 7,004 states each (for the a-pawn on a2: 3,441 with White to move and 3,496 with Black to move). The promotion boundary refers to the four pawnless 3-man classes, which the same script solves first (KQvK: 368,452 states, longest mate 20 plies; KRvK: 399,112, longest 32 plies; KBvK and KNvK: all draws; every white-to-move KQvK or KRvK state is a win). KPvK itself: with White to move 124,960 wins and 38,368 draws; with Black to move 70,420 draws and 97,604 losses; the longest win is 38 plies to the promotion boundary (`8/8/8/k7/8/K7/6P1/8 b`). The retrograde order of the strata is the order of the pawn's rank: the 7th-rank strata reference only the pawnless classes, the 2nd-rank strata reference everything above them. `verify_strata.py` runs Theorem 2 on the table: the 48 strata are verified in that order with checks (T), (B), (R) (ranking `rho = DTM`, lower strata and the pawnless classes as boundary); every stratum passes, the largest has 6,937 states and 41,794 internal edges against 6,536 boundary edges, and the whole verification takes 2 seconds.

## 4. Certificates: traps and rankings

Fix a defender `D` and attacker `A`. For a set `F` of states of `G_inf` (possibly spanning several strata) say `F` is **closed** (a `D`-trap) if for every `s in F`:
- `D` to move: `s` is a stalemate or a dead position, or some legal move leads into `F`;
- `A` to move: `s` has no legal move (then `A` is mated or stalemated, both at least a draw for `D`), or every legal move leads into `F`.

**Theorem 3 (traps; PROVED).**
(a) *Soundness.* Every `s in F` has `v_D(s) >= 0`: `D` plays moves that stay in `F`; the play never reaches a state where `D` is mated, because such a state has `D` to move, no legal move and is not a stalemate.
(b) *Completeness and the lattice.* The union of traps is a trap; the set `W>=(D) = {s : v_D(s) >= 0}` is a trap (by determinacy, a non-losing strategy stays inside it); hence `W>=(D)` is the largest trap, the greatest fixed point of the safety operator `S(F) = {s : D to move and (terminal-draw or some move into F)} union {s : A to move and (no move or all moves into F)}`, and every trap is contained in it.
(c) *Locality.* Checking closure of `F` costs one move generation per state of `F`; it does not depend on the depth of the game tree.
(d) *Width.* For `D` to move at `s in F` let `w_F(s)` be the number of legal moves into `F` (plus one if a certified draw by a boundary move exists). Then `F subset F'` implies `w_F <= w_F'`; the maximal trap maximises the defender's reserve pointwise. A certificate smaller than `W>=(D)` buys description length with optionality.
(e) *Extraction.* For any finite candidate region `R`, the largest trap inside `R` is obtained by starting from `R` and repeatedly deleting states that violate closure; the result is `nu X. (R intersect S(X))`, computed in `chesslib.extract_trap` with predecessor lists and a work-list. Its members are proved at least draws by (a), using only the move generator and the certified boundary.

**Proposition 4.4 (rankings; PROVED, standard).** A set `W` with a function `rho : W -> N` such that at `D`-to-move states some move leads to a mate or to a state of `W` with smaller `rho`, and at `A`-to-move states every move leads to a state of `W` with smaller `rho` and `A` is not stalemated, certifies `v_D = +1` on `W`; `W_win(D)` with `rho = DTM` is the largest such set (least fixed point of the attractor operator). A ranking is a well-founded object; a trap is a closed one. This is the invariant/variant (safety/liveness) duality of program verification.

**Proposition 4.5 (one-sided spuriousness; PROVED).** Let `L` be a labelling correct on terminals and Bellman-consistent at every non-terminal state. Then `L(s) = v(s)` whenever `v(s) != 0`. `L` may still label a drawn state `+1` (witness: two states each with the other as unique successor and no terminals; `L = (+1, -1)` is consistent, the value is `(0, 0)`).
*Proof.* Induction on the DTM `d` of a state with `v(s) = +-1`. `d = 0`: a mate, correct by (T). If `v(s) = +1` with DTM `d`, some move reaches `s'` with `v(s') = -1` and DTM `d-1`; by induction `L(s') = -1`, so the maximum in the Bellman equation is `+1`. If `v(s) = -1` with DTM `d`, every move reaches a state with `v = +1` and DTM at most `d-1`, all labelled `+1` by induction, so `L(s) = -1`. ∎
So the circularity danger discussed in the second note and in Pavlov's abstract has a precise shape: an all-draw labelling is *not* Bellman-consistent in a class that contains a mate (mates force their predecessors); what local consistency cannot exclude is a *spurious win* on a cycle, which is exactly what a ranking (R) excludes. Draw certificates need no well-foundedness; win certificates do.

**Definition (fortress).** A fortress is a trap with a short description: a predicate `pi` on states whose extension `F_pi` is closed, or whose maximal sub-trap `nu X.(F_pi intersect S(X))` has a short description of its exceptions. The Guid-Bratko detector (a winning evaluation that stops rising under iterative deepening) is bounded search for a ranking failing to find one; it is evidence of a trap, never a certificate. The discovery of `pi` may be heuristic or learned; the certificate is checked by closure alone.

## 5. Certified fortresses (FINITE-EXACT, `traps.py`)

All traps are for the defender Black. Boundary moves (captures, promotions) are certified by the exact tables of section 3 (KQvK, KRvK, KBvK, KNvK, KPvK) and by the dead-position rule; an uncertified boundary move counts as unsafe, which is sound. Candidate regions are enumerated by a predicate on squares (both sides to move, all legal placements), and moves leaving the region count as escapes. Mirror controls use the h-file.

**5.1 KPvK.**
- *Independent path.* The extractor on the whole class (`R` = all 331,352 states, defender Black) returns 108,788 states, exactly the set `{Black to move: v != -1} union {White to move: v != +1}` of the retrograde solve. The two algorithms share only the move generator.
- *The rook-pawn corner.* `R = {White pawn on the a-file, Black king on a8, b8, a7 or b7}` has 2,507 legal states (1,310 with Black to move); **all of them survive: the predicate is closed**, hence a fortress. Every state of `R` is drawn in the exact table (no drawn state of `R` needs to leave `R`; no state of `R` is lost). Widths: 2 stalemates (width 0, terminal draws), 41 states with a single safe move, 239 with two, 975 with three, 53 with four. The mechanism is the one every textbook gives: the bare king cannot be driven off a8/b8/a7/b7 because the only way to take b7 and b8 away from it (`Kb6` with the pawn on a7 or a6) stalemates it, and the pawn cannot promote on a square the king occupies or attacks. The h-file mirror gives 2,507 as well.
- *Other predicates.* "Black king directly in front of the pawn": 5,396 states, 5,330 drawn, maximal sub-trap 4,854 (the predicate is not closed; 476 drawn states can only be held by leaving it, e.g. by stepping back diagonally). "In front at distance at most 2": 9,820 / 9,742 / 8,024. "On the queening square": 5,536 / 4,612 / 870 (a poor invariant: the king usually has to leave the square to keep the opposition). "Inside the square of the pawn, ahead of it" (the rule of the square as a predicate): 116,372 / 77,736 / 64,222. The numbers measure how much of the drawn set each human rule *certifies by itself*; the rest of the drawn set needs the full table or a better predicate.

**5.2 KBPvK with the wrong bishop (dark bishop, a-pawn).**

Boundary: `Kxa-pawn` leads to KBvK (dead), `Kxbishop` to the KPvK table (where the corner is drawn, 5.1), and a promotion is certified only when Black can capture the new piece at once into a dead position (the one-ply escape oracle); everything else is uncertified. **All 72,720 states survive: the predicate is closed.** Widths: 215 stalemates, 3,027 states with one safe move, 14,099 with two, 21,350 with three, 1,314 with four. *The whole sub-class (dark bishop, a-pawn; 1,182,440 states), under the ASSUMPTION that a promotion the defender cannot capture at once into a dead position is lost:* 357,209 states (30.21%) are not lost for Black. **5.3 KRvKB: the safe corner and the wrong corner.** Black bishop on dark squares; the light corner a8 is the "safe" corner of the textbooks, the dark corner a1 the "wrong" one. `R` = all legal states with the Black king within Chebyshev distance 2 of the corner (9 king squares, 32 bishop squares, 64 x 64 for the White pieces, both sides to move): 1,604,648 states near a8 and 1,599,519 near a1. Result: the maximal trap near a8 has **1,237,588** states (77.1% of the region; 784,046 with Black to move and 453,542 with White to move; all nine king squares occur, from 130,834 (c6) to 145,472 (a8); 24 stalemates, otherwise widths from 1 to 20 safe options, mode 7); near a1 it has **214,316** (13.4%; 201,674 with Black to move but only 12,642 with White to move; 177,544 of the Black-to-move members have exactly one safe option). Mechanism (`traps_analysis.py`; closure of both traps re-verified with `verify_trap`, 0 violations). Of the 453,542 attacker-to-move members of the safe-corner trap, 347,214 are quiet (the rook is not attacked), 9,668 have the rook attacked but defended and 96,660 have the rook en prise; of its 784,046 defender-to-move members, 597,242 rely on internal safe moves only, 170,060 have both an internal safe move and a boundary draw, 16,720 only a boundary draw (capturing the rook or forcing a dead position), and 24 are stalemates. Of the 12,642 attacker-to-move members of the wrong-corner trap, 10,147 have the rook en prise and only 1,263 are quiet; of its 201,674 defender-to-move members, 161,410 have nothing but an immediate boundary draw (a rook capture). The wrong-corner trap is tactical, the safe-corner trap is positional: 347,214 against 1,263 quiet attacker-to-move states, a factor of 275. The quiet wrong-corner states include the bishop sitting on the corner square itself with the king beside it (e.g. `8/8/8/8/3R4/2K5/8/bk6 w`: the rook cannot take the bishop because the king recaptures, and a first-rank check is met by the king stepping to a2); by king square they number between 102 (c3) and 155 (a3, c1).

*Exceptions (`traps_krvkb_exceptions.py`).* The 367,060 states of the a8 region outside the trap split into 53,242 with Black to move (6.4% of the Black-to-move states of the region) and 313,818 with White to move (40.9%). Of the White-to-move exclusions, 217,198 have the bishop attacked (91.6% of all White-to-move states with the bishop attacked are excluded), and the single predicate "White to move, the rook attacks the bishop and the bishop is undefended" contains 213,896 excluded states against 3,422 trap members: one three-atom clause describes 58% of the complement with 1.6% error. The remaining exclusions are the tactical ones (forks, skewers and checks that win the bishop in more than one move); no single feature among king square, king distance, bishop distance or check isolates them. So the safe-corner fortress is, to first order, "the corner region minus the states where the bishop hangs with White to move".

**5.4 KRvKN: the region must be large enough for the defence (`traps_krvkn.py`).** Rook against knight is a draw in most positions when the defending king stays central and the knight close to it. Boundary: `RxN`, `KxN` lead to the KRvK table, `NxR`, `KxR` to a dead position (all certified). The predicate "Black king on d4, e4, d5 or e5" (1,421,936 states) keeps only 161,464 (11.4%), with 5,368 White-to-move members, none of them quiet (the rook is attacked in all of them) and 135,756 of the 156,096 Black-to-move members relying on an immediate rook capture: the four-square box is not a fortress, because the defence needs king moves that leave it. The predicate "Black king in the central 4x4 (c3-f6) and the knight within Chebyshev distance 2 of it" (2,178,344 states) keeps 1,769,224 (81.2%): 1,111,416 with Black to move (1,087,132 with an internal safe move, median width 4, maximum 13) and 657,808 with White to move, 542,280 of them quiet; 307,696 of the White-to-move members have the knight adjacent to the king and 350,112 at distance two. Closure re-verified (0 violations). The corner control (king on a1) keeps 8.9%, nothing quiet. A fortress predicate must contain the defender's manoeuvring room; the textbook rule, taken with its second clause, is closed to first order, and the 19% it loses is the usual tactical rim (the knight hanging or forkable with White to move).

**What the four computations say together.** A human fortress rule is a predicate; the predicate is a certificate iff it is closed; when it is not, the extractor returns its largest closed part, and the gap between that part and the drawn set is exactly the part of the defence that needs a reachability argument (a ranking for the defender's *escape* into the trap). The two layers compose: "reach the corner" (ranking) then "stay in the corner" (trap).

## 6. Optionality (Theorem 4 and `optionality.py`)

**Theorem 4 (the optionality asymmetry; PROVED).** (a) Let `s` have `v_D(s) >= 0`. Any policy for `D` that always chooses a value-preserving move (one leading to a state with `v_D >= 0`) guarantees at least a draw, whatever secondary criterion orders those moves: the drawn set is a trap (Theorem 3b). (b) Let `v_D(s) = +1`. A policy that always chooses a win-preserving move need not win: the play may cycle inside the winning set, which in rule-chess is a draw by repetition or by the 50-move rule. (c) A policy that always chooses a `DTM`-decreasing move wins; so does any tie-break (optionality included) *among* `DTM`-optimal moves. *Proof.* (a) is Theorem 3(a). (b) by the witnesses below. (c) `DTM` is a ranking. ∎

**Witnesses (FINITE-EXACT).** White to move in every winning state of KRvK (175,168 states) and KQvK (144,508); Black defends `DTM`-optimally; ties are broken deterministically. Secondary scores for a candidate move: `O1` (geometric) = the number of legal White moves at White's next turn, minimised over Black's replies; `O2` (strategic) = the number of win-preserving White moves at White's next turn, minimised over Black's replies.
- KRvK, `O1` among win-preserving moves: mated from 1,720 states, cycled from 173,448; the longest successful line is 5 plies. First cycling start: `8/8/8/8/8/8/8/k1KR4 w` (mate in two exists).
- KRvK, `O2` among win-preserving moves: mated from 2,817 states, cycled from 172,351; the longest successful line is 11 plies; first cycling start `8/8/8/8/8/8/8/k3KR2 w`.
- KRvK, `O2` among `DTM`-non-increasing moves (weak progress): mated from 41,823 states, cycled from 133,345 (longest successful line 49 plies; first cycling start `8/8/8/8/8/3K4/1R6/k7 w`): even forbidding regress leaves equal-DTM cycles.
- KRvK, `O2` among `DTM`-optimal moves: mated from all 175,168 states (longest line 31 plies, the DTM maximum), as Theorem 4(c) says.
- KQvK: `O1` among win-preserving moves: 3,365 mated, 141,143 cycled (longest line 11 plies); `O2`: 7,406 mated, 137,102 cycled (longest 21 plies); first cycling start for both `8/8/8/8/8/8/8/k2KQ3 w`; `O2` among `DTM`-optimal moves: all 144,508 mated (longest 19 plies); weak progress: 34,719 mated, 109,789 cycled (longest 37 plies).
- Defender: in KPvK, from all 70,420 drawn Black-to-move states, the greedy defender (among drawing moves, maximise the number of drawing replies next turn, against a White policy that minimises it) loses 0 games, as Theorem 4(a) requires.
The lesson for the first note's "outcome first, optionality second": the rule is exactly right for the side holding a draw (co-inductive goal) and exactly wrong for the side trying to win (inductive goal), where the secondary criterion must sit *below the ranking*, not below the value.

## 7. The pawn mask is digital topology (`visibility.py`)

For a pawn set `P` the third note defines rook, bishop, queen visibility `V^P_X(u, v)` (aligned, no pawn strictly between) and knight, king, pawn-attack layers.

**Lemma V1 (ray components = step components; PROVED, checked on 300 random masks x 3 piece types).** Two empty squares are joined by a chain of unobstructed rook rays iff they are joined by a chain of orthogonal single steps through empty squares; the same for bishop rays and diagonal steps, queen rays and king steps. *Proof.* A ray is a chain of steps through its interior squares, which are empty by definition; conversely a step is a ray. ∎ Hence: rook components = 4-connected components of `S \ P`; bishop components of colour `c` = diagonal-step components of `(colour c) \ P`; queen components = 8-connected components. A "visibility cut" for the rook is an 8-connected curve of pawns (the digital Jordan theorem); for the queen a 4-connected one; for a bishop a cut inside one colour.

**Lemma V2 (colour locality; PROVED, checked).** The bishop graph of colour `c` depends only on `P intersect (colour c)`: a ray between two squares of colour `c` passes through squares of colour `c` only.

**Census (FINITE-EXACT; pawns on ranks 2-7).** The largest region sealable with `p` pawns (size of the second-largest component): rook `0, 0, 1, 2` for `p = 1..4` (e.g. a2 b3 a4 seals a3); dark bishop `1, 2, 5, 14` (b2 seals a1; d2 d4 e5 e7 seal 14 dark squares); queen `0` up to `p = 4` (an 8-connected cut of a rank-2..7 square needs 5 pawns). The restriction of pawns to ranks 2-7 is what makes corners expensive for the rook: a8 needs a7 and b8, and b8 is not a pawn square.
Named structures: initial position: rook `[32, 8, 8]` (the four middle ranks; rank 1; rank 8), each bishop colour `[16, 1 x 8]` (every back-rank square is sealed by the pawn in front of it), queen `[32, 8, 8]`. French advance (d4/e5 vs d5/e6): rook `[48]`, each bishop `[20, 1, 1, 1, 1]`. Carlsbad (White a2 b2 d4 e3 f2 g2 h2, Black a7 b7 c6 d5 e6 f7 g7 h7): rook `[23, 14, 12]`, dark bishop `[22, 1, 1, 1]`, light bishop `[18, 3, 1, 1, 1]`, queen `[49]`. The full a1-h8 diagonal: rook `[28, 28]`, dark bishop `[12, 12]`, light bishop `[32]`, queen `[56]`.

**How this feeds the certificates.** By Corollary 3.2 the mask is constant on a stratum, so a barrier predicate ("the rook's component does not contain the king's zone") is a legitimate conjunct of a candidate region; closure must then be checked against the stratum-leaving moves (pawn pushes and captures), which Theorem 2 routes to lower strata. Distances are *not* preserved by Lemma V1 (a rook crosses an open rank in one move), only components.

## 8. The symmetric diagonal and the tempo (Niche; CITED data)

**Proposition 2.1 (value symmetries; PROVED).** (a) `sigma` = rank reflection + colour swap + side swap + swap of castling rights + reflection of the en-passant square is an involution on legal states with `v(sigma s) = v(s)` (mover's view), hence `V(sigma s) = -V(s)`. (b) For states without castling rights the file reflection `rho` preserves `v`, and `{1, rho, sigma, rho sigma}` is a Klein four-group of value symmetries; `rho sigma` is the 180-degree rotation with colour swap. (c) `rho sigma` is not a symmetry of states with castling rights: it moves a king with rights to the d-file, where no castling right is legal. In particular the second note's `sigma` ("exchange colours, rotate by 180 degrees") does not fix the initial position; the correct involution fixing it is (a). (d) For pawnless states without castling rights all eight board isometries composed with the colour swap are value-negating.

**Definition.** A board `B` fixed by some value-negating involution `tau` has a **tempo** `t(B) = v(B, White to move) = -v(B, Black to move) in {+1, 0, -1}`; `t = -1` is a mutual zugzwang. A0 says `t(s0) >= 0`; section 2.3 says `t >= 0` holds for every symmetric board of chess-with-passing.

**Census (CITED: Syzygy WDL via lichess; all responses archived).** For the 4-man classes KPvKP, KRvKR, KQvKQ, KBvKB, KNvKN and the involutions `sigma` (rank reflection) and `rho sigma` (rotation), all legal boards fixed by the involution:

| class | involution | legal symmetric boards | representatives (file reflection) | evaluated | mover wins | draw | mutual zugzwang |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| KPvKP | rank reflection | 2,184 | 1,092 | all 1,092 | 450 | 642 | 0 |
| KPvKP | rotation | 2,716 | 1,358 | sample 300 | 83 | 217 | 0 |
| KRvKR | rank reflection | 2,496 | 1,248 | sample 200 | 167 | 33 | 0 |
| KQvKQ | rank reflection | 2,096 | 1,048 | sample 200 | 175 | 25 | 0 |

The exhaustive row is the one that matters for the axiom's shape: with kings and pawns only, on the rank-reflection diagonal (the pawns face each other on the same files), the mover never loses, and wins in 41% of the boards; the tempo is a resource and never a liability there. The seeded samples (seed 2026) of the rotation diagonal of KPvKP (300 of 1,358 representatives; the pawns stand on mirrored files and can attack each other) and of the rank diagonals of KRvKR (200 of 1,248) and KQvKQ (200 of 1,048) show no mutual zugzwang either, and a mover's winning share that grows with the range of the material: 41% (KPvKP rank, exhaustive), 28% (KPvKP rotation sample), 84% (KRvKR sample), 88% (KQvKQ sample). On a symmetric board the tempo converts into a win far more often with long-range pieces, where the first check or skewer decides; with pawns it is mostly a draw, and never a liability in the data. The known reciprocal zugzwangs of pawn endings (the trebuchet: White Kd5 Pe4, Black Kf4 Pe5) are point-symmetric about the midpoint of the two pawns, not about the board's centre, so they lie on no diagonal; the question whether a reciprocal zugzwang can be made rank-symmetric with more pawns is C1 below. Lichess throttled the census (HTTP 429 after a few hundred requests per minute), so only KPvKP was exhausted; the other rows are seeded samples and are typed NUMERICAL.

Exhaustive in KPvKP (0 of 2,184), untested with two or more pawns per side; KPPvKPP on the rank diagonal (about 54,000 boards) is the cheapest decisive test. ## 9. The material lattice and the audit of the three pasted notes

**Lattice counts (FINITE-EXACT, `strata_census.py`).** Per side without promotion: `(n, r) in {0,1,2}^2`, bishops in `{none, light, dark, both}`, `q in {0,1}`: 72 inventories; times pawn counts 0..8: 648; joint 419,904; capture edges in the joint lattice 3,125,952 (the second note's 72 / 576 / 331,776 / 648 / 419,904 are confirmed). These count the lattice before retrograde legality; the capture DAG has height 30 and a capture-or-promotion chain has length at most 46.

**Corrections to the pasted notes.**
1. *The symmetry.* Not the 180-degree rotation (Proposition 2.1c) but the rank reflection; the rotation is a symmetry only without castling rights.
2. *The defender clause.* "Defender to move implies some move into F" excludes stalemates and therefore the stalemate fortresses (the 2 and 215 width-0 members of 5.1 and 5.2). The clause must read "stalemate, or some move into F".
3. *Promotion as an absorbing boundary* (second note's first model) is unnecessary and loses the queen-capture escapes that make 5.2 work; promotion is a `Phi`-decreasing boundary move like a capture (Theorem 1).
4. *"Quiet moves can cycle indefinitely within a material universe."* Pawn pushes are quiet and change nothing material, yet they are irreversible; only the `Phi`-preserving moves cycle (Theorem 1). The right unit is the stratum, not the material class (Theorem 2 and the KPvK count: 48 strata).
5. *The false fixed point.* Precisely one-sided (Proposition 4.5): local consistency can over-claim wins, never under-claim them; the needed extra object is a ranking.
6. *"At least draw" versus "exact draw".* Confirmed: a trap certifies `v_D >= 0`; an exact draw needs a trap for each side.
7. *Optionality as a tie-breaker below the outcome.* Correct for the defender, wrong for the attacker (Theorem 4 and the 173,448 cycling starts); the tie-break belongs below the ranking.
8. *The visibility atlas.* Components are digital topology (Lemma V1); "cuts" are separating curves; the knight layer is unaffected by pawns and the bishop layer of one colour by pawns of the other (Lemma V2).
9. *The 168 knight edges, the diagonal lengths and the ring sizes* are confirmed (THM-4550 Proposition 1.3 and the two 2026-10-06 notes).
10. *Pavlov's preprint* exists (arXiv:2604.07907; the abstract reports 517 endgames up to six pieces checked against full retrograde); the second note's description matches the abstract. The Stockfish/Syzygy and Guid-Bratko descriptions were not re-verified beyond the citations above.
