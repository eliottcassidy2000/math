## 1. Exact statement and repaired necessity proof

For odd `m>=3`, `k>=1`, and `b=±1`, let

`E_k^b(m)={(x modm, (3x+b)2^(-k) modm): x odd}`.

Every residue modulo `m` has an odd representative. For every integer
`t>=0`,

`E_(k+t)^-(m) = {(-x,y):(x,y) in E_k^+(m)}`

**if and only if** `2^t=-1 modm`.

Sufficiency follows for each source because

`(3(-x)-1)2^(-(k+t))=-(3x+1)2^(-k)2^(-t)`.

For necessity, use source residue **zero**, represented by odd integer
`x=m`. The equality of tables forces
`-2^(-(k+t))=2^(-k) modm`; multiply by the unit `2^(k+t)` to obtain
`2^t=-1 modm`. This works whether or not `3|m`.

The incoming sentence that derives this by comparing two sources is
insufficient as written: it only compares slopes and introduces a factor of
three. When `m=3`, the slopes are identically zero for all shifts, whereas
the constant terms forbid the shift `t=0`. The source-zero argument repairs
the proof without changing the theorem.

If a shift exists, the least positive one is `ord_m(2)/2`: within the cyclic
subgroup generated by two, `-1` is its unique order-two element. All shifts
are the odd multiples of this half-order. This is exactly the incoming
halving-shift interpretation of odd-modulus tables, with proper credit to
KP-S4; it complements rather than replaces the inverse-fibre lift theorem.

The modulus **one** is a separate trivial boundary. Its tables are all
`{(0, 0)}`, so every shift gives equality; `-1=1` and an even-order/half-order
description is not appropriate. The nontrivial theorem must state `m>=3`.

## 2. Complete prime-factor compatibility criterion

Write `m=product p_i^e_i` with distinct odd primes, and
`d_i=ord_(p_i^e_i)(2)=2^s_i u_i`, where `u_i` is odd.

**Theorem.** A halving shift exists if and only if all values

`v2(ord_(p_i)(2))`

are equal to one common **positive** integer `s`.

**Prime-power stability.** Reduction modulo `p` forces `ord_p(2)` to divide
`ord_(p^e)(2)`. Conversely if `2^d=1 modp`, repeated binomial lifting shows
`2^(d p^(e-1))=1 modp^e`. Hence the quotient of these two orders is a power
of the odd prime `p`, so their two-parts agree. This proves that the criterion
can use prime orders, with no prime-power exception or squarefreeness
assumption.

**Local condition.** Modulo an odd prime power, the only square roots of one
are `±1`: the two factors of `(a-1)(a+1)` have gcd dividing two, so all the
prime-power divisibility must lie in one factor. Consequently `-1` belongs
to the subgroup generated by two exactly when `d_i` is even, and then

`2^t=-1 mod p_i^e_i  iff  t=d_i/2 mod d_i`.

Every such `t` has `v2(t)=s_i-1`. A common shift therefore requires all the
`s_i` to be the same positive integer.

**Sufficiency and an explicit least shift.** Suppose all `s_i=s>0`, and put

`U=lcm(u_1,...,u_r)`, `t=2^(s-1) U`.

Since `U/u_i` is odd, `t` is an odd multiple of every `d_i/2`. It therefore
gives `2^t=-1` at every prime-power factor, hence modulo `m` by CRT. Also
`ord_m(2)=lcm(d_i)=2^s U`, so the constructed shift is exactly half the
global order and is least positive. This completes the all-parameter proof.

Examples: modulo 3 and 5 the local shifts exist, but their order two-parts are
respectively 2 and 4; hence modulo 15 they cannot synchronize. Modulo 7 the
order is3, so no local shift exists. Modulo 33, the prime orders 2 and 10 share
two-part 2, and `t=5` works. Modulo 65, the orders 4 and 12 share two-part 4, and
`t=6` works. Prime powers retain each prime's compatibility class.

## 3. Distinct operation from the exact inverse-fibre alternation

The KP-S4 operation changes **residue source to its negative**, sheet to
its opposite, and halving label from `k` to `k+t`; the target is retained
only **modulo `m`**, and only when the order criterion holds.

The inverse-fibre operation in the original audited packet is instead

`(n,b,a,q)->(2n+b,-b,a+1,q)`.

It retains an **exact integer** target for every signed odd source, and its
source transformation is affine rather than negation. It works independently
of the modulus. These are not competing descriptions of one operation.

Nor does the shifted modular table certify the source height or valuation.
For example, `U_+(1)=1` has valuation 2. Modulo 3 the incoming shift is1, so
its shifted-minus row has source residue `-1=2`, label 3 and target residue 1.
But the literal integer source `-1` on the minus sheet has valuation 2 and
target `-1`, not valuation 3. An authenticated integer realization of that
shifted modular row is `n=35`, since `3*35-1=8*13`; it has the required
source and target residues, but not the original exact endpoint 1. By contrast
the inverse-fibre lift takes `(1,+1)` to `(3,-1)` with exact target 1 and
valuation 3. This is why the extra binary guard and signed carry remain
essential when joining the two interpretations.
