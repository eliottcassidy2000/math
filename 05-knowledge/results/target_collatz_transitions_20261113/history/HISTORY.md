## 1. An old Pell half-step whose arithmetic orbit leaves its object class

The October19
[Gold/Pell half-step](../../gold_pell_collatz_halfstep_20261019.md)
has the integer equality curve

    Q(m,n)=n²−mn−(m−1)(m−2)=0.

Its positive solutions are advanced by R(m,n)=(2m+3n−3,3m+5n−6),
but the exact integer square root S(m,n)=(3−m−n,3−m−2n) sends positive
dimensions to negative formal dimensions. Under X=5m−6,Y=2n−m, S is
multiplication by−φ² on X+Y√5. The finite mod11 carrier even gives a
literal conjugacy with a ten-point rational Collatz carrier. It does
NOT make negative dimensions into posets or prove convergence of positive
integer Collatz trajectories.

**Typed transfer.** Source: a norm-preserving arithmetic action with an
object-entry chamber. Target: the mirrored tournament norm
(6p+2h)²−5h², with actual observations h=H(M),p=P_M(2). Map: transport
the unit matrix to(x,h). Preserved predicate: the norm value. Destroyed
information if one follows only that orbit: positivity and the physical
capacity inequality p≥(|M|+1)h. Sidecar: module rows and order. Cheap
test: start from the singleton's(x,h)=(14,1), target191.

**New outcome.** [NORM_CHAMBER§2](NORM_CHAMBER.md) proves that every
nontrivial integral norm-one unit leaves x/h≥14 permanently at that
step, even allowing signs and conjugation; every such transformed
absolute slope is less than3. Two powers of the basic unit produce

## 2. Berggren's same addresses do not transfer the missing host edge

The October19
[square-sum/Berggren absorber bijection](../../square_sum_berggren_absorber_bijection_20261019.md)
classifies all positive integer solutions of a²+b²=c²+1 into three
ternary trees with roots(1,1,1),(2,1,2),(1,2,2). The matrices are the
same A,B,C used for primitive Pythagorean triples. Their unique positive
inverse is selected by signs, and strictly lowers c. This is an exact
branch-address correspondence between two different quadratic shells.

For a,b>1 and x+y=t², its actual square-sum operation is

    u=a²(x+y)−x, v=b²(x+y)−y,
    u−v  ↦  u−x−y−v.

Every new edge has square sum because the coefficient triple has norm1.
The boundary rays with a=1 or b=1 are not legal absorbers: a host becomes
one of the inserted vertices. Even off those rays the address theorem
does not prove the host edge u−v lies in a given Hamiltonian backbone.

**Typed transfer.** Source: a complete arithmetic address tree with a
separate host-edge condition. Target: the complete Bezout address line
for a desired tournament correction. Map: enumerate arithmetic addresses
only after fixing the intended response equation. Preserved predicate:
the exact local algebraic identity. Lost predicate: existence of the
required host, here an actual tournament with the specified(H,P2).
Sidecar: literal module and legal external incidences. Cheap test:
apply the HP-cut inequality to the proposed partner modules.

**New outcome.** [FAREY_PARTNERS§3](FAREY_PARTNERS.md) proves that the
infinitely many positive Bezout solutions for a fixed left module yield
only finitely many possible actual right modules. Their orders have an
explicit bound. This is the target analogue of retaining the host-edge
condition in the absorber construction, with an exact finite consequence
rather than a warning alone.

## 5. The repaired question

The symmetric norm chart is proved unable to reach ANY7p with p prime
other than7: inert prime7 would have even valuation. An actual polarized
pair TT2,TT1 reaches371=7·53 with a primitive marked state, showing
exactly which equality constraint had caused that obstruction. The new

Neither a complete quadratic address tree, a finite rational Collatz
conjugacy, nor the infinite integer Bezout line supplies the missing
physical entries. The useful next obligation is a changing-module rule
that realizes successive determinant±1 pairs while retaining enough
target choice. That obligation remains open; the present work makes its
local equation and each fixed-source search finite and concrete.
