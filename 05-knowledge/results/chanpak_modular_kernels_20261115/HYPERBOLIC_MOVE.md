# Hyperbolic kernels: a reflected cone and normalized full-band growth

**Proved:** fixed-core arithmetic inverse; an obstruction to cancellation and to filling a fractional band by subtracting from a dyadic baseline; a physical reflected cone; and a different, fixed-core construction with `Theta(K)` exact counts in every fixed open fractional subband of the height-`K` band. **Finite-exact:** module profile census through order 9. No universal integer placement or new exclusion from the unrestricted tournament spectrum is claimed.

## 1. Positivity does not disappear in the factored coordinates

Use the inherited THM-5133 two-visit kernel, and write its nonnegative integer visit matrix as
`G=[[a,b],[c,d]]` with `a,d>0` and `D=bc−ad>0`.
For nonempty modules `A,B` write `h=H(A), p=P_2(A)` and `h'=H(B),q=P_2(B)`, where `P_2` counts ordered pairs of **nonempty** paths. Then

    N = a h h' + b h q + c p h' + d p q
    dN = (d p+b h)(d q+c h') − D h h'.

Here `a>0` is forced by the existence of a Hamiltonian path in the kernel; `b,c>0` follows from `D>0`. The inherited factorization applies only when all visits involving either port are at most two and `d>0`.

For `n=|A|,m=|B|`, there are `(n−1)h` distinct two-path covers made by splitting a Hamiltonian path at one of its edges: the ordered pair uniquely determines the uncut path and the split. Thus

    p≥(n−1)h, q≥(m−1)h'.
    (D h h')/[(d p+b h)(d q+c h')]
        ≤ D/[(b+d(n−1))(c+d(m−1))].

In particular the upper bound is `D/(bc)<1`, attained by two singletons, and tends to zero as either core grows. So a *fixed kernel* cannot make large counts by nearly canceling its two determinant terms. In the language of [Wave 33 §5 P2](https://github.com/eliottcassidy2000/math/blob/45f0be3a7425b629a7f6eab8817118dc4c9d7414/05-knowledge/results/chanpak_waves_20261113/README.md#wave-33-a-survey-of-tangential-prior-work-and-what-it-points-to), `L/h-b_*/a_* ≥ ad/c+d(n−1)`, with `L=dp+bh`, `a_*=dq+ch'`, and `b_*=Dh'`.

**Dyadic baseline corollary.** Restrict any finite bank of these kernels to pairs for which the factored baseline `(dp+bh)(dq+ch')/d` is a power of 2. This class of outputs has density zero and for each `δ>0` no sufficiently large output lies in any interval `[2^{K-1},(1−δ)2^K]` *strictly below its own dyadic baseline* `2^K`. More quantitatively, if `k=max(n,m)`, then `N≤(2k+s)!` for `s` the fixed quotient order, so `k=Ω(log N/log log N)`. The relative width below its baseline is therefore `O(log log N/log N)`. Summing the interval lengths over baseline powers up to `2X` proves `O(X log log X/log X)` support up to `X` (small powers once below `sqrt X` have only `O(sqrt X)` total support). This concerns the **dyadic-factorized subclass**, not hyperbolic kernels generally. An analogous one-sided bound when using `Q=P_2+2H` as the left coordinate needs no fixed kernel: any physical `N=alpha Q-eta h` with `eta>0` has `eta<2alpha`, hence the relative deficit from `alpha Q` is (<2/(n+1)).

## 2. Exact fixed-core inverse

Fix `B` and set `U=ah'+bq`, `V=ch'+dq`. Then `N=Uh+Vp` with `U,V>0`. By complementary nonempty subsets, `p` is even. Thus solve `Uh≡N (mod 2V)`; if `gcd(U,2V)` divides `N`, there is exactly one residue for `h` modulo `2V/gcd(U,2V)`. For each residue candidate use `p=(N−Uh)/V`. If `N<U`, impossible. Otherwise the module order is bounded by

    n ≤ floor(log_2(2+floor((N−U)/V))).

Indeed all `2^n−2` nonempty proper subsets support at least one path on both sides, and `h≥1`. At each order `n` a further bound is `h≤min((N−V(2^n−2))/U, N/(U+V(n−1)))`. A candidate is a physical inverse exactly when the profile appears in the resulting finite profile catalogue; the catalogue is generated by adding a vertex in all orientations, retaining one canonical class at each level. This is a terminating exact characterization, not an efficient all-scale profile supply and not a positivity claim. Use `inverse.py` for the finite catalogue commensurate with the census.

## 3. Physical reflected cone

The literal order-8 marked pair from [THM-5130 (D4)](https://github.com/eliottcassidy2000/math/blob/main/01-canon/theorems/THM-5130-sign-flip-towers-gadget-transfer-law-and-seeds-of-both-signs.md) is an anti-seed with offset `−1` and slope `1024`. Its closing law accepts **any** module, not just transitive ones. With `A ⇒ TT_t` as module and `Q(A)=P_2(A)+2H(A)`, multiplicativity of `Q` under ordered joins gives

    H(physical closer with A ⇒ TT_t) = 2^{10+t} Q(A) − H(A), t≥0.

Here `t=0` simply means the nonempty module `A` without padding. If `Q(A)=2^j`, the operation therefore sends every eligible seed `h=H(A)` to `2^K−h` for all `K≥j+10`. All such values begin in `11` in binary, since `Q(A)≥2h`, and the deficit is at most `2^{K-11}`. For example the known `(H,Q)=(5,32)` gives every `2^K−5` with `K≥15`. The existing THM-5109 bank `(5,5),(19,8),(217,12),(223,12),(599,13)` of pairs `(H,v_2(Q))` works by arbitrary ordered products; the exact order-9 additions appear in CENSUS.txt. The reflected construction is the module substitution of the old anti-seed, not a new anti-seed.

Moreover the exact dyadic-offset support at height `K` is the set of products of the `H(C)` from strong modules with `Q(C)=2^{j(C)}`, with total `sum j(C)≤K−10`, allowing products of singletons and excluding the empty tournament. Every tournament is an ordered join of strong components; `Q` multiplicative forces each component to have dyadic `Q`, and the converse follows by taking the ordered join. In particular each required component has order at most `K−10` as `Q(C)≥2^{|C|}`. This is a reusable finite inverse for exact offsets, and explains why merely taking more products from a fixed bank never fills the band: one gets at most a polynomial in `K` many exponent vectors.

## 4. Different physical baseline: fill the *geometry*, not the odd lattice

Use the first hyperbolic kernel `N=3Q(A)Q(B)-H(A)H(B)` of THM-5133 and fix `B` to be a singleton. The literal five-vertex block `C` with canonical code `548` (ascending lexicographic unordered vertex pairs; bit 1 means the smaller vertex beats the larger) has `H(C)=11,Q(C)=100`. Let `A=C ⇒ ... ⇒ C ⇒ TT_t` with `j` copies, not empty. This realizes

    N(j,t) = 6·100^j·2^t − 11^j.

Every `j≥1` gives counts coprime to `2·3·5·11`: the center is divisible by 2,3,5 and coprime to 11, while the offset is a power of 11. In particular occupancy of the geometric band need not come from obvious multiples of 3. This construction exhibits no blanket primality claim.

Fix an arbitrary nonempty open subinterval `I ⊂ (1,2)`. For each large `K`, take integer `1≤j≤K/(4 log_2 100)` and choose the unique `t` such that `6·100^j2^t/2^K∈[1,2)`. Then `t≥3K/4−O(1)`, the center exceeds `2^K` by at least `2^t` (not equal, since it has factor 3), and the offset is `<2^{K/4}`. This stays strictly in the same band and the normalized correction tends uniformly to zero. On the logarithmic axis the normalized centers are the irrational rotations `{log_2 6+j log_2 100}`. Every block of sufficiently many consecutive indices contains an index in any fixed open interval: a finite initial segment of the irrational orbit is an epsilon-net, and all translations of the segment preserve its mesh. Applying this to a closed subinterval with interior strictly inside `log_2(I)` supplies `Ω_I(K)` counts in `2^K I`, provided they are distinct. Distinctness here follows from the congruences `N(j,t)≡-11^j (mod 2^t)`: if distinct indices collide then `2^{min(t,t')}` divides `11^{|j−j'|}−1`. The left exponent is linear in `K` but `v_2(11^r−1)=1` for odd `r` and `2+v_2(r)` for even `r`, by factoring successive squares. This is impossible for large `K`.

Conversely there are only `O(K)` pairs in any height-`K` band: `N(j,t)` is bounded below by five sixths of its center, so `j=O(K)`, and for fixed `j` there are at most two `t` in that band. Summing heights gives `Theta((log X)^2)` distinct values at most `X`. Thus the normalized counts become dense in *every* fixed fractional subband, even on a prime-compatible family, but remain sparse in exact absolute counts. This is a specific distinction between an asymptotically full geometric band and the unresolved absolute placement lemma.

**Explicit mesh without a deep approximation theorem.** For each `m≥2`, pigeonhole the first `m+1` multiples of `θ=log_2 100` to find `1≤q≤m` with `δ=||qθ||≤1/m`. Since `100^q` and the nearest-exponent power of 2 differ by a nonzero integer and are within a fixed ratio, `δ≥c100^{-q}≥c100^{-m}` for a fixed `c>0` (mean value theorem for logarithms). The multiples of the step `qθ` up to `floor(1/δ)` form a circular `δ`-net; the largest required index is at most `C m100^m`. Taking `m=floor(log_{100} K/2)` fits inside the above linear bound for `j`, even after starting at `j=1`. Consequently there is a normalized max-gap bound `O(1/log K)`, with the same order for edge gaps at 1 and 2, and the subtraction changes normalized values only exponentially little. The absolute mesh is still enormous: there is also a gap of size `Ω(2^K/K)` for this palette by the `O(K)` upper bound. This gives explicit scales for what a separate window-correction mechanism would have to improve.
