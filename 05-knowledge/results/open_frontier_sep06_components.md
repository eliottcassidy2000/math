## 3. Compact containment forces strict prefix inequalities

**Lemma 2.** Let K be a nonempty finite union of closed intervals and
points, and let O be a finite union of disjoint open intervals. Assume
K is contained in O and has positive total length. If ell and c are their
respective sorted component lengths, padded by zeros, then

    P_k(ell)<P_k(c) for every k>=1.                       (1)

This is stated on an interval or circle after a cut outside O. In the LRC
consumer zero is outside every relevant cross-comb and body safe set.

**Proof.** Select up to k positive-length components of K that give
P_k(ell). Each lies in one component of O. Group the selected intervals
by that containing component. Their total length in each used component
is strictly smaller than its open interval length: finitely many compact
intervals contained in an open interval leave positive endpoint slack.
At most k open components are used. Summing gives a strict upper bound
by the sum of those lengths, itself no greater than P_k(c). If k exceeds
the number of positive K components, use all of them; at least one is
positive, so strictness is retained. Isolated points never manufacture
positive length or join two different components.

This lemma need not reverse. Length lists lose where the intervals lie.
It is stronger than separate inequalities for total length and maximum.

    P_k(T_g(a))=P_r(a)+(s/g)a_(r+1).                     (2)
