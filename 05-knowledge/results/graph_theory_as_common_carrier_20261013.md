## 2. The Cayley carrier: THM-4631

For `p` an odd prime let `T_p(n) = n/2` or `(pn+1)/2`, `theta_p = log2 p`, and let `M_(j,k)(s)` be THM-4629's compressed transfer operator on the class means modulo `2^j p^k` with `p` in place of `3` (`u = zeta^alpha e^(-s)`, `v = zeta^(alpha+beta) e^(s(theta_p - 1))`). Then for every `j, k` and every character

`det(I - M_(j,k)(s)) = [1 - u/2 - v/2] prod_(i=1)^(k) [1 - (u/2)^(L_i)]^(phi(p^i)/L_i)`, `L_i = ord_(p^i)(2)`.

The proof is THM-4629's filtration argument with `p` for `3`: the odd branch lowers `p`-adic depth, the halving branch acts on the graded piece of depth `i` as the pullback of `rho -> rho 2^(-1)`, which on the additive characters of exact conductor `p^i` is the permutation `a -> a 2^(-1)` of `(Z/p^i)^x`, i.e. the Cayley graph `Cay((Z/p^i)^x, {2^(-1)})`: `[(Z/p^i)^x : <2>]` disjoint directed cycles of length `L_i`, each contributing `1 - (u/2)^(L_i)`; the 2-adic averaging contributes the even density `1/2` and nothing else (section 5). Verified symbolically in 12 cases, by exact rational determinants at three random points in 25 cases to size 529 (`p = 7` at `k = 3` with three doubled levels, `p = 23` at `k = 2`), numerically in 8 more including `1093` and `3511` (`graph_carriers_level_factorisation_20261013.out`, Part 1).

What the factorisation unifies:

- **The silence of THM-4629.** Every extra mode of every finite compression sits at `|u/2| = 1`, decay `1/2` per octave, for every `p`: the `p`-adic hierarchy is spectrally silent because its graded pieces are permutation matrices scaled by the even density. The arithmetic of `p` enters only through multiplicities.
- **Artin.** The multiplicity is `1` at every level iff `2` is a primitive root modulo `p^2`. Below `100` this holds for `p = 3, 5, 11, 13, 19, 29, 37, 53, 59, 61, 67, 83` (12 of 24; Artin's constant `0.3739558` predicts `9.0` on average, Part 2). The repository's "Artin coordinate" of the coalescence atlas (`collatz_coalescence_20261004_proof_strategies_and_bold_predictions.md`) and the Artin audit of S18 are the same object seen as the simplicity of the fast spectrum.
- **Wieferich.** For `1093` and `3511` (`2^(p-1) = 1 mod p^2`) the order of `2` does not grow from `p` to `p^2`, so the level-2 multiplicity is `p` times the level-1 one: `3 -> 3279` and `2 -> 7022`. For every other prime the orders are `L_1 p^(i-1)` and the multiplicity is constant in `i` (Part 2, `p < 100`).
- **Paley.** `ord_7(2) = 3`, `<2> = {1, 2, 4}` is the subgroup of squares, and the two doubling cycles `{1,4,2}` and `{3,5,6}` are the out- and in-neighbourhood of `0` in the Paley tournament on seven vertices and the two halves of the S17 code; the level-1 factor is `(1 - (u/2)^3)^2`. The primes with exactly two cycles are those with `ord_p(2) = (p-1)/2`: `7, 17, 23, 41, 47, 71, 79, 97` below `100`, and the Paley tournament exists for those congruent to `3` modulo `4`: `7, 23, 47, 71, 79`. The multiplicity `8` at `p = 73` and `89`, `6` at `31`, `3` at `43` are the other indices of `<2>`.
- **Mersenne clocks.** `p | 2^n - 1` iff `L_1 | n`: the cycle lengths of the Cayley carrier are the periods of the unit-minus-one clocks of the S15 and S18 notes (`mersenne` threads), now read as the lengths of the fast-mode cycles.

## 3. The Farey carrier: the characters of THM-4629 are the colours of the Farey graph

**The modes and the Farey graph.** THM-4629 has a mode at the frequency `Q/m` for every pair `(Q, P)` with `P` the integer of the right residue nearest to `Q theta`, with decay `exp(-4 pi^2 theta_f^2/(2 - theta)^3)`, `theta_f = (Q theta - P)/m`; the census lines are the pairs with `|Q theta - P| < 0.07`, a two-dimensional set in `(Q, P)` (a Bohr set), whereas the geodesic of `log2 3` in the Farey tessellation is one-dimensional. The relation is this. (i) A primitive pair `(Q, P)` is the vertex `P/Q` of the Farey graph (vertices the reduced fractions and `1/0`, edges the pairs with `|PQ' - P'Q| = 1`); a non-primitive pair `j (Q, P)` is the `j`-th unfolding of the primitive line `(Q, P)` (HYP-9260 statement 3; for `m = 2` the odd multiples keep the character and the even ones carry the trivial class) and is not a Farey vertex. (ii) The record lines, those whose defect `|Q theta - P|` is smaller than at every smaller `Q`, are exactly the convergents `P_n/Q_n` with `n >= 1`: `2/1, 3/2, 8/5, 19/12, 65/41, 84/53, 485/306, 1054/665, ...` (Lagrange's theorem on best approximations of the second kind, CITED from memory; checked to `Q <= 20000` in `graph_carriers_line_classification_20261013.out`), and a primitive pair with `|Q theta - P| < 1/(2Q)` is a convergent (Legendre, CITED). (iii) In the Farey tessellation the geodesic from `i infinity` to `log2 3` crosses the ideal triangles whose vertices are the convergents and the semiconvergents `(P_(n-1) + t P_n)/(Q_(n-1) + t Q_n)`, `1 <= t < a_(n+1)`, its cutting sequence being the continued fraction (Series, CITED); between `84/53` and `485/306` the semiconvergents are `149/94, 233/147, 317/200, 401/253` (`149/94` is the `omega`-line at `f = 47` of HYP-9260, which S29 called a convergent: MISTAKE-602). (iv) Most lines are neither. Of the `80` lines of THM-4629's `m = 2` census, `5` are convergents, `7` semiconvergents, `15` non-reduced multiples (`(36,57) = 3 (12,19)`, `(106,168) = 2 (53,84)`, `(159,252) = 3 (53,84)`, ...) and `53` other reduced fractions, of which `46` are Farey neighbours of a convergent or semiconvergent (fan vertices beyond the semiconvergent range, e.g. `t = 7..13` in the fan `(41 + 53t, 65 + 84t)` around `84/53`) and `7` are neighbours of no geodesic vertex with `Q' <= 10^7` (`(142,225), (183,290), (289,458), (395,626), (501,794), (576,913), (629,997)`); the first draft of this note said the lines are the vertices of the crossed triangles, which the audit refuted (MISTAKE-603). So the Farey graph organises the lines without containing them: the primitive lines are its vertices, the record lines are the turning points of the geodesic, the strong non-record lines are the fans around them, and the non-primitive lines are unfoldings.

**The colouring (PROVED).** Write a Farey vertex as a primitive pair `(Q, P)` with `Q >= 0` (`(0, 1)` for `1/0`); its colour is the signed pair `(Q, P) mod m`, one of the `J_2(m) = m^2 prod_(p | m) (1 - p^(-2))` primitive pairs modulo `m` (`3, 8, 12, 24, 24, 48` for `m = 2..7`; the quotient by `+-1` is also a proper colouring, with half as many colours for `m >= 3`; for the convergents `Q_n, P_n > 0` fixes the sign). Two Farey-adjacent vertices satisfy `PQ' - P'Q = +-1`, so their reductions are distinct and even a basis of `(Z/m)^2`. Hence the colouring is proper; for `m = 2` its three colours are the three points of `P^1(F_2)`, the parity types (odd, even), (even, odd), (odd, odd) of `(Q, P)`, the classical 3-colouring of the Farey graph. ## 4. The observer lens as the symmetry axis of the carrier

The owner's recurring instruction is to see a structure from its marked origin. Across the carriers this is the question of how much the local view determines the observer, and the five carriers sit at the two extremes:

- **Rigid carriers.** The Collatz graph (THM-4523: the backward tree identifies the vertex, `Aut = 1`), the chess game graph, the integer-sequence trees of Hofstadter's `Q`. The local view is complete information about the observer; the open question is global (one component? the value of the root?). Nothing can be learned by averaging over observers because no two are alike.
- **Transitive carriers.** The Farey graph (`PSL(2, Z)` is vertex-transitive on it), the Cayley graphs of section 2, the Paley graphs and tournaments, the full 2-shift. The local view carries no information about the observer; everything is global (a geodesic, a spectrum, a character), and averaging is the natural operation.

THM-4629 and THM-4631 move the Collatz question from the rigid carrier to a transitive one: the transfer law lives on the Farey and Cayley carriers, where the lines are forced by Diophantine approximation and the fast modes by group theory, and the whole difficulty is compressed into the step back to the rigid carrier, the `3`-adic closure. The repository's two rigidity facts, prefix fixed points as 2-adic convergents (observer identity, S15 fourteenth note) and THM-4523 (`Aut = 1`), suggest, as an inference and not as a statement of those theorems, that the step back cannot be made by any symmetry and must be made by a measure, as in the places where the rigid carrier is averaged: THM-4581's Haar coalescence over affine shifts, THM-4627's equal-time coalescence, THM-4610's base-`p` laws, HYP-9244's trichotomy, and THM-4629's own bin means. This is a reading, not a theorem, but it is the reading under which the five carriers are one picture.

## 5. The Pascal and de Bruijn carriers: words, their quotients, and why the 2-adic refinement is spectrally empty

Parity words are the free monoid on two letters; its Cayley graph is the binary tree, and counting words by their letter counts collapses it to the Pascal graph, whose generating function `1/(1 - x - y)` is the scalar law. Keeping only the last `j` letters collapses the tree to the de Bruijn graph `B(2, j)`, and Terras's bijection between residues modulo `2^j` and the first `j` parities (CITED) says that the Collatz class graph modulo `2^j` is `B(2, j)` with each vertex's single out-branch split into its two lifts. THM-4629's even-class averaging `E_j v(r) = [r even] (1/2)(v(r/2) + v(r/2 + 2^(j-1)))` is the halving half of that weighted de Bruijn walk (the full walk on `W_(j,0)`, with the odd branch, is `M|V_0`, whose nonzero spectrum is the single value `(u + v)/2` and whose rank is `2^(j-1)`, THM-4629 proof 2(d)). `E_j` has the single nonzero eigenvalue `1/2` with `rank(E_j^q) = 2^(j-q)` for `q <= j`: rank one plus a nilpotent part of index exactly `j` (the memory of the last `j` letters dies in `j` steps), by the closed form `E_j^q v(r) = [2^q | r] 2^(-q) sum_(x = r/2^q mod 2^(j-q)) v(x)` (`q <= j`); Part 5 checks `j <= 7`. This is why the 2-adic refinement of the transfer law is exact and yet adds no mode at any depth: the de Bruijn carrier is spectrally the Pascal carrier. The same statement for the `p`-adic carrier fails in the opposite way, section 2: the depth-`i` piece is a permutation, nothing dies, and the modes are all there, at decay `1/2`.

The Conway recursion's limit constant is the Pascal carrier read with the Pascal-adic measure (Codex), the price classification is the Pascal carrier read with exchangeable measures (de Finetti), the refuel bill is the Pascal carrier read as a prefix code (Kraft), and Landau's theorem is the Pascal carrier read as peakless Motzkin paths. The parity words of a Collatz orbit are a single path in it. The three readings differ in the measure, not in the graph.

## 6. Cocycles and certificates

A proper 2-colouring of a graph is a `Z/2`-valued 0-cochain whose coboundary is the all-ones 1-cochain; it exists iff that cochain is a coboundary iff every cycle is even. The repository's parity bits are instances: the colour-character parity bit of the glued boards, the tempo parity of chess (the game graph is bipartite by the side to move; HYP-9246's tempo diagonal concerns the mover's value on `tau`-fixed boards, a different object on the same bipartition), and the carry cocycle of the run-compressed pair chains (THM-4604). Certificates are the logical face of the same picture: a finite subgraph witnesses a `Pi_1` failure (a `5`-chromatic unit-distance graph witnesses `chi(R^2) >= 5`; THM-4614's port relation is a statement about which finite contexts can change a colour; a Collatz cycle would witness the failure of the conjecture; THM-4613's fortress certificates are greatest fixed points in a finite game graph), while a `Pi_2` statement (the generalised Collatz problem, Kurtz-Simon) has no finite witness of failure in general. The six carriers are therefore not six subjects but one object with six structures, and the type of the open question (`Pi_1`, `Pi_2`, a measure statement) is read off the carrier.

## 7. Numerology watch

The resonator of Hofstadter's `Q` (THM-4630) has poles at the roots of `2 z^2 + z + 1`, which are the reciprocals of the roots of `lam^2 + lam + 2`, the minimal polynomial of `(-1 + sqrt(-7))/2`: the non-trivial eigenvalues of the Paley tournament on `7` vertices and a prime above `2` in `Q(sqrt(-7))` (the field of the S17 note; `2` splits because `-7 = 1 mod 8`; the Ramanujan-Nagell solutions `2^n - 7 = x^2` are `n = 3, 4, 5, 7, 15`, Part 4). The explanation is formal: a doubly regular tournament on `n = 4t + 3` vertices has eigenvalues `(-1 +- i sqrt n)/2`, roots of `lam^2 + lam + (n+1)/4`, and the two-branch, weight-`1/2`, lags-`1`-and-`2` linearisation of `Q` has `lam^2 + lam + 2`; they agree iff `(n+1)/4 = 2`, i.e. `n = 7`. There is no bijection between the two objects; NUMEROLOGY, recorded so that it is not rediscovered.
