# Holonomy tomography: commutator depth becomes an error-correcting code

**Status: PROVED from the explicit S168 transport-kernel and lower-central-series theorems, with independent finite controls.** This packet sharpens S169's closed threshold-return theorem and supplies optimal initial-sheet test banks. The mathematical bridge is an equality of binary function spaces, not a numerical resemblance: the lower central series of the signed-basis transport group is a nested family of Reed–Muller codes.

The coding-theoretic facts are standard. The contribution here is their explicit interface to the inherited transport, runner-wall, and constant-profile tournament models. No literature-priority claim is made. This does not prove a lonely-runner existence theorem, make a scheduler intrinsic, or identify formal sheets with tournament classes.

The new question is operational: **how many carefully chosen initial units must be tested to know the return on every unit, and what does a missed defect look like?** A path summary stores an arbitrary transformation; a probe bank measures a transformation promised to belong to a specified subgroup. The concept board is: balanced threshold words / lower central series / affine-flat incidence / Boolean interpolation / literal profile-invisible hardware. The least-used retained coordinate is the initial sheet, together with the algebraic promise on the path word.

## 2. Setup and the stronger closed-threshold constraint

Fix Cayley–Dickson level n≥3, put d=n−1 and M=2^d, and use the inherited product convention

    (a,b)(c,d)=(ac−conjugate(d)b, da+b conjugate(c)).

A signed unit is (s,y,t), where s,t are bits and y∈F_2^d. The group G_n is generated by the positive basis left transports L_a. Write

    D_h(s,y,t)=(s+h(y),y,t).

The inherited exact statements are

    ker(G_n → F_2^n) = {D_(g+ct): sum_y g(y)=0, c∈F_2},
    γ_r(G_n) = {D_h: h∈I^r},              2≤r≤d,
    I^(d+1)=0.

Here I is the augmentation ideal in F_2[F_2^d], whose elements are truth tables, with convolution multiplication. In particular

    I²={h: sum_y h(y)=0 and sum_y y h(y)=0}.

The second equation is a vector equation over F_2. The group operations compose rightmost first.

**Theorem 1 (balanced return has a Hamming syndrome).** A closed threshold path with a fixed transport L_(a_i) on the positive first-bit crossing of coordinate i and its inverse on a negative crossing has return D_h with

    sum_y h(y)=0,              sum_y y h(y)=0.       (1)

In particular its sign defect is independent of the newest index bit t.

**Proof.** The first bit of every coordinate returns to its initial value, so the number of positive traversals equals the number of negative traversals for that coordinate. The resulting word has exponent sum zero in every named generator. Its image in the abelianization of the subgroup generated by those labels is therefore zero. Its image in G_n lies in [G_n,G_n]=γ_2(G_n)=D_(I²). This proves (1). Identity-labelled second-bit moves do not change the argument. ∎

The same proof applies to any closed binary-coordinate path with fixed inverse-edge labels. It does not require the path to come from runners. Conversely, a general G_n word or even a zero-index-displacement word need not satisfy (1). The S167 associator defect is precisely a hostile example outside this balanced-word promise.

Equation (1) says the subset of lower indices on which sign reverses is an even set whose XOR is zero. The sign table is the extended binary Hamming code, equivalently RM(d−2,d). At n=3 it is only {0,1}: **every balanced octonion threshold return is either identity or global sign reversal.** A single initial unit certifies such a promised return, despite being insufficient for an arbitrary octonion transport. At n=4 the table is affine in three bits, so a nontrivial return reverses exactly half or all of the 32 signed sheets.

## 3. The code hidden in every commutator layer

Let RM(k,d) denote truth tables of square-free Boolean polynomials of degree at most k on F_2^d.

**Theorem 2 (exact code identification).** For 1≤r≤d,

    I^r = RM(d−r,d),
    dim I^r = K_r := sum_(j=r)^d binom(d,j),
    min{weight(h):0≠h∈I^r} = 2^r.                  (2)

For 2≤r≤d this identifies γ_r(G_n) with that code via h↦D_h.

**Proof of the equality.** Products of r independent group differences, followed by translation, are indicators of affine r-flats. Such an indicator is a product of d−r independent affine linear equations and has Boolean degree at most d−r. Hence I^r⊆RM(d−r,d). Conversely a degree-k monomial is the indicator of a coordinate affine flat of dimension d−k≥r, and that indicator belongs to I^(d−k)⊆I^r. Monomials span RM(d−r,d). Their number gives the dimension.

For completeness, any nonzero degree-k Boolean polynomial on d variables has weight at least 2^(d−k). Induct on d and write p(x,z)=a(x)+z b(x). If b=0, its weight doubles from the smaller cube. If b≠0, at each x with b(x)=1 exactly one of p(x,0),p(x,1) is one; induction for deg b≤k−1 gives at least 2^(d−k) such x. An affine r-flat indicator attains equality. ∎

This is the standard Reed–Muller dimension/distance theorem; see [Abbe, Shpilka and Ye, *Reed-Muller Codes: Theory and Algorithms*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.03317). The all-level transport identification is the new use of it here, conditional on the inherited lower-central description.

**Corollary 3 (visibility versus depth).** Any nonidentity element of γ_r(G_n), 2≤r≤d, moves at least 2^(r+2) signed units. This is sharp. It takes exactly K_r sign probes in the worst case to identify an unknown promised element of γ_r(G_n), or to certify that it is identity. The same lower bound holds for adaptive deterministic probing.

Each y with h(y)=1 reverses both signs at both values of t, accounting for the factor four. The probe theorem is proved next. Thus deeper commutator histories have fewer independent parameters but every nonzero defect occupies more sheets.

Examples, listed as (n,r; minimum probes; minimum moved sheets / total sheets):

    (3,2; 1; 16/16)
    (4,2; 4; 16/32),       (4,3; 1; 32/32)
    (5,2; 11; 16/64),      (5,3; 5; 32/64),      (5,4; 1; 64/64)
    (6,2; 26; 16/128),     (6,3; 16; 32/128),    (6,4; 6; 64/128).

## 4. Optimal initial-sheet tomography and noise

For a promised γ_r return, put k=d−r and probe the positive units

    S_r={e_(y,0): Hamming_weight(y)≤k}.             (3)

There are K_r of them. Write h(y)=sum_(|A|≤k) c_A product_(i∈A)y_i. For each |A|≤k,

    c_A = sum_(B⊆A) h(1_B),                        (4)

where 1_B is the bit vector supported on B. Equation (4) is Boolean Möbius inversion, so these probes recover every coefficient and therefore every signed-sheet action. One can certify identity simply by finding that every measured sign is unchanged.

For the lower bound, evaluation at q initial units gives at most q binary linear measurements on the K_r-dimensional space I^r. If q<K_r, some nonzero h vanishes on every probe. For adaptive probing, follow the all-zero transcript: the fewer-than-K_r chosen evaluation functionals still have a nonzero common kernel, so the algorithm cannot distinguish identity from that defect. Negative initial units and the other top bit give the same measurement and do not improve this bound.

If instead every lower index y is probed once, the M observed bits form the code RM(d−r,d), with minimum distance 2^r. It detects any nonzero pattern of fewer than 2^r sign readout errors, uniquely corrects up to 2^(r−1)−1 arbitrary errors, and uniquely recovers from up to 2^r−1 erasures. These are information-theoretic guarantees by minimum distance, not a claim of an efficient decoding algorithm. More generally 2e+s<2^r suffices for e errors and s erasures. The error model corrupts sign readouts; it does not allow corrupted transport labels, promised depth, or sheet identities.

The optimum K_r probes have no uniform one-error protection: their evaluation code is all of F_2^(K_r). Redundancy, not just a better interpretation of the same K_r bits, is necessary for fault detection.

## 5. Arbitrary transport needs a larger bank

The general group has |G_n|=2^(M+n), while its diagonal kernel has dimension M. A probe's index output identifies the common translation, but only its sign tests the diagonal kernel.

**Theorem 4 (sharp full-group base size).** Exactly M initial signed-unit probes are necessary and sufficient to distinguish every element of G_n. One optimal bank is all positive e_(y,0) except one y=y*, together with e_(y0,1) for a retained y0≠y*.

**Proof.** Fewer than M evaluations cannot separate the M-dimensional zero-translation kernel. In the proposed bank, any kernel element fixing the two probes above y0 has c=0. Fixing the other M−1 distinct y values forces g=0 there, and even parity forces g(y*)=0. Thus its pointwise stabilizer is trivial. Two arbitrary group elements agreeing on the bank have a quotient in that stabilizer, so they are equal. ∎

This exactly refines the earlier M+n transport-memory bound: one common index translation gives n bits and the M probe signs supply the remaining M bits. It is a sampling statement, not a new group-order theorem.

A complete criterion for an arbitrary probe set is also simple. Ignore duplicate choices differing only by initial sign; let q be the number of distinct lower indices y and let a doubled y mean both values of t are probed.

- If q<M and no y is doubled, its pointwise kernel has dimension M−q.
- If q<M and some y is doubled, that dimension is M−q−1.
- If q=M and some y is doubled, the kernel is zero.
- If q=M with exactly one t_y above each y, the kernel is zero exactly when sum_y t_y=1; otherwise it has dimension one.

Indeed the equations are g(y)=c t on each probe, together with even parity of g. A doubled y kills c; when all y are present the parity equation kills c exactly if the chosen top bits have odd parity. In particular **testing the whole lower half t=0 still misses D_t**. More coverage is not the same as a well-chosen transversal.

## 6. Explicit smallest-support commutators

Let H=2^(n−1) and take d≥2. With [A,B]=ABA^(-1)B^(-1), define

    W_2=[L_1,L_(H+2)],
    W_(r+1)=[L_(2^r),W_r],             2≤r<d.      (5)

Then

    W_r = D_(1_{span(e_1,...,e_r)}).                (6)

Here e_i denotes the i-th coordinate vector of the lower index space, not an algebra basis unit. Thus the bounds in Corollary 3 are attained by explicit transport words.

For W_2, use the S168 commutator formula. The canonical lower generators L_1,L_2 anticommute, giving constant one; the newest-bit term contributes the same constant plus the double difference of the nonzero-indicator δ. The constants cancel, leaving the indicator of {0,1,2,3}. The possible commutator translation leaves this plane unchanged. Further commutation differentiates the diagonal table in a fresh lower-coordinate direction. The two disjoint translates of an r-flat form the next (r+1)-flat, proving (6).

The displayed word lengths are 4,10,22,..., namely 3·2^(r−1)−2 in generator letters. This is an explicit realization bound, not a shortest-word claim.

At n=4, conjugate W_2 by L_4. Its support is {4,5,6,7}, so it fixes e_0 but reverses e_4 and moves exactly 16 of 32 sheets. A binary-coordinate loop with labels 4,1,10 realizes this shifted defect by the chronological itinerary

    +4,+1,+10,−1,−10,−4.                           (7)

The coordinate bits start and finish at 000 and run

    000,100,110,111,101,100,000.

Thus “one identity-sheet check suffices for every balanced return” fails already at n=4. The strongest survivor is the octonion-only statement from Theorem 1, or the optimal multi-sheet bank (3). The missing coordinate is the lower-index support of the sign defect.

### Every codeword is attainable by a closed binary circuit

There is a useful sufficiency statement before imposing runner geometry. For distinct nonzero lower indices u,v, put

    C_(u,v)=[L_u,L_(H+v)] [L_u,L_v] [L_1,L_2].

The same inherited commutator formula gives

    C_(u,v)=D_(1_{span(u,v)}).                       (8)

Indeed the first two commutator tables cancel their lower-level contribution, leaving the indicated plane plus the constant one; the canonical commutator [L_1,L_2]=Z removes that constant. Conjugating by any transport with lower translation z moves the plane to z+span(u,v). Affine two-flat indicators span I², so these constructions realize every h∈I².

Each factor in (8) is the return of a four-edge binary square. A conjugating word is a whisker, followed by that closed square itinerary and its inverse whisker. To keep all traversals legal with fixed coordinate labels, allocate separate controller coordinates for the whisker. A single fixed binary cube suffices: use M−1 coordinates labelled by the nonzero lower L_u, M−1 labelled by L_(H+v), and d additional coordinates carrying the canonical lower translations. This is

    m=2M+d−2=2^n+n−3

controller coordinates. Products of the resulting closed paths stay in the same cube and realize all I². The reverse inclusion follows from Theorem 1, so its closed-path transport group is **exactly** D_(I²). This is an explicit nonminimal controller-width bound. Consequently the optimal probe lower bounds are sharp within the family of scheduled closed binary circuits, not merely within a formal group with no path realization. They are not claimed sharp for actual runner movies with prescribed speeds or a fixed smaller coordinate bank.

## 7. A literal tournament test fixture, with its sidecars

Use the inherited strong five-vertex seeds B_0=8 and B_1=10 in lexicographic pair-bit convention. They differ only at arc {0,2}, are nonisomorphic, and share

    P_B(z)=9z+33z²+31z³+10z⁴+z⁵,
    F_B(u)=u⁵+6u³+2u.

A K-bit word becomes the ordinal sum of K ordered seed blocks. It is recovered from the ordered strong components; one bit change is a literal one-arc reversal; every word has profile F_B(u)^K. This is the inherited S168 binary compiler, independently checked for the seeds here.

One signed-unit register needs n+1 blocks, hence 5(n+1) tournament vertices. A whole bank of q independent registers can be run in parallel on 5q(n+1) vertices, all in the common profile F_B^(q(n+1)); alternatively one register can be reset and reused for separate trials. The fixed initial-unit addresses and observer are part of the model.

For (7), retain three controller bits as well as the five signed-unit bits. The verifier constructs every tournament on 40 vertices in two experiments, initially e_0 and e_4, and verifies every actual reversal. Every microstate has profile F_B^8 and Hamilton count 9^8. The controller returns in both experiments; the signed-unit register returns only in the e_0 experiment. This turns the abstract “missed sheet” into distinct literal endpoints hidden by a common complete profile.

The schedule, register grouping, initial sheet, and macrostep latches are necessary sidecars. The transports are implemented by paths of physical flips, not single-flip automorphisms. The example is a closed labelled binary circuit, **not asserted to be the wall itinerary of any specified runner instance**. Theorem 1 applies to actual S169 runner movies in the forward direction, but the converse realization problem remains open here.

## 8. Homotopy test and connection ledger

A graph-cover transport extends over an attached two-cell only when its boundary word is identity on every sheet. If the boundary transport is proved to lie in γ_r, the bank S_r is therefore a complete and optimal **promised-family** test for that all-sheet obstruction. The bank does not establish the promise; it must come from the boundary-word algebra. Nor does it show the boundary loop is nullhomotopic in the original graph. It tests whether the chosen representation kills the relation being imposed.

The ledger is exact:

- Source: a boundary or threshold word whose group image lies in γ_r.
- Target: a Reed–Muller truth table on the lower index cube.
- Map: record the diagonal sign defect h(y).
- Preserved: the full transport on signed units, equality, support, and identity.
- Destroyed: the word, its geometric embedding, event times, wall owners, and causal schedule. Different words can have the same h.
- Required sidecar: level and basis convention, a proof of the subgroup promise, initial sheet addresses, and the actual physical schedule when a tournament implementation is claimed.
- Decisive hostile: (7) fixes e_0 but not e_4 at n=4; the lower-half bank misses D_t for unrestricted transport.

A useful new research question is now sharply bounded: which codewords in I² arise from genuine rational runner wall movies with a fixed number of coordinates and prescribed speeds? The present results give necessary syndromes and complete observers, not that realization theorem.
