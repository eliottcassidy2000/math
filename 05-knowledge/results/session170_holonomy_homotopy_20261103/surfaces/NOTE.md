# Full signed-unit transport on surfaces and singular complexes

**Status: explicit elementary constructions, conditional on the inherited S17 normalization and S168 transport structure; exact finite controls supplied.** No literature-priority claim is made. These covers are formal transport objects, not new tournament isomorphism classes. None resolves the planar Jacobian conjecture, a tournament spectrum conjecture, or a plane-coloring problem.

The main theorem is sharp: full Cayley–Dickson left transport at doubling level n≥3 can be carried by a closed orientable surface exactly when its genus is at least n. The same construction gives an exact capacity criterion for wedges of surfaces and circles and thereby returns to S17's singular finite-field complexes. The first-homology bound is sufficient here because the inherited minimal generators can be explicitly paired into commuting handle pairs; it is not a general principle for arbitrary target groups.

## 1. Full transport has sharp orientable genus n

Retain n≥3 and d=n−1. Use the Cayley–Dickson convention `(a,b)(c,d)=(ac−conj(d)b,da+b conj(c))`, and let G_n be the permutation group generated by positive basis left multiplications on the 2^(n+1) signed units. Write a signed unit as (s,y,t), with y∈F₂^d and t∈F₂. We use the inherited [S168 kernel and generator theorem][tower]:

    D_(h+ct)(s,y,t)=(s+h(y)+ct,y,t)

belongs to G_n when h has even truth-table parity; the full group is generated by

    X_j=L_(2^j),   E_j=D_(δ₀+δ_(2^j))   (0≤j<d),
    T=L_(2^d),     U=D_t.

Here δ_a(y) is the indicator of y=a. There are 2n generators, and exactly 2n are necessary because the abelianization is F₂^(2n).

The following n pairs commute:

    (T,E₀), (X₀,U), (X₁,E₁), …, (X_(d−1),E_(d−1)).       (1)

Indeed T leaves y unchanged; every lower X_j leaves t unchanged; and δ₀+δ_(2^j) is invariant under y↦y+2^j. Diagonal conjugation depends only on the index translation, not its sign function. The union of the pairs in (1) is exactly the inherited generating set.

**Theorem 1 (sharp surface capacity).** A connected finite cover of a closed connected orientable surface Σ_g with full G_n monodromy on all signed units exists if and only if g≥n.

**Proof.** A surjection π₁(Σ_g)→G_n induces a surjection from the mod-two first homology F₂^(2g) onto G_n/[G_n,G_n]=F₂^(2n), so g≥n. For g=n use the presentation

    π₁(Σ_n)=⟨a₁,b₁,…,a_n,b_n | ∏[a_i,b_i]=1⟩

and send (a_i,b_i) to the n pairs (1). Each individual commutator is identity, while their union generates full G_n. The action is transitive, hence the corresponding 2^(n+1)-sheet cover is connected. For larger g send extra handle generators to identity. ∎

The construction is nonabelian: commuting inside each handle pair does not require commuting across different handles. It thus complements the S169 torus obstruction rather than contradicting it. The cover is not generally regular; the inherited centralizer calculation gives deck group {1,Z}.

**Corollary 1.1 (surface wedges with incidence loops).** Let X be a connected finite CW complex homotopy equivalent to a finite wedge of closed orientable surfaces of total genus H and b additional circles. Then a full G_n signed-unit cover exists if and only if

    2H+b ≥ 2n.                                           (2)

Necessity follows from mod-two first homology. For sufficiency, assign some complete commuting pairs in (1) to handles and the others to disjoint pairs of free-circle generators. There are H+⌊b/2⌋ such slots, and (2) says at least n are available. Unused generators map to identity. Each individual surface relation holds, not merely their product. This is an exact all-n construction conditional on the inherited G_n structure.

## 2. A concrete return to S17's singular finite-field surfaces

The independently audited [S17 homotopy supplement][homotopy] gives, for q=p^r odd,

    V=(q²−1)/2,  m=q/p,
    c=q(q²−1)/(p(p²−1)),
    g_p=1+(p²−1)(p−6)/24,
    b=(m−1)V−c+1.

Its complex K_q is homotopy equivalent to a wedge of c closed orientable genus-g_p surfaces and b circles. The original singular vertices are not removed by this homotopy statement. Applying Corollary 1.1 proves:

**Corollary 2.1.** For n≥3, K_q carries a full G_n signed-unit cover exactly when

    2c g_p+b ≥ 2n.

Examples:

| q | Normalization components c | Genus g_p | Incidence circles b | H₁ rank | Largest allowed n≥3 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 7 | 1 | 3 | 0 | 6 | 3 |
| 9 | 30 | 0 | 51 | 51 | 25 |
| 11 | 1 | 26 | 0 | 52 | 26 |
| 13 | 1 | 50 | 0 | 100 | 50 |
| 25 | 130 | 0 | 1119 | 1119 | 559 |

For q=9 and q=25, all normalization components are spheres. Their large transport capacity comes entirely from incidence loops at the singular identifications. For q=11 and q=13 the capacity instead resides in genuine handles. Treating the normalization as the original complex would erase exactly the first mechanism.

This is a formal covering construction over the S17 complexes, not an assertion that either their simplicial vertices or the added sheets are tournament classes. The astronomical sheet counts at large n are existence/capacity statements, not enumerated objects.
