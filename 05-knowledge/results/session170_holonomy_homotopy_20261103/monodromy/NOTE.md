# Flatness has a price: rigid signed-unit quotients and a literal tournament torus

**Status.** Proved constructions and quotient classifications, with exact finite controls. The signed-unit results use the explicit Cayley–Dickson kernel established in S168. The physical torus uses S168's six literal tournament seeds, independently rechecked here. The topology and the permutation-action dictionary are standard; no literature-priority claim is made for this composition.

There are two genuinely different ways to remove noncommuting transport. One can forget state until the old transports commute, or retain state and choose different transports. In the full Cayley–Dickson signed-unit action, the first operation necessarily loses every algebraic sign. The second operation can preserve sign, and S168's physical hexagons supply a concrete torus model of S169's sign-detecting abelian transport.

“Physical” in this note means vertices are specified **labelled** tournaments and edges reverse one actual arc. Extra square and higher cells are formal topological interpolations, not continuous families of tournaments. The construction is not the blue/black complement-line metagraph, does not realize an arbitrary formal cover over S166's fixed quotient by tournament classes, and is not an autonomous evolution rule.

## 1. The finest quotient that survives a prescribed homotopy

Let B be a connected graph, with loops and parallel edges retained. Let E→B be a connected finite cover with sheet set U over a chosen root. A spanning tree identifies its monodromy with a transitive permutation group G≤Sym(U). Attach disks along based loop words r_α, obtaining a two-dimensional CW complex X. If an attaching loop was originally based elsewhere, transport it to the root; different choices only conjugate its monodromy.

Write g_α∈G for each attaching-loop permutation and put

    N = normal closure in G of all g_α.

**Theorem 1 (universal relator quotient).** The quotient sheet set U/N, whose points are N-orbits, carries a cover of X. It is the finest monodromy-compatible quotient of the original sheets whose graph cover extends across all the attached disks. In particular, E itself extends exactly when every g_α is the identity on every sheet.

**Proof.** Normality makes the G-action on N-orbits well defined. Every g_α acts trivially there, so the action factors through π₁(X), giving the corresponding cover. Equivalently, every attaching walk has a closed lift through every quotient sheet, allowing one disk to be attached there. If a G-equivariant quotient p:U→S extends, every g_α acts trivially on S, as do its conjugates and their products. Consequently p is constant on each N-orbit and factors uniquely through U/N. The identity criterion is the special case without a quotient. ∎

The “normal closure” matters. Killing a relation at one root is not a one-sheet operation: its conjugates describe that same relation after transport to other states. Checking only one initial sheet is insufficient.

Only π₁ controls an ordinary covering. Once the two-cell relations are satisfied, attaching higher-dimensional cells produces no additional covering obstruction: the attaching spheres in dimensions at least three are simply connected. A nontrivial square return is an obstruction to a proposed flat connection, not by itself a measurement of π₂ or a higher-homotopy invariant.

**S166 consequence.** The actual non-self-converse black double cover has monodromy in C₂, recorded by its binary voltage σ. It extends over proposed disk fillings if and only if every attaching walk has σ-sum zero. If even one filling has odd σ-sum, the finest extending quotient identifies the converse pair. This is a statement about a specified modification of the actual quotient graph, not permission to regard its black loops as null-homotopic without adding those disks.

## 2. Full signed-unit transport admits only one kind of information loss

At Cayley–Dickson level n let

    U_n = {(s,x): s∈F₂, x∈F₂ⁿ},

where (s,x) denotes (−1)^s e_x. Let G_n be generated by all positive-basis left multiplication permutations. Every element has the form

    (s,x) ↦ (s+f(x), x+a).

Use the convention

    (a,b)(c,d) = (ac−conjugate(d)b, da+b conjugate(c)).

For n≥3 write x=(y,t), y∈F₂^(n−1). The S168 kernel theorem states that G_n contains precisely the zero-translation operators

    D_(h+ct)(s,y,t)=(s+h(y)+ct,y,t),
    Σ_y h(y)=0,    c∈F₂.

A transport-compatible state quotient means an equivalence relation preserved by every G_n permutation. The quotient map need not be linear, and its target is not assumed a group.

**Theorem 2 (all proper quotients erase sign).** For n≥3, every proper transport-compatible quotient of U_n identifies (s,x) with (s+1,x) for every x. In fact, all such quotients are exactly

    (s,x) ↦ x+W ∈ F₂ⁿ/W,

where W is a linear subspace of F₂ⁿ. The only other compatible partition is the discrete partition retaining every signed unit.

**Proof.** Let two distinct states be identified. Transitivity lets us carry one to +e₀. If the other is −e₀, the desired sign identification has already occurred. Otherwise it is ±e_x with x≠0.

The stabilizer of +e₀ contains an operator fixing +e₀ and reversing the sign at e_x. If x=(y,1), use D_t. If x=(y,0) with y≠0, choose z≠0,y, which is possible since n−1≥2, and use h=1_{y}+1_{z}. This h has even parity, vanishes at zero, and equals one at y. Applying that stabilizer to the assumed identification shows that +e₀ is identified with both +e_x and −e_x. Carrying e_x back to e₀ identifies +e₀ and −e₀. Transitivity then identifies every sign pair.

After signs have been identified, all transports act on the index set by translations, and all index translations occur. A translation-invariant equivalence on F₂ⁿ is exactly a coset relation: its zero class W is closed under addition because x∼0 and y∼0 imply x+y∼y∼0. All other classes are translates of W. Conversely each displayed quotient is plainly preserved by every transport. ∎

This classifies **partitions**, not arbitrary choices of labels for their quotient sets. It is stronger than a binary-observation obstruction: even a nonlinear many-state observation cannot discard some information while retaining any distinction between opposite algebraic signs.

At n=3 there are 16 subspaces of F₂³, hence 17 compatible partitions including the discrete one. At n=4 there are 67 subspaces, hence 68 partitions. The verifier independently enumerates the full congruence lattices by closing pairs under all transport generators and joining congruences; it obtains these counts without assuming the theorem. The quaternion control n=2 has six partitions, including the discrete one, consistent with its regular Q₈ action.

The hypothesis is **full G_n compatibility**. A selected subgroup can have many more quotients. In particular, S169's elementary abelian regular subgroup has a compatible binary observation that detects sign. There is no contradiction: that observation does not intertwine every transport in G_n.

### A sharp flattening theorem

For n≥2, the canonical operators L₁ and L₂ anticommute and have square Z, where Z reverses global sign. Thus [L₁,L₂]=Z. The unsigned-index action is abelian.

**Corollary 3 (maximal flat observation).** For n≥3, the finest transport-compatible observation on which all original G_n transports commute is exactly the unsigned index x∈F₂ⁿ. It retains 2ⁿ of the original 2^(n+1) states and necessarily loses algebraic sign.

**Proof.** Commuting induced transports force their commutator Z to act trivially, so signs must be identified. The index observation already makes all transports commute and imposes no further identification. ∎

An abelianization of the **transport group** and a quotient of the **sheet set** are different objects. S168 gives |G_n^ab|=2^(2n), whereas the finest flat sheet observation has only 2ⁿ states. Group elements summarize all-sheet effects; sheets are the states being acted on.

### Even a local associator defect destroys the entire sign layer under filling

Suppose B has full G_n monodromy and each disk relation has zero index translation. Then all relators and their conjugates lie in the diagonal kernel, so their N-orbits never mix indices.

**Corollary 4 (all-or-nothing zero-index filling).** If every relator is identity, no quotient is necessary. If at least one is nonidentity, the finest extending quotient is exactly the unsigned index cover. There is no intermediate loss affecting only some sign pairs.

**Proof.** A nonidentity relator makes U_n/N a proper compatible quotient, so Theorem 2 forces all sign pairs to merge. Since N is diagonal, no two distinct indices merge. ∎

For the S167 octonion defect

    R=L₃^−1 L₁ L₂,
    R(s,x)=(s+[x has bit 4],x),

half the signed states are fixed. In particular R fixes +e₀. Nevertheless attaching a disk with this relation to a full-monodromy cover forces all 16 signed sheets down to eight unsigned sheets. This is an explicit example where “the root came back” misses the global obstruction.

The full-monodromy hypothesis cannot be silently dropped. If the only available transport is this defect R itself, its fixed states remain singleton N-orbits and only the affected four sign pairs merge. Transport to all indices is what spreads the obstruction.

## 3. An actual tournament graph carrying a flat torus cover

Use the six pinned order-six tournament masks from S168, with lexicographic pair-bit order and bit one meaning the smaller label points to the larger:

    B₀=153, B₁=8345, B₂=8337,
    B₃=8369, B₄=177, B₅=185.

They induce a literal hexagon: successive arc reversals are

    {3,5}, {0,4}, {1,2}, {3,5}, {0,4}, {1,2}.

Every seed is strong, their common unordered path-cover polynomial is

    P(z)=29z+131z²+162z³+77z⁴+15z⁵+z⁶,

and their common labelled-slot polynomial is

    f(r)=r⁶+12r⁴+16r².

The involution p=(0 4)(1 2)(3 5) relabels B_h to B_(h+3). There are exactly three isomorphism classes, indexed by h modulo three. S168 establishes these facts; the verifier independently checks the actual arcs, relabelling, strong connectivity, exact isomorphism classes, and profiles by two separate counting methods.

For h∈(Z/6Z)^N form the ordinal sum

    T_h=B_(h₁)⊕⋯⊕B_(h_N),

on fixed labelled blocks. Its literal flip graph is C₆^□N; its displayed class quotient is C₃^□N=K₃^□N. Every state has order 6N and

    F_(T_h)(r)=f(r)^N,    H(T_h)=29^N.

Now give each C₆ and C₃ its usual polygon-circle realization, retaining the polygon edges rather than filling the polygons with disks. Take their Cartesian products as cubical cell complexes.

**Theorem 5 (literal flat torus model).** The product graph cover extends to the ordinary torus cover

    p_N:(R/6Z)^N → (R/3Z)^N.

Its vertices are the displayed physical tournament states; its one-cells are literal single-arc reversals. It is connected, regular, and has deck group (C₂)^N, acting by h_i↦h_i+3. A based loop with winding vector w∈Z^N has deck return

    h ↦ h+3w (mod 6).

Thus every mixed-coordinate square has identity transport, while each coordinate triangle is a noncontractible circle with a nontrivial deck return.

**Proof.** Reduction modulo three in each real coordinate is a local homeomorphism, and its fiber consists of the 2^N independent shifts by three. Its one-skeleton is exactly the inherited graph map, because the circle cells are the consecutive unit intervals. A loop winding w lifts to displacement 3w, proving the monodromy and every stated square/triangle property. ∎

Attaching mixed squares is legitimate because their transports commute. Attaching a disk inside a coordinate triangle is a different operation and is not legitimate for the unchanged double cover. If instead each circle factor is replaced by a filled triangle and their full product is taken, the base is contractible; in either construction killing all coordinate generators forces the one-state sheet quotient. The distinction is between a circle and a filled disk, not between drawings of the same triangle.

For N=2 the base one-skeleton has nine vertices and 18 edges, hence graph cycle rank ten. Its nine mixed squares change that free graph group into Z². The original 4-sheet physical cover survives because all these square relations are already flat. The torus still has two independent winding directions.

No tournament is assigned to an interior point of a square or higher cell. These cells specify permitted path homotopies on an exactly realized discrete transition graph. The resulting topological model is literal at its vertices and edges, which is the level at which tournament states and arc changes exist.

## 4. S169's sign-detecting flat algebra now has physical sheets

Fix n≥3 and take N=n+1 in Theorem 5. Over a logical vertex x∈{0,1,2}^N, each physical representative has a unique chart

    h_i=x_i+3r_i (mod 6),    r_i∈F₂.

Identify these N deck bits with a signed-unit state by

    q=r₁,
    y=(r₂,...,r_n)∈F₂^(n−1),
    t=r_(n+1),
    s=q+tδ(y),              δ(y)=[y≠0].

This is a bijection between each physical fiber and all 2^(n+1) signed units. The corrected sign is q, while s is the algebraic sign coordinate.

The S169 operators are

    Z(s,y,t)   =(s+1,y,t),
    B_u(s,y,t) =(s+t(δ(y)+δ(y+u)),y+u,t),
    J(s,y,t)   =(s+δ(y),y,t+1).

They form the elementary abelian regular group E_n≅(C₂)^(n+1), contained in G_n.

**Theorem 6 (physical E_n realization).** Under this chart, the first coordinate winding acts as Z, the next n−1 coordinate windings act as the independent B_u, and the last coordinate winding acts as J. Consequently the literal torus cover realizes the complete sign-detecting E_n action on its physical tournament fibers. Every generating winding is three actual arc reversals in one six-vertex block.

**Proof.** A coordinate winding toggles exactly its bit r_i. Toggling q changes s alone. Toggling a y-bit changes y by u and changes s by t(δ(y)+δ(y+u)). Toggling t changes s by δ(y). These are exactly the displayed formulas. The coordinate circle has three edges, whose lift is half of its physical hexagon. ∎

The edge labels in this chart are explicit: on an oriented base edge 0→1 or 1→2 the deck bit is unchanged, while on 2→0 it is toggled. Reverse edges use the same involution when they cross that chart cut. Thus this is not only an abstract isomorphism between deck groups; each individual physical edge projects with the stated transport.

For the octonion sheet set, n=3 and N=4. The model has:

- 81 logical class vertices and 1,296 pinned physical vertices;
- 16 physical sheets over each logical vertex;
- order 24 for every tournament;
- common complete profile f(r)^4 and Hamilton count 29^4=707,281.

The verifier constructs all 192 individual arc flips in the four three-edge generator loops from each of the 16 sheets over the zero logical vertex, and checks their signed-unit actions. It checks the coordinate formulas through n=7.

This supplies a physical **pinned** realization of S169's abelian sheet action over a different explicit base. The members of a fiber are isomorphic tournaments, distinguished by their labels. It does not make the 16 sheets into 16 distinct tournament classes, does not realize full G₃ as torus monodromy, and does not identify this torus with S166's Q₁. The latter remains a separate physical-realization problem.

Theorem 2 also explains why this cannot be obtained by an equivariant information-loss map from full G_n while retaining sign. Here the set of allowed transports was restricted and reorganized to E_n; the sheet set itself was retained.

## 5. Reversible computation and runner clocks: what winding retains

Any scheduled path of S168's single-cell reversible trit gates is a path in the logical product graph. Let ε_k∈{−1,+1} be the signed unit increment for each nontrivial gate, recorded in the coordinate where that gate acts. If the logical state returns, each coordinate's signed increment sum is divisible by three. Define

    w_i=(1/3) Σ_(updates of cell i) ε_k ∈ Z.

**Corollary 7 (winding receiver).** The physical return is exactly the deck vector w mod 2. In particular it is invariant under every path homotopy through mixed squares, and under swapping adjacent independent updates. Its ith bit is also the parity of the number of nontrivial updates at cell i.

**Proof.** Theorem 5 gives the winding rule. Since 3 is odd, w_i modulo two is the signed sum modulo two, which equals the unsigned number of its ±1 summands. ∎

This recovers S168's p-versus-2p orbit criterion as a topological statement. A logical periodic orbit closes physically after one logical lap exactly when w is even; otherwise two laps are necessary and sufficient. That conclusion assumes the repeated controller makes the same logical path each lap. Arbitrary controlled gates need not commute: only genuinely independent updates correspond to the mixed-square homotopies invoked here.

A primitive integer torus flow t↦3vt modulo 3 has base period one. Its lift in this cover returns after exactly two laps, because at least one coordinate of v is odd. This continuous flow lies in the formal torus; a literal arc-by-arc tournament clock additionally needs a specified edge-path discretization. It is an E_n winding clock, not S169's different C₄-containing A_n cover, whose return can take four laps.

A runner threshold movie retains different data. A product trit word with retained time atoms records threshold states, but it does not itself choose either this flat torus transport or a noncommuting signed-unit transport. S169's simultaneous-wall ambiguity is resolved here only when the chosen updates really are the commuting torus ones. One must not use winding to infer an improved lonely interval, or infer a winding vector from an unmarked sequence lacking its coordinate updates and orientations.

## 6. Reproduction and boundaries

Run with Python's standard library:

    python3 check.py > certificate.json
    python3 -O check.py > certificate.optimized.json
    cmp certificate.json certificate.optimized.json

Checks use explicit exceptions, so optimization does not remove them. The verifier imports no earlier session verifier. It independently:

- builds Cayley–Dickson left permutations by recursive basis multiplication and enumerates the groups of orders 8, 128, and 4,096;
- enumerates every compatible quotient partition at levels two, three, and four;
- checks the stabilizer-separation witnesses through level seven;
- closes the octonion associator relator under all transports and verifies exactly eight resulting unsigned sheets;
- recomputes each seed's entire path-cover profile by subset/set-partition counting and by independent permutation/cut counting, converts it to the slot polynomial, and checks all seed isomorphism classes by exhaustive relabelling;
- checks induced physical product graphs and local covering bijections through three blocks;
- verifies 648 mixed-square returns and 648 coordinate-triangle deck changes;
- checks 3,552 E_n coordinate actions through level seven and 192 literal order-24 arc flips for the octonion flat model.

The all-n statements rest on the proofs and the stated inherited kernel, not on extrapolation from these checks. No completeness claim about all tournament switches, no general Lonely Runner theorem, and no unmarked computational universality is implied.
