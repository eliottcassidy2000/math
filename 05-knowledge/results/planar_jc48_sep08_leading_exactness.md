# A rational Jacobian mate forces an algebraic leading-coefficient primitive

**Status: PROVED + FINITE-EXACT + INDEPENDENTLY AUDITED.**
This note gives a necessary condition for arbitrary rational mates, with
no bound on their degree or poles. The condition is sufficient for the
pure-monomial model only. The planar Jacobian conjecture remains open.

## 1. Statement and inheritance

Work over C, with Jacobian convention `J(F,G)=F_x G_t-F_t G_x`.

**Theorem.** Let `F in C(x)[t]` have positive t-degree `n`
and leading coefficient `A(x)!=0`. Choose `a` with `a^n=A`, and put
`K=C(x)(a)`. If some `G in C(x,t)` satisfies `J(F,G)=1`, then

    dx/a=dH for some H in K.                         (1)

The same conclusion holds for any nonzero constant Jacobian, by scaling
G. No squarefreeness or irreducibility of `Z^n-A` is assumed: K is the
field generated by one root, and its degree may be a proper divisor of n.
Different choices of a multiply (1) by a nonzero complex constant.

The closest canon is
[THM-2071, quadratic-fiber-square-parity-gate](../../01-canon/theorems/THM-2071-quadratic-fiber-square-parity-gate.md),
which uses top coefficients and descent in the polynomial quadratic-fibre
setting. The present map keeps the radical coefficient field and allows
an arbitrary rational mate. Within this session, the compact-fibre
relative differential, individual residues and exact pole capacities
were paid in [quartic common-root necessity](planar_jc48_sep08_quartic_common_root.md)
and [two finite fourfold roots](planar_jc48_sep08_two_finite_four_four.md).
The new view expands at t=infinity before examining each finite fibre.

Classical Newton and edge Laurent expansions of Jacobian pairs are
antecedents. In particular, Makar-Limanov and Trakhtenberg,
[*Properties of a Jacobian mate*, MPIM preprint 2024-33](https://archive.mpim-bonn.mpg.de/5148/1/mpim-preprint_2024-33.pdf),
use radical edge expansions and subtraction of powers of the first
coordinate. This note credits that related mechanism and makes no
external-priority claim for the coefficient-field exactness formulation.

The concept board here is: a formal inverse at infinity; a radical
coefficient field; an exact algebraic differential; labelled residues;
and the lower coefficients discarded by this necessary test. The source
is a rational Jacobian pair, the target is a one-variable differential
on the normalized radical cover, and the map extracts one Laurent
coefficient. It preserves the primitive equation but loses every lower
coefficient obstruction. The necessary sidecar is the derivation on K.
A multiplicity-n root is the cheapest decisive hostile probe.

## 2. Formal inverse and the one coefficient that matters

The derivation `d/dx` of C(x) extends uniquely to K, since characteristic
is zero. In particular `a'=A'/(n a^(n-1))`. Extend it coefficientwise
to `K((v))`, holding the independent variable v fixed.

Write `F=A t^n+f_(n-1)t^(n-1)+...+f_0`. We solve `F(x,T)=v^-n`
formally. Set `U=vT` and solve

    A U^n+f_(n-1)U^(n-1)v+...+f_0 v^n=1,
    U(0)=a^-1.

The derivative in U at v=0 is `n A(a^-1)^(n-1)=na`, a nonzero
element of the field K. Formal Hensel recursion therefore gives a
unique `U in K[[v]]` and

    T=a^-1 v^-1+O(1) in K((v)).                      (2)

Substitution `t->T` embeds C(x,t) in K((v)). Indeed a nonzero polynomial
`sum p_j(x)t^j` has a unique least v-valuation term, coming from its
highest nonzero t-degree, because T has valuation -1 and each nonzero
coefficient p_j has valuation zero. Its image is nonzero and invertible
as a Laurent series. This also pays every rational denominator of G.

Put `Gtilde=G(x,T)`. The two formal derivations commute and the ordinary
rational chain rule is valid after substitution. Holding v fixed gives

    F_x+F_t T_x=0,
    Gtilde_x=G_x+G_t T_x
            =(G_x F_t-G_t F_x)/F_t=-1/F_t.          (3)

Differentiating `F(x,T)=v^-n` in v gives

    F_t T_v=-n v^(-n-1),
    Gtilde_x=(1/n)v^(n+1) T_v
            =-1/(na) v^(n-1)+O(v^n).              (4)

Let `b=[v^(n-1)]Gtilde`, an element of K. Coefficientwise differentiation
in (4) gives `b'=-1/(na)`. Thus `H=-nb` proves (1).
Nothing here uses analytic convergence, a degree bound on G, a generic
choice of A, or a polynomial rather than rational denominator. For n=1
the extracted coefficient is the constant coefficient b_0, as required.

## 3. Immediate residue consequences

An exact meromorphic differential on a smooth curve has zero residue
at every point, by differentiating its local Laurent series. This
applies to the normalization of the projective curve with function
field K; disconnected presentations of `Z^n-A` are not substituted for
that field.

**Multiplicity-n consequence.** If A has a finite zero at p of order
exactly n, no rational Jacobian mate exists. Locally write
`A=(x-p)^n B`, with `B(p)!=0`. Every normalization branch admits the
unramified expansion `a=(x-p)b`, `b^n=B`, `b(p)!=0`. Hence

    dx/a=(b(p)^-1/(x-p)+regular) dx

has a nonzero residue. This argument also works when K=C(x).

**Two rational pole consequence.** More generally, suppose

    A=kappa*(x-p)^(an)*(x-q)^(bn),
    kappa!=0, p!=q finite, a,b positive integers.

Then K=C(x), after a constant choice of nth root, and the residue of
`dx/((x-p)^a(x-q)^b)` at p is

    (-1)^(a-1) binomial(a+b-2,a-1) (p-q)^(-a-b+1).    (5)

It is nonzero. Formula (5) is the coefficient of `(x-p)^(a-1)` in
the binomial expansion of `(p-q+x-p)^(-b)`. Thus this entire leading
coefficient class excludes rational mates, for arbitrary lower rows.
The distinctness and finite-location assumptions are essential.

## 4. Consumer for the actual global quartic class

In the fixed surface and source chart of
[the DG filtration](planar_jc48_sep08_dg_quadratic.md),

    W=(P1_x x P1_z) minus {z=x^2},  t=1/(z-x^2),
    omega=dx wedge dt,
    F=H^2+L,  H in L2, L in L1,

let `N0(x)` be the nonzero finite polynomial representing H's complete
binary-octic boundary restriction. It has degree at most eight; its
missing degree is exactly the multiplicity at infinity. H has t-degree
two and leading N0, while L has t-degree at most one. Thus F has t-degree
four with leading `N0^2`. Choosing `a=sqrt(N0)` in the theorem forces

    dx/sqrt(N0) exact in C(x)(sqrt(N0)).              (6)

This application uses the actual source coordinates; no PGL2
transformation or source-volume-preserving boundary translation is
asserted. The theorem has discarded the lower global-section data, so
passing (6) is not a claim of existence of a global pair.

Every finite multiplicity-two root of N0 violates (6), because it is
a multiplicity-four root of the leading coefficient of F. Consequently:

* Every binary-octic partition `(4,2,2)` excludes rational mates of
  every degree at every placement of its three distinct boundary points.
  At most one point is infinity, so at least one double root is finite.
* A `(6,2)` partition with its double point finite excludes rational
  mates, including when the sixfold point is infinity.
* The two-distinct-finite `(4,4)` case is recovered directly from (5),
  with a=b=2. Its independently proved earlier boundary argument remains
  valid and records the actual moving-root mechanism.

There is no assertion here about `(6,2)` with the double point at
infinity: `N0=kappa*(x-p)^6` makes (6) exact. The previously paid
constant-D/M-unit subclass at that boundary has extra hypotheses.

## 5. Sharpness for the pure monomial and failure of general sufficiency

**Pure-monomial equivalence.** If `F=A(x)t^n`, the necessary condition
(1) is sufficient for a rational mate. The extension K/C(x) is Galois:
all roots of `Z^n-A` lie in K, since C contains all roots of unity. Its
Galois group acts by `tau(a)=chi(tau)a`.

Given any primitive H, replace it by

    Hbar=(1/|Gal|) sum_tau chi(tau)*tau(H).

Then `Hbar'=1/a` and `tau(Hbar)=chi(tau)^-1 Hbar`. Consequently

    B=-Hbar/(n a^(n-1)) in C(x),
    G=B t^(1-n) in C(x,t).

Direct differentiation, using A=a^n, gives

    J(F,G)=-(n-1)A'B-nAB'=1.                         (7)

This includes n=1 and extensions whose degree properly divides n.
It is not an extension of sufficiency to arbitrary lower coefficients.

A nontrivial radical-field positive control is

    F=x^2 t^3,  G=-1/(x t^2),  J(F,G)=1.

Here `a=x^(2/3)` and `dx/a=d(3x^(1/3))`; demanding a primitive in
C(x) would be false. A nonconstant lower-row control is

    F=x^3t^2+x,  G=xt/F,  J(F,G)=1.

Its coefficient b_1 is `1/sqrt(x)`, so `-2b_1` is the correct primitive.

A passing octic with two odd roots is `N0=x^5(x-1)^3`. Under
`x=1/(1-z^2)`, choose `a=z^3/(1-z^2)^4`. Then

    dx/a=d(-2/z-4z+(2/3)z^3).

For the pure monomial `F=x^10(x-1)^6 t^4`, the descended mate is

    G=(8x^2-4x-1)/(6x^9(x-1)^5 t^3).

This is an exact rational pair, not an asserted global DG section pair.

Finally `F=x^2+t^2` passes (1), since its leading coefficient is 1,
but has no rational mate. On the generic fibre F=c, put `h=x+i t`.
Then `x=(h+c/h)/2`, `t=(h-c/h)/(2i)`, and

    -dx/F_t=-i dh/(2h)

has nonzero residue. The first failed implication would be treating
the extracted leading coefficient as all fibre periods. The strongest
survivor is the necessary theorem and its pure-monomial equivalence.
The missing information is precisely the lower-coefficient deformation
of the relative form. The next question is which later Laurent
coefficients give complete obstructions for the surviving octics.
