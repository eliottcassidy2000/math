# What really couples modular levels 7, 8, 9 and 72?

The genus and congruence statements are exact. None assigns a
Hamiltonian-path count to a modular curve. Compare the archived
[5–6–7 flag-carrier session](https://github.com/eliottcassidy2000/math/blob/1b91742ce55a202b73c80d56561ba56d00b503a6/05-knowledge/results/geometry_567_flag_carriers_20261004.md)
and [archived Camion–Busch and Paley notes](https://github.com/eliottcassidy2000/math/blob/1b91742ce55a202b73c80d56561ba56d00b503a6/05-knowledge/results/camion_busch_gaps_polyhedra_collatz_20261002.md):
one must retain which group, rotation data and incidences are actually
acting, not just a repeated small number.

## Genus and three special equalities

Write G_N=SL₂(Z/N)/⟨−I⟩; take exactly the global sign quotient, not a
quotient by every scalar root of 1. For N≥3 put
J₂(N)=N²∏_(p|N)(1−p^−2), µ=NJ₂(N)/2. The three inertia orders of the
cover X(N)→X(1) are 2,3,N. The orientable triangular map thus has
V=µ/N, E=µ/2, F=µ/3 and g=1+(N−6)J₂(N)/24. See
[Dolgachev, Lecture 8](https://sites.lsa.umich.edu/idolga-new/wp-content/uploads/sites/1467/2024/08/ModularBook.pdf)
for the modular index and genus formula. In particular:

| N | µ | V | E | F | genus | 2g+1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
|5|60|12|30|20|0|1|
|6|72|12|36|24|1|3|
|7|168|24|84|56|3|7|
|8|192|24|96|64|5|11|
|9|324|36|162|108|10|21|

The first odd hyperbolic levels do give 7 and 21 under the final
column. Level 7 and level 8 even have the same vertex count, but
level 9 does not. Three uniqueness tests constrain these equalities
in three different arithmetic families:

1. **The cusp tie.** For odd prime p and b≥2, V(X(p))=V(X(2^b))
   says that the consecutive coprime factors (p−1)/2,(p+1)/2
   have product 3·2^(2b−4). The odd factor must be 1 or 3. The
   consecutive possibilities (1,2),(2,3),(3,4) give only the last
   product of the required form; so (p,b)=(7,3).
2. **Genus doubling.** For a≥2,b≥3, g(X(3^a))=2g(X(2^b)) only
   at (a,b)=(2,3). Put x=3^(a−1), y=2^(b−1), z=y−1. The equation
   is z(z²−3)=2x²(x−2). Hence 3|z, b is odd, and v₃(z)+1=2a−2.
   Since v₃(z)=1+v₃(b−1) by LTE on 4^((b−1)/2)−1,
   b−1≥2·3^(2a−4). Meanwhile y³/4≤y²(y−3)=2x²(x−2)−2<2x³,
   so y<2x and b−1≤2a−2. This contradicts a≥3 and leaves b=3
   when a=2.
3. **Three times genus plus one.** For odd prime p≥7 and a≥2,
   g(X(3^a))=3g(X(p))+1 only at (a,p)=(2,7). With x=3^(a−1)
   the equation factors as (p−3)(p−5)(p+2)=8x²(x−2). Exactly one
   left factor is divisible by 3, forcing p+2≥x². If x≥9, then
   p−5≥x²−7>2x, so the three factors have product greater than
   8x³, impossible. For x=3 the left product is strictly
   increasing for p≥7 and p=7 works.

These are genuine arithmetic coincidences with small rigidity
results, but genus doubling is not an unramified degree-two
cover: g−1 would have to multiply by the degree. In fact X(9)
cannot be an unramified cover of X(8) or X(7) of any degree, since
9 is divisible by neither 4 nor 2. Nor is a degree-three covering
of X(7) inferred from g(X(9))=3g(X(7))+1. The natural principal-level
reduction cover X(M)→X(N) instead requires N|M.

## The actual relative sign at level 72

Level 6 gives G₆≅S₃×A₄, order 72, exponent 6. Thus its group
does not act on anything as a single 72-cycle. Its translation
subgroup has order 12 and is the commutator A₃×V₄; the rotation
quotient is C₆. In the Eisenstein flat model the oriented triangle
group (2,3,6) is Z[ω]⋊C₆, with ω a primitive cube root of 1.
The kernel of its map onto G₆ has no rotational part, since
abelianization is C₆ on both sides and the induced map is
surjective. It is a rotation-invariant translation lattice of
index 12, so equals the unique norm-12 Eisenstein ideal
(2)(1−ω) (2 inert, 3 ramified). The geometric group-order
factorization is 12·6, not a cyclic mod-72 control.

Actual **level 72** has a different relation to 8 and 9. The
Chinese remainder theorem first gives a product of special linear
groups, then one divides by the diagonal global sign. Thus
```
1 → C₂ → G₇₂ → G₈ × G₉ → 1.
```
The kernel's nontrivial element is represented by 17I, sign + at
8 and sign − at 9. It is central but the extension does not split:
over the G₈ factor alone the preimage is SL₂(Z/8), and the image of
S=[[0,−1],[1,0]] has order 2 while its two lifts both square to
−I and have order 4. This is an actual relative-sign sidecar.

Moreover that central involution acts freely on the compact
curve X(72). The only nontrivial point stabilizers are conjugates
of cyclic inertia at the branch points, generated by images of
S, ST, or T=[[1,1],[0,1]]. A nontrivial central class lies in
one such conjugate iff it lies in the inertia group itself.
The mixed scalar is neither S nor a power of ST (order 3), nor a
power of the unipotent T. The normalized fiber product
Y=X(8)×_{X(1)}X(9) is connected (the joint deck quotient is
surjective) and is this degree-two unramified quotient of X(72).
One gets µ₇₂=124416, g(X(72))=9505, g(Y)=4753.
For any collection of pairwise coprime levels N_i≥3, the same
argument gives a free relative-sign deck group C₂^(k−1) at their
product level. Level 6 has no such spare sign because −I=I at 2.

## Why even the scalar mod-72 test cannot see the holes

Every odd residue modulo 72 occurs as H(T), in fact infinitely many
times. Ordinal sums multiply Hamiltonian-path counts. The following
outgoing row masks (vertex 0 is low bit) are direct witnesses:
```
H=3:  4 1 2
H=5:  8 1 3 6
H=11: 24 1 3 6 14
H=13: 18 8 3 5 14
```
In the unit group modulo 72, 5·13≡1 mod 8 and ≡2 mod 9, so
it generates the full mod-9 unit factor. Independently 5 and 11
generate the mod-8 unit group. Hence their multiplicative closure
gives every unit mod 72. Multiplying by 3 gives all odd residues
with 3-adic valuation one modulo 72; multiplying by 9 gives
every remaining odd residue. Powers of 5⁶≡1 mod72 then produce
infinitely many different absolute counts at each residue.
For the two holes explicitly, 5³·11=1375≡7 and
3·5·11=165≡21. Thus a residue-only test cannot prove their
nonrealization, though a carrier with extra state is not excluded.

The same four witnesses in fact fill every odd residue modulo
2^a 3^b, for all finite a,b. It suffices to consider a≥3,b≥2
and then reduce. The unit group has a 2-primary factor with
mod-squares quotient U(8)×U(3); the mod-72 result shows that
5,11,13 span this quotient. In a finite abelian 2-group P,
K P²=P implies K=P (otherwise the nontrivial quotient P/K
would equal its squares). On the 3-primary factor, 65 generates
the full order-3^(b−1) subgroup after projecting to that
factor: v₃(65²−1)=1, and LTE gives its order mod 3^b. Both
primary projections are realized by suitable group powers of
our generators, so the total generated group is all units.
For a residue divisible by exactly 3^v, divide that factor
out and lift its unit part; for 0 mod 3^b use 3^b. Multiplication
of counts by H=3 supplies these factors. Thus even joint finite
2/3-adic arithmetic cannot exclude an absolute count without
some further noncongruence information.

The same distinction holds for actions: a single X(9) flag
orbit of size 7 or 21 cannot occur, since 7 does not divide
|G₉|=324. At X(7), a Paley tournament does carry an order-21
subgroup of G₇, but its free action on Hamilton paths gives a
divisibility condition, not H=21. The old archive explicitly
separates those two readings.
