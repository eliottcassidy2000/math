## 0. Typing rule

A result **overcomes** barrier X (`O`) when its conclusion is false for the control X, whether provably or conjecturally. Its proof therefore uses a feature that X lacks, and the cell names that feature. A result is **blind** to X (`B`) when its conclusion still holds for X, whether proved, conjectured or trivial. In that case neither the result nor any method that proves only it can rule out X's failure mode for `3x+1`.

Failure modes:
* **SHEET:** extra integer cycles, as for `3x+1` on the negative integers: `{-1}`, `{-5,-7,-10}`, `{-17,...,-34}`.
* **DRIFT:** divergent orbits, as expected for `5x+1`.
* **DEFECT:** a cycle or divergent orbit planted on a density-zero set.
* **INTEGRAL:** rational cycles in `Z_(2)`, one for every periodic parity word.
* **UNIFORM:** `O` means the proof uses map-specific data or proves a statement that is decidable for each map (bounded complexity). `B` means the proof runs unchanged on an explicit class of generalized maps.
* **DIM:** `O` means the argument closes on a thin exceptional set or needs no descent certificate. `B` means the set it leaves unresolved is the `h(log_3 2)=0.9500`-dimensional non-descending set.

`n/a` marks a result about another system, or an equivalent reformulation. A reformulation moves every barrier into its target statement, so only what it actually proves gets typed.

## 2. What the pattern says

1. **The conjecture splits along the barriers.** Bernstein–Lagarias show that the Periodicity Conjecture (no rational divergent orbits) is equivalent to "no `3x+k` map with `k≡±1 (mod 6)` has a divergent integer orbit". Monks–Yazinski show it is equivalent to `Ω(Q_odd)⊆Q_odd`.
   * *No divergent orbits* is therefore a statement about all sheets at once. It faces DRIFT, DEFECT, DIM and UNIFORM, but not SHEET or INTEGRAL.
   * *No nontrivial cycles* faces SHEET, INTEGRAL and DEFECT, but not DRIFT: `5x+1` has cycles, and Steiner's method settles circuits for each `Q`.
2. **Published mechanisms split the same way, and their blind spots are complementary.**
   * Every mechanism that overcomes DRIFT is blind to SHEET, INTEGRAL and DEFECT: the Terras/Everett → Korec → Tao line, the KL and Wirsching counting results, and the K–L models. For Tao this blindness is now a theorem (GGM cover `3N−1`).
   * Every mechanism that overcomes SHEET or INTEGRAL (Steiner, Eliahou, SdW, Hercher, Monks–Yazinski's self-conjugacy theorems, verification) combines positivity, integrality and a verification bound. Each is limited to bounded complexity (`≤91` minima, `n<2^71`, or self-conjugate cycles) and says nothing about divergence.
3. **Never overcome for `3x+1` by a published all-orbits mechanism: SHEET and DRIFT at unbounded complexity.** No argument uses the sign for arbitrary orbits, and none uses the drift for every orbit; not even one `5x+1` orbit is proved divergent (K–L).
   * DIMENSION has been overcome only in relaxations or analogues: A–L (choice through wild multipliers), Caraiani (`5x+1`), Farkas's variant (also proved automatically by YAH) and the `F_2[x]` analogue. This session's E-graph is a candidate only: its thinness is FINITE-EXACT evidence plus a conjecture (HYP-9120).
   * Each of these is blind to SHEET and DRIFT, or has no sheets at all.
4. **Removing DIMENSION by choice exposes SHEET.**
   * A–L's single uneliminable class, `−1 mod 2^j`, is the 2-adic neighbourhood of the minus sheet's fixed point `−1`. The E-graph's hostile points are likewise negative rationals accumulating at `−1`.
   * A–L escape `−1` with an infinite multiplier family, and that escape is sheet-blind: the weak statement also covers every integer that reaches a known `3x−1` cycle (§4), and it holds for `5x+1` (Caraiani).
   * A proof by the choice route needs a sheet-sensitive escape for the `−1` family. None was found in the literature searched.
5. **UNIFORM binds only uniform certificate searches.**
   * YAH's automated proofs succeeded on residue statements whose negative-integer analogues also hold. Their prize conjectures 4.12–4.14 fail on the negative integers (§4), so any proof of them must use the sign. Conj. 4.11 is not refuted there.
   * Results of bounded complexity escape UNIFORM because they are decidable for each map.
   * Lehtonen gets undecidability with multiplier 3 and divisor 2 fixed, by varying the additive term (the sheet parameter) non-periodically.
6. **Hybrids with complementary blind spots:**
   * (a) Mixing (`O`: DRIFT) plus Diophantine cycle exclusion (`O`: SHEET, INTEGRAL, DEFECT) covers four barriers. What remains between a log-density-zero exceptional set and cycles with at most 91 minima is exactly the orbits of unbounded complexity.
   * (b) Choice certificates (`O`: DIM) plus a sheet-sensitive escape for `−1`, which would need the kind of positivity and integrality used in cycle exclusion.
   * (c) Predecessor counting (`O`: DRIFT) plus integrality of tree roots.

## 3. Corrections and refinements to the barrier list

* **UNIFORM is overstated.** Kurtz–Simon's `Π^0_2`-completeness rules out a uniform decision procedure and any r.e. proof system that is *complete* for the family. It does not rule out a uniform *sound* method, such as YAH's search, proving `3x+1` itself. GGM draw the weaker, correct conclusion: a proof must use special properties of the Collatz map. Per-map statements of bounded complexity (m-cycles for fixed `m`, verification) are decidable, so UNIFORM never binds them.
* **SHEET is confirmed, with a caveat.** B–L 1996 state that `x↦kx` conjugates `T` to `T_{3,k}` for odd `k`, and Wegner (arXiv 2101.08060) lists the three negative cycles. Caveat: a residue-class statement depends on `b` as well as the parity word, so it must be transported by `x↦−x`. Comparing the same residues for `3x−1` on the positives is wrong, because negation swaps classes, e.g. 3 ↔ 5 mod 8.
* **DRIFT is confirmed.** `5x+1` divergence is conjectural for every single orbit (K–L). The Terras mechanism provably transfers upward (K–L Thm 7.2), and "positive measure" is a statement about the 2-adic model.
* **INTEGRAL: attribution.** Rational periodic points exist for every periodic word because `Φ(Q∩Z_2)⊆Q∩Z_2` (Bernstein 1994, restated in B–L 1996) and by the Böhm–Sontacchi cycle formula. Lagarias 1990 studies which denominators occur, through the cycles of `T_k`.
* **DIMENSION: the value is not in print (§5).**
  * A–L needed an infinite multiplier family `(2^j+1)/3` and a see-saw with the wild-numbers statement just to handle *one* exceptional class. "Finitely many escape lemmas" should read "finitely many parametrized escape families".
  * The criterion is a heuristic, not a theorem. It is consistent with every closed induction found (A–L, Caraiani, Farkas/YAH, `F_2[x]`).
  * Korec's `log_4 3` is the quantitative ceiling of a single residue horizon.
* **The choice relaxation is not free.** A–L overcome DIM while being PROVED blind to DRIFT (Caraiani) and FINITE-EXACT blind to SHEET (§4).

## 5. Q1: is `dim_H` of the infinite-stopping-time set stated in the literature?

**Not found.** Sources checked:
* Lagarias's bibliographies I (1963–1999) and II (2000–2009). The only "Hausdorff dimension" entries are Lagarias–Sloane's approximate-squaring `p`-adic exceptional set and a random-product model.
* The [2021 overview](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.02635), which has no occurrence.
* Tao, B–L, and Monks–Yazinski.
* [Rozier 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.00133), whose dimension-1 result concerns a planar embedding, a different object.
* Targeted searches (Hausdorff/Eggleston/Billingsley with stopping time, 2-adic, 3x+1).

The classical ingredients are measure zero (Terras/Everett; the 2-adic form is in Lagarias 1985) and the fact that the parity-vector map is an isometry (B–L App. A). The value `h(log_3 2)` follows from these by Besicovitch–Eggleston, but no source states it. Treat it as a standard consequence, UNVERIFIED as a literature statement, with no priority claimed.

The only dimension claim found is in [Chang 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.11066), an unrefereed, LLM-assisted preprint. It gives `≈0.6942` for the odd 2-adics whose Syracuse valuations all lie in `{1,2}`. That value is exactly `log_2 φ` (from `2^{-s}+2^{-2s}=1`), and the set is different: neither set contains the other. Valuations `(2,2,…)` stop at once, and never-descending words can contain valuation 3, e.g. the prefix `1111001`. Chang also gives a heuristic `≈0.68` for a "candidate divergent set".

The nearest precedent is [Lagarias 2009](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0512006) on Erdős's ternary digits of `2^n`. It proves `dim E^{(1)}(Z_3)=log_3 2` and conjectures that the 3-adic exceptional set `E(Z_3)` has dimension 0 (Erdős's conjecture is equivalent to `1∉E(Z_3)`). Collatz is the case where the set that integers must avoid has *positive* dimension.

## 6. Q2: is the E-graph relaxation studied?

**Yes as a graph; not as a reachability problem, as far as found.** [Le & Smith, arXiv 2109.01180](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.01180) (v4 2023, math.GM, unrefereed) define the "Loosened Collatz Graph" by `l(n)={n/2, 3n+1}` for even `n` and `{3n+1}` for odd `n`, with `n≥1`. This is exactly `E`.
* They extend the Böhm–Sontacchi cycle formula to tuples with zero entries.
* They prove that admissible tuples are closed under rotation, and that the tuples with a fixed value form a unique-factorization monoid.
* Conj. 1: every `n` with `3∤n` lies on a cycle.
* Conj. 2: every admissible tuple other than `(2,…,2)` contains a 0, which would rule out nontrivial Collatz cycles.

They do **not** state Q1, Q2 or strong connectivity. Q1∧Q2 implies their Conj. 1 (a cycle through 1), but not conversely.

Related but different relaxations:
* The Farkas/A–L semigroup: multiplicative choice by any wild multiplier; proved.
* [Knill 2013](https://arxiv.org/abs/1311.4261): networks on `Z_n` generated unconditionally by `x↦2x` and `x↦3x+1`. They are experimentally connected, and he calls them unrelated to Collatz.
* Althöfer's [3n±1 game](https://althofer.de/collatz-prizes.html) (2023, web only, with a prize): two players choose `3n+1` or `3n−1` and then halve down to an odd number. The page reports a claimed proof under review as of 2026-09-07. The one-player version is trivial: exactly one of `3n±1` is divisible by 4, so `(3n±1)/4<n`.

Searches for "nondeterministic Collatz", "Collatz with choice", "Collatz relation", "weak Collatz" and "reachability" found nothing else. The only "weak" hit is the A–L semigroup.
