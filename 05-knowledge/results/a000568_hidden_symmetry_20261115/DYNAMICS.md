# A local law for arc and directed-triangle reversals

Write $\kappa(T)=H(T)2^{n-1}/n!$. Random means independent fair orientations, unless a given triple is conditioned to be a directed triangle. The difference $\Delta$ is the original count minus the count after reversal. For any designated edge $e$ and directed triangle $C$,
$$
\mathbb E[(\Delta_e\kappa)^2]=32n^{-3}+O(n^{-4}),
\qquad
\mathbb E[(\Delta_C\kappa)^2\mid C\text{ cyclic}]
=288n^{-5}+O(n^{-6}).
$$
Thus a score-preserving local move has a typical root-mean-square scale smaller by a factor asymptotic to $3/n$. This is a law for count **increments**, not a local limit theorem for hitting integer targets.

## Proof: the first response that survives the move

Use the exact [even-run Walsh expansion, THM-4761](https://github.com/eliottcassidy2000/math/blob/main/01-canon/theorems/THM-4761-random-tournaments-the-forest-identity-is-the-walsh-chaos-decomposition-of-kappa.md). The degree $L$ coefficient of an even-run linear forest is its signed $2^p/(n)_L$, with $p$ nonempty path components. The degree-two part is $1-\|S\mathbf1\|^2/(n)_2$, with $S_{ij}=1$ for $i\to j$ and $-1$ otherwise.

**Edge.** For $e=\{u,v\}$, write $t_u=\sum_{w\ne u,v}S_{uw}$ and similarly $t_v$. The degree-two difference has square expectation $32(n-2)/(n)_2^2$, since it equals $-4S_{uv}(t_u-t_v)/(n)_2$. The sum over edges of the degree-$L$ flip energies is $4L\operatorname{Var}(\kappa_L)$ by orthogonality; all edges share this equally. The variance formula in THM-4761 implies, for fixed $r$, $\sum_{L\ge2r} L\operatorname{Var}(\kappa_L)=O(n^{-r})$. For a uniform summable bound put $L=2j$, use $(n)_L\ge(n/e)^L$ (the geometric mean of the $L$ largest factors is at least the mean for all $n$ factors), $(n-L)_p\le n^j$ for $p\le j$, and $\sum_p 2^p\binom{j-1}{p-1}/p!\le2\cdot3^{j-1}$. Thus the degree-four and higher edge energy is $O(n^{-4})$. This argument plus the ordinary CLT for the independent signs in $t_u-t_v$ gives $n^{3/2}\Delta_e\kappa\Rightarrow N(0,32)$.

**Triangle.** Fix its three orientations cyclically, for example $0\to1\to2\to0$. All scores stay the same under its reversal, so the degree-two component vanishes. An even-run forest cannot contain all three internal edges. Therefore surviving terms contain exactly one internal edge, and degree $L$ reduces to degree $L-1$ on the independent external signs. Different degrees stay orthogonal under this conditioning.

For degree four, the shapes are one length-four path or two length-two paths (cherries). In a two-cherry term of outside support four, the cherry containing the internal edge has an outside edge incident to a triangle vertex $i$. The other cherry is fully outside. Exchange the internal edge's other endpoint with the unused triangle vertex. The exterior monomial is unchanged and the coefficient changes sign, since the two arcs out of $i$ within the triangle have opposite signs. This cancels the top support. In outside support three there are only two surviving patterns:
* one outside-outside edge, joined at one end to a triangle vertex $j$, and a disjoint external spoke from a different triangle vertex $i$. The length-four and two-cherry contributions have opposite signs and magnitudes $4,8$; the net magnitude is $4$;
* three spokes to two different triangle vertices, two on $i$ and one on $j$. The only completion uses the internal edge from $j$ to the third vertex in the cherry containing the single spoke; magnitude $8$.

For the first pattern choose ordered $i,j$ in six ways and assign the roles of three distinct outside labels in six ways (one at the isolated spoke, one at the joined spoke, one at the far end of the outside edge). In the second choose $i,j$ in six ways and the outside label at the single spoke in three ways. So each outside triple contributes $36\cdot4^2+18\cdot8^2=1728$ to the squared integer numerator. The remaining terms use at most two outside vertices and contribute $O(n^2)$. This gives $288n^{-5}+O(n^{-6})$ after division by $(n)_4^2$. The accompanying enumerator verifies the sharper exact degree-four numerator $576\binom{n-3}{2}+1728\binom{n-3}{3}$ by checking each external support size $0,\dots,4$ on a fixed set; this reduction is exhaustive and uses no statistical fit.

For degree six, any shape except three cherries uses at most eight vertices. With a specified internal edge it has at most six outside vertices, hence energy $O(n^6/(n)_6^2)$; at most three forests can yield one external monomial, because specifying its missing internal edge reconstructs the entire forest. In the three-cherry shape, all terms using seven outside vertices cancel by the same exchange: the other two cherries are fully outside. What remains is again $O(n^{-6})$. For $L\ge8$, telescope the fixed triangle reversal into three single-edge reversals. The expectation of each square under any specified orientation of the triangle is at most eight times its unconditioned expectation. Cauchy–Schwarz for the three increments, the edge energy rule and the tail bound with $r=4$ give $O(n^{-6})$. This completes the proof.

## From labelled moves to isomorphism classes

The same leading estimates hold for uniform isomorphism classes of tournaments marked with one unordered pair, or with one cyclic triangle, respectively. Label sampling and class sampling differ only by the stabilizer tilt. To bound this tilt let $s$ be the moved support of a nonidentity permutation fixing a tournament. It must have odd order; every moved unordered pair lies in an orbit of length at least three, and the only fixed unordered pairs use two fixed vertices. Hence the probability of fixing a random orientation is at most $2^{-s(2n-s-1)/3}$. There are at most $n^s$ permutations of support $s$, and $s\ge3$; summing gives $\mathbb E(|\operatorname{Aut}|-1)=O(n^3 2^{-n})$. Conditioning on the triangle event costs at most a factor four. The stabilizer of a mark is bounded by the whole group, and the labelled law tilted by the marked stabilizer is exactly uniform on marked isomorphism classes. This gives exponentially small total variation. For squared increments, the uniform bound $\kappa=O(1)$ follows from the Hamiltonian-path maximum in [Divoux–Wang](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23755), so the change of measure also preserves the leading second moments.

The absolute scale of either increment still grows rapidly with $n$. A score-preserving move is finer, but generic random moves cannot be declared exact $\pm2$ repairs. A candidate selector should look for atypical boundary cancellation, not just degree balance.
