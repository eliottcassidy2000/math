## 1. Prime-order dictionary and full automorphism sign

The only unordered-edge cycles that return with endpoints exchanged are the antipodal pairs in those vertex cycles; exactly |A|/r such cycles occur. Since |A|/r equals the primitive-root weight, the displayed sign follows. Edges incident with zero contribute no endpoint exchange. Translation automorphisms have odd order and therefore trivial sign.

## 2. Integration, indegrees, and routing

The derivative of an antiperiodic word is m-periodic with odd total weight by telescoping. Conversely the two integrations of any odd-total word are complementary and are rotations by m positions of each other. This proves the source-necklace correspondence, including phase and uniqueness on types.

The indegree trichotomy is correct. For an even-total target of least period d, integration gives z_(i+d)=z_i plus its primitive-root parity. Even parity yields two inequivalent predecessors of exact period d. Odd parity yields complementary predecessors of exact period 2d, identified by a d-step rotation. Odd-total targets have no predecessors.

The path lemma remains valid with loops and isolated one-vertex paths. Deleting one incoming edge at each branch leaves the original sources unchanged and makes the deleted-edge tails the terminals. A finite retained source path cannot enter a cycle, and a terminal's backwards path cannot emerge from a cycle. These give mutually inverse source-to-branch maps.

For m=e q with q odd, the stated U is invariant under rotation and differentiation. D has no nonzero kernel in U and therefore permutes U; D^e=I+R^e sends every word into U. A necklace periodic under D is represented by a word periodic under an actual power of D, so the periodic necklaces are exactly those in U. Each U-target has one predecessor inside U and one outside. Cutting the outside predecessor prevents any source path from entering U, giving the claimed bound e, including the final restored edge. The inverse has the same bound.
