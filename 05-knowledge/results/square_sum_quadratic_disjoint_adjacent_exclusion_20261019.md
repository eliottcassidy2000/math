# Excluding the two adjacent allocations for disjoint quadratic contact poles

**Status: PROVED by explicit exact ideal-membership certificates and elementary rational exclusions.** The certificate verifier uses sparse rational polynomial addition and multiplication, not a Groebner-basis or equation solver.

## 1. Scope and normalization

Use the notation of [the low-degree contact obstruction](square_sum_low_degree_contact_obstruction_20261019.md):

\[
 f(t)=\frac{4t(t^2-1)}{(t^2+1)^2},\qquad
 k_\eta(t)=\frac{t^2+2\eta t-1}{t^2+1},\qquad
 y^2=1+\varepsilon f(t)+\eta f(h(t)).
\]

Suppose `deg h=2` and its two distinct conjugate pole pairs are disjoint from `±i`. The earlier theorem proves that all four poles belong to `Q(i)`.

We may set `epsilon=+1` by replacing the input variable by `-t` if necessary; this merely interchanges the two adjacent patterns treated below. We can absorb `eta` into `h`, since `f(eta h)=eta f(h)`. Thus it suffices to use `z=k_+(h)` and

\[
 r=y-z,\qquad s=y+z,\qquad rs=f(t),\qquad z=(s-r)/2.
\]

Let `D=t²+1`. The divisor argument gives monic quadratics `q_A,q_B`, a nonzero rational `mu`, and a two-element subset `S` of `{0,1,-1,infinity}`, with

\[
 r=\frac4\mu\frac{R_Sq_A}{Dq_B},\qquad
 s=\mu\frac{R_{S^c}q_B}{Dq_A}.                        \tag{1}
\]

The homogeneous products satisfy `R_S R_{S^c}=t(t²−1)` in the affine chart. Swapping `r,s` replaces `z` by `-z`; the latter is still a contact composition, since `k_+(-1/h)=-k_+(h)`. Thus complementary allocations are equivalent. The six allocations reduce to the three representatives containing zero:

\[
 \{0,1\},\quad\{0,-1\},\quad\{0,\infty\}.             \tag{2}
\]

This note excludes the first two. It does not rely on an unproved symmetry between them.

Write

\[
 q_B=(t-a)^2+b^2=t^2-2at+q,\qquad q=a^2+b^2,
\]
\[
 q_A=(t-c)^2+d^2=t^2-2ct+\rho,
 \quad a,b,c,d\in\mathbb Q,
 \quad b d\ne0,\quad \rho=c^2+d^2.                   \tag{3}
\]

Because `h` has coprime numerator and denominator, the quadratic `P+iQ` has one root from each conjugate pair. Choosing the signs of `b,d` accordingly, write it as a nonzero Gaussian rational constant times

\[
 (t-a-bi)(t-c-di)=X+iY,
\]
\[
 X=t^2-(a+c)t+ac-bd,\qquad Y=-(b+d)t+ad+bc.
\]

Then `X²+Y²=q_Aq_B`, and there are rational `U,V` such that

\[
 z=\frac{U(X^2-Y^2)-2VXY}{q_Aq_B},\qquad U^2+V^2=2.   \tag{4}
\]

Indeed, if `P+iQ=gamma(X+iY)`, use the complex coefficient `(1-i)gamma/conjugate(gamma)`. Allowing every rational `U,V` on this norm-two circle only enlarges the necessary system; no converse is needed.

In both adjacent patterns, `R_S` has degree two and its complement has degree one. Comparing the leading coefficient of (1) and (4) gives

\[
 U=-2/\mu.
\]

Put `m=mu` and `v=mV`; then

\[
 v^2-2m^2+4=0.                                      \tag{5}
\]

The numerator in (1) must be divisible by `D`, because `z` has no poles at `±i`. At `i`, this says

\[
 \left(\frac{q_A(i)}{q_B(i)}\right)^2
   =\frac{m^2 R_{S^c}(i)}{4R_S(i)}.                  \tag{6}
\]

The square root in (6) has two signs. Simultaneously replacing `y,z` by `-y,-z` changes `m` to `-m`, leaves the two pole pairs fixed, and is realized again by `h -> -1/h`. Thus either square-root sign is covered by taking the positive displayed sign below and allowing every nonzero rational `m`.

## 2. The two complete coefficient systems

### Pattern A: `S={0,1}`

Here `R_S=t(t−1)`, `R_{S^c}=t+1`. Equation (6) becomes `q_A(i)/q_B(i)=im/2`, so

\[
 c=-\frac m4(q-1),\qquad \rho=1+ma.                 \tag{7A}
\]

### Pattern B: `S={0,-1}`

Here `R_S=t(t+1)`, `R_{S^c}=t−1`. Equation (6) becomes `q_A(i)/q_B(i)=m/2`, so

\[
 c=ma/2,\qquad \rho=1+\frac m2(q-1).                 \tag{7B}
\]

For either pattern substitute the corresponding (7) in (3) and (4), and define

\[
 E(t)=4D(X^2-Y^2+vXY)
       +m^2R_{S^c}q_B^2-4R_Sq_A^2.                  \tag{8}
\]

Equations (1), (4), and (5) imply identically `E(t)=0`. Its degree-six leading coefficient cancels exactly, so its coefficients at degrees `0,...,5` all vanish. Together with

\[
 16(d^2-\rho+c^2)=0,\qquad v^2-2m^2+4=0,             \tag{9}
\]

these are eight explicit rational polynomial equations in `(a,b,d,v,m)`. Their integer numerator forms are generated exactly by the standard-library-only `rational_contact_certificate_core.py`; multiplying any row by a nonzero rational constant does not change its vanishing condition. No nonzero-parameter division other than the already justified `m!=0` was used to obtain this necessary system.

## 3. Pattern A: a positive polynomial belongs to the ideal

Let `I_A` be the ideal generated by the eight equations (8)–(9) using (7A). The supplied exact certificate proves

\[
 \boxed{m^2+4\in I_A.}                               \tag{10}
\]

This has no real zero. In particular the necessary coefficient system has no rational solution, and Pattern A is impossible.

The certificate is a finite straight-line polynomial combination with 349 nodes. Each input node is one displayed equation; every subsequent node is a sum of polynomial multiples of earlier nodes, all with rational coefficients. The last node is exactly `m²+4`. This is an explicit ideal-membership proof, not a numerical root search or an inference from modular arithmetic.

## 4. Pattern B: rational candidates force a degenerate pole configuration

Let `I_B` be the ideal generated using (7B). The second certificate proves

\[
 \boxed{(m-2)^3(m+2)(m^2+20m+52)\in I_B.}            \tag{11}
\]

The last quadratic has discriminant `192=64*3`, so its roots are irrational. Every rational solution therefore has `m=2` or `m=−2`.

If `m=2`, (7B) gives `c=a,rho=q`, hence `q_A=q_B`. This contradicts the distinct-pole-pair hypothesis.

Suppose `m=−2`. Equation (5) gives `v=2sigma` for `sigma in {±1}`. The coefficient of `t^5` in (8) is `-8(2a+sigma(b+d))`, hence

\[
 d=-2\sigma a-b.                                    \tag{12}
\]

After that substitution, the coefficient of `t^4` and the first relation in (9), divided by nonzero numerical constants, give

\[
 3a^2+2a-b^2+1=0,\qquad
 3a^2+2\sigma ab+b^2-1=0.                            \tag{13}
\]

Adding them yields `2a(3a+1+sigma b)=0`.

If `a=0`, (13) gives `b²=1`, so `q_B=t²+1=D`. This contradicts disjointness from the input pole pair.

If `a!=0`, then `b=−sigma(3a+1)`. Substitution in the second equation of (13) gives `2a(3a+2)=0`, so necessarily

\[
 a=-2/3,\quad b=\sigma,\quad d=\sigma/3.
\]

But the coefficient of `t` in (8) is then `128/27`, not zero. This final contradiction excludes Pattern B.
