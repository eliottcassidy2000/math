1. Front promotion

A strong tournament on at least three vertices has an in-neighbour of its current first vertex. Its last in-neighbour cannot be the second vertex. Removing the first vertex and reinserting it after that last in-neighbour leaves all consecutive arcs forward, including the new outgoing arc by maximality.

The recurrence argument is correctly quantified. A vertex w that is eventually never first has nonincreasing positive integer position, hence a stabilized position. If it dominates a recurrent first vertex u, a sufficiently late step starting at u inserts u after w and lowers that stabilized position. Therefore the recurrent first set S dominates its complement. It is nonempty by finiteness and cannot be proper in a strong tournament. No unproved time bound is used.

2. Initial strong component

The SCC condensation of a tournament is a transitive tournament. Every directed Hamilton word traverses its components as contiguous blocks in the unique forward order. Front promotion operates within the initial strong block and preserves every join to a later block. If the initial component is a singleton, its vertex is already first. Thus a chosen vertex of the initial component can always be made first and is then removable. T-v need not be strong.

3. Common deletion and genuine edges

If P and Q are distinct Hamilton words differing by relocation of u, removing u yields a directed common word R. For endpoint u all retained adjacencies are already in P. For internal u with neighbours a,b, the only potentially missing adjacency is a->b; it is present in Q, since Q inserts u in a different gap. This covers moves in either direction, including positions adjacent to the old gap.

One must not infer that arbitrary deletion preserves directedness. In the directed triangle a->b->c->a, deleting b from (a,b,c) leaves the invalid word (a,c).

4. Edge lifting and fibres

The old and new insertion gaps for u are valid in R. Any valid gap for the new vertex v can be used. Separate gaps cause no conflict. In a shared interior gap (a,b), both vertices individually satisfy the incoming and outgoing outer arcs. Ordering u,v according to their mutual arc therefore gives a directed pair. Endpoint gaps simply omit the nonexistent constraint. Removing v from the lifts recovers P,Q, and removing u from both gives the same R with v inserted. The lifts are distinct and truly adjacent, not merely equivalent after quotienting.

All insertions of v above a fixed base path form a nonempty clique. These cliques and the lifted base edges connect the full v-removable subgraph. The promotion lemma then connects every other word to that subgraph, completing induction.

5. Quotient, reflection and metagraph scope

Aut(T) acts freely on ordered lists containing every vertex, preserves relocation, and identifies precisely equal normalized fixed-path tilings. The quotient of a connected graph is connected, although moves can become loops. The directed triangle has three labelled words but one quotient vertex, so positive quotient nonloop degree does not follow.

Coordinate reflection represents word reversal together with tournament converse. It connects the two ordinary fibres of a non-self-converse merged class, and remains within an ordinary fibre in the self-converse case. Hence relocation plus reflection has exactly the merged fibres as components.

These auxiliary moves are not complement lines, blue/black metagraph edges, Hamming-one flips, or arbitrary coordinate flips. LOCAL_FOREST_ADDENDUM.txt retains all the original coordinate-coverage, complement, colour and exact-node-count conditions.
