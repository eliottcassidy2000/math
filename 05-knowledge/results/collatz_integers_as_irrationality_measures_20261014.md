## 1. The owner's picture, made precise

The irrationality exponent `mu(x)` of a real number sorts the reals into classes indexed by `[1, infinity]`: `mu = 1` exactly for the rationals; `mu = 2` for almost every real (Khinchin), for every algebraic irrational (Roth), and for `e` and the Hurwitz numbers `e^(1/k)`, `tanh(1/k)` (Euler's continued fraction of `e` has linearly growing partial quotients; Davis 1978 for the exact measure, CITED from memory), while for `pi`, `log 2`, `zeta(3)` and `log2 3` the value `2` is conjectured and only effective upper bounds are known; `mu = n >= 3` for the Jarnik numbers, which are constructed explicitly by continued fractions whose partial quotients grow like `q_n^(n-2)` and which form a set of Hausdorff dimension `2/n` (Jarnik, Besicovitch); `mu = infinity` for the Liouville numbers. The owner's sentence "the integers above 2 each correspond to a constructed continued fraction or family of them" is Jarnik's theorem; "1 and 2 are the abnormality" is Roth's dichotomy (degree one is the only algebraic degree with `mu = 1`; every other algebraic number sits at `2`, the same class as almost everything).

THM-4632 puts the Collatz transfer law on this ladder. The untwisted law `R_x(s) = 1/(1 - e^(-s)/2 - e^(s(x-1))/2)` makes sense for every parameter `x` in `(1, 2)` (`x = theta = log2 3` is the Collatz case; the trivial character of THM-4629), and its boundary growth exponent on the line `Re s = 0` is

`limsup_(f -> infinity) log |R_x(2 pi i f)| / log f = 2 (mu(x) - 1)`.

So the integer `n >= 2` labels the laws whose transfer function grows like `f^(2n - 2 + o(1))`, and the rational parameters (`mu = 1`) are the laws with actual poles on the line, `2 pi i Q`-periodic, the rational approximants of HYP-9260, of exponent `+infinity`; `n = 2` is the generic class, where the Collatz law is expected to live; `n >= 3` are Jarnik laws, a continuum of dimension `2/n` each. The Collatz conjecture-adjacent statement "`mu(log2 3) = 2`" is exactly "the Collatz transfer function grows no faster than `f^(2 + epsilon)` on its line"; Rhin's effective measure gives `f^(26.6)`.

The pole at `s = 0` is the empty word `(Q, P) = (0, 0)`, the normalisation of the step-count law, present for every parameter, and for irrational `x` it is the only pole on the line (THM-4632 statement 3). ## 4. The sources, typed

**(a) `mu(log r) = 2` (Liu, Jiang, Zhang, arXiv:2610.10192, 7-8 October 2026; PREPRINT CLAIM, abstract only).** The claim is `mu(log r) = 2` for every positive rational `r != 1`, by a "separated-weight determinant argument" with an interpolation theorem for logarithmic jets, with consequences for nonzero rational linear combinations of such logarithms, rational affine changes, and logarithms of positive numbers raised to a rational positive integer power. The best refereed bounds are `mu(log 2) <= 3.57455` (Marcovecchio 2009) and `mu(log 3) <= 5.125` (Salikhov, after Rhin's `8.616`); an aggregator reports the preprint as "improving `3.5746` to `2`". For this repository the relevant number is the ratio `theta = log 3/log 2`, not a logarithm of a rational: `mu(theta)` is the exponent of the linear form `|Q ln 3 - P ln 2|` in two free integers, and `mu(log 2) = mu(log 3) = 2` says nothing about it (a ratio of two numbers of exponent `2` can have any exponent). ## 5. The characters are the cusps of the Platonic modular curves

HYP-9261 colours a Farey vertex `P/Q` by the signed primitive pair `(Q, P) mod m`; up to sign there are `J_2(m)/2` colours for `m >= 3` (`3` for `m = 2`), and `J_2(m)/2 = m^2 prod_(p | m)(1 - p^(-2))/2` is the number of cusps of the principal congruence modular curve `X(m)` (Part 4: `3, 4, 6, 12, 12, 24, 24, 36, 36, 60, 48` for `m = 2..12`).
