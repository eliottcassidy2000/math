# Prime-field surfaces and finite-field vertex identifications

**Status:** PROVED by the direct arguments below; FINITE-EXACT controls at
orders 3, 5, 7, and 9. Independent publication review is still required.
**Scope:** odd finite fields; an explicitly two-dimensional triangle complex.
**Role:** separate addendum to `session17_prime_shapes_20261020`; the earlier
25-path/F5/icosahedron packet is not modified.
**Priority:** no novelty claim. The underlying graphs are the classical
Platonic graphs over finite fields. The contribution here is a self-contained
structural proof and its precise connection to the marked session-17 model.

## 1. Inheritance, exact object, and theorem

The inherited five-field bridge identifies the twelve nonzero sign-orbits of
F5² with a marked quotient of the 25 Hamiltonian paths of T_(3,3), and with one
previously certified regular-five return component. Its edge predicate is
`det(v,w) = ±1`. See `F5_PATH_PAIR_ICOSAHEDRON.md` in the separate
`session17_prime_shapes_20261020` packet. This addendum retains that predicate
and changes the field, not the path-marking claim.

Let q=p^r be an odd prime power, let k=Fq, and let k0=Fp be its prime subfield.
Write [v]={v,-v}. Define a graph G_q by

- vertices: (k² minus {0})/{±1};
- adjacency: [v]~[w] exactly when det(v,w) belongs to {1,-1}.

The predicate does not depend on the representatives. There are no loops,
since det(v,v)=0. Let K_q be the **two-dimensional simplicial complex** whose
vertices and edges are those of G_q and whose two-faces are all graph triangles.
There are no simplices of dimension three or more in this definition. In
characteristic three this restriction is essential: K_3 is the tetrahedron
boundary, not the filled tetrahedron.

### Theorem FF-SURFACE

1. G_q is connected and q-regular. Every edge belongs to exactly two triangles.
   The link of every vertex of K_q is a disjoint union of q/p cycles C_p.
   All triangular faces admit a consistent orientation.
2. K_q is a connected closed orientable surface if and only if r=1.
   For q=p its counts and genus are

   V=(p²-1)/2,  E=p(p²-1)/4,  F=p(p²-1)/6,

   g_p = 1 + (p²-1)(p-6)/24.

3. For general q, split each vertex into one copy for each connected component
   of its link. This combinatorial normalization is the disjoint union of

   c(q,p) = q(q²-1) / (p(p²-1))

   copies of K_p. Its components are naturally indexed by
   SL2(Fq)/SL2(Fp), meaning the left cosets g SL2(Fp). Each original vertex is
   the image of q/p normalized vertices, one in each of q/p distinct surfaces.
   Distinct surfaces can meet only at vertices, never along an edge or face.

Here "normalization" means precisely the link-component splitting above; no
algebraic-geometric normalization is being asserted.

## 2. Links and the two triangles at an edge

SL2(k) acts on nonzero vectors, transitively: any nonzero v can be completed
to an ordered basis (v,w) with determinant one. It therefore acts transitively
on our vertices and preserves adjacency. It suffices to calculate a link at
N=[(1,0)].

Every neighbor of N has a unique representative U_x=(x,1), for x in k.
Uniqueness follows because only one of the two representatives has second
coordinate one, since the characteristic is odd. Directly,

  det(U_x,U_y)=x-y.

Thus two neighbors are joined in the link exactly when x-y=±1. The additive
subgroup generated by 1 is k0. The link components are the additive cosets
x+k0, each with cyclic edges t~t±1. Each is C_p, including C_3 when p=3.
There are q/p components. This proves regularity and the asserted links.

Now take an edge and choose representatives v,w with det(v,w)=1. Any common
neighbor has a representative z=av+bw. The two adjacency conditions force
b=±1 and a=±1. After quotienting by simultaneous sign there are exactly two
possibilities:

  [z]=[v+w] or [v-w].

They are distinct in odd characteristic and distinct from [v] and [w]. Hence
every edge is in exactly two triangular faces. There are no additional
triangles omitted by this calculation.

## 3. Connectivity and orientation

### 3.1 Connectivity without assuming the prime-field conclusion

Let C be the connected component of G_q containing N. Every upper unipotent

  T_t = [[1,t],[0,1]],  t in k,

fixes N, so its automorphism preserves C. The matrix

  S = [[0,-1],[1,0]]

sends N to [(0,1)], a neighbor of N, and therefore also preserves C. These
matrices generate SL2(k): conjugating upper unipotents by S gives lower
unipotents, and elementary row reduction expresses every determinant-one
matrix using upper and lower unipotents. More explicitly, elimination first
produces a diagonal matrix diag(a,a^-1), and this diagonal matrix is also a
product of unipotents, since

  U(a) L(-a^-1) U(a) = [[0,a],[-a^-1,0]],
  [[0,a],[-a^-1,0]] [[0,-1],[1,0]] = diag(a,a^-1).

Here U(t) and L(t) denote upper and lower unipotents. If the initial upper-left
entry vanishes, multiplication by S supplies a nonzero pivot. Consequently
SL2(k) preserves C. Transitivity gives C=V(G_q).

### 3.2 A globally consistent face orientation

For a face, choose representatives a,b,c satisfying

  a+b+c=0 and det(a,b)=1.

Such representatives exist by the two-third-vertex calculation. For the face
[v],[w],[v+w], use (a,b,c)=(v,w,-v-w). For the other face at that edge, use
(a,b,c)=(-w,v,w-v). The resulting cyclic ordering of the three vertex classes
is well-defined: the sign choices satisfying a+b+c=0 are unique up to a
simultaneous sign, because v,w are linearly independent; cyclic permutation
preserves the determinant, while reversal negates it. Simultaneous sign does
not change the determinant or the vertex classes.

The two displayed orientations traverse their common edge in opposite
directions, respectively [v] to [w] and [w] to [v]. Thus all faces are
consistently oriented. SL2(k) preserves this orientation, and any linear
transformation with determinant -1 reverses it.

When q=p the links are single circles, so K_p is a closed two-manifold. It is
connected by Section 3.1 and orientable by Section 3.2. For r>1 a vertex link
has more than one component, and a sufficiently small punctured neighborhood
of the vertex is disconnected. Such a point is not a two-manifold point.

## 4. Counts and genus

There are (q²-1)/2 vertices. Degree q gives q(q²-1)/4 edges. Two faces at each
edge and three edges per face give q(q²-1)/6 faces. Hence, for every odd q,

  χ(K_q) = (q²-1)(6-q)/12.

Only when q=p may one directly apply χ=2-2g to this connected complex. In that
case the genus is the stated g_p. In particular:

| p | vertices | edges | triangles | genus |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 3 | 4 | 6 | 4 | 0 |
| 5 | 12 | 30 | 20 | 0 |
| 7 | 24 | 84 | 56 | 3 |

K_3 is the tetrahedron boundary. K_5 is the icosahedron, with the explicit
coordinate certificate inherited from the five-field packet. For p≥7 the
one-skeleton itself is nonplanar: its edge count pV/2 is greater than 3V-6.
No geometrical realization in Euclidean three-space is implied by the genus
calculation for larger primes.

**No-torus corollary.** No K_p has genus one: p=3 and p=5 give genus zero,
and p≥7 gives g_p≥3. Consequently no normalization component over any odd
finite field is a torus. Surface genus is an incidence invariant here; no knot
embedding or torus-knot interpretation follows.

## 5. The normalization and its exact coset index

### 5.1 Each edge selects one prime-field plane

Let [v]~[w] and choose det(v,w)=1. Define the two-dimensional k0-linear plane

  W(v,w) = k0 v + k0 w  within k².

Changing representatives or swapping the endpoint classes does not change W,
because -1 is in k0. Both third vertices v+w and v-w lie in W. Moreover, each
pair of vertices in either face spans the same W. Thus W is constant along
edge-sharing chains of triangular faces.

For any such W, an oriented determinant-one k0-basis identifies W with k0².
This identification preserves det=±1, signs, edges, and all triangular faces.
The subcomplex with vertex set (W minus {0})/{±1} is therefore isomorphic to
K_p, including its orientation. Different determinant-one bases differ by
SL2(k0); a particular basis marking is not canonical.

Distinct W-subcomplexes share no edge. Indeed, an edge in their intersection
would have two k0-independent endpoints spanning both W's. In particular they
share no triangle. They may share several isolated vertices: "only at
vertices" does not mean "at at most one vertex."

Every edge and face belongs to one of these W-subcomplexes, by its construction.
A K_p surface has connected face-adjacency graph: faces incident to one vertex
form a connected cycle in its link, and the one-skeleton is connected. Thus
the W-subcomplexes are exactly the face-adjacency components, rather than just
unions of such components.

### 5.2 Components as SL2 cosets

The planes W above are exactly g(k0²), for g in SL2(k): the columns of g are
a determinant-one basis. The stabilizer of k0² is exactly SL2(k0), because a
matrix preserving that plane has its two columns in k0², and its determinant
is one. Consequently W's are indexed by the left cosets

  {g SL2(k0): g in SL2(k)}.

To count SL2(Fs), choose its first nonzero column in s²-1 ways. For each first
column v, the linear equation det(v,w)=1 has s solutions w. Hence
|SL2(Fs)|=s(s²-1), yielding the component count in Theorem FF-SURFACE.

### 5.3 Why splitting the links gives precisely these components

At N=[e1], the W's containing N have the form

  W_x = k0 e1 + k0 (x,1),  x in k.

Indeed, any such W has a vector of determinant one against e1, after scaling
by a nonzero element of k0. Two displayed planes coincide exactly when x-y
belongs to k0. Their sets of neighbors of N are exactly the individual link
cycles x+k0 found in Section 2.

Transitivity transports this identification to every vertex. Splitting that
vertex according to its q/p link components separates precisely the incident
W-subcomplexes. The resulting complex is their disjoint union, each a K_p.
The quotient map back to K_q is one-to-one on open edges and open faces and
has exactly q/p points over each original vertex. It preserves each face's
orientation and forgets which local prime-field sheet a vertex belongs to.
This establishes the claimed combinatorial normalization and all its fibres.

### 5.4 Explicit extension-field consequences

For q=9, p=3:

- K_9 has 40 vertices, 180 edges, 120 faces, and χ=-20;
- its normalization is 30 tetrahedron boundaries;
- exactly three surfaces pass through each original vertex;
- each vertex link consists of three disjoint triangles.

It is incorrect to call K_9 a genus-11 surface by substituting χ=-20 into
2-2g. The link test is the first failed implication.

For q=25, p=5, the theorem gives 312 vertices, 3900 edges, and 2600 faces.
Its normalization is 130 icosahedra, five through each original vertex. This
is a proved formula consequence, not an independently enumerated q=25 control.

## 6. Hostile boundaries and loss contract

### 6.1 Characteristic two is excluded for a reason

In F2 the sign quotient is trivial, the graph has three vertices and is K3,
and the triangle complex is one filled triangle. Each edge has just one
incident face. The division-by-two and two-distinct-third-vertices mechanisms
both fail. The checker verifies this exact hostile control.

### 6.2 Do not silently replace the triangle complex by the clique complex

K_3 has a four-clique, but its tetrahedron interior is not a face of K_3. More
generally, characteristic-three prime-field sheets are tetrahedron boundaries.
Filling every clique in every dimension would fill these tetrahedra and destroy
the stated surface-normalization theorem. For characteristic different from
three, no four-clique occurs: the two third vertices at an edge have determinant
±2, which is not ±1.

### 6.3 Fields are not residue rings

F_(p^r) and Z/(p^r) have different additive groups for r>1. The neighbor-step
1 has order p in the field but order p^r in the residue ring. The link splitting
proved here is a finite-field statement and must not be transferred to a ring
merely because its cardinality agrees.

### 6.4 Connection contract for the session-17 path model

- **Source:** the marked 25-path set for T_(3,3), with its distinguished standard
  path and its involution J, as certified in the earlier packet.
- **Map at q=5:** remove the standard path; apply the marked bijection to
  F5² minus {0}; quotient J as vector negation; fill triangles of det=±1.
- **Target:** K_5, the icosahedron surface, and the earlier marked regular-five
  return component at the level of its one-skeleton.
- **Preserved:** the specified determinant adjacency, its triangles and cyclic
  face orientation, provided the field marking is retained.
- **Lost:** the choice of representative in each sign-orbit, and any appearance
  that field addition and multiplication are intrinsic to a bare path set.
- **Sidecar:** the actual Boolean-to-field marking from the earlier packet.
- **Generalization:** replacing F5 by Fq defines the explicit K_q family and its
  proved local-sheet decomposition. No family of tournament path sets for all

- **Cheapest decisive tests:** p=3 exposes the clique-complex boundary; F9 exposes

The tetrahedral prime-divisor fibre map, the divisor equation F=U+S, and the

## 7. Literature and verification ledger

### 7.1 Known carrier, no priority claim

Bogdan Nica, *Unimodular graphs and Eisenstein sums*, arXiv:1505.05034v2,
Section 1.3 (printed page 5) and Section 8, defines Platonic graphs using
unimodular vectors modulo sign and determinant ±1. Over a field, the
unimodular vectors are exactly the nonzero vectors, so this is precisely G_q.
The graph family is therefore inherited mathematical literature, not a new
construction. [Primary paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1505.05034).

Nica's reference [11] points to M. DeDeo, D. Lanphier, and M. Minei,
*The spectrum of Platonic graphs over finite fields*, Discrete Mathematics
307 (2007), 1074–1081. This is a provenance pointer only; its full text is not
used as a proof dependency. No spectral theorem, modular-curve identification,
or priority claim for the normalization statement is imported here.
