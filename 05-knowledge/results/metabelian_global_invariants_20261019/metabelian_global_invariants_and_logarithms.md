# Global first integrals and autonomous logarithms in the two-flow group

19 October 2026. **Self-contained classification, characteristic zero.** This note distinguishes rational maps, rational Hamiltonian logarithms, and polynomial Hamiltonian logarithms within the two-flow family. The tests are exact global principal-part conditions, not finite-jet tests. No historical-novelty claim is made.

## 1. Setting and conclusions

Let K have characteristic zero. In K[[x,y]], with m=(x,y), use {A,B}=A_xB_y−A_yB_x and D_H={−,H}. Let Γ_K be generated by all scalar-time flows

    M_t=(x/(1−tx), y(1−tx)²),
    T_c=(x,y+cx²),                         t,c∈K,

of x²y and x³, respectively (the second time parameter is rescaled). Every element is a rational map regular at the origin, fixes the origin, and is tangent to the identity.

In the canonical coordinates z=1/x,w=−yx², each Φ∈Γ_K has the unique form

    Φ(z,w)=(z−a,w−F(z)),
    F(z)=Σ_t c_t/(z−t)⁴,                             (1)

where the finite support lies in K, and equal sites are combined. These formulas and the polynomial intersection of Γ_K are proved in the [two-flow companion](../metabelian_sharp_jet_length_20261019/metabelian_word_closure_and_sharp_jet_length.md). The needed normal-form identities are also immediate from M_t:(z,w)↦(z−t,w) and T_c:(z,w)↦(z,w−c/z⁴).

Assume first that a≠0. Then:

### Rational invariant/logarithm criterion

The following are equivalent:

1. Φ preserves a nonconstant rational function in K(x,y).
2. Its unique normalized formal Hamiltonian logarithm is rational in x,y.
3. For every translation orbit C=t+aZ in K,

       Σ_(u∈C) c_u=0.                               (2)

4. There is a finite fourth-order partial fraction R(z), with sites in K, such that

       F(z)=R(z)−R(z−a).                            (3)

When these conditions hold, choose the unique R tending to zero at infinity. Then

    J=x²y+R(1/x),
    H_log=aJ,
    K(x,y)^Φ=K(J).                                  (4)

The expression R(1/x) is regular at x=0 and has order at least four. Thus H_log belongs to m³ as required.

### Polynomial invariant/logarithm criterion

For a≠0, the following more restrictive conditions are equivalent:

1. Φ preserves a nonconstant polynomial in K[x,y].
2. Its normalized formal Hamiltonian logarithm is polynomial.
3. For some d∈K,

       F(z)=d[z^(−4)−(z−a)^(−4)].                   (5)

4. Φ is the polynomial-shear conjugate

       Φ=T_(−d) ∘ M_a ∘ T_d.                       (6)

In this case

    J=x²y+d x⁴,
    H_log=a(x²y+d x⁴),
    K[x,y]^Φ=K[J].                                  (7)

All these maps are nonpolynomial, since a≠0 makes their first coordinate x/(1−ax) nonpolynomial. The existence of a polynomial logarithm or polynomial first integral does not assert polynomiality of the time map.

## 2. The unique formal logarithm and its shape

For any tangent-to-identity formal map Φ, its pullback U satisfies (U−I)(m^n)⊂m^(n+1). Therefore

    log U=Σ_(j≥1) (−1)^(j+1)(U−I)^j/j

converges in the filtration. The exponential and logarithm series are inverse in this complete filtered operator algebra.

Here log U is a derivation and preserves the Poisson bracket. One direct justification avoids an analytic parameter: on every finite jet, the binomial series U^s=Σ_j binom(s,j)(U−I)^j is polynomial in s. For every nonnegative integer s it preserves products and the Poisson bracket, so those identities hold polynomially in s over the infinite characteristic-zero field. Differentiating at s=0 gives the derivation and Poisson identities for log U. This argument is compatible with every finite quotient and hence with the full completion.

A filtration-raising Poisson derivation of K[[x,y]] is Hamiltonian. If it sends x to A and y to B, Poisson preservation gives A_x+B_y=0. Integrating A with respect to y and then adjusting by a function of x gives a unique H∈m³ with H_y=A and −H_x=B. Characteristic zero permits the required divisions.

For (1), the restriction of U to K[[x]] is the time-a flow of x²∂_x. Uniqueness of the filtered logarithm therefore gives

    (log U)(x)=a x².

The normalized Hamiltonian has the form

    H_log=a x²y+h(x),       h∈x³K[[x]].              (8)

This assertion uses the original expansion point and point-adic filtration. The birational z,w coordinates are used only to derive rational identities, not to replace the original formal convergence convention.

## 3. A rational periodic-function lemma

If a≠0 and B(z)∈L(z), where L is any characteristic-zero extension field of K, satisfies B(z−a)=B(z), then B is constant.

Indeed, over an algebraic closure of L a finite pole would force infinitely many distinct translates of that pole. Hence B is polynomial. The difference of a nonconstant polynomial of degree n has nonzero leading coefficient proportional to na, so it cannot vanish. This also proves the lemma when B is a Laurent polynomial.

## 4. Every rational first integral forces a coboundary

Let Q(z,w) be a nonconstant rational invariant of (1). Expand it at w=∞ as a Laurent series over K(z):

    Q(z,w)=A_n(z)w^n+A_(n−1)(z)w^(n−1)+...,
    A_n≠0.

The leading coefficient in Q(z−a,w−F(z))=Q(z,w) satisfies A_n(z−a)=A_n(z), hence A_n=A∈K* by the periodic-function lemma. If n=0, subtract the constant A. Unless Q was constant, the resulting invariant has strictly negative leading w-degree. Thus we may assume n≠0.

Comparing the next coefficient gives

    A_(n−1)(z−a)−nA F(z)=A_(n−1)(z).

Therefore (3) holds with

    R(z)=−A_(n−1)(z)/(nA) ∈ K(z).

Conversely, if (3) holds, then −w+R(z) is an invariant. It is nonconstant because it is linear in w.

After the rational change of coordinates J=−w+R(z), the map is simply (z,J)↦(z−a,J). Applying the periodic-function lemma over L=K(J) shows that its entire rational invariant field is K(J). This proves the first-integral portions of (4).

## 5. Finite-support translation cohomology

Suppose F=R(z)−R(z−a) with rational R. The polynomial part of R must be constant, since F is proper and a nonconstant polynomial has nonzero translation difference. At every pole and every pole order, the partial-fraction coefficients obey a finite-support difference equation along a translation orbit.

More explicitly, write the fourth-order part of R as

    R(z)=Σ_t b_t/(z−t)⁴.

Then (3) is equivalent to

    c_t=b_t−b_(t−a).                                (9)

No other pole orders can occur in R: their differences would have zero coefficients in F, so their coefficients would be constant along an infinite translation orbit and also finitely supported, forcing zero. The same argument rules out poles outside K. Such a pole's orbit cannot meet the K-valued support of F; all its coefficients must vanish. These arguments can be made after algebraic closure and then return the displayed K-valued coefficients.

Summing (9) over any orbit gives the necessary condition (2). Conversely, choose an origin t_0 on an orbit and index its coefficients by t_0+na, n∈Z. If their finite sum is zero, set

    b_(t_0+na)=Σ_(j≤n) c_(t_0+ja).

These coefficients are zero sufficiently far in both directions and solve (9). Doing this for each of the finitely many relevant orbits constructs R over K. Uniqueness after requiring R(∞)=0 follows from the periodic-function lemma.

Thus (2) and (3) are equivalent, with exact finite principal-part data. This is an algebraic classification; no claim of an effective integer-orbit membership algorithm is made for an arbitrary presented field K.

## 6. Rational and polynomial logarithms

For a rational h(x) regular at zero, put B(z)=h(1/z). The Hamiltonian (8) becomes

    H=−a w+B(z).

Its time-one flow, expanded in the original point-adic germ, satisfies

    z↦z−a,
    w↦w+[B(z−a)−B(z)]/a.                           (10)

This follows by integrating dz/ds=−a and dw/ds=−B′(z). The rational identity, when expanded at x=0, has the correct initial value and satisfies the formal Hamiltonian differential equations. Filtration-raising uniqueness identifies it with the original formal flow.

If the logarithm is rational, (10) gives (3) with R=B/a. Conversely, a rational R from section 5 gives the Hamiltonian a(x²y+R(1/x)), whose time-one map is (1). Formal-log uniqueness gives (4). This proves the rational-logarithm equivalence.

Now suppose the logarithm is polynomial. Then B(z)=h(1/z) is a polynomial in z^−1 with pole orders at least three. In (10), its principal parts at z=0 are unaffected by B(z−a), whose possible pole is at z=a. Since F has only fourth-order principal parts, B can contain only the z^−4 term. Therefore h(x)=ad x⁴ for some d∈K, yielding (5) and (7). Conversely, that Hamiltonian gives (5) directly.

Finally, direct composition in z,w verifies (6): T_d subtracts d/z⁴, M_a shifts z to z−a, and T_(−d) adds d/(z−a)⁴. This proves the polynomial-logarithm/conjugacy equivalence.

## 7. Polynomial first integrals are stricter than rational ones

Suppose P(x,y)∈K[x,y] is a nonconstant invariant and a≠0. In canonical coordinates it is a polynomial in w with Laurent-polynomial coefficients:

    P(1/z,−wz²)=Σ_(j=0)^n A_j(z)w^j,
    A_j∈K[z,z^−1].

If n=0, periodicity in z makes P constant, a contradiction. Hence n≥1. The leading two coefficients, as in section 4, show that A_n is constant and F is the translation difference of the Laurent polynomial −A_(n−1)/(nA_n). Its positive powers must vanish by properness of F; its negative powers must consist only of z^−4 by the principal parts at 0 and a. Therefore (5) holds.

Conversely, (5) preserves the polynomial J in (7). Under the polynomial conjugacy (6), the invariant ring is transported from that of M_a. For M_a itself, the same expansion in w has periodic coefficients, all constant, so its polynomial invariant ring is K[x²y]. Transporting back gives exactly K[x²y+d x⁴]. This completes (7).

Thus a rational invariant may exist without any polynomial invariant; the distinction is controlled here by the location of the poles of its primitive R.

## 8. Zero translation and explicit separating examples

If a=0, the map is the vertical shear

    (x,y+f(x)),   f(x)=Σ_t c_t x²/(1−tx)⁴.

Its logarithm is simply f(x)∂_y, with normalized Hamiltonian

    H_log=−(1/3)Σ_t c_t x³/(1−tx)³.                 (11)

Indeed, d[x³/(1−tx)³]/dx=3x²/(1−tx)⁴. Thus every zero-translation word has a rational Hamiltonian logarithm. It is polynomial exactly when every nonzero site is absent, that is, the map is T_(c_0).

If f≠0, its rational invariant field is K(x) and its polynomial invariant ring is K[x]: translation by the nonzero f(x) in the variable y has no nonconstant rational invariant over K(x), by the periodic-function lemma. The identity has the full rational function field and polynomial ring as invariants. Consequently the equivalence between a polynomial first integral and a polynomial logarithm must not be extended from a≠0 to a=0.

For a=1, three examples separate the possibilities:

1. F(z)=z^−4. The coefficient sum on its translation orbit is 1. The map is rational, but it has no nonconstant rational first integral and no rational Hamiltonian logarithm. Its formal logarithm still exists.
2. F(z)=(z−2)^−4−(z−3)^−4. The orbit sum is zero, with R(z)=(z−2)^−4. Its rational logarithm is

       x²y+x⁴/(1−2x)⁴,

   and it has a rational first integral, but neither a polynomial logarithm nor a nonconstant polynomial first integral.
3. F(z)=z^−4−(z−1)^−4. Its polynomial logarithm is x²y+x⁴ and its polynomial invariant ring is K[x²y+x⁴]. The time map remains nonpolynomial.

All three examples are exact Γ_K words by the normal-form construction.

## 9. No finite jet detects the rational-integrability obstruction

Fix a≠0 and an integer n≥1. Choose the n+1 sites and coefficients

    t_j=ja/(n+2),
    c_j=(−1)^j binom(n,j),             0≤j≤n,

and set

    F_n(z)=Σ_(j=0)^n c_j/(z−t_j)⁴,
    Φ_n(z,w)=(z−a,w−F_n(z)).                       (12)

Every coefficient is nonzero. The sites belong to distinct translation orbits modulo aZ: the difference (i−j)/(n+2) is a nonzero rational number of absolute value less than one, hence is not an integer. Each orbit sum is consequently a single nonzero c_j. The exact criterion (2) proves that Φ_n has no nonconstant rational first integral and no rational normalized Hamiltonian logarithm. It is nevertheless an exact finite Γ_K word, by the normal form.

The finite-difference identities

    Σ_(j=0)^n (−1)^j binom(n,j) j^k=0       for k<n,
    Σ_(j=0)^n (−1)^j binom(n,j) j^n=(−1)^n n!

follow by applying the nth forward difference to the monomials T^k. Expanding (12) at z=∞ therefore gives

    F_n(z)=α_n z^(−n−4)+O(z^(−n−5)),
    α_n=(−1)^n binom(n+3,3)n![a/(n+2)]^n≠0.        (13)

Returning to x,y,

    Φ_n(x,y)=(x/(1−ax),
              y(1−ax)²+(1−ax)²x^(−2)F_n(1/x)).

It follows that

    Φ_n−M_a=(0,α_n x^(n+2))+O(m^(n+3)).             (14)

Thus Φ_n and M_a agree exactly through degree n+1, and their first difference occurs in degree n+2. For every N≥2, taking n=N−1 gives an exact rational word with the same N-jet as M_a and with no rational first integral or rational Hamiltonian logarithm. M_a itself has the polynomial invariant x²y and polynomial logarithm a x²y.

This is a concrete failure of finite-jet certification of the global orbit condition, inside this fixed family and over every characteristic-zero field. These are rational germ approximants, not polynomial Keller-map counterexamples.

## 10. Research scope and verification

This gives a precise global invariant criterion beyond a local jet for one infinite-dimensional metabelian family. It also exhibits rational time maps whose formal logarithms are not rational. The polynomial-map classification from the earlier autonomous note remains consistent: in the nonzero-translation cases even a polynomial logarithm is not locally nilpotent, since its x-component is a x².

No arbitrary Keller-map reduction, Hadwiger–Nelson bound improvement, or Jacobian-conjecture resolution is claimed. All field statements above hold over every characteristic-zero K without descent of a complex conjugator.
