# A finite inverse graph turns the selector base locus into the exact omitted set

20 October 2026. **PROVED scoped reduction; independent review pending.** This applies only under the stated global trace regularity. It proves neither that regularity for all Keller maps nor the planar Jacobian conjecture. The elimination improvement was obtained jointly with the coordinating researcher.

## 1. Main theorem

Let F=(P,Q):A²_C→A²_C have constant nonzero Jacobian and generic function-field degree d=m+1≥3. Choose affine source coordinates x,y so that P is monic in y and x is primitive for C(x,y)/C(P,Q). Write u=P, v=Q and let

    χ(Z)=Z^d+c₁Z^(d−1)+…+c_(d−1)Z+c_d

be the monic minimal polynomial of x over C(u,v). Suppose

    T_k=Tr(x^k) ∈ C[u,v],      1≤k≤d−2.           (L)

The conclusion below is conditional on (L), not a claim that all Keller maps satisfy it.

Write c_(d−1)=A/H in lowest terms with A,H∈C[u,v] and gcd(A,H)=1. Then:

1. c₁,…,c_(d−2) are polynomial, and c_d=B/H for a polynomial B.
2. The nonproper-value set C is exactly V(H). If h is the squarefree part of H, C=V(h).
3. The omitted target set O=A²\F(A²) is exactly

       O=V(H,A)=V(h,A),

   and V(H,A,B) is empty. In particular B is a unit in the finite intersection algebra C[u,v]/(H,A).
4. Every fiber over C has zero or one source point. Consequently

       #O=(d−1)(1−χ_c(C)).                        (1)

5. The selector/intersection budget obeys

       (d−1)(1−χ_c(C))
         = #V(h,A)
         ≤ deg(h) deg(A),                         (2)

   with the right side zero when A is constant. The middle cardinality is reduced cardinality; the Bézout bound includes intersection multiplicity on the right.

The theorem links an explicit inverse-coefficient numerator, the actual omitted points, and the topology of the entire nonproperness curve. It is stronger than merely saying that a formal finite branch exists at a generic boundary point.

For the even-degree-six maximal-cancellation sector, (1) becomes

    #V(h,A)=5(1−χ_c(C)),

while the local trace matrix has rank four, four Smith exponents −a_D, and minimal integral-lattice repair index 2a_D at each boundary component. These are explicit tests for a specified degree-six candidate sector, not an exclusion of all degree-six maps or a claim that (L) is automatic.

Planar degree four is already excluded by Domrina's general theorem; the quartic specialization #O=3(1−χ_c(C)) is a consistency control, not a new globally open planar target. See the literature correction in the [local packet](TRACE_HYPERBOLIC_PACKET.md).

## 2. The common denominator is forced by one finite sheet

The inherited finite-sheet lemma says that every affine target prime divisor has at least one actual finite inverse sheet. At a nonproper divisor D, primitivity together with the monic transverse equation makes x detect every escaping source sheet.

Under (L), the earlier local moment lemma forces exactly m=d−1 escaping x-values and one bounded value. The first polar trace T_m has pole order a_D>0. The local calculation in the companion note gives

    c₁,…,c_(m−1) integral at D,
    v_D(c_m)=−a_D,
    v_D(c_(m+1))≥−a_D.                            (3)

At every divisor outside C all source coordinates, hence all characteristic coefficients, are integral. Thus the support of the reduced denominator of c_m is exactly C. Let H be its full reduced denominator, retaining each pole order a_D. Formula (3) says Hc_(m+1) is regular at every affine prime divisor. Since C[u,v] is a UFD, Hc_(m+1)=B is polynomial.

Newton identities and (L) likewise make c₁,…,c_(m−1) polynomial globally. This proves claims 1 and 2. No equality of denominators for c_m and c_(m+1) is assumed: B may share factors with H, and c_d can have a smaller actual denominator.

## 3. The finite graph is the indispensable extra structure

Define the actual inverse-coordinate graph map

    G:A²_source→A³_(u,v,Z),       (x,y)↦(P(x,y),Q(x,y),x).

Its coordinate image ring is S=C[P,Q,x]. Because P is monic in y, y satisfies a monic equation P(x,y)−u=0 over S. Therefore C[x,y]=S[y] is a finite integral S-module. The map onto Spec S is finite and surjective by lying over.

The kernel of C[u,v,Z]→S is the height-one prime generated by the primitive cleared minimal polynomial

    R(u,v,Z)=H Z^d+Hc₁Z^(d−1)+…+Hc_(d−2)Z²+A Z+B.    (4)

To justify this exactly: χ is irreducible over C(u,v), and gcd(H,A)=1 makes the displayed polynomial primitive in C[u,v][Z]. Gauss's lemma makes R irreducible. It belongs to the kernel, and both define the same degree-one-height prime in the UFD C[u,v,Z]. Hence

    Spec S = V(R),

with no additional deleted component or saturation clause. This is an actual finite algebraic image, not the informal graph of a rational root.

Now fix a target point p=(u₀,v₀). The fiber of V(R) over p is exactly the zero set of R(p,Z), and the finite map from the source is surjective onto it.

- If H(p)≠0, R(p,Z) has positive degree d and hence has a complex root. The target point is in the image.
- If H(p)=0 and A(p)≠0, the fiber polynomial is linear, with root Z=−B(p)/A(p). Again p is in the image.
- If H(p)=A(p)=0 and B(p)≠0, the fiber polynomial is a nonzero constant, so p is omitted.
- If H(p)=A(p)=B(p)=0, the fiber of V(R) is the whole Z-line. A finite surjective map onto that line forces the original F-fiber to be positive-dimensional. This contradicts Keller quasi-finiteness.

The last case is therefore impossible. The preceding cases prove

    O=V(H,A),           V(H,A,B)=∅.                (5)

Because A and H are coprime, O is finite. The absence of a common zero with B also says that the class of B in C[u,v]/(H,A) is a unit, by the Nullstellensatz. This is valid for the possibly nonreduced finite algebra, not just at its reduced points.

This finite-graph argument is why the result is stronger than an isolated Hensel selector. Without monicity in the companion coordinate, an apparent finite root of a primitive inverse equation can still leave that companion coordinate polar. Without quasi-finiteness, the completely vanished fiber polynomial may represent a contracted source curve.

## 4. A generic singleton boundary fiber is a singleton everywhere it exists

For each irreducible component of C, the generic fiber has exactly one finite source point, by the low-trace packet argument.

Suppose some p∈C had two distinct source preimages. The inverse-function theorem gives disjoint neighborhoods of these preimages, each mapped biholomorphically to a neighborhood of p. Their image neighborhoods overlap. Pick a generic smooth point of a component of C through p inside that overlap. It then has at least two source preimages, contradicting the generic singleton property. Thus

    #F^(-1)(p)≤1       for every p∈C.              (6)

The restriction F:F^(-1)(C)→C\O is therefore bijective. As a restriction of an étale map it is open in the relative topology, so it is a homeomorphism. Equivalently one can use an étale radicial morphism's open-immersion property. The topological conclusion suffices below.

## 5. Euler integration and the exact numerical constraint

Use compactly supported Euler characteristic. Off C, the map is a finite étale covering of degree d; on C\O it is a homeomorphism by Section 4. Additivity and the covering formula give

    1 = χ_c(A²_source)
      = d χ_c(A²_target\C)+χ_c(C\O)
      = d(1−χ_c(C))+χ_c(C)−#O.

Rearrangement proves (1). Since h and A have no common curve component, affine Bézout gives #V(h,A)≤deg(h)deg(A), proving (2).

For a candidate curve whose normalization is a disjoint union of c copies of A¹, write b_p for the number of normalization points above each singular point p. Additivity under the finite normalization gives

    χ_c(C)=c−Σ_p(b_p−1),
    #O=(d−1)[Σ_p(b_p−1)−c+1].                    (7)

This specialization requires that stated normalization; it is not inferred solely from a picture of the curve. A node contributes one to the sum; a unibranch cusp contributes zero. Intersection points of different components contribute through their total branch count. No δ-invariant or Milnor number is being substituted for b_p−1.

For an irreducible candidate with A¹ normalization, (7) becomes

    #O=(d−1)Σ_p(b_p−1).

Thus if its normalization identifies exactly one pair of points and has any additional unibranch singularities, the low-trace sector requires exactly d−1 omitted points, each a genuine intersection of h and the numerator A. A numerator with too small an intersection budget excludes that candidate sector.

## 6. The selector's poles and the remaining obstruction

On C\O the actual finite inverse coordinate is exactly

    x=−B/A.

At every omitted point A=H=0 while B≠0. Consequently the selector has a genuine pole on every normalization branch of C through such a point; its order is the zero order of A along that branch. The numerator cannot cancel it there. Conversely A is nonzero on C\O, so there are no other finite selector poles on the normalization.

The local Hensel approximants in the companion note were never a global polynomial inverse. This theorem locates their first gluing obstruction exactly: the intersections of the nonproperness denominator and the linear-coefficient numerator. The obstruction is codimension two on the target, but it still corresponds to genuine missing affine source points and finite selector poles.

The concrete next lemma for a prescribed higher-degree family is now an intersection/topology comparison: prove that its actual numerator A has fewer distinct intersections with h than (d−1)(1−χ_c(C)), or prove that the required B-unit condition fails at one intersection. Either gives a contradiction within that family. The polynomial-source boundary forest and the companion coordinate remain legitimate sidecars for obtaining such a bound; they are not supplied by the trace form alone.

## 7. Scope, controls, and sources

All global conclusions use an actual polynomial Keller map, the primitive x-coordinate, a monic transverse equation, and global (L). These assumptions are not properties of an arbitrary formal symplectic germ, a rational cotangent map, a positive-real-Jacobian map, or a local root packet.

The proof uses the already proved finite-sheet and trace-denominator mechanisms, elementary integrality/Gauss/lying-over arguments, local invertibility, and standard Euler additivity for finite algebraic coverings. No additional classification theorem or current conjectural degree bound is used in proving the gate.

The standalone checker `verify_selector_euler_gate.py` verifies the complete specialized-fiber case split for a finite coefficient universe, exact finite-graph hostile controls, and normalization/Euler accounting for explicit curve models. Those controls do not substitute for the universal finite-integrality proof or construct a Keller realization.
