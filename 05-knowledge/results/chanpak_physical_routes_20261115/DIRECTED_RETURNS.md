# An absolute return certificate needs no reversible move

The [voltage formulation](NUMBER_THEORY.md) turns physical routes into
integer changes of the quotient (H−r)/M. Here is a version for
irreversible constructions such as adding a physical module.

## Guarded directed return theorem

Take a finite strongly connected directed graph of marked interfaces.
Each arc has an integer shift, and from **every physical realization**
of its source interface at quotient height h≥B it has a literal lift
at height h+shift, preserving the designated readout. No reverse
arcs or positive-height realizations at every integer are assumed.
Let g be the gcd of the shifts of all directed cycles, with signs
retained and gcd nonnegative. Suppose at least one directed cycle
has positive shift.

For any chosen base interface there is an explicit threshold such
that a single physical anchor at or above that threshold generates
all sufficiently large quotient heights in its class modulo g.
In particular g=1 implies a whole upper interval, not just a dense
set or an arbitrarily fine collection of congruences.

**Proof.** The shifts of directed closed walks at a fixed base have
gcd g. One direction decomposes a closed walk into simple cycles.
For the other direction, join the base to an arbitrary cycle's
vertex and back by directed paths; the two base returns with and
without one lap differ by exactly the cycle's shift. There are only
finitely many simple cycles, so finitely many chosen base returns
suffice. A positive loop at a different vertex becomes a positive
base return by taking enough laps to dominate its connectors.
Let its positive shift be p. Prepend enough copies of it to each
chosen return to make the chosen shift strictly positive. This
does not change their gcd together with p. All these words are
physical directed words, not formal inverses.

Let L be the maximum downward prefix excursion of this finite
list. An anchor at h≥B+L makes each return executable. Because
each has positive net shift, every concatenation is executable.
Divide their shifts by g. The resulting positive integers have
gcd one, so their nonnegative sums contain all large integers.
Constructively, modulo the least generator a, the positive
semigroup generated by the other strides is the full cyclic group
(finite inverses are positive repetitions). Record one actual word
for each residue and then append the generator a as often as
needed. The residue step here is only choosing among already
proved *integer* physical returns. This proves the theorem.

This replaces the need for a one-unit physical edge, reversal, or
even a one-unit closed walk. The target can be decoded by its
residue within a finite coin problem, but the hard premise remains:
the same literal returns must lift from every sufficiently high
realization of the interface, not just from increasingly rare
heights. Two finite brackets or even an unbounded sequence of
local paths do not imply the premise. This is the difference from
the finite-quotient affine orbit, where inverses exist automatically.

The repo's older two-independent-step interval constructions
(e.g. the 145/39 half-steps in the composition session) already
use finite boxes of move budgets; this theorem is not a novelty
claim for that coin arithmetic. It states exactly how to replace
each finite budget by a proved directed return with a uniform
guard. The physical construction of such returns for arbitrary
Chan–Pak targets is still open.
