### THM-4516 (perfect-power clocks are Fermat–Catalan identities)

16. **Dictionary.** HOLDS: `2^K - q^X` is odd, so `m` is odd; `p | m` would give `p | 2^K`; hence `gcd(m, 2p) = 1` and `(2, p, m)` with exponents `(K, sX, r)` is a primitive solution; Darmon–Granville applies when `1/K + 1/(sX) + 1/r < 1`; by THM-4484(1) the shape `(K,X)` is free for `T_{q, +-m^r}` because the clock *equals* `+-d`. The four Fermat–Catalan solutions with a pure power of two among the ten known are exactly `1 + 2^3 = 3^2`, `2^5 + 7^2 = 3^4`, `7^3 + 13^2 = 2^9`, `2^7 + 17^3 = 71^2` (checked by inspection of the list).

HOLDS WITH CORRECTION on method: the session's `power_clocks.py` identifies the exponent with `gmpy2.iroot(n, r)` for `r <= 64` only and its `power_data` returns `None` otherwise, so a perfect power whose least prime exponent exceeds 64 would have been *dropped* from censuses (i) and (ii) (the `_wide` script keeps such an entry with `r = None`). 23. **Beal shadow, first half.** HOLDS: a perfect-power clock with `K, sX, r >= 3` is a coprime Beal solution.
24. **Beal shadow, conclusion "so Beal implies no map `py +- m^r` (`r >= 3`) has a free mixed shape `(K,X)` with `K >= 3`, `sX >= 3`, `X >= 2`" (note 2.5, THM-4516 status (5), and the title's "Beal implies no free shape with all three exponents >= 3").** FAILS. Counterexample: `3y + 125 = 3y + 5^3` has the free shape `(5,3)` because its clock `2^5 - 3^3 = 5` divides 125: both necklaces give integer cycles (`475 -> 775 -> 1225 -> 1900 -> 950` and `575 -> 925 -> 1450 -> 725 -> 1150`), with `K = 5, sX = 3, r = 3, X = 3` and no Beal solution anywhere. Likewise `3y + 47^3` with the free shape `(7,4)`. The correct consequence is only "Beal implies no perfect-power *clock* `2^K - p^{sX} = +-m^r` with `K, sX, r >= 3`".
25. **The `(4,3,17)` statement.** Recording it as UNVERIFIED is right and I did not try to settle it. FAILS: the sentence "Its clock shadow is empty in any case: no term of `x^4 + y^3 = z^17` can be a pure power of two while the other two are powers of one odd base" is unsupported — a coprime solution has exactly one even variable, and the clock shadow is precisely the sub-case where that variable is a power of two (`x = 2^a` gives `2^{4a} = z^{17} - y^3`, a clock of shape `(4a, 17)` for `T_{z, -y^3}`; with `K` in `{3,4,17}` for the exact multiset). No elementary argument excludes it: the sum cases `2^K = u^a + v^b` (`{K,a,b} = {4,3,17}`) are finite and have no solution, the difference cases are an unbounded search (none with `u < 3000`), and the emptiness of the shadow is as open as the equation itself on that sub-case. Internal-consistency caveat: "the smallest open Beal signature `(3,5,7)`" next to "among `(3,4,n)` only `n = 4, 5` are solved" is consistent only if "smallest" means by largest exponent (or if `(3,4,6)`, `(3,4,7)`, ... count as solved by reduction); the ordering sense should be stated. The survey's content itself is CITED and was not checked (no browsing).
