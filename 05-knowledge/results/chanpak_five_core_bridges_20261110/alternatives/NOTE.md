## 6. A primitive-word collision and its arithmetic boundary

The two H9 inner words are also a special meeting of two explicit families.
The one-apex word `10^r` has count `2^r+1`. The alternating word `(10)^m`
has inherited count `(4^(m+1)+6m+5)/9`, proved in THM-5123 and
`session246_unifying_principles_20261110/periodic/PROOF.md` by the common
flag/Jordan recurrence. Equality for positive m,r occurs exactly at

    (m,r,H)=(1,1,3), (2,3,9).

Indeed equality gives `9*2^r=4^(m+1)+6m-4`. For m>=3 put
X=4^(m+1), c=6m-4. Then `0<c<X/9`, so `2^r=(X+c)/9>c`, while
`2^r<X`, hence r<2m+2. Since `2^r` divides X, the equality forces it
to divide c, a contradiction. The m1 andm2 cases give the two displayed
solutions. The elementary inequality starts at m3 and is preserved when
X multiplies by4 and c increases by6.

This explains why the first nontrivial doubling/alternating collision lands
on the two different H9 charts. It does not classify all one-apex words.
The Fermat-prime condition on `2^r+1` restricts r when that number is prime;
our construction counts need not be prime, and H9 is composite. The
mechanism is collision of two actual counting laws, not primality transfer.
