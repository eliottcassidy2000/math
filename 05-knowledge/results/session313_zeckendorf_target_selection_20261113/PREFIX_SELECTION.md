# Greedy allocation beyond the regular ternary tower

The [ternary marking chart](../session312_cluster_pythagorean_symmetry_20261113/CLUSTER_CHART.md) makes each named leaf pay for the previously untouched ancestors on its path. The same idea survives irregular address spaces. The relevant structure is laminarity, not the number three. This parallels the old [threshold and branch-label work](../../../07-reflections/cross-frontier-support-passports-laminar-cuts-and-branch-labels-codex-20260803.md), but here the allocation acts on actual leaves, not projected capacity marginals.

## Weighted prefix selection

Take any finite rooted tree with nonnegative vertex weights. Choose exactly $k$ leaves to maximize the weight of their ancestor union (leaf weights may be zero). **At every step, choosing any leaf with maximum newly covered weight is optimal at every budget, including arbitrary ties.**

Here is an exact proof and algorithm. At each leaf its marginal list consists of its weight. At an internal vertex merge the children's marginal lists in nonincreasing order and add the vertex's weight to the first entry. This is again nonincreasing. By induction the sum of its first $k$ entries is the optimum: once the subtree is entered its root weight is paid once, and allocating leaf counts among children is maximized by the top $k$ child increments. They can be chosen prefixwise in each child because those lists are nonincreasing. For the actual greedy walk, the first selected leaf is optimal in its child and chooses a child with greatest first increment; the root's initial bonus is the same for all choices and then disappears. At every later step the live increments are the first unused entries from the child lists, by the induction hypothesis. Any tie therefore realizes the same optimum. A root with zero weight, or a unary vertex, requires no change in the argument.

For an iterated cyclic join of three arbitrary child tournaments at each node, give an internal node weight one if its three child classes coincide and zero otherwise; use rigid leaf tournaments. The maximal modules at a cyclic join are exactly its children. After fixing named leaves individually, only an initially active rotation with *no* marked descendant survives. Thus greedy is exactly optimal for pointwise symmetry loss even on irregular hierarchies and with nonconstant child colors. On a monochromatic ternary tower the ordinary maximum spanning-tree formula is a second description of this same optimization. The merge proof works without regular levels or a digit-reversal order.

## Zeckendorf address charts and terminal roles

In the ordinary Zeckendorf trie, use the digit positions of $1,2,3,5,\ldots$ from high to low and forbid adjacent ones. The two children of a free prefix have Fibonacci capacities: with $r$ remaining digits there are $F_{r+2}$ completions, with the first zero subtree of size $F_{r+1}$ and the first one subtree of size $F_r$ (with the next digit forced zero). The legal lex order agrees with numerical order: the best smaller legal suffix sums to one less than the next weight, by this same two-branch induction. Each prefix therefore labels a contiguous integer interval. The smaller block sizes themselves encode the choice of legal branch.

The repo's [marked-unit construction](../zeckendorf_guard_automaton_20261003.md) adds a second and sometimes third representation with the same value. They can be handled as distinct terminal leaves, not as extra copies of the same state: the ordinary and black roles always exist for positive values, and the blue role exists only when the ordinary representation of $n-1$ omits its unit. This records a row choice; it does not infer a continuation of the user's supplied diagonal stripe. In particular the historical 35th symbol differs from the trailing-zero candidate, and the specific descending-piece grammar has its documented 35-to-36 boundary obstruction.

Here is an exact Plücker chart for any finite ordered prefix tree with at most three children, including these constrained Fibonacci tries. Pad addresses after their terminal node to maximum depth $D$; use child digits $0,1,2$ in order. Distinct original leaves have a divergence before either terminates. For a leaf of rank $i$, set
$$
x_i=3^D i+\sum_{j=0}^{D-1}a_j(i)3^j .
$$
Then $x_i$ is strictly increasing and $v_3(x_j-x_i)$ is common prefix length. For increasing quadruples, the three longer interval valuations are the minima of the intervening adjacent ones, so the tropical exchange has no cancellation, by the same minimum identity as in Session 312. The signed leading unit records the actual orientation at each split if a binary split is read from digit 0 toward 1 and a ternary split is read cyclically. This is exact on these address charts, not a rank-two assertion about a physical internal tournament. With triangular row labels $T_{n-1}+j$ ($1\le j\le n$) used in place of rank, the same construction still works whenever those labels order the leaves lexicographically. Their gaps do not affect the 3-adic divergence.

## Optimization does not give an exact target

The greedy theorem gives the *largest* payable prefix weight at each budget. An arbitrary target need not occur. Even two leaves with exclusive positive weights 1 and 3 have budget-one values $\{1,3\}$, missing 2. For a physical example take a cyclic join with one monochromatic height-two child and two distinct rigid children of the same order and full path-cover polynomial. The root is inactive. Mark one leaf; the symmetry exponent drops by 2 inside the monochromatic child and by 0 in a rigid child, never by 1. To construct the two rigid height-two children, use the two rigid classes of Session 311. Thus the gap persists even in a strong equal-polynomial tournament tower.

A separate exact feasibility recursion does work. For each node and budget $k>0$, take all child budgets summing to $k$, form the Minkowski sums of the attainable child-weight sets, take their union, and add the root weight; the budget-zero set is $\{0\}$. Backtracking the retained child budgets produces an actual marking. If each needed child set is known to be an interval, sorted sums cover an interval exactly when consecutive intervals overlap or touch in integer steps. Without this test an optimizer cannot serve as an exact target selector. Nor does the feasibility recursion for marking give Hamiltonian-count changes: that requires an exact physical transfer that preserves named continuation ports.
