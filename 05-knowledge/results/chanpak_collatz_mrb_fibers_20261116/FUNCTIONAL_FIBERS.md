# One arrow needs a state: exact Hamiltonian fibers and a smaller controller

A Collatz vertex is an integer: its parity determines the unique successor. In the physical rank-four return bank the Hamiltonian count is only a projection of the state, and that projection does **not** determine the next exact target. This is distinct from whether one could choose a canonical representative for each attainable count.

## A real equal-count fiber with disjoint exact successor menus

Begin with the same strong seed (0,0,3,5,2,4), H=25. Use the [established rank-four bank and its lattice](https://github.com/eliottcassidy2000/math/blob/3a2423f0bb11b8a63dc81b7b3616a881c101b3f6/05-knowledge/results/chanpak_eight_braid_20261116/THREE_LEASHES.md). A block is its steering triple followed by the common S=(0,3,5,2,4). The two steering triples (7,6,7) and (7,1,7) both yield **H=33,423** at the same order and with the same exact image. Their respective coordinates are

    (−55391, −6247, 6714, 67639)
    (−55413, −6224, 6655, 67675).

Take every one of the 168 next blocks at each of these two states. The two lists each contain 136 distinct integer counts and are **disjoint**. Their closest pair is 18,060,541 and 18,060,529 (distance 12); one common block producing that pair has steering triple (3,1,5). Yet both lists separately hit every odd residue modulo 32. Thus inspecting H, the image, or all eight dyadic half-increment lanes cannot say which exact next value is possible across this fiber. The replay verifies the displayed starting counts independently by subset DP on the two literal tournaments; the successor menus are then computed from the authenticated exact lattice.

In the whole first-return layer, 136 distinct coordinate states give 135 distinct H values, with this the sole doubled fiber of *states*. Counting literal words instead would already count some equivalent actions twice. This is a small precise analogue of the multiplicity question for Euler's totient, not a claim about the existence of multiplicity-one Hamiltonian counts in the unrestricted tournament spectrum. Padding and isomorphism must be treated separately; the result concerns exact *continuation states* at one fixed layer.

## What residues actually forget: the exact observable lattice

Let f=(32,14,10,27) be the Hamiltonian row and A the 168 integer return actions on column states. Compute the row lattice generated by f A over those one-step actions. Its exact Hermite basis is

    D = diag(4, 2, 2, 1).

The empty readout f belongs to this lattice, and for every physical A the matrix D A D⁻¹ is integral. Thus this is also the lattice generated by **all finite continuation readouts**: it contains f and is invariant under right action, and already the one-step rows generate it. For states u,v and any modulus M, the complete equivalence test is therefore

    all future counts agree mod M
    iff D (u−v) ≡ 0 mod M.

The assertion is about raw scalar readouts from the listed returns, not a count of nonisomorphic tournaments. Over the integers all future counts agree iff the full coordinate states agree. For odd modulus D is invertible, so there is no reduction in coordinates from this lattice. At powers of two it gives a genuine reduction.

At M=32 the sufficient and necessary residue coordinates are v₁ mod 8, v₂ mod 16, v₃ mod 16 and v₄ mod 32. Each physically reachable residue state has odd H; in these coordinates this is equivalent to its fourth coordinate being odd. The earlier online policy had 524,288 reachable states when all four raw coordinates were stored mod 32. The exact quotient here has only **32,768 reachable states**. The compressed verifier exhausts that orbit; all 16 odd mod-32 targets remain reachable in two blocks, and there is no coarser congruence-state quotient that preserves every finite continuation count. The result does not say that no smaller automaton can satisfy *only* a reachability specification without preserving the entire observable language.

The finite state now gives a genuinely functional **policy** after choosing a requested residue: take the shortlex first legal block path to it. That is the honest analogy with “one outgoing arrow.” In Collatz the arrow is part of the number rule itself; here it is an imposed choice on a state sufficient to predict the future. For a prescribed absolute H the disjoint menus above explain why there is no such state-independent one-block rule on H alone across both witnesses.
