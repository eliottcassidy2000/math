# Session 302: when a triangle multiplies without becoming a component

**Result:** an exact strong continuation 63 → 189 → 567 → 1701, an interface principle explaining every step and its resource limit, and a fixed-chart obstruction distinguishing divisibility from exact ternary recurrence. Chan–Pak completeness remains open.

## Change the question

The first attained count on the 7-ray is 63. There are no 7 or 21 tournaments, so neither can supply a strong component of a 63 tournament. The known two birth classes at order eight are converses ([birth atlas](../session224_first_occurrence_20261107/README.md), [all-order fiber](../session266_fiber_radix_20261111/fibers/PROOF.md)). In contrast to ordinary multiplication by an external directed triangle, ask what operation can give exact multiplication **inside** a strong core.

For positive odd d prime to 3, define e(d)=min{j≥0: H=d·3^j occurs}, with infinity if no member occurs. Appending a triangle as a separate strong component makes every finite ray a tail. Define e_S(d) similarly but using only nontrivial strong realizers. For d>1, e(d) is the minimum of Σe_S(d_i) over factorizations d=∏d_i, d_i>1: in a minimal witness no strong component has a pure power of 3 for its count, since removing it would lower j. The converse direction assembles optimal strong witnesses as an ordinal word. Set e(1)=0. At a prime ray birth there is only one nontrivial strong component. The target is e(7)=2 (known), e(d)=0 for all other odd 3-free d (open). With the known exceptional atoms 49,63,343, it suffices to realize the odd primes p≠7 and the products 7p with prime p≥5, p≠7 ([Session 299](../session299_three_and_two_holes_20261113/README.md)). Closing finitely many seeds only under ordinary multiplication and 3H+2 cannot hit all primes 1 mod 3: a prime is not a nontrivial product and 3H+2 lies in the other unit class. Exact ×3 is useful, but cannot be the only recurrence.

## Exact strong multiplication with a finite budget

Consider an apex v above a linearly ordered family of nonempty blocks. Between blocks all arcs point forward, and each block is uniform to v. Let F_k(C) count ordered spanning k-path covers of a block C. In a full Hamilton path, each block has at most two runs: in the segment before v, and in the segment after v. Thus the full count is a polynomial with nonnegative integer coefficients in the block responses F_1,F_2, homogeneous of degree one in each pair. Proof: replace each block-run by its block name, count admissible skeletons (including which side of v each run lies), and fill its ordered slots with an ordered path cover. This is a restriction of the general substitution kernel, not a new claim about unmarked counts alone ([Session 293](../session293_cycle_returns_20261113/control/MODULE_IDEAL.md)).

It follows that **replacing any block by another whose two coordinates are scaled by t multiplies the full count by exactly t**, including when the block lies inside the strong core. For a transitive pair TT2 the pair is (1,2); for a directed triangle C3 it is (3,6). A triangle can be formed by one vertex birth into a pair, keeping the pair's old arc and all its uniform incidences. In a general uniform context, the exact formula is H_new=3H_old+6a_3, where a_3 counts exterior skeletons visiting the block three times; these have no counterpart for TT2. Hence the operation is exact ×3 precisely when there is no such skeleton. Equivalently the bipartite digraph of edges from out-neighbors to in-neighbors of the contracted vertex has matching number at most one (the matching support theorem recalled in Session 299). In the apex-over-ordinal architecture, each excursion must traverse the apex, so there can be only one.

Use spine blocks 1 | 00 | 11 | 00, where bits say whether the apex beats the block. The initial full count is 63. The apex beats the first block and loses to the last, so every vertex reaches and is reached from the apex (via the last/first blocks), for every choice of the three internal modules. Upgrade any subset of the three TT2 blocks to directed triangles: this yields strong tournaments with H=63·3^j for every 0≤j≤3, on 8+j vertices, each upgrade a literal induced one-vertex extension. In the published birth labels one order is masks (243,229,129), with old i→new iff bit i set. All 8 subset upgrades can be checked directly. This mechanism differs from replacing a **singleton** inside a strong component by a triangle (which introduces additional runs and excess over ×3; see Session 299).

More generally put G(C)=2H(C)+F_2(C)=Σ_{U⊆C} H(C[U])H(C−U), using H(empty)=1. G and H multiply over ordinal sums: each of the two parts independently traverses the components in order. Thus TT_(2r) and an ordinal sum of r directed triangles have (H,G)=(1,4^r) and (3^r,12^r). They can also be connected by r literal one-vertex births, partitioning the TT_(2r) into ordered pairs and upgrading each to a triangle. This yields **arbitrarily long strong exact ×3 ladders** in the same apex-block architecture, but with larger initial seeds; it does not renew a fixed birth seed.

Indeed F_2(C)≥(n−1)H(C), since splitting a Hamilton path at each of its n−1 cuts injects into ordered two-covers (concatenation recovers the path and the cut). Thus any replacement preserving F_2/H has order at most 1+F_2/H. In particular the pair and the triangle share ratio 2; once the triangle is present, **no larger block** with the same ratio exists. These individual upgrades spend a finite resource. The stronger branching check in [grow.cpp](grow.cpp) goes beyond uniform block upgrades: among all strong one-vertex births from the chosen labeled birth core, keeping exactly ×3 each time, there are 3,6,6,0 labeled branches at depths 1,2,3,4. The converse initial core has the reversed search tree. Omitted all-in/all-out incidences preserve H, not ×3. This result excludes an exact one-vertex-per-stage chain to 5103 from the two first creators, **not** strong 5103 realizers in general.

## The binary inversion board and its exact limits

Delete the apex and suppose its spine is transitive; encode its out-neighbors with ones. All directed cycles involve the apex. The odd-cycle packing formula therefore gives H=1+Σ W(j−i) over inversion pairs i<j with bit 1 then 0. For adjacent positions W(1)=2; otherwise W(d)=2^(d−1). Indeed, choose an even-sized subset of the intermediate spine vertices to close an odd directed cycle. A count can be computed left to right: if X is the binary prefix value and b its last bit (both 0 initially), append e and increment H by (1−e)(X+b), then set X←2X+e and b←e. Leading zeros and trailing ones are singleton components and do not affect H. Reversal of all arcs becomes reverse-complement of the active word.

For active width L, the endpoint inversion gives a bound 1+W(L−1); a sharp lower envelope also includes Σ min(W(i−1),W(L−i)) for i=2,…,L−1. Choose zeros followed by ones internally to attain the envelope: there are no internal inversions, and each interior bit chooses the smaller endpoint cost. This makes any exact target a finite width problem in the one-apex class. Scanning the entire class by this bound gives no 7 or 21, 63 only as 1001100 and 1100110, no 189,567,1701,5103,15309, and two words at 45927. The strong 189 extension above must change the deletion core, and does.

At fixed numbers of zeros and ones, the elementary adjacent inversion 01→10 is strictly increasing for H. If X,b encode the prefix before the swap and z_j is the suffix starting at j=0, the exact increment is
```
X+2−b + Σ_{j:z_j=0} 2^j − [z_0 exists and equals 0].
```
The two swapped steps give X+2−b; the binary prefix changes by +1 and the correction bit by −1 for the first suffix step. This is a weighted Ferrers-board growth, not a unit-step fill. It is a precise comparison with the [balanced Collatz carry ladder](../../../01-canon/theorems/THM-4716-balanced-collatz-cycles-are-three-smooth-ladders-and-are-free.md): there an adjacent swap gives a single 2,3-smooth increment, whereas the tournament swap reads both flanks. Neither ordering lemma implies gapless coverage. Compare also the square-sum partial-reflection words in [Session 292](../session292_triple_bridges_20261113/README.md): a formal word closes algebraically, but its admissible positions determine which sizes really realize it.

The three-width family 1 0^a 1^b 0^c, with a,b,c≥1, has
```
H=A·2^a+B,   A=1+2^b(2^c−1),   B=2+(2^b−1)(2^c−1).
```
The first zero block contributes 2^a to H−1 (sum 2+2+4+… through the appropriate distance, with a=1 giving 2), and summing inversions into the last zero block gives the displayed formula. For b=c=2 this is 13·2^a+11, passing through 63 at a=2. If v3(A)≠v3(B), all a give their minimum valuation. If equal, all valuations at least this minimum occur infinitely often: divide out the common power and use that 2 generates the units modulo every 3^j (by the order at 3 and the valuation of 2^2−1). For j≥1 the zero class modulo the order has exactly one lift that remains zero modulo 3^(j+1); the other two give exact j. In particular the 63 chart has unbounded v3 without holding its 3-free part fixed.

## Why a fixed chart is the wrong place to demand a whole ray

**Fixed finite dyadic palette.** For any d>0, a fixed finite signed sum of dyadic monomials Σc_i·2^u_i meets the ray d·3^j at only finitely many distinct j, even if the nonnegative exponents u_i vary independently. Terms with zero coefficients can be removed. In any equality choose a minimal vanishing subsum containing −d·3^j. Divide by d·3^j; each variable 2^u_i·3^−j belongs to the rank-two S-unit group, and no nonempty subsum of the left vanishes. The [Evertse–Schlickewei–Schmidt theorem](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/2002/155-3/p04) gives finitely many normalized tuples for each of finitely many subsum supports. Each normalized coordinate determines j by its 3-valuation. This includes the three-width formula above, but not unbounded run complexity. No effective enumeration cutoff is claimed from this theorem.

**Fixed single transitive replacement.** For every fixed rooted tournament T,v, replacing v with TT_m meets a fixed exact ray d·3^j at only finitely many j. The skeleton expansion is Σ_k a_k k!S(m,k)=Σ_i c_i i^m, where S is the Stirling number of the second kind and a_k counts skeletons with k runs in the module. The coefficient c_1=Σ_k(−1)^(k−1)k a_k is odd: a_1=H(T) is odd, k=2 contributes an even term, and for k≥3 the (k−1)! free permutation of disjoint exterior excursions makes a_k even. For sufficiently large m, every nonempty subsum of the nonzero source terms is nonzero, by its largest distinct base. Normalize by d·3^j and apply the same S-unit theorem, now to the group generated by 3 and the primes in the finitely many integer bases. The base-1 variable is 3^−j and determines j. If the replacement is constant, the conclusion is immediate. This treats even fixed charts having a 3^m mode: a ternary mode is not a pure ternary clock.

## Next proof obligation

Use two-run equivalence as an interface, not a scalar heuristic. The new question is whether an exact parent-to-child construction can **replace the spent two-run block by a fresh ready interface**, changing the decomposition as needed, while hitting the other prime-indexed columns as well. The finite single-birth obstruction and the S-unit obstruction say what kinds of reuse cannot achieve this. The strong chain proves that feedback does not invariably charge a positive excess for exact ×3: the entire relevant response can scale proportionally. That local positive result does not fill arbitrary prescribed counts.
