# Local rotation defects recover the exact Collatz cycle denominator

Date: 20 October 2026.
Status: PROVED by the elementary argument below; exact controls accompany it. Independent review is still required before promotion to canon. This is a cycle-denominator theorem, not a proof of Collatz convergence.

## 1. Inheritance and the actual new mechanism

The closest mechanism is THM-4716, `balanced-collatz-cycles-are-three-smooth-ladders-and-are-free.md`: adjacent swaps between balanced rotations produce 2–3-smooth carry differences. The present argument removes the balanced-word assumption. Arbitrary rotational disagreement cores generate an ideal whose positive generator is exactly the cancellation factor of the rational cycle.

The general affine cocycle comes from THM-4604, `collatz-words-are-the-reducible-locus-carries-are-the-extension-cocycle-and-traces-only-hear-the-tuning.md`. The new step is an exact connected-graph content identity, rather than another trace or real-height inequality.

The canonical hostile is the word `000011`: its clock is 55 and carry is 80, so the cancellation factor is 5. Short local differences need not be units. A second hostile is a repeated word: `0101` has cancellation factor 7 although its underlying rational cycle is the ordinary 1–2 cycle. Primitivity must not be silently assumed.

The live concept board is: affine carry; cyclic rotation; localized disagreement; integer ideal/content; source-sensitive descent. The anchor is the local denominator invariant. The niche is sparse Bezout certificates among unbalanced rotations. The wildcard is the precise failure of finite alternating norm shapes to supply integer descent.

No literature-priority claim is made. This note does not use THM-4717's continuum-limit or topological assertions.

## 2. Definitions and the local carry identity

Let w be a binary word of length q containing p ones, with 0<p<q. Words are chronological for the shortcut map T(x)=x/2 on even integers and T(x)=(3x+1)/2 on odd integers. Define

    B(w) = sum over i with w_i=1 of 2^i 3^(number of ones strictly after i),
    D(w) = 2^q − 3^p.

The affine branch is (3^p x+B(w))/2^q and its rational fixed point is B(w)/D(w). Since D is odd, this fixed point has the stated parity word on odd-denominator rationals. Its reduced denominator is

    denominator(w) = |D| / g(w),   g(w)=gcd(|D|,B(w)).

For words a,b, with |a| the length and #b its number of ones,

    B(ab) = 3^(#b) B(a) + 2^|a| B(b).                  (2.1)

If two words of the same shape have the form P u S and P v S, where u,v have the same length ell and the same number h of ones, then

    B(PuS) − B(PvS) = 2^|P| 3^(#S) [B(u)−B(v)].       (2.2)

Thus identical outside context contributes only a 2–3-smooth unit relative to D. One may strip the maximal common prefix and suffix, but maximality is unnecessary.

Carry injectivity is elementary. For a word of known length and known number p of ones, its first bit is B(w) modulo 2. Writing that bit epsilon, the suffix carry is

    B(suffix) = [B(w) − epsilon 3^(p−epsilon)]/2.

This reconstructs the entire word recursively. In particular distinct equal-shape cores have distinct carries, so every disagreement below has a nonzero difference.

## 3. Exact local-defect ideal theorem

Let R(w) be the set of distinct cyclic rotations of w. Let G be ANY connected undirected graph with vertex set R(w). On every edge e, write its endpoint words as P_e u_e S_e and P_e v_e S_e and put

    delta_e = B(u_e) − B(v_e).

Then

    gcd over e in E(G) of |delta_e| = gcd(|D(w)|,B(w)).  (3.1)

Equivalently, the integer ideal generated by these local defect differences is precisely g(w) Z. This identity holds for primitive words and word powers alike. A spanning tree is enough; neither a sorted ladder nor all pairs of rotations are needed.

### Proof

Write B_i for the carry of the rotation starting at position i. Moving the first bit to the end gives the exact integer identity

    2 B_(i+1) = 3^(w_i) B_i + w_i D.                 (3.2)

Let h be the gcd of the full differences along the edges of G. Connectivity implies all B_i are congruent modulo h. Since w contains a zero, (3.2) at a zero position says 2 B_(i+1)=B_i; reduction modulo h forces B_i=0 modulo h. Therefore every B_i is divisible by h. Since w also contains a one, (3.2) at that position then forces h to divide D. Hence h divides g(w).

Conversely, g(w) is odd and divides D and one B_i. Equation (3.2), with 2 invertible modulo g(w), propagates divisibility to every rotation. Thus g(w) divides all full differences, and h=g(w).

Now (2.2) says that each full difference is a 2–3-smooth integer times delta_e. Put c=gcd_e |delta_e|. Then c divides h. Since h divides D and D is coprime to 6, h dividing each full difference also implies h divides each delta_e. Hence h divides c. This proves c=h=g(w). ∎

### Sparse certificates and exact denominators

For ANY subset of rotation pairs, connected or not, g(w) divides every local difference. Consequently

    g(w) divides gcd(|D|, delta_1,...,delta_s).

A short Bezout combination equal to 1 proves g(w)=1. More generally a connected-graph certificate computes g exactly, not merely a bound. One can stop a gcd computation early when it reaches 1. One can also remove factors 2 and 3 from individual local differences without changing the final gcd, since g is coprime to 6.

An integer cycle for 3x+1 requires D to divide B(w). Therefore it requires

    |D| divides delta_e for EVERY rotation pair.      (3.3)

For a graph spanning all distinct rotations, these divisibilities are also sufficient: (3.1) then gives g=|D|, making the rational cycle integral. Positivity is the additional condition D>0; B_i>0 automatically. This is an exact integer-cycle recognition criterion on a supplied word. It does not find or exclude all possible words.

More generally, for the shortcut 3x+d map with odd nonzero d, the cycle attached to w is integral exactly when |D|/g divides d. Its positivity condition is d/D>0. The generalization replaces 3 by any odd integer a>=3, with identical proof and context factors 2 and a. If B_a denotes the base-a carry and b is an odd nonzero additive parameter, the numerator-carry list is b B_a and its edge-difference content is |b| gcd(|2^q−a^p|,B_a). The actual rational cycle vertices are b B_a/D; the integer content statement applies to the numerator list, not to that divided list. The reduced denominator after scaling is |D|/gcd(|D|,b B_a). The oddness of b matters for the claimed parity itinerary; even b does not define an integer-valued odd branch on odd integer inputs.

## 4. A local width/weight obstruction

Among binary words of length ell with h ones, the least carry is attained uniquely at 1^h 0^(ell−h), and the greatest uniquely at 0^(ell−h) 1^h. Their carries are respectively

    3^h−2^h,   2^(ell−h)(3^h−2^h).

Proof: swapping adjacent 10 to 01 increases the carry by the positive 2–3-smooth factor supplied by its outside context. Repeating swaps reaches the displayed extremal words. Hence every distinct equal-shape core pair satisfies

    0 < |B(u)−B(v)| <= (2^(ell−h)−1)(3^h−2^h).       (4.1)

In an integer 3x+1 cycle every pair of distinct rotations therefore satisfies

    |D| <= (2^(ell−h)−1)(3^h−2^h).                  (4.2)

The inequality is necessary, not sufficient. Exact divisibility (3.3) is stronger. If a rotation pair differs only by one adjacent swap, then ell=2,h=1 and its difference is 1, so g=1. This recovers the arithmetic conclusion of the balanced ladder without using its full ladder: one such pair already suffices. The balanced family has the required pairs by THM-4716. The general theorem also handles unbalanced words through larger cores.

For a positive integer cycle, every distinct rotational core must be sufficiently wide or carry a sufficiently large local difference to accommodate D. This gives a combinatorial target: find forced short disagreement cores, or a small set of cores with incompatible common divisors, in a proposed class of near-critical cycle words.

For odd a>1, the right side of (4.1) is

    (2^(ell−h)−1)(a^h−2^h)/(a−2).

## 5. Concrete positive and hostile controls

### An unbalanced sparse certificate

Take w=0011011, q=7,p=4,D=47,B(w)=340. It is unbalanced: length-two factors include both 00 and 11.

- Rotation 4 is 0110011. Comparing with w strips prefix 0 and suffix 011, leaving 110 versus 011, with local difference 5−10=−5.
- Rotation 3 is 1011001. Comparing with w strips suffix 1, leaving 101100 versus 001101, with local difference 29−92=−63.

The two differences have gcd 1; explicitly 25·(−5)−2·(−63)=1. Thus the cancellation factor is 1 and the reduced denominator is 47. This certificate works without a connected graph, because the two edges already generate the unit ideal. Every pairwise local difference in this example remains larger than 1 after removing factors 2 and 3: a single smooth ladder edge is unavailable. Two arithmetically incompatible local defects recover what that edge would have supplied.

### A genuine nonunit content

For w=000011, D=55 and B(w)=80, g=5 and the reduced denominator is 11. Its local core 110 versus 011 has difference −5, so one must not infer freeness merely from a short core. A full connected-graph gcd is exactly 5. The word gives a positive rational 3x+1 cycle, and an integer 3x+11 cycle after scaling.

### Repetition preserves the reduced denominator

For w^k, put H=(2^(kq)−3^(kp))/(2^q−3^p), a positive integer. Cocycle summation gives

    B(w^k)=H B(w), D(w^k)=H D(w), g(w^k)=H g(w).

Thus the reduced denominator is unchanged. Example: w=01 has D=1,B=2, while w^2=0101 has D=7,B=14,g=7. A theorem saying every balanced word is free needs the primitive/coprime-shape qualification; (3.1) needs no such qualification.

### Actual signed integer cycles

The 1–2 cycle has word 10, D=1, g=1. The negative cycle starting at −17 has shortcut word 11110111000, D=−139, B=2363 and g=139. It is a hostile to any unrestricted claim that all primitive necklaces have unit defect content. The theorem handles both signs, while the positive-cycle target separately requires D>0.

## 6. What this contributes to the geometry question

A cyclic carry list can be drawn as points, a polygon, normalized simplex coordinates, or a norm section. The arithmetic payload here is the integer ideal generated by its pairwise differences, after removing known invertible context factors. Connectivity retains enough relations to recover that ideal exactly.

There is a precise lattice interpretation. The graph incidence matrix sends the integer vertex vector (B_i) to its edge-difference vector. Every spanning tree generates the same difference ideal as the complete graph: paths express arbitrary differences as signed sums of tree-edge differences. The positive generator is the single nonzero Smith invariant of this one-row list. Stripping context factors is localization at 2 and 3 followed by integer recovery; recovery is justified because the full ideal generator is coprime to 6. An arbitrary geometric normalization has no such automatic integral recovery. This is an incidence/lattice bridge, rather than a shared-shape analogy.

Changing among simplex, ellipsoid/sphere and box descriptions does not itself prove source descent. For the affine lift (x,1), any finite bank of fixed positive homogeneous norm gauges is uniformly comparable to x for x>=1: there are constants 0<c<=C with

    c x <= V_j(x) <= C x

for every mode j. The previously proved expanding lawful return J has multiplier lambda>1 and positive carry; for every L it has arbitrarily high positive sources following J^L. Along such a segment y/x>lambda^L. If the chosen gauges were nonincreasing at every J block, then c y<=C x, contradicted once lambda^L>C/c. Alternating two shapes, or any finite bank of uniformly comparable shapes, cannot evade this argument. This is a direct corollary of the inherited expanding-return obstruction, not a new general impossibility theorem for Lyapunov methods.

Unbounded source-dependent state, nonuniform metric distortion, selected return times, and an actual arithmetic carry/height coordinate are outside that obstruction. The local ideal above supplies a genuine arithmetic coordinate for periodic words; it is not yet an all-orbit height rank.

## 7. The next precise frontier

The existing source-descent frontier remains the guard-refined bank theorem: unrestricted word length, up to 1000 expansion episodes, with original-source repair. Removing its episode bound by the existing finite-forest method already requires coefficient stopping for every rational d−4912087879/88519643 with d>=56. Nothing in this note discharges that requirement or arbitrary bank entry.

The new cycle lane has a different exact target:

    For a rigorously specified class of primitive positive near-critical parity necklaces, force local disagreement differences whose gcd is a proper divisor of D.

This is sufficient to exclude that class. Balanced words are the unit-difference case. A useful next experiment is a bounded-defect-from-balanced class with exact edit locations and wrap-around data retained, followed by a symbolic gcd proof. It would be incorrect to assume every unbalanced word has such a certificate: the −17 cycle supplies a signed hostile, and the positivity restriction is substantive. The theorem offers a local arithmetic test, not a claimed all-positive-necks conclusion.
