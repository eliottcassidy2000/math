# Grow a prescribed unit flip from a neutral directed square

**PROVED.** Let two tournaments differing in one arc have Hamiltonian path counts \(h,h+2\), where \(h\notin\{1,3,9,13\}\). Delete their common exterior singleton components and erase the distinguished arc \(uv\). The remaining strong partial digraph can be grown from a directed four-cycle in which \(u,v\) are opposite. Each move adds either one vertex having both an incoming and an outgoing contact with the current set, or two vertices \(c,b\) with the uniform orientations \(S\to c\to b\to S\). All intermediate partial digraphs are strong. At least one one-vertex move changes the parity of the half-difference of the two completion counts. At exponent 61 this gives a necessary internal structure for every solution, on top of the order bound. [Strong primitive pair](README.md) · [Concurrent nested-contact classification](../chanpak_cech_perfect_seams_20261122/NESTED-CONTACTS.md)

The nested-contact theorem gives vertices \(x,y\) with \(u\to x\to v\) and \(v\to y\to u\). These are in the middle, and the four form a directed cycle already in the partial digraph. Either completion induces the unique strong tournament of order four, with five paths. Choose any such witnesses. If a vertex outside a current strong set \(S\) has contacts of both signs, adjoin it. Otherwise all outside vertices either dominate \(S\) or are dominated by it. Strongness of the whole partial digraph forces both sides to be nonempty and an arc from the dominated side to the dominating side. Its endpoints give the two-vertex move. These operations strictly enlarge the set, preserve strongness and eventually exhaust the middle. Conversely each step really does build a strong missing-arc digraph in both orientations. Heights of both endpoints are nondecreasing throughout by the odd-cycle packing expansion. This permits target ceilings at intermediate steps even when the difference changes sign. A marked cycle with its other diagonal specified and a history of moves can present the same final isotype several times.

## The parity test at a one-vertex step

For a tournament \(D\) and a set \(I\) of old vertices beaten by the new vertex, let \(a_D(I)\) be the parity of the number of directed paths on a positive even number of vertices from \(I\) to its complement. By the odd-cycle formula, a new packing contains one cycle through the new vertex. Deleting that vertex from its cycle leaves such an even-order path. Its complementary path count is odd, so the half-increase of the ordinary count equals \(a_D(I)\) modulo two. For a marked pair with completions \(D,D'\), the parity of the half-gap therefore toggles precisely if \(a_D(I)\ne a_{D'}(I)\). This criterion concerns actual paths and can be evaluated before attaching the new vertex. [Directed endpoint-cut formula](../chanpak_minimal_core_grammar_20261122/README.md)

Write \(R(D)\) for the two-vertex move on a completion, and \(Q_j(D)\) for its ordered \(j\)-slot path count, allowing empty slots. The archive gives
\[
 Q_j(R(D))=j^2 Q_j(D)+j\big(Q_{j+1}(D)-Q_{j-1}(D)\big).
\]
In particular \(H(R(D))=H(D)+Q_2(D)\) for nonempty \(D\). For an equal-order pair the difference of the two \(Q_2\) readings is a multiple of 4, and a multiple of 8 at even order. Indeed the path polynomial counts a disjoint packing of odd cycles \(\mathcal C\) with contribution \(2^{|\mathcal C|}j^{n-\sum_{C\in\mathcal C}(|C|-1)}\): each cycle chooses one of the \(j\) colors, as do the vertices it does not meet. The common empty-packing term cancels. Every other term has even coefficient and positive exponent congruent to \(n\) modulo two. Substitute \(j=2\). Thus every two-vertex move preserves the parity of the half-gap. The initial square has half-gap zero and the desired one is odd, proving that an **odd number** of the one-vertex moves pass the parity-toggle test in any such history. ## The last two-vertex move has a prescribed residue at every odd exponent

For equal input order \(n\), let \(\delta\) be the signed difference of the two completion counts before a uniform return and \(\delta'\) the difference afterwards. The same packing expansion gives the stronger transformation
\[
 \delta'\equiv 3\delta\pmod{12}\quad(n\ {
m odd}),\qquad
 \delta'\equiv 5\delta\pmod{24}\quad(n\ {
m even}).
\]
Indeed the difference polynomial is \(f(j)=2j^e A(j^2)\) for an integer polynomial \(A\), with \(e=1\) in odd order and \(e=2\) in even order. Thus \(f(2)-2^e f(1)\) is divisible by \(3\cdot 2^{e+1}\). The new difference is \(f(1)+f(2)\). The corresponding divisibility for a previously neutral pair is an instance of the [factorial divisibility theorem for changing readers](../chanpak_changing_sources_readers_20261114/FACTORIAL_LOCK.md).

For an individual host of order \(n\ge3\), remove its leading monomial \(j^n\) before applying the same argument. This adds \(2^n-2^e\) to the residue of the new count. For odd \(n\ge3\) this correction is 6 modulo 12, and for even \(n\ge4\) it is 12 modulo 24. Hence **every odd-order input gives a multiple of three after the return**. At even order the absolute law is \(H(R(D))\equiv5H(D)+12\pmod {24}\). An exact positive unit flip after a uniform return therefore requires an even-order predecessor with signed difference 10 modulo 24. In particular a uniformly wrapped unit flip cannot immediately give another unit flip in either direction.

At every odd exponent \(p\ge5\), \(E_p\equiv16\pmod{24}\), whether or not it is perfect. If a uniform return is the last step in an exact straddle, the two predecessor counts in lower-to-upper order are forced to be **15 and 1 modulo 24**. At any odd total order, only a mixed one-vertex attachment can finish such a square history. This is an absolute criterion on the predecessor, stronger than knowing that a switching class elsewhere contains every odd residue.

The first contacts of the square already illustrate the mixed rule. Label its cyclic order \(0,1,2,3\), set the other diagonal \(1\to3\), and take the distinguished arc initially as \(0\to2\). The two row vectors are \((6,12,8,1)\) and \((2,12,9,1)\). If the next vertex beats precisely \(\{1\}\), the counts become 13 and 11. If it beats \(\{0,2\}\), they become 13 and 15. Thus the very same neutral square can give either direction of a unit edge after one vertex.

## A finite test of what repeated two-vertex moves can distinguish

For two tournaments of the same order \(n\ge3\), let \(r=\lfloor(n-1)/2\rfloor\). Their entire ordered-slot polynomials agree if and only if their ordinary Hamilton counts agree after each of \(0,1,\ldots,r-1\) successive two-vertex moves. This applies to any pair of equal-order tournaments. In particular two completions invisible to those finitely many readings stay indistinguishable under any number of uniform returns.

The difference polynomial \(f(j)\) has zero leading coefficient and the parity of \(n\). The packing expansion shows that its possible powers are the positive integers at most \(n-2\) of that parity. This leaves exactly \(r\) coefficients. The triangular transfer above expresses the difference after \(m\) moves as \(m! f(m+1)\) plus a linear combination of \(f(0),\dots,f(m)\). The leading term is the unique path of \(m\) upward shifts. Since \(f(0)=0\), vanishing for those \(r\) moves gives \(f(1)=\cdots=f(r)=0\). Factoring out \(j\) or \(j^2\) gives a polynomial of degree at most \(r-1\) in \(j^2\), with \(r\) distinct roots, so \(f\) vanishes identically. The archive's all-depth injectivity and its transfer are the starting point. The finite equal-order stopping bound follows by taking parity into account.

There are actual neutral arcs for which one uniform return detects a difference, attaining the neutral divisors in both parities. On six vertices the rows \((6,12,24,33,11,23)\) and their flip at \((0,3)\) have counts 37 and 37, and two-slot readings 376 and 352. Their wrapped counts are 413 and 389. On seven vertices, flipping \((3,6)\) in \((2,12,73,49,39,71,27)\) keeps 91 paths and changes the two-slot count from 1,256 to 1,244. The differences 24 and 12 attain the respective divisors with a single physical marked arc. Through order five, enumerating every isomorphism class and marked arc reveals no neutral arc with unequal two-slot counts. By the finite stopping rule above none can be distinguished at greater depth. Thus six is the first possible order.
