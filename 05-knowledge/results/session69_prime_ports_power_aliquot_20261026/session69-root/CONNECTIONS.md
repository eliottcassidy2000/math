## 1. The two first-six statements are different predicates

For the odd-root doubling forest, n=2^a u with u odd has n² in the chain rooted at u². Positive doubling and a later root mean a>=1 and u>2^a. Six is first; allowing arbitrary even roots makes four earlier via 4²=2·8. This is a widespread square-later event (density1/2), not the rarity of a fixed point. Moreover nine is already a power of three; the least all-powers or repeated-square seed of36 is3.

For the aliquot map s(n)=sigma(n)-n on positive integers, six is the first self-return and first finite multiple target fibre: F(6)={6,25}, whereas F(1) was already infinite. The twin-prime identity

    s(2p)=s((p+2)²)=p+3

explains both F(6)'s displayed witnesses and F(8)'s {10,49}. Only p=3 solves 2p=p+3. This identity neither proves infinitely many twin primes nor says every such fibre has just these two members.

There is no directed-graph isomorphism between the two declared positive-integer carriers, even after forgetting operation colours. The doubling/squaring generator graph has exactly one loop, at1; all generator steps strictly increase integers greater than1. The aliquot graph has at least two loops,6->6 and28->28. If the unit square loop is suppressed, the first graph instead has no loops, so the obstruction remains. A quotient can discard this distinction, but then it is not a lossless dynamical correspondence.

## 3. ABC requires an additive branch and its removed scale

The equality6²=4·9 is multiplicative, not an ABC triple. The positive square-later difference does give a reversible additive construction:

    n=2^a u  <->  (u-2^a)+2^a=u,

where a>=1 and u is odd and larger than2^a. Six maps to1+2=3; the first radical-excess example in this carrier is n=72, giving1+8=9, whose radical is6. The newly needed coordinate is rad(u-2^a), not just the powers already visible in the multiplicative row.

The aliquot construction is different. Normalize n+s(n)=sigma(n) by g=gcd(n,s(n)); every perfect number then becomes1+1=2. Discarding g erases which perfect number supplied the equation. The two sources in F(6) yield different normalized triples:6 gives(1,1,2), while25 gives(25,6,31), of radical930. Neither the common target nor the numerical appearance of six identifies one exceptional ABC triple.

The fixed-core prime-extension inequality in the aliquot lane is unconditional but has a core-dependent constant. That is a guarded result for a family, not a uniform ABC bound.
