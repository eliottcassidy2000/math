## One-node growth and proof of termination of the three-piece phase

Adding n adds exactly edges `{n,s²-n}` with `n<s²<2n`. At n=13, neighbours 3 and 12 merge the first and third paths. At n=14, neighbours 2 and 11 join the remaining component. Thus component counts are 3,2,1 at n=12,13,14.

For every n>=6 put s=floor(sqrt(n))+1. Then n<s²<=n+2sqrt(n)+1<2n, since n>=6 implies 2sqrt(n)+1<n. Hence every subsequent vertex attaches to an earlier one. Connectivity at 14 persists forever; this is not a permanent three-way partition of all positive integers.

The exact founder criterion for ANY target-filtered companion graph is `T intersect [n+1,2n-1]` empty. If both 4 and 6 are founders, T avoids [5,7] and [7,11]; therefore it avoids [6,9], making 5 a founder too. Thus no target filter has exactly founder set {1,4,6}. This sharp impossibility prevents quietly renaming the three square-sum components with the closure holes.

## Concrete connection to the displayed square/even balances

The common mechanism is the excluded pair diagonal. In Q_n a vertex m loses a would-be self-edge exactly when 2m is a square, i.e. m=2j². The first such vertices are **2,8,18,32,...**, precisely the starting terms of the user's consecutive-even identities, proved in SUMMAND_SQUARES.md.

This is an exact equality of the two formulas, not merely a visual pattern: even-block first term E_k=2k² satisfies `2E_k=(2k)²`, so its diagonal square target would be `(2k)²`. It does NOT mean all these vertices are isolated or leaves. For example 32 is born with two earlier neighbours 4 and 17 (sums 36,49), despite the missing diagonal sum 64. The historical leaf anomaly at m=2,8,18 stops before 32. Thus the same numbers identify diagonal exclusion at every scale, but not a repeating component or leaf pattern.

Each edge of Q_n acts as reflection x->s²-x; two successive edges act as translation by a difference of squares. This supplies a literal addition/squaring connection while preserving the graph's admission rule. It is distinct from Gaussian squaring of Pythagorean triples and provides no abc estimate.
