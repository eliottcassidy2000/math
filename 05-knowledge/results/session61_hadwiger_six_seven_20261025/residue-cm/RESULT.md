## 1. The local-integrality criterion is an iff

**Proposition 1.** Let L be a conjugation-stable number field, c its complex conjugation, and P a finite prime. Then the following are equivalent:

1. `cP=P`.
2. Every `u∈L` with `u c(u)=1` is P-integral.
3. Every such u is a P-unit.

**Proof.** If P is fixed, `0=v_P(u c(u))=2v_P(u)`. Conversely, if P and cP are distinct, choose an algebraic integer `a∈P\cP`. Then `c(a)∉P`, so `u=a/c(a)` has positive P-valuation, while `u⁻¹` is a norm-one vector with negative P-valuation. Since norm-one vectors are closed under inversion, conditions2 and3 agree. ∎

The obstruction cannot be repaired just by choosing another basepoint for a graph component. An edge of difference `u⁻¹` cannot have both endpoints in one translate of the local ring. Thus this is an exact boundary for the *translated-local-ring reduction mechanism*, not a universal no-go for every conceivable coloring construction.

When P is fixed, the residue reduction gives the graph homomorphism in THM-4558, component by component. At an unramified fixed prime with residue extension `F_(q²)/F_q`, the residues of norm-one edge vectors are **all** of `μ_(q+1)`: lift any nonzero residue w and reduce `w/c(w)`, obtaining `w^(1−q)`. This finite-field power map has image precisely the norm kernel. On the entire local ring, a coloring factoring through this residue map is proper iff its coloring of the whole norm graph

`G_q=Cay(F_(q²), μ_(q+1))`

is proper: every residue vertex lifts and each residue edge lifts by adding a lifted norm-one vector. For the global component-by-component version, let Γ be the additive group generated by unit vectors. Its residue image is the additive subgroup H generated by μ_(q+1). Every finite graph component is a translate of `Cay(H,μ_(q+1))`, and every edge of that component lifts from Γ. Thus the minimum number of colors required by the factoring method is still χ(G_q), even without assuming that the finite graph is connected. This is a minimum for the **factoring method**, not an equality of chromatic numbers of source and target graphs.

## 2. The Eisenstein triangle excludes the prime7 from this method

**Proposition 2.** If `K⊂L`, no prime of L over a rational prime `p≡1 mod3` is fixed by conjugation. In particular, **7 is unavailable at every such extension L**.

**Proof.** For `p≠3`, `X²−X+1` splits over Fp iff `p≡1 mod3`: its roots are the primitive sixth roots for odd p (the p=2 case is irreducible). Thus such p splits into two primes of K exchanged by c. A c-fixed prime of L would contract to a c-fixed prime of K, impossible. ∎

More precisely, if a fixed unramified prime of L has residue `F_(q²)/F_q` in characteristic `p≠3`, then

`q ≡ −1 mod3`.

Indeed ζ3 survives with exact order3 after reduction, and c both inverts it and acts as q-Frobenius, so `ζ3^q=ζ3⁻¹`. At residue characteristic3 this argument is unavailable because the order-three root collapses.

There is an exact finite-graph counterpart: **G_q contains a triangle iff `q≡2 mod3` or `char F_q=3`.** Normalize a putative triangle to `(0,1,z)` by translation and multiplication by a norm-one element. Its conditions are `N(z)=N(z−1)=1`, hence `Tr(z)=1` and `z²−z+1=0`. If characteristic is not3, these roots supply a conjugate norm-one pair exactly when q acts by inversion on their order-three part. In characteristic3, `z=−1` supplies a triangle. Consequently the triangle-free graph G7 (49 vertices, norm-circle size8) is **not** the K7 lattice quotient discussed next.

At7 an explicit valuation witness avoids any abstract splitting ambiguity. Let

`α=2+ω`, `αbar=3−ω`, `N(α)=N(αbar)=7`, `u=α/αbar`.

At the prime with `ω→5`, u has positive valuation; at the conjugate prime with `ω→3`, u has a pole. Both u and u⁻¹ have Euclidean length1. Thus lattice reduction at7 exists, but it cannot extend by the same local-ring construction to the full field-plane unit-distance graph.

## 3. Quotient lower bounds do not lift: K4 and K7 controls

The unit-distance graph on O is the triangular lattice. Its six edge differences are

`±1, ±ω, ±(1−ω)`.

The map `a+bω → a+2b mod3` is proper on all six differences, and `(0,1,ω)` is a triangle. Therefore this source graph has chromatic number exactly3.

* Reduction modulo2 has residue F4. Its three nonzero differences all occur as images of the six units, so the image graph is **K4**. The map is vertex- and edge-surjective. This is genuinely the norm-torus graph G2: every nonzero element of F4 has norm1 to F2. It is not the graph defined by `x²+y²=1` in characteristic2; that different form is the square of a linear form.
* Reduction modulo `(2+ω)` is `a+bω→a+5b mod7`. The six units map bijectively to all six nonzero residues. Hence the quotient is **K7**, again vertex- and edge-surjectively. The alternative prime `(3−ω)=(2−ζ)` gives `ω→3`, equivalently `ζ→2`, matching the other lane's norm-seven convention.

In each case, edges lift individually, but no coherent clique of size4 or7 lifts. The elementary inequality furnished by a graph homomorphism is

`χ(source) ≤ χ(target)`,

not the reverse. In particular, a lower bound on χ(G_q) gives only a lower bound on how many colors this residue method needs. THM-4628's residue requirement of at least7 is not a lower bound7 for its characteristic-zero field graph or for the plane.

The full field graph G(K), not just its lattice, is also exactly3-chromatic. The unique ramified prime above3 is fixed by c and has residue F3 with c acting trivially. A norm-one vector reduces to ±1, so THM-4558's translated-component construction gives a 3-coloring; the same triangle gives the matching lower bound.

## 4. Adjoining the actual triangle carrier makes the residue obstruction at least14

Put

`L'=Q(ζ55,ζ3)=Q(ζ165)`.

The right side is increasing for q≥1. At659 it is strictly greater than13 because

## 5. Why the same splitting test controls the j=0 factors in the opposite role

For every prime p≥5, the displayed elliptic models E− and E+ have good reduction. E− is supersingular exactly when `p≡2 mod3`; it is ordinary when `p≡1 mod3`. The latter geometric classification is classical; an elementary trace calculation exposes the same mechanism without appealing to CM heuristics.

Write `a_p=p+1−#E−(Fp)`. If `p≡2 mod3`, cubing is a permutation of Fp, so

`a_p=−Σ_x χ(x³−1)=−Σ_t χ(t−1)=0`.

Conversely let `m=(p−1)/2`. Reducing the character sum modp shows that a_p is congruent to the coefficient of `x^(p−1)` in `(x³−1)^m`: only the positive exponent p−1 can contribute to the sum of powers, since the degree is below2(p−1). For `p≡1 mod3`, this coefficient is

`(-1)^(m−(p−1)/3) binom(m,(p−1)/3)`,

which is nonzero modp because all factorial arguments are belowp. Thus `a_p≠0 modp`. The standard criterion for elliptic curves over Fp, p≥5, identifies these cases with supersingular versus ordinary. The quadratic twist E+ has the same status.

The maps from C6 to E− and E+ preserve the two independent differential directions exactly as in Session60; the coefficient2 remains invertible for p≥5. Hence the actual shared bridge is **the same Eisenstein CM field and its Frobenius action**. It is not a claim that Picard rank is a chromatic invariant.

Thus2 and3 are exactly the primes requiring extra care for these explicit branch descriptions; at7 and13 the configuration is preserved. This is a statement about the displayed integral models, **not** a classification of intrinsic or potential good reduction at2 or3.
