# Inheritance, recovered mechanisms and the synthesis

**PROVED/CITED inherited statements are distinguished from the new assembled
arguments.** A chronological note supplies provenance only. The source versions
in the manifest and explicit corrected statements below take precedence.

## Inheritance pass and concept board

The closest proved mechanism is the **odd matching-thickening descent theorem**
in [odd_prime_function_cycle_20261018d.md](../odd_prime_function_cycle_20261018d.md):
with fixed edge shades and uniformly replicated binary pins, majority descends
from an odd cover. The other direct parent is the spanning-tree root calculation
in [SHARED_CLOCKS.md](../attachment_lattices_square_sum_20261023/square_sum/SHARED_CLOCKS.md).

The canonical hostile controls are the triangle whose double cover becomes
bipartite, the 81/27 tournament pair, and the genuine 5x+1 cycle
13->33->83->13. The corrected near miss is the claim that Hamiltonian count
digits recover separate odd-cycle packing coefficients. They do not:
the correction in THM-466 and the six-vertex witnesses are preserved below.
The least-used relevant sidecar is the **fixed-zero fibre** in the September
odd-residue floor law; its analogue here is a designated value that rounding
must retain, rather than an average that can overlook it.

The Anchor / Niche / Wildcard portfolio is:

- Anchor: LRC(14), specifically whether native phase/port certificates admit
  rounding that preserves the actual marked target. LRC(14) remains open.
- Niche: signed affine equations with integral values and all-modulus counts.
- Wildcard: local probability denominators as exact cover-degree obstructions.

The five live concepts are **specified involution**, **native shared ports and
clocks**, **two-primary integral obstruction**, **constraint-preserving
rounding**, and **target-sensitive weights**. Each proposed transfer was
compared with all five; the resulting failures and repairs are in the table.

### Replaying the corrected tournament examples

The normal and optimized checker independently compare subset path dynamic
programming with literal directed odd-cycle enumeration.

- The two historical five-vertex classes have H=11 and H=13, four triangles
  each, and respectively one and two directed five-cycles.
- At six vertices, alpha=(1,2,1) and alpha=(1,4,0) both evaluate to H=9.
  The full polynomial distinguishes them; all residues of H do not.
- The inherited 12-vertex pair retains exactly 67 common labelled cyclic
  triples while its groups have orders81 and27. Its regular binary
  attachment-mask counts remain1620 and1728.

The first pair has the same **number** of cyclic triples, not the same
labelled triple relation. It must not be conflated with the81/27 pair.

The Hamiltonian identity is classical: [Irving–Omar, Corollary20](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.10572v3)
credits Grinberg–Stanley. Cycles are counted modulo rotation, not reversal;
distinct cycles sharing a support remain distinct objects. In this language
H=I(Omega(T),2), with Omega the literal cycle-conflict graph. Arbitrary
independence graphs need not lie in this tournament image.

## The common mechanism, including its opposite directions

The [recent Fano carrier](../scaled_count_fft_carriers_20261030/THEORY.md),
S1–S2, gives another exact connection. Its seven coordinates have syndrome
sum_a e_a*a in F_2^3, with16 masks in each of eight syndrome classes.
Odd XOR preserves each affine class; median need not. Masks0,7,25 have
zero syndrome, but their bitwise majority is mask1 with nonzero syndrome.
The checker exhausts all6528 unordered triples with repetitions in the
eight classes. P3b proves the all-odd-arity XOR statement. This uses the
Fano sign-table carrier; it is not an identification of that construction
with the distinct seven-prime square-class realization.

Graph covers and fractional local data also have established coding-theory
precedent: [Vontobel–Koetter](https://arxiv.org/abs/cs/0512078) study graph-cover
decoding and its relation to linear-programming decoding. P4b supplies its
own elementary factor-graph matching construction and the exact denominator
valuation formula. It does not claim to originate that correspondence.

There are two different uses of an odd integer:

| Operation | Effect on a two-primary obstruction |
|---|---|
| Reduce an equation modulo an odd q | Two becomes invertible; half-integral obstructions may disappear |
| Use an odd-degree extension with a genuine transfer identity | Multiplication by the degree is invertible on two-primary torsion; the obstruction cannot disappear |
| Use an odd number of compatible ordered tuples | A middle order statistic exists; both increasing and decreasing fixed maps preserve it |
| Pass from integral pair coordinates to rational even/odd projections | Division by two erases an integral parity compatibility condition |
| Retain local marginals over Z_(2) | Odd denominators are allowed but the obstruction at two remains visible |

This distinction is the cohesive explanation. The theorem is not that odd
objects are interchangeable. It is that **a specified sign action, a specified
integral lattice and an allowed rounding operation determine which binary
obstructions survive a representation change**.

P4b sharpens this into an exact invariant: the smallest two-adic exponent of
a feasible cover degree is computed on the maximal support face. In the
signed-affine case only exponent0 or1 is needed. A cyclic q-state relation
requires degree divisible by q, showing why arbitrary arithmetic clocks are
not reduced to sign reversal.

For the tournament map, vertices are the original named vertices; the
pairwise observable is the sign of the oriented edge, with the common
vertex ordering as gauge. Odd majority has no ties. The preserved target
is a labelled tournament; neither its transitivity nor its automorphism
group is preserved. No runner or arithmetic object is forced into a
tournament merely because a majority operation is available.

## Incoming work changes the next tests

The current [terminal-fees packet](../terminal_fees_phase_covers_20261030/README.md)
already closes all nine E11 price families and leaves seven E12 families
open. Older handoffs calling E11 open are superseded. Its adaptive controller
keeps actual source residue and admits all lifts when precision rises;
this is an upper enclosure, not a deterministic quotient. A common marginal
rounding must not silently replace those joint source/clock choices.

The same packet gives full positive-thirteenth-speed phase covers for28
declared twelve-speed cores at clearance1/14; arbitrary-input LRC(14)
remains open. These offer a finite, exact testbed for whether a proposed
rounding language retains actual boundary points.

The incoming [Session118 shared-gate frontier](../session118_shared_gate_deletions_20261030/frontier/FRONTIER.md)
has the exact region 0<=c<=4 and b>=max(c,2c-2,4c-8); the endpoint weights
are(1,4,2) with dimension charge8. Its local result does not verify the
external candidate's whole circuit family. Its lesson for this packet
is concrete: identify the actual feasible face before optimizing or
rounding. No circuit lower bound is deduced from P4.

The earlier [rectangle counting compiler](../rectangular_counting_compiler_20261030/README.md)
uses odd moduli to recover a known bounded integer after a dyadic producer
scale. P2's half-integral hostile is compatible with that result:
reconstructing a known integer is different from proving that a rational
solution can be integral.

### Late incoming exact overlap and the odd-prime boundary

[Session119](../session119_odd_transport_classifications_20261030/README.md)
arrived during publication. Its complete inhomogeneous signed-graph theorem
independently contains the tree-coordinate/Smith/modular mechanism used in
P2. That overlap is explicitly credited; it is not presented here as a
priority claim. The cover compression, positive-face denominator invariant
and choice of symmetric rounding operation are the present packet's focus.

Its [weighted-cycle classification](../session119_odd_transport_classifications_20261030/arithmetic/WEIGHTED_CYCLE_OBSTRUCTIONS.md)
adds a decisive hostile. Clock words(1,1,1,2,1,1,4) and(1,1,1,1,1,2,4)
both have D=2^11-3^7=-139, but ordered carries2363 and2123. The first
gives the integral negative cycle starting at-17; the second is nonintegral.
Both rational solutions have only odd denominators. Our native checker
replays the numerators and every rational cycle equation.

Thus the abstract Smith group does not specify the obstruction of the
actual right-hand side, and the prime2 invariant sees neither odd
denominator. Native Collatz applications need the ordered carry and
appropriate odd-prime data as well. This is compatible with P4, which
uses Ub, not merely the diagonal Smith entries, and only claims a
specified denominator valuation. The incoming negative-cycle example is
not an exclusion of positive cycles.
