# Ranked next questions

**OPEN, not implied solved by the forecasts or finite controls.**

1. **A decreasing Chan–Pak inverse.** Find finite physical macros on an integral
   returning cone, with compatible residue coverage and a strictly decreasing
   height. Keep the positive36-state image or an explicitly transported lattice
   in the rational17 quotient. Deliverable: an all-target certificate or a
   precise obstruction for that grammar, not another huge finite atlas.
2. **Boundary law of H/r^m.** The occupied-slot theorem gives bounded log
   fluctuations and positive pressures. Identify a limiting distribution in
   law, possible suffix-driven nonconvergence, exact tails and negative-moment
   pressure. Support-only rare-event envelopes are lower bounds, not equalities.
3. **A growing prime alphabet.** Quantify how maximum prime order/description
   length must grow to cross the finite-alphabet entropy and half-edit barriers.
   Finite seed trees plus subquadratic seam edits cannot do it. A concise
   generator of infinitely many prime types is not excluded.
4. **Unified realizable interfaces.** Combine q, rooted path-cover tensors,
   characteristic polynomials, resolvent matrices and actual quotient
   stabilizers. Classify the physical image constraints, rather than treating
   arbitrary numerical states as tournaments. Find which small interfaces
   support both target descent and symmetry/spectral control.
5. **High-order forecast selection.** Improve the support envelope and choose
   support/defect cutoffs by requested correction accuracy, especially near
   intermediate n. The fixed-order transseries is inherited; a uniform
   growing-order theorem or optimal complexity result is not supplied.
6. **Interval-ready numerical forecasts.** Add directed high-precision intervals
   when exact leading-digit certification is genuinely needed. The present
   mpmath tables intentionally separate mathematical truncation from numerical
   evaluation. Do not spend effort printing exact huge integers merely to
   validate asymptotic predictions.
7. **Weighted Gaussian observables.** For an observable without closed Gaussian
   moments, test the identity tilt against deterministic finite-support
   quadrature. Obtain a growing-support variance bound before claiming a
   general fast sampler. Fixed-sector variance is not enough.
8. **Rare conditional ensembles.** The U/L mixture controls bounded events
   absolutely and q means relatively. Regular, doubly regular, self-converse
   and other exponentially rare conditioned classes need sharper conditional
   stabilizer laws. The concurrent switching-class parity refutation at N=24
   remains respected; no claim here uses the refuted form of HYP-9667.

A complete answer to the user's broad classification goal must always declare
carrier, operations, equivalence, observer and scale. The present atlas closes
several of these precise questions and preserves the others as explicit work.
