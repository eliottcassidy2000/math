# Session 110: additional proofs and boundaries

## Odd abelian prime Cayley tournaments

**Theorem.** Every nontrivial finite abelian group of odd order admits an indecomposable Cayley tournament, except C3 × C3. This is an elementary derivation here; priority is not asserted.

A Cayley tournament of odd order is strong: its condensation is a linearly ordered list of strong components, and vertex transitivity forces that list to have one component. In a composite strong tournament the canonical maximal proper modules partition the vertices. Translation preserves this partition. The block containing zero is therefore a subgroup H. Consequently it suffices to exclude all proper nontrivial subgroup-coset module partitions.

Write N=|G|. There are A=2^((N−1)/2) Cayley connection sets. For a fixed subgroup H of size h, the sets making its cosets modules number
2^((h+N/h−2)/2):
choose a tournament on H and a tournament on G/H. Thus the proportion of composite states is at most
Σ_H 2^{−(h−1)(N/h−1)/2}.

Each proper subgroup can be generated by at most floor(log_3 N)−1 elements. Padding a generating tuple by zeros bounds their number by N^{floor(log_3 N)−1}. Since h,N/h≥3, the displayed proportion is at most
R(N)=N^{floor(log_3 N)−1} 2^{1−N/3}.
This is strictly below one for N≥37. On 37≤N<81 its logarithmic derivative is 2/N−log(2)/3<0, and
R(37)^3=1369^3/2^34<1.
For k≥4, at N=3^k,
log_2 R(N)<2k(k−1)+1−3^{k−1}.
The right side is −2 at k=4 and decreases thereafter. Within each interval [3^k,3^{k+1}) the bound decreases.

Prime-order groups have no proper nontrivial subgroups. The remaining odd composite orders below37 are 9,15,21,25,27,33,35. The portable checker enumerates every abelian group of these orders and verifies the union bound is strict except for C3². In C3² each of four lines supports four module events; events for distinct lines are disjoint. Indeed, for independent a,b in two such lines, constancy off each line gives orientation(a+b)=orientation(a)=orientation(b), but orientation(a−b)=orientation(a)=orientation(−b), a contradiction. All16 states are therefore composite.

The finite values in the receipt are union bounds, not exact prime counts, except the separately exhausted exceptional case.

## Three essential cuts in a dual-number ring

Let R=(Z/p²)[ε]/(ε²), p odd, and impose invariance under g=1+ε. Since g−1 generates ε, invariant additive subgroups are ideals. The canonical-module argument above, with g included in the symmetry group, reduces compositeness to ideal-coset cuts.

Put
t=2^((p−1)/2), B=2^((p²−1)/2),
C=2^((2p²−p−1)/2), D=2^((3p²−2p−1)/2).
Then the number of prime labelled invariant connection sets is
P=D−2tC−B²+3t²B−t⁴.

The proper nonzero ideals are L=(pε), pR, the p cyclic ideals I_s=<(ps,1)> for s∈Fp, and m=(p,ε). Here I_0=E=(ε). Only L,E,m need be retained in the union of module events. If a cut at I_s with s≠0 occurs, its cyclic order-p² quotient has induced clock 1−ps. That clock makes each nonzero residue class modulo p monochromatic, forcing the larger m cut. The pR quotient is the order-p² shear ring and gives the same implication.

The individual counts for L,E,m are tC,B²,tC. Each pair intersection has t²B states, and the triple has t⁴. Inclusion–exclusion proves the formula. This is a non-chain ideal lattice: replacing it by a single scalar depth loses necessary joint cut data.

The complete ideal/subgroup checks at p=3,5,7 are in portable_checks/dual_number_primes.js. In particular the p=5 bank has 4,244,832,000 prime states out of 2^32. These are labelled bank states, not graph isomorphism classes.

## Source-preserving residuals and the tail boundary

For the killed odd Collatz map, use original source index j=(n+1)/2≥2 and weight w_j=1/[j(j+1)]. Its total mass is 1/2. Let Q push mass along the native map and kill it at the target. If τ_j is the hitting time, set a_j=1−z^{τ_j}, taking z^∞=0 for 0<z<1. Then a=(1−z)1+zQ* a.

For any signed summable v, set e=w+zQv−v. The exact identity is
<w,a>=(1−z)Σv+<e,a>.
Given certified l≤a≤u, it yields the upper bound
(1−z)Σv+Σ e_+u−Σ e_-l.
Signs and original source weights cannot be discarded.

If v has finite support, e=w outside the finite set consisting of its support and its forward image. For J beyond this set, the residual positive tail is exactly 1/(J+1). Finite rearrangements do not reduce that coefficient. With certified endpoint bounds on j≤J one obtains
D_z≤(1−z)Σv+Σ_{j=2}^J(e_+u−e_-l)+1/(J+1).
A bound strictly below w_s excludes nontermination at source s. A bound below w_s(1−z^{K+1}) proves τ_s≤K. Increasing J with unchanged endpoint bounds u=1 only repartitions mass.

An infinite first-reset construction can have tail coefficient z/(4−3z), while every finite truncation still has coefficient1. This is not a contradiction: truncating by original sources gives ℓ1 convergence, but the asymptotic tail coefficient is not continuous in that norm. The cutoff at which the asymptotic behavior appears can escape to infinity.

The concurrent source-cutoff work adds an essential limit: when upper endpoint data are already a Bellman supersolution, finite signed-flow optimization has a nonnegative optimum and creates no new termination coverage beyond the contained certified paths. Improvement of one particular estimate is not a universal convergence argument.
