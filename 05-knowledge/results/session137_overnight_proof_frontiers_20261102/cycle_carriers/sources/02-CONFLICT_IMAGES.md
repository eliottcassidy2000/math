# Natural odd-cycle conflict graphs: sparse does not mean a tree

Status: authored elementary proofs, one explicitly cited standard tournament theorem, and finite-exact checks. No novelty claim.

## Findings first

1. The connected components of the natural odd-cycle conflict graph Ω(T) correspond exactly to the nontrivial strongly connected components (SCCs) of T.
2. Every connected component of Ω is either K1, K2, or contains K4. Consequently Ω is a forest iff all SCCs of T have order at most four; these forests are precisely matchings with isolated vertices. In particular P3 and every larger tree are impossible as Ω(T).
3. If additionally m=v−1, the forest cases are only K1 and K2, with Hamiltonian-path counts 3 and 5. But nonforest cases exist infinitely often.
4. First nonforest sparse example: a 14-vertex tournament with Ω=K4 ⊔ 3K1, v=7, m=6, H=243. Order14 is minimal, not merely a construction size.
5. Infinite family: Ω=tK4 ⊔ (2t+1)K1, t≥1, realized on 11t+3 tournament vertices, with (v,m)=(6t+1,6t) and H=3^(4t+1).

## Definitions and inheritance

Vertices of Ω are **literal directed simple odd cycles of length at least three, modulo cyclic rotation only**, not vertex supports. Distinct cycles sharing a support remain distinct vertices. Two vertices are adjacent iff their cycle supports intersect. The inherited odd-cycle formula is H(T)=I(Ω(T),2), where I(G,z)=Σ_{independent S}z^|S| and H counts Hamiltonian paths as vertex words.

Closest mechanism: the [Session66 odd-cycle conflict interface](../session66_root27_aliquot_graph_interfaces_20261026/session66-graph-pairs/REPORT.md). Corrected near miss: cycles cannot be identified merely by supports. Canonical hostile: I(K3,2)=7 while no tournament has H=7. Least-used sidecar here: SCC support and cyclomatic excess, not just scalar H. Live concepts: literal cycles; SCC direct sum; forest obstruction; component debt; missing image constraints.

## 1. SCC/component theorem (elementary proof)

Partition V(T) by the equivalence relation generated by putting the three vertices of every directed triangle in one class. A nonsingleton class induces a strong subtournament: a chain of overlapping strong triangles has strongly connected union. Singleton classes are strong trivially.

Let A be a class and x outside it. If x has both an out-neighbor and an in-neighbor in A, take a directed path in A from an out-neighbor to an in-neighbor. At a consecutive transition a→b we have x→a→b→x, a triangle crossing the classes, contradiction. Thus x is uniformly oriented toward A. Applying this to both classes shows every pair of classes has a uniform orientation. Their quotient tournament has no directed triangle (any such triangle lifts), hence is transitive. Therefore these classes are exactly the SCCs.

Within a nonsingleton class the intersection graph of its triangles is connected by construction. Every vertex belongs to one of these triangles. Any odd cycle lies entirely within an SCC, and intersects a triangle at any of its vertices. Thus adding the remaining odd-cycle vertices leaves one connected component per nontrivial SCC and creates no cross-SCC edges. This proves the claim.

Singleton SCCs are invisible to Ω; therefore Ω does not recover tournament order or the placement of singletons.

## 2. The K4 gap and forest classification

**Cited input:** every strong tournament is vertex-pancyclic, hence every strong tournament on at least five vertices contains a directed 5-cycle. Its support induces a strong five-vertex tournament S.

Primary research source for the classical result: J. W. Moon, *On Subtournaments of a Tournament*, Canadian Mathematical Bulletin9(3),1966,297–301, DOI https://doi.org/10.4153/CMB-1966-038-7 . Bibliographic publication verified at https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/canadian-mathematical-bulletin/article/on-subtournaments-of-a-tournament/5D7CCBE66AB07BB3F90F771D1CD3CD08 . The exact theorem statement was checked in Meierling–Volkmann–Winzen, *Restricted arc-connectivity of generalized tournaments*, Australasian Journal of Combinatorics40(2008),269–278, Theorem1.3, https://ajc.maths.uq.edu.au/pdf/40/ajc_v40_p269.pdf . Original Moon full proof was not retrieved; no claim of independently reproving its general theorem.

Here is an independent elementary lower bound on the odd cycles of S, so no inherited strong-H minimum is needed. Every strong five-vertex tournament has outdegrees in {1,2,3}, summing to10. The number of directed triangles is

    C(5,3) − Σ_x C(d⁺(x),2).

Under these constraints Σ C(d,2)≤7, with maximum at the multiset1,1,2,3,3. Thus S has at least three triangles. These and the displayed 5-cycle give four distinct odd cycles. Any two subsets of a five-element set of size at least three intersect. Hence they span K4 in Ω(T).

For a strong three-vertex tournament Ω=K1. For a strong four-vertex tournament the degrees are1,1,2,2, so its triangle count is C(4,3)−2=2; these triangles intersect, giving Ω=K2. Together with the SCC theorem this proves:

    Ω(T) is a forest ⇔ every SCC has size≤4 ⇔ Ω(T) is a disjoint union of K1 and K2.

Conversely any such disjoint union is realized by a dominance-ordered sum of directed triangles and strong four-vertex tournaments. In particular no connected natural conflict graph can be K3 or a tree of order≥3. The obstruction is stronger than the scalar exclusion of7: it excludes entire graph shapes independently of H.

## 3. Exact sparse-deficit law

Write the nontrivial SCCs as a copies of order3, b copies of order4, and large SCCs S1,…,Sr of order≥5. Let Fi=Ω(Si), vi=|V(Fi)|, mi=|E(Fi)|, and δi=mi−vi. Since Fi is connected and contains K4, δi≥2: a connected graph with K4 has at least vi+2 edges. Then

    m(Ω)−v(Ω) = Σ_i δi − a − b.

Consequently the **exact iff** is

    m(Ω)=v(Ω)−1 ⇔ a+b = 1+Σ_i δi.

This is a complete reduction to connected large-SCC blocks, not a full classification of which graphs can occur as those blocks. The Hamiltonian count is

    H(T)=3^a 5^b ∏_i I(Fi,2).

Any chosen finite collection of large tournament blocks can be balanced: append exactly 1+Σδi directed-triangle blocks, or any mixture of that many triangle and strong-four blocks, ordered by dominance. This is an intrinsic tournament direct-sum construction, not padding an arbitrary graph while pretending it remains Ω of the original tournament. It changes H and tournament order explicitly.

For r=0 the equality requires a+b=1: only Ω=K1 or K2. For r≥1 it forces at least three small blocks. More sharply, a+b≥2r+1, and the tournament has at least5r+3(2r+1)=11r+3 vertices. Thus the first nonforest sparse Ω requires at least14 tournament vertices.

In standard graph language, β=m−v+c is the total cyclomatic number. Sparse equality m=v−1 means β=c−1, not β=0; only if c=1 does it force a tree. Here large components have β≥3 and small components β=0.

## 4. Explicit procedural families

For n≥3 define R_n on0,…,n−1: orient i→j for i<j, except reverse the long edge to n−1→0. This tournament is strong, as witnessed by0→1→…→n−1→0.

Every directed cycle must use the sole backward edge n−1→0. Its remaining vertices form an increasing subsequence of1,…,n−2. An odd cycle therefore corresponds precisely to a nonempty odd-cardinality subset of those n−2 interior vertices. There are

    q_n = 2^(n−3)

such subsets. All cycles share0 and n−1, so

    Ω(R_n)=K_(q_n),   H(R_n)=1+2q_n=1+2^(n−2).

For n≥5 put q=2^(n−3) and d=q(q−3)/2. The dominance sum

    R_n ⊕ (d+1) C3

has Ω=K_q ⊔ (d+1)K1, m=v−1, and H=(2q+1)3^(d+1). Here ⊕ means all arcs point from the earlier to the later block.

At n=5, R5 has precisely the triangles (0,1,4),(0,2,4),(0,3,4) and the 5-cycle(0,1,2,3,4), all in directed cyclic order. Thus Ω(R5)=K4. Appending three C3 gives the order14 minimal example.

More economically, taking t copies of R5 and2t+1 copies of C3 gives

    Ω=tK4 ⊔ (2t+1)K1,
    |V(T)|=11t+3,
    v=6t+1, m=6t,
    H=9^t 3^(2t+1)=3^(4t+1).

This has t dense components and2t+1 singleton components, so β=3t=c−1 as required.

## Boundary / surviving question

This proves a sharp natural-image obstruction and a broad family of genuine sparse conflict graphs. It does **not** show every graph with m=v−1 occurs, nor that every dense connected graph containing K4 occurs, nor determine the full Hamiltonian-path spectrum. Connected-image classification beyond K1/K2/K4 containment remains open here. In particular scalar equality I(G,2)=H(T) alone is not a certificate G≅Ω(T).

Additional independent numerical check: endpoint/subset dynamic programming counts Hamiltonian paths directly (without the odd-cycle identity), confirming H(R_n)=1+2^(n−2) through n=8 and H=243 on the explicit14-vertex dominance-sum tournament.

Sharpness refinement: any nonforest sparse natural Ω has at least seven conflict vertices and six edges, since a large component has at least four vertices and needs at least three small compensating components. Equality forces Ω=K4 ⊔ 3K1. Likewise equality in the tournament-order bound14 forces one order5 SCC and three order3 SCCs, again giving this unique conflict-graph shape (not a claim of unique tournament isomorphism type).

Verification hardening (independent audit correction): every validity test now calls an explicit `check(condition,message)` helper that raises `RuntimeError` on failure; there are no Python `assert` statements. Both normal and optimized (`python -O`) executions passed and produced byte-identical output, also identical to the original verification output. Optimization therefore cannot silently disable the checks.

Independent audit: a separate recursive cycle enumerator checked all 33,867 labelled tournaments through six, including repeated cycles on the same support, and confirmed the SCC and forest laws. The public verifier retains the stated through-five census and larger explicit constructions.
