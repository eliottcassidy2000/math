# Reflection balance: complete arithmetic inverse, physical two-bank tape

## The triangular center

Let the left block be the consecutive integers with offsets `-n,...,0` from `x` and the right block the offsets `1,...,n`. The central unpaired term is the only one of its kind. For the first power each mirror pair contributes `-2j`; for squares each contributes `-4xj`. Thus the signed totals are
$$
x-n(n+1),\qquad x(x-2n(n+1)).
$$
For positive blocks the roots are `x=2T_n` and `x=4T_n`; the first blocks begin at `n²` and `n(2n+1)`, respectively. The initial square identity is `3²+4²=5²`; the next are `10²+11²+12²=13²+14²` and `21²+22²+23²+24²=25²+26²+27²`. Square-triangular scales are the special case `T_n=y²`, equivalently `(2n+1)²-8y²=1`. Starting with the trivial `(x,y)=(1,0)`, the positive Pell scales are generated by `(x,y)→(3x+8y,x+3y)`, and at those scales the quadratic balance center is literally the square `(2y)²`.

There is a complete arithmetic inverse even without requiring the two block lengths to differ by one. For `1≤a<b≤c`,
$$
\sum_{i=a}^{b-1}i=\sum_{i=b}^{c}i
\iff T_{a-1}+T_c=2T_{b-1}
\iff (c+a)^2+(c-a+1)^2=(2b-1)^2.
$$
Consequently linear two-block balances biject with triples `X>Y>0`, `X²+Y²=Z²`, with opposite-parity legs. The inverse is
$$
a=(X-Y+1)/2,\qquad b=(Z+1)/2,\qquad c=(X+Y-1)/2 .
$$
Here `a<b` follows from `Z>X-Y`, and `b≤c` follows because the positive even difference `X+Y-Z` is at least two. Primitivity is not required: odd multiples are admissible, even multiples are not. The owner's consecutive-length family gives the triples `(2n(n+1),2n+1,2n(n+1)+1)`, precisely the triples with consecutive Euclid parameters `(n+1,n)`. The first balance, `1+2=3`, and the square-sum chain from 8 to 9 both recover the same triple `(3,4,5)` through different maps.

## A literal tournament with two independent banks of choices

Take a transitive tape on vertices `0,...,N-1` oriented forward; tape vertex `N` is a sentinel receiving from every earlier tape vertex. Add one head `x`, beaten by precisely the marked tape vertices `V⊆{1,...,N-2}` and by the sentinel, and beating all other vertices. This is strong: x reaches 0, 0 reaches the tape, and the tape reaches x via the sentinel. Let
$$
K(d)=2^{d-1}+[d=1],\qquad J(V)=\sum_{u<v\text{ in }V}K(v-u).
$$
Then
$$
H=1+2^N+\sum_{v\in V}(2^v-2^{N-1-v})-J(V).
$$
**Direct verification.** The tape including sentinel retains increasing order in any contiguous run; the head separates at most two such runs. Write `w_i=1` when the head beats tape vertex i, zero otherwise. We have `w_0=1,w_N=0`, hence one head-first path and one head-last path. For internal head, let i be its predecessor and j its successor. If `j<i`, the middle vertices strictly between j and i can go into either run, giving `2^(i−j−1)` choices. If `i<j`, they must be consecutive or no valid path exists; when consecutive the assignment is unique. Adjacent 10 transitions exceed the number of 01 transitions by exactly one, so the formula is `H=1+Σ_{j<i} K(i−j)w_j(1−w_i)`. Relative to empty V, flipping the bit at v creates the previous-position term `Σ_{d=1}^vK(d)=2^v`, removes the sentinel term `K(N−v)=2^(N−1−v)`, and shared pairs between selected v account for exactly the subtraction of J.

Put a core of width `2n+1` within the tape, with left and right unmarked guards of lengths `a,b≥1`. Label core positions by their signed offsets `r=-n,...,n` from the center. Split the available radii into two disjoint banks:
- At radius in A, independently elect zero marks or two marks at its reflected positions. A may also contain the center bit.
- At radius in B, elect exactly one of its two reflected positions as mark.

Keep any remaining choices fixed. Then the output decomposes exactly as
$$
H(A,B)=C+F(A)+G(B)
$$
for all choices and all guard lengths. Indeed a change of A is reflection-even, a change of B is reflection-odd, and `K(|r-s|)` is invariant when both signs reverse. Their mixed bilinear finite difference equals its own negative, hence vanishes. Linear terms cancel from the finite difference automatically. This produces all four corners of any such rectangle as actual strong tournaments, and allows both banks to have arbitrarily many switches as n grows. It does **not** assert independent switches inside one bank, or completion of an arbitrary interval.

### Two all-height pairs at the same seam, and the boundary of the central shortcut

Fix r in `{1,2}`, n>r, and use precisely one radius in B. At equal guards a=b, mark only one of its two positions, optionally also toggling the center. Toggling the center decreases H by exactly two in either orientation. With `m=n+a`, the two pairs have anchors
$$
1+2^{2m+1}\;\pm\;2^m(2^r-2^{-r}),
$$
each followed by its anchor minus two. There is no second anchor at distance four: the gap is `2^(m+1)(2^r−2^(−r))`, always at least twelve whenever one unmarked vertex is available outside the core.

A complementary **nearly fully marked** scaffold has unequal guards a=2,b=1. Mark the entire core except one hole at signed radius ±r and optionally one additional center hole. The full-core count is `H₀=9·2^(2n+1)+1`. The resulting outputs are
$$
H_0\;\pm\;3\cdot2^n(2^r-2^{-r}),\qquad
H_0\;\pm\;3\cdot2^n(2^r-2^{-r})-2.
$$
To see this, on a full marked core of length `L=2n+1`, `J=2^L-2` (sum K over pairs or count optional two-slot assignments with both slots occupied). Its sum of signed linear terms is `(2^a−2^b)(2^L−1)`; hence the displayed H₀. For any interior core position with local index i, its contact energy against all other core positions is `2^i+2^(L−1−i)`. The corresponding signed linear term is `4·2^i−2·2^(L−1−i)`. Removing it therefore shifts H by `3(2^(L−1−i)−2^i)`. Removing the center alone is neutral, and removing both holes adds a cross-adjustment of `-K(r)=-2`. The interior restriction n>r excludes the boundary correction at a core endpoint.

The two center-unit scaffolds exhaust the possibilities if at least one one-mark B radius is present and the center is the unit move. Adding the center changes H by
$$
(2^a-2^b)2^n -\sum_{v\text{ marked}}K(|v|),
$$
where the sum ranges from 0 to `2^(n+1)`. For equal guards its magnitude is 2 exactly when the sole other mark is at radius 1 or 2. For a>b, only `(a,b)=(2,1)` can be within two of the available contact range; then the sum of **missing** contact energies must equal two, so there is precisely one hole at radius 1 or 2. For a<b, the signed term is negative with magnitude at least `2^(n+1)≥4`, and subtracting nonnegative energy cannot give a unit move. Thus the product bank does not give three consecutive odd counts *via its single central unit plus an opposite orientation*. In the two cases above the unit moves occur in disjoint pairs. Other edits, larger head assemblies, or coincidental hits using a different symmetric move are not ruled out.

## Implication for targeting the exact count

The primitive triple does not by itself provide a tournament. Its real lesson is that a reflection which kills a mixed term is valuable only when the unmatched term also solves a literal integer inverse problem. Here the two physical banks do cancel their mixed terms, and the center supplies a genuine absolute unit, but the antisymmetric orientation switch is not itself a second unit-scale motion. To use this product for consecutive placement, one must change what moves in at least one bank (for example, a noncentral block involution or different head assembly). Growing the two guards alone cannot fix the explicitly computed gap.

## An exponentially growing bank of exact absolute pairs

The guard asymmetry can reside in the entire tape, not just the fixed fully marked core. Use `N=2R+4`, `R≥2`, with pivot `i=R+2`. The first and last tape vertices remain unmarked. There are eligible radii `1,...,R+1` to the left and `1,...,R` to the right. The signed linear term at the pivot is `2^(R+1)`. Divide the pivot's contact weights by two: radii 1 and 2 each have weight one, then radius d has weight `2^(d−2)`. In particular let x,y describe marks at radii at least two on the left and right as ordinary binary addresses. The two radius-one bits are u,v. Then the pivot flip has exact increment
$$
2\bigl(2^R-x-y-u-v\bigr),
\quad 0≤x<2^R,\quad 0≤y<2^{R-1},\quad u,v∈\{0,1\}.
$$
This classifies the pivot-unit masks at each even width: **exactly `2^(R+1)` choices** give increment +2 (each y,u,v uniquely determines an admissible x), and **`2^(R+1)−4` choices** give −2 (the four excluded choices have `y+u+v<2`).

To get a provably large bank of **distinct absolute anchors**, use the +2 branch with u=v=0. Then x is the length-R binary complement of y. Equivalently, force a left mark at radius R+1, choose exactly one marked position at every radius `d=2,...,R`, and leave radius one empty. Choose the right position when bit `d−2` of y is one. Let `H_y` be the readout with the pivot unmarked. Both `H_y` and `H_y+2` are physical. All `2^(R−1)` pairs are distinct, and their anchors are strictly increasing in y, with large separation.

Indeed when incrementing y, the carry flips the position at one radius t from left to right and every radius d=2,...,t−1 from right to left. All marked positions in this changed group are reflected simultaneously, so their mutual interaction is unchanged. Put `λ_d=2^d−2^(−d)`. The net gain in the signed linear term is
$$
3\cdot2^{R+1}\Delta,qquad
\Delta=\lambda_t-\sum_{d=2}^{t-1}\lambda_d=9/2-3\cdot 2^{-t}>0.
$$
Every remaining marked radius r>t interacts with changed radius d through `K(r+d)−K(r−d)=2^(r−1)λ_d−[r=d+1]`. The sign is the same across the changed set apart from the carry signs already in Δ. Thus the absolute change of all mixed interactions is at most `Σ_{r=t+1}^{R+1}2^(r−1)Δ+1 < 2^(R+1)Δ+1`. This is smaller than the signed gain by order `2^R`. Inserting the same pivot in every graph does not change the comparison because its own step is always +2.

The absolute size bound is `2^(N−2) < H < 2^(N+1)` for tapes of width N≥4: the negative linear terms have total magnitude at most `2^(N−1)−2`, all pair terms are bounded by the full internal interval's `2^(N−2)−2`, and the direct positive run sum is at most `1+Σ_{i=1}^N2^i`. These bounds give `Θ(sqrt X)` distinct anchors below X over the constructed bank (lower bound from a single adequately sized width; upper bound by summing the geometric number of choices across surviving widths). Neither the exact mask multiplicity nor the square-root supply proves a single anchored interval. In fact the anchored pairs in this selected branch remain far apart at any fixed large width. Achieving another order of freedom, enough to refill those separations, is the next physical target.

## Why a fixed pivot cannot give a universal constant-scale refill by signature matching

Let V be an inner mask around the pivot (excluding the pivot), with
signed positions v. Write E(V) = Σ K(|v|) and
D(V) = Σ (2^v−2^(−v)). Outside marks at larger absolute radii
contribute to the readout difference via two scalars L and M, so
the placement-sensitive part of an inner difference is
L·ΔΣ2^v − M·ΔΣ2^(−v), apart from the internal J difference.
Specifically L = 2^i − (1/2)Σ_(left outside)2^(−v) and
M = 2^(N−1−i) + (1/2)Σ_(right outside)2^v. The only interaction
not accounted for in those scalars is the adjacency bonus, when
an outside mark and an inner mark are adjacent. For a pivot +2
unit, L−M = E(V)+2 (all outside distances are ≥2, and radius 2
has no adjacency bonus against the pivot). Thus a tempting refill
scheme is to match E and D between two inner masks, killing the
dependence on the common exterior scalar M.

**Rigidity.** When both sides of the pivot are at least two from
the tape's two ends (so its bare linear term is divisible by 4),
the pair (E,D) distinguishes every two different inner masks
compatible with a unit pivot on the same exterior, provided the
outer marks are all at radii ≥3. Proof: write
D = Σ ε_r(2^r−2^(−r)), with ε_r in {−1,0,1}.
These signed digits are unique. In a nonzero difference take the
highest occupied radius t. If its coefficient is ±1, multiplying
by 2^t gives an odd integer, impossible for a zero difference.
If it is ±2, its real magnitude exceeds twice the sum of all
lower basis elements, so it cannot cancel either. Therefore D
pins which radii are singly occupied and on which side. E then
determines the other pair occupancies by binary uniqueness,
except for the duplicate weights at radii 1 and 2. The only
potential ambiguity swaps two marks at ±1 for two at ±2, with
the other radius entirely empty. Both configurations have total
contact divisible by four (the combined inner contact is 4 and
every farther mark contributes a multiple of four). They cannot
give pivot change ±2, since the bare linear term is divisible by
four too. This rules out that particular matched-signature
route to two different anchors at all widths.

Next experiments should let the pivot itself change, allow
mismatched signatures with a target-selected exterior scalar,
or use a second head. The lemma does not rule out any such move.
