# Predictions after the spectrum atlas and the complement census

1. **Strong completeness.** The set of odd numbers that are not the Hamiltonian-path count of any strong tournament is exactly {1, 7, 21}. The all-tournament form is S. H. Chan and I. Pak, *Computational complexity of counting coincidences*, Theoret. Comput. Sci. 1015 (2024) 114776, arXiv:2308.10214, Conjecture 6.2 (h takes all odd values except 7 and 21), due to MathOverflow user bof and Gordon Royle (2016, MO 232751); Royle verified it for all odd values below 80,557 with tournaments on at most eleven vertices (THM-1370 records the omissions of 7 and 21); the strong form is HYP-9029's tiling law, with strong witnesses for every odd value of [65, 80265] (THM-4102 / THM-4104) and complete inventories below 625 (THM-4744). This session reproduces it to 6,000: exact below 200 by augmentation (THM-4810 (B)), THM-4097's exact S_9 re-found as 1,482 certificates, order-10 certificates to 6,000. Falsified by an odd v >= 23 shown, by the strong-ear exhaustion of the orders n with f(n) <= v, to be attained by no strong tournament. Test: THM-4744's strong-ear inventories beyond 625.
2. **Components of the move graph.** For n = 0, 3 (mod 4), n >= 4, the native complement move graph on labelled n-tournaments is connected; for n = 1, 2 (mod 4), n >= 5, its components are exactly the two parity classes of THM-4810 (C). The eccentricity e(n) of the transitive tournament (7, 6, 7, 13 at n = 4..7) satisfies e(n) >= n for n = 0, 3 (mod 4) and the diameter is attained at the transitive tournament. Test: order 8 was run after filing (BFS over 2^28 states): connected, e(8) = 17, prediction **CONFIRMED** (connected, e(8) = 17 >= 8); next, order 9 (two components) by sampling parity classes, and order 11 for the eccentricity law.
3. **Unique strong sources.** F_s(v) = 1 exactly for v = 3, 5, 11, 13; every other attained odd v has at least two strong sources. THM-4744's inventories verify this for every odd v <= 623 (every fibre of [375, 623] has at least 56 classes); this session reproduces it to 123 (minimum 10 on [75, 123]). Test: THM-4744's strong-ear inventories beyond 625.
4. **The quadratic bound is nearly sharp.** THM-4810 (C) gives d(P_q, transitive) >= (q^2 - 1)/8 = 6, 15, 45, 66, 120 at q = 7, 11, 19, 23, 31 and d(P_11, forest frames) >= 5. Prediction: d(P_q, transitive) - (q^2 - 1)/8 stays bounded (it is 1 at q = 7), and the forest-frame distance of P_11 is exactly 5, i.e. there is a word of five complements from P_11 to a forest-framed tournament along which L decreases by exactly one at every step (the depth-two census shows the decrease is tight through depth two, and session73's depth-three witness with f = 12 shows it is tight at depth three). Falsified by an exhaustive depth-five search from P_11 without a forest frame, or by d(P_q) - (q^2 - 1)/8 growing with q.
5. **Feedback deficit.** q(q-1)/4 - f(P_q) = 3.5, 7.5, 21.5, 34.5, 52.5 at q = 7, 11, 19, 23, 31; the ratio to q^(3/2) is 0.19, 0.21, 0.26, 0.31, 0.30. Prediction: f(P_43) between 350 and 365 (the quasi-random order q^2/4 - c q^(3/2) with c near 0.3). Test: the subset DP at q = 43 is out of reach (2^43); a branch-and-bound or an exact ILP would decide it.
