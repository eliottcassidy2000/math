# HYP-9732 - the crossing lemma

**Setting.** A new vertex x beats exactly X ⊆ V(T), with X ≠ ∅, V. A Hamiltonian path P of T has
1 + #XX^c(P) insertion slots for x. The remaining Hamiltonian paths of T + x put x between u ∉ X and w ∈ X with
w → u; there are R of them. With Z the paths of shape [X^c][X] and E the excess crossings,

    δ(X) := H(T + x) − 2H(T) = E + R − Z.

**Original conjecture (refuted).** If E ≥ 1, then δ(X) ≥ 3.

The [literal strong order-11 witness and direct replay](../results/chanpak_near_floor_crossing_20261115/CROSSING.md) give defect −1 with 156 twice-crossing paths. Its two-shelf family makes crossing defect arbitrarily negative at strong orders. An [independent one-growing-shelf formula and min-cut reformulation](../results/chanpak_near_floor_crossing_20261115/SHELF.md) give a second unbounded family. These results do not refute HYP-9712 itself.

**Proposed consequences (the monotonicity claim is refuted).**
- Unit cuts are monotone (E = 0, R = Z ± 1). Every unit extension's out-set is met by each Hamiltonian path in at
  most two blocks, the second of which is final.
- This is the structural core of sign freezing (HYP-9712). Unit cuts are rare and rigid: at most two child types
  per sign through order 9. A second unit move must be monotone with respect to the first newborn.

**Evidence.** Exhaustive through order 8 and sampled at order 9; the lower bound 3 is attained.

**Approaches tried.**
- The bound R ≥ R₀ = H(X)H(X^c) − Z gives δ ≥ E + H(X)H(X^c) − 2Z, which is too weak when Z is large.
- Simple path-shape formulas for δ fail. Neither A − Z (A the [X][X^c] paths) nor A − Z ± (three-block shapes)
  reproduces δ on the unit cuts.

A proof probably needs an involution matching the deficient [X^c][X] paths with reverse-junction covers, with two
spare elements generated by a doubly crossing path.

## Update (wave 28, mac-mini-2026-11-15-bottlenecks)

**Second form of the identity (PROVED).** Write a Hamiltonian path of T + x as Q₁ x Q₂. Let s_{X^c} count the
Hamiltonian paths of T starting in X^c, and e_X those ending in X. Then

    δ(X) = Π(X) − s_{X^c} − e_X,

where Π(X) counts ordered pairs of vertex-disjoint nonempty paths covering V, the first ending in X^c and the second
starting in X. This follows from H(T + x) = s_X + e_{X^c} + Π(X) and H = s_X + s_{X^c} = e_X + e_{X^c}.

**The small-order picture (FINITE-EXACT, all cuts of orders 4–8; it does not persist).**
- δ = 3 with E ≥ 1 occurs only when H(T) = 5, with (E, R, Z) = (2, 2, 1). That is the strong 4-tournament with
  sources and sinks attached, and X alternating around its Hamiltonian 4-cycle.
- Through order 6, E = 1 forces δ ≥ 5.
- At order 8 the minimum of δ over doubly crossing cuts rises with H: 3 at H = 5, 35 at H = 125, 93 at H = 301 and
  657 at H = 641 (`w28_crossing_min_by_H_order8.out`).
- This growth is a small-order artefact. The order-11 witness above (H = 815, δ = −1, E = 156) and the two-shelf
  family make δ arbitrarily negative on doubly crossing cuts. So no lower bound in terms of H survives either.

**Concurrent (credited).** The 7-21 session (notes/06 E6, in progress) found the same H = 5 equality case through
order 7. It also reduced the lemma to a one-vertex deletion step,

    δ_T(X) − 2δ_{T−v}(X − v) = δ_{T−v+x}(Y_x) − 2δ_{T−v}(Y),

verified through order 8 and proved for sources and sinks. By the order-11 witness, that step must fail by order 11.
