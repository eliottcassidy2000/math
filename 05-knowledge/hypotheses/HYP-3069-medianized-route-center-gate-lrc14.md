# HYP-3069: Medianized route-center gate for LRC14

The final LRC14 proof interface should include a medianization check over
packet, route, certificate, sidecar, and discharge states:

```text
serious route triples should have a unique center
after legal sidecars are attached.
```

This turns the prompt's `2,6,12,20,30,42=n(n+1)=2*T_n` cue into an operational
test.  The sequence is not scalar evidence.  It is the same address appearing
in three places:

- directed edges of the `n`-simplex 1-skeleton,
- line count of the adjacent diagonal bridge `K_{n,n+1}`,
- Faulhaber odd-moment carrier `u=n(n+1)`.

S231 already checked the bridge-rank side: `K_{n,n+1}` has `n(n+1)` lines,
rank `2n`, and rectangle debt `n(n-1)`.  S235 adds the next gate: once route
triples are represented in the sidecar cube, the center is coordinate-wise
median.  If projecting away sidecars makes the center ambiguous, the quotient
is not legal yet.

It sits downstream of HYP-3067's Desargues-median warning and HYP-3068's
owner/root spine: those name graph-level empty centers and missing owner/root
objects, while HYP-3069 asks what happens after the named route/certificate
sidecars are represented as a Boolean cube and closed under median.

## Why this helps the final proof

Many current LRC carriers have the same abstract shape:

```text
coarse packet row -> route/certificate evidence -> forgotten sidecar -> exit
```

The median gate asks a sharper question.  Given three serious routes to the
same packet region, is there a canonical center object preserving the shared
payload?  If yes, the route triangle has a Helly-like center and can be
compressed.  If no, the missing center is a named obstruction rather than a
vague incompatibility.

This suggests several concrete proof angles.

1. **Median Helly gate.**  Prove that legal packet carriers form a median
   subalgebra after the fields above are attached.  Then every three-route
   local comparison has a canonical center.

2. **Center-completion theorem.**  Build the median completion of the current
   HYP-2963 packet classifier.  Every added center must be discharged by an
   existing certificate or become a finite F7/THM-572 entry.

3. **Directed-simplex address.**  Use `n(n+1)` as an oriented edge-sector
   address for the center, not as a count.  The rank split `2n + n(n-1)` tells
   which part is cut potential and which part is rectangle debt.

4. **Faulhaber odd-moment bridge.**  Treat `u=n(n+1)` as the coordinate on
   which the median center records odd-moment compatibility.  This connects
   HYP-2454/HYP-2458 to the S231 bridge ledger.

5. **Hodge-cycle lift.**  Feed the median completion into HYP-3066's
   cycle-class map.  A center is legal only if its residual class is generated,
   annihilated, descended, boundary, or named.

6. **Out-of-box stress test.**  Search for triples whose raw center is
   `exact_M` alone but whose full lifts require incompatible sidecars.  These
   are likely where a hidden route condition, not a stronger scalar estimate,
   has to enter.

## Assumption challenge

The tempting assumption is that `n(n+1)` contributes a proof weight because it
shows up as directed simplex edges and Faulhaber `u`.  HYP-3069 challenges
that.  The count is a coordinate address inside a median sidecar cube.  The
LRC predicate preserved by the quotient is not the scalar count; it is whether
the sidecar-completed route triple has a legal center.  Projecting away
simplex/Faulhaber/bridge/Hodge sidecars destroys that legality data.

Thus the final proof should not say "the doubled triangular numbers are the
answer."  It should say:

```text
the doubled triangular address is one coordinate system in which
the final packet/route/certificate/discharge median center is visible.
```
