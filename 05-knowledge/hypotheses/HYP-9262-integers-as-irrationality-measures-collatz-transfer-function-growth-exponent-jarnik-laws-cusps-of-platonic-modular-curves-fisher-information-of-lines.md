# HYP-9262 — integers as irrationality measures

## Statements

1. **(The ladder; PROVED, THM-4632.)** For every irrational `x` in `(1, 2)` the formal law `R_x(s) = 1/(1 - e^(-s)/2 - e^(s(x-1))/2)` has `limsup log|R_x(2 pi i f)|/log f = 2(mu(x) - 1)` on the line; the integer `n >= 2` labels the laws of exponent `2n - 2`, a continuum of Hausdorff dimension `2/n` (Jarnik), with explicit members `a_(k+1) = ceil(q_k^(n-2))` for `n >= 3`; the rational class (`mu = 1`) is exactly the class of laws with poles on the line other than the normalisation pole, of exponent `+infinity` (HYP-9260's rational approximants); the Collatz law `x = log2 3` has exponent `2(mu(log2 3) - 1)`, conjecturally `2`, at most `26.6` by Rhin's effective measure.
2. **(The only abnormality; PROVED for the law, corrected by the audit.)** The only pole of the Collatz law on its line is the normalisation pole `s = 0` (the empty word `(0, 0)`, present for every parameter); every near-return is a strictly off-line pole seen as a Lorentzian peak. For the Collatz map, the trivial cycle `1 -> 2 -> 1` is the word with one odd step and one halving, `(Q, P) = (1, 2)`, the first convergent `2/1` of `theta`, the near-return of largest defect (`theta - 2 = -0.415`) and weakest line (`|R| = 1.04`), whose word `z -> (3z + 1)/4` has the integer fixed point `1` because `2^2 - 3 = 1` (Gersonides: `2^P - 3^Q = +-1` exactly for `(Q, P) = (1, 1), (1, 2), (2, 3)`, the words of the cycles `{-1}`, `{1, 2}` and `{-5, -7, -10}`; the fourth integer cycle, of `-17`, is the near-return `(7, 11)` with `2^11 - 3^7 = -139`). The cycle statement for the integers needs `2^P - 3^Q | B_w`, the numerator of THM-4600's cycle point `c_w = B_w/(2^P - 3^Q)`; THM-4633 (mac-mini, concurrent) turns the Diophantine input into convergent budgets (`2/1` realised at `m = 1`; the later convergents to `Q_23` excluded by the verified minimum `2^71`, Hercher), and the exclusions combine an effective `mu(theta)` with the verified range.
3. **(The sources; typed.)** Liu-Jiang-Zhang's `mu(log r) = 2` (PREPRINT CLAIM) would put `log 2` and `log 3` in the generic class and leaves `mu(log 3/log 2)` open (a ratio of two numbers of exponent `2` can have any exponent); the odd zeta values are in the same position (`mu(zeta(3)) <= 5.513891`; `zeta(5)` irrational iff the law with parameter `zeta(5)` has no pole on its line). Kaczynski's boundary-function theorems (Lean-formalised) do not apply to a function continuous up to its line: no bridge.
