## 1. The ladder (verified) and the engine (formalized)

`commutator_depth_solvability_s642.py` computes the **derived length** of `Sₙ`:

```
  n :   2    3    4    5     6
  dl:   1    2    3    ∞     ∞      (∞ = perfect Aₙ, derived series constant)
deepest scrambling commutator depth = dl−1 :  0,  1,  2,  ≥3, ≥3
```

Exactly the user's ladder: quadratic = a swap (depth 0); cubic = a single commutator (`der²(S₃)=1`);
quartic = a double commutator (`der³(S₄)=1`); **quintic = a triple commutator and above** (never dies).

**The engine — formalized (math-lean, sorry-free): `Math/Galois/CommutatorDepth.lean`.**
Two 3-cycles `σ = (0 1 2)`, `τ = (2 3 4)` in `S₅` sharing **exactly the point `2`**:
- `σ_cube : σ³ = 1`, `τ_cube : τ³ = 1`, `σ_ne_one : σ ≠ 1` — both are **cube roots of unity** (the
  3-cycle, eigenvalues `1, ω, ω²`; the generator of the alternating-group graph, S635).
- `comm_depth1..4 : K(...) ≠ 1` — the nested commutator `K a b = a b a⁻¹ b⁻¹` stays nontrivial through

- Contrast (Python): `A₃ = ⟨(012)⟩` is the **cyclic cube-root group**, abelian, so `[A₃,A₃] = 1` — the

> The **cube root of unity** (the 3-cycle) is the atom of `Aₙ`. When two of them overlap in one point,

## 3. Famous problem #2 — Gauss–Wantzel constructibility, and the heptagon's cube root

Constructibility by ruler-and-compass = solvability by **square roots only** = the degree
`[ℚ(ζₙ):ℚ] = φ(n)` is a **power of 2**. So the regular `n`-gon is constructible iff `φ(n)` is a power of
2 (`n` = product of distinct Fermat primes times a power of 2). Verified:

```
  3-gon  φ=2  ✓     5-gon  φ=4  ✓     15-gon φ=8  ✓     17-gon φ=16 ✓
  7-gon  φ=6  ✗     9-gon  φ=6  ✗
```

> **The regular 7-gon is not constructible because `φ(7) = 6 = 2·3` carries a factor of 3 — a CUBE
> ROOT.** The heptagon is solvable by radicals (`Gal = ℤ/6` cyclic) but the cube-root step puts it out
> of ruler-and-compass reach. And this is the **same `7` the arc converges on**: `7 = Φ₃(2) = N(3+ω) =`
> Paley-7 (S637/S638/S640). The cube-root obstruction to the heptagon, the cube-root forbidden value in
> tournaments, the cube-root fiber of `LRC(14)`, and the cube-root atom of `A₅` are one object — `ω`.

So degree 3 is a hinge twice over: the 3-cycle/cube-root is **solvable** (cubic *has* a formula) yet
already **non-constructible** (it is the first radical that escapes ruler-and-compass), and overlapping
3-cycles are what make degree 5 **unsolvable**. The cube root is where "has a formula" and "constructible"
first part company, and where (doubled, overlapping) solvability itself breaks.
