# HYP-9258 — rates and residues of Collatz coalescence along lags

## Statements

(i) **Rate.** `beta(N) := (1/N) #{n <= N : tau(n) != tau(n+1)}` tends to `0` (THM-4627) and `beta(N) = N^(o(1))`-slowly: conjecturally `beta(N) ~ c (log N)^(-gamma)` with `gamma` between `1/2` (THM-4581's merge law with `T` of order `log N`) and the observed slower decay; the mean length of a maximal run of consecutive integers with equal `tau` is `1/beta(N)`.
(ii) **Residues.** Among `n` with `tau(n) != tau(n+1)`, the difference `tau(n+1) - tau(n)` is, with positive density for each value, in the lattice generated by `8` and `19`, with the six commonest values `+-19, +-38, +-27` (then `+-46, +-8, +-57`); the general law should be that unequal-time coincidences occur at time offsets that are denominators of convergents or semiconvergents of `log_2 3`.
(iii) **Equidistribution.** `E[(-1)^tau(n)] -> 0`; equivalently the sign of `(-1)^tau`, which is constant along runs of length tending to infinity, changes sign on a scale that is `o(N)`.

## Cheapest decisive tests

- (i): compute the un-merged mass of the `(y, y+1)` pair chain at depths `K = 26, ..., 40` exactly (dyadic rationals) and fit the tail; compare with `beta` at `N = 2^K`.
- (ii): census of `tau(n+h) - tau(n)` for `h = 2, 3, 4` and of the time offsets of first unequal-time coincidences; test whether `65` (`3^41/2^65`) and `84` appear next.
- (iii): `E[(-1)^tau]` to `2^34` with a block-variance estimate; a persistent sign would refute (iii).

## Why

THM-4627 makes `(-1)^tau` locally constant along lags on a density-one set; the three statements quantify the complement: how fast the exceptional density decays, what the exceptional differences are, and whether the global average still cancels.
