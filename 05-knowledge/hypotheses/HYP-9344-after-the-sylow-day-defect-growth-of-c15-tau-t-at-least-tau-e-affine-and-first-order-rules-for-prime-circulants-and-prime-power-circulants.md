# HYP-9344 — what the census, the identity and the concurrent packets leave open

## Statements

1. **(Sign of the defect; NUMERICAL.)** `tau_T(H) >= tau_E(H)` for every odd-order `H <= S_n`. Evidence: every computed mixed-prime class through `n = 13` (THM-4728; defects `0, 1, 4, 6, 6` here, the last two for the product and the synchronised class of type `(3,3,5,1,1)` at `n = 13` and `16` in the Codex row), the six Codex probes at `n = 13, 14` (the four at `n = 13` agree with this census; `1376` against `1375` at `n = 14` for the diagonal `F21` and `F21 x C3`), and equality for `p`-groups. The identity `tau_T - tau_E = W + E_even + E_wit - E_T` does not force the sign: `E_T` is positive in the diagonal `F21` and synchronised `C15` rows (`E_T = W`), and `E_T = 32 < W + E_even = 48` at `n = 12`. A counterexample would be an odd `H` with a tournament type carrying many `Aut`-classes of `H` and few witnesses.

Either outcome refutes the other; a value outside `{64, 88}` refutes both.

3. **(Uniform rules for prime circulants; CITED and OPEN.)** The Codex packet `affine_circulant_ideal_obstruction_20261020` proves that an `F_2`-affine multiplier-equivariant type bijection from circulant tournaments onto sign-even circulant graphs exists iff `m = (p-1)/2` is odd or `m = 2` (every affine equivariant rule is `h(R) Delta`; the admissible ones are exactly `gcd(h, x^m - 1) = x + 1` for odd `m`; for even `m > 2` the image would be an ideal containing `1 + x`, hence the sign-odd all-ones word), and `circulant_boolean_degree_20261020` proves that the Boolean degree of any equivariant type bijection is at least `ceil((e-1)/a)` for `e = 2^a` the dyadic part of `m`, `e >= 4` (exactly `2` at `p = 13`, `3` at `p = 17`, unbounded along prime divisors of Fermat numbers). This session reproduced the affine dichotomy independently before the packets were seen (the valuation criterion `v_(x+1)(h) >= 2^(v_2 m) - 2^(v_2 d) + 1`, equal to the packet's Lemma 5, and the exhaustive ideal search for `p <= 31`, beyond the packet's `m <= 12`); it is recorded in the source note, not as a theorem. Open: a first-order formula in the ring language with the input predicate that is a type bijection for every `p` (HYP-9341 (c) conjectures none; the Codex packets explicitly separate Boolean degree, generator-free naturality and first-order definability); the threshold `v_2(p - 1) >= 2` is the first arithmetic invariant any such formula would have to see, and the odd part of `p - 1` is not uniformly definable. Further obstructions now in canon: THM-4724 (mac-mini) proves that every derivative rule `D_c`, `c != 1`, sends the `K_max(p)`-symmetric tournaments to the odd complete graph at infinitely many primes, and the Codex packet `circulant_power_sum_exact_boundary_20261020` decides exactly when the unit-power-sum detector sees multiplier symmetry (`m = 1`, `m` an odd prime power, or `e = 1` and `m = qr`); mac-mini's reserved THM-4725 (no uniform dilation rule; the power-equivariance obstruction) and HYP-9342 (what a uniform rule can be: first-order, power-equivariant, canonical normalisations) are the live continuation of this item.
