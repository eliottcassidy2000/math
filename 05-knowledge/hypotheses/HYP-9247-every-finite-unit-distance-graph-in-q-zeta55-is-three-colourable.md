# HYP-9247 — every finite unit-distance graph in Q(zeta55) is three-colourable

## Statement

Let `L = Q(zeta55)` with the unit-distance graph `x ~ y` iff `(x-y)(bar x - bar y) = 1`. **Conjecture:** every finite subgraph is three-colourable; equivalently (de Bruijn-Erdos) `chi(L) = 3`.

## Why it matters here

THM-4614 proves that a finite context `G` in `L` containing the eight ports changes the six-colour boundary relation only if `G-B` is not three-colourable. So the conjecture, if true, closes the eleventh-root six-colour program at these ports completely; if false, its witness is exactly the first object the program needs.

## Evidence

* **Lower bound 3 (PROVED):** unit five-cycles (`alpha` powers) and the length-eleven closed walk `5 + 3v + 3 bar v = 0` of the thirds lattice.
* **Triangle-free (inherited, PROVED):** a unit equilateral triangle needs `zeta3`, which is not in `L`.
* **Every tested context is three-colourable (FINITE-EXACT):** the mixed-generator graph (8,436 vertices, 24,238 edges), the thirds context (2,278; 6,405), their union (10,598; 30,506), the apex context (846; 2,187) and four balls over the tenth and eleventh roots of unity, the ten Mersenne directions `(2-gamma^j)/(2-gamma^(-j))` and the thirds unit: radius two (1,304 and 1,408 vertices) and radius three (4,974 and 6,000 vertices; 14,224 and 17,170 complete unit edges). All colourings are verified on every complete unit edge, with the edges found by the two-prime filter plus the exact norm.
* **No cheap upper bound (PROVED):** an additive colouring `S -> Z/m` of the unit-path component `S` (a ring containing `1/p` for every prime `p` with `-1` outside the subgroup generated by `p` modulo 55, hence for 2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13, 17) needs `m` supported on conjugation-fixed primes, the least being 19; the residue colouring at the least conjugation-fixed prime with residue degree two, `ell = 109`, is the Cayley graph of `F_(109^2)` on its 110 norm-one residues, whose least eigenvalue `-19.64` gives `chi >= 7` by Hoffman. So any colouring with at most six colours, and in particular a three-colouring, must be a nonlinear rule, as the periodic rule `c(A,B) = c_5(A mod 5) + B mod 3` on the thirds lattice already is.

## Why it might fail

Triangle-free four-chromatic unit-distance graphs exist in the plane (Wormald 1979, 6,448 vertices; Chilakamarri 1995; O'Donnell 1995, 2000, arbitrary girth; Hochberg-O'Donnell's 23-vertex fish). Their coordinates involve specific rotation angles; whether some four-chromatic triangle-free unit graph has all coordinates in `Q(zeta55)` is the open question. `L` has infinitely many unit directions (every `u/bar u`), so it is a rich field, and vertex-transitive balls are a weak probe of chromatic number four, which in known examples is carried by delicate odd-cycle arrangements rather than by density.

## Cheapest decisive tests

* Place a known small four-chromatic triangle-free unit graph (the 23-vertex fish, O'Donnell's 40-vertex graph) in `L`: solve its rigidity equations over `L` with the exact engine; a solution refutes the conjecture and supplies the forcing context's first ingredient.
* Mycielski-type search: unit five-cycles in `L` with shadow vertices (the pentagram apexes are the shadows of the regular pentagon) and a common unit neighbour of the shadows; SAT over the exact unit graph of such configurations.
* A residue-type proof of three-colourability is impossible (above); a proof would need a global nonlinear rule, for example a rule on the exact localization `S` built from the periodic thirds rule and the `alpha`-power rule `a+b+c+d mod 3` on `Z[alpha]`.
