# HYP-2321 — The shell-tower IS a solvable filtration: tournament Aut is odd-order/solvable (Feit–Thompson), the dual of the polynomial side being unsolvable (Abel–Ruffini)

---

## 0. The answer, in one line

It is a solvable filtration, and the "no formula" wall is the **one wall the arc does not cross**:

> **Polynomials (roots, `Sₙ`): UNSOLVABLE for `n ≥ 5`** (Abel–Ruffini — needs the *swap*, S₅ is generated
> by transpositions). **Tournaments (perspectives, `Aut(T)`): always SOLVABLE** — a tournament has **no
> automorphism of order 2**, so `|Aut(T)|` is **odd** (Cauchy) ⟹ **solvable** (Feit–Thompson), and in
> fact `Aut(T) ⊆ Aₙ`. The arc's involution `σ : v ↦ −v` (the swap, S638) is precisely what is forbidden
> as an automorphism — it is the **converse**, an *anti*-automorphism, deliberately outside `Aut(T)`.

So the **two great solvability theorems bracket the roots↔perspectives duality** (FTA, HYP-2275):
Abel–Ruffini on the root side, Feit–Thompson on the perspective side.

## 1. No involution-automorphism (formalized)

A tournament `beats` is irreflexive and, off the diagonal, exactly one direction holds. If `σ` preserves
`beats` and `σ² = 1`, then `σ = 1`: an order-2 `σ` would send some `v` to `w = σ(v) ≠ v` and back, so it
maps the single arc `v→w` to `w→v` — both can't hold. Formally `beats (σv)(σw) ↔ beats v w` at `(v,σv)`
gives `beats(σv) v ↔ beats v (σv)`, while the tournament axiom gives `beats v (σv) ↔ ¬beats(σv) v`;
chaining yields `P ↔ ¬P`. □

**Formalized (math-lean, sorry-free): `Math/Tournaments/AutOddOrder.lean`**
- `tournament_no_involution : (σ preserves beats) → σ*σ = 1 → σ = 1` (general `V`).
- `autGroup beats : Subgroup (Perm V)` — the automorphism group as a subgroup.
- `autGroup_orderOf_ne_two : σ ∈ autGroup beats → orderOf σ ≠ 2` — no element of order 2.

**Classical corollaries** (cited, not re-proved): `2 ∤ |Aut(T)|` (Cauchy: order-2 element ⟹ involution);
`|Aut(T)|` odd ⟹ `Aut(T)` solvable (Feit–Thompson); odd-order permutations are even ⟹ `Aut(T) ⊆ Aₙ`.

Verified exhaustively (`aut_solvable_dichotomy_s643.py`): every tournament on `n ≤ 6` vertices (up to
32768 of them at `n=6`) has **odd** `|Aut|`, **no** order-2 automorphism, and **`Aut ⊆ Aₙ`** (every
automorphism is an even permutation). Max `|Aut|`: `1,1,3,3,5,9` for `n=1..6`.

## 2. The shell-tower realized: `Aut(Paley₇) = ℤ/7 ⋊ ℤ/3`, order 21 (the forbidden value)

The Paley-7 tournament (S638; `i→j` iff `j−i ∈ QR = {1,2,4} = ⟨2⟩ =` cube roots of unity, S640) has

```
  |Aut(Paley₇)| = 21 = 3·7 = the forbidden tournament H-value (S626/S628)
  derived series:  21 → 7 → 1     (solvable, derived length 2; NOT perfect)
  structure:  ℤ/7 (additive, normal)  ⋊  ℤ/3 (the doubling ⟨2⟩ = the cube root, S640)
  element orders:  {1, 3, 7}  (all odd)
```

> So the LRC/tournament **shell-tower is literally a solvable filtration**: `Aut(Paley₇)` peels the cube
> root `ℤ/3` (top layer = the doubling `⟨2⟩`, S640) then the cyclic `ℤ/7` (bottom). The **cube root is a
> solvable LAYER here, not a source of unsolvability.** And its order is exactly the forbidden value
> `21 = 3·Φ₃(2)` — the arc's forbidden number reappears as the size of the perspective group.

The non-solvable `A₅` only arises from the **full symmetric group** (generic polynomial), which is
generated by swaps; tournaments forbid the swap (no involution), so they never reach a perfect group.

## 3. The cyclotomic / LRC side is abelian ⟹ solvable

The LRC cyclotomic Galois group `(ℤ/n)*` is **abelian** (derived length ≤ 1), trivially solvable. The
doubling "shells" `⟨2⟩`-orbits live inside the (multiplicative) structure of `ℤ/n`; for prime `p` the
shell `⟨2⟩` sits in `(ℤ/p)*`. So every cyclotomic/LRC symmetry is solvable — the radical tower is real,
and it is *abelian* at the cyclotomic level, *metacyclic* (`ℤ/p ⋊ ⟨2⟩`) at the tournament level.

## 4. New threads that emerged

1. **The two-theorem bracket.** Abel–Ruffini (root side, `Sₙ`, unsolvable) and Feit–Thompson
   (perspective side, `Aut(T)`, odd ⟹ solvable) are the *same duality* (roots↔perspectives, FTA) seen
   from its two ends. The arc's central duality is bracketed by the two deepest solvability theorems.
2. **`σ` is structurally exiled.** The involution `σ` (antipode/swap/converse, the spine of the whole
   arc, S638/HYP-2185) is exactly the element a tournament cannot have as an *automorphism*; it is an
   *anti*-automorphism. The perspective key's `σ` lives in `Sₙ \setminus`-the-automorphisms — which is
   *why* `Aut(T)` is odd. The swap is the boundary, not an interior symmetry.
3. **`Aut(T) ⊆ Aₙ` = "the discriminant is a square."** Galois `⊆ Aₙ` ⟺ the discriminant is a square;
   tournaments are *always* in `Aₙ`. The tournament "discriminant" is always a square — a perspective-side
   analogue worth chasing (does a natural tournament invariant play the discriminant's role?).
4. **The forbidden value as a group order.** `|Aut(Paley₇)| = 21` = the forbidden `H`. Conjecture/thread:
   the forbidden tournament `H`-values `{7,21}` (cyclotomic `Φ₃(2)`, `3Φ₃(2)`) are the orders of the
   *doubly-regular / Paley* automorphism groups `ℤ/q ⋊ ℤ/((q−1)/2)` at `q = 7` — the cube-root-layered
   solvable groups. Are forbidden `H`-values exactly Paley-type Frobenius group orders?
5. **Infinite caveat (honest).** The no-involution argument holds for *all* tournaments incl. the
   countable random one (S638): `Aut(Rado-tournament)` has **no involutions** either. But it is infinite,
   so "odd order ⟹ solvable" does not apply; `Aut` of the generic tournament is likely a non-solvable
   (simple) infinite group with no involutions. So the clean "solvable" statement is a **finite**
   phenomenon; the *no-involution* (no-`σ`) phenomenon is universal.
