# HYP-9244 — the Polya trichotomy for Haar coalescence

## Setting

* **The map.** `d ≥ 2`. For each residue `i mod d` there is a branch `T(x) = (m_i x + r_i)/d` on `x ≡ i mod d`, where:
  * `m_i ≥ 1` is prime to `d`;
  * `d | m_i i + r_i`.
* **Haar digits.** Each branch maps its class onto `Z_d`. So under Haar measure the branch sequence of `y` is i.i.d. uniform: the d-adic Terras property.
* **The pair chain.** It is written `u = M v + e`.
  * If `v` takes branch `j` and `u` takes branch `i`, then
    `M' = M m_i/m_j` and `e' = (m_i e + r_i − (m_i/m_j) M r_j)/d`.
  * `j` is a fresh uniform digit, and `i ≡ M j + e (mod d)`.
  * The state `(M, e) = (1, 0)` is an equal-time merge. A merge with `M ≠ 1` is null, because it pins `v` to a fixed point.
* **The debt group.** `Γ ⊂ Q_(>0)^×` is generated by the ratios `m_i/m_j`, and `ρ = rank Γ`.
* **The contraction exponent.** `Λ = (1/d) Σ_i ln(m_i/d)`.
* **The debt walk.** `M_n` is a mean-zero walk on `Γ ≅ Z^ρ`. Both marginal digits are uniform, so `E[ln(m_i/m_j)] = 0`.
  * Its step law at each time is one of finitely many laws: the images of the uniform law under the coupling permutations `j ↦ M j + e (mod d)`.
  * Which law is used depends on `(M, e) mod d`, i.e. on the past.

## Statement

For non-degenerate maps, and a start `(1, e)` with `e ∈ Z ∖ {0}`, the following hold. Non-degenerate means the coupling laws that occur span `Γ ⊗ R`, and in rank 0 the finite residual `e`-dynamics reaches 0.

| regime | P(no equal-time meeting by T) |
|---|---|
| `Λ > 0` (expanding), any `ρ` | tends to a positive limit |
| `Λ < 0`, `ρ = 0` | `≤ C e^(−cT)` |
| `Λ < 0`, `ρ = 1` | `≍ T^(−1/2)` |
| `Λ < 0`, `ρ = 2` | `≍ 1/log T` |
| `Λ < 0`, `ρ ≥ 3` | tends to a positive limit |

The mechanism has two factors:
* the offset `e` (normalized by `max(M, 1)`) is multiplied by a fresh uniform multiplier `m/d`, whose mean log is `Λ`;
* absorption needs the debt walk to return to `M = 1`.

So coalescence holds iff the map contracts and the debt walk is recurrent. The tail is the walk's first-return tail times a geometric number of returns.

* **Rank 2 against rank 3.** In rank 2, the mean number of debt visits to `M = 1` among still-unmerged chains grows by a roughly constant 0.22–0.34 per factor 4 of T, i.e. `~log T`. In rank 3 it stops at 0.54, and q freezes.
* **The expanding rows show the second factor alone.** Their debt walks are recurrent (visits `~√T` or `~log T`), but the offset expands, so returns no longer absorb.

## Reading

* **Collatz is rank one in every clock.**
  * In Terras time the equal-time debt group is `3^Z`. The powers of 2 are spent on the clock.
  * In the odd-step clock it is `2^Z`: the letters differ, and the 3-powers are spent.
  * With free times (grand orbits) THM-4581 6(b) reduces again to rank one.
  * So Collatz coalescence is always a one-dimensional null-recurrent debt walk with a contracting offset. Its signature is the `T^(−1/2)` tail.
  * The `1/2` of the orphan law (HYP-9242) is the same rank-one first-return exponent. A rank-two analogue would have orphans decaying like `1/log`; a rank-three one, a positive orphan density.
* **P12, made concrete.** The atlas's rank-two principle (2 and 3 multiplicatively independent) splits here as clock + debt = 1 + 1. A third independent unit would make the debt rank two (slow coalescence); a fourth would destroy coalescence.
* **P1.** Every statement here depends only on the multipliers `(m_i, d)`. The additive constants `r_i` (the "sheet") enter only through rank-0 connectivity. So any argument built on coalescence is sheet-blind, as the atlas requires of divergence arguments.
* **Why this is not just Polya.**
  * The step law of the debt walk is chosen from finitely many mean-zero laws by a rule that reads the past. Some of those laws are degenerate (identity couplings give zero steps).
  * Peres, Popov and Sousi show that such adaptive choices can change the type: two 3-dimensional laws always give transience in `d = 3`, but three laws can produce recurrence.
  * The conjecture is that the arithmetic rule `j ↦ Mj + e mod d` never conspires.

## What would refute it

* A contracting rank-2 map whose debt-zero visits saturate (transient).
* A contracting rank-3 map with q → 0.
* A rank-1 contracting map with a tail exponent other than 1/2.
