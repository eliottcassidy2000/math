# HYP-1822: Natural-number operation graphs are labeled cospans

**Status:** EXPLORATORY synthesis.

## Statement

The natural-number oriented graphs generated by

```text
x+y=z
x*y=z
```

should be treated as labeled operation cospan hypergraphs, not merely as simple
directed graphs.

After forgetting operation fibers, addition collapses to the complete
transitive tournament/order:

```text
x -> z iff x < z.
```

Multiplication, by contrast, forgets to the divisor DAG:

```text
x -> z iff x is a proper divisor of z.
```

Therefore the fundamental additive data lives in the fiber labels `(x,y)` with
`x+y=z`, while multiplicative data survives already in the simple shadow
through prime factorization.

## Evidence

THM-362 proves the additive collapse, the multiplicative divisor-DAG
description, and the prime Hasse reduction.

`natural_operation_graphs_s365.py` verifies the finite shadows.  At `N=80`,
the additive shadow has all `3160` possible transitive-order edges, while the
nonunit multiplicative shadow has only `209`.  The multiplicative Hasse
skeleton is exactly the prime-step graph `x -> x*p`.

This also explains why the earlier summand graph work needed distinct or
labeled summands.  Without labels, every positive complement exists and the
additive graph becomes just the natural order.

## Consequences

- "Incomplete tournament" is a good phrase for the multiplicative shadow, but
  the additive object is only incomplete before forgetting pair labels.
- Goldbach, partition counts, Zeckendorf backbones, and the summand graph are
  fiber phenomena, not simple-edge phenomena.
- Prime factorization is the residue that remains after forgetting
  multiplicative fibers.
