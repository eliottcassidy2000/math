## Everything meets at 6 (and the forbidden 7 is Mersenne) — verified + formalized
- **6 is the FIRST PERFECT NUMBER** (`6 = 1+2+3 = 2·M₂`, Euclid–Euler with the first Mersenne prime `M₂ = 3`). So the
  number my arc keeps returning to — `6 = 2·3 = 3·2` the unique doubled/tripled prime (S630), `lcm(2,3)`, the
  hexagonal/`π/3`/`dZ=1/6` (S623–S628) — is *also* the first perfect number. Formalized `six_perfect`,

- **The forbidden H-value `7 = M₃ = 2³−1 = Φ₃(2)`** (the 2nd Mersenne prime = the cube-root cyclotomic value, S628 =
  the forbidden atom), and **`21 = M₂·M₃`**. So the permanent forbidden pair `{7,21}` are **Mersenne-monoid
  elements** `{M₃, M₂·M₃}`. Formalized `mersenne_three` (`M₃=7`), `forbidden_seven_eq_mersenne_eq_cyclotomic`
  (`M₃ = Φ₃(2)`).
- **The 2nd perfect number `28 = 2²·M₃`** is built from the forbidden-H Mersenne prime `7`. Formalized
  `twentyeight_perfect`, `twentyeight_eq_four_mul_mersenne_three`.
- **Even perfect numbers are even ⟹ never an H-value** (Rédei: `H` is odd). Formalized `six_not_odd`,
  `twentyeight_not_odd`. So perfect numbers lie outside the H-spectrum; the **odd-perfect-number problem** (no odd

## The H-spectrum = multiplicative monoid of strong values (converse of Rédei) — verified
H is multiplicative over strongly-connected components (S626), so the **achievable H-spectrum = the multiplicative
monoid generated by the strong (strongly-connected) H-values** (the atoms). Verified (n≤6): atoms
`{1,3,5,9,11,13,15,17,19,23,25,…}`; the monoid they generate; the **non-monoid odds = `{7,21}` permanently**
(`{M₃, M₂·M₃}`; `35,47,49` fill in at higher n via new strong primes). This *closes the converse-of-Rédei question
below the monoid*: an odd number is an H-value iff it factors into strong atoms; the only sub-monoid gaps are the
Mersenne pair. (Formalization of the monoid multiplicativity — `H(T₁⊕T₂)=H(T₁)·H(T₂)` over the ordered join — is the
next target; the free `3^k` baseline and `forbidden_seven_mul_three_pow` are done, S624b/S626.)

## Tournaments with ties = incomplete oriented graphs (which appear in LRC & unit distance)
Allowing ties (Reframe 2, S631) = removing edges ⟹ general oriented graphs, not complete, not connected. The relevant
families:
- **Unit distance: the unit-distance graph (UDG) IS the tie-graph** — the `=1` edges of the distance tournament
  (ties), an incomplete (often disconnected) graph we *maximize*. Its independence polynomial / `H` is the
  partition-function object; the disproof = inflating the algebraically-coherent tie-set `𝒪_K^1` (S631).
- **LRC: the tight-graph** — at the lonely time the runners achieving the minimum (the active constraints) form an
  incomplete graph: the flow-shell σ-pairs (S619), a *matching* of antipodal pairs (one tight σ-pair per unit clock).
  The "danger graph" at a clock (runners at the origin, `gcd`-many) is its complete-bipartite-ish degeneration.
Both are the **tie-subgraph** of the respective distance/clock tournament — the incomplete oriented graph whose
edge-structure controls the extremal problem. Studying these (independence polynomial of incomplete oriented graphs)
is the shared object: maximize the tie-graph (unit distance) / characterize the tight-graph (LRC).
