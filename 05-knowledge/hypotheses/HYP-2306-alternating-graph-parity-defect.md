# HYP-2306 — the alternating group graph and the parity-defect ladder

Extending the χ-bridge (S634): both LRC and unit distance are `χ(Cay(G, U))`. The **alternating group
graph `AG_n = Cay(A_n, {(1 2 i),(1 i 2)})`** is the **non-abelian, permanently-odd** rung — and it
turns out to be the *uniform* "odd sector" object.

## What AG_n is (verified)

- **`χ(AG_n) = 3` for all `n ≥ 3`** (verified n=3,4,5; AG_6 non-bipartite + 3-colorable). Constant —
  it does **not** grow with `n`.
- **`α(AG_n) = N/3`** exactly (`N=|A_n|`, verified n=3,4,5): **balanced tripartite** — three equal
  independent classes. So AG_n is the canonical **arity-3 multi-sieve / 3-corrector** object.
- **Permanently non-bipartite** (odd-girth 3), because `A_n = ker(sign)` admits **no homomorphism to
  ℤ/2** — the parity 2-coloring is unavailable. Contrast: `Cay(S_n, transpositions)` *is* bipartite
  (`χ=2`, the sign 2-coloring). For `n ≥ 5`, `A_n` is **simple**, so the 3-coloring is not from any
  group homomorphism — it is genuinely graph-theoretic.

## The parity-defect ladder: χ − 2 = "how far past parity"

A Cayley graph is bipartite (`χ=2`) iff a **sign/parity grading** `G→ℤ/2` sends the generators to 1.
Define the **parity defect** `= χ − 2` = the number of independent odd obstructions stacked:

| defect | χ | object | regime |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 2 | `S_n` transpositions; even cycle; even-`n` LRC at the `n`-clock | parity suffices (single-corrector) |
| **1** | **3** | **`AG_n`** (all `n`); odd cycle; odd-`n` LRC | **parity gone, minimally — the odd sector** |
| 2 | 4 | Moser spindle (two glued rhombi) | stacked rigidity |
| 3–5 | 5–7 | the plane (Hadwiger–Nelson) | high rigidity |

**The alternating group is the universal defect-1 object**: it is exactly where parity runs out
(`ker sign`), and no further (`χ=3`, uniform). This is the algebraic embodiment of the canon's
"**worry = the odd sector**" (S587) and the **2-adic seam** (THM-407: even/odd `n` is `χ`-parity).
Hard LRC = positive parity defect = the alternating structure.

## Why χ(AG_n) is flat while χ(plane) grows

`χ` counts *stacked* odd obstructions. `AG_n` has only the **girth-3 obstruction** (the 3-cycle
generator) — one layer, no rigidity tower — so `χ=3` regardless of how large `A_n` gets. The plane
stacks obstructions (spindle on spindle) so `χ` climbs. **The knob is not the size of `G` but the
depth of stacked rigidity in `U`.** Abelian-cyclic (`C_m`) toggles 0/1 by parity; the alternating
group is pinned at 1; the Euclidean lattice stacks to 3+.

## The 3-cycle = the parity unit (canon core)

`AG_n` is generated by **3-cycles** — the smallest odd permutations, the atoms of the project's
Odd-Cycle Collection / Rédei parity theory. `χ=3` is the 3-cycle's own chromatic number propagated
across the whole group: a structure built from 3-cycles is 3-chromatic. This ties the χ-bridge to the
**parity-in-tournaments core**: the tournament's 3-cycles are the parity generators, and their Cayley
graph is the uniform 3-sieve.
